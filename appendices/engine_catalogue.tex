The computational layer is a finite verification surface, not a
substitute for a proof.  It records executable witnesses for finite
identities, normalisation checks, obstruction gates, and source-surface
invariants.  A theorem may use such a witness only at the coordinate
where the test actually lives: the level, chart datum, and ambient
completion must still be stated in the theorem or in the comparison
map that invokes the computation.

count.  The source predicates above are guarded by

\section{Interpretation}
\label{sec:verification-interpretation}

The verification surface has three legitimate uses.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item It supplies finite witnesses for algebraic identities, sign
conventions, normalisations, and small-rank examples that are explicitly
encoded in the named engine.
\item It falsifies over-promotions: a local toric check does not build a
compact Hall algebra, a scalar Borcherds denominator does not construct
the source-side BPS product, and a finite-height PBW check does not
prove an uncompleted pro-object.
\item It records regression tests for reader-facing scope discipline,
including the separation of positive-half CoHA, Drinfeld double,
Fock/evaluation image, terminal scalar shadow, and compact recognition
gates.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently, a computational citation in the manuscript must name the
engine or test, the finite object being evaluated, and the comparison
map whose hypotheses connect that finite object to the surrounding
theorem.  Passing tests alone never prove an unconstructed compact
CoHA, a global \(d=3\) functorial comparison, a Hall--Borcherds
identification, or an analytic completion statement.
