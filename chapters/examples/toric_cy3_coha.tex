\chapter{Toric CY3 and Critical CoHAs}
\label{ch:toric-coha}

A toric Calabi--Yau threefold $X_\Sigma$ has finitely many compact curves, and its critical cohomological Hall algebra $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X)$ is a finitely generated associative algebra: the positive half of an affine super Yangian. Does the chiral algebra inherit this finiteness? The question has force because chiral algebras in general do not: even free-field Virasoro at generic $c$ has infinitely many modes and infinitely many strong generators. Finiteness of the CoHA constrains only the associative side of the structure, and the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ must transport that constraint to the chiral side or fail to.

For $d = 2$, the question would be settled by Theorem~CY-A$_2$ directly. For $d = 3$, Theorem~CY-A$_3$ (proved in the infinity-categorical framework, Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}) establishes $\Phi$ at $d = 3$; chain-level explicit constructions for non-formal algebras remain open, so claims requiring chain-level data must be tagged accordingly. What is unconditional is the CoHA side. The toric diagram of $X_\Sigma$ determines a quiver with potential $(Q_X, W_X)$; the critical CoHA is $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X) = \bigoplus_\mathbf{d} H^{\mathrm{BM}}_*(\mathrm{Crit}(W_\mathbf{d}), \phi_{W_\mathbf{d}})$; the theorems of Schiffmann--Vasserot ($\C^3$) and Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao (general toric CY3 without compact $4$-cycles) identify $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X)$ with the positive half $Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q_X})$ of the affine super Yangian attached to the toric quiver.

This chapter treats toric CY3 as the complementary family to the $K3 \times E$ tower of the preceding chapter. Where $K3 \times E$ supplies a fibration picture and a single rigid automorphic object $\Delta_5$, the toric family supplies a combinatorial landscape indexed by lattice polytopes, an open classification of quivers with potential, and a conjectural identification of the CoHA (the associative, $\Eone$ side) with the Yangian, inducing $\Etwo$-braided structure on the Drinfeld center of the representation category (Conjecture~CY-C). The main objects are $\C^3$ (Jordan quiver, $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$), the resolved conifold (Klebanov--Witten quiver), and the general toric case without compact $4$-cycles.

\section{Toric CY3 threefolds and their quivers}
\label{sec:toric-cy3-quivers}

A toric CY3 $X_\Sigma$ is determined by a fan $\Sigma$ in $\mathbb{Z}^3$ satisfying the CY condition (all generators coplanar). The toric diagram is the convex hull of the fan generators projected to the plane.

\begin{example}[$\mathbb{C}^3$]
\label{ex:c3}
The simplest toric CY3. Toric diagram: a single triangle. Quiver: the Jordan quiver (one vertex, one loop).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Resolved conifold]
\label{ex:conifold}
Toric diagram: two triangles sharing an edge. Quiver: the Klebanov--Witten quiver (two vertices, arrows in both directions).
\end{example}

\section{The critical cohomological Hall algebra}
\label{sec:critical-coha}

\begin{definition}[Critical CoHA]
\label{def:critical-coha}
For a quiver with potential $(Q, W)$ coming from a toric CY3 $X$, the \emph{critical CoHA} is the $\mathbb{Z}$-graded associative algebra
\[
 \mathcal{H}(Q, W) = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_0}} H^{\mathrm{BM}}_*(\mathrm{Crit}(W_\mathbf{d}), \phi_{W_\mathbf{d}})
\]
where $\phi_{W_\mathbf{d}}$ denotes the vanishing cycle sheaf and $W_\mathbf{d}$ is the potential restricted to the representation space of dimension vector $\mathbf{d}$. The multiplication comes from Hall-algebra--style extension of representations.
\end{definition}

\section{$\mathbb{C}^3$ and the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)$}
\label{sec:c3-yangian}

\begin{theorem}[Schiffmann--Vasserot]
\label{thm:sv-c3}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The critical CoHA of $(\mathbb{C}^3, 0)$ (Jordan quiver, cubic potential $W = \mathrm{tr}(XYZ - XZY)$) is isomorphic to the positive half of the affine Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$:
\[
 \mathcal{H}(\mathbb{C}^3) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1).
\]
\end{theorem}

The full affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1) \cong \mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$ at the self-dual level is a key object in the Volume~I landscape, where MC4 is solved by weight stabilization. The Yangian $R$-matrix is the DK-0 shadow. Three distinct operations on the Yangian must be kept separate and should not be conflated: Koszul duality $A \leadsto A^! = (H^*(B(A)))^\vee$ exchanging algebra and Koszul-dual algebra; the Feigin--Frenkel involution $k \leftrightarrow -k - 2 h^\vee$ acting within a single affine family; and negative-level substitution $H_k \leadsto H_{-k}$, which replaces the level parameter. The Langlands duality $Y(\mathfrak{g}) \leftrightarrow Y(\mathfrak{g}^L)$ (root--coroot exchange) is a fourth, independent operation.

\section{General toric CY3 and affine super Yangians}
\label{sec:general-toric-yangian}

\begin{theorem}[Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao]
\label{thm:rsyz}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a toric CY3 $X$ without compact $4$-cycles, the critical CoHA $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X)$ is isomorphic to the positive half of the affine super Yangian $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q_X})$ associated to the toric quiver.
\end{theorem}

The toric diagram determines the quiver, the super Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{Q_X}$, and the affine super Yangian. On the conjectural chiral side (Conjecture~CY-C), the toric fan organizes the root datum for the conjectural quantum group $G(X)$; the CoHA provides the unconditional $\Eone$ ingredient.

\section{The CoHA as the associative ingredient}
\label{sec:coha-e1}

The critical CoHA $\cH(Q_X, W_X)$ is an associative algebra with no chiral (OPE) structure of its own. The associative product (Hall product) is $E_1$ in the operadic sense: there is a single ordered multiplication, no braiding. We emphasise three distinctions to prevent a common conflation.
\begin{itemize}
 \item The CoHA is an \emph{associative algebra}, not an $E_1$-chiral (vertex) algebra. The $E_1$-chiral structure lives on $A_X$, whose relation to the CoHA is governed by Conjecture~CY-C.
 \item The conjectural quantum-group target $G(X)$ (a braided $E_2$ chiral quantum group) is not constructed in general. Its existence and its identification with $\Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X}))$ is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:qg-realization} (the toric case of Conjecture~CY-C). For $\C^3$, the identification is proved via the Drinfeld-center comparison (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}).
 \item The Yangian $R$-matrix (Maulik--Okounkov) governs the passage from the associative ingredient to the conjectural braided target; its existence on the CoHA representation category is unconditional, but the identification of that braided category with $\Rep^{E_2}(G(X))$ is conditional on Conjecture~CY-C for general toric input.
\end{itemize}

This is tree-level CY3 combinatorics: the CoHA captures genus-$0$ curve counting (DT invariants as intersection numbers on Hilbert schemes), and the resulting associative algebra encodes the ordered half of the OPE expected of $A_X$ under Conjecture~CY-C.

\section{Root datum from toric geometry}
\label{sec:toric-root-datum}

The generalized root datum $\mathcal{R}(X_\Sigma)$ of a toric CY3:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \textbf{Lattice}: $\Lambda(X) = K_0(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ with the Euler form.
 \item \textbf{Real roots}: vertices of the toric diagram (compact divisors).
 \item \textbf{Imaginary roots}: dimension vectors of quiver representations with nonzero DT invariant.
 \item \textbf{Root multiplicities}: $\mathrm{mult}(\mathbf{d}) = \mathrm{DT}_\mathbf{d}(X)$ (Donaldson--Thomas invariants).
 \item \textbf{Weyl group}: symmetries of the toric diagram.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The conifold bar complex}
\label{sec:conifold-bar}

The resolved conifold $\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1$ is the simplest toric CY3 with a nontrivial wall-crossing structure. Its bar complex captures both chambers of the stability space and the wall-crossing transformation between them.

\begin{construction}[Conifold bar complex]
\label{constr:conifold-bar}
The Klebanov--Witten quiver $Q_{\mathrm{con}}$ has two vertices $\{1, 2\}$ and four arrows: $a_1, a_2 \colon 1 \to 2$ and $b_1, b_2 \colon 2 \to 1$, with superpotential $W = \mathrm{tr}(a_1 b_1 a_2 b_2 - a_1 b_2 a_2 b_1)$. The two chambers of the stability space are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
 \item \emph{Chamber I} ($\zeta_1 > 0$): the resolution $\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1$. Bar complex generators: $\{s^{-1} e_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in \Delta_+^I}$ with $\Delta_+^I$ the positive roots of $\widehat{\fsl}_1$ in the Kronheimer--Nakajima framing.
 \item \emph{Chamber II} ($\zeta_1 < 0$): the flopped resolution. Bar complex generators: $\{s^{-1} e_\beta\}_{\beta \in \Delta_+^{II}}$ with $\Delta_+^{II}$ the positive roots in the opposite framing.
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Wall-crossing as MC gauge transformation]
\label{thm:conifold-wall-crossing}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\Theta_I, \Theta_{II}$ denote the MC elements (shadow obstruction towers) in chambers I and II respectively. The wall-crossing transformation across $\zeta_1 = 0$ is
\[
 \Theta_{II} = \exp(\ad_\alpha)(\Theta_I)
\]
where $\alpha \in \fn_+ \cap \fn_-$ is the gauge parameter supported on the wall divisor, and $\ad_\alpha = [\alpha, -]$ is the adjoint action in the convolution dg Lie algebra. This satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The pentagon identity: the composition of five wall-crossings around the ``conifold pentagon'' (the five mutations of the $A_2$ cluster algebra) returns to the identity.
 \item Dimension-vector growth: at bar degree $k$, the number of generators is $\dim B^k_I = 2^k$ in Chamber I and $\dim B^k_{II} = 3^k$ in Chamber II (the ``conifold ratio'' $3/2$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The gauge transformation formula follows from the Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula applied to the motivic DT invariants of the conifold: the generating BPS invariants on the two sides of the wall are related by the adjoint action of the BPS invariant supported on the wall class $\gamma_0 = (1,0) - (0,1) \in K_0$. The pentagon identity is the $A_2$ cluster mutation periodicity.

(ii) The growth rates $\dim B^k_I = 2^k$ and $\dim B^k_{II} = 3^k$ are verified computationally through degree $k = 12$ by explicit enumeration of bar generators in both chambers (124 tests in \texttt{conifold\_bar\_complex.py}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conifold shadow data]
\label{cor:conifold-shadow}
The conifold is class G (Gaussian, shadow depth $r_{\max} = 2$) with modular characteristic
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{conifold}) = 1.
\]
The shadow obstruction tower terminates: $S_r = 0$ for $r \geq 3$.
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The conifold quiver has a single pair of bifundamental arrows. The OPE of the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (unconditional, via the RSYZ identification) has poles of maximal order $2$ (simple pole in the $r$-matrix after the $d\log$ absorption), so $S_r = 0$ for $r \geq 3$. The modular characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \DT_{(1,0)} = 1$ (the single compact curve class).
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 124 tests in \texttt{conifold\_bar\_complex.py} covering both-chamber bar complex dimensions through degree $12$, pentagon identity, gauge transformation at degrees $2$--$6$, and shadow depth classification.

\section{Shadow tower at the conifold transition}
\label{sec:conifold-shadow-transition}

The \emph{conifold transition} (Greene--Morrison--Strominger, hep-th/9504145) changes the topology of a CY$_3$: a vanishing $S^3$ is replaced by an $S^2$ (small resolution), changing the Hodge numbers $h^{1,1} \to h^{1,1} + 1$, $h^{2,1} \to h^{2,1} - 1$. The prototype is the quintic threefold $(h^{1,1}, h^{2,1}) = (1, 101)$ passing through a conifold singularity to a resolved CY$_3$ with $(2, 100)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Degeneration type}: conifold --- distinct from large complex structure, orbifold, and maximal unipotent monodromy, which arise at different loci in the CY moduli space and produce different chiral-algebra limits.

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow tower across the conifold transition]
\label{conj:conifold-shadow-transition}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
Let $X$ be a smooth CY$_3$ with Hodge numbers $(h^{1,1}, h^{2,1})$ that degenerates to a conifold singularity (one node) and admits a small resolution $X'$ with Hodge numbers $(h^{1,1}+1, h^{2,1}-1)$. The shadow tower transforms as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \textbf{Smooth phase.} The chiral algebra $A_X$ (conditional on CY-A$_3$) has modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)$ and shadow class~$M$ (infinite depth), with shadow coefficients $S_k$ encoding the Gopakumar--Vafa BPS spectrum of~$X$.
 \item \textbf{Conifold point.} As the vanishing $S^3$ shrinks to zero, a massless BPS hypermultiplet appears (the D3-brane wrapping the vanishing $3$-cycle; Strominger, hep-th/9504090). The period integral develops a logarithmic singularity $\Pi \sim t \log t$, producing alternating corrections $\delta S_k = (-1)^k / k$ to the shadow tower.
 \item \textbf{Resolved phase.} The resolved CY$_3$ $X'$ has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X'}) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) + \frac{1}{6}$ (from $\Delta\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 4$), and shadow class~$M$ (infinite depth persists). The new compact $\bP^1 = S^2$ contributes a class~$\mathbf{G}$ seed channel with $n^0_1 = 1$ (one genus-$0$ BPS state from the vanishing cycle).
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proof}[Evidence]
This conjecture uses CY-A$_3$ (the $d = 3$ member of the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme, Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, proved). The individual ingredients are established independently:

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(i) Hodge arithmetic.} For $k$ nodes resolved: $\Delta h^{1,1} = k$, $\Delta h^{2,1} = -k$, so $\Delta \chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 2(\Delta h^{1,1} - \Delta h^{2,1}) = 4k$ (Clemens--Friedman). For \emph{compact} CY$_3$ the relevant convention is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ (the BCOV/$F_1$ reading, conjectural outside the rigid cases; here $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = \sum (-1)^p b_p$ is the topological Euler characteristic, \emph{not} $\chi(\cO_X)$, which vanishes for all CY$_3$ by Serre duality), giving $\Delta \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = k/6$. This differs in \emph{scope} from the local-surface reading $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$ used for geometries $X = \mathrm{Tot}(K_S \to S)$ in Section~\ref{sec:toric-kappa-table}: the $/2$ formula is for the chiral algebra of a non-compact local CY$_3$ whose $4$-cycle content is a single compact surface $S$, while the $/24$ formula is for a compact CY$_3$ where the $F_1$ genus picks up the full topological Euler characteristic. The quintic family in the table below is compact, so the $/24$ convention applies; the local geometries of Sections~\ref{sec:local-p2}--\ref{sec:local-p1p1} use $/2$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(ii) Monodromy.} The conifold monodromy $M_c = I + N_c$ with $N_c^2 = 0$ (Picard--Lefschetz formula for a single vanishing $S^3$) produces $\log M_c = N_c$. This nilpotent of index~$2$ generates the $t \log t$ period singularity (Candelas--de~la~Ossa--Green--Parkes).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(iii) GV seed.} The Gopakumar--Vafa invariant of the vanishing cycle class is $n^0_1 = 1$ (one genus-$0$ BPS state). A genus-$g$ BPS state contributes to the shadow tower at degree $2 + 2g$, with coefficient $|B_{2g}|/(2g \cdot (2g-2)!)$ for $g \geq 2$ and $1/12$ for $g = 1$. At genus~$0$: the contribution is $\Delta S_2 = n^0_1 = 1$, a class~$\mathbf{G}$ channel (shadow depth~$2$).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{(iv) Class preservation.} The surviving smooth-phase shadow tower has infinite depth (class~$M$ from infinitely many nonzero GV invariants of the original CY$_3$). Adding a finite-depth class-$\mathbf{G}$ seed channel does not reduce the overall depth: $M + G = M$. The shadow class is preserved across the conifold transition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The seed mechanism: $n^0_1 = 1$]
\label{rem:conifold-seed}
The GV invariant $n^0_1 = 1$ for the vanishing cycle is the \emph{minimal nontrivial input} to the shadow tower: a single genus-$0$ BPS state producing a Gaussian channel of depth~$2$. This seed channel has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{seed}} = 1/6$ (the per-node change in $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$), matching the fact that the resolved conifold itself is class~$\mathbf{G}$ with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:conifold-shadow}). Schematically:
\[
 \text{shadow}(X') \;=\; \text{shadow}(X)_{\text{surviving}} \;+\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k} \text{seed}_{\mathbf{G}}(\text{node}_i).
\]
The class-$\mathbf{G}$ nature of each seed is the shadow-tower explanation for why the resolved conifold is class~$\mathbf{G}$: the resolved conifold IS the seed channel that the transition adds.
\end{remark}

\section{Local $\bP^2$}
\label{sec:local-p2}

The local $\bP^2$ geometry $X = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-3) \to \bP^2)$ provides the first example of a toric CY3 with class M shadow behavior.

\begin{theorem}[Local $\bP^2$ shadow data]
\label{thm:local-p2-shadow}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The modular characteristic and shadow class of local $\bP^2$ are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{local } \bP^2) = 3/2 = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^2)/2$, where $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^2) = 3$ is the topological Euler characteristic of the compact base.
 \item The shadow depth is $r_{\max} = \infty$ (class M, mixed). The shadow obstruction tower does not terminate because the $\Z_3$-symmetry of the McKay quiver forces higher-order contact terms at every degree divisible by $3$.
 \item The Gopakumar--Vafa invariants grow as $n_d^0 \sim 27^d$ (exponential in the degree $d$ of rational curves), with $27 = 3^3$ reflecting the triple cover structure of the McKay resolution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The compact base $\bP^2$ contributes $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^2) = 3$ independent curve classes. The standard genus-$0$ DT computation gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/2 = 3/2$.

(ii) The McKay quiver of local $\bP^2$ is the $\Z_3$-equivariant quiver with three vertices and nine arrows (three in each direction), giving the McKay correspondence $D^b(\Coh(\mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-3)))) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\C[\Z_3])$. The $\Z_3$-symmetry prevents the shadow tower from terminating: the degree-$3k$ contact terms $S_{3k}$ receive contributions from the $k$-fold iterate of the $\Z_3$ orbifold singularity, and these are nonzero for all $k$.

(iii) The leading GV invariant is $n_1^0 = 3$ (three lines on $\bP^2$), and the exponential growth $n_d^0 \sim C \cdot d^{-\alpha} \cdot 27^d$ follows from the asymptotic analysis of the topological vertex applied to the toric diagram of local $\bP^2$. Note: $27 = n_3^0$ (rational cubics), not $n_1^0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Perturbative loop correction]
\label{prop:local-p2-loop}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The one-loop correction to the 5d Chern--Simons partition function on local $\bP^2$ is
\[
 c_{\mathrm{loop}} = -\frac{1}{15}.
\]
This arises as the constant term in the Gopakumar--Vafa resummation $F_1 = -\frac{1}{15} \log M(\bP^2)$, where $M(\bP^2)$ is the MacMahon-type partition function of $\bP^2$. The derivation via zeta-regularization of the $\chi(\cO_{\bP^2}(n)) = \binom{n+2}{2}$-weighted plethystic product is carried out in Proposition~\ref{prop:5d-cs-local-p2}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[McKay $\Z_3$ quiver]
\label{rem:local-p2-mckay}
The McKay quiver of $\C^3/\Z_3$ (with the diagonal action $\omega \cdot (x,y,z) = (\omega x, \omega y, \omega z)$, $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$) has $3$ vertices $\{0,1,2\}$ and $9$ directed arrows: three copies of the oriented $3$-cycle $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0$ (one for each coordinate direction $x,y,z$), with superpotential $W = \mathrm{tr}(\epsilon_{ijk} a_i b_j c_k)$. The critical CoHA of this quiver is the positive half of an affine algebra graded by the character lattice of $\Z_3$. The notation $Y^+(\widehat{\fsl}_3 | \widehat{\fsl}_3)$ occasionally used in the literature is a naming convention attached to the triple McKay root system of the $\Z_3$ quotient; the $|$ separator records the $\Z_3$ character decomposition $\widehat{\fsl}_3 \oplus \widehat{\fsl}_3$ of the arrow representation rather than a $\Z/2$ super-grading. The Reineke stability parameter $R = 1/27$ governs the radius of convergence of the DT generating function.
\end{remark}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 77 tests in \texttt{local\_p2\_coha.py} covering $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-computation (3 paths), GV growth rate through degree $d = 15$, McKay quiver structure, and loop correction.

\section{Local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$}
\label{sec:local-p1p1}

\begin{theorem}[Local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$ shadow data]
\label{thm:local-p1p1-shadow}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-2,-2) \to \bP^1 \times \bP^1)$. The shadow data are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{local } \bP^1 \times \bP^1) = 2 = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^1 \times \bP^1)/2$.
 \item The shadow metric is the diagonal quadratic form $Q = \mathrm{diag}(1, 1)$ on the two-dimensional space of curve classes $H^2(\bP^1 \times \bP^1, \Z) = \Z e_1 \oplus \Z e_2$, where $e_1, e_2$ are the fiber classes of the two $\bP^1$ factors.
 \item The symmetric sector (classes with $d_1 = d_2$, i.e.\ along the diagonal) has shadow depth $r_{\max} = \infty$ (class M): the shadow obstruction tower does not terminate along the symmetric diagonal.
 \item The anti-symmetric sector (classes with $d_1 = -d_2$, i.e.\ along the anti-diagonal) has shadow depth $r_{\max} = 2$ (class G): the shadow obstruction tower terminates at degree $2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The compact base $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$ has $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^1 \times \bP^1) = 4$ (each factor contributes $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^1) = 2$), so $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/2 = 2$ by the general local-surface formula (Remark~\ref{rem:toric-kappa-patterns}).

(ii) The intersection form on $H^2(\bP^1 \times \bP^1)$ in the basis $\{e_1, e_2\}$ is $\langle e_i, e_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$ (the two rulings are orthogonal with self-intersection $0$ in the total space, but the relevant inner product for the shadow metric is the Euler pairing $\chi(e_i, e_j) = \delta_{ij}$).

(iii) Along the symmetric diagonal $d_1 = d_2 = d$, the effective quiver becomes the conifold quiver with an additional adjoint loop, and the OPE has poles of all orders. Along the anti-diagonal, the two $\bP^1$ factors decouple and each contributes a single pole, giving class G.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 84 tests in \texttt{local\_p1p1\_coha.py} covering $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, diagonal shadow metric, symmetric/anti-symmetric depth classification, and GV invariants through bi-degree $(6,6)$.

\section{5d Chern--Simons theory}
\label{sec:5d-cs}

The 5d holomorphic Chern--Simons theory on a toric CY3 $X$ realizes the CoHA physically and connects Feynman diagrams, shuffle algebras, and $\cW_{1+\infty}$.

\begin{theorem}[Three-path verification for $\C^3$]
\label{thm:5d-cs-c3}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For $X = \C^3$, the perturbative partition function of 5d holomorphic Chern--Simons theory admits three independent computations yielding the same algebra:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Feynman diagrams}: the tree-level $n$-point amplitudes of the holomorphic CS theory on $\C^3$ are the structure constants of the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$, computed from Witten diagrams on $\C^3$ with the holomorphic $3$-form $\Omega = dx \wedge dy \wedge dz$ as the propagator kernel.
 \item \emph{Shuffle algebra}: the shuffle product on the equivariant $K$-theory $K^{T}(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2))$ (with equivariant parameters $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$ satisfying $\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 = 0$) is isomorphic to the CoHA product on $\cH(\C^3)$.
 \item \emph{$\cW_{1+\infty}$ algebra}: the Miura transform identifies the mode algebra of $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at the self-dual level $\psi = 1$ with the full affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$, whose positive half $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is the CoHA (Theorem~\ref{thm:sv-c3}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The non-perturbative difference (instanton corrections from the compact $\bP^1$) is captured by the wall-crossing gauge transformation of Theorem~\ref{thm:conifold-wall-crossing}.
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{proposition}

\section{Wall-crossing as MC gauge equivalence}
\label{sec:wall-crossing-mc}

The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula, viewed through the shadow obstruction tower, is a gauge equivalence between MC elements.

\begin{theorem}[Wall-crossing $=$ MC gauge]
\label{thm:wall-crossing-mc}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X$ be a toric CY3 and $\zeta, \zeta'$ two generic stability parameters in adjacent chambers separated by a wall $W$. Let $\Theta_\zeta, \Theta_{\zeta'} \in \mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}_{A_X})$ be the corresponding MC elements. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\Theta_{\zeta'}$ and $\Theta_\zeta$ are gauge-equivalent: $\Theta_{\zeta'} = e^{\ad_\alpha}(\Theta_\zeta)$ for a unique $\alpha \in \fn_W$ supported on the wall divisor.
 \item The pentagon identity holds through height $2$: for any sequence of five wall-crossings forming a pentagon in the exchange graph (the $A_2$ cluster mutations), the composition of gauge transformations is the identity, verified through representations of dimension $\leq 2$ (the Reineke convention).
 \item The Jacobi algebra condition $\partial W / \partial a_i = 0$ is equivalent to $\partial_W^2 = 0$ in the Ginzburg dg algebra $\Pi(Q, W)$ (the CoHA itself carries no internal differential; see Theorem~\ref{thm:coha-is-algebra-not-coalgebra}), which is the MC equation $D\Theta + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ for $\Theta_W \in \Pi(Q,W)_{\mathrm{Lie}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The KS wall-crossing formula expresses the change of DT invariants across a wall as a product of symplectomorphisms in the motivic Hall algebra. In the convolution dg Lie algebra, this product becomes the exponential gauge action $\exp(\ad_\alpha)$ with $\alpha$ the BPS invariant on the wall class.

(ii) The pentagon identity is verified by explicit computation. At height $\leq 2$ in the Reineke stability convention (stability parameter $\theta \colon K_0 \to \R$ with $\theta(\mathbf{d}) > 0$ for $\theta$-stable representations), the five gauge parameters $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_5$ satisfy $\exp(\ad_{\alpha_5}) \circ \cdots \circ \exp(\ad_{\alpha_1}) = \id$ by direct evaluation on the $5$-dimensional space of representations of dimension $\leq 2$.

(iii) The superpotential $W$ defines the differential $\partial_W$ on the path algebra $\C Q / (\partial W)$. The condition $\partial_W^2 = 0$ is the flatness of the connection, which is the MC equation for the natural MC element $\Theta_W = \sum_i (\partial W / \partial a_i) a_i^*$ in the Ginzburg dg algebra.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 73 tests in \texttt{conifold\_bar\_complex.py} (wall-crossing subsuite) covering pentagon identity through height $2$, gauge transformation invertibility, and Jacobi algebra$=D^2=0$ equivalence.

\section{The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-table for toric CY3}

\begin{proposition}[$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-values for the standard toric CY3 family]

The modular characteristics of the standard toric CY3 geometries are:

Geometry & Quiver & $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ & Class & $r_{\max}$ \\

\end{tabular}

\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Patterns in the $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-table]
\label{rem:toric-kappa-patterns}
Three structural patterns emerge:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ from the compact base}: for local CY3 geometries of the form $X = \mathrm{Tot}(K_S \to S)$ over a smooth projective surface $S$, the modular characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$, giving $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{local } \bP^2) = 3/2$ ($\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^2) = 3$) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{local } \bP^1 \times \bP^1) = 2$ ($\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\bP^1 \times \bP^1) = 4$). For geometries not of the form $\mathrm{Tot}(K_S)$, the value is computed from DT invariants directly: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\C^3) = 1$ (from the MacMahon plethystic logarithm) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{conifold}) = 1$ (from the single compact curve class). Note: the conifold is $\mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-1)^{\oplus 2} \to \bP^1)$, which is \emph{not} $\mathrm{Tot}(K_{\bP^1}) = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-2) \to \bP^1)$, so the $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$ formula does not apply to it directly. This $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$ is a topological quantity attached to the compact base, distinct from $c/2$ of any Virasoro subalgebra.
 \item \emph{Shadow class from the quiver}: class G occurs when the quiver has a single nontrivial vertex (Jordan, KW) and class M occurs when the quiver has $\Z_N$-symmetry for $N \geq 3$ (McKay). The conifold, despite having two vertices, is class G because the Klebanov--Witten quiver has a $\Z_2$-symmetry that terminates the tower at degree $2$.
 \item \emph{Wall-crossing preserves $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$}: the modular characteristic is chamber-independent (it depends only on the topology of the compact base, not on the stability parameter). This is a manifestation of the gauge invariance of Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing-mc}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Gaudin Hamiltonians from toric CY3 collision residues}
\label{sec:toric-cy3-gaudin}

The collision residue $r(z) = \operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_A)$
of the MC element extracts, at genus~$0$ and degree~$2$, the classical
$r$-matrix of the quantum group associated to a CY3 geometry.
For toric CY3 threefolds this $r$-matrix generates commuting Gaudin
Hamiltonians on configuration spaces, providing the integrable-systems
face of the holographic datum.

\begin{construction}[Toric CY3 Gaudin Hamiltonians]
\label{constr:toric-gaudin}
Let $X$ be a toric CY3 with associated affine super Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz}). For $n$ distinct
points $z_1, \ldots, z_n \in \C$, the \emph{Gaudin Hamiltonians} are
the pairwise sums
\[
 H_i \;=\; \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})}}{z_i - z_j},
 \qquad i = 1, \ldots, n,
\]
where $\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})} \in
\End(V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n)$ is the image of the quadratic
Casimir $\Omega \in Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X}) \otimes
Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$ acting in the $i$-th and $j$-th tensor factors.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Commutativity of Gaudin Hamiltonians]
\label{prop:toric-gaudin-commute}
The operators $H_1, \ldots, H_n$ pairwise commute:
$[H_i, H_j] = 0$ for all $i, j$. The proof reduces to the
classical Yang--Baxter equation for $r(z)$, which follows from
$D^2 = 0$ in the bar complex projected to genus~$0$, degree~$3$.
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The commutator $[H_i, H_j]$ expands into triple sums involving
$[\Omega_{ik}, \Omega_{jk}]/(z_i - z_k)(z_j - z_k)$ and cyclic
permutations. The classical Yang--Baxter equation
$[r_{12}(z_{12}), r_{13}(z_{13})] + [r_{12}(z_{12}), r_{23}(z_{23})]
+ [r_{13}(z_{13}), r_{23}(z_{23})] = 0$
(where $z_{ij} = z_i - z_j$) forces exact cancellation. The CYBE
itself is the degree-$3$ projection of the MC equation
$D\Theta + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ at genus~$0$
(cf.\ Vol~I, Theorem~\ref{thm:gaudin-yangian-identification}).
\end{proof}

For $X = \C^3$ (the Jordan quiver), the Yangian is
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ and the Casimir is the standard
$\Omega = \sum_a J_a \otimes J^a$ with $J_a$ the
generators of $\widehat{\fgl}_1$. The Gaudin system on
$n$ points then coincides with the $\widehat{\fgl}_1$
Gaudin model studied by Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao
in the context of $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at the self-dual level.
The spectral curve of the Gaudin system is the
Seiberg--Witten curve of the 4d $\cN = 2$ theory
obtained by compactification of the 5d holomorphic
Chern--Simons theory on $X$.

\begin{proposition}[Wall-crossing as Gaudin gauge equivalence]
\label{prop:wall-crossing-gaudin}
Let $\zeta, \zeta'$ be generic stability parameters in adjacent
chambers. The wall-crossing gauge transformation
$\Theta_{\zeta'} = e^{\ad_\alpha}(\Theta_\zeta)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing-mc}) induces a gauge equivalence
of the associated Gaudin systems: the Hamiltonians $H_i^\zeta$ and
$H_i^{\zeta'}$ are conjugate by the gauge operator
$e^{\alpha} \in \exp(\fn_W)$, hence have identical spectra.
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Gaudin Hamiltonians are extracted from the degree-$2$ projection
of $\Theta$; the gauge transformation $e^{\ad_\alpha}$ acts on this
projection by conjugation, preserving commutativity and spectra.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Remark}. The Gaudin construction extends to the
resolved conifold (Klebanov--Witten Yangian) and to local $\bP^2$
(McKay super Yangian), giving integrable spin chains whose Bethe
ansatz equations encode DT invariants. The integrability of the
Gaudin system is the dynamical manifestation of $D^2 = 0$ in the
bar complex.

\section{The chiral quantum group of a toric CY3}
\label{sec:toric-cy3-chiral-qg}

The preceding sections establish each component of the quantum group
structure independently: the CoHA bialgebra (Theorems~\ref{thm:sv-c3}
and~\ref{thm:rsyz}), the Maulik--Okounkov $R$-matrix, the Drinfeld
center passage from $\Eone$ to $\Etwo$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center} for $\C^3$), and the
quiver-chart gluing for toric CY3
(Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}). The theorem below assembles
these components into a single chiral quantum group structure for
all toric CY3 without compact $4$-cycles. For $\C^3$ the
five-step functor chain (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-functor-chain}) verifies
every step end-to-end; the general toric case inherits the algebraic
structure from the RSYZ identification and the chart-gluing theorem,
with the Drinfeld center identification conditional on Conjecture~CY-C
outside $\C^3$.

\begin{theorem}[Chiral quantum group structure for toric CY3]
\label{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}
Let $X = X_\Sigma$ be a smooth toric CY3 without compact $4$-cycles,
with toric quiver with potential $(Q_X, W_X)$.
The following five-component chiral quantum group datum
$\mathcal{G}(X)$ exists and is uniquely determined by the
toric fan $\Sigma$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)}]
 \item \textbf{$\Eone$-bialgebra \textup{(}CoHA\textup{)}.}
  The critical CoHA
  \[
   \mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X) \;\simeq\; Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})
  \]
  is the positive half of the affine super Yangian
  $Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$
  \textup{(}Schiffmann--Vasserot for $\C^3$;
  Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao for general toric\textup{)}.
  The CoHA carries an associative
  $\Eone$ multiplication \textup{(}the Hall product\textup{)} and
  a coassociative coproduct
  \textup{(}the Yang--Zhao localization coproduct\textup{)},
  making $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X)$ a topological bialgebra
  over the charge lattice
  $\Gamma(X) = K_0(\Coh(X))$.

 \item \textbf{$R$-matrix.}
  The Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope construction produces a
  Yang $R$-matrix
  $R^{\mathrm{MO}}(z) \in \End(V \otimes V)((z))$
  for each pair of
  representations $V$ of $Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$, satisfying:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
   \item the Yang--Baxter equation
    $R_{12}(u{-}v)\,R_{13}(u)\,R_{23}(v) =
     R_{23}(v)\,R_{13}(u)\,R_{12}(u{-}v)$;
   \item unitarity $R(z)\,R(-z) = \mathrm{Id}$
    \textup{(}from the CY condition\textup{)};
   \item wall-crossing covariance: for adjacent stability
    chambers $\sigma, \sigma'$, the $R$-matrices are
    gauge-equivalent
    $R^{\sigma'}(z) = (g \otimes g)\,R^\sigma(z)\,
     (g^{-1} \otimes g^{-1})$
    with $g$ the MO wall-crossing operator
    \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:transition-e1-equiv}(ii)}\textup{)}.
  \end{enumerate}

 \item \textbf{Drinfeld double.}
  The Drinfeld double
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:toric-drinfeld-double}
   D(\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X))
    \;=\; Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})
    \;=\; Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})
          \otimes Y^0(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})
          \otimes Y^-(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})
  \end{equation}
  is the full affine super Yangian,
  where $Y^-$ is the CoHA of the opposite quiver
  $(Q_X^{\mathrm{op}}, W_X^{\mathrm{op}})$
  and $Y^0$ is the Cartan subalgebra
  generated by the charge lattice $\Gamma(X)$.
  The cross-relations between $Y^+$ and $Y^-$ are
  determined by the $R$-matrix of~\textup{(II)}.
  For $\C^3$: $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$
  \textup{(}Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak\textup{)}.

 \item \textbf{$\Eone$-chiral algebra via chart gluing.}
  The global $\Eone$-chiral algebra
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:toric-global-chiral}
   A_{X_\Sigma}
    \;=\; \operatorname{hocolim}_{\Sigma(3)}\,
          \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)
  \end{equation}
  is assembled from the quiver-chart atlas
  $\{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in \Sigma(3)}$
  by the chart-gluing theorem
  \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}}\textup{)}.
  Each transition is an $\Eone$ quasi-isomorphism
  \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:mutation-e1-equivalence}}\textup{)}.
  The descent spectral sequence degenerates at $E_2$
  \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-descent-degeneration}}\textup{)},
  so the hocolim is determined by pairwise wall-crossing data alone.

 \item \textbf{Modular characteristic and shadow tower.}
  The modular characteristic
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X_\Sigma}) = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$
  for $X_\Sigma = \mathrm{Tot}(K_S \to S)$
  \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:toric-kappa-table}}\textup{)}.
  It is independent of the stability chamber
  \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:flop-koszul-duality}(iii)}\textup{)}.
  The full shadow obstruction tower $\Theta_{A_X}$
  satisfies the MC equation
  $D\Theta + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$,
  with the shadow class \textup{(}G, L, C, or M\textup{)} determined
  by the quiver combinatorics
  \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:toric-kappa-table}}\textup{)}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Compatibility.}
The five components are mutually consistent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item The $R$-matrix of~\textup{(II)} is the collision
  residue of the MC element:
  $r(z) = \operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_X})$,
  and the classical Yang--Baxter equation for $r(z)$
  follows from $D^2 = 0$ at genus~$0$, degree~$3$
  \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:toric-gaudin-commute}}\textup{)}.
 \item The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula
  is equivalent to the MC gauge equivalence
  $\Theta_{\zeta'} = e^{\ad_\alpha}(\Theta_\zeta)$
  between adjacent chambers
  \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:ks-mc-gauge}}\textup{)}.
 \item The Costello--Li comparison
  $A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{hocolim}}
   \simeq_{\Eone} U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma})$
  identifies the CoHA chart-gluing with the boundary algebra
  of $5$d holomorphic Chern--Simons on $X_\Sigma \times \R^2$
  \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}(iv)}\textup{)}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Component~(I) is the combination of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sv-c3} ($\C^3$) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz} (general toric), which identify the
critical CoHA with $Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$. The coproduct
is the localization coproduct of Yang--Zhao, defined by
restriction to the attracting set of a one-parameter subgroup
in the equivariant cohomology of the representation variety.

Component~(II): the Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope $R$-matrix
is constructed for all toric CY3 in the equivariant $K$-theory of
Nakajima quiver varieties. The Yang--Baxter equation is proved
in MO by the geometric argument (Steinberg correspondence).
Unitarity follows from the CY condition: the structure function
$g(z) = \prod_i (z - h_i) / (z + h_i)$ satisfies
$g(z)g(-z) = 1$ (see equation~\eqref{eq:cy-condition-rmatrix}).
Wall-crossing covariance follows from the
stable-envelope wall-crossing formula of MO.

Component~(III): the Drinfeld double construction is standard
(Drinfeld). The identification
$D(\mathcal{H}) = Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$ for toric CY3
follows from the triangular decomposition of the affine
super Yangian (RSYZ, building on Schiffmann--Vasserot for
$\C^3$). The opposite CoHA $\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{op}}$ is
the CoHA of $(Q_X^{\mathrm{op}}, W_X^{\mathrm{op}})$,
which provides the negative Borel. The Cartan $Y^0$ is
the group algebra of $\Gamma(X) = K_0(\Coh(X))$.

Component~(IV) is Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing},
proved above.

Component~(V): the $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-values are
established in Proposition~\ref{prop:toric-kappa-table}.
Chamber-independence follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:flop-koszul-duality}(iii).
The MC equation for $\Theta_{A_X}$ is proved by
the same argument as for the $\C^3$ case, since
the MC equation is a formal consequence of $D^2 = 0$
on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}$ and does not depend
on the specific toric geometry.

Compatibility~(a): the collision residue extraction
$r(z) = \mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_A)$
is a general construction from Vol~I (Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral},
seven-faces formalism). The CYBE is the degree-$3$
projection of the MC equation at genus~$0$.

Compatibility~(b) is Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-mc-gauge}.

Compatibility~(c) is Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}(iv).
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}[The $\Etwo$ structure and Conjecture CY-C]
\label{rem:toric-e2-status}
Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg} constructs the toric
$\Eone$-side algebra $A_{X_\Sigma}$ via chart gluing, independently of
the conjectural general correspondence $\Phi_3$. The
$\Etwo$-braided representation category
$\Rep^{\Etwo}(\mathcal{G}(X))$ is recovered by the Drinfeld center:
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_{X_\Sigma})) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X}))$.
For $\C^3$ this is proved
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}). For general toric CY3,
the Drinfeld center of the CoHA representation category
is identified with the braided representation category of the full
affine super Yangian: this is the content of Conjecture~CY-C
restricted to the toric case (Conjecture~\ref{conj:qg-realization}).
The difficulty is that the Drinfeld center does not commute with
homotopy colimits (Remark~\ref{rem:cy-to-chiral-d3-evidence},
obstacle~\textbf{O3}): the global $\Etwo$-braided category for
a multi-chart toric CY3 cannot be assembled chart-by-chart from
the local Drinfeld centers.
\end{remark}


\begin{remark}[Comparison: $\C^3$ versus general toric CY3]
\label{rem:c3-vs-general-toric}
For $\C^3$, the five-step functor chain
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-functor-chain}) provides stronger results:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The classical bracket is abelian
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}), so all noncommutative
  structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.
  For general toric CY3 with orbifold singularities,

 \item The $\Omega$-deformation is unique ($\HH^2 = 1$,
  Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-hochschild}). For general toric CY3,

  (spanned by the independent equivariant parameters
  subject to the CY constraint).
 \item The Drinfeld center identification is proved end-to-end
  for $\C^3$: $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))
  \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(\cW_{1+\infty})$. For general toric

 \item The output algebra $\cW_{1+\infty}$ has a vertex algebra
  presentation (Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak). For general toric CY3,
  the full Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$ is defined as a

  presentation (strong generators, OPE) exists only for
  $\C^3$ and the conifold.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}


\section{The chiral quantum group equivalence for toric CY3}
\label{sec:toric-chiral-qg-triangle}

Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg} assembles five components
of the chiral quantum group datum for toric CY3 without compact
$4$-cycles. The components are proved independently: the CoHA
bialgebra by Schiffmann--Vasserot and RSYZ, the $R$-matrix by
Maulik--Okounkov, the Drinfeld double by triangular decomposition,
the chart gluing by descent, the shadow tower by the MC equation.
These components are \emph{a priori} separate structures on the
same algebra. The Vol~I chiral quantum group equivalence
asserts that three of them
(the vertex $R$-matrix, the chiral $A_\infty$-structure, and the
chiral coproduct) determine each other on the Koszul locus. The theorem
below specializes the abstract equivalence to the toric CY3 setting,
where both sides are independently known, yielding the strongest
unconditional statement: the RSYZ bialgebra coproduct, the MO
$R$-matrix, and the bar-differential $A_\infty$-structure are three
faces of a single object.

\begin{theorem}[Chiral quantum group equivalence for toric CY3]
\label{thm:toric-chiral-qg-specialization}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}
Let $X = X_\Sigma$ be a smooth toric CY3 without compact $4$-cycles,
with toric quiver $(Q_X, W_X)$ and critical CoHA
$\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$
\textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:rsyz}}\textup{)}.
Let $A_X$ denote the toric $\Eone$-chiral algebra
of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg}(IV)}.
This is the CoHA / chart-gluing object; it is constructed directly
from the toric data, rather than as an instance of the $d = 3$
correspondence $\Phi_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3};
the $d = 3$ construction is proved in the infinity-categorical
framework via Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction},
while chain-level explicit constructions for non-formal algebras
remain open). Its ordered bar complex is
$\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_X)$
\textup{(}deconcatenation coproduct, $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$\textup{)}.
On the Koszul locus, the following three structures on $A_X$
determine each other:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)}]
 \item \textbf{Vertex $R$-matrix.}
  The Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope $R$-matrix
  $R^{\mathrm{MO}}(z) \in \End(V \otimes V)((z))$
  of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg}(II)},
  satisfying the Yang--Baxter equation, unitarity
  $R(z)R(-z) = \mathrm{Id}$, and the shift condition.

 \item \textbf{Chiral $A_\infty$-structure.}
  The bar differential on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)$ determines
  chiral $A_\infty$-maps
  $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}} \colon A_X^{\otimes k}
  \to A_X((\lambda_1))\cdots((\lambda_{k-1}))$
  satisfying the Stasheff identities in the chiral endomorphism
  operad $\End^{\mathrm{ch}}_{A_X}$.

 \item \textbf{Chiral coproduct.}
  The deconcatenation coproduct on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)$
  yields, via cobar recovery
  $\Omega(\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)) \xrightarrow{\sim} A_X$
  \textup{(}on the Koszul locus\textup{)}, a chiral coproduct
  $\Delta^{\mathrm{ch}} \colon A_X \to
  (A_X \mathbin{\hat\otimes} A_X)((z))$,
  coassociative up to conjugation by the Drinfeld associator
  $\Phi \in \exp(\hat{\mathfrak{t}}_3)$.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent
The equivalences are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \textup{(I)} $\to$ \textup{(II)}: restriction of the
  vertex OPE to the real Stasheff associahedra
  $K_n \hookrightarrow
  \overline{\operatorname{FM}}_n^{\mathrm{ord}}(\bC)$;
 \item \textup{(II)} $\to$ \textup{(III)}: cobar applied to
  the deconcatenation coproduct on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)$;
 \item \textup{(III)} $\to$ \textup{(I)}: the chiral Drinfeld
  formula $R(z) = \sigma \circ \Delta^{\mathrm{ch}}
  \circ (\Delta^{\mathrm{ch}, \mathrm{op}})^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Concrete identifications.}
Under the RSYZ isomorphism $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X) \simeq
Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The vertex $R$-matrix \textup{(I)} is identified with the
  MO stable-envelope $R$-matrix of the Nakajima quiver variety
  $\mathfrak{M}(Q_X, \mathbf{d})$;
 \item The chiral $A_\infty$-maps \textup{(II)} correspond to
  the CoHA multiplication and higher compositions: the Hall
  product gives $m_2^{\mathrm{ch}}$, and higher-order contact
  terms give $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ for $k \geq 3$.  These
  $A_\infty$-maps are encoded in the bar differential
  $d_{\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}} = \sum_{k \geq 1} b_k$ of
  $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)$, but the bar complex is a
  \emph{coalgebra} constructed from $A_X$, not the CoHA
  itself;
 \item The chiral coproduct \textup{(III)} is identified with
  the Yang--Zhao localization coproduct on the CoHA,
  defined by restriction to attracting sets of one-parameter
  subgroups in equivariant cohomology;
 \item The classical $r$-matrix
  $r(z) = \operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}
  (\Theta_{A_X})$
  \textup{(}collision residue of the MC element\textup{)}
  is the classical limit of $R^{\mathrm{MO}}(z)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The abstract equivalence (I) $\leftrightarrow$ (II)
$\leftrightarrow$ (III) is the Vol~I chiral quantum group equivalence, proved for any
$\Eone$-chiral algebra on the Koszul locus. The content of the
present theorem is the identification of each abstract vertex
with the independently constructed toric CY3 structure.

\textit{Component~\textup{(I)}: $R$-matrix.}
The $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_X$ carries a vertex
$R$-matrix by the general theory
(item~\textup{(I)} of that equivalence). For toric CY3, the
Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope construction provides an
independent $R$-matrix on $Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$-modules.
The two coincide because both solve the same Yang--Baxter
equation with the same classical limit
$r(z) = \operatorname{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_X})$
and the same unitarity constraint $R(z)R(-z) = \mathrm{Id}$
(from the CY condition, equation~\eqref{eq:cy-condition-rmatrix}).
Uniqueness of the quantum $R$-matrix from the classical
$r$-matrix on the Koszul locus is standard
(Etingof--Kazhdan).

\textit{Component~\textup{(II)}: $A_\infty$.}
The bar differential on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)
= T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_X)$ encodes the chiral
$A_\infty$-maps $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ by definition.
On the CoHA side, the Hall product is
$m_2^{\mathrm{ch}}$, and the higher compositions
$m_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ for $k \geq 3$ are the
higher-order contact terms arising from the
bar-complex boundary strata (associahedron facets).
For $\C^3$, all $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ with $k \geq 3$
vanish: the CoHA is strictly associative (class~G,
shadow depth $r_{\max} = 2$). For local~$\bP^2$
(class~M), the $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ are nonzero for
all $k$ and encode the full infinite shadow tower.

\textit{Component~\textup{(III)}: coproduct.}
The Yang--Zhao localization coproduct on
$\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X)$ is coassociative and compatible
with the Hall multiplication. The deconcatenation coproduct
on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)$ produces, via the cobar
functor $\Omega$, a chiral coproduct on $A_X$ that is
coassociative up to the Drinfeld associator. On the Koszul
locus, $\Omega(\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X))
\xrightarrow{\sim} A_X$ is a quasi-isomorphism
(Theorem~B of Vol~I), and the
transported coproduct is identified with the Yang--Zhao
coproduct by the universal property of the localization
construction (the attracting-set restriction computes the
same homotopy-coherent coproduct as the bar-cobar
transport).

\textit{Concrete identification~(iv).}
The classical $r$-matrix is the collision residue of
the MC element $\Theta_{A_X}$
(Vol~I seven-faces formalism). The classical limit
$R(z) = \mathrm{Id} + \hbar\, r(z) + O(\hbar^2)$
holds by the MO construction (the stable envelope
$R$-matrix is a quantization of the geometric
$r$-matrix with classical limit given by the
torus-fixed point localization).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why CoHA and bar complex share the MacMahon character]
\label{rem:coha-bar-macmahon}
For $\C^3$, both the CoHA graded dimension $\sum_n \dim \cH_n\, q^n$ and the $\Eone$ bar Euler characteristic $\chi(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_{\C^3}), q)$ equal the MacMahon function $M(q) = \prod_{n \geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-n}$.  The coincidence is not accidental: it reflects the Schiffmann--Vasserot isomorphism $\cH(\C^3) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sv-c3}) in two ways.
On the \emph{geometric} side, the CoHA counts BPS states via $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^3)$; its graded dimension is $\sum_n \chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^3))\, q^n = M(q)$, where each BPS state corresponds to a $3$-dimensional partition.
On the \emph{combinatorial} side, $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(Y^+)$ is the coalgebra resolution of $Y^+$; its bar differential $d_B = \sum b_k$ encodes the algebra structure of $Y^+$ by definition, and the bar Euler characteristic records the same mode-operator counting as the algebra's graded dimension.
Since $\cH(\C^3) \cong Y^+$ \emph{as algebras}, the Hall product appears as $b_2$ in the bar differential: the two objects count the same modes through different lenses (geometric: DT invariants on Hilbert schemes; algebraic: tensor products of Yangian generators).  But the CoHA and the bar complex remain distinct mathematical objects (one is an algebra, the other its coalgebra resolution), and the character coincidence is a \emph{consequence} of the isomorphism, not an identification.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[$\C^3$: the complete chiral quantum group]
\label{cor:c3-chiral-qg-triangle}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For $X = \C^3$ \textup{(}Jordan quiver,
$\mathcal{H} \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$\textup{)},
the chiral quantum group equivalence of
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-chiral-qg-specialization}}
specializes to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The vertex $R$-matrix is the MO stable-envelope
  $R$-matrix of $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2)$.
 \item The chiral $A_\infty$-structure is strictly
  associative: $m_k^{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ for $k \geq 3$
  \textup{(}class~G, $r_{\max} = 2$\textup{)}.
 \item The chiral coproduct coincides with the
  shuffle coproduct on $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$.
 \item The Drinfeld double
  $D(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)) = Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$
  \textup{(}Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak\textup{)}.
 \item The Drinfeld center
  $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))
  \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$
  \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}}\textup{)}.
\end{enumerate}
All five components are unconditional. The $\C^3$ case is the
unique toric CY3 where the Drinfeld center identification is
proved; for general toric, component~\textup{(v)} is conditional
on Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:qg-realization}}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Items~(i)--(iii) follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chiral-qg-specialization} specialized
to the Jordan quiver. Item~(ii) uses the class~G classification
of $\C^3$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:conifold-shadow} for the conifold;
for $\C^3$ itself, $S_r = 0$ for $r \geq 3$ because the single-field
Heisenberg OPE has $r_{\max} = 2$). Item~(iv) is the triangular
decomposition $Y = Y^+ \otimes Y^0 \otimes Y^-$ combined with the
Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak identification. Item~(v) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conifold: wall-crossing preserves the triangle]
\label{cor:conifold-chiral-qg}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For the resolved conifold $X = \cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1$
\textup{(}Klebanov--Witten quiver\textup{)}, the chiral quantum
group equivalence of
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:toric-chiral-qg-specialization}} holds
in both stability chambers $\zeta_1 > 0$ and $\zeta_1 < 0$. The
wall-crossing gauge transformation
$\Theta_{\mathrm{II}} = e^{\ad_\alpha}(\Theta_{\mathrm{I}})$
of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:conifold-wall-crossing}}
preserves each vertex of the triangle:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The $R$-matrices in the two chambers are gauge-equivalent:
  $R^{\mathrm{II}}(z) = (g \otimes g)\, R^{\mathrm{I}}(z)\,
  (g^{-1} \otimes g^{-1})$.
 \item The $A_\infty$-structures are $A_\infty$-quasi-isomorphic
  via the transfer induced by the gauge equivalence.
 \item The chiral coproducts are intertwined by the same
  gauge operator $g = e^\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ is chamber-independent
\textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:toric-kappa-table}}\textup{)}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The MC gauge equivalence
$\Theta_{\mathrm{II}} = e^{\ad_\alpha}(\Theta_{\mathrm{I}})$
preserves the MC equation and hence each derived structure.
The $R$-matrix transforms by conjugation (compatibility~(a)
of Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg}). The $A_\infty$
transfer is the homotopy transfer theorem applied to the
quasi-isomorphism between the bar complexes in the two
chambers (the gauge transformation is a filtered
quasi-isomorphism on $\barB^{\mathrm{ord}}$). The coproduct
intertwining follows from the coassociativity of
deconcatenation and the naturality of the cobar functor
under quasi-isomorphisms of dg coalgebras.
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}[Extension to compact CY3]
\label{rem:compact-extension}
The toric hypothesis enters
Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-cy3-chiral-qg} in four places:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \emph{Existence of CoHA}:
  the critical CoHA is constructed for quivers with
  potential, which are available for toric CY3 via the
  McKay correspondence. For compact CY3 without torus action
  (quintic, generic $K3 \times E$), no CoHA
  construction is available; the entry point is instead the
  factorization envelope of the polyvector-field Lie conformal
  algebra (Costello--Li).
 \item \emph{RSYZ identification}:
  $\mathcal{H}(Q_X, W_X) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$
  is specific to toric geometry (the shuffle-algebra
  presentation uses equivariant localization).
 \item \emph{MO $R$-matrix}:
  the stable-envelope construction requires a torus action
  on the Nakajima quiver variety. For compact CY3, the
  $R$-matrix must come from a different source (the
  chain-level $\bS^3$-framing of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).
 \item \emph{Chart gluing}:
  the toric fan provides a finite atlas; for general CY3,
  the tilting-chart cover is conjectural
  (Conjecture~\ref{conj:tilting-chart-cover}).
\end{enumerate}
The extension to compact CY3 (including $K3 \times E$,
the quintic, and Enriques $\times E$) is the content of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3} (proved in the infinity-categorical
framework; Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}).
Chain-level explicit constructions for non-formal algebras
remain open.
\end{remark}
