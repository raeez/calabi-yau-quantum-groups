\chapter{The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ Stratification by CY Dimension}
\label{ch:cy-d-kappa-stratification}

The two-stage factorisation functor
\[
  \Phi_d \;=\; \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d
  \colon\; \mathrm{CY}_d\text{-}\mathrm{Cat}
  \;\longrightarrow\; \EdHolFA(X)
  \;\longrightarrow\; \EnHolFA(C)
\]
(see \texttt{chapters/theory/e1\_chiral\_algebras.tex},
Definitions~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}--\ref{rem:multiple-e1-specialisations})
assigns to a CY$_d$ category $\cC$ first a holomorphic factorisation
algebra $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the CY$_d$ target $X$, then a chiral algebra
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ on a curve $C$ via factorisation
homology over $\Sigma_{d-1}$. The two stages produce scalar invariants of
different provenance that must be distinguished.

\begin{itemize}
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$: a native invariant of the
  $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ stage; it is the degree-two shadow coefficient of the
  factorisation algebra on $X$, computed from the $(0,\bullet)$ Hodge
  column of $X$ via Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}.
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(X) = \chi(\cO_X)$: a native invariant of the
  CY$_d$ \emph{variety} $X$, independent of the $\PhiFA_d$ stage;
  K\"unneth-multiplicative on products.
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$: an invariant of the
  Borcherds-lift $\SpCh$ specialisation
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}); it records
  the weight of the automorphic product attached to the specialisation
  cycle, not of the $\PhiFA_d$ output directly.
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$: tracks the fibre of the specialisation
  cycle in $\SpCh$; it equals the rank or Euler characteristic of the
  fibering data attached to the $\SpCh$ map.
\end{itemize}

The naive formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}=\chi(\cO_X)$ holds on the proved
CY$_2$ slice with $h^{1,0}=0$
\textup{(}Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-kappa-d2}\textup{)} and fails on the
product $K3 \times E$, where
\[
  \chi(\cO_{K3 \times E})=0
  \qquad\text{but}\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi_3(D^b\Coh(K3 \times E)))=3.
\]
This chapter evaluates the Hodge supertrace dimension by dimension for
$d \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ and stratifies the comparison between
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$
by Beauville--Bogomolov holonomy type. The chapter does not close
Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification}; it records the
tri-stratum obstruction to that conjecture's naive form.

The universal formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_N(0)/2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}) demonstrates
that the additive decomposition
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$
fails at every $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is a
$\SpCh$-shadow invariant, not a sum of $\PhiFA_d$ outputs.

The unit of analysis throughout is the dimensional parity of $X$ and the
Hodge column $h^{0,\bullet}(X)$. The companion engine is
\texttt{compute/lib/cy\_d\_kappa\_d3.py} with stratification test
\texttt{compute/tests/test\_cy\_d\_kappa\_stratification.py}.

\section{Setup: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, $\chi(\cO_X)$, and the Hodge
supertrace}
\label{sec:cy-strat-setup}

Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau manifold of complex dimension $d$, so
that $\omega_X \simeq \cO_X$ and the Serre functor on $D^b(\Coh(X))$ is
the shift $[d]$. Write $h^{p,q} = h^{p,q}(X) = \dim_\C H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$
for its Hodge numbers and $\chi_p = \chi_p(X) = \sum_q (-1)^q h^{p,q}$
for its holomorphic Euler characteristics (the $p = 0$ case recovers
$\chi(\cO_X)$).

Fix the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_d$ and write $\cA_X =
\Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ for the chiral algebra associated to $X$. The
\emph{algebraization scalar} $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$ is the
degree-two shadow invariant of Vol~I: the coefficient of $[b \mid b]$
in the quadratic piece of the Maurer--Cartan obstruction on
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA_X)$. It is an algebraization
invariant in the sense of a second algebraization of the same
topological CY$_d$ manifold can produce a different
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. By contrast, $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(X)$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(X)$ are topological manifold invariants;
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ depends on the Borcherds frame shape.

\subsection{Three candidate comparators}
\label{subsec:cy-strat-three-candidates}

We compare $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ against three Hodge-derived scalars:

\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{holomorphic Euler characteristic}
$\chi(\cO_X) = \sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(X) = \chi_0(X)$.
\item The \emph{Hodge-filtered supertrace} of the structure sheaf column
\[
 \Xi(X) \;:=\; \sum_{q = 0}^{d} (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X).
\]
Because $h^{0,q}$ counts antiholomorphic forms of pure type $(0,q)$,
$\Xi(X) = \chi(\cO_X)$ as \emph{numbers}; the distinction is conceptual
(we shall see the $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ route produces the supertrace
even when $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ is forced to zero by Serre). The notation
$\Xi$ underlines the supertrace view and suppresses the
$h^{0,q} = h^{d,d-q}$ identification that flattens it back to
$\chi(\cO_X)$.
\item The \emph{explicit algebraic Hochschild sum}
$\mathrm{HH}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) = \sum_{p,q} (-1)^{p - q}\, h^{p,q}(X)$.
This is the signed Hodge sum appearing in the HKR--Caldararu formula;
on compact K\"ahler $X$ one has $(-1)^{p-q} = (-1)^{p+q}$, so
$\mathrm{HH}_{\mathrm{alg}}(X) = \sum_p (-1)^p \chi_p(X) = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$.
\end{itemize}

The Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification} below shows
that $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the \emph{supertrace} $\Xi(X)$: the
numerical value $\chi(\cO_X)$, but computed from the
chiral/categorical side through the $(0,q)$ anti-holomorphic column
without any appeal to Kodaira vanishing or Serre cancellation. Only
after that identification is established does the Serre
anti-symmetry $h^{0,q} = h^{0,d-q}$ intervene to forbid the
identification $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ at odd $d$.

\section{The Hodge supertrace identification}
\label{sec:cy-strat-hodge-supertrace}

\begin{theorem}[$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the Hodge supertrace of the
$h^{0,\bullet}$ column]
\label{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau manifold of complex dimension $d$ and
let $\cA_X = \Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ be the chiral algebra of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3} (for $d = 3$) or the analogous CY-A$_d$
construction (for other $d$). Then
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) \;=\; \Xi(X) \;=\;
   \sum_{q = 0}^{d} (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X),
\]
an unconditional identity at generic parameters of $\Phi$. At $d = 2$
with $h^{1,0}(X) = 0$ the right-hand side collapses to $\chi(\cO_X)$,
recovering Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-kappa-d2}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The argument proceeds in three steps.

\emph{Step 1. HKR and the CY trace.} By
Theorem~\ref{thm:hkr-cy}, the Hochschild homology of $D^b(\Coh(X))$
decomposes as
\[
 \mathrm{HH}_\bullet(D^b(\Coh(X))) \;\simeq\;
   \bigoplus_{p + q = \bullet}\, H^q(X, \Omega^p_X),
\]
intertwining the Connes $B$-operator on the left with the de Rham
differential on the right up to the Caldararu twist. The CY structure
determines the negative cyclic lift $\HC^-_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ of the
trace class, which is the $S^d$-framing datum required by
$\Phi_d$.

\emph{Step 2. The shadow scalar from $\HC^-_d$.} The Vol~I shadow
construction evaluates the degree-two shadow $S_2$ of $\cA_X$ on the
composition $\HC^-_d \to \mathrm{HH}_\bullet \to
\bigoplus_{p+q} H^q(\Omega^p)$. The $r$-matrix on $\cA_X$ is the Mukai
pairing (Proposition~\ref{prop:serre-duality-cy}), which is graded by
the Hodge filtration $F^\bullet$ on $\mathrm{HH}_\bullet$. The
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ scalar is by construction the value of $S_2$ on
the first graded piece
$F^0/F^{-1} \cong \bigoplus_q H^q(X, \cO_X)$,
the structure-sheaf column $(0, \bullet)$. Higher Hodge columns
$H^q(X, \Omega^p_X)$ with $p \geq 1$ contribute to the full categorical
trace on $\HH_\bullet$ but enter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ only through the
graded projection to $F^0/F^{-1}$; in CY dimension $d$ the Hodge symmetry
$h^{p, q} = h^{q, p}$ makes the $(0, q)$ supertrace equal to the full
$\chi(\cO_X)$ when the middle $h^{1, 0}, h^{2, 0}, \ldots$ vanish, but
it is the $(0, \bullet)$ filtered piece that appears in the chiral shadow,
not the full Hochschild class.

\emph{Step 3. Supertrace through the $(0,q)$ column.} The surviving
$p = 0$ column contributes $H^q(X, \cO_X)$ with sign $(-1)^q$, the
cohomological sign from the chiral bar complex. Summing over $q$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = \sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(X) = \Xi(X)$.

The three steps together establish the supertrace identity
unconditionally at $\Phi$-generic parameters. At $d = 2$ with
$h^{1,0} = 0$ the column $(1, 0, 1)$ gives $\Xi(\mathrm{K3}) = 2 =
\chi(\cO_{\mathrm{K3}})$ and we recover Proposition
\ref{prop:cy-kappa-d2}. Outside that scope $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ is forced
by Serre (Lemma~\ref{lem:chi-O-vanishes-odd-d}) while
$\Xi(X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$ need not vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Serre-enforced vanishing of $\chi(\cO_X)$ at odd $d$]
\label{lem:chi-O-vanishes-odd-d}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For any compact Calabi--Yau $X$ of odd complex dimension $d$,
$\chi(\cO_X) = 0$. The proof is Serre pairwise cancellation:
$h^{0,q}(X) = h^{0, d-q}(X)$ and, for $d$ odd, $(-1)^q + (-1)^{d-q} = 0$
for every pair $(q, d-q)$ with $q \neq d - q$; there is no middle term.
See Griffiths--Harris, \emph{Principles of Algebraic Geometry}, Ch.~0
and the engine routine \texttt{cy\_d\_kappa\_d3.chi\_O\_vanishes\_odd\_d}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Why the identification $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} =
\chi(\cO_X)$ survives only at $(d, h^{1,0}) = (2, 0)$]
\label{rem:why-only-k3}
The supertrace $\Xi(X)$ and the number $\chi(\cO_X)$ are
computationally identical (both are $\sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}$). The
distinction is that the supertrace is a well-defined invariant of the
CY category (it is the degree-two shadow $S_2(\cA_X)$ passing through
$\HC^-_d$), whereas the phrase ``$\chi(\cO_X)$'' invokes Serre cancellation
whenever $d$ is odd. At $E$ with $(d, h^{0,\bullet}) = (1, (1, 1))$ the
Serre pairing $h^{0,0} = h^{0,1} = 1$ cancels the two contributions in
the written-out sum, so $\Xi(E) = 1 - 1 = 0 = \chi(\cO_E)$; the
independent chiral computation $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_E) = 0$ agrees,
coming from the abelian $\pi_1$-cohomology of $E$, not from a pairing
with the vacuum. Supertrace and chiral calculation agree ($0 = 0$);
the $E$ case exhibits the supertrace as a \emph{cancellation}, not an
\emph{equality} between nonzero pieces. The only case where no cancellation occurs --
every nonzero entry of the $h^{0,\bullet}$ column lies at even $q$ --
is K3. This is the structural reason $(d, h^{1,0}) = (2, 0)$ is
singled out.
\end{remark}

\section{Dimension-by-dimension stratification}
\label{sec:cy-strat-stratification}

The Hodge-filtered supertrace formula
\textup{(}Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}\textup{)}
collapses the entire CY-D landscape into three structural strata,
discriminated by the parity of $d$ and by the presence of a
holomorphic-symplectic form.  This unifies Theorem~B at $d = 2$
\textup{(}K$3$\textup{),} the $d = 3$ case study
$K3 \times E$, the $d = 4$ sextic and hyperk\"ahler examples, the
$d = 5$ Universal Serre Cancellation, and every higher-dimensional
example below.

\begin{theorem}[CY-D tri-stratum theorem]
\label{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X$ be a compact CY$_d$ manifold with Hodge column
$h^{0, \bullet}(X) = (h^{0, 0}, h^{0, 1}, \ldots, h^{0, d})$.  The
Hodge-filtered supertrace
$\Xi(X) := \sum_{q = 0}^d (-1)^q h^{0, q}(X)$
takes one of three forms, determined by the parity of $d$ and the
holomorphic-symplectic structure of $X$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[\textup{(I)}] \emph{Odd $d$ stratum.} If $d$ is odd, then
       $\Xi(X) = 0$ for every compact CY$_d$.  Mechanism: Serre duality
       $h^{0, q} = h^{0, d - q}$ pairs every term with its sign-opposite,
       and $d$ odd means there is no middle term ($q = d/2$ is
       non-integral); pairs cancel uniformly.
 \item[\textup{(II)}] \emph{Even $d$ strict-CY stratum.} If $d$ is even
       and $X$ has holonomy exactly $\mathrm{SU}(d)$ \textup{(}so
       $h^{0, k} = 0$ for $0 < k < d$; the unique global holomorphic
       form is the Calabi--Yau volume\textup{),} then
       $\Xi(X) = 2$.  Examples: sextic fourfold in $\mathbb{P}^5$,
       decic $5$-fold in $\mathbb{P}(1^5, 5)$, and every strict CY of
       even dimension.
 \item[\textup{(III)}] \emph{Even $d$ holomorphic-symplectic stratum.}
       If $d$ is even and $X$ is irreducible holomorphic-symplectic
       \textup{(}holonomy $\mathrm{Sp}(d/2)$, with
       $h^{0, k} = 1$ for every even $0 \leq k \leq d$ and
       $h^{0, k} = 0$ for $k$ odd\textup{),} then
       $\Xi(X) = (d/2) + 1$.  In particular: K$3^{[n]}$ at
       $d = 2n$ has $\Xi = n + 1$, recovering $\Xi(K3) = 2$ at $n = 1$
       and $\Xi(K3^{[2]}) = 3$ at $n = 2$.
\end{enumerate}
Strata \textup{(II)} and \textup{(III)} overlap precisely when
$\mathrm{SU}(d) = \mathrm{Sp}(d/2)$, which occurs only at $d = 2$
\textup{(}where $\mathrm{SU}(2) = \mathrm{Sp}(1)$; both strata assign
$\Xi(K3) = 2$\textup{).} For $d \geq 4$ the strata are disjoint, and
together with \textup{(I)} they exhaust the Beauville--Bogomolov
classification of compact CY holonomies on connected simply-connected
$X$ with $h^{1, 0} = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Stratum (I) is direct Serre cancellation: for $d$ odd,
$h^{0, q} = h^{0, d - q}$ pairs each $q < d/2$ with $d - q > d/2$,
contributing $(-1)^q + (-1)^{d - q} = (-1)^q + (-1)^{d - q}$;
since $(-1)^{d - q} = (-1)^d (-1)^{-q} = -(-1)^q$ for $d$ odd, each
pair contributes $0$.  No middle term exists since $d/2$ is
non-integral.

Stratum (II) uses the strict-CY definition: for $X$ with holonomy exactly
$\mathrm{SU}(d)$, the holonomy representation on $\Omega^q$ has no trivial
summand for $0 < q < d$, so $h^{0, q} = 0$ in this range.  The only
nonzero $h^{0, q}$ are $q = 0$ (constants) and $q = d$ (the Calabi--Yau
volume form).  A second holomorphic form at $q = d/2$ would reduce the
holonomy to $\mathrm{Sp}(d/2)$, placing $X$ in stratum \textup{(III)}
instead.  Hence $\Xi(X) = h^{0, 0} + (-1)^d h^{0, d} = 1 + 1 = 2$, since
$d$ is even.

Stratum (III) uses the holomorphic-symplectic Hodge structure of
Beauville--Bogomolov: for $X$ irreducible holomorphic-symplectic of
complex dimension $d = 2n$, the holomorphic-symplectic form $\sigma$ and
its powers $\sigma^k$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, n$ generate the only nonzero
$h^{0, q}$ with $q$ even.  Specifically $h^{0, 2k} = 1$ for $0 \leq k \leq n$
and $h^{0, q} = 0$ for $q$ odd.  The supertrace is
$\Xi = \sum_{k = 0}^n h^{0, 2k} = n + 1 = (d/2) + 1$.

Mutual exclusivity follows from Beauville--Bogomolov: every compact
K\"ahler CY manifold with $c_1 = 0$ admits a finite \'etale cover that
decomposes as $T^d \times \prod_i \mathrm{HK}_i \times \prod_j Y_j$
where $T^d$ is a torus, $\mathrm{HK}_i$ are irreducible holomorphic-symplectic,
and $Y_j$ are strict CYs.  Restricting to $h^{1, 0} = 0$ \textup{(}the
universal hypothesis on the present chapter\textup{)} eliminates the
torus factor; restricting to one Bogomolov factor gives one of the three
strata exclusively.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ landscape has structure only at
                even $d$]
\label{rem:kappa-ch-landscape-even-d}
Stratum (I) collapses the entire odd-$d$ landscape to the single value
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = 0$.  At odd $d$, every algebraic richness
of the chiral output $\cA_X$ \textup{(}detected through OTHER invariants
such as shadow-tower depth, BCOV partition function, MO-stable-envelope
R-matrix, etc.\textup{)} is invisible to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ alone.
The rich $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ landscape lives entirely in even $d$,
distributed across strata (II) and (III) by the holomorphic-symplectic
form.

This is the structural reason that the K$3$ chiral algebra at $d = 2$ is
the canonical exemplar: the two stratifying formulas \emph{agree} on K$3$.
Stratum (III) gives $\Xi = (d/2) + 1 = 2$ at $d = 2$, $n = 1$. Stratum (II)
with the K$3$ column $h^{0, \bullet}(\mathrm{K}3) = (1, 0, 1)$ and the
$h^{0, d/2} = h^{0, 1} = 0$ middle term gives
$\Xi = 1 + (-1)^{1} \cdot 0 + 1 = 2$. Both routes land on the same value
because K$3$ is simultaneously strict CY (holonomy $\mathrm{SU}(2)$) and
irreducible holomorphic-symplectic (holonomy $\mathrm{Sp}(1) = \mathrm{SU}(2)$),
an accident of low dimension: $\mathrm{SU}(2) = \mathrm{Sp}(1)$. In higher
even dimension the two holonomy classes are distinct and the two
stratum formulas diverge, with stratum (II) giving $2$ (at vanishing
middle holomorphic form) and stratum (III) giving $n + 1$ for K$3^{[n]}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[BCOV $F_g$ vanishing across all even $d$]
\label{cor:bcov-fg-vanishing-all-even-d}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Theorems~\ref{thm:bcov-f2-zero-correction-d4} \textup{(}$d = 4$\textup{)}
and~\ref{thm:bcov-fg-zero-correction-d5} \textup{(}$d = 5$\textup{)} are
the chain-level expressions of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}: the
BCOV $F_g$ holomorphic anomaly equation respects the underlying Serre
symmetry of stratum (I) and the holomorphic-symplectic Hodge structure of
stratum (III), so the supertrace is the COMPLETE answer in both strata.
At stratum (II), the BCOV $F_g$ contribution is similarly forced to zero
by the constancy $F_1 = \chi/24$ on the BTT moduli of strict CYs.
\end{corollary}

\section{Shadow-class stratification by CY holonomy}
\label{sec:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum} controls the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$
by the Hodge column $h^{0, \bullet}$. A complementary, logically
independent question is: what is the \emph{shadow class}
$\mathrm{Cl}(\cA_X) \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ of the chiral algebra
$\cA_X = \Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ per stratum? The shadow class is
determined by the higher shadow coefficients $(S_3, S_4, \ldots)$
(the OPE-pole structure of the chiral $r$-matrix), not by $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = S_2$
alone. The classification below uses the Vol~I shadow-depth
hierarchy $G < L < C < M$ indexed by $r_{\max} \in \{2, 3, 4, \infty\}$
together with the Vol~III closed $\kappa_\bullet$ menu.

\begin{theorem}[CY-D shadow-class stratification by holonomy]
\label{thm:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere\ \textup{(}at the level of the tree-level
Hodge-supertrace input to the shadow tower, unconditional at
$d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ via CY-A$_2$ and the infinity-categorical CY-A$_3$
resolution of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}; conditional on CY-A$_d$
at $d \geq 4$\textup{)}
\ClaimStatusConditional\ \textup{(}at $d \geq 4$: conditional on
CY-A$_d$; the BCOV $F_g$ instanton promotion of stratum (I$_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$)
is unconditional per cited case but is not universal over the set of
compact CY$_3$ manifolds\textup{)}
Let $X$ be a compact CY$_d$ manifold with $h^{1, 0}(X) = 0$ and
$\cA_X = \Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$, all statements at generic parameters of
$\Phi$ and ambient-qualified per stratum below:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[\textup{(I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$)}] \emph{Odd $d$ stratum, tree level.}
       If $d$ is odd, the Hodge-supertrace input to the shadow tower is
       $(S_2, S_3, S_4) = (0, 0, 0)$ by Serre pairwise cancellation of
       every degree (not only $S_2$): the same involution $q \mapsto d - q$
       that kills $\Xi(X)$ also kills the $(0, \bullet)$ contribution to
       $S_3, S_4$ at tree level. The tree-level shadow class is $G$
       \textup{(}trivially: all shadow coefficients vanish\textup{),} and
       $r_{\max}^{\mathrm{tree}}(\cA_X) = 2$.
 \item[\textup{(I$_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$)}] \emph{Odd $d$ stratum, BCOV-promoted.}
       At compact odd-$d$ CY with nontrivial Gopakumar--Vafa spectrum, the
       BCOV instanton corrections promote the shadow class to $M$
       \textup{(}depth $r_{\max} = \infty$\textup{)}. The quintic
       $X_5 \subset \mathbb{P}^4$ realizes this promotion: classical
       $S_4 = 0$ gives tree-level class $L$ once the $\sigma_3 = 5$
       triple-intersection Yukawa is retained, but the Gopakumar--Vafa
       genus-zero invariants $n^0_d$ are nonzero for every $d \geq 1$,
       driving $S_4^{\mathrm{instanton}} \neq 0$ and hence class $M$
       \textup{(}Proposition~\ref{prop:quintic-shadow-coefficients}, with
       the quintic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}} = \chi/24 = -25/3$
       separately --- see Remark~\ref{rem:kappa-ch-quintic-two-views}
       below for the supertrace-vs-BCOV disambiguation\textup{)}.
 \item[\textup{(II)}] \emph{Even $d$, strict CY stratum.}
       If $X$ has holonomy exactly $\mathrm{SU}(d)$ \textup{(}strict CY,
       $h^{0, k} = 0$ for $0 < k < d$\textup{),} then the tree-level
       algebra $\cA_X$ is the rank-$2$ lattice VOA on the holomorphic
       $(0, 0)$ and $(0, d)$ sector \textup{(}the vacuum and the
       Calabi--Yau volume form\textup{),} which is a free-field algebra.
       Free-field algebras have $S_3 = S_4 = 0$ and shadow class $G$ with
       $r_{\max} = 2$. Sextic $X_6 \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ and decic
       $\subset \mathbb{P}(1^5, 5)$ \textup{(}with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$\textup{)}
       are canonical class-$G$ representatives.
 \item[\textup{(III)}] \emph{Even $d = 2n$, holomorphic-symplectic stratum.}
       If $X$ is irreducible holomorphic-symplectic \textup{(}holonomy
       $\mathrm{Sp}(n)$\textup{),} then $h^{0, 2k} = 1$ for
       $0 \leq k \leq n$ and the tree-level algebra $\cA_X$ is the
       rank-$(n + 1)$ lattice VOA generated by the $\sigma^k$ tower
       \textup{(}the $n + 1$ powers of the holomorphic-symplectic form\textup{).}
       This is a free-field algebra: $S_3 = S_4 = 0$, shadow class $G$,
       $r_{\max} = 2$.  K3 \textup{(}$n = 1$\textup{),} $\mathrm{K3}^{[n]}$,
       and the Beauville--Donagi Fano variety of lines $F(Y)$ on a cubic
       fourfold are canonical class-$G$ representatives, with
       $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = n + 1$.  At $n = 1$ \textup{(}K3\textup{),}
       strata \textup{(II)} and \textup{(III)} coincide and both yield
       class $G$ on the $\Phi_2$-output vertex algebra, consistent with
       the Mukai lattice VOA computation
       \textup{(}Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra},
       $S_3 = S_4 = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta = 0 \Rightarrow$ class $G$\textup{).}
\end{enumerate}
The tree-level shadow class is thus uniformly $G$ across every compact
CY$_d$ stratum \textup{(}I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$\textup{), (II), (III).}
Promotion to higher shadow classes requires either
\textup{(a)} compact odd-$d$ BCOV $F_g$ instanton corrections
\textup{(I$_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$)},
\textup{(b)} non-compact toric promotion via McKay quiver
$\mathbb{Z}/N$-symmetry \textup{(}see
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow}: local $\mathbb{P}^2$ is class $M$
with $r_{\max} = \infty$ by $\mathbb{Z}/3$-McKay\textup{),} or
\textup{(c)} K3 \emph{sigma-model} enhancement of the $\Phi_2$-output
lattice VOA to an $\widehat{\fsu}_2^{\oplus n}$ subalgebra at level $1$
\textup{(}Kummer point, Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra}: enhanced
subalgebra is class $L$ with $r_{\max} = 3$\textup{).}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We prove each stratum separately.

\emph{Stratum (I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$).} At odd $d$ with $h^{1, 0} = 0$,
the Hodge column $(1, h^{0, 1}, \ldots, h^{0, 1}, 1)$ paired by
Serre produces, at tree level (before BCOV $F_g$ corrections), a
free-field lattice VOA structure on $\cA_X$: the paired generators
$\{1, \omega_{d}\}$ at degrees $0$ and $d$, together with the Serre-dual
pairs $\{\eta_q, \eta_{d - q}\}$ at intermediate degrees
$q \in \{1, \ldots, d - 1\}$, mutually commute at tree level because
\textup{(a)} Serre duality pairs $\eta_q$ with $\eta_{d - q}$ in a
degree-zero pairing $\langle \eta_q, \eta_{d - q}\rangle = \int_X
\eta_q \wedge \eta_{d - q} = \mathrm{vol}_X$, giving a symplectic
($d$ odd, so $\eta$-pair is in antisymmetric position) rank-$2$
block per pair, and \textup{(b)} cross-pairs between distinct
$q, q'$ vanish by bigrading conservation.  The total structure is a
rank-$(d + 1)$ symplectic lattice VOA, which is free-field
(Frenkel--Ben-Zvi \emph{Vertex algebras and algebraic curves} 2001
Chapter 3, free-field lattice VOA construction).  Free-field
lattice VOAs have $S_3 = S_4 = 0$ (no cubic or quartic OPE pole;
every OPE has at most a double pole by free-field Wick contraction),
so $\Delta = 8 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4 = 0$ and the shadow class is
$G$ with $r_{\max} = 2$.  Equivalently: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$
from the Serre-cancellation of $\Xi(X)$ (stratum (I) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}), and the higher shadow
coefficients are zero from the free-field structure.  The two inputs
(vanishing scalar; free-field higher) converge on class $G$.

\emph{Stratum (I$_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$).} At compact odd-$d$ CY with
nontrivial Gopakumar--Vafa spectrum, the BCOV holomorphic-anomaly
equation (Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa 1994, Theorem~11.1)
propagates to the shadow tower via
$S_4^{\mathrm{instanton}} = \sum_{d \geq 1} n^0_d / \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:quintic-shadow-coefficients}, clause (iii)).
For the quintic: $n^0_1 = 2875 \neq 0$ (Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--Parkes 1991),
forcing $S_4^{\mathrm{instanton}} \neq 0$ and hence class $M$ with
$r_{\max} = \infty$. The class-$M$ conclusion applies conditionally:
\textup{(i)} to the quintic unconditionally, via the explicit nonzero
$n^0_1 = 2875$ computation; \textup{(ii)} to compact non-product CY$_3$
families with a nonvanishing leading Gopakumar--Vafa invariant at
degree~$1$ or at some higher degree for which the BCOV mirror-symmetry
series is known to be nontrivial (this is proved case by case; universal
nonvanishing of the GV spectrum for every compact non-product CY$_3$ is
an open conjecture and is NOT claimed here). The open frontier of
Remark~\ref{rem:cy-strat-open-frontier} is precisely the set of compact
non-product non-toric CY$_3$ for which the GV-nonvanishing input is not
independently established.

\emph{Stratum (II).} At strict CY$_d$ with holonomy exactly
$\mathrm{SU}(d)$ (even $d$), the nontrivial Hodge column is
$\{h^{0, 0}, h^{0, d}\} = \{1, 1\}$ with $h^{0, k} = 0$ for
$0 < k < d$. The chiral algebra $\cA_X$ at tree level is generated by
the $H^0(X, \cO_X) = \mathbb{C}$ vacuum $|0\rangle$ and the
$H^d(X, \cO_X) = \mathbb{C}\omega$ volume form, giving a Fock-module
structure of Heisenberg type: the pair $(|0\rangle, \omega)$ generates
a rank-$1$ Heisenberg VOA $\mathcal{H}_1$ via the mode expansion of
$\omega(z) = \sum_n \omega_n z^{-n - 1}$ with canonical commutation
$[\omega_m, \omega_n] = m \delta_{m + n, 0}$ (the standard Heisenberg
relation, Frenkel--Ben-Zvi 2001 Proposition~2.2.1). The shadow tower
of a Heisenberg VOA $\mathcal{H}_k$ has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = k$,
$S_3 = 0$ (no cubic pole: Heisenberg OPE is
$\omega(z) \omega(w) \sim k / (z - w)^2$, a pure double pole with no
cubic term), and $S_4 = 0$ (no quartic pole either).  The critical
discriminant $\Delta = 8 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4 = 0$ gives class $G$
with $r_{\max} = 2$ (Vol~I shadow-class-$G$ criterion: $S_3 = S_4 = 0
\Leftrightarrow \Delta = 0$ and shadow tower terminates at degree $2$).
The Hodge supertrace $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \Xi(X) = 2$ from Stratum (II)
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum} identifies the Heisenberg level
$k = 2$.  The sextic $X_6 \subset \mathbb{P}^5$, decic $\subset
\mathbb{P}(1^5, 5)$, and every higher even-$d$ strict CY realize this
Heisenberg pattern at tree level.

\emph{Stratum (III).} At irreducible holomorphic-symplectic $X$ of
complex dimension $d = 2n$, the Hodge column has $h^{0, 2k} = 1$ for
$0 \leq k \leq n$ and $h^{0, q} = 0$ otherwise (Beauville--Bogomolov
decomposition).  The chiral algebra $\cA_X$ at tree level is generated
by the $n + 1$ classes $\sigma^k \in H^{0, 2k}(X, \mathbb{C})$
($k = 0, \ldots, n$) with corresponding bosonic modes
$\sigma^k(z) = \sum_m \sigma^k_m z^{-m - 1}$. The Fujiki relations
(Fujiki \emph{Hodge structures on hyperk\"ahler manifolds}, Publ.~Inst.
Math.\ IHES 1987, cubic Fujiki constant $c_X$) give the pairing
$\int_X \sigma^k \wedge \bar\sigma^{n - k} = c_X \cdot \mathrm{vol}(X)$
pairwise, and orthogonality for distinct Hodge weights $(k, k')$ with
$k + k' \neq n$. The induced mode commutator is
$[\sigma^k_m, \sigma^{n - k}_p] = m \cdot c_X \cdot \delta_{m + p, 0}$
(pure Heisenberg central-charge relation with $\sigma^k$ and
$\sigma^{n - k}$ forming a conjugate pair), and
$[\sigma^k_m, \sigma^{k'}_p] = 0$ for $k + k' \neq n$.
This is exactly the defining commutator of the rank-$(n + 1)$
Heisenberg VOA $\mathcal{H}^{\otimes (n + 1)}$ at level $c_X$
(equivalently: $n + 1$ mutually-commuting free-boson generators, each
a copy of $\mathcal{H}_{c_X}$).  The shadow coefficients of a tensor
product of free-boson VOAs are $S_3 = S_4 = 0$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \sum_{k = 0}^{n} c_X = (n + 1) c_X$ at the
standard normalization $c_X = 1$, recovering $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = n + 1$
from Stratum (III) of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}.
The critical discriminant $\Delta = 0$ gives class $G$ with
$r_{\max} = 2$.  K3 ($n = 1$, rank-$2$ Heisenberg tensor),
$\mathrm{K3}^{[2]}$ ($n = 2$, rank-$3$ Heisenberg tensor), and the
Beauville--Donagi cubic-4-fold lines $F(Y) \sim_{\mathrm{deform}}
\mathrm{K3}^{[2]}$ (Beauville--Donagi 1985) all realize this pattern;
the full rank-$24$ Mukai lattice VOA on K3 of
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra} is an enlargement to the full Mukai
lattice but the class-$G$ conclusion is preserved
($S_3 = S_4 = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta = 0 \Rightarrow$ class $G$).

\emph{Overall conclusion.} The tree-level shadow class is $G$
uniformly across strata. Promotion mechanisms (a) compact odd-$d$ BCOV,
(b) non-compact toric McKay $\mathbb{Z}/N$-symmetry, (c) K3-sigma-model
enhancement are the three documented routes out of class $G$; each is
explicit in the indicated chapter or stratum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Disambiguation: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}$ vs
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}}$ at the quintic]
\label{rem:kappa-ch-quintic-two-views}
The quintic threefold carries two distinct invariants that each go by
the name $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$; the $\Phi$-output
invariant must be distinguished from the construction-level
invariant.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(\cA_{X_5}) = \Xi(X_5) = 0$,
       the Hodge supertrace of the $(0, \bullet)$ column
       $(1, 0, 0, 1)$: vanishes by Serre
       (Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}).
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}}(\cA_{X_5}) = \chi(X_5) / 24 = -25/3$,
       the BCOV $F_1$ one-loop free energy divided by the universal
       Euler coefficient: nonzero, fractional
       (Proposition~\ref{prop:quintic-shadow}).
\end{enumerate}
Both are ``$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$'' of ``the quintic chiral algebra'',
but they are invariants of \emph{different constructions}:
(1) is the supertrace trace on $\HC^-_3$ of the shadow tower of
$\cA_{X_5} = \Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(X_5)))$ (the categorical $\Phi_3$ output),
while (2) is the BCOV-genus-$1$ coefficient of the topological-string
partition function on the quintic (a separately constructed worldsheet
invariant).  These two invariants are both legitimate and both denoted
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ in the standard literature (Vol~III
\texttt{chapters/connections/bar\_cobar\_bridge.tex} at
Proposition~\ref{prop:quintic-shadow} for the BCOV route, and this
chapter Section~\ref{sec:cy-strat-stratification} for the Hodge
supertrace route), but they are not the same number and must not be
conflated.
Six routes to $G(K3 \times E)$ are six different
constructions, not six $\Phi$ applications; the same discipline applies here:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}}$
are two-route analogs at the quintic.

The shadow-class stratification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification} uses the Hodge
supertrace input \textup{(}tree level, stratum (I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$)\textup{),}
and promotes to class $M$ via BCOV instanton corrections to $S_4$
\textup{(}stratum (I$_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$)\textup{).} The two routes
converge on the same shadow-class outcome at the quintic
\textup{(}class $M$\textup{)} but differ on the degree-$2$ scalar:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}} = 0$ from stratum-(I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$)
Serre cancellation, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{BCOV}} = -25/3$ from
$F_1$-constant-map formula.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Shadow class and CY dimension: the universal compact
class-$G$ signature at tree level]
\label{cor:universal-tree-class-G}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For every compact CY$_d$ with $h^{1, 0} = 0$, the tree-level shadow class
$\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathrm{tree}}(\cA_X)$ is $G$ uniformly across
$d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$. (At $d = 1$, the $h^{1, 0} = 0$ scope is vacuous:
every elliptic curve has $h^{1, 0} = 1$, so there is no compact $d = 1$
CY with $h^{1, 0} = 0$ to classify; the $d = 1$ entry is treated
separately at Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d} via the
$\Xi(E) = 0$ Serre cancellation.)
The tree-level shadow depth is $r_{\max}^{\mathrm{tree}} = 2$
throughout the $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ scope.
Higher shadow classes $L, C, M$ appear only through
\textup{(a)} compact odd-$d$ BCOV $F_g$ instanton promotion,
\textup{(b)} non-compact toric McKay $\mathbb{Z}/N$-enhancement, or
\textup{(c)} K3-sigma-model $\widehat{\fsu}_2^{\oplus n}$ enhancement.
Equivalently: the CY dimension $d$ alone does NOT determine the shadow
class; the shadow class is determined by the compactness, the holonomy,
and the presence/absence of a promotion mechanism, NOT by $d$ directly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Strata (I$_{\mathrm{tree}}$), (II), (III) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification} each yield class $G$
at tree level, and the three strata exhaust the compact CY$_d$
landscape with $h^{1, 0} = 0$ per Beauville--Bogomolov
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}). Hence class $G$ is universal at
tree level across $d$. The three documented promotion mechanisms are
enumerated in the last paragraph of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification}; each is
conditional on auxiliary data (BCOV instanton spectrum, McKay quiver
symmetry, K3 sigma-model enhancement) beyond the raw CY dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cross-volume implication: Vol~I four-class landscape]
\label{rem:cy-d-shadow-class-cross-vol-implication}
The Vol~I four-class landscape $\{G, L, C, M\}$ is populated by four
archetype vertex algebras: Heisenberg $\mathcal{H}_k$ (class $G$),
affine Kac--Moody $V_k(\mathfrak{g})$ (class $L$), $\beta\gamma$ (class
$C$), Virasoro $\mathrm{Vir}_c$ (class $M$).  The Vol~III
$\Phi_d$-output stratification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-shadow-class-stratification} realizes class $G$
universally at tree level; classes $L, C, M$ are accessed only through
the three promotion mechanisms.  In particular:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Class $L$ (affine Kac--Moody) is accessed from $\Phi_d$ only via
       the K3 $\mathcal{N} = 4$ sigma-model enhancement to
       $\widehat{\fsu}_2^{\oplus n}$ at level $1$ on the Kummer point
       (Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra}).
 \item Class $C$ ($\beta\gamma$) is accessed from $\Phi_d$ only at
       non-compact local surfaces (Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow}:
       local $\mathbb{P}^2$ via the $\beta\gamma$ boundary algebra is
       class $C$ at $r_{\max} = 4$; see also the conifold bichamber
       computation of Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}).
 \item Class $M$ (Virasoro-type) is accessed via BCOV instanton
       corrections at compact odd-$d$ CY (quintic, $K3 \times E$,
       non-product CY$_3$) or via the K3 sigma-model full
       $\mathcal{N} = 4$ superconformal enhancement (Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra},
       full sigma model class $M$).
\end{itemize}
This population pattern is the Vol~III-specific analog of the Vol~I
averaging-map principle: the averaging map
$\mathrm{av}: \mathfrak{g}^{E_1} \to \mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}$
collapses every standard chiral algebra into one of four archetypes;
the $\Phi_d$-output tree-level algebra $\cA_X$ lands on the Heisenberg
archetype (class $G$) uniformly, and higher classes require ambient-category
enhancement beyond the bare $\Phi_d$ output.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Stratification of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ by
$(d, h^{0,\bullet})$]
\label{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X$ be a compact CY$_d$ and $\cA_X$ the associated chiral algebra.
For the standard families of Vol~III, the invariant
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$ is determined by the Hodge column
$h^{0,\bullet}(X)$ via
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification} and takes the
following values.

\textbf{$d = 1$ (elliptic curve $E$).} Column $h^{0,\bullet} = (1, 1)$,
so $\Xi(E) = 1 - 1 = 0$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_E) = 0$.  Two Heisenberg invariants must be
distinguished: the shadow scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ of the chiral
output $\cA_E = \Phi_1(D^b(\Coh(E)))$, which vanishes by the supertrace
$1 - 1 = 0$; and the Heisenberg level $\kappa_{\mathrm{Heis}} = k$, which
parametrizes the free-boson lattice VOA $H_k$ of rank $1$ and equals $1$
at the basic representation $H_1$.  The lattice VOA $H_1$ is a separate
construction from $\Phi_1(D^b(\Coh(E)))$, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{Heis}}(H_1)
= 1$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi_1(D^b(\Coh(E)))) = 0$ are invariants
of different algebras.  In this chapter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ always
denotes the supertrace on the $\Phi_d$ output; the $d = 1$ value is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi_1(D^b(\Coh(E)))) = 0$.

\textbf{$d = 2$, K3 ($h^{1,0} = 0$).} Column $(1, 0, 1)$, so
$\Xi(K3) = 2$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{K3}) = 2 = \chi(\cO_{K3})$.
This is the honest case where the supertrace and the naive
$\chi(\cO_X)$ agree.

\textbf{$d = 2$, abelian surface.} Column $(1, 2, 1)$, so
$\Xi(E \times E) = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{E\times E}) = 0$. The cancellation is
pairwise: the two $h^{0,1}$ forms subtract exactly from
$h^{0,0} + h^{0,2} = 2$.

\textbf{$d = 2$, bielliptic surface.} Column $(1, 1, 0)$, so
$\Xi(\mathrm{biell}) = 1 - 1 + 0 = 0$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{\mathrm{biell}}) = 0$. No holomorphic
$2$-form; $h^{1,0} = 1$ forces a cancellation at $q = 1$.

\textbf{$d = 3$, generic quintic.} Column $(1, 0, 0, 1)$, so
$\Xi(X_5) = 1 - 0 + 0 - 1 = 0$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{X_5}) = 0$.

\textbf{$d = 3$, $\mathrm{K3} \times E$.} Kunneth gives the column
$(1, 1, 1, 1)$: $h^{0,0} = 1$, $h^{0,1} = h^{0,0}_{K3} \cdot h^{0,1}_E
+ h^{0,1}_{K3} \cdot h^{0,0}_E = 1 + 0 = 1$, $h^{0,2} = 0 + 1 = 1$,
$h^{0,3} = 1$. Then $\Xi = 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 = 0$.
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{K3 \times E}) = 0$.

\textbf{$d = 3$, local toric.} For a non-compact toric CY$_3$, the
$h^{0,\bullet}$ column is not the correct input: Serre duality on
compact $X$ is replaced by compactly-supported duality on the total
space. The supertrace evaluates instead on the Dolbeault column of the
base: for local $\P^2 = \mathrm{Tot}(\omega_{\P^2})$ this is
$\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\P^2) = \frac{3}{2}$, matching
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow}.

\textbf{$d = 4$, CY$_4$.} Column $(1, h^{0,1}, h^{0,2}, h^{0,3},
h^{0,4})$ with $h^{0,4} = h^{4,0} = 1$ and $h^{0,q} = h^{0,4-q}$ by
Serre. The supertrace $\Xi(X) = 1 - h^{0,1} + h^{0,2} - h^{0,3} +
h^{0,4} = 2 - 2 h^{0,1} + h^{0,2}$, using $h^{0,3} = h^{0,1}$ and
$h^{0,4} = 1$. For the sextic $X_6 \subset \P^5$ with $h^{0,1} = 0$ and
$h^{0,2} = 0$, $\Xi(X_6) = 2$.

\textbf{$d = 5$, odd case.} Column $(1, h^{0,1}, h^{0,2}, h^{0,3},
h^{0,4}, h^{0,5})$ with $h^{0,q} = h^{0,5-q}$ and $h^{0,5} = 1$. The
supertrace $\Xi = 1 - h^{0,1} + h^{0,2} - h^{0,3} + h^{0,4} - h^{0,5}
= (1 - 1) + (-h^{0,1} + h^{0,4}) + (h^{0,2} - h^{0,3}) = 0$ term by
term under Serre. So $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ for every compact
CY$_5$, just as at $d = 1, 3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each case reduces to plugging the Hodge column into
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}. The K3
calculation reproduces Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-kappa-d2}. The abelian
surface, bielliptic surface, quintic, $\mathrm{K3} \times E$, and
$E^3$ values are verified independently by K\"unneth multiplicativity of
the Hodge supertrace,
$\Xi(X \times Y) = \Xi(X) \cdot \Xi(Y)$, using
$\Xi(\Phi_1(E)) = 0$ and $\Xi(\Phi_2(K3)) = 2$: every product entry has a
factor with vanishing supertrace and therefore collapses to zero.
The local $\P^2$ value $3/2$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow} and
agrees with $\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\P^2) = 3/2$. The sextic
value $\Xi(X_6) = 2$ is independently $\chi(\cO_{X_6})$ since
$h^{1,0} = h^{2,0} = h^{3,0} = 0$ at $d = 4$ with $h^{4,0} = 1$.
The odd $d = 5$ vanishing is purely Serre: every pair cancels.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The BCOV $F_2$ zero-correction theorem}
\label{subsec:cy-strat-d4-bcov-f2}

\begin{theorem}[BCOV $F_2$ contributes zero to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ at $d = 4$]
\label{thm:bcov-f2-zero-correction-d4}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For any compact CY$_4$ manifold $X$, the BCOV genus-$2$ holomorphic
anomaly contributes identically zero to the algebraization scalar:
\[
   \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{F_2\text{-correction}}(\cA_X) \;=\; 0.
\]
The full $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X)$ at $d = 4$ equals the leading
Hodge supertrace $\Xi(X)$, with no $F_2$ correction. The mechanism is
$F_1$ moduli-independence: $F_1(X) = \chi(X)/24$ depends only on the
topological Euler characteristic, hence has zero moduli derivatives,
forcing $\bar\partial F_2 = 0$ on the BTT moduli space $M_X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation at genus $g = 2$
(Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa, Comm.~Math.~Phys.~165 (1994) 311)
reads
\[
   \bar\partial_i F_2 \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\, \bar{C}_i^{\,jk}\,
   \bigl(D_j D_k F_1 \;+\; D_j F_1\, D_k F_1\bigr),
\]
with the right-hand side comprising (a) the handle-creation term
$D_j D_k F_1$ (the unique $r = g - 1 = 1$ contribution) and (b) the
factorization term $D_j F_1\, D_k F_1$ (the unique $r = 1$
contribution).

The BCOV one-loop free energy is universal:
\[
   F_1(X) \;=\; \frac{\chi(X)}{24},
\]
the Euler characteristic divided by $24$ (Klemm--Pandharipande
\emph{Enumerative geometry of Calabi--Yau 4-folds}, Theorem~2.3, 2007;
Costello--Li \emph{Quantization of open-closed BCOV theory I}, 2015,
for the chain-level construction). Since $\chi(X)$ is a topological
invariant of $X$ that is preserved under complex-structure
deformations, $F_1$ is constant on the BTT moduli $M_X$:
\[
   D_i F_1 \;=\; 0 \quad\text{identically on } M_X.
\]
Both terms on the right of the genus-$2$ anomaly equation therefore
vanish: $D_j D_k F_1 = D_j(0) = 0$, and $D_j F_1\, D_k F_1 = 0 \cdot 0 = 0$.
Consequently $\bar\partial_i F_2 = 0$ on $M_X$, i.e., $F_2$ is
holomorphic on the BTT moduli space.

A holomorphic function on the contractible BTT base pairs against the
deformation classes $\sigma_3 \in H^{3,1}(X)$ and
$\sigma_4 \in H^{2,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X)$ via the classical Yukawa
contractions only. The $\sigma_3$ pairing vanishes by Hodge type
(mismatch with the $(4,0)$ trace channel), and the $\sigma_4$ pairing
gives the classical Yukawa quartic
$Y^{(4),\mathrm{cl}}_{ijkl} = \int_X \omega_i \omega_j \omega_k \omega_l$
(BCOV classical four-point Yukawa coupling -- a Hodge-pairing tensor,
disjoint from the closed Vol III $\kappa_\bullet$ menu
$\{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\}$, which is why we use the symbol $Y^{(4),
\mathrm{cl}}$), which contributes only to the
moduli-independent constant-map sector
\[
   F_2^{\mathrm{const}}(X) \;=\; \frac{\chi(X)}{5760}
\]
(Klemm--Pandharipande 2007, constant-map formula at $d = 4$). This is
\emph{classical}: it depends only on $\chi(X)$ and lies in the
$F^0 H^4(X)$ channel already accounted for by the leading Hodge
supertrace $\Xi(X)$. The \emph{quantum} (anomalous) contribution to
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ from $F_2$ is identically zero. Hence
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{F_2\text{-correction}}(\cA_X) = 0$, and the
full $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = \Xi(X)$ with no quantum correction.
\end{proof}

\section{$d = 5$ explicit examples and the Universal Serre Cancellation Theorem}
\label{sec:cy-strat-d5-explicit}

At odd dimension $d = 5$ the Hodge supertrace vanishes uniformly;
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d} records the vanishing
$\Xi(X) = 0$ at the stratification level. What remains open is
whether a BCOV $F_g$ correction could reinstate a non-vanishing
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ at higher genus, and whether the vanishing
survives the derived-equivalent non-birational Borisov--Caldararu
Pfaffian pair (arXiv:0710.5901). The \emph{Universal Serre Cancellation
Theorem} below settles both: $\Xi(X) = 0$ for every compact CY$_5$
regardless of Hodge profile, with no $F_g$ correction at any genus.
The explicit checks --- septic $X_7 \subset \P^6$, Borisov--Caldararu
pair, K\"unneth product $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ --- furnish the
stratum-level verification.

\subsection{The four $d = 5$ families}
\label{subsec:cy-strat-d5-families}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
$X$ & $h^{0,\bullet}$ & $\Xi(X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ & $F_g$ corr.~$(g \geq 2)$ &
mechanism & reference \\
\midrule
Septic $X_7 \subset \P^6$ & $(1,0,0,0,0,1)$ & $0$ & $0$ & strict CY hypersurface &
Cox--Katz Ch.~7 \\
$\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ & $(1,0,1,1,0,1)$ & $0$ & $0$ & Kunneth + Serre cancel &
Griffiths--Harris \\
generic strict CY$_5$ & $(1,0,0,0,0,1)$ & $0$ & $0$ & strict universal &
universal Serre \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The three families share a single output value $\Xi = 0$, partitioned
into two Hodge patterns: (i) \emph{strict CY column} $(1,0,0,0,0,1)$ for
the septic and the generic strict CY$_5$, annihilated directly by the
$(0, 5)$ Serre pair; (ii) \emph{nontrivial middle column}
$(1,0,1,1,0,1)$ for the $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ product, where
K\"unneth produces $h^{0,2} = h^{0,3} = 1$ that cancel pairwise by
Serre. Any derived-equivalent CY$_5$ pair is automatically captured by
both patterns: by Caldararu HKR, derived equivalence preserves
$\bigoplus_q H^{0,q}$, hence the supertrace, which is already zero by
the theorem below.

\begin{theorem}[$d = 5$ Universal Serre Cancellation]
\label{thm:cy-d-d5-stratification}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For every compact Calabi--Yau manifold $X$ of complex dimension $d = 5$,
the Hodge-filtered supertrace satisfies
\[
   \Xi(X) \;=\; \sum_{q = 0}^{5} (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X) \;=\; 0,
\]
and consequently $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = 0$ under the supertrace
identification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-hodge-supertrace-identification}. The vanishing
is unconditional and depends on no specific Hodge profile.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Serre duality on a compact CY$_5$ gives $h^{0,q}(X) = h^{0,5-q}(X)$ for
every $q$. The Hodge column has the structure
$(1, h^{0,1}, h^{0,2}, h^{0,2}, h^{0,1}, 1)$, with three Serre pairs
$(0, 5)$, $(1, 4)$, $(2, 3)$ and \emph{no middle term} (because $5$ is
odd, $q = 5/2$ is not integral). Direct computation:
\[
 \Xi(X) \;=\; (1 - 1) + (-h^{0,1} + h^{0,1}) + (h^{0,2} - h^{0,2})
        \;=\; 0 + 0 + 0 \;=\; 0,
\]
each Serre pair contributing zero by the identity
$(-1)^q + (-1)^{5-q} = (-1)^q (1 + (-1)^5) = 0$. The septic
$X_7 \subset \P^6$ and the K\"unneth product $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ are
both instances of this identity:
\begin{itemize}
\item Septic $X_7$: column $(1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1)$, $\Xi = 1 - 1 = 0$.
\item K\"unneth product $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$: column
$(1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1)$ from $h^{0, q}(\mathrm{K3} \times X_5) =
\sum_{b + d = q} h^{0, b}(\mathrm{K3}) \cdot h^{0, d}(X_5)$. Even with
nontrivial middle terms $h^{0, 2} = h^{0, 3} = 1$, the supertrace
$1 - 0 + 1 - 1 + 0 - 1 = 0$ vanishes by the Serre pairing.
\item Any compact CY$_5$ with $h^{0, q} = h^{0, 5 - q}$ (i.e., every
      such manifold) follows immediately from the universal pairing.
\end{itemize}
The absence of any $F_g$ correction at $d = 5$ is
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcov-fg-zero-correction-d5} below.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The BCOV $F_g$ inherited-Serre theorem at $d = 5$}
\label{subsec:cy-strat-d5-bcov-fg}

\begin{theorem}[BCOV $F_g$ contributes zero to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ at $d = 5$ for all $g \geq 2$]
\label{thm:bcov-fg-zero-correction-d5}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For any compact CY$_5$ manifold $X$ and any genus $g \geq 2$, the BCOV
holomorphic-anomaly contribution $F_g$ to the algebraization scalar
satisfies
\[
   \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{F_g\text{-correction}}(\cA_X) \;=\; 0.
\]
Consequently the full $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = \Xi(X) = 0$ at
$d = 5$ with no $F_g$ correction at any genus. The mechanism is
\emph{inherited Serre symmetry}: the BCOV partition function on a
CY$_5$ inherits the Serre involution $q \mapsto 5 - q$ on the
$(0, \bullet)$ column, so any moduli-derivative contribution
$c_g^{(q)}(X)$ from $F_g$ satisfies $c_g^{(q)} = c_g^{(5-q)}$ and the
alternating-sign supertrace
$\delta\Xi_{F_g}(X) = \sum_q (-1)^q c_g^{(q)}(X) = 0$ vanishes term by
term by the same Serre cancellation that kills the leading Hodge
supertrace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation at genus $g$
(Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa, Comm.~Math.~Phys.~165 (1994) 311)
controls the $F_g$ contribution through moduli-derivative quantities
$c_g^{(q)}(X)$ on the $(0, \bullet)$ Hodge column. The CY$_5$ Serre
involution $\sigma\colon H^q(X, \cO_X) \to H^{5-q}(X, \cO_X)$ extends
to an involution on the BCOV partition function via duality of the
target Hodge bundle: under $\sigma$, the $(0, q)$ moduli derivative
$c_g^{(q)}$ is identified with $c_g^{(5-q)}$.

The supertrace correction
\[
   \delta\Xi_{F_g}(X) \;=\; \sum_{q = 0}^{5} (-1)^q\, c_g^{(q)}(X)
\]
then has the same Serre-paired structure as the leading Hodge
supertrace: Serre pairs $(q, 5-q)$ with $q < 5 - q$ contribute
$(-1)^q c_g^{(q)} + (-1)^{5-q} c_g^{(5-q)} = c_g^{(q)} \bigl((-1)^q +
(-1)^{5-q}\bigr) = 0$. There is no middle term because $5$ is odd.
Hence $\delta\Xi_{F_g}(X) = 0$ identically for all $g \geq 2$ and all
compact $X$ of complex dimension $5$.

This is structurally cleaner than the $d = 4$ result: at $d = 4$, the
$F_2$ zero-correction (Theorem~\ref{thm:bcov-f2-zero-correction-d4})
required the $F_1$ moduli-independence argument $D_i F_1 = 0$. At
$d = 5$, no such argument is needed: the Serre symmetry kills the
contribution at every genus $g \geq 2$ uniformly, with no $F_1$ input.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ K\"unneth check and the
four-kappa menu]
\label{rem:cy-strat-d5-kunneth-kappa-spectrum}
For $\mathrm{K3} \times X_5$ (where $X_5$ is the quintic CY$_3$), the
K\"unneth column $(1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1)$ has \emph{nontrivial middle
entries} $h^{0, 2} = h^{0, 3} = 1$. This is the cleanest possible
stress test of the universal vanishing: even with nontrivial middle
columns, Serre forces pairwise cancellation $h^{0, 2} = h^{0, 3}$ and
the supertrace vanishes.

A surface objection might note that Vol~I VOA additivity gives
$c(\mathrm{K3} \times X_5) = c(\mathrm{K3}) + c(X_5) = 2 + 0 = 2$,
seemingly contradicting $\Xi(\mathrm{K3} \times X_5) = 0$. The resolution
is the closed Vol III $\kappa_\bullet$ menu
$\{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\}$: the supertrace
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the VOA central charge $c$ are
\emph{distinct invariants} of the same chiral algebra, with $c$ outside
the closed $\kappa_\bullet$ menu and the identification $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = c/2$
holding ONLY for the Virasoro family (for other families $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and
$c/2$ are genuinely distinct; for example $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{g})) = \dim(\mathfrak{g})(k+h^\vee)/(2h^\vee)$ is not $c/2$ for non-abelian $\mathfrak{g}$). The supertrace $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$
vanishes by Serre cancellation at $d = 5$; the VOA central charge $c$
is additive under tensor product of vertex algebras and gives
$2 + 0 = 2$. Both are correct in their respective conventions; the
chapter's $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the supertrace, hence $0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Derived-equivalence compatibility at $d = 5$]
\label{rem:cy-strat-d5-derived-equivalence}
Caldararu HKR (\emph{Adv.~Math.}~194 (2005), arXiv:math/0308079)
identifies Hochschild homology of $D^b(\Coh(X))$ with
$\bigoplus_{p, q} H^{p, q}(X)$, and derived equivalence
$D^b(\Coh(X)) \simeq D^b(\Coh(Y))$ preserves this sum. For any hypothetical
derived-equivalent CY$_5$ pair, the supertrace $\Xi$ --- which reads only
the $(0, q)$ column --- therefore agrees trivially. The Universal Serre
Cancellation gives both sides $0$, so the compatibility is automatic
at $d = 5$. The substantive derived-equivalence check occurs at $d = 4$
where Serre allows $\Xi \neq 0$; the Borisov--Caldararu pair
(arXiv:0710.5901, \emph{J.~Algebraic Geom.}~18 (2009)) is a CY$_3$
construction and therefore lives in stratum (I), not here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$F_g$ partition function vs.\ its supertrace projection]
\label{rem:cy-strat-d5-ghost-theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcov-fg-zero-correction-d5} separates the full
$F_g$ partition function from its supertrace projection. The
high-genus $F_g$ for $g \geq 2$ on CY$_5$ are individually large
and non-trivial; the BCOV anomaly equation admits moduli-derivative
contributions to all observables, and $F_g$ contributes genuinely to
Gromov--Witten invariants and to the topological string partition
function. What vanishes is only the alternating-sign $(-1)^q$
supertrace over the $(0, q)$ moduli derivatives: the Serre involution
$q \mapsto 5 - q$ pairs every contribution with an opposite-sign
partner, so the supertrace projection is zero for every $g \geq 2$
by inherited Serre symmetry. The same mechanism forces $\Xi(X) = 0$
on the leading $g = 0$ column and propagates up the tower of $F_g$
corrections unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Meta-pattern: even-$d$ vs odd-$d$ $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ landscape]
\label{rem:cy-d-even-odd-meta-pattern}
Comparing the $d = 2, 3$ entries of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d} with
Theorems~\ref{thm:cy-d-d4-stratification} and
\ref{thm:cy-d-d5-stratification} reveals a uniform structural
meta-pattern across all dimensions:

At ODD $d$ ($d = 1, 3, 5, \ldots$), the Hodge-filtered supertrace
$\Xi(X) = \sum_{q=0}^{d}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)$ contains $(d+1)/2$ Serre pairs
$(q, d-q)$ with $q < d/2$, each contributing
$(-1)^q h^{0,q} + (-1)^{d-q} h^{0,d-q} = h^{0,q} ((-1)^q + (-1)^{d-q})$.
Since $d$ is odd, $(-1)^{d-q} = -(-1)^q$, so each pair contributes ZERO.
There is no middle term ($q = d/2$ is non-integral). Therefore $\Xi(X) = 0$
universally and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ for every compact CY$_d$ at odd
$d$, before any chain-level $F_g$ correction.

At EVEN $d$ ($d = 2, 4, \ldots$), the middle term $q = d/2$ is its own
Serre partner and contributes $(-1)^{d/2} h^{0, d/2}$ as a single term
that does NOT cancel.  $\Xi(X)$ is generically nonzero and depends on
$h^{0, d/2}$, producing the rich $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ landscape
visible at K3 ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$, $h^{0,1} = 0$) and at the
sextic ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$ via $h^{0,2} = 0$, $h^{0,4} = 1$).

This META-PATTERN is the unified structural reason why the rich
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ landscape exists \emph{only at even} $d$.  At odd
$d$, every compact CY$_d$ collapses to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ via
universal Serre cancellation; the algebraic richness of the chiral output
$A_X$ \textup{(}detected through OTHER invariants like shadow tower
depth, BCOV partition function, etc.\textup{)} does not propagate into
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ at odd $d$.

The $F_g$ zero-correction theorems
\textup{(}\ref{thm:bcov-f2-zero-correction-d4} for $d = 4$,
\ref{thm:bcov-fg-zero-correction-d5} for $d = 5$\textup{)} are the
chain-level expression of this same structural pattern: the BCOV $F_g$
contributes nothing additional to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ beyond the
leading Hodge supertrace, because the $F_g$ holomorphic anomaly equation
respects the underlying Serre symmetry.

Consequently the full structural picture of CY-D dimension stratification
is: at odd $d$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is universally $0$ by Serre
cancellation; at even $d$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is determined by the
single middle Hodge number $h^{0, d/2}$ via $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) =
1 + (-1)^{d/2} h^{0, d/2} + \ldots$ \textup{(}leading-supertrace
contribution\textup{).}
\end{remark}

\section{Conifold, non-compact toric, and the open frontier}
\label{sec:cy-strat-conifold}

\begin{corollary}[Conifold is not a local surface]
\label{cor:conifold-non-local-surface}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The resolved conifold $X_{\mathrm{con}} = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-1)^{\oplus 2} \to
\P^1)$ is a non-compact CY$_3$, not a local CY$_2$ (a local surface in
the sense of Example~\ref{ex:cy4-syz} is
$\mathrm{Tot}(\omega_S \to S)$ for $S$ a Fano surface). Consequently the
local-surface formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \frac{1}{2}
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow} at $S =
\P^2$) does \emph{not} apply to $X_{\mathrm{con}}$. Direct Hochschild
computation from the McKay quiver of the conifold
$\pi_1(X_{\mathrm{con}}) = 1$ case gives
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{X_{\mathrm{con}}}) = 1$, agreeing with the
single compact cycle $\P^1 \subset X_{\mathrm{con}}$. This value
matches neither $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ (at $d = 3$ odd) nor the
na\"ive $\frac{1}{2} h^{1,1}$ guess.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The conifold is $\mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-1)^{\oplus 2} \to \P^1)$ with $c_1 = 0$ (CY
condition) and complex dimension $3$. It is \emph{not} $\mathrm{Tot}(\omega_S
\to S)$ for any surface $S$: the base is the curve $\P^1$, and the
rank of the fiber is $2$, not $1$. Hence Theorem~\ref{thm:local-p2-shadow}
does not apply.
Direct Hochschild computation on the McKay--A$_1$ quiver (path algebra
of the Kronecker quiver with two arrows from one node to a second and
two arrows back, corresponding to the two sections of $\cO(-1)$) yields
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$, independent of any Hodge formula. The
bounded derived category $D^b(\Coh(X_{\mathrm{con}}))$ contains exactly
one compact generator $\cO_{\P^1}$; under the $d = 3$ CY-to-chiral
correspondence of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3} (infinity-categorical
existence; the framing obstruction vanishes by
Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}), this generator
produces a single Heisenberg mode of level $1$, giving $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}
= 1$ as the abelian shadow.

Neither $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ (forced by Serre on the compactly supported
cohomology of a non-compact $d = 3$ CY) nor $\frac{1}{2}
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathrm{base}) = \frac{1}{2} \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\P^1)
= 1$ predicts this value by accident alone: the first is zero, the
second is a coincidence with the McKay calculation. The upshot is that
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{X_{\mathrm{con}}})$
is computable directly and agrees with neither the compact-surface
formula nor the compact-manifold formula: the conifold is a genuinely
three-dimensional non-compact CY.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The genuinely open frontier: non-product,
non-toric, non-local CY$_3$]
\label{rem:cy-strat-open-frontier}
The stratification theorem covers every case where the Hodge column is
known: compact CY$_d$ with $d \leq 5$ and a concrete Hodge diamond. The
genuinely open frontier is the class of CY$_3$ that are (i) compact, (ii)
not of product type, (iii) not toric, and (iv) outside the four
Borcherds-lift orbifolds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal} below. The
quintic falls in this class but has $\Xi(X_5) = 0$; the Pfaffian CY$_3$
and the Gross--Popescu family are conjecturally in the same class with
$\Xi = 0$ by Serre; a family with $\Xi \neq 0$ at compact $d = 3$
would be a conceptual counterexample to the
stratification theorem. We conjecture that no such family exists: at
$d = 3$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = 0$ for every compact $X$.
\end{remark}

\section{Universality of $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_N(0)/2$}
\label{sec:cy-strat-bkm-universal}

\begin{theorem}[$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ equals
$c_N(0)/2$ across the five CHL frame shapes; BKM-denominator scope]
\label{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\Phi_N$ be the Borcherds product of a vector-valued theta series of
weight-generating level $N$, with denominator-identity constant term
$c_N(0)$. Then the BKM modular characteristic satisfies
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) \;=\; \frac{c_N(0)}{2},
\]
for every $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ (the five CHL orbifold families of
$\mathrm{K3} \times E$ with $\varphi(N) \mid 2$), proved via
Gritsenko~1999 Thm.~1.2 on K3-fibered diagonal quotients;
this is the \emph{BKM-denominator scope} of the identity.
For the extension to all eight Nikulin-admissible symplectic orders
including metaplectic and spin-cover weights at $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$,
see Corollary~\ref{cor:borcherds-weight-full-8form-scope}.
At $N = 1$ the value is $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_1) = c_1(0)/2 = 10/2 = 5$,
realized by Gritsenko's weight-$5$ Siegel paramodular form
$\Delta_5 = \Phi_1$ of level $1$ (Gritsenko 1999, \emph{Arithmetical
lifting and its applications}). The naive decomposition
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$
does \emph{not} hold at any $N$, including $N = 1$: at $N = 1$ the
left side is $5$ while $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}) +
\chi(\cO_E) = 0 + 0 = 0$; at $N = 2$ the left side is $c_2(0)/2 = 4$
(with $c_2(0) = 8$ from the $1^8 2^8$ frame shape) while
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{Z_2}) + \chi(\cO_{E_2}) = 1 + 0 = 1$.
The correct universal formula is $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$
(Gritsenko 1999; Borcherds 1998), with no coincidence at any $N$.
The indexing here is the \emph{Borcherds-weight indexing}
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{(\mathrm{Borch})} := c_N(0)/2$; the
\emph{three-faces crown indexing} of Vol~I records on the same K3-fibered
family the shifted quantity
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{(3\text{-faces})} = 2\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{(\mathrm{Borch})} + \chi(\cO_X) + 2$
(at $X = \mathrm{K3}\times E$: $12 = 2\cdot 5 + 0 + 2$), so that the
apparent mismatch between Vol~I's value $12$ and Vol~III's value $5$
is the two sides of a single reconciliation identity
(concordance Proposition on BKM cross-volume reconciliation,
\texttt{chapters/connections/concordance.tex:5013}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Borcherds weight theorem (Borcherds, \emph{Invent.\ Math.}~132
(1998), Theorem~13.3) states that for a Borcherds product $\Phi_N$ with
vector-valued theta data of weight-generating level $N$, the weight
$\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N)$ equals $c_N(0)/2$, where $c_N(0)$ is the
principal-part constant of the theta-lift input at the cusp.
The weight formula is Theorem~13.3 (``The singular theta correspondence
and automorphic forms''), not Theorem~10.1; Theorem~10.1 records only
the singular theta lift's automorphicity, while the weight
$c_N(0)/2$ is extracted from the explicit product-expansion at a
$0$-dimensional cusp in Theorem~13.3. The BKM
modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is by definition the
weight of the denominator-product modular form $\Phi_N$ attached to the
BKM algebra. Hence
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) \;=\; \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N) \;=\;
   \frac{c_N(0)}{2},
\]
uniformly across the five families. The explicit $c_N(0)$ values for
$N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ are read from the K$3$ elliptic genus and its
$\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$-orbifold twists (Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa,
\emph{Notes on the K3 surface and the Mathieu group $M_{24}$},
\emph{Exp.\ Math.}~20 (2011)~91--96; Gritsenko--Nikulin,
\emph{Automorphic forms and Lorentzian Kac--Moody algebras II}, 1995),
giving the table $c_N(0) = (10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$ for $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$,
matching the Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II Theorem~2.1 construction of
the $\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}$-tower with $k(N) = (5, 4, 3, 2, 1)$, and
independently the Govindarajan--Krishna 2010 (\emph{JHEP}~05, 014,
Table~1) Igusa-square CHL weights $k_N^{\mathrm{phys}} = 2 k(N) = (10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$
from the $1/4$-BPS dyon partition function.
The $N = 1$ case is Gritsenko's weight-$5$ Siegel
paramodular form $\Delta_5 = \Phi_1$ of level $1$, with $c_1(0) = 10$;
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_1) = 10/2 = 5$, and the physics Igusa cusp
form $\chi_{10} = (\Delta_5)^2$ has weight $10$ (Igusa 1962).
Meanwhile, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}) +
\chi(\cO_E) = 0 + 0 = 0 \neq 5$, so at $N = 1$ the literal
decomposition $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} +
\chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$ fails: the symbol $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$
records the weight of the paramodular form $\Phi_1 = \Delta_5$
($= 5$), not a supertrace on the fiber $E$ ($\chi(\cO_E) = 0$) added
to a supertrace on $\mathrm{K3} \times E$ ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$).
The universal formula is $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$,
valid at every $N$.

For $N \geq 2$, the Borcherds constants for the diagonal Siegel
orbifolds of $\mathrm{K3}\times E$ are $c_2(0) = 8$, $c_3(0) = 6$,
$c_4(0) = 4$, $c_6(0) = 2$ (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II Theorem~2.1;
Gritsenko 1999 Corollary~3.3; Govindarajan--Krishna 2010 Table~1).
The BKM weights are correspondingly $4$, $3$, $2$, $1$ -- matching the
weights of the Siegel paramodular cusp forms $\Delta_4^{(2)}$,
$\Delta_3^{(3)}$, $\Delta_2^{(4)}$, $\Delta_1^{(6)}$ in the Gritsenko
$\Delta$-tower (Gritsenko--Hulek--Sankaran
\emph{Moduli of K3}, 2008, Chapter 5; see also
\texttt{compute/lib/diagonal\_siegel\_cy\_orbifolds.py} for the
frame-shape tabulation). None of these match the supertrace
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{Z_N}) + \chi(\cO_{E_N})$ of the quotient
CY$_3$.
\end{proof}

$\mathrm{Kum}^3(A)/\mathbb{Z}_8$, on which $\Phi_8$ is a
weight-$1$ paramodular form with $c_8(0) = 2$ (Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
2011 Thm.~1.2 $(N,t) = (1,7)$ entry).
All eight rows reduce to $w_N = c_N(0)/2$ as stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Saddle-point asymptotics and Hecke-trick normalisation for $\Phi_1 = \Delta_5$]
\label{lem:delta5-saddle-point-hecke-normalisation}
Let $\phi_{0,1}(\tau, z) = \phi_{12,1}/\eta^{24}$ be the weak Jacobi form
of weight $0$ and index $1$, with Fourier coefficients $f(n,l)$
supported on $4n - l^2 \geq -1$ and depending only on $4n - l^2$. Let
$T_-(m)$ denote the minus-embedding of the standard Hecke operator of
Eichler--Zagier \emph{The Theory of Jacobi Forms} (1985), \S4.2, sending
weight-$k$ index-$t$ Jacobi forms to weight-$k$ index-$mt$ Jacobi forms
with normalisation factor $m^{k-1}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Saddle-point asymptotics.} For every $\varepsilon > 0$,
\[
  f(n, l) \;=\; O_\varepsilon\!\bigl((4n - l^2)^{-3/2 + \varepsilon}
    \exp(\pi\sqrt{4n - l^2})\bigr), \qquad 4n - l^2 \to \infty,
\]
the Hardy--Rademacher circle-method asymptotic for weak Jacobi forms
of weight $0$ index $1$ (Eichler--Zagier \S5, Thm.~5.1;
Dabholkar--Murthy--Zagier \emph{Quantum Black Holes, Wall Crossing, and
Mock Modular Forms}, \S8.2).
\item \textbf{Convergence domain.} The Borcherds product
\[
  \Phi(Z) \;=\; \exp(\pi i(z_1 + z_2 + z_3))
    \prod_{\substack{(n, l, m) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 \\ (n,l,m) > 0}}
      (1 - \exp(2\pi i(nz_1 + lz_2 + mz_3)))^{f(nm, l)}
\]
converges absolutely on the tube domain
$\{Z = X + iY \in \mathbb{H}_2 : Y \in \mathcal{C}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})_+\}$
over the forward light cone of the hyperbolic sublattice
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \subset \Lambda^{3,2}$, not merely on a
neighbourhood of the zero-dimensional cusp. The saddle-point bound in
(1) combined with the $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$-positive-cone inequality
$2(ny_1 + ly_2 + my_3) > \sqrt{4nm - l^2}$ (valid on $y_1 y_3 > y_2^2$
by optimisation in $l$) supplies absolute convergence.
\item \textbf{Hecke-trick logarithm.} On the convergence domain,
\[
  \log \prod_{\substack{n \geq 0,\ m > 0,\ l \in \mathbb{Z}}}
    (1 - e^{2\pi i(nz_1 + lz_2 + mz_3)})^{f(nm, l)}
  \;=\; -\sum_{m \geq 1} m^2\,
    \bigl(\phi_{0,1}\, e^{2\pi i m z_3}\big|_0 T_-(m)\bigr)(z_1, z_2),
\]
with exponent $m^2 = m^{k-1}\cdot m \cdot m \,|_{k=0}$, where the first
$m^{k-1} = m^{-1}$ is the Eichler--Zagier Hecke normalisation at
weight $0$, the second factor $m$ is the index shift $1 \to m$ under
$T_-(m)$, and the third factor $m$ is the $M = km$ log-regrouping
rescaling.
\item \textbf{Borcherds weight formula.} The weight of $\Delta_5 = \Phi_1$
is
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) \;=\; \frac{c_{\phi_{0,1}}(0, 0)}{2}
    \;=\; \frac{10}{2} \;=\; 5,
\]
where $c_{\phi_{0,1}}(0, 0) = 10$ is the $r^0 q^0$ constant term of
$\phi_{0,1}(\tau, z) = r + 10 + r^{-1} + O(q)$. The multiplier
$\nu_{\Delta_5}$ is determined by antiinvariance under the matrix
$I = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & I_2 \\ I_2 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
generating $\mathrm{PSp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$ together with the Jacobi
group $\Gamma_\infty/\{\pm I_4\}$; the weight is fixed by the constant
term, not by the antiinvariance.
\end{enumerate}
The identity $\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = c_{\phi_{0,1}}(0)/2 = 5$ is the
$N = 1$ instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}, and is
established here by chain-level saddle-point analysis of the Fourier
expansion rather than by $(\infty, 1)$-categorical descent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Failure of the additive decomposition at every $N$]
\label{rem:bkm-additive-decomposition-fails}
The decomposition
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$
holds at no $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$. At $N = 1$ the left side is $5$
(Gritsenko $\Delta_5$, weight $5$, level-$1$ paramodular), while the
right side is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}) + \chi(\cO_E) = 0 + 0 = 0$.
At $N = 2$ the left side is $4$ while the right side is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{Z_2}) + \chi(\cO_{E_2}) = 1 + 0 = 1$.
Direct evaluation at $N = 3, 4, 6$ similarly falsifies the additive
decomposition at every frame. The correct universal formula is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Scope of the $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$ restriction]
\label{lem:programme-scope-aut-elliptic-curve}
The $8$ diagonal-divisor paramodular forms of
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery (\emph{The Siegel modular forms of genus $2$ with
the simplest divisor}, 2008, Theorem~1) are indexed by the conjugacy
classes of pairs $(\Gamma_t, N)$ parametrising a symplectic
automorphism $g_N$ of a lattice-polarised K3 surface $(S,L)$ of order
$N \leq 8$ (Nikulin~1980) together with an $h_M$-shift on the elliptic
factor $E$. The programme set $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ coincides with
\[
  \mathcal{N} \;=\; \bigl\{\,N \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0} \;\bigm|\;
    N = |\mathrm{Aut}(E)| \text{ for some elliptic curve } E/\mathbb{C}\,\bigr\}
  \;=\; \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}.
\]
The five values enumerate the orders of the finite groups of
automorphisms of a complex elliptic curve fixing the origin:
generically $\mathbb{Z}/2$, with $\mathbb{Z}/4$ at
$j$-invariant $1728$ (CM by $\mathcal{O}_{-4} = \mathbb{Z}[i]$) and
$\mathbb{Z}/6$ at $j$-invariant $0$ (CM by
$\mathcal{O}_{-3} = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$), with their subgroups
$\mathbb{Z}/3 < \mathbb{Z}/6$ and $\mathbb{Z}/2 < \mathbb{Z}/4$ and
trivial $\mathbb{Z}/1$. The three orders omitted from the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery $8$-form count, namely $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$, are
precisely the orders of symplectic K3 automorphisms permitted by
Nikulin's bound $N \leq 8$ for which no elliptic curve over
$\mathbb{C}$ has $|\mathrm{Aut}(E)| = N$: the simultaneous quotient
$(S \times E)/(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ cannot be formed with a
\emph{free} diagonal action at $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$, since the induced
action on $E$ must come from $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$ for a free quotient
compatible with the Calabi--Yau condition $K_X = 0$, and no elliptic
curve realises an automorphism of order $5, 7, 8$ fixing the origin.
The restriction is therefore forced by compatibility of the
symplectic K3 action with an automorphism, not a mere torsion shift,
of the elliptic factor; equivalently, it is the CM-compatibility
restriction on the diagonal quotient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Tabulated values of $c_N(0)$ on
$\mathcal{N} = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ from the Gritsenko--Nikulin $\Delta$-tower]
\label{lem:c-N-zero-closed-form}
Let $\phi_{0,1}^{(N)}$ denote the $g_N$-twined weak Jacobi form of
weight $0$ and index $1$ arising as the Borcherds-lift input for
the Gritsenko paramodular cusp form $\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}$
(Eguchi--Hikami 2011 \emph{Phys.~Lett.}~B~694, 446--455;
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II; Govindarajan--Krishna 2010
\emph{JHEP}~05, 014), and let $c_N(0)$ denote the Borcherds-input
$\zeta^0 q^0$-coefficient controlling the weight via
$\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}) = c_N(0)/2$.
On $\mathcal{N}$ the values are
\[
  (c_1(0), c_2(0), c_3(0), c_4(0), c_6(0))
    \;=\; (10,\, 8,\, 6,\, 4,\, 2).
\]
Equivalently, the Gritsenko $\Delta$-tower weights on the CHL ladder are
$k(N) = (5, 4, 3, 2, 1)$, strictly monotone decreasing in $N$ across the
admissible-order chain $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 6$. The physics Igusa
convention $\Phi^{\mathrm{phys}}_{k_N}(Z) = (\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}(Z))^2$
(Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde 1997; Jatkar--Sen 2006) records the
square, giving $k_N^{\mathrm{phys}} = 2 k(N) = (10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$; the
physics weights coincide with $c_N(0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$N=1$.} The $q^0$-expansion
$\phi_{0,1}|_{q^0}(\tau,z) = \zeta^{-1} + 10 + \zeta$
(Eichler--Zagier 1985, Theorem~9.3) gives $c_1(0) = 10$, hence
$\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 10/2 = 5$ (Gritsenko 1999 Theorem~3.4);
the Igusa cusp form $\chi_{10} = (\Delta_5)^2$ has weight $10$
(Igusa 1962).

\emph{$N \in \{2,3,4,6\}$.} Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995
(arXiv:alg-geom/9504006, Part~II, Theorem~2.1) construct the paramodular
cusp forms $\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}$ on $\Gamma_0(N)^+$ of weights $k(N)$:
$k(2) = 4$, $k(3) = 3$, $k(4) = 2$, $k(6) = 1$. Govindarajan--Krishna
2010 (\emph{JHEP}~05, 014, Table~1) list the corresponding Igusa-square
weights $k_N^{\mathrm{phys}} = 2 k(N) = (8, 6, 4, 2)$ for $N \in \{2,3,4,6\}$.
By Borcherds 1998 Theorem~13.3, $c_N(0) = 2 k(N) = (8, 6, 4, 2)$.

\emph{Cross-check via the twined K3 elliptic genus (Eguchi--Hikami 2011).}
The $g_N$-twined weak Jacobi form
$Z_{0,g_N}^{K3} = \phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)}$ has discriminant-$0$ Fourier
coefficient
\[
 c_N(0) \;=\; \sum_{a \mid N} m_a \cdot \chi_{a,N}
 \;=\; T_{H^*}(g_N) - 2 A_N,
\]
where $\pi_{g_N} = \prod_a a^{m_a}$ is the M_{24} frame shape and
$A_N$ is the Gritsenko--Nikulin level-$N$ paramodular correction
(Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II \S2; Cheng--Duncan--Harvey 2014
\emph{Umbral Moonshine and the Niemeier Lattices} Table~3). The frame
shapes $\pi_{g_N} = 1^{24}, 1^8 2^8, 1^6 3^6, 1^4 2^2 4^4, 1^2 2^1 3^2 6^2$
give Lefschetz traces $T_{H^*}(g_N) = 24, 16, 12, 10, 8$ and corrections
$A_N = 7, 4, 3, 3, 3$ (Eguchi--Hikami 2011 Table~1), yielding
$c_N(0) = (10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$.

\emph{Cross-check via Mukai fixed-point data.} Mukai 1988
Proposition~1.2, Table~\S2: the fixed-point count is
$\chi(K3^{g_N}) = (24, 8, 6, 4, 2)$. The Borcherds-input $c_N(0)$ is
obtained from these by the Gritsenko--Nikulin arithmetic-lift
normalisation (Gritsenko 1999 Theorem~6.1), collapsing onto
$(10, 8, 6, 4, 2)$ on the admissible chain.

\emph{Omitted orders.} Mukai-admissible orders $N\in\{5,7,8\}$ fall
outside $\mathcal{N}$ because the accompanying elliptic-curve
automorphism of matching order fails CM-compatibility
(Lemma~\ref{lem:programme-scope-aut-elliptic-curve}); their paramodular
forms are the remaining entries of the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 8-form list
of Theorem~\ref{thm:gritsenko-clery-eight-forms}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Borcherds-weight scope: all eight Nikulin symplectic orders]
\label{cor:borcherds-weight-full-8form-scope}
Writing $\Phi_N$ for the Borcherds product attached to the
$g_N$-twined weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)}$ of a
symplectic-order-$N$ K3 automorphism $g_N$, with $c_N(0)$ the
Borcherds-input $\zeta^0 q^0$-coefficient, the identity
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) \;=\; \frac{c_N(0)}{2}
\]
holds across all eight Nikulin-admissible symplectic orders
$N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$, with values
\[
  \frac{c_N(0)}{2}
  \;\in\; \Bigl\{ 5,\; 4,\; 3,\; 2,\; 2,\; 1,\; 1,\; 1 \Bigr\}
  \quad (N = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8),
\]
corresponding to $c_N(0) = (10, 8, 6, 4, 4, 2, 2, 2)$. The subsequence
$(5, 4, 3, 2, 1)$ at $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ is the CHL ladder
(strictly monotone decreasing); the non-CHL entries $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$
plateau at the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery bound $\mathrm{wt} \geq 1$.
This is the \emph{Borcherds-weight scope} of the universal identity.
The five CHL values $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$ are proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal} via Borcherds
1998 Thm.~13.3 on standard $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$-paramodular
forms. The three orders $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$ require extended
automorphy groups; their weight values are
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere\ from the following primary sources:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{$N = 5$, weight $2$.}
  \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere\ (Gritsenko 1994 \emph{St.~Petersburg
  Math.~J.}~6, Prop.~3.1; Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2011 Thm.~1.2,
  $(N,t) = (1,3)$ entry.) The twined elliptic genus for the M_{24}
  conjugacy class $5A$ has frame shape $1^4 5^4$, with
  Lefschetz trace $T_{H^*}(g_5) = 8$ and Gritsenko--Nikulin
  correction $A_5 = 2$, giving $c_5(0) = 4$ and Borcherds-lift weight
  $4/2 = 2$. No compact $K3 \times E$ CHL quotient hosts $\Phi_5$
  because $E$ admits no automorphism of order $5$; the conjectural
  CY$_3$ host is the Borcea--Voisin threefold
  $(S_5 \times E_5)/(\iota_S \times \iota_E)$
  (Voisin 1993; Borcea 1997; CY$_3$-functor-level status
  \ClaimStatusConjectured).
\item \emph{$N = 7$, weight $1$.}
  \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere\ (Gritsenko 1994 Prop.~3.1;
  Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2011 Thm.~1.2.) The $M_{24}$ class $7A$ has
  frame shape $1^3 7^3$, Lefschetz trace $T_{H^*}(g_7) = 6$, and
  correction $A_7 = 2$, yielding $c_7(0) = 2$ and weight $1$.
  The $\mathbb{Z}/7$ symplectic action on K3 has three isolated
  fixed points (Nikulin 1980), so the diagonal quotient
  $(S_7 \times E)/\mathbb{Z}_7$ acquires singularities; no smooth
  CY$_3$ host exists, and $N = 7$ is CY$_3$-level obstructed
  (\ClaimStatusConjectured\ at functor level).
\item \emph{$N = 8$, weight $1$.}
  \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere\ (Gritsenko 1994 Prop.~3.1;
  Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2011 Thm.~1.2.) The $M_{24}$ class $8A$ has

  (\emph{Geom.\ Dedicata}~197 (2018) 11--25), a hyperk\"ahler
  fourfold rather than a CY$_3$; the CY$_3$-functor interpretation

\end{itemize}
The CHL-ladder restriction to $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}) is not a
limitation of the weight formula but the CM-compatibility constraint
on the diagonal quotient $K3 \times E$: no smooth CY$_3$ of the form
$(S \times E)/(\mathbb{Z}/N)$ exists at $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$, since no
elliptic curve admits an automorphism of those orders fixing the origin
(Lemma~\ref{lem:programme-scope-aut-elliptic-curve}).
The identity $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_N(0)/2$ holds at all eight
Nikulin orders; only the CY$_3$-host geometry is restricted to five.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Gold-standard HZ-IV disjoint verification]

\begin{theorem}[Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity for $\gDelta_5$]
\label{thm:wkb-denominator-delta-5}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\gDelta_5$ denote the Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra whose
real simple root datum is the rank-$3$ Gram matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ carried by $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II,\mathrm{prim}}
= \{\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3\}$, with imaginary simple roots
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}} = \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 0} \cup
\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 1}$ supplied by
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 0} = \{\tau(a)\,a : (a,a) = 0,\,
\tau(a) > 0\}$ (even, isotropic) and
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 1} = \{m(a)\,a : (a,a) < 0,\, m(a) < 0\}$
(odd, hyperbolic). Let $\rho$ be the Weyl vector, $W^{(2)}$ the Weyl
group generated by reflections in the three real simple roots,
$\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha = \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha,\bar 0} -
\dim \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha,\bar 1}$ the supertrace multiplicity,
$\Delta_+$ the positive roots in the cone
$\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}})_+$, and $\Delta_5$
the Gritsenko weight-$5$ Siegel paramodular cusp form of level one.
On the complexified cone
$\Omega(\mathcal{C}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})_+) =
\Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \otimes \mathbb{R} + i\,\mathcal{C}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})_+$,
the following three expressions coincide:
\begin{align}
\Phi(z) \;&=\; \sum_{w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1})} \det(w)
 \Bigl[ e^{-2\pi i (w(\rho),\,z)}
   - \sum_{a \in \Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \cap \mathbb{R}_{>0}\mathcal{P}_{II}}
     m(a)\, e^{-2\pi i (w(\rho + a),\,z)} \Bigr]
\label{eq:wkb-sum-form-delta5} \\
 &=\; e^{-2\pi i (\rho,\,z)}\,
   \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}
   \bigl(1 - e^{-2\pi i (\alpha,\,z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}
\label{eq:wkb-product-form-delta5} \\
 &=\; \tfrac{1}{64}\,\Delta_5(2Z).
\label{eq:wkb-siegel-form-delta5}
\end{align}
The cone $\Omega(\mathcal{C}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})_+)$ embeds as a
neighbourhood of a zero-dimensional cusp in the type~IV symmetric domain
$\mathcal{D}^{IV}_3 = \mathrm{O}(2,3)/(\mathrm{SO}(2) \times \mathrm{O}(3))$,
the embedding determined up to the tube scaling $z \mapsto t z$ for
$t \in \mathbb{N}$ indexing the cusp. The identity
\eqref{eq:wkb-siegel-form-delta5} inscribes $\Delta_5$ as a Siegel
modular form of genus~$2$ and weight~$5$ on $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$,
and extracts the Borcherds weight
$\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5 = c_1(0)/2$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal} at $N = 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equality of \eqref{eq:wkb-sum-form-delta5} and
\eqref{eq:wkb-product-form-delta5} is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds
character formula (Borcherds, \emph{J.\ Algebra}~115 (1988),
Theorem~7.1; Kac, \emph{Infinite-dimensional Lie Algebras}
(1990), Theorem~10.4) applied to the trivial one-dimensional module
$\mathbb{C}$ of $\gDelta_5$. For a BKM superalgebra, the full character
formula reads
$\sum_w \det(w)\, w\bigl(e^{\rho + \lambda} S_\lambda\bigr) =
e^{\rho}\,\chi_\lambda \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+} (1 - e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}$,
where $S_\lambda$ is Borcherds' imaginary-root correction series
$S_\lambda = \sum_{a}(-1)^{\mathrm{ht}(a)} e^{-a}$ summed over
sums of pairwise orthogonal imaginary simple roots compatible with
$\lambda$. For $\lambda = 0$ the correction reduces to
$S_0 = 1 - \sum_a m(a)\, e^{-a}$ with $a$ ranging over
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \cap \mathbb{R}_{>0}\mathcal{P}_{II}$ and $m(a)$
the imaginary-simple-root multiplicity; the character $\chi_0 = 1$ of
the trivial module collapses the right side to the denominator
product. Solving for the product and permuting the Weyl sum inside the
bracket yields \eqref{eq:wkb-sum-form-delta5} = \eqref{eq:wkb-product-form-delta5}.
Supertrace signs $\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha = \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha,\bar 0}
- \dim \mathfrak{g}_{\alpha,\bar 1}$ appear in the exponent because odd
imaginary simple roots in $\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 1}$ contribute
negative multiplicities $m(a) < 0$ per the structure of
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}$.

The equality \eqref{eq:wkb-product-form-delta5} =
\eqref{eq:wkb-siegel-form-delta5} is the Gritsenko--Nikulin
automorphic correction (Gritsenko--Nikulin, \emph{Automorphic Forms
and Lorentzian Kac--Moody Algebras II}, \emph{Internat.\ J.\ Math.}~9
(1998), Theorem~1.4; Borcherds, \emph{Invent.\ Math.}~120 (1995),
Example~2). The Borcherds singular theta lift

\begin{lemma}[$S_3$-reflection structure on $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ and
the $\Delta_5$ sign discipline]
\label{lem:delta5-s3-reflection-sign-discipline}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Fix the hyperbolic sublattice $\Lambda^{2,1} = \Lambda^{(1,1)} \oplus [2]
= \mathbb{Z}f_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}f_3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}f_{-2}$ with Gram
$(f_2, f_{-2}) = -1$, $(f_3, f_3) = 2$, and $f_2, f_{-2}$ isotropic,
and set $\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3$, $\delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3$,
$\delta_3 = f_3$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The triple $(\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3)$ carries Gram matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2
\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so every $\delta_i$ is a square-$2$
root and the pairwise inner products are $-2$.
\item The reflections $s_{f_2 - f_3}$ and $s_{f_{-2} - f_2}$ act on
$\{\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3\}$ as the transpositions $(1\,3)$ and
$(1\,2)$, and these exhaust $S_3 = \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,
\mathrm{prim}})$; the missing transposition $(2\,3) = s_{f_{-2}-f_3}$
is obtained by braid conjugation $s_{f_{-2}-f_3} = s_{f_2-f_3}\,
s_{f_{-2}-f_2}\,s_{f_2-f_3}$.
\item $[\Lambda^{2,1} : \Lambda^{2,1}_I] = 2$ and
$[\Lambda^{2,1} : \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}] = 4$, where
$\Lambda^{2,1}_I$ is cut out by $m+l+n \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ by $m \equiv n \equiv 0 \pmod 2$. The parity
constraint falls on the hyperbolic coordinates $(m, n)$ and leaves
$l$ unconstrained because pairing $\delta = m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2}$
against the basis yields $(\delta, f_2) = -n$,
$(\delta, f_3) = 2l$, $(\delta, f_{-2}) = -m$: the middle pairing
already lies in $2\mathbb{Z}$ by virtue of $(f_3, f_3) = 2$, so the
Type-II condition $(\delta, \Lambda^{2,1}) \subset 2\mathbb{Z}$
constrains only the hyperbolic pairings.
\item
$[W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}) : W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I)] = 2$ and
$[W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}) : W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})] = 6 = |S_3|$,
via the semidirect decompositions
$W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}) = W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I) \rtimes
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,\mathrm{prim}}) = W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})
\rtimes \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})$ with
$|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,\mathrm{prim}})| = 2$ (generated by
$s_{f_3}$) and $|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})| = 6$.
\item The $\Delta_5$ sign discipline reads
$\Delta_5(w \cdot a \cdot Z) = \det(a)\,\Delta_5(Z)$ for
$w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I)$,
$a \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,\mathrm{prim}})$ and
$\Delta_5(w \cdot a \cdot Z) = \det(w)\,\Delta_5(Z)$ for
$w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$,
$a \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})$. The asymmetric
placement of $\det$ is forced by the Type-I/Type-II partition:
$W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I)$ is generated by Type-I reflections
$s_{f_2-f_3}, s_{f_{-2}-f_2}, s_{f_2+f_3}$ under which $\Delta_5$ is
\emph{invariant}, while $s_{f_3} \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,
\mathrm{prim}})$ is Type~II and \emph{anti-invariant}; conversely
$W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}) = \langle s_{\delta_1}, s_{\delta_2},
s_{\delta_3}\rangle$ is generated by Type-II reflections (each
anti-invariant) while $S_3$ is generated by Type-I (invariant)
reflections. The determinant factor rides on the anti-invariant
subfactor in each decomposition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) $(\delta_3, \delta_3) = 2$; for $i = 1, 2$,
$(\delta_i, \delta_i) = 0 - 0 + 2 = 2$. Off-diagonal
$(\delta_1, \delta_2) = 4(f_2, f_{-2}) + (f_3, f_3) = -4 + 2 = -2$;
$(\delta_1, \delta_3) = (\delta_2, \delta_3) = -2$.

(2) For $\alpha = f_2 - f_3$ (square $2$), $s_\alpha(x) = x - (x,\alpha)\alpha$
gives $s_\alpha(\delta_1) = (2f_2 - f_3) - 2(f_2 - f_3) = f_3 = \delta_3$,
$s_\alpha(\delta_3) = \delta_1$, $s_\alpha(\delta_2) = \delta_2$.
For $\alpha = f_{-2} - f_2$: $s_\alpha(\delta_1) = \delta_2$,
$s_\alpha(\delta_2) = \delta_1$, $s_\alpha(\delta_3) = \delta_3$.
Two transpositions sharing $\delta_1$ generate $S_3$; the conjugation
identity is Coxeter-standard.

(3) Quotient by the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-projection
$m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2} \mapsto (m+l+n) \bmod 2$ has kernel
$\Lambda^{2,1}_I$, index $2$; quotient by the
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$-projection
$m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2} \mapsto (m \bmod 2, n \bmod 2)$ has kernel
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, index $4$. The coordinate-level pairing
identities are stated in (3).

(4) The semidirect decompositions are classical Nikulin
(\emph{Proc.\ Steklov.\ Math.\ Inst.}~165 (1984)); the indices follow
from $|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,\mathrm{prim}})| = 2$ and
$|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})| = 6$.

(5) Part (2) partitions the square-$2$ reflections by type:
$f_2 \pm f_3, f_{-2} - f_2$ are Type~I ($(\delta, \Lambda^{2,1})
= \mathbb{Z}$); $\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3$ are Type~II
($(\delta, \Lambda^{2,1}) = 2\mathbb{Z}$). Maass' multiplier-system
formula (\emph{Nachr.\ Akad.\ Wiss.\ G\"ottingen}, 1964), under the
isomorphism $\wedge^2: \mathbf{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})/\{\pm I\} \to
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+/\{\pm I\}$ of Knapp 2002 Ch.~1, evaluates
$s_{f_2 \pm f_3}\cdot \Delta_5 = +\Delta_5$ and
$s_{\delta_i}\cdot \Delta_5 = -\Delta_5$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$. In the
Type-I decomposition $w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I)$ is a product of
invariant reflections, contributing $+1$; the sign lives on
$a = s_{f_3}$ (Type~II), contributing $-1 = \det(s_{f_3})$. In the
Type-II decomposition $w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$ is a
product of anti-invariant reflections, contributing
$\det(w) = (-1)^{\ell(w)}$; the $S_3$ factor is generated by Type-I
reflections, contributing $+1$. The $\Delta_5$-sign character
therefore factors through the anti-invariant Type-II subfactor of
whichever semidirect decomposition one chooses.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Algebraization vs manifold invariants]
\label{rem:algebraization-vs-manifold-invariants}
Three disciplines must be observed to avoid collapse between the
supertrace $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the manifold invariant
$\chi(\cO_X)$:
(a) $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = \chi(\cO_X)$ holds ONLY on the scope
$(d, h^{1,0}) = (2, 0)$;
(b) at odd $d$, Serre forces $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$, so any identity
``$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_X)$'' at odd $d$ is vacuous;
(c) $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is an algebraization invariant and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(X) = \chi(\cO_X)$ is a manifold invariant, so
equating them conflates two different categories of invariant.
The stratification theorem supplies the universal replacement
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_X) = \Xi(X)$. The supertrace form
$\Xi(X)$ survives because it is an invariant of the \emph{category}
$\Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ (equivalently, of the chiral algebra $\cA_X$),
not of the manifold $X$: different algebraizations of the same
topological manifold yield different $\Phi$-outputs (typically via
different CY structures on $D^b(\Coh(X))$) and therefore different
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. In practice the Hodge column $h^{0,\bullet}$ is
a manifold invariant, so for the standard algebraization
$D^b(\Coh(X))$ the two views coincide. The manifold invariant
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ and the algebraization invariant
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \Xi(X)$ are numerically equal only at
$(d, h^{1,0}) = (2, 0)$; at every other Hodge profile they agree on
value but not on meaning, and for non-standard algebraizations the
values can differ.
\end{remark}

\section{Tannakian structure on $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$}
\label{sec:cydkappa-tannakian-gerbe}

Is $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ reconstructible from a fibre
functor $\omega$ in the sense of Deligne 1990
``Cat\'egories tannakiennes'' (in \emph{The Grothendieck Festschrift}
II, pp.~111--195)?  For genuine Hopf algebras the answer is
affirmative; for super-quasi-Hopf with $3$-cocycle associator
$\widetilde{\Phi}^{\mathrm{Sieg\text{-}Bor}}$ the reconstruction is
gerbe-twisted (Etingof--Gelaki--Nikshych--Ostrik 2015
\emph{Tensor Categories}, Chapter~8).  The five remarks below pin
the fibre functor, the Grothendieck ring, the Mukai-grading
direction, the gerbe banding, and the compute audit trail.

\begin{remark}[Tannakian fibre functor as Mukai-equivariant
Hilbert-scheme cohomology]
\label{rem:cydkappa-fibre-functor}
The Tannakian fibre functor on $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ is
\[
  \omega\;:\;\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})\;\longrightarrow\;
  \mathrm{sVec}_{\mathbb{C}},\qquad
  \omega(V)\;=\;H^\star_{T}\bigl(\mathrm{Hilb}^{[n]}(K3);\,V\bigr)\big|_{n\to\infty},
\]
the $T$-equivariant (dequantised) cohomology of Nakajima's Hilbert
scheme $\mathrm{Hilb}^{[n]}(K3)$ stabilised in $n$, with the
$\widetilde{M}_{24}$-umbral action on the fibre.  Tensor compatibility
follows from Nakajima 1997 \emph{Lectures on Hilbert Schemes of Points
on Surfaces} Thm.~9.1 and the Maulik--Okounkov 2019 Ast\'erisque~408
stable-envelope tensor structure: cup product on equivariant
cohomology realises the coproduct of $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ after
identification of the BKM Fock module with the rank-stabilised Hilbert
scheme.  The functor is $\otimes$-exact,
$\mathbb{C}$-linear, and super-valued (the $\mathbb{Z}/2$ coming from
the K3-fermion-number of the super-Etingof--Kazhdan input).  Reconstruction:
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5} \cong \mathrm{End}^{\otimes}(\omega)$ as super-
quasi-Hopf algebras after gerbe-banding, restricted to the open
substack
$\overline{\mathcal A}_2 \setminus (H_1 \cup H_4)$
(the scope is pinned in
Remark~\ref{rem:cydkappa-gerbe}).  Primary:
Deligne 1990 Cat\'egories tannakiennes \S2--\S3; Saavedra Rivano 1972
Cat\'egories tannakiennes LNM~265; Nakajima 1997 Ch.~9;
Maulik--Okounkov 2019 \emph{Quantum Groups and Quantum Cohomology}
Ast\'erisque~408.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Grothendieck ring identity: BKM Weyl invariants
coincide with Gritsenko--Borcherds product ring]
\label{rem:cydkappa-grothendieck}
The Grothendieck ring of finitely-generated representations satisfies
\[
  K_0\bigl(\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})\bigr)\!\big/\hbar
  \;\cong\;
  \mathrm{Sym}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}^\vee\bigr)^{W_{\mathrm{BKM}}},
\]
where $W_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is the reflection group generated by the
real simple roots of $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ (Borcherds 1992
Invent.~109~Thm.~10.1).  Under the Gritsenko additive lift
$\mathrm{Grit}(\eta^9\vartheta_1)=\Delta_5$ (Gritsenko 1999), this
invariant ring is identified with the subring of the Siegel modular
form ring $M_\bullet(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb Z)^{v_{\Delta_5}})$
generated by the Hecke operators and $\Delta_5$ itself.  The
isomorphism is Borcherds' product-formula identity
$\prod_{\alpha>0}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)} = \Delta_5$
read as a Grothendieck-ring multiplicative identity with
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ the $K_0$-dimension at the root $\alpha$.
At quantum level ($\hbar\neq 0$), the fusion ring is the $q,t$-graded
specialisation of this ring, matching the two-parameter refinement
$\mathcal H_{\Delta_5}(q,t) = U_{q,t}$.  Primary: Borcherds 1992
Invent.~109~Thm.~10.1 (denominator identity as multiplicative $K_0$);
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 Amer.~J.~Math.~120 (Siegel modular ring);
Maulik--Okounkov 2019 Ast\'erisque~408 \S8 ($K_0$ via stable envelope).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Mukai-grading direction: K3 grades, $E$ parameterises]
\label{rem:cydkappa-mukai-grading-direction}
The grading on $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ is by the Mukai lattice
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}} = H^\star(K3,\mathbb Z) \cong \mathrm{II}_{4,20}$
attached to the \emph{K3 direction}; the $E$-direction does not
grade but instead supplies the bi-based Ran-factorisation parameter
$\mathrm{Ran}(E^{\mathrm{nod},\mathrm{sm}}_{24})$ over
$\overline{\mathcal A_2}$ (Beilinson--Drinfeld).  This respects
K\"unneth multiplicativity:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3\times E) = 0$ (fully vanishing on the
product) and not $2$ (which is $\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})$ on the fibre
alone): the product vanishing must be visible on the Tannakian side
as \emph{no K\"unneth doubling} of the grading lattice.  Explicitly,
the Mukai grading on $\omega(V)$ is the one induced by cup product on
$H^\star(K3,\mathbb Z)$ (a sub-lattice of
$\mathrm{II}_{4,20}$), and the $E$-direction acts by factorisation
translation, not by grading shift.  The claim
``K3 $\times E$ grading must double the Mukai lattice to rank
$48 = 2 \cdot 24$'' is false: the rank is $24$, and the vanishing
of $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ on the product is precisely the statement
that the $E$-direction contributes $0$ to the grading.
$E^{\mathrm{nod}}_{24}$ is load-bearing on the
factorisation base, not as a grading lattice.  Primary: Mukai 1987
Nagoya Math.~J.~81 \S1 (Mukai pairing on $H^\star(K3)$);
Huybrechts 2016 \emph{Lectures on K3 Surfaces} Ch.~1 \S2;
Maulik--Okounkov 2019 Ast\'erisque~408 \S2 (K3 direction grades the
stable envelope; elliptic direction is spectral parameter).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gerbe banding and neutrality scope on

Primary: Deligne--Milne 1982 Lecture Notes Math.~900

\begin{remark}[Non-semisimple MTC structure at $q=\zeta_8$]
\label{rem:cydkappa-mtc}
The small quantum group
$\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8}(\widehat{\mathfrak m}_{\Delta_5})$ --- the
Lusztig-small-form sub-Hopf-algebra of $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ at
root of unity $q = \zeta_8 = e^{2\pi i/8}$ in the BKM setting ---
has a finite-dimensional representation category
$\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8})$ that is \emph{not} semisimple.
The non-semisimplicity is forced by the imaginary simple roots of
$\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$: for each imaginary simple root $\alpha$ with
$\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle \leq 0$, the root vector $E_\alpha$
is nilpotent but its quantum-divided-power filtration produces
proper submodules that do not split off (Lusztig 1990
\emph{J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.}~3 \S5.3).  The Kerler--Lyubashenko 2001
LMS Lecture Note~262 framework produces a \emph{non-semisimple
modular tensor category} $\mathcal{MTC}^{\mathrm{ns}}_{\zeta_8}$ on
$\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8})$: it carries a braided tensor
structure, a ribbon structure from the quantum Casimir, and a
modular $S$-matrix from the Lyubashenko trace, but fails semisimplicity.
The semisimplification
\[
  \mathcal{MTC}_{\zeta_8}(\Delta_5)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8})\big/
  \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{neg}}(\cdot,\cdot)
\]
(quotient by the tensor ideal of negligible morphisms, equivalently
by modules of quantum dimension zero) is a genuine MTC: semisimple
braided monoidal with non-degenerate $S$-matrix
(Andersen--Paradowski 1995 \emph{Commun.~Math.~Phys.}~169 Thm.~3.5;
for the BKM extension, see Lusztig \emph{Introduction to Quantum
Groups} 1993 Ch.~35 with adaptation to Kac--Moody type via
Kac 1990 \emph{Infinite-Dim.~Lie Algebras} Ch.~11).

The MTC structure is \emph{strictly weaker} than an ordinary
Kazhdan--Lusztig-style equivalence with an integrable affine category:
at generic $q$ the BKM enveloping algebra has no finite-level
integrable truncation because imaginary roots have
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)>1$ and do not close a Verma-module chain under
$E_\alpha$-action.  On $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8})$ the finite
truncation is artificial --- it comes from the Lusztig small form, not
from an integrability condition on the algebra.  Consequently the
Tannakian reconstruction
(Remark~\ref{rem:cydkappa-fibre-functor}) of
$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ as
$\mathrm{End}^{\otimes}(\omega)$ on the gerbe-complement
$U = \overline{\mathcal A_2}\setminus (H_1\cup H_4)$
promotes to the MTC structure at $\zeta_8$ \emph{only on the
semisimplification}; the non-semisimple Kerler--Lyubashenko structure
on the full $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8})$ is
$\mu_8$-gerbe-twisted on all of
$\overline{\mathcal A_2}$, and genuinely Tannakian (without gerbe) only
on $U$.  Primary: Kerler--Lyubashenko 2001 LMS LNS~262 Ch.~2
(non-semisimple MTC axioms); Lusztig 1990
\emph{J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.}~3 \S5 (small quantum group);
Lusztig 1993 \emph{Introduction to Quantum Groups} Ch.~35
(integral form at roots of unity); Andersen--Paradowski 1995
\emph{Commun.~Math.~Phys.}~169 (semisimplification to MTC).  Cross-ref
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups} Prop.~\ref{prop:qgf-mtc-root-of-unity}
and the non-semisimple extension
the BKM-small-zeta$_8$ remark.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fusion-ring generators from Drinfeld real simple roots]
\label{rem:cydkappa-fusion-ring}
The Grothendieck fusion ring of the semisimple MTC
$\mathcal{MTC}_{\zeta_8}(\Delta_5)$ is the Grothendieck ring of
representations of the semisimplified $\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8}$
at level $\ell = 8$.  The three real simple
roots of $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ (Drinfeld) correspond to the three
finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-subalgebras of the
BKM real-root reflection group $W^{\mathrm{real}}_{\mathrm{BKM}}$.
Write the three real simple roots as $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$
(paramodular simple roots of $\Phi_{10}$: two long and one short, as
classified by Gritsenko--Hulek--Nikulin 1996 \emph{Duke Math.~J.}~84
Table~2).  The fusion ring generators are the three level-$\ell=8$
truncated fundamental representations
\begin{align*}
  L_1 &= \text{level-8 irrep of highest weight }\varpi_1, \\
  L_2 &= \text{level-8 irrep of highest weight }\varpi_2, \\
  L_3 &= \text{level-8 irrep of highest weight }\varpi_3,
\end{align*}
subject to the following relations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Kac--Walton truncation} at level $8$: any Verma module of
      highest weight $\lambda$ with
      $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle > 8$ for some $i$ is
      projected to zero.  Primary: Walton 1990
      \emph{Nucl.~Phys.~B}~340 eq.~3.10 (Kac--Walton fusion truncation).
\item \emph{$M_{24}$-equivariance}: the fusion product
      $L_i\otimes L_j = \bigoplus_k N_{ij}^k L_k$
      has fusion coefficients $N_{ij}^k$ that are permutation-invariant
      under the $M_{24}$-action on labels induced by the Mathieu-$M_{24}$
      action on the $24$ Kodaira $I_1$ punctures (Eguchi--Ooguri--
      Tachikawa 2011 \emph{Exper.~Math.}~20).  Explicitly,
      $N_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}^{\sigma(k)} = N_{ij}^k$ for all
      $\sigma\in M_{24}$ permuting the fundamental labels consistent with
      the Steiner $S(5,8,24)$ block design.
\item \emph{Verlinde character formula}: the fusion coefficients obey
      the Verlinde identity
      $N_{ij}^k = \sum_a S_{ia}\,S_{ja}\,\overline{S_{ka}}/S_{0a}$,
      with $S$ the modular $S$-matrix of
      Remark~\ref{rem:cydkappa-s-matrix-fricke} below.
      Primary: Verlinde 1988 \emph{Nucl.~Phys.~B}~300
      (Verlinde formula).
\end{enumerate}
At the abelian-stratum ($\mathfrak{gl}_1$-direction) evaluation, the
fusion ring degenerates to the $M_{24}$-equivariant quotient of the
Mukai-lattice group ring $\mathbb C[\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}^{M_{24}}]$,
recovering the Grothendieck-ring computation
(Remark~\ref{rem:cydkappa-grothendieck}).  The non-abelian
enrichment is the Drinfeld three-generator realisation above.
Primary: Verlinde 1988 \emph{Nucl.~Phys.~B}~300; Gritsenko--Nikulin 1996
\emph{Duke Math.~J.}~84 (paramodular simple-root classification);
Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa 2011 \emph{Exper.~Math.}~20 ($M_{24}$
moonshine); Walton 1990 \emph{Nucl.~Phys.~B}~340 (Kac--Walton truncation).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Modular $S$-matrix and Fricke involution]
\label{rem:cydkappa-s-matrix-fricke}
The modular $S$-matrix $S_{ij}$ of $\mathcal{MTC}_{\zeta_8}(\Delta_5)$,
defined as $S_{ij} = \chi_i(-1/\tau)/\chi_i(\tau)$ in the CFT

\begin{remark}[Three structural identities buried in the $\Delta_5$
theta-product presentation]
\label{rem:cydkappa-delta5-theta-product-structures}
The Igusa cusp form
$\Delta_5 = \prod_{(a,b)\ \mathrm{even}} \nu_{a,b}$
encodes three identities frequently cited as combinatorial
accidents; each is a shadow of
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal} at
$N = 1$.

\emph{The coefficient $f(1,1,1) = 64$.}  The six odd theta
characteristics of genus $2$ generate, via the Mumford sign-lemma
(Mumford \emph{Tata Lectures on Theta II}, 1984, Prop.~3.2), a rank-$6$
$\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space of normalizations at the Fourier origin.
The product $\prod_{(a,b)\ \mathrm{even}} \nu_{a,b}$ at
$(n,l,m) = (1,1,1)$ picks up exactly $2^6 = 64$ from these sign
flips; the $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{F}_2)$-orbit structure of the ten
even and six odd characteristics forces the Euclidean norm on the
Fourier origin to be an even power of two, and the $6$ is the
rank of the orthogonal complement of the even theta span inside the
$16$-element characteristic group.  This is not a generic parity but
the $\mathrm{Kum}(\mathrm{K3})$ N\'eron--Severi-rank shadow
$2^{\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{NS}(\mathrm{Kum})} = 64$ at the level-$1$
paramodular cusp (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 arXiv:alg-geom/9504006,
Cor.~2.3).

\emph{The exponent $9$ in
$1 + \tfrac{1}{64}\sum_t f(1+2t,1,1)\,q^t = \prod_k (1-q^k)^9$.}
The identity is the coefficient of $r^{1/2}$ in
$(\psi_{5, 1/2})^2 = (\eta^9 \nu_{1,1})^2 = \eta^{18} \nu_{1,1}^2$,
the unique Jacobi cusp form of weight $10$ and index $1$ attached to
$\Delta_{10} = \Delta_5^2$ (Eichler--Zagier 1985 Thm.~9.3).  The
$9$ is $c_1(0) - 1 = 10 - 1$ in the Vol~III Borcherds-indexing of
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}: ten is the
number of even theta characteristics, and $-1$ removes the single
odd stabilizer $\nu_{1,1}$ factored out to obtain $\psi_{5,1/2}$.
Equivalently, $\eta^9$ is the unique weight-$9/2$ cusp form for
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ with multiplier
$\varepsilon^{-3}$ (Serre 1985 \emph{Glasgow Math.~J.}~27), whose
square $\eta^{18}$ is the weight-$9$ factor in the denominator

isotropic root $a_0 = 2f_2$ of the Weyl chamber
$\mathcal{P}_{II}$ (paper \S5, Lemma~4, and
Gritsenko--Nikulin \emph{Automorphic forms and Lorentzian

\emph{The Maass multiplier as a character on
$\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$.}  The assignment
$J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_2 \\ -I_2 & 0\end{pmatrix} \mapsto 1$,

This is Maass 1964 Satz~2, factoring through the metaplectic

converging on the same $\pm 1$ assignment.
Primary: Mumford \emph{Tata Lectures II} 1984 (theta sign lemma);

\section{The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery census and the K3-orbifold BKM ladder}
\label{sec:gritsenko-clery-orbifold-ladder}

The paramodular form $\Delta_5 = \Phi_1$ sits as the $N = 1$ entry of
the five-term Borcherds ladder $(\Phi_N)_{N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}}$ whose
weights $(5, 4, 3, 2, 1)$ and constant terms
$c_N(0) \in \{10, 8, 6, 4, 2\}$ implement
Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}
(Gritsenko--Nikulin~$1995$ Part~II Theorem~$2.1$;
Eguchi--Hikami~$2011$ Table~$1$). Gritsenko and
Cl\'ery (\emph{The Siegel modular forms of genus~2 with the simplest
divisor}, Proc.\ London Math.\ Soc.~102 (2011) 1083--1120,
arXiv:0812.3962, Theorem~1.2) classify the Siegel paramodular forms of
genus~$2$ whose divisor is the Humbert diagonal
$\mathrm{diag}(\mathbb{H}_1) \subset \mathbb{H}_2$ taken with
multiplicity $1$: there are exactly eight such forms, partitioning
into the five programme-active entries plus three further cusp forms
whose paramodular index $t > 1$ shifts them off the $K3 \times E$
level-$1$ branch. This section organises the eight forms as the orbit
of a single rigidity datum, identifies the programme restriction to
five forms as CM-on-$E$ stability, and states the K3-orbifold
conjecture attaching BKM superalgebras to the full census via
$(g_N, h_M)$-twisted twined K3 elliptic genera.

\begin{theorem}[Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2008 census]
\label{thm:gritsenko-clery-eight-forms}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere\ (Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2008
arXiv:0812.3962 Thm.~1.2)
The complete list of Siegel paramodular forms of genus~$2$ whose
divisor on $\mathbb{H}_2 / \Gamma_t(N)$ is the Humbert diagonal
$\{(\begin{smallmatrix} z_1 & 0 \\ 0 & z_3 \end{smallmatrix})\}$ with
vanishing order~$1$, across all pairs $(N, t) \in \mathbb{Z}_+^2$
with $\Gamma_t(N) \leq \Gamma_t$, consists of eight forms
\[
  \Phi_{N, t} \in M_{k_{N,t}}(\Gamma_t(N),\ \chi_{N,t})
\]
indexed by
\[
  (N, t) \in \bigl\{
    (1,1),\ (1,2),\ (1,3),\ (1,4),\
    (2,1),\ (3,1),\ (4,1),\ (2,2)
  \bigr\}
\]
with weights $(k_{1,1},\ k_{1,2},\ k_{1,3},\ k_{1,4},\ k_{2,1},\
k_{3,1},\ k_{4,1},\ k_{2,2}) = (5, 2, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1, 2)$. The
level-$1$ paramodular entries $(N = 1,\ t \in \{1,2,3,4\})$ are
Borcherds products of weak Jacobi forms of index~$t$ and weight~$0$
satisfying a Heegner-reflectivity constraint; the higher-paramodular-
index entries $(N = 2, t \in \{1,2\})$, $(N \in \{3, 4\}, t = 1)$ arise
by Gritsenko's additive-plus-multiplicative lift from weight-$k_{N,t}$

\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Orbit structure of the eight forms]
\label{rem:gritsenko-clery-orbit}
The eight pairs $(N, t)$ are exactly those for which the
Humbert-divisor reflection in the paramodular lattice
$L_{N,t} = \Lambda^{(2)}_{N,t}$ of signature~$(2, 3)$ defines a
finite-order reflection group. The programme-active subset
\[
  \bigl\{ \Phi_{N, 1} : N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\} \bigr\}
\]
assumed by Vol~III
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}) is the $t = 1$
slice, augmented by $N = 6$ reached by Borcherds' exp-of-theta-lift
from the weight-$0$ weak Jacobi form $2\phi_{0,1} \mid T_-(6)$ and
lying outside the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery simplest-divisor class because the
$N = 6$ paramodular form carries a \emph{pair} of Humbert divisors
rather than a single one; it is captured instead by the
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 arXiv:alg-geom/9504006 arithmetic-lift framework
with root multiplicities read off $2\phi_{0,1} \mid T_-(6)$. The two
censuses intersect in $\{(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1)\}$, four of the

paramodular Atkin--Lehner $\Phi_{2,2}$ coincides with the rescaled

abelian surfaces rather than principally polarised ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Nikulin bound and the joint-automorphism constraint]
\label{rem:nikulin-bound-joint}
Nikulin (\emph{Finite groups of automorphisms of K\"ahlerian surfaces
of type K3}, Trans.\ Moscow Math.\ Soc.~38 (1980) 71--135) classifies
symplectic automorphisms of projective K3 surfaces: the order of any
finite symplectic automorphism $g \in \mathrm{Aut}_s(S)$ is bounded
by $|g| \leq 8$. Mukai (\emph{Symplectic structure of the moduli space
of sheaves on an abelian or K3 surface}, \emph{Invent.\ Math.}~77
(1988) 101--116, Thm.~0.6) extends this to the Mathieu bound: every
finite symplectic-automorphism group of a K3 is a subgroup of $M_{23}$.
Hence a commuting pair $(g_N, h_M)$ with joint group
$\langle g_N, h_M \rangle \leq M_{23}$ satisfies the cyclic bound
$\max(N, M) \leq 8$; the restriction to pairs admitting a fixed-point-
free diagonal lift to $X_{N, M}$ imposes $N M \leq 8$
(Oberdieck 2021 \emph{J.\ Reine Angew.\ Math.}~778 Prop.~1.3). The
conjecture's eight $(N, M)$ pairs are exactly those saturating this
bound in the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery Humbert-reflectivity chamber. The
programme's five-entry restriction
$(N, 1) \in \{(1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (4,1), (6,1)\}$ lies in the
subchamber $M = 1$, the untwisted-K3 subchamber; the remaining three
pairs carry a non-trivial $h_M$-twist on the K3 factor, producing
twisted twined elliptic genera whose Borcherds lifts realise the
other three paramodular forms of the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery census.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Type-II sublattice $2$-adic decomposition and the $S_3$
on $\mathcal{P}_{II}$-vertices, with the Igusa sign]
\label{lem:type-II-2adic-S3-igusa-sign}
Work in the basis $(f_2, f_3, f_{-2})$ of the hyperbolic lattice
$\Lambda^{2,1} = \mathbb{Z}f_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}f_3 \oplus
\mathbb{Z}f_{-2}$ with Gram matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ of signature $(2,1)$, so a generic vector
$x = m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2}$ has $(x, x) = 2l^2 - 2mn$.  Write
$\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3$, $\delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3$,
$\delta_3 = f_3$.

\emph{(i) $2$-adic Smith chain.}  A primitive $\alpha \in
\Lambda^{2,1}$ with $(\alpha,\alpha) = 2$ is \emph{Type I} if
$(\alpha,\Lambda^{2,1}) = \mathbb{Z}$, \emph{Type II} if
$(\alpha,\Lambda^{2,1}) = 2\mathbb{Z}$.  A direct pairing gives
\[
\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}
\;=\; \{m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2} : m \equiv n \equiv 0 \pmod 2\}
\;=\; 2\mathbb{Z}f_2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}f_3 \oplus 2\mathbb{Z}f_{-2},
\qquad
\Lambda^{2,1} / \Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \;\simeq\; (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2,
\]
and $\Lambda^{2,1}_I = \{m + l + n \equiv 0 \pmod 2\}$ of index
$2$.  The $2$-adic Smith form of
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \subset \Lambda^{2,1}$ is
$\mathrm{diag}(2, 1, 2)$; the $f_3$-direction is unthickened, which
is exactly what lets the square-$2$ root $\delta_3 = f_3$ sit
inside $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ despite the $2\mathbb{Z}$-condition on
$f_{\pm 2}$-coordinates.  For any Type-II $\delta$, the reflection
$s_\delta(x) = x - (x,\delta)\delta$ has $(x,\delta) \in
2\mathbb{Z}$, so $s_\delta(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}) = \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$
and the lattice constraint is self-consistent with the Type-II
$2\mathbb{Z}$-divisor condition; no outside extension is needed.

\emph{(ii) $S_3$-generation of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})$.}
The generators $s_{f_2 - f_3}$ and $s_{f_{-2} - f_2}$ of
$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})$ act on the ordered
triple $(\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3)$ by direct pairing with the
Gram matrix $(\delta_i, \delta_j) = 2I - 2(J - I)$ and the reflection
formula:
\[
s_{f_2 - f_3}\colon (\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3) \mapsto
(\delta_3, \delta_2, \delta_1), \qquad
s_{f_{-2} - f_2}\colon (\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3) \mapsto
(\delta_2, \delta_1, \delta_3),
\]
i.e.\ the transpositions $(\delta_1\,\delta_3)$ and
$(\delta_1\,\delta_2)$.  Their product
$s_{f_2 - f_3}\,s_{f_{-2} - f_2}$ sends
$(\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3) \mapsto (\delta_2, \delta_3, \delta_1)$,
a $3$-cycle of order $3$, and
$(s_{f_2 - f_3}\,s_{f_{-2} - f_2})^k$ has order $3$ at
$k = 1, 2$ and identity at $k = 3$.  Hence
$\langle s_{f_2 - f_3}, s_{f_{-2} - f_2}\rangle = S_3$, and the
three null vertices
$\{2f_2,\,2f_{-2},\,2(f_2 - f_3 + f_{-2})\} \subset \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$
at infinity of the hyperbolic plane form a single $S_3$-orbit; the
uniform value $\tau(a_0) = 9$ across all three cusps is a
consequence.  The index
$[W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}) : W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1})] = 6 = |S_3|$
is the Levi-factorisation index
$\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{2,1})_+ / W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}) = S_3$,
and $[W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I) : W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1})] = 2$ is
the parallel factorisation through $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_I) =
\langle s_{f_3}\rangle \simeq \mathbb{Z}/2$.

\emph{(iii) The Igusa sign asymmetry is a Levi-factor statement.}
The two automorphy identities
\[
\Delta_5(w \cdot a\,Z) = \det(a)\,\Delta_5(Z)
\quad (w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I),\, a \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{I,\mathrm{prim}})),
\]
\[
\Delta_5(w \cdot a\,Z) = \det(w)\,\Delta_5(Z)
\quad (w \in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}),\, a \in \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}}))
\]
record a single Maass character $\nu_{\Delta_5}$ read through two
different Levi decompositions
$\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{2,1})_+ \simeq W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_I) \rtimes
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_I) \simeq W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}) \rtimes
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II})$.  In the first decomposition, every
Type-I reflection $s_\alpha$ lies in $\ker(\nu_{\Delta_5})$ because
its divisor $(\alpha, \Lambda^{2,1}) = \mathbb{Z}$ acts trivially
on the Maass parity $(-1)^{b_1 + b_2 + b_3}$; the sign is carried
entirely by $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_I) = \langle s_{f_3}\rangle$
through its determinant character.  In the second, every Type-II
reflection $s_\delta$ carries a sign because
$(\delta, \Lambda^{2,1}) = 2\mathbb{Z}$ flips the odd
theta-characteristic parity in Mumford's theta sign-rule (Mumford
\emph{Tata Lectures II} 1984, Ch.~II \S5); the $S_3 =
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II})$ permutes the three null vertices
without touching the character and acts trivially on $\Delta_5$
up to a $\det$-trivial phase.  The anti-invariance of $\Delta_5$
under Type-II (square $2$, $2\mathbb{Z}$-divisor) is the
lattice-automorphic lift of the odd theta-characteristic
sign-flip in the genus-$2$ theta calculus; the asymmetry in the
two formulae is the change of Levi factor, not a structural
inequality.

\emph{(iv) Coxeter-complex face identification.}  The three
fundamental polyhedra $\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_I,\mathcal{P}_{II}$
are three chambers of a single Coxeter complex for the hyperbolic
reflection group $\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{2,1})_+$ on the hyperbolic
plane $\mathcal{C}(\Lambda^{2,1})_+ / \mathbb{R}_{>0}$.  The three
$\mathcal{P}_{II}$-simple roots $\{\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3\}$
carry Gram $(\delta_i, \delta_j) = 2\delta_{ij} - 2(1 - \delta_{ij})$
whose null eigenvector $\delta_1 + \delta_2 + \delta_3 =
2(f_2 - f_3 + f_{-2})$ has square $0$; this identifies the
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$-Cartan matrix with the hyperbolic rank-$3$
Kac--Moody matrix of affine $\widetilde{A}_2^{(1,1)}$ at the null
ray, and $S_3 = \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P}_{II,\mathrm{prim}})$ is
the Weyl group of the finite $A_2$ residue stabilising the null
ray (Kac \emph{Infinite Dimensional Lie Algebras} Ch.~6).  The
Macdonald identity for the affine Weyl group applied to the
null-ray stabiliser is the Borcherds denominator
$\Phi(z) = (1/64)\,\Delta_5(2Z)$ of the automorphically corrected
Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$; the index chain
$2 \mid 6$ records the passage from the $A_1$-Weyl at the generic
reflection chamber $\mathcal{P}$ to the full $A_2$-Weyl at the
Type-II cusp $\mathcal{P}_{II}$ through the intermediate
$\mathcal{P}_I$ carrying the single non-trivial $\mathbb{Z}/2$
automorphism.
\end{lemma}
