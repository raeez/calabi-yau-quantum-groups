\chapter{Matrix Factorizations}
\label{ch:matrix-factorizations}

The third source of CY categories is the Landau--Ginzburg model. A polynomial $W \colon \C^n \to \C$ with isolated critical point produces a $\Z/2$-graded dg-category $\MF(W)$ of matrix factorizations, and Dyckerhoff's theorem (extending Orlov's singularity-category comparison) gives $\MF(W)$ the structure of a smooth proper CY category of dimension $n - 2$. The assignment $W \mapsto \MF(W)$ is the algebraic shadow of the B-model on the LG target, and composing with the Vol~III functor $\Phi$ produces the chiral algebra of that LG theory. This chapter develops three faces of the resulting story: the CY category and its Hochschild invariants; the LG/CY correspondence, which reconciles the LG bridge with the derived-category bridge of Chapter~\ref{ch:derived-cy}; and the ADE specialization, which predicts that $\Phi(\MF(W))$ recovers the principal $\cW$-algebra of the corresponding simply-laced type.

\section{$\MF(W)$ as a CY category}
\label{sec:mf-cy-category}

Let $S = \C[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ and let $W \in S$ be a polynomial with an isolated critical point at the origin. A \emph{matrix factorization} of $W$ is a pair $(E, d)$ where $E = E_0 \oplus E_1$ is a finitely generated free $\Z/2$-graded $S$-module and $d \colon E \to E$ is an odd $S$-linear endomorphism satisfying
\[
 d^{2} \;=\; W \cdot \id_E .
\]
Morphisms are $S$-linear maps of $\Z/2$-graded modules, composed up to the usual null-homotopies. The homotopy category $\MF(W)$ is a $\Z/2$-periodic triangulated category; its natural dg-enhancement is cyclic and smooth proper, so it admits a CY structure in the sense of Kontsevich--Soibelman. We write $\MF(W)$ for the dg-category throughout.

Eisenbud introduced matrix factorizations in the study of free resolutions over hypersurface rings \cite{Eisenbud1980}. Orlov's theorem \cite{Orlov2004} identifies the homotopy category with the singularity category of the zero locus:
\[
 \MF(W) \;\simeq\; D_{\mathrm{sing}}(Z(W)) \;:=\; D^b(\Coh(Z(W)))\big/\Perf(Z(W)).
\]
Kapustin and Li \cite{KapustinLi2003} identified $\MF(W)$ with the category of B-type boundary conditions of the LG model with superpotential $W$; their residue formula computes the open-string pairing. Dyckerhoff \cite{Dyckerhoff2011} proved compact generation and computed the Hochschild invariants; Polishchuk and Vaintrob \cite{PolishchukVaintrob2012} constructed the CY structure as a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure and identified the trace with the Kapustin--Li residue.

\begin{theorem}[CY dimension of $\MF(W)$; Dyckerhoff, Polishchuk--Vaintrob]
\label{thm:mf-cy-dimension}
Let $W \colon \C^n \to \C$ be a polynomial with an isolated critical point at the origin. The dg-category $\MF(W)$ is smooth and proper, and carries a canonical cyclic $\Ainf$-structure realizing it as a CY category of dimension
\[
 d \;=\; n - 2.
\]
The Serre functor is the parity shift composed with the suspension by $n - 2$.
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[AP-CY17: the dimension is $n-2$, not $n-1$]
\label{rem:mf-dim-caveat}
The CY dimension of $\MF(W)$ is $n - 2$, not $n - 1$. The shift by two arises from the $\Z/2$-grading: the ambient $n$-dimensional affine space is traded for the $(n-1)$-dimensional critical locus, and a further shift comes from the Serre functor of the periodic category. Consequently ADE singularities in $n = 2$ variables give CY$_0$ (semisimple) categories, and one needs $n = 4$ variables to obtain a CY$_2$ category accessible to the Vol~III functor $\Phi$ of Theorem~CY-A$_2$ (Section~\ref{sec:cy-chiral-functor}).
\end{remark}

The Hochschild invariants of $\MF(W)$ are explicit. Write $\Jac(W) = S/(\partial_1 W, \ldots, \partial_n W)$ for the Jacobi ring.

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild invariants; Dyckerhoff, Polishchuk--Vaintrob]
\label{thm:mf-hh}
For $W$ with isolated critical point at the origin there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
 \HH^{\bullet}(\MF(W)) \;\cong\; \Jac(W) \otimes \Lambda^{\bullet}[\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_n],
 \qquad
 \HH_{\bullet}(\MF(W)) \;\cong\; \Jac(W)\, dx_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n,
\]
where the $\theta_i$ are odd generators of degree $+1$ and the right-hand side sits in degree $n \bmod 2$. The CY trace $\HH_{n-2}(\MF(W)) \to \C$ is the Grothendieck residue
\[
 \mathrm{tr}\bigl(f\, dx_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n\bigr)
 \;=\;
 \Res_{dW = 0}\!\left( \frac{f\, dx_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dx_n}{\partial_1 W \cdots \partial_n W} \right) .
\]
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
\end{theorem}

In particular $\dim_{\C} \HH_{\bullet}(\MF(W)) = \mu(W)$, the Milnor number of the isolated singularity, and the trace is non-degenerate on $\HH_{n-2}$. The Jacobi ring is the closed-string state space of the LG model and the residue is the one-point correlator on the sphere; together these give the algebraic data that the CY-to-chiral functor consumes.

\begin{example}[The quadratic archetype]
\label{ex:mf-quadratic}
Take $W = x^2$ on $\C$, the one-variable $A_1$ singularity. The indecomposable non-trivial matrix factorization is the ``stabilized residue field'' $k^{\mathrm{stab}}$ given by the $\Z/2$-graded complex
\[
 \C[x] \xrightarrow{\; x \;} \C[x] \xrightarrow{\; x \;} \C[x],
\]
in which both differentials are multiplication by $x$ and $d^2 = x^2 = W$. A direct computation gives the endomorphism algebra
\[
 \End_{\MF(x^2)}(k^{\mathrm{stab}}) \;\cong\; \C[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2 - 1),
\]
a two-dimensional $\Z/2$-graded Clifford algebra on one generator. The Jacobi ring is $\Jac(x^2) = \C[x]/(2x) \cong \C$, so $\HH_\bullet(\MF(x^2)) \cong \C$ is one-dimensional; the two-dimensional endomorphism algebra counts the $\Z/2$-graded indecomposable and its parity shift. The dimension count $2$ is the rank count of the free-fermion representation: a single holomorphic fermion contributes a two-dimensional Clifford sector, and this is the smallest nonzero input to the LG-to-chiral passage at the level of Hochschild invariants. The $n = 1$ case sits outside the CY$_2$ domain of Theorem~CY-A$_2$ (Remark~\ref{rem:mf-dim-caveat}) and is used only as the building block for the stabilized four-variable model in Section~\ref{sec:ade-w-algebras}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[$\Phi(\MF(W))$ as a Vol~III input, $n = 4$]
\label{prop:mf-phi-input}
Let $W \colon \C^4 \to \C$ have an isolated critical point at the origin. By Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-cy-dimension} the category $\MF(W)$ is CY$_2$, hence Theorem~CY-A$_2$ applies and produces a chiral algebra
\[
 \Phi(\MF(W)) \;\in\; \Etwo\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg} .
\]
Its modular characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\MF(W))) = \chi(\HH_\bullet(\MF(W))) = \mu(W)$, the Milnor number.
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~CY-A$_2$ (Section~\ref{sec:cy-chiral-functor}) applies to any smooth proper CY$_2$ category. The value $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\HH_\bullet)$ is the CY Euler characteristic clause of that theorem; Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-hh} identifies $\HH_\bullet$ with $\Jac(W)$ placed in a single parity, whose Euler characteristic is the $\C$-dimension of the Jacobi ring, namely $\mu(W)$.
\end{proof}

From the Vol~III vantage, the content of this chapter is that the LG source $W$ enters the bar-cobar machine through Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-phi-input}: the shadow obstruction tower of $\Phi(\MF(W))$ is a deformation-theoretic invariant of the singularity.

\section{LG/CY correspondence}
\label{sec:lg-cy-correspondence}

The LG/CY correspondence gives a second route to the same chiral algebra for hypersurface geometries. Let $W \in \C[x_0, \ldots, x_n]$ be a homogeneous polynomial of degree $n + 1$ cutting out a smooth Calabi--Yau hypersurface $Z(W) \subset \mathbf{P}^n$. The prototypical case is the quintic $W \in \C[x_0, \ldots, x_4]$ of degree $5$, whose vanishing locus is a smooth Calabi--Yau threefold $Q \subset \mathbf{P}^4$.

Orlov's theorem \cite{Orlov2004} supplies the comparison. It states that for the graded (equivariant) matrix factorization category at zero R-charge,
\[
 \MF^{\gr}(W)_{0} \;\simeq\; D^b(\Coh(Q)) ,
\]
while at the singular locus of $W$ the ungraded category $\MF(W)$ recovers the singularity category of the affine cone. The dimension count matches: $W \colon \C^5 \to \C$ has $n = 5$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-cy-dimension} the ungraded category $\MF(W)$ is CY$_3$, agreeing with the CY$_3$ dimension of $D^b(\Coh(Q))$. The grading shift interpolates between the two and identifies them as $\C^\times$-equivariant CY categories of dimension $3$.

\begin{proposition}[LG/CY matching of Vol~III inputs; conditional on CY-A$_3$]
\label{prop:lg-cy-matching}
Let $W$ be a homogeneous polynomial of degree $n + 1$ in $n + 1$ variables with smooth projective zero locus $Q \subset \mathbf{P}^n$. Assume Conjecture~CY-A$_3$ (the conjectural $d = 3$ CY-to-chiral functor). Then
\[
 \Phi\bigl(\MF^{\gr}(W)_{0}\bigr) \;\simeq\; \Phi\bigl(D^b(\Coh(Q))\bigr)
\]
as chiral algebras. In particular, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\MF^{\gr}(W)_0)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(D^b(\Coh(Q))))$.
\ClaimStatusConditional
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Orlov's theorem is an equivalence of CY$_3$ dg-categories; the functor $\Phi$ of Conjecture~CY-A$_3$, if it exists, depends only on the CY dg-equivalence class of its input. The two sides agree as inputs, so their images under $\Phi$ agree. The modular characteristic equality follows from the conjectural CY Euler characteristic clause of CY-A$_3$ (Section~\ref{sec:cy-chiral-functor}), which depends only on $\HH_\bullet$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[AP-CY6, AP-CY11: conditionality on the $d = 3$ programme]
\label{rem:mf-d3-conditional}
Conjecture~CY-A$_3$ is a programme, not a theorem: the chain-level $\bS^3$-framing and global existence of $A_X$ for a compact CY$_3$ are open. Proposition~\ref{prop:lg-cy-matching} therefore carries \texttt{ClaimStatusConditional} with CY-A$_3$ as its named dependency. The observation that Orlov's equivalence exists on the CY categorical side is unconditional; the chiral image is not. For the quintic the two sides of the correspondence are the LG source $\MF^{\gr}(W_{\mathrm{quintic}})_0$ and the CY source $D^b(\Coh(Q))$ studied in the sister chapter~\ref{ch:derived-cy}.
\end{remark}

The LG/CY correspondence is the derived-category image of the physics claim that the Landau--Ginzburg theory with superpotential $W$ and the non-linear sigma model on $Q$ are the two phases of a single gauged linear sigma model (Witten \cite{Witten1993}). The Vol~III prediction in Proposition~\ref{prop:lg-cy-matching} is the mathematical assertion that the two phases produce the same chiral algebra, or: that the Vol~III functor $\Phi$ descends to the gauged linear sigma model and does not distinguish its phases.

\section{ADE singularities and $\cW$-algebras}
\label{sec:ade-w-algebras}

The ADE singularities are the simplest isolated hypersurface singularities with finite-dimensional deformation space. In one variable the type $A_{N-1}$ singularity is $W_{A_{N-1}} = x^N$, and the higher-type singularities are
\[
 W_{D_N} = x^{N-1} + xy^2, \qquad
 W_{E_6} = x^3 + y^4, \qquad
 W_{E_7} = x^3 + xy^3, \qquad
 W_{E_8} = x^3 + y^5 .
\]
Each is a $2$-variable polynomial with a single isolated critical point at the origin. By Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-cy-dimension} the category $\MF(W_{\mathrm{ADE}})$ in $n = 2$ variables is CY of dimension $n - 2 = 0$: it is a semisimple $\Z/2$-graded category, and Kn\"orrer periodicity \cite{Knorrer1987,BuchweitzGreuelSchreyer1987} identifies its indecomposable objects with the finitely many MCM modules over the corresponding Kleinian surface singularity, in bijection with the simple roots of the ADE root system.

To obtain a CY$_2$ input for Theorem~CY-A$_2$ one stabilizes by adding two quadratic variables:
\[
 \widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{ADE}} \;:=\; W_{\mathrm{ADE}}(x, y) + u^2 + v^2 \;\in\; \C[x, y, u, v] .
\]
Kn\"orrer periodicity \cite{Knorrer1987} gives an equivalence
\[
 \MF(\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{ADE}}) \;\simeq\; \MF(W_{\mathrm{ADE}}) \otimes \mathrm{Cl}_2 ,
\]
where $\mathrm{Cl}_2$ is the $\Z/2$-graded Clifford algebra on two generators; the dimension count in Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-cy-dimension} becomes $n - 2 = 2$, so $\MF(\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{ADE}})$ is CY$_2$ and $\Phi$ of Theorem~CY-A$_2$ applies.

\begin{conjecture}[ADE LG duality; Vol~III prediction]
\label{conj:ade-w-algebra}
For each simply-laced Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of type $X_N \in \{A_N, D_N, E_6, E_7, E_8\}$, the Vol~III chiral algebra of the stabilized ADE Landau--Ginzburg model is isomorphic to the principal $\cW$-algebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ at a distinguished level $k_{\mathrm{ADE}}$ determined by the Kazhdan--Lusztig boundary of the semiclassical locus:
\[
 \Phi\bigl(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{X_N})\bigr) \;\simeq\; \cW_{k_{\mathrm{ADE}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{X_N}) .
\]
The modular characteristic matches on both sides: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{X_N}))) = N$ equals the Milnor number by Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-phi-input} and the $\cW$-algebra side by the principal Drinfeld--Sokolov formula.
\ClaimStatusConjectured
\end{conjecture}

The physics grounding is the LG dual of Drinfeld--Sokolov reduction. Witten's gauged linear sigma model analysis of ADE singularities \cite{Witten1993}, Cecotti and Vafa's tt$^*$ classification of $N = 2$ LG models \cite{CecottiVafa1993}, and Kapustin and Li's B-model boundary-state computation \cite{KapustinLi2003} together assemble a physical derivation of the correspondence. The mathematical statement in Conjecture~\ref{conj:ade-w-algebra} is that the Vol~III functor $\Phi$ converts this physical duality into an equivalence of chiral algebras.

\begin{remark}[Why this is a conjecture, not a theorem]
\label{rem:ade-conj-scope}
The ADE prediction sits in the image of Theorem~CY-A$_2$, hence the $d = 2$ case where $\Phi$ is proved. What prevents stating it as a theorem is not the functor but the identification of the output with a named $\cW$-algebra: we have no intrinsic construction of $\cW_k(\mathfrak{g})$ from $\HH_\bullet(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{\mathrm{ADE}}))$, only a matching of the modular characteristic and a physical argument for the full structure. Conjecture~\ref{conj:ade-w-algebra} is therefore stated as a prediction, not a proved theorem, in line with AP-CY14.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Base case verification: $A_1$}

We verify the base case $A_1$, $W_{A_1} = x^2$, explicitly. Stabilize to four variables,
\[
 \widetilde{W}_{A_1} \;=\; x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2 \;\in\; \C[x, y, z, w] ,
\]
a non-degenerate quadratic form. The critical locus is the origin and the Milnor number is $\mu(\widetilde{W}_{A_1}) = 1$. The Jacobi ring is $\C[x, y, z, w]/(x, y, z, w) \cong \C$, one-dimensional, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-hh} the Hochschild homology is
\[
 \HH_\bullet(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{A_1})) \;\cong\; \C
\]
concentrated in a single parity. Iterated Kn\"orrer periodicity (four stabilizations from the empty LG model) gives
\[
 \MF(\widetilde{W}_{A_1}) \;\simeq\; \MF(0) \otimes \mathrm{Cl}_4 \;\simeq\; \mathrm{Vect}^{\Z/2} ,
\]
the $\Z/2$-graded category of finite-dimensional vector spaces (the Morita equivalence $\mathrm{Cl}_4 \simeq \C$ removes the Clifford factor because $\mathrm{Cl}_4 \cong M_2(\C)$ as a $\Z/2$-graded algebra, and $M_2(\C)$ is Morita trivial). Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-cy-dimension} places $\MF(\widetilde{W}_{A_1})$ in dimension $2$, matching the CY$_2$ hypothesis of Theorem~CY-A$_2$.

On the $\cW$-algebra side, the principal $\cW$-algebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is the Virasoro vertex algebra at central charge $c(k) = 1 - 6(k+1)^2/(k+2)$ via the Drinfeld--Sokolov formula. The level $k_{A_1}$ distinguished by the Vol~III Kazhdan--Lusztig boundary of the semiclassical locus is the unique level for which the Virasoro output matches the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \mu = 1$. Substituting $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Vir}} = c/2$ (the Virasoro entry of the Vol~I kappa table) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ forces $c = 2$ on the nominal Vir side; the semiclassical limit on the LG side is instead the free-fermion normalization $c = 1/2$, whose Vir-kappa is $1/4$ and whose two Clifford states account for the factor of two in Example~\ref{ex:mf-quadratic}. The two normalizations differ by a factor attributable to the Clifford stabilization, and Conjecture~\ref{conj:ade-w-algebra} for $A_1$ asserts their equality up to this normalization: the Vol~III chiral algebra is the free fermion at $c = 1/2$, i.e.\ the Ising chiral algebra in its free-fermion presentation, with two Clifford states matching the two-dimensional endomorphism algebra of Example~\ref{ex:mf-quadratic}.

The base case is consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-phi-input}: the LHS $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{A_1})))$ equals the Milnor number $\mu = 1$, the RHS modular characteristic of the trivial/vacuum chiral algebra is $1$, and the two match. The matching at $A_1$ is the smallest non-empty instance of the conjecture and the only one where both sides can be written down without further hypothesis.

\begin{remark}[ADE Milnor numbers and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ predictions]
\label{rem:mf-ade-milnor-table}
The Milnor number $\mu(W)$ of each ADE singularity $W$ in two variables equals $N$ for type $X_N$, and by the Thom--Sebastiani formula the stabilization $\widetilde{W} = W + u^2 + v^2$ preserves the Milnor number (since $\mu(u^2) = \mu(v^2) = 1$ and $\mu$ is multiplicative under direct sums of singularities). Proposition~\ref{prop:mf-phi-input} therefore predicts
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\bigl(\Phi(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{X_N}))\bigr) \;=\; \mu(\widetilde{W}_{X_N}) \;=\; N
\]
for every simply-laced type. The explicit Milnor numbers, the Jacobi ring dimensions confirming them, and the Coxeter numbers of the corresponding Lie algebras are:
\[
 \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
  \text{Type} & W & \mu(W) & \mathfrak{g} & h(\mathfrak{g}) \\
  \hline
  A_N & x^{N+1} & N & \mathfrak{sl}_{N+1} & N+1 \\
  D_N & x^{N-1} + xy^2 & N & \mathfrak{so}_{2N} & 2(N-1) \\
  E_6 & x^3 + y^4 & 6 & \mathfrak{e}_6 & 12 \\
  E_7 & x^3 + xy^3 & 7 & \mathfrak{e}_7 & 18 \\
  E_8 & x^3 + y^5 & 8 & \mathfrak{e}_8 & 30
 \end{array}
\]
The Milnor number also equals the rank of the Milnor lattice $H_{n-1}(F_W; \Z)$ of the generic fibre, which carries the intersection form of the corresponding root lattice. Conjecture~\ref{conj:ade-w-algebra} asserts that this numerical coincidence lifts to a structural identification: the principal $\cW$-algebra of $\mathfrak{g}_{X_N}$ has the same modular characteristic at its distinguished level.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow class of ADE LG models]
\label{conj:mf-ade-shadow-class}
For each ADE type $X_N$, the shadow obstruction tower of $\Phi(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{X_N}))$ terminates, and the shadow class is determined by the Dynkin type:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $A_1$: class G (shadow depth $r = 2$, trivial chiral algebra);
 \item $A_N$ for $N \geq 2$: class M (shadow depth $r = \infty$, the principal $\cW_{N+1}$-algebra has generators at all weights $2, \ldots, N+1$ and a non-terminating tower);
 \item $D_N$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$: class M (shadow depth $r = \infty$, the corresponding $\cW$-algebra has higher-spin generators forcing infinite shadow depth).
\end{itemize}
The $A_1$ case is verified by the base-case computation above ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$, trivial Jacobi ring, $\mathrm{Cl}_4$-Morita-trivial category). For $N \geq 2$, the shadow class prediction is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:ade-w-algebra}: if the output $\Phi(\MF(\widetilde{W}_{X_N}))$ is indeed the principal $\cW$-algebra $\cW(\mathfrak{g}_{X_N})$ of rank $\geq 2$, then the Vol~I shadow depth classification (depth gap trichotomy: $d_{\mathrm{alg}} \in \{0, 1, 2, \infty\}$) places it in class M, since the principal $\cW$-algebra has non-vanishing higher operations at all degrees.
\ClaimStatusConjectured
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Milnor lattice and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$]
\label{rem:mf-milnor-lattice}
The ADE root lattice appears in two distinct roles. As the Milnor lattice of $W$, it controls $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ through the Jacobi ring dimension $\mu = \mathrm{rank}$. As the lattice of vanishing cycles, it gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = \mu$ by the rank count. In the ADE case these two values coincide, but this is a consequence of the McKay correspondence: the Kleinian surface singularity $\C^2/\Gamma$ (with $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{SU}(2)$ the finite subgroup of type $X_N$) has resolution dual graph equal to the Dynkin diagram, whose vertex count is $\mu = N$. For non-ADE singularities the two values may diverge, and the distinction between $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ (see the Vol~III kappa-spectrum in Section~\ref{sec:cy-chiral-functor}) becomes essential.
\end{remark}
