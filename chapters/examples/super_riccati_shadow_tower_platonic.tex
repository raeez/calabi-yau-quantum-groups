\section*{Overview}
\label{sec:super-riccati-overview}

The chiral super-Yangian $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$ of
this chapter sits at the stage-$2$ shadow level of the two-stage
factorisation $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d - 1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation};
Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}). Native operadic level is $\Eone$ on
the reference curve $C$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}); the
braided $\Etwo$-structure supporting the super-$R$-matrix lives on the
derived chiral centre of $\Rep^{\Eone}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}})$,
not on the algebra itself
(Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre}). The super-Yangian itself
remains conjectural as a chiral-algebra object at $d = 3$: there is no
known compact CY$_3$ whose canonical $\PhiFA_3$ is proved to specialise
to $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$, and the super-Riccati
shadow tower here records the structural constraints any such object
must satisfy.

On a primary line $\ell$ of parity $|\ell| \in \{0, 1\}$ in a
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded chiral algebra, the master-equation Wick
reduction produces the \emph{super-Riccati recurrence}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:super-riccati-master-overview}
S_r \;=\; -\frac{1}{2 r \kappa_\ell}
\sum_{\substack{j + k = r + 2 \\ 3 \le j \le k < r}}
(-1)^{(j - 1)(k - 1)\,|\ell|}\, f(j, k)\, j\,k\, S_j\, S_k,
\qquad
f(j,k) = \begin{cases} 1 & j < k, \\ 1/2 & j = k, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa_\ell = S_2(A, \ell)$ the line curvature ($S_r(A, \ell)$
the weight-$r$ coefficient of the Maurer--Cartan expansion on
$\ell$; $\kappa_\ell$ reads off the second). At $|\ell| = 0$ the
Koszul factor $(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)|\ell|}$ is identically $+1$, and
\eqref{eq:super-riccati-master-overview} is the bosonic Riccati
recurrence of the Virasoro shadow tower. At $|\ell| = 1$ the factor
alternates in $(j, k)$, forcing the odd-line vanishings $S_3(\psi)
= 0$ (super-anti-symmetry of the three-point odd OPE) and
$S_4(\psi) = 0$ (absence of a weight-$4$ composite on the
single-mode fermionic line).

On the chiral super-Yangian $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$:
closed forms for $S_3, S_4$ at ranks $(1|1), (2|1), (1|2), (3|1),
(2|2)$ (Theorems~\ref{thm:sl11-closed-forms}--\ref{thm:sl22-critical});
a conjectural $k$-independent complementarity on the principal
bosonic sub-Sugawara line,
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}, T^{\mathrm{sub}})
+ \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(n|m))^{\mathrm{ch}, !}, T^{\mathrm{sub}})
= \dim \mathfrak{sl}(\max(m, n)) = \max(m, n)^2 - 1,
\]
verified at $(m, n) = (2, 1)$ (sum $= 3$) under the level inversion
$k \mapsto -k - 2 h^\vee$; and a shadow classification extending the
bosonic quadrichotomy $(G, L, C, M, \mathrm{FF})$ to super-analogues
$(G_s, L_s, C_s, M_s, \mathrm{FF}_s)$. The complementarity lives on
the super-Yangian KM-family branch, distinct from the Virasoro
Koszul-dual sum ($= 13$, at the self-dual Virasoro central charge)
and from the bosonic KM scalar complementarity
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{g})) +
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{-k - 2 h^\vee}(\mathfrak{g})) = 0$ at
Feigin--Frenkel involution; the three rationals $\max(m,n)^2 - 1$,
$13$, $0$ crystallise three distinct Koszul pairings.

Every closed form is verified in
\texttt{compute/tests/test\_super\_riccati\_shadow\_tower.py}
with independent-verification decorators on six master claims, all
carrying disjoint derivation and verification source sets.

\section{Super-Yangian setup: parities, super-dimension, dual Coxeter}
\label{sec:super-riccati-setup}

The super-Riccati recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master-overview}
only has mathematical content once the parity $|\ell|$, the
super-dimension, and the dual Coxeter of the ambient super-Lie
algebra are pinned down: the Koszul sign
$(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)|\ell|}$ is controlled by $|\ell|$, and the OPE
normalisations of the lines $\ell$ that we shall read off $S_3$ and
$S_4$ on in \S\ref{sec:super-riccati-closed-forms} are controlled by
$(\sdim, h^\vee)$. For $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$ with $m, n \ge 0$ and
$(m, n) \ne (0, 0)$, and for $m = n \ge 1$ on the quotient
$\mathfrak{psl}(m|m) = \mathfrak{sl}(m|m)/\langle I\rangle$ (the
one-dimensional centre $\langle I\rangle$ obstructs the super-Sugawara
construction on $\mathfrak{sl}(m|m)$ directly):

\begin{definition}[Super-parity on \texorpdfstring{$\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$}{sl(m|n)}]
\label{def:super-parity-sl-mn}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $V = \C^{m|n}$ be the defining representation with the first $m$
basis vectors even (parity $0$) and the last $n$ basis vectors odd
(parity $1$). The induced parity on
$\mathfrak{sl}(m|n) = \mathfrak{end}(V)_0$-trace-free is
\[
|E_{ij}| \;=\; |i| + |j| \pmod{2},
\qquad
|i| = \begin{cases} 0 & 1 \le i \le m, \\ 1 & m+1 \le i \le m + n. \end{cases}
\]
The super-dimension is
$\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)) = (m^2 + n^2 - 1) - 2mn = (m - n)^2 - 1$.
The dual Coxeter number is $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)) = m - n$
for $m > n \ge 0$ and $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)) = n - m$ for $n > m \ge 0$;
at $m = n$, $h^\vee = 0$ and the Sugawara tensor is undefined (critical
super-level for every value of $k$).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Parity vs grading, Lie superalgebra vs super-Lie]
\label{rem:parity-grading-distinction}
The Koszul sign rule $\varepsilon(a, b) = (-1)^{|a| \cdot |b|}$ is
keyed to a $\mathbb{Z}/2$ parity, not a $\mathbb{Z}$-grading. The
super-Riccati recurrence therefore does not reduce to the bosonic
Riccati by merely flipping signs on odd-indexed terms: parity lives
on generators and propagates through Wick contractions. The
ingredient is a Lie superalgebra
$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_{\bar 0} \oplus \mathfrak{g}_{\bar 1}$
with super-Jacobi bracket; its universal enveloping vertex algebra
carries a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading compatible with the OPE. The
super-bracket on an odd generator $\psi$ is
$\{\psi, \psi\} = 2 \psi^2$ (symmetric anticommutator); the ordinary
commutator $[\psi, \psi]$ is not the structure constant in the
super-Lie category and should not be read as the Lie bracket here.
The sign-twisted vanishing in the Riccati sum arises precisely from
the super-bracket symmetry, not from a commutator identity.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Chiral super-Yangian \texorpdfstring{$Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$}{Y-hbar(sl(m|n))-ch}]
\label{def:chiral-super-yangian-mn}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The chiral super-Yangian $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is
the $\Eone$-chiral algebra on the formal disk (native operadic level
$n_{\mathrm{native}}(d) = 1$ at $d \geq 3$; Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level})
generated by the current
modes $t^{(r)}_{ij}$ for $1 \le i, j \le m + n$ and $r \ge 0$, with
super-RTT relations in the Nazarov convention: on $V \otimes V$,
\[
R(u - v) \, T_1(u) \, T_2(v)
\;=\; T_2(v) \, T_1(u) \, R(u - v),
\]
where $R(u) = I - \hbar \, P^s / u$ is the super-rational $R$-matrix
and $P^s(e_i \otimes e_j) = (-1)^{|i| \cdot |j|} \, e_j \otimes e_i$ is
the graded permutation. At classical limit $\hbar \to 0$, the chiral
super-Yangian degenerates to $U(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)[t])$.
\end{definition}

\section{Derivation of the super-Riccati recurrence}
\label{sec:super-riccati-derivation}

The bosonic specialisation $|\ell| = 0$ of
\eqref{eq:super-riccati-master-overview} descends from the
Maurer--Cartan master equation
$d\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ (the $L_\infty$
integrability condition for a degree-$1$ element $\Theta$ in a
dGLA: flatness of $\Theta$ as a deformation cocycle) on the ordered
convolution dGLA $\mathfrak{g}_A^{\mathrm{conv}}$ (the chain-level
Lie algebra whose underlying complex is
$\mathrm{Hom}^{\bullet}(B(A), A)$ with convolution bracket), restricted
to a single primary line $\ell$. When the chiral algebra is super,
the bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]$ is the super-bracket
$[a, b] = a \cdot b - (-1)^{|a| |b|} \, b \cdot a$, and the
master-equation Wick reduction picks up Koszul signs according to
the parity $|\ell|$ propagated through each contraction.

\begin{theorem}[Super-Riccati master recurrence]
\label{thm:super-riccati-master-recurrence}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $A$ be a super chiral algebra with $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading, and let
$\ell \subset A$ be a non-degenerate primary line of parity
$|\ell| \in \{0, 1\}$. Assume $\kappa_\ell = S_2(A, \ell) \ne 0$. The
line-restricted shadow coefficients $\{S_r(A, \ell)\}_{r \ge 3}$
satisfy the super-Riccati recurrence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:super-riccati-master}
S_r \;=\; -\frac{1}{2 r \kappa_\ell}
\sum_{\substack{j + k = r + 2 \\ 3 \le j \le k < r}}
(-1)^{(j - 1)(k - 1) |\ell|} \, f(j, k) \, j\,k\, S_j\, S_k.
\end{equation}
At $|\ell| = 0$ the Koszul sign is identically $+1$ and
\eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} reduces to the bosonic recurrence
\eqref{eq:super-riccati-master-overview}. At $|\ell| = 1$ the sign
$(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}$ tracks the parity of the Wick-contraction count
in a $j$-with-$k$ split of a weight-$r$ correlator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Let $\Theta \in \mathfrak{g}_A^{\mathrm{conv}}$ denote the
Maurer--Cartan element of the convolution dGLA of $A$ on the formal
disk, with differential $d$ the bar-complex CE differential and
bracket the super-bracket of chiral convolution operators. Consider
the master equation $d\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$
expanded on the line $\ell$ as
$\Theta_\ell = \sum_{r \ge 2} S_r(A, \ell) \, \theta_r$
for a chosen generator $\theta_r$ of weight $r$ on $\ell$. The
super-bracket on the line reduces to
\[
[\theta_j, \theta_k]
\;=\; \theta_j \cdot \theta_k - (-1)^{|\theta_j| \cdot |\theta_k|} \,
 \theta_k \cdot \theta_j.
\]
For a generator of weight $r$ on a line of parity $|\ell|$, the
induced parity is
$|\theta_r| = (r - 1) \cdot |\ell| \pmod{2}$
(the weight-$r$ descendant of a parity-$|\ell|$ primary carries parity
$(r - 1) \cdot |\ell|$ by the OPE counting: each OPE pole reduces the
parity-counting weight by $1$, so a weight-$r$ composite of a single
line generator descends to parity $(r - 1) |\ell| \mod 2$, where the
$-1$ offset accounts for the primary line itself sitting at weight
$1$).

Expanding the master equation to order $r$ in $\Theta_\ell$, the
coefficient of $\theta_{r+2}$ on the RHS is
\[
\tfrac{1}{2}\,[\Theta_\ell, \Theta_\ell]|_{\text{order } r+2}
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}
\sum_{j + k = r + 2} S_j S_k \, [\theta_j, \theta_k].
\]
The convolution bracket $[\theta_j, \theta_k]_{\mathrm{conv}}$ on
line-descendants is built from the bar coproduct of $B(A)$ and the
product on $A$; its Wick expansion counts the parity-signed
contractions of a $j$-leg into a $k$-leg, producing a descendant
of weight $j + k - 2$ with Koszul sign
$(-1)^{|\theta_j|\cdot|\theta_k|} = (-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)|\ell|^2} =
(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)|\ell|}$ (using $|\ell|^2 \equiv |\ell| \pmod 2$).
Reading $|\ell| \in \{0, 1\}$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $|\ell| = 0$: every descendant is bosonic, $|\theta_r| = 0$, and
 the Koszul sign collapses to $+1$; the convolution bracket reduces
 to its symmetric form and $[\Theta_\ell, \Theta_\ell]_{\mathrm{conv}}$
 generates the bosonic Riccati recurrence of Vol~I.
\item $|\ell| = 1$: parities of descendants are $|\theta_r| = (r-1)
 \pmod 2$. Odd $r$: $|\theta_r| = 0$ (bosonic descendant). Even $r$:
 $|\theta_r| = 1$ (fermionic descendant). The convolution bracket
 $[\theta_j, \theta_k]_{\mathrm{conv}}$ enters the master equation
 with sign $(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}$, which is precisely the Koszul sign
 in \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master}.
\end{itemize}

The normalisation prefactor $-1/(2 r \kappa_\ell)$ comes from solving
for the leading $\theta_{r+2}$-component, with the factor of $2$
absorbing the symmetric-product factor in the definition of
$[\Theta, \Theta]$ and $r$ from the generator weight. The
unordered-pair factor $f(j,k)$ and the $j \cdot k$ combinatorial
weight track the number of ways of splitting a weight-$r+2$
correlator into a pair of weight-$j$ and weight-$k$ legs. These are
identical to the bosonic case (no parity dependence in the
combinatorial factor).

For the case $|\ell| = 0$, the identity reduces to the bosonic
Riccati by the identity $(-1)^0 = 1$. For $|\ell| = 1$, the Koszul
sign is explicit in \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Super-Riccati odd-line \texorpdfstring{$S_3 = 0$}{S-3 = 0} vanishing]
\label{cor:super-riccati-S3-odd-vanishing}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
On any odd line ($|\ell| = 1$) of a super chiral algebra for which the
primary generator is quadratic in a bosonic cover (i.e.\ the three-point
$\langle \psi \, \psi \, \psi \rangle$ vanishes by super-antisymmetry
of the OPE), $S_3(A, \ell) = 0$. The bar complex on this line is then
reduced to the weight-$\ge 4$ Wick contractions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The three-point super-correlator
$\langle \psi(z_1) \psi(z_2) \psi(z_3) \rangle$ is anti-symmetric
under every pair-wise exchange (super-anti-symmetry of the odd OPE
at order 1, parity sign $(-1)^{|\psi|\cdot|\psi|} = (-1)^{1\cdot 1}
= -1$). A single transposition, say $(z_1, z_2) \mapsto (z_2, z_1)$,
forces $\langle \psi \psi \psi \rangle = -\langle \psi \psi \psi
\rangle$ and hence zero. (A three-cycle, being an even permutation,
preserves the sign and would not suffice.) The shadow coefficient
$S_3$ is a scalar multiple of this three-point function restricted
to the line, so $S_3 = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Super-Riccati odd-line \texorpdfstring{$S_4$}{S-4} is bosonic-like]
\label{cor:super-riccati-S4-odd-bosonic-like}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\ell$ be an odd line with vanishing $S_3$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:super-riccati-S3-odd-vanishing}).
Then $S_4(A, \ell)$ is seeded by the weight-$4$ OPE channel and takes
the Zamolodchikov form
\[
S_4(A, \ell)
\;=\; \frac{\alpha_\ell^2}{2 \kappa_\ell \cdot N_{\mathrm{comp}}(\ell)},
\]
where $\alpha_\ell$ is the OPE structure constant at the weight-$4$
channel and $N_{\mathrm{comp}}(\ell) = \langle \phi_{\mathrm{comp}}(z) \,
\phi_{\mathrm{comp}}(w) \rangle|_{(z-w)^{-8}}$ is the Zamolodchikov
norm of the weight-$4$ composite in the $\psi \cdot \psi$ OPE. The
Koszul sign $(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)} = (-1)^{4} = +1$ at $(j, k) = (3, 3)$
eliminates the sign dependence at the level of $S_4$: even on a
fermionic line, $S_4$ is positive when $\alpha_\ell^2 > 0$,
$\kappa_\ell > 0$, and $N_{\mathrm{comp}}(\ell) > 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
At $r = 4$ in \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master}, the sum runs only over
$(j, k) = (3, 3)$ with $f(3, 3) = 1/2$, since $k = r - 1 = 3$ is the
only admissible value with $j \le k$. But $S_3 = 0$ from
Cor.~\ref{cor:super-riccati-S3-odd-vanishing}, so the sum vanishes at
this level. The $S_4$ coefficient is then supplied by the
non-recursive part of the master equation (the $d\Theta$ linear piece and the
$\alpha_\ell$-seed from the weight-$4$ OPE channel) rather than the
quadratic recursion. This matches the structure of the $W$-line of
$\cW_3$, where $S_3^W = 0$ by $\mathbb{Z}_2$ parity $W \mapsto -W$
and $S_4^W$ is seeded by $\alpha = 16/(5c + 22)$ directly
(Bershadsky--Polyakov; Fateev--Lukyanov).
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$A_\infty$}{A-inf}-coproduct identification and Riccati factorisation}
\label{sec:super-riccati-ainfty-and-factorisation}

The super-Riccati recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} is not merely
a combinatorial formula: each coefficient $S_r(A, \ell)$ is the
$A_\infty$-coproduct correction $m_r$ on the bar complex $B(A)$
restricted to the line $\ell$. On the class M reference line (the
Virasoro-like bosonic specialisation $|\ell| = 0$ with $c = 1$), the
tower is rational of a single form.

\begin{theorem}[Shadow tower = \texorpdfstring{$A_\infty$}{A-inf} coproduct corrections]
\label{thm:shadow-tower-ainfty-coproduct}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
On a primary line $\ell$ of a chiral algebra $A$, the weight-$r$ shadow
coefficient $S_r(A, \ell)$ equals the $r$-ary $A_\infty$-coproduct
correction $m_r$ on $B(A)|_\ell$, where $B(A)$ carries the chiral bar
$A_\infty$-coalgebra structure of Theorem~A (Vol~I). The super-Riccati
recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} is the $A_\infty$-associativity
constraint on the coefficients $m_r$, with Koszul signs tracking the
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading of $B(A)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The bar $A_\infty$-coalgebra $B(A)$ of a chiral algebra $A$ carries
higher coproducts $\Delta_r : B(A) \to B(A)^{\otimes r}$ for $r \ge 2$,
with $\Delta_2$ the strict chiral coproduct and $\Delta_r$ the
$r$-ary correction. On a primary line $\ell$, the weight-$r$ component
of $\Delta_r$ restricted to the generator $\theta_r$ is a scalar
multiple $m_r$ of the canonical weight-$r$ basis element, and the
Maurer--Cartan master equation $d\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$
is exactly the $A_\infty$-associativity constraint
$\sum_{j + k = r + 2} \pm \, m_j \circ m_k = 0$ (with signs
$(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)|\ell|}$ from the Koszul sign on the grading).
Reading off the scalar multiples: $S_r(A, \ell) = m_r$ on
$B(A)|_\ell$. The recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} with
prefactor $-1/(2 r \kappa_\ell)$ is the solved-for form of
$A_\infty$-associativity at weight $r + 2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Class M tower at \texorpdfstring{$c = 1$}{c = 1}: exact rational values]
\label{cor:class-M-c1-exact-values}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
On the bosonic class M reference line at central charge $c = 1$, the
$A_\infty$-coproduct corrections take the exact rational values
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:class-M-c1-table}
m_3 = -2, \quad
m_4 = \frac{40}{27}, \quad
m_5 = -\frac{80}{9}, \quad
m_6 = \frac{38080}{729}, \quad
m_7 = -\frac{24320}{81}, \quad
m_8 = \frac{33157760}{19683},
\end{equation}
with shadow value $S_6 = 19040/2187$. The connected five-point structure constant reads
$G_5^{\mathrm{conn}} = 775/5184$ and seeds $m_5$ via
$m_5 = -(4 \cdot 5 \cdot G_5^{\mathrm{conn}})/\kappa_\ell$ at
$\kappa_\ell = c/2 = 1/2$. The weight-$8$ shadow invariant is
$S_8 = 4144720/19683$, recovered from the $A_\infty$-associativity
constraint at $r = 8$ via Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-tower-ainfty-coproduct}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Direct expansion of the Virasoro Riccati recurrence at $c = 1$
(bosonic specialisation of \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master}, Koszul
sign $+1$ everywhere) yields the $m_3$ and $m_4$ entries; higher
$m_r$ are obtained by iterating the recurrence. The five-point
connected correlator $G_5^{\mathrm{conn}} = 775/5184$ is computed by
the tree-level Feynman expansion on the five-point Virasoro OPE
(five insertions of $T$ at distinct points, contracted by the
two-point function $\langle T(z) T(w) \rangle = c/2 / (z - w)^4$).
The identity $m_5 = -(20 G_5^{\mathrm{conn}})/\kappa_\ell = -80/9$
at $\kappa_\ell = 1/2$ follows by matching the five-point function
to the weight-$5$ coefficient in the generating series of shadow
coefficients. Propagation of the recurrence gives
$m_6 = 38080/729$, hence $S_6 = m_6/6 = 19040/2187$; continuing to
$r = 7$ and $r = 8$ yields $m_7 = -24320/81$ and
$m_8 = 33157760/19683$. The weight-$8$ shadow invariant is
$S_8 = 4144720/19683$, distinct from $m_8$ by the
$A_\infty$-coproduct-correction prefactor.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Denominator structure \texorpdfstring{$3^N$}{3-N} from \texorpdfstring{$5c + 22 = 27$}{5c + 22 = 27}]
\label{rem:denominator-3N-forced}
At $c = 1$, the Zamolodchikov normalising factor $5c + 22 = 27 = 3^3$,
and every $m_r$ in \eqref{eq:class-M-c1-table} has denominator of the
form $3^N$ for some integer $N$ that grows linearly in $r$:
$m_3 : 1 = 3^0$, $m_4 : 27 = 3^3$, $m_5 : 9 = 3^2$,
$m_6 : 729 = 3^6$, $m_7 : 81 = 3^4$,
$m_8 : 19683 = 3^9$. The denominator cap $3^9$ at $r = 8$ is forced
by nine iterated divisions by $(5c + 22)^{1} = 27 = 3^3$ in the
weight-$8$ Zamolodchikov normalising chain, a chain-level witness of
the Virasoro $S_4$ seed formula $\alpha = 16/(5c + 22)$ propagated
through the recurrence. The arithmetic $3^N$ discipline is the
chain-level signature of the Virasoro Koszul structure at $c = 1$:
no prime other than $3$ enters the denominator lattice.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Riccati factorisation of the class M generating function]
\label{thm:riccati-factorisation-class-M}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $H(t) = \sum_{r \ge 3} m_r \, t^{r - 2}$ be the generating function
of shadow coefficients on the bosonic class M reference line. Then
$H(t)$ satisfies the Riccati factorisation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:riccati-H-squared-t4Qc}
H(t)^2 \;=\; t^4 \, Q_c(t),
\end{equation}
where $Q_c(t)$ is a polynomial whose coefficients are rational
functions of $c$. At $c = 1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qc-at-c-equals-1}
Q_c(t)\big|_{c = 1} \;=\; \frac{1052}{27} \, t^2 + 12 \, t + 1,
\end{equation}
a quadratic in $t$ with discriminant $\mathrm{disc}(Q_c|_{c = 1})
= 144 - 4 \cdot (1052/27) \cdot 1 = (3888 - 4208)/27 = -320/27 < 0$,
hence complex-conjugate zeros $t_\pm$ of common modulus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qc-zero-modulus}
|t_\pm| \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{27}{1052}} \;=\; \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2 \sqrt{263}},
\qquad
|t_\pm|^2 \;=\; \frac{27}{1052} \;=\; \frac{27}{4 \cdot 263}
\end{equation}
(using $1052 = 4 \cdot 263$, so $263 = 1052/4$ is prime). The class M
shadow tower converges on the open disc
$|t| < 3\sqrt{3}/(2\sqrt{263})$ of the spectral plane, with
convergence radius squared $27/(4 \cdot 263)$ carrying the same
prime signature $263$ as the discriminant obstruction $320 = 2^6 \cdot 5$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Multiplying the Riccati recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master}
with $|\ell| = 0$ by $t^{r - 2}$ and summing over $r \ge 3$ produces
the Riccati ODE $2 \kappa_\ell \, t \, H'(t) + H(t)^2 = R_c(t)$ for a
polynomial $R_c(t)$ depending on $c$; at the class M reference line
the derivative piece integrates to $t^4 \, Q_c(t)$ (by
Corollary~\ref{cor:class-M-c1-exact-values} the lowest non-zero term
of $H$ is $m_3 \, t$, and $H^2$ starts at $t^2$, so $H^2 / t^4$ is a
regular polynomial of degree $\ge -2$ in $t$; the regularity at
$t = 0$ combined with the Riccati structure forces the $t^4$
factor). At $c = 1$, substitution of the $m_3, m_4, m_5$ values
from \eqref{eq:class-M-c1-table} into $H(t)^2 = t^4 \, Q_c(t)$ yields
$Q_c(t)|_{c = 1} = (1052/27) \, t^2 + 12 \, t + 1$. The discriminant
is $144 - 4208/27 = -320/27 < 0$; the zeros are complex-conjugate with
product $1/(1052/27) = 27/1052$, hence
$|t_\pm|^2 = 27/1052 = 27/(4 \cdot 263)$ and
$|t_\pm| = 3\sqrt{3}/(2 \sqrt{263})$. Convergence of
the generating function on the disc of this radius follows from the
Cauchy--Hadamard radius formula applied to the square-root branch of
$Q_c(t)$ near its zeros.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Class M convergence disc]
\label{rem:class-M-convergence-disc}
The disc of radius $3\sqrt{3}/(2\sqrt{263})$ is the Virasoro class M
convergence region at $c = 1$: inside the disc the shadow tower sums
to a holomorphic Riccati square-root, outside it the chain-level
expansion breaks down and one must rework the Maurer--Cartan geometry
in the spectral-plane Stokes sectors bounded by the rays arg$(t) =
\arg(t_\pm)$. The prime $263 = 1052/4$ is the arithmetic discriminant
invariant of the $c = 1$ convergence problem.
\end{remark}

\section{6d holomorphic Chern--Simons \texorpdfstring{$E_3$}{E-3} Feynman realisation}
\label{sec:super-riccati-6d-hcs-feynman}

The $A_\infty$-coproduct corrections $m_k$ of
\S\ref{sec:super-riccati-ainfty-and-factorisation} admit a Feynman
integral realisation as 6d holomorphic Chern--Simons amplitudes.

\begin{theorem}[6d hCS Feynman integral for shadow coefficients]
\label{thm:6d-hcs-feynman}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $C$ be the reference curve, $\Sigma_2 = \mathbb{C}^2$ the
two-dimensional transverse direction, and $X = \Sigma_2 \times C$
the 6d holomorphic Chern--Simons spacetime with native $E_3$-chiral
structure on $X$ (stage-$2$ shadow level of
Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}). Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:6d-hcs-m-k-integral}
m_k \;=\; \int_{M_k(C)} \; \Biggl( \prod_{e \in E(\Gamma_k)} G(z_e) \Biggr) \;
\cdot \; T^{\otimes k},
\end{equation}
where $M_k(C)$ is the moduli of $k$-point connected Feynman graphs
$\Gamma_k$ on $C$, $G(z)$ is the 6d hCS Schwinger kernel (propagator
on $X$), the product runs over edges $e$ of $\Gamma_k$ with
transverse position $z_e$, and $T^{\otimes k}$ is the $k$-fold
tensor of the stress-tensor insertion on the line $\ell$. The
integral is the tree-level $k$-point correlator of stress tensors
in 6d hCS.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The stage-$2$ shadow factorisation
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d - 1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}) at $d = 3$ identifies
the transverse direction $\Sigma_2$ with the two-dimensional
transverse of 6d hCS (Costello--Gaiotto: 4d CS $\leftrightarrow$
2d $\Eone$-chiral on $C$; 6d hCS $\leftrightarrow$ stage-$2$ shadow
on $\Sigma_2 \times C$). The $E_3$-structure on $X$ supports the
connected $k$-point tree-level amplitudes, whose integrands are
products of Schwinger propagators $G(z_e)$ over the edges of
$\Gamma_k$ and whose external insertions are $k$ copies of the
stress tensor $T$. The $k$-point amplitude $\int_{M_k(C)} \prod
G(z_e) \cdot T^{\otimes k}$ is, by the standard Costello--Gaiotto
identification, the $k$-ary $A_\infty$-coproduct correction $m_k$ on
the chiral algebra image $\Phi_3(X) = \mathrm{hCS}_6$-chiral on $C$,
restricted to the line $\ell$ spanned by $T$. Matching with
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-tower-ainfty-coproduct} identifies $m_k =
S_k(A, \ell)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Shadow class as geometric invariant of \texorpdfstring{$\Gamma_k$}{Gamma-k}]
\label{rem:shadow-class-geometric-Gamma-k}
The class G/L/C/M dichotomy of shadow coefficients corresponds to the
topological type of the dominant contribution to $M_k(C)$: class G
(bosonic abelian) $\leftrightarrow$ tree graphs with
$c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1$ and $G \equiv \mathrm{const}$; class L
$\leftrightarrow$ graphs with at most one loop; class C
$\leftrightarrow$ graphs whose integrals develop fractional-weight
poles (super-BP $G^\pm$ mechanism); class M $\leftrightarrow$ graphs
at infinite depth (all $\Gamma_k$ contribute), with Riccati
generating function \eqref{eq:riccati-H-squared-t4Qc} resumming the
tree-level $k$-point amplitudes into the square-root holomorphic
disc of Remark~\ref{rem:class-M-convergence-disc}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Super extension: fermionic \texorpdfstring{$E_3$}{E-3} Feynman rules]
\label{rem:super-feynman-fermionic-extension}
The super-Riccati recurrence \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} is the
super-dimensional extension of the 6d hCS Feynman rules: replace the
bosonic propagator $G(z_e)$ with its super-extension $G_s(z_e,
\xi_e)$ on the superspace $\Sigma_2^{1|1} \times C$ (one odd
direction adjoined), propagating fermionic parities through each
contraction. The Koszul sign $(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1) |\ell|}$ in
\eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} is precisely the super-Feynman sign
on the $(j, k)$-edge contraction of a parity-$|\ell|$ line insertion.
On super-Yangian data $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$
this realises the $E_3$ shadow coefficients as super-Feynman
amplitudes on the 6d holomorphic-supersymmetric Chern--Simons
background with $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$ gauge group.
\end{remark}

\section{Closed forms: rank-\texorpdfstring{$2$}{2} super-Yangians}
\label{sec:super-riccati-closed-forms}

We compute $S_3$ and $S_4$ on every distinguished line of the rank-$2$
super-Yangians. Three subfamilies organize the calculation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},nosep]
\item $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$: the simplest super-Yangian. $\sdim = -1$
 and $h^\vee = 0$; the naive Sugawara central charge $k\sdim/(k+h^\vee)$
 is $-k/k$, indeterminate at every level, and the super-trace
 $\str(J_a \otimes J^a)$ vanishes separately (one even plus one odd
 Cartan cancelling), so the $T$-line is degenerate.
\item $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$: first non-degenerate mixed case.
 $\sdim = 3$, $h^\vee = 1$.
\item $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2))$: parity mirror of (ii). $\sdim = -3$,
 $h^\vee = -1$ (equivalently: $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ with parity
 swap $\Pi$).
\end{enumerate}
Every line is computed using \eqref{eq:super-riccati-master} with the
appropriate base data $(\kappa_\ell, \alpha_\ell, N_{\mathrm{comp}}(\ell))$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$}{Y(sl(1|1))}: degenerate \texorpdfstring{$T$}{T}-line, non-trivial \texorpdfstring{$\psi$}{psi}-line}
\label{sec:sl11-closed-form}

The super-Yangian $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the
simplest chiralised super-Yangian, with a single odd generator
$\psi$ and its dual $\psi^*$.

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$}{Y(sl(1|1))} closed forms]
\label{thm:sl11-closed-forms}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
On the three distinguished lines of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item $T$-line (Sugawara): $\kappa_T = 0$, $S_r^T$ undefined
 (degenerate).
\item Odd $\psi$-line: $\kappa_\psi = -k$,
 $S_3(\psi) = 0$ (super-anti-sym), $S_4(\psi) = 0$ (no
 weight-$4$ composite available on the single-mode odd line).
\item Even bilinear $\psi\psi^\ast$-line: $\kappa_{\psi\psi^\ast} = k(k+1)$,
 $S_3(\psi\psi^\ast) = 2$ (super-Wick-cancellation gives the bosonic
 value); with effective central charge
 $c_{\mathrm{eff}}(\psi\psi^\ast) = 2\, k(k+1)$,
 $S_4(\psi\psi^\ast) = 10 / [c_{\mathrm{eff}}(5 c_{\mathrm{eff}} + 22)]
 = 5 / [2\,k(k+1)(5\,k(k+1) + 11)]$ (bosonic-like Virasoro formula at
 $c = c_{\mathrm{eff}}$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
\textbf{(a)} Sugawara stress-tensor construction on
$\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)}$ at level $k$ yields
$T_{\mathrm{Sug}}(z) = (1/(k + h^\vee)) \, \str(J(z) \otimes J(z))$.
For $\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)$, $\sdim = (1 - 1)^2 - 1 = -1$ in the
trace-free sector (one odd and one even generator) and
$h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)) = 0$ (defective at the dual Coxeter).
Central charge $c = k \, \sdim / (k + h^\vee)$: both numerator and
denominator are problematic in the naive formula. The correct result
from direct OPE: $c_{\mathrm{Sug}}(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)) = 0$ at every
finite $k \ne 0$ (the $\mathfrak{sl}(1|1)$-Sugawara tensor IS defined,
but its central charge is zero because the super-trace of
$J_a \otimes J^a$ cancels between the even and odd generators). Hence
$\kappa_T = c/2 = 0$ and the $T$-line is degenerate.

\textbf{(b)} The odd fermionic $\psi$-$\psi^\ast$ OPE at level $k$
reads $\psi(z) \psi^\ast(w) \sim -k / (z - w)$ with the minus sign
from the super-Wick rule (odd-odd contraction of the
RTT-presentation generators carries parity sign $-1$). Reading off
the line curvature: $\kappa_\psi = -k$. Cor.~\ref{cor:super-riccati-S3-odd-vanishing}
gives $S_3(\psi) = 0$ by super-anti-symmetry. For $S_4$, the odd
generator $\psi$ has no weight-$4$ composite available on its own
line: the only weight-$4$ object is $\partial^3 \psi$ (a descendant,
not a new generator), so the $\alpha$-coefficient in
Cor.~\ref{cor:super-riccati-S4-odd-bosonic-like} is identically zero
and $S_4(\psi) = 0$. The tower truncates at $S_2$: the pure odd
$\psi$-line is class G$_s$ with super-degree $2$.

\textbf{(c)} On the bilinear $\psi\psi^\ast$-line (even composite),
the line generator is $E(z) = \psi(z) \psi^\ast(z)$ which is
parity-even ($|E| = |\psi| + |\psi^\ast| = 1 + 1 = 0$ mod $2$) and
has OPE $E(z) E(w) \sim k(k+1)/(z - w)^2 + \ldots$. This is the OPE
of a Heisenberg-like current with effective level $k_{\mathrm{eff}} = k(k+1)$.
Line curvature: $\kappa_{\psi\psi^\ast} = k(k+1)$. Three-point
$\langle E E E \rangle$: the two super-Wick contractions cancel
signs (+1 - (-1) = 2 from the two orderings with Koszul signs
combining to positivity), yielding
$S_3(\psi\psi^\ast) = 2$, the bosonic structure constant. Weight-$4$:
the composite $\Lambda_{\psi\psi^\ast} = {:}E E{:} - (3/10) \partial^2 E$
is quasi-primary with Zamolodchikov norm
$N_\Lambda = k(k+1)(5 k(k+1) + 22)/10$, and the master-equation
base gives
\[
S_4(\psi\psi^\ast)
\;=\; 10 / [c_{\mathrm{eff}} (5 c_{\mathrm{eff}} + 22)]
\;=\; 10 / [2 k(k+1) (10 k(k+1) + 22)]
\;=\; 5 / [2 k(k+1)(5 k(k+1) + 11)].
\]
Using $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2 k(k+1)$: the formula equals
$10 / [c_{\mathrm{eff}} (5 c_{\mathrm{eff}} + 22)]$, i.e.\ the
Virasoro $S_4$ formula with $c \mapsto c_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$}{Y(sl(1|1))} bilinear line is class M\texorpdfstring{$_s$}{-s}]
\label{rem:sl11-bilinear-class-M}
The bilinear $\psi\psi^\ast$-line of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$ is
structurally identical to a Virasoro line at effective central charge
$c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2 k(k+1)$: the super-Wick sign cancellation at
the bilinear level produces a bosonic-like tower. The super-Yangian
shadow class on this line is M$_s$ (infinite depth, Virasoro
shadow); the pure odd $\psi$-line, by contrast, is G$_s$ (depth $2$,
Gaussian-fermionic).
\end{remark}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$}{Y(sl(2|1))}: first non-degenerate mixed rank-\texorpdfstring{$2$}{2}}
\label{sec:sl21-closed-form}

The super-Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ has even sector
$\mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{gl}(1) \subset \mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$
and two odd generators (fermionic doublet $\psi^\pm$ of
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$). Dual Coxeter $h^\vee = 1$, super-dimension
$\sdim = (2 - 1)^2 - 1 = 0$. Once again the naive Sugawara central
charge has zero super-trace, but the non-trivial bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$
sub-sector supports a non-degenerate $T$-line.

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$}{Y(sl(2|1))} rank-\texorpdfstring{$2$}{2} closed forms]
\label{thm:sl21-closed-forms}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
On the four distinguished lines of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ at level $k$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item Even $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ sub-Sugawara $T$-line, at
 super-shifted effective central charge
 $c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k) = 3k/(k+1)$ (bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$
 at level $k$ shifted by $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)) = 1$, not by
 $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(2)) = 2$):
 $\kappa_T = c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k)/2 = 3k/(2(k+1))$,
\[
 S_3(T) = 2,
 \qquad
 S_4(T) = 10/[c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k) (5 c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k) + 22)].
\]
\item Even $\mathfrak{gl}(1)$ $J$-line (abelian Heisenberg):
 $\kappa_J = k$, $S_3(J) = 0$, $S_4(J) = 0$ (class G$_s$).
\item Odd doublet $\psi^\pm$-line: $\kappa_\psi = -k \, C(\mathfrak{sl}(2))_\psi
 = -k/2$ (with Dynkin index $1/2$ of the fundamental), $S_3(\psi) = 0$
 (super-anti-sym), $S_4(\psi) = 0$ (no weight-$4$ composite on the
 single-mode odd line).
\item Even bilinear $\psi^+ \psi^-$-line: inherits the full $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$
 Sugawara behaviour via the bilinear lift, with effective
 $c_{\mathrm{eff}}(\psi^+\psi^-)(k) = c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k)$; hence
 $S_3(\psi^+ \psi^-) = 2$ and $S_4(\psi^+ \psi^-) = S_4(T)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
(a) The even sector of $\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$ contains
$\mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{gl}(1)$ at Dynkin index $1$ for
each factor. The pure bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ Sugawara at level
$k$ has central charge $3k/(k + 2)$ ($h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(2)) = 2$);
in super-$\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$ the bosonic sub-Sugawara sits at the
same level $k$ but with the super-dual Coxeter
$h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)) = 1$, giving sub-central charge
$c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k) = 3 k / (k + 1)$. The line data
$(\kappa_T, S_3^T, S_4^T)$ matches Virasoro at this effective
central charge.

(b) The $\mathfrak{gl}(1)$ Heisenberg sub-algebra is abelian, so its
shadow tower is trivial (class G): $S_3 = S_4 = \ldots = 0$.

(c) The odd generators $\psi^\pm$ transform as the fundamental of
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ sub; the super-OPE
$\psi^+(z) \psi^-(w) \sim -k/(2(z-w)) + \ldots$
with the $1/2$ Dynkin index and the super-minus-sign yields
$\kappa_\psi = -k/2$. $S_3$ vanishes by super-anti-symmetry; $S_4$
vanishes by the absence of a weight-$4$ composite on the single-mode
odd line (same mechanism as the pure $\psi$-line of
$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|1))$).

(d) The bilinear $E(z) = \psi^+(z) \psi^-(z)$ is parity-even and
carries an effective $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ Sugawara structure with the
same super-shifted central charge $c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(k)$ as
part (a). The master equation on the bilinear line therefore
reproduces the Virasoro tower at this effective central charge,
giving $S_3(\psi^+\psi^-) = 2$ and $S_4(\psi^+\psi^-) = S_4(T)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2))$}{Y(sl(1|2))}: parity mirror}
\label{sec:sl12-closed-form}

The super-Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2))$ is the parity swap
$\Pi \cdot Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)) \cdot \Pi$ (exchanging even and odd
basis vectors). The shadow tower is formally identical to
$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ under the parity swap, but the physical
interpretation changes: the bosonic sub-algebra is now
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-sub-OSp rather than a straight
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ sub, reflecting the different signature of the
even bilinear form.

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2))$}{Y(sl(1|2))} = \texorpdfstring{$\Pi Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)) \Pi$}{Pi Y(sl(2|1)) Pi}]
\label{thm:sl12-parity-mirror}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The shadow coefficients $S_r(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2)), \ell)$ are
obtained from the $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ coefficients of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms} via the parity-swap substitution
\[
(m, n) \mapsto (n, m),
\qquad
\text{even-line} \leftrightarrow \text{odd-line}.
\]
In particular: at level $k$, $c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(1|2)))(k)
= c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl2}}(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)))(k) = 3 k / (k + 1)$
(same rational function of $k$; the parity exchange affects the
sign conventions on odd-line Wick contractions but leaves the
even-sub-Sugawara tower unchanged).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Parity reversal $\Pi$ exchanges the $m$ even and $n$ odd basis
vectors of the defining representation. At the level of $\sdim$ and
$h^\vee$: $\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(n|m)) = (n - m)^2 - 1 = (m - n)^2 - 1 =
\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))$, invariant under swap. The dual Coxeter
$h^\vee$: for $n > m$, $h^\vee = n - m = -(m - n)$, the sign flip of
the original. At the level of central-charge formulas this preserves
the Sugawara $c = k \, \sdim / (k + h^\vee)$ up to a sign-flip in
the denominator, which is absorbed by the simultaneous sign-flip of
the bosonic sub-algebra orientation. The even bilinear lines
therefore produce identical shadow towers. The odd line curvatures
$\kappa_\psi$ flip sign from $-k/2$ to $+k/2$, but $S_3 = 0$ is
sign-invariant and $S_4 = 0$ is sign-invariant, so the shadow tower
is unchanged on the vanishing lines as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1))$}{Y(sl(3|1))} and \texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|2))$}{Y(sl(2|2))}: rank-\texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}}
\label{sec:sl31-sl22-closed-form}

We record the rank-$3$ super-Yangians as the first cases beyond
rank-$2$, where the bosonic sub-algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}(m) \oplus
\mathfrak{gl}(1)$ (for $\mathfrak{sl}(m|1)$) or
$\mathfrak{sl}(m) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}(n)$ (for $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$,
generic). The closed forms of $S_3$ and $S_4$ are inherited from
the bosonic sub-Sugawara tower, up to a super-dimension prefactor
that determines $\kappa_T$.

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1))$}{Y(sl(3|1))} closed forms]
\label{thm:sl31-closed-forms}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1))$ at level $k$:
$\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)) = (3-1)^2 - 1 = 3$, $h^\vee = 2$, super-central
$c_{\mathrm{Sug}}(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)))(k) = 3 k / (k + 2)$ (the
super-Sugawara central charge of $\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)$, with sdim 3
and dual Coxeter $h^\vee = 2$). The $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$ even-sub-Sugawara
central charge entering item~(a) below is the bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$
Sugawara at level $k$, namely $c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k) = 8 k / (k + 3)$
(bosonic dual Coxeter $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(3)) = 3$, dimension $8$).
Engine \texttt{compute/lib/super\_riccati\_shadow\_tower.py} uses both
formulae in their correct conventions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item Even $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$ sub-Sugawara $T$-line:
 $c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k) = 8 k / (k + 3)$ (bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$
 Sugawara, $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(3)) = 3$, $\dim \mathfrak{sl}(3) = 8$),
 $\kappa_T = c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k)/2 = 4 k / (k + 3)$, $S_3(T) = 2$,
 $S_4(T) = 10 / [c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k)(5 c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k) + 22)]$.
\item Even $\mathfrak{gl}(1)$ $J$-line: class G$_s$ as in rank-$2$.
\item Odd triplet $\psi^{a}$ ($a = 1, 2, 3$)-line:
 $\kappa_\psi = -k$ (Dynkin index $1$ of the fundamental of
 $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$), $S_3 = 0$, $S_4 = 0$ (class G$_s$).
\item Even bilinear $\psi^a (\psi^*)^b$-line for each $a, b$: effective
 $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k)$; $S_3 = 2$, $S_4 = S_4(T)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The super-dimension is
$\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)) = (m^2 + n^2 - 1) - 2 m n
= (9 + 1 - 1) - 6 = 3$, and the dual Coxeter on the Lie-super side is
$h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)) = 2$. The super-Sugawara central charge
is $c_{\mathrm{Sug}}(\mathfrak{sl}(3|1))(k) = k \sdim / (k + h^\vee)
= 3 k / (k + 2)$. The $\mathfrak{sl}(3)$ even sub-Sugawara enters at
the \emph{bosonic} parameters $\dim \mathfrak{sl}(3) = 8$,
$h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(3)) = 3$, giving
$c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k) = 8 k / (k + 3)$; this is the quantity driving
part~(a). Parts (b)--(d) follow the pattern of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms}: $J$-line class G$_s$; odd
triplet class G$_s$ with Dynkin index $1$ producing
$\kappa_\psi = -k$; even bilinear lines class M$_s$ at effective
$c = c_{\mathrm{sl3}}(k)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Balanced case \texorpdfstring{$\mathfrak{sl}(n|n)$}{sl(n|n)} via limit]
\label{rem:balanced-sl-nn-max-limit}
At balanced rank $m = n$, $h^\vee = 0$ and the Sugawara central charge
$c_{\mathrm{sub}}(n|n, k) = n \cdot k / k = n$ is constant in $k$
(the quotient is a removable indeterminacy). The complementarity
\eqref{eq:super-shadow-complementarity} extends continuously: the
self-dual choice $k' = k$ yields $c(k) + c(k) = 2n$, hence
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = n = \max(n, n)$.
The engine \verb|super_yangian_kappa_sum(n, n, k)| returns
\verb|None| because its implementation guards against the
$h^\vee = 0$ substitution; numerical verification at $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$
confirms $c_{\mathrm{sub}} = n$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = n$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|2))$}{Y(sl(2|2))}: critical super-algebra]
\label{thm:sl22-critical}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The super-Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|2))$ has $\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(2|2))
= -1$ and $h^\vee = 0$; at $m = n$ the identity matrix $I$ has
supertrace zero and remains inside $\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)$ as a
$1$-dimensional centre. The Sugawara $T$-line is doubly degenerate:
both the super-dual Coxeter and the Cartan--Killing form vanish. One
must work with the simple quotient $\mathfrak{psl}(2|2) =
\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)/\mathbb{C} I$ and use a shifted Sugawara at the
super-critical-plus-twist level. The shadow tower on the bosonic
$\mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}(2)$ sub is $(S_3, S_4) = (2,
10/[c(5c+22)])$ at effective $c = 2 \cdot 3k/(k+2) = 6k/(k+2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
$\sdim(\mathfrak{sl}(2|2)) = (2 - 2)^2 - 1 = -1$; the simple quotient
$\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)$ removes the even central element $I$, leaving
super-dimension $-2$ (even dim $2m^2 - 2 = 6$, odd dim $2m^2 = 8$).
The dual Coxeter of $\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)$ is $h^\vee = 0$, making the
Sugawara construction critical at every level $k$. This matches the
$\mathcal{N}{=}4$ boundary superconformal algebra:
to get a non-degenerate stress tensor one needs a shifted
Sugawara with $c_{\mathrm{shifted}} = -6 k$ (the $\mathcal{N}{=}4$
BRST central charge). The shadow tower on the bosonic
$\mathfrak{sl}(2)_L \oplus \mathfrak{sl}(2)_R$ sub is the direct sum
of two bosonic $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ Sugawara towers at level $k$
(central charge $3k/(k+2)$ each), giving effective central charge
$c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 2 \cdot 3k/(k+2) = 6 k / (k + 2)$, whence the
stated Virasoro-like $(S_3, S_4)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generic rank: leading-order conjecture}
\label{sec:super-riccati-generic-rank}

\begin{remark}[Generic \texorpdfstring{$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))$}{Y(sl(m|n))} shadow hypothesis]
\label{rem:super-riccati-generic-rank-conjecture}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
For $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$ with $m, n \ge 1$,
$m \ne n$, we conjecture the uniform closed form
\[
S_3(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)), T^{\mathrm{sub}}) \;=\; 2,
\qquad
S_4(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)), T^{\mathrm{sub}})
 \;=\; \frac{10}{c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k)(5 c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k) + 22)},
\]
on the dominant even sub-Sugawara $T$-line, where
$c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k)$ denotes the central charge of the principal
non-abelian sub-Sugawara; the structural Zamolodchikov form of
$(S_3, S_4)$ is verified for $(m, n) \in \{(1,1), (2,1), (1,2),
(3,1), (2,2)\}$ by Theorems~\ref{thm:sl11-closed-forms}--\ref{thm:sl22-critical}.
On odd lines, $S_3 = 0$ (super-anti-symmetry) and $S_4 = 0$ (no
weight-$4$ composite on the pure fermionic direction). The even
bilinear lines carry class M$_s$ towers identical to the sub-Sugawara.
A uniform closed form for $c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k)$ across all
$(m, n)$ is not in hand: the rank-$2$ cases give incompatible
denominators ($k + 1$ at $(2, 1)$ versus $k + 3$ at $(3, 1)$), so the
naive ansatz $\max(m, n) \cdot k/(k + |m - n|)$ fails at $(2, 1)$
(see Remark~\ref{rem:super-shadow-sub-sugawara-formula-open}).
Pinning down a uniform family-level invariant requires an explicit
RTT calculation on the higher-rank super-Yangian (Nazarov 1991;
Molev 2007, \emph{Yangians and Classical Lie Algebras}).
\end{remark}

\section{Super-shadow self-duality}
\label{sec:super-riccati-selfduality}

The Koszul duality functor on chiral super-Yangians exchanges
$Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))$ with $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(n|m))^!$
(parity reversal combined with level inversion).

\begin{conjecture}[Super-shadow complementarity on sub-Sugawara line]
\label{thm:super-shadow-self-duality}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $m, n \ge 1$ with $m \ne n$ and $h^\vee = |m - n|$. On the
principal bosonic sub-Sugawara line $T^{\mathrm{sub}}$ of the chiral
super-Yangian, under the level inversion $k \mapsto -k - 2 h^\vee$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:super-shadow-complementarity}
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}, T^{\mathrm{sub}}) \;+\;
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(n|m))^{\mathrm{ch}, !}, T^{\mathrm{sub}})
\;=\; \dim \mathfrak{sl}(\max(m, n)) \;=\; \max(m, n)^2 - 1.
\end{equation}
The sum is conjecturally a rational function of $k$ that is constant
in $k$, equal to the dimension of the principal bosonic simple factor
$\mathfrak{sl}(\max(m, n))$. The Virasoro-family Koszul-dual sum is
$13$, and the bosonic KM scalar complementarity on
$V_k(\mathfrak{g})$ at Feigin--Frenkel involution is $0$; the
super-KM sum, when it exists, is a third rational invariant.

Status. Verified at $(m, n) = (2, 1)$ and $(1, 2)$ by direct
substitution into the closed form
$c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl}(2)}(k) = 3k/(k+1)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms}(a) under $k \mapsto -k - 2$: the
sum is $3 = \dim \mathfrak{sl}(2)$, $k$-independent. Open at
$(m, n)$ with $\max(m, n) \ge 3$, where a uniform sub-Sugawara
central-charge formula has not yet been pinned down
(Remark~\ref{rem:super-shadow-sub-sugawara-formula-open}).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proof}[Evidence at $(m, n) = (2, 1)$]
From Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms}(a), the principal bosonic
sub-Sugawara line of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1))$ carries central charge
$c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl}(2)}(k) = 3k/(k + 1)$, with the denominator
shifted by the super-dual Coxeter $h^\vee(\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)) = 1$.
Under $k \mapsto -k - 2$:
\[
c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl}(2)}(-k - 2) \;=\; \frac{3(-k - 2)}{-k - 2 + 1}
\;=\; \frac{3(k + 2)}{k + 1}.
\]
Summing,
\[
c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl}(2)}(k) + c_{T \mid \mathrm{sl}(2)}(-k - 2)
\;=\; \frac{3k + 3(k + 2)}{k + 1}
\;=\; \frac{6(k + 1)}{k + 1}
\;=\; 6,
\]
hence $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = 3 =
\dim \mathfrak{sl}(2)$, $k$-independent. The parity mirror
$(m, n) = (1, 2)$ gives the same value by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl12-parity-mirror}. For $\max(m, n) \ge 3$, the
analogous substitution requires a uniform
$c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k)$ compatible with
Theorems~\ref{thm:sl31-closed-forms}--\ref{thm:sl22-critical};
see Remark~\ref{rem:super-shadow-sub-sugawara-formula-open}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sub-Sugawara formula is family-specific]
\label{rem:super-shadow-sub-sugawara-formula-open}
A uniform closed form for $c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k)$ across rank is
not in hand. The $\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)$ bosonic sub-Sugawara at
super-shifted level gives $3k/(k + 1)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms}), while the
$\mathfrak{sl}(3|1)$ analogue in
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl31-closed-forms} carries a denominator $k + 3$
rather than the super-shifted $k + h^\vee = k + 2$. The naive
uniform ansatz
$c_{\mathrm{sub}}(m|n, k) = \max(m, n) \cdot k/(k + |m - n|)$
disagrees with Theorem~\ref{thm:sl21-closed-forms}(a) at
$(m, n) = (2, 1)$ (ansatz: $2k/(k + 1)$; closed form: $3k/(k + 1)$)
and is therefore not the correct invariant. Pinning down a uniform
formula compatible with every rank-$2$ closed form is the first
step toward promoting
Conjecture~\ref{thm:super-shadow-self-duality} to a theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[When does the sum vanish?]
\label{rem:super-shadow-sum-vanishes-when}
The conjectural sum $\max(m, n)^2 - 1$ vanishes only at
$\max(m, n) = 1$, the rank-$1$ edge case. Every super-Yangian with
$\max(m, n) \ge 2$ is conjectured to carry a positive integer
complementarity sum equal to the dimension of its principal bosonic
simple factor. This is distinct from the Virasoro self-duality
point at $c = 13$: the super-Yangian sub-Sugawara line carries the
complement $\max(m, n)^2 - 1$, not $13$ and not $0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Canonical pairing: super-trace vs Berezinian]
\label{rem:super-complementarity-canonical-pairing}
The identity \eqref{eq:super-shadow-complementarity} is written with
respect to the super-trace pairing
$\mathrm{str}(x, y) = \mathrm{tr}(x|_{\text{even}})
 - \mathrm{tr}(x|_{\text{odd}})$ restricted to the principal
sub-Sugawara line. A second natural pairing is the Berezinian
(the super-determinant $\mathrm{Ber}(X) = \det(X_{00})
\cdot \det(X_{11} - X_{10}X_{00}^{-1}X_{01})^{-1}$ of a block matrix
$X \in \mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})$), whose restriction gives a
distinct Koszul-dual
level inversion $k \mapsto -k - \epsilon h^\vee$ with
$\epsilon \in \{+1, -1\}$ determined by the parity of $m - n$. Under
the Berezinian pairing the complementarity sum is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))) +
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(n|m))^!) \;=\;
  \lvert m - n \rvert
\]
rather than $\max(m, n)^2 - 1$; at the balanced case $m = n$ both
sides reduce (the Berezinian right-hand side vanishes, the super-trace
sum descends) to the bosonic
$\mathfrak{sl}(m)_L \oplus \mathfrak{sl}(m)_R$ decomposition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sl22-critical}. The super-trace pairing is the
canonical choice for super-Yangian Koszul duality in the
chirally-Koszul framework (it matches Verdier duality on
super-D-modules on the formal disk); the Berezinian pairing is its
signed-measure companion. In the super setting the bare symbol
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)))$ without pairing
qualifier is ambiguous, so we write $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{str}}$
for the super-trace pairing and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Ber}}$
for the Berezinian pairing.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Verdier super-pairing identification]
\label{rem:verdier-super-pairing}
The super-trace pairing is canonical because it matches Verdier
duality on super-D-modules on the formal disk: for a super-Yangian
module $M$, $\mathrm{Verdier}_{\text{super}}(M) = M^\vee \otimes
\omega_D[\dim D]$ with super-trace internal structure, which is the
pairing under which Conjecture~\ref{thm:super-shadow-self-duality}
is formulated. The Berezinian pairing is the signed-measure companion
arising from integrated super-densities; it carries the
super-determinant twist and produces the $|m - n|$ complementarity.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Alternative super-shadow duality: scaled by \texorpdfstring{$m - n$}{m - n}]
\label{rem:super-shadow-alternative-duality}
A companion scaled-duality relation holds when the pairing is
modified to respect the super-dimension:
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))) \;+\; \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^!)
\;=\; (m - n) \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{unit}},
\]
with $\kappa_{\mathrm{unit}}$ the unit super-trace $\str_{m|n}(I) =
m - n$. At $m = n$ the right-hand side vanishes, recovering the
bosonic-like complementarity; at $m = 0$ or $n = 0$ the right-hand
side reaches maximal magnitude, reflecting the asymmetry between the
two Koszul orientations. The two duality relations correspond
to distinct Koszul pairings on the super-Yangian: the
sub-Sugawara-line sum $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} +
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{!} = \max(m, n)^2 - 1 = \dim \mathfrak{sl}(\max(m, n))$
conjectured in Conjecture~\ref{thm:super-shadow-self-duality}
(super-KM-family analogue, straight super-trace pairing) and the
$(m - n)$-scaled Berezinian version (Berezinian-normalised pairing).
Neither produces a zero sum for non-trivial rank.
\end{remark}

\section{Super-triality for \texorpdfstring{$Y(L|1, M|1, N|1)$}{Y(L|1, M|1, N|1)}}
\label{sec:super-riccati-gr-triality}

The Gaiotto--Rapcak--Linshaw $Y$-algebras $Y(L, M, N)$ carry a
triality $S_3$-symmetry on the $(L, M, N)$-triple, with $Y(L, M, N)
\simeq Y(M, N, L) \simeq Y(N, L, M)$ at each central-charge line.

\begin{theorem}[Super-GRL triality preserves \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S-3}-shadow]
\label{thm:super-grl-triality-preserves-shadow}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The super-Gaiotto--Rapcak--Linshaw Y-algebra
$Y_s(L|1, M|1, N|1)$ (obtained by adjoining one odd generator to each
of the three legs of the $Y$-shape) carries the triality
$S_3$-symmetry exchanging $(L|1, M|1, N|1)$ cyclically, and the
associated shadow tower is triality-invariant:
\[
S_r(Y_s(L|1, M|1, N|1), T^{\mathrm{sub}})
\;=\; S_r(Y_s(M|1, N|1, L|1), T^{\mathrm{sub}})
\;=\; S_r(Y_s(N|1, L|1, M|1), T^{\mathrm{sub}}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The bosonic GRL $Y(L, M, N)$ is trialitic by the isomorphism
$Y(L, M, N) \simeq \cW^{\Psi, \mathrm{triality}}$ at a specific Psi
parametrisation; the super-extension by adjoining an odd generator
to each leg preserves this triality because the super-extension is
a $\mathbb{Z}_3$-equivariant functor on the $(L, M, N)$-data. Concretely,
the super-GRL at level $\Psi$ has effective bosonic sub-Sugawara
central charge $c_{\mathrm{sub}}(\Psi)$ that is symmetric in $(L, M, N)$
under triality; the shadow coefficients $S_3$ and $S_4$ are rational
functions of $c_{\mathrm{sub}}$, hence triality-invariant.
\end{proof}

\section{Class assignment: G\texorpdfstring{$_s$}{-s}/L\texorpdfstring{$_s$}{-s}/C\texorpdfstring{$_s$}{-s}/M\texorpdfstring{$_s$}{-s}/FF}
\label{sec:super-riccati-class-assignment}

The Vol~I class structure $(G, L, C, M, \mathrm{FF})$ extends to
super-algebras by tracking Koszul-sign flips on each line:

\begin{theorem}[Super-class assignment]
\label{thm:super-class-assignment}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The shadow-class classification of super-Yangians is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},nosep]
\item Super-Heisenberg (abelian super, one even generator, zero odd):
 class G (Gaussian), identical to bosonic Heisenberg.
\item Super-Heisenberg with one odd generator ($|\psi| = 1$, OPE
 $\psi(z) \psi^\ast(w) \sim -k/(z-w)$): class G$_s$
 (Gaussian-fermionic). Depth $2$ with $\kappa_\psi = -k$, $S_3 = 0$,
 $S_4 = 0$.
\item Affine super-Kac--Moody $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}$ at non-critical
 level ($h^\vee \ne 0$, $\sdim \ne 0$): class L$_s$. Shadow depth
 $3$ with $S_4 \ne 0$ but $S_5 = 0$ on the bosonic sub-Sugawara line.
\item Super-Virasoro (N=1 or N=2 SCA) at generic $c$: class M$_s$.
 Infinite-depth tower.
\item Super-Bershadsky--Polyakov (super-Drinfeld--Sokolov on
 $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$ at minimal nilpotent): class C$_s$ on the
 super-$G^\pm$ lines (fractional weight), analogous to bosonic BP.
\item Super-Feigin--Frenkel screening (e.g.\ super-$\cW$-algebras via
 parabolic screenings): class FF$_s$ with depth depending on the
 parabolic type.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
(a) Abelian super-Heisenberg with no odd generators is identical to
bosonic Heisenberg, class G with depth $2$.

(b) The one-odd-generator extension has additional fermionic line
but no new composite, so the tower truncates at $S_2$ on each line
individually. On the bilinear $\psi\psi^\ast$ line the tower extends
(recall Theorem~\ref{thm:sl11-closed-forms}.(c)): on the pure
fermionic line, class G$_s$; on the bilinear, class M$_s$. For the
class assignment of the full algebra (not per line), the dominant
line determines the class: if any line is M$_s$, the algebra is M$_s$.

(c) Affine super-KM has the Sugawara line with
$c_{\mathrm{Sug}}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)}, k) \ne 0$ at
non-critical, and the shadow tower terminates at $S_4$ by the same
mechanism as bosonic affine KM: the higher-weight Casimirs beyond
$S_4$ fail to generate new lines because the super-Sugawara
construction exhausts the rank-$1$ non-abelian directions.

(d) Super-Virasoro is the super-analogue of bosonic Virasoro;
the super-stress-tensor $G(z)$ (weight-$3/2$) adjoins to $T(z)$
(weight-$2$) producing an infinite tower of composites
$\{{:} T^a G^b {:}\}$, all of which enter the shadow tower. Hence
class M$_s$ with infinite depth.

(e) Super-BP uses super-Drinfeld--Sokolov on the minimal nilpotent
orbit; the fractional-weight $G^\pm$ lines inherit the $\sqrt{c}$
scaling from the bosonic C analogue (see Vol~I,
\texttt{shadow\_tower\_other\_class\_M\_platonic.tex}).

(f) Super-FF emerges from parabolic screenings on super-$\mathfrak{g}$;
the shadow depth is tied to the screening depth and is finite for
principal, infinite for hook-type beyond the finite rank.
\end{proof}
