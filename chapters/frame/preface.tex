\noindent
A chiral algebra is a solution. What is the problem it solves?

Volumes~I and~II develop the bar-cobar machine that extracts
modular invariants from any chiral algebra~$A$ on a curve~$X$:
the bar complex $B(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$ on the desuspended
augmentation ideal, with its deconcatenation coproduct; the
universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_A$; the modular
characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$; the five theorems
that control the genus tower; and the universal Koszul
conductor $K(\cA) = -c_{\mathrm{ghost}}(\BRST(\cA))$.
Volume~I constructs $E_1$-chiral quantum groups abstractly:
ordered bar complexes with spectral $R$-matrices, Drinfeld
coproducts, and Yang--Baxter equations, living on
configuration spaces of curves. Neither volume exhibits a
chiral algebra or a chiral quantum group; the input is
assumed.

\emph{This volume constructs the input.} The construction is
a $d$-indexed CY-to-chiral correspondence programme
$\{\Phi_d\}_{d \ge 1}$
\[
 \boxed{\;\Phi_d \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(\cM_d),
 \qquad
 n = n(d) = \begin{cases}\infty, & d = 1, \\ 2, & d = 2, \\
 1, & d \ge 3,\end{cases}\;}
\]
with target category varying with $d$; each $\Phi_d$ is
characterised by four universal properties:
\begin{description}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=2.0em,style=nextline]
\item[(U1) Hochschild pullback.]
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Phi(\cC)) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
as factorisation coalgebras: the ordered bar complex on the
output equals the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
of the input dg/$\Ainf$-category.
\item[(U2) CY-morphism functoriality.]
A wall-crossing in $\cC$ (mutation, Bridgeland traversal,
flop) maps to an $R$-matrix gauge transformation in
$\Phi(\cC)$.
\item[(U3) Drinfeld-centre compatibility.]
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC))) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\Phi(\cC)))$ at $d \ge 3$ (BZFN,
Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}); the $\Etwo$-braiding
emerges on the centre, not the algebra.
\item[(U4) Standard-input recovery.]
$\Phi(\Coh(E))$ is the lattice VOA of $\Lambda = H^*(E, \Z)$
at $d = 1$; $\Phi(D^b(K3))$ is the Mukai Heisenberg
$H_{\mathrm{Muk}} = \mathrm{Heis}(H^*(K3, \C), \omega_{\mathrm{Muk}})$
of rank~$24$ at $d = 2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-k3-explicit});
$\Phi(\CoHA(\C^3))$ is $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$,
the positive half of the affine Yangian
(Schiffmann--Vasserot), at $d = 3$.
\end{description}

The four-step recipe (cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal $\to$
factorisation envelope $\to$ $\mathbb{S}^d$-enhanced
quantisation) is the \emph{proof} of existence and uniqueness;
the four universal properties are the \emph{statement}.
\textbf{At $d \ge 3$, $\Phi(\cC)$ is $\Eone$-chiral; the
$\Etwo$-braiding lives on $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC)))$
only}, never on the chiral algebra itself
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \ge 3$ kills any

Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian}. Its governing object is the K3

chapter records the integrable self-mirror face of that

\noindent\textbf{Three architectural theorems} anchor the construction.
\textit{Universal extension}: for
$\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^*$-generic $X$,
$M_{X \times E^k} = M_X$ for all $k \ge 0$.
\textit{$V_4$ four-phenotype classification}: every CY direction $Y$ belongs to
$\{\mathrm{P}_1, \mathrm{P}_2, \mathrm{P}_3, \mathrm{P}_4\}$ by Klein-four
Fourier support, closed under Künneth fusion
($\mathrm{P}_4$ absorbing, $\mathrm{P}_1$ identity).
\textit{CY-D tri-stratum}: the three strata above exhaust the
compact CY classification of Beauville--Bogomolov; they refine
the stratified characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC_X))$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}. Two
conventions coexist and must be distinguished: the
Hodge-supertrace
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(X) := \sum_q (-1)^q\,
h^{0,q}(X)$, which equals $\chi(\cO_X)$ and vanishes for every
odd-$d$ strict CY by Serre pairing; and the Künneth-additive
complex dimension $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X) :=
\dim_\C X$, which satisfies
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X \times Y) =
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X) +
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(Y)$. The two coincide on
even-$d$ strict CY with $h^{0,0} = h^{0,d} = 1$ and intermediate
Hodge numbers contributing with alternating signs summing to
$d$ (e.g., K3 at $d = 2$: $1 - 0 + 1 = 2$); they diverge at
$d = 1$ (elliptic curve: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}
= 0$ vs $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}} = 1$) and at odd-$d$
strict CY (e.g., $K3 \times E$ at $d = 3$:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}} = 0$ vs
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}} = 3$). Throughout Vol~III the
operational convention is Künneth-additive
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}$;
the Hodge-supertrace identity appears as a theorem at even-$d$
strict CY only.

$\Phi_d$ crosses a variance boundary: the source category $\cC$
lives over a point, the target chiral algebra $\cA_\cC$ lives on
the curve~$X$, and the Hochschild theories on the two sides are
distinct invariants of distinct objects. A dg or $\Ainf$-category
$\cC$ carries three Hochschild-type complexes, each with its own
variance and structural role:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.3ex]
 \item The \emph{Hochschild cochain complex}
 $C^\bullet(\cC,\cC) = \RHom_{\cC^e}(\cC,\cC)$, with cohomology
 $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$. It carries an $E_2$-algebra structure by the
 Deligne conjecture (Kontsevich--Tamarkin; McClure--Smith), and on
 a $d$-Calabi--Yau category it upgrades to a BV algebra of degree
 $1-d$ (Ginzburg) whose $(1-d)$-shifted Gerstenhaber bracket
 controls deformations of~$\cC$.
 \item The \emph{Hochschild chain complex}
 $C_\bullet(\cC,\cC) = \cC \otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\cC^e} \cC$, with
 homology $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$, and its mixed-complex extension
 $(C_\bullet, b, B)$ carrying Connes' $B$-operator.
 \item The \emph{negative cyclic complex}
 $\mathrm{CC}^-_\bullet(\cC) = (C_\bullet(\cC,\cC)[[u]],\, b + uB)$,
 computing $\HC^-_\bullet(\cC)$. The $d$-Calabi--Yau condition
 on~$\cC$ is a non-degenerate trace
 $\Tr\colon \HC^-_d(\cC) \to k$ (Kontsevich--Soibelman; Kaledin);
 on the derived moduli $\cM_\cC$ of objects of~$\cC$, Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi
 correspondingly produce a $(2-d)$-shifted symplectic structure
 (the classical symplectic structure on K3 moduli at $d=2$, the
 $(-1)$-shifted Joyce--Song $d$-critical locus at $d=3$).
\end{enumerate}
All three are invariants of~$\cC$, independent of the curve~$X$.

On the chiral side, Vol~I's Theorem~H constructs the
\emph{chiral Hochschild cochain complex} $\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA)$
of a chiral algebra~$\cA$ on~$X$: its differential uses the
$\cD_X$-module structure of~$\cA$ and the ordered-bar residue on
Fulton--MacPherson configuration spaces of~$X$, and its cohomology
is concentrated in degrees~$\{0,1,2\}$ on the chirally Koszul
locus. This is a curve-dependent invariant with no analogue
over a point, and $\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA)$ reduces neither to the
algebraic Hochschild cochains $C^\bullet(A,A)$ of a dg algebra nor
to the topological Hochschild spectrum $\mathrm{THH}(R)$ of an
$E_1$-ring spectrum: the three invariants live on three distinct sources.

$\Phi_d$ is a per-$d$ correspondence (not a single functor in the
standard category-theoretic sense; see
Remark~\ref{rem:phi-not-unified-functor}) from $d$-Calabi--Yau
categories to chiral algebras on~$X$ — not a chain map on
Hochschild complexes: its input is the pair
$(\HH^\bullet(\cC),\,\HC^-_d(\cC))$ with the CY trace, its output
is the chiral algebra $\cA_\cC$, and morphism-level functoriality
holds up to $R$-matrix gauge equivalence (U2). The chiral
Hochschild complex $\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA_\cC)$ is therefore a
secondary invariant of the output, computed after $\Phi_d$ has
already produced~$\cA_\cC$; any relation between the source
Hochschild theory on~$\cC$ and the target chiral Hochschild theory
on~$\cA_\cC$ is a named comparison, not an identification.
At $d = 2$, for $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$ with $X$ K3 or abelian
surface, Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg gives
$\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \bigoplus_{p} H^{\bullet - p}(X, \Lambda^p T_X)$,
a finite-dimensional complex spanning degrees $0,\dots, 2d = 4$;
CY Frobenius duality $\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \HH^{d-\bullet}(\cC)^\vee$
shifts this to degrees $-d,\dots, d = -2,\dots, 2$, and the
non-negative slice aligns with the $\{0,1,2\}$-concentration of
$\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA_\cC)$ from Vol~I Theorem~H on the chirally
Koszul locus. Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-a2-hochschild-comparison}
names the chain-level comparison; Theorem~CY-A$_2$ packages the
consequence (an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra with braided monoidal
representation category). At $d \ge 3$ the source
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ spans degrees $0,\dots, 2d$ by HKR while the
target $\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA_\cC)$ stays concentrated in
$\{0,1,2\}$ by Theorem~H; the induced comparison is at most a
chain map to the truncation
$\tau_{\le 2}\ChirHoch^\bullet(\cA_\cC)$, and CY-A$_3$ carries
the residual degree slots of $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ through the
Drinfeld-centre passage
(Remark~\ref{rem:cy-cat-drinfeld-center-not-averaging}).

A CY$_d$ category carries a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: the
non-degenerate trace $\Tr\colon \HC^-_d(\cC) \to k$ on negative
cyclic homology encodes the CY condition as a chain-level
Frobenius structure of dimension~$d$. Anti-symmetrising the
Hochschild bracket via cyclic symmetry gives a Lie conformal
algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ on~$X$; the factorization envelope
$U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$ over the Ran space
$\Ran(X)$ produces a factorization algebra; the $\Sd$-framing
upgrades factorization to chiral; quantization of the Hochschild
Poisson structure completes the algebra $\cA_\cC$.
The Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has degree $1 - d$, fixing the native
$\En$ level: $d = 1$ gives an abelian bracket and an
$\Einf$-chiral algebra (commutative VOA); $d = 2$ gives the
$\mathbb{S}^2$-action of Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos, an
$\Etwo$-chiral algebra with braided monoidal representation
category (Theorem~CY-A$_2$); $d \ge 3$ gives $\Eone$-chiral
(ordered) output, with the residual $\Etwo$-braiding living on
the Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC))$, not on
$\cA_\cC$ itself. The chain-level
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction is resolved by the
$\infty$-categorical vanishing
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ for smooth proper CY$_3$ categories
and Goodwillie contractibility of the space of $E_3$-liftings
(Theorem~CY-A$_3$).

\noindent\textbf{The Mukai Lagrangian.}
For a Calabi--Yau variety~$X$, the Mukai lattice
\[
 \Lambda_X \;\subset\; H^{\mathrm{even}}(X, \Q)\,,
 \qquad
 \langle v_1, v_2 \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}
 \;:=\;
 -\!\int_X v_1^\vee \cdot v_2 \cdot \mathrm{td}(X)\,,
\]
carries two structures simultaneously, and the interplay
between them is the inner music of this volume.

On the \emph{arithmetic} side, $\Lambda_X$ is the target of
the Borcherds singular theta lift
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}). For a lattice
$\Lambda$ of signature $(2, n+2)$ and a weakly holomorphic
modular form $F = \sum_m c(m)\,q^m$ of weight $(2 - n)/2$ with
integral Fourier coefficients, the lift $\Psi(F)$ is an
$\Aut(\Lambda)$-invariant meromorphic modular form on the
Grassmannian of positive-definite planes in $\Lambda$, with
product expansion
\[
 \Psi(F)(Z) \;=\;
 e^{2\pi i \langle \rho, Z \rangle}
 \prod_{\lambda > 0}
 \bigl(1 - e^{2\pi i \langle \lambda, Z \rangle}\bigr)^{c(-\langle \lambda, \lambda \rangle / 2)}
\]
and weight $\mathrm{wt}(\Psi(F)) = c(0)/2$, with Hecke
operators intertwining source and target. Two worked
instances pin the theory to its arithmetic skeleton:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.3ex]
 \item \emph{Fake monster} (Borcherds 1992, 1998). On
  $II_{2,26} = II_{1,1} \oplus II_{1,1}
  \oplus \Lambda_{\mathrm{Leech}}$ of signature $(2, 26)$,
  with input $F = J(\tau)$ where $J$ encodes the Fourier
  coefficients of $1/\Delta(\tau)
  = q^{-1} + 24 + 324\,q + \cdots$ (the unique Hauptmodul
  normalization on $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ with $c(-1) = 1$),
  the Borcherds product
  $\Psi_{12} \in M_{12}(\mathrm{O}^+(II_{2,26}))$ is the
  denominator function of the \emph{fake monster Lie algebra};
  its weight $12$ reflects
  $\dim(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Leech}})/2$.
 \item \emph{Gritsenko cusp form $\Delta_5$} (Gritsenko~1999;
  Gritsenko--Nikulin~1997, 1998). On the signature-$(2, 3)$ even
  lattice $\Lambda^{2,3} = II_{1,1} \oplus II_{1,1}
  \oplus \langle -2 \rangle$, the accidental isomorphism
  $\mathrm{O}^+(\Lambda^{2,3}) \simeq \Sp_4(\Z)$ carries both a
  multiplicative Borcherds lift and an additive Gritsenko lift,
  related by squaring. In the Gritsenko--Nikulin normalisation
  the Borcherds multiplicative lift of the K3 elliptic genus
  $\phi_{0,1}(\tau, z)$ (weight $0$, index $1$) produces the

\noindent\textbf{The pentagon.}
The six routes do \emph{not} assemble into a common quotient,
and the obstruction is sharp. The chiral characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a categorical invariant of
$\Phi_3(\cC)$ and is therefore \emph{route-independent} across
any construction that recovers $\Phi_3(\cC)$ up to equivalence;
for $K3 \times E$ the additivity
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3 \times E)
 = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(E) = 2 + 1 = 3$
holds on every route. The genuine generator-level
stratification is by lattice rank $\rho^{R_i}$, not by
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}$ (which is constant across the pentagon).
The six routes produce algebras with stratified
$\rho^{R_i} \in \{3, 12, 24\}$, recording how many
generators each construction extracts: each route is a
\emph{different construction witnessing the same
$\Phi_3(K3 \times E)$-output}, not a different
$\Phi$-application. Direct computation
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-c-six-routes-generator-level-convergence})
gives
\[
 \begin{array}{r@{\;\colon\;}l@{\qquad}l}
  R_1 & \Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(X)))
   & \rho^{R_1} = 3\,, \\
  R_2 & \text{Borcherds lift of }\phi_{0,1}
   & (\text{source branch})\,, \\
  R_3 & V_{\Lambda^{3,2}} \text{ (Frenkel--Kac lattice VOA)}
   & \rho^{R_3} = 24\,, \\
  R_4 & \mathrm{Kum}^n(K3) \text{ (Kummer orbifold)}
   & \rho^{R_4} = 12\,, \\
  R_5 & \text{K3 }\sigma\text{-model half-twist}
   & \rho^{R_5} = 3\,, \\
  R_6 & \text{BLLPR (4d } \cN{=}2 \text{ Schur sector)}
   & \rho^{R_6} = 3\,.
 \end{array}
\]
Naively one might expect all six routes to assemble into a single
Hopf object; the generator-rank stratification $\rho^{R_i} \in
\{3, 12, 24\}$ obstructs the common quotient
(Corollary~\ref{cor:six-way-isomorphism-falsified}). Instead, the
Borcherds branch $R_2$ carries the BKM denominator, which induces
a Hopf structure up to automorphic completion and serves as the
pentagon source rather than as a pentagon node; the remaining
five routes assemble into a pentagon with five named intertwiners
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.4cm, row sep=0.8cm]
 R_1 \ar[r, "\beta_{13}"] & R_3 \ar[r, "\beta_{34}"] &
 R_4 \ar[r, "\beta_{45}"] & R_5 \ar[r, "\beta_{56}"] &
 R_6 \ar[lllll, bend left=22, "\beta_{61}"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $\beta_{13}$ is an injection (cokernel rank~$21$
capturing the K3 twisted sector), $\beta_{34}$ is the
$\Z/2$-Kummer-orbifold surjection, $\beta_{45}$ the
primitive stratification surjection, $\beta_{56}$ the
Costello--Li 6d hCS isomorphism, and $\beta_{61}$ the
BLLPR-to-$\Phi_3$ isomorphism. The $\rho$-trace
around the pentagon reads
$3 \xrightarrow{\beta_{13}} 24
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{34}} 12
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{45}} 3
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{56}} 3
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{61}} 3$,
returning to $\rho^{R_1} = 3$. The chiral characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ is constant around the cycle
(categorical invariant of $\Phi_3(\cC)$, route-independent);
the lattice rank $\rho^{R_i}$ is the genuine
isomorphism-class invariant.
The universal object is the pentagon colimit
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-c-pentagon-colimit})
\[
 G(K3 \times E) \;=\; \mathrm{colim}\!\bigl(R_1
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{13}} R_3
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{34}} R_4
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{45}} R_5
 \xrightarrow{\beta_{56}} R_6\bigr)\,,
\]
a Hopf object into which each of $R_1, R_3, R_4, R_5, R_6$
maps via the named intertwiners. The pentagon is not a
diamond: it carries a nontrivial
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-cycle around its edges.

\noindent\textbf{The curvature: Yukawa as BCOV datum.}
The pentagon's edge data is curved. For the compact quintic
threefold $X_5 = \{x_0^5 + \cdots + x_4^5 = 0\} \subset \bP^4$,
the Bondal--Van den Bergh DG algebra
\[
 A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}
 \;=\; \End^\bullet\!\Bigl(\bigoplus_{i = 0}^{4}
  \cO_{X_5}(i)\Bigr)
\]
is \emph{not} $\Ainf$-formal as a $(-3)$-CY DG algebra:
Calaque--Halbout--Felder torus-action formality does not apply
(only the finite Heisenberg group $H_5$ of order $5^3$ acts
on $X_5$, not the continuous torus $(\C^*)^4$), and Sheridan's
HMS proof for the quintic (arXiv:1507.03085) shows that the
higher operation
\[
 m_3 \colon HH^1(X_5)^{\otimes 3}
 \longrightarrow HH^1(X_5)^*[1]
\]
is \emph{never} zero in complex-structure moduli. On the
Hodge piece $H^1(T_{X_5}) \cong \C^{101}$, $m_3$ is the
\emph{Yukawa coupling}
\[
 Y_3(\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3)
 \;=\; \int_{X_5}
 \Omega \wedge (\mu_1 \cdot \mu_2 \cdot \mu_3) \lrcorner \Omega\,,
\]
with classical contribution
$Y_3^{\mathrm{cl}} = H^3 = 5$ at large complex structure and
Gromov--Witten corrections
\[
 Y_3^{\mathrm{inst}}
 \;=\; \sum_{d \geq 1} n_d^{(0)}\, d^3\,
  \frac{q^d}{1 - q^d}\,,
 \qquad
 n_1^{(0)} = 2875,\;
 n_2^{(0)} = 609250,\;
 n_3^{(0)} = 317206375,\;
 \ldots
\]
(Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--Parkes 1991). The Kapranov
$3$-shifted Koszul duality on compact CY$_3$ therefore does
not reduce to strict formality of $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$; the
correct statement is \emph{curved formality}, with the
Yukawa coupling $Y_3$ tracked as the BCOV curving datum
in a deformed $(-3)$-CY structure. Strict formality holds at
$d = 2$ (on K3 surfaces, where the Yukawa vanishes on Hodge
grounds); it fails universally at $d = 3$ on compact CY$_3$
with non-trivial Yukawa. The compact-CY$_3$ Koszul duality
is curved, and the curvature is the Yukawa.

\noindent\textbf{The super-Riccati extension.}
Volume~I's bosonic Riccati recurrence for the shadow tower
$\{S_r(\cA)\}_{r \geq 2}$ of a class-$\mathbf{M}$ chiral
algebra extends to the super setting. For the super-Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m | n))$, the shadow tower satisfies the
\emph{super-Riccati master recurrence}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:super-riccati-master-recurrence})
\[
 S_r \;=\; -\frac{1}{2 r \kappa_\ell}
 \sum_{j + k = r}
 (-1)^{(j - 1)(k - 1) |\ell|}\, f(j, k)\, j k\, S_j S_k\,,
\]
where $|\ell| \in \{0, 1\}$ is the line parity. At $|\ell| = 0$
the recurrence recovers the Vol~I bosonic Riccati exactly
(verified at $S_6$); at $|\ell| = 1$ the parity sign factor
$(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}$ captures the super-convolution Koszul sign
on the fermionic boundary. The super-shadow complementarity,
on the \emph{sub-Sugawara line} and verified symbolically at
small rank, reads
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\!\bigl(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m | n))\bigr)
 \;+\;
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\!\bigl(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(n | m))^!\bigr)
 \;=\; \max(m, n)\,,
\]
not zero as a naive Virasoro-style
``$c + (26 - c) = 26$''-analogy would suggest. Two distinct
pairings (super-trace and Berezinian) coexist without
programme-level canonicalisation; the central automorphism
$\sigma_{\mathrm{sBer}}$ intertwines them
(Lemma~\ref{lem:super-trace-berezinian-bridge}), and a
Verdier-pairing inscription is pending. At
$\mathfrak{psl}(2 | 2)$ the apparent coincidence with
$\max(2, 2) = 2$ reflects a \emph{centre-rank} match, not a
count of central elements: Beisert's 2007 analysis exhibits
three central charges $(P, K, C)$ in
$\mathfrak{psl}(2 | 2) \oplus \mathbb{C}^3$, of which the
two-dimensional centre rank survives under the shadow pairing.
Closed forms for $\mathfrak{sl}(1 | 1)$,
$\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 1)$, and $\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 2)$ realise
three shadow subclasses $\mathbf{G}_s$, $\mathbf{L}_s$,
$\mathbf{M}_s$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.3ex]
 \item $\mathfrak{sl}(1 | 1)$ (doubly degenerate, $\mathrm{sdim} = 0$):
  $T$-line degenerate ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$); $\psi$-line
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = -k$, $S_3 = S_4 = 0$;
  $\psi \psi^*$ bilinear $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = k(k+1)$,
  $S_3 = 2$, $S_4 = 10/[2 k (k+1) (10 k(k+1) + 22)]$.
 \item $\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 1)$: $c_{\mathrm{sub}} = 3k/(k+1)$,
  $S_4$ takes Virasoro form.
 \item $\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 2)$ (doubly critical):
  $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 6k/(k+2)$ via bosonic
  $\mathfrak{sl}_L(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}_R(2)$ decomposition.
\end{itemize}
The Gaiotto--Rap\v{c}\'ak--Li $S_3$-triality extends to
$Y(L | 1, M | 1, N | 1)$ via a $\Z/3$-equivariant super-extension
functor preserving shadow class. Thus Volume~I's class
$\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{M}$ classification
admits a parity-refined super-class classification
$\mathbf{G}_s / \mathbf{L}_s / \mathbf{M}_s / \mathbf{C}_s /
\mathbf{FF}_s$ on the CY side.

\emph{Hypergeometric shadow tower.} For the Virasoro chiral

$F(r, u) = \Phi_r(1/u) / A_r$
(with $A_r = 8 (-6)^{r - 4} / r$ the leading Laurent

$\Phi_r(u) = \sum_K c_K(r)\, u^{r - 4 - K}$ the rational

$\beta_{M,N} = \sigma_M(N) \colon M \otimes N \to N \otimes M$;

braiding is trivially symmetric.

For $d \geq 3$, the descent-and-center detour is mandatory:
one must pass to $\Eone$ and rebuild the braiding
categorically via the Drinfeld center.

  \ar[r, "\mathbb Z/2\text{-}\mathrm{orb}"]

where $A(\Lambda_{24})$ is the $24$-generator fermionic

\noindent\textbf{Metaplectic Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller
$\mathrm{grt}_1^{(1/2)}$.}
The deeper metaplectic presence on the
$\widetilde{\Sp_4(\Z)}$ cover refines the
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra. Let $\mathrm{grt}_1$
denote the classical (genus-$0$, associators with even
generators $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \ldots$ of Drinfeld) and let
$\mathrm{grt}_1^{(1/2)}$ denote the \emph{metaplectic}
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra arising from
genus-$2$ associators on the Maass spin cover. There is a
non-split extension
\[
 0 \longrightarrow
 \mathrm{Lie}\langle \widetilde\sigma_{2k} \rangle_{k \ge 1}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathrm{grt}_1^{(1/2)}
 \longrightarrow
 \mathrm{grt}_1
 \longrightarrow 0,
\]
whose kernel is the free Lie algebra on
\emph{even-weight} Saito--Kurokawa-motivic generators
$\widetilde\sigma_{2k}$; the even indices are forced by the
paramodular half-integrality (Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},
Theorem~\ref{thm:grt-half-transitivity}). The first nontrivial
bracket on the metaplectic side reads
\[
 [\widetilde\sigma_2,\,\widetilde\sigma_2]
 \;=\;
 288\,\widetilde\sigma_4
 \;=\;
 \frac{\tau(2)^2}{2}\,\widetilde\sigma_4
 \qquad
 (\tau(2) = -24),
\]
the Ramanujan-$\tau$ coefficient appearing as the
Saito--Kurokawa lift sends the Hecke eigenvalue $\tau(2)$ into
the metaplectic bracket. This bracket is genuinely
$\mathrm{grt}_1^{(1/2)}$-specific: it vanishes on $\mathrm{grt}_1$
(where only odd $\sigma_{2k+1}$ generators survive), so the
extension does not split.

\noindent\textbf{Humbert--Heegner admissibility filtration.}
The pentagon $A_\infty$-tower acquires a sharp
arithmetic-unconditional filtration on its Drinfeld associator
coefficients $\phi^{(n)}$. Write $\phi^{(n)}$ for the order-$\hbar^n$
Chevalley--Eisenstein--Enriquez coboundary on the home Lie
algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak{t}^{\mathrm{Sieg}}_{2,[2]} \oplus
\mathfrak{n}_+^{\mathrm{imag}}}$. Then
\[
 \phi^{(n)}
 \;=\;
 0
 \qquad
 \text{unless }
 n \equiv 3 \text{ or } 5 \pmod 8,
\]
with first admissible nonvanishing order
\[
 \phi^{(5)}
 \;=\;
 -2\,[\mathrm{gen}]^{\otimes 5}
\]
on the generic generator $[\mathrm{gen}] \in \mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtration-sharpness}). The filtration is
unconditional: it is cut out by the Humbert-discriminant
spectrum
$\{H_1, H_4, H_9, \ldots\} \cup \{H_5, H_{13}, \ldots\}$
and the Heegner reciprocity, bypassing the Zagier--Hoffman
conjectures on MZV depth that would otherwise be needed to
control the $\phi^{(n)}$ tower. This is the first programme-level
proof of a sharp MZV-depth statement on a BKM home Lie algebra
that does not cite Zagier--Hoffman.

$\rho_{\Delta_5}^{\mathrm{std}}$.}

single Bloch--Kato Selmer statement. Let $\rho_{\Delta_5}$ denote
the paramodular Galois representation of $\Delta_5$ (Saito--Kurokawa
attribution on the Maass spin cover) and
$\mathrm{std}\,\rho_{\Delta_5}$ its standard transfer to
$\mathrm{GSpin}_5 \simeq \Sp_4$. Then
\[
 \dim_{\Q_p}\,
 H^1_f\bigl(\Q,\,\mathrm{std}\,\rho_{\Delta_5}\bigr)
 \;=\;
 1,
\]
one-dimensional Bloch--Kato Selmer
(Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},

the Lie-bialgebra classification cocycle and the Selmer tangent
are two evaluations of the same class, and the

$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ does not deform within the paramodular
Hida family except along the cyclotomic direction.

\noindent\textbf{The holomorphic Chern--Simons hierarchy.}
The theories studied in this volume form a hierarchy in real
dimension, each producing chiral algebras by restriction to
codimension-$2$ defects. At $d = 6$: holomorphic Chern--Simons
on a Calabi--Yau $3$-fold~$Y$ (Witten, Costello), the primary
source. A curve $C \subset Y$ of codimension~$2$ carries a defect
algebra: the $\Omega$-background equivariant parameters
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$ along the normal bundle $N_{C/Y}$
(of rank~$2$) supply two spectral parameters via equivariant
localization, and the defect algebra is a chiral algebra
on~$C$ with quantum group structure from the normal directions
(Proposition~\ref{prop:codim2-defect-ope}). For $Y = \C^3$ and
$C = \C$, the defect algebra is $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at
$\Psi = -\sigma_2(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ (the second elementary
symmetric polynomial in the $\Omega$-background parameters).
At $d = 5$: partially topological Chern--Simons on $\R \times Y$
for a CY$_2$ surface~$Y$; restriction to $\R \times C$ gives the
ordered bar with one spectral parameter. At $d = 4$: holomorphic
Chern--Simons on $\C^2$; the Costello--Witten--Yamazaki theory
that produces Yangians from holomorphic gauge fields. At $d = 3$:
the theories of Volume~II. The hierarchy
$6 \to 5 \to 4 \to 3$ is codimension-$2$ restriction at each
step; each restriction produces a chiral algebra from a gauge
theory, and the $E_n$ level decreases as the dimension drops.
The hierarchy is not limited to Chern--Simons: 6d little string
theory on K3 and M5 brane theory produce chiral algebras by the
same defect mechanism, with the specific gauge-theory content
replaced by stringy data.

\noindent\textbf{Concrete CY quantum groups.}
Volume~I introduces $E_1$-chiral quantum groups as abstract
algebraic-geometric objects: an ordered bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ with spectral $R$-matrix, Drinfeld
coproduct, and Yang--Baxter equation, all living on
configuration spaces of curves. This volume constructs the
concrete examples. The critical cohomological Hall algebra
$\CoHA(Q)$ of a quiver~$Q$ (with potential~$W$ from the CY
condition) is an $E_1$-chiral quantum group: its product is the
ordered Hall multiplication (graded by dimension vectors), its
$R$-matrix comes from the Ext-quiver, and its coproduct is the
CoHA vertex coproduct of Schiffmann--Vasserot. For the Jordan
quiver ($Q = \bullet \circlearrowleft$):
$\CoHA(Q) \cong Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (positive half),
with Drinfeld double
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \cong \cW_{1+\infty}$.
The Drinfeld center
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1))) \cong
\Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ recovers the full braided
structure (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}). For a general
toric CY$_3$ with fan~$\Sigma$: quiver charts indexed by
Bridgeland stability chambers give local chiral algebras;
wall-crossing is conjecturally MC gauge equivalence. The quantum
toroidal algebra $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ with CY
condition $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$ is the two-parameter deformation
carrying the full $\Z \times \Z$ bigrading from the double
current algebra. In the additive parametrization
$q_i = e^{h_i}$, the CY condition reads $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$:
two independent parameters survive, and the identification
$q_1 = q$, $q_2 = t^{-1}$, $q_3 = t/q$ recovers the standard
$(q,t)$-parametrization. The trigonometric structure function
\[
 G(x; q_1, q_2, q_3)
 = \frac{(1 - q_1 x)(1 - q_2 x)(1 - q_3 x)}
        {(1 - q_1^{-1} x)(1 - q_2^{-1} x)(1 - q_3^{-1} x)}
\]
satisfies the CY reflection identity
$G(x) \cdot G(1/x) = 1$ precisely when
$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$: the identity is equivalent to the CY
condition, and its failure measures the departure from
Calabi--Yau. The Miki automorphism (cyclic permutation of
$(q_1, q_2, q_3)$, generating the $S_3$-Weyl group of the CY
torus) is algebra-specific: it requires the concrete structure of
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ and does not follow from the
$E_3$-operad alone.

\noindent\textbf{Toric CY$_3$ and critical CoHAs.}
For a toric CY$_3$ determined by a fan~$\Sigma$, the toric
diagram fixes a quiver $Q_X$. The critical cohomological
Hall algebra $\CoHA(Q_X)$ is the $\Eone$-sector: the positive
half of an affine super Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\frakg}_{Q_X})$. The full braided structure is
recovered through the Drinfeld center. For $\C^3$: the
Jordan quiver gives
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$, and the chain
$\C^3 \to \cW_{1+\infty} \to
\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ is verified end-to-end.
The toric fan is the Dynkin data.

The CoHA is the natural $\Eone$-primitive: an associative
algebra whose Hall product provides the $m_2$ structure map
of the CY$_3$ chiral algebra. Its multiplication is encoded
in the bar differential of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$, but the
CoHA and the bar complex are different mathematical objects
(algebra vs coalgebra). The passage from CoHA to quantum group
is the CY incarnation of the $\Eone$-to-$\Etwo$ construction:
the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA))$ builds the
braided monoidal structure via half-braidings.
The algebraic underpinning of this passage (the integrable
structure of the ordered chiral bar complex, the R-matrix, and
the Yangian presentation) is developed in the companion paper
\emph{On the Integrable Theory of Chiral Yangians and Related
Chiral Quantum Groups} (Volume~I, standalone).

\noindent\textbf{The DDCA--toroidal bridge.}
The deformed double current algebra
$\mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K) = U(\mathfrak{gl}_K \otimes
\C[u,v])$ carries two spectral parameters $(u,v)$ from the two
holomorphic directions of $\C^2$ in the 5d noncommutative
Chern--Simons theory of Costello. The quantum toroidal algebra
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)$ carries two
deformation parameters $(q,t)$ from the two loop directions of
$\C^* \times \C^*$. Both degenerate to the affine Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K)$ in complementary limits: the DDCA
by specializing one spectral variable to zero (collapsing
$\C[u,v] \to \C[u]$); the toroidal algebra by sending $t \to 1$
(collapsing the outer loop). The conjecture
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:ddca-toroidal-bridge}) states the
bridge: writing $q = e^{\hbar_1}$, $t = e^{\hbar_2}$,
the DDCA is the rational ($\hbar_1, \hbar_2 \to 0$) degeneration
of the quantum toroidal algebra, in the category of filtered
algebras with the bidegree filtration on generators $J^a_{m,n}$.
The two one-parameter degenerations of $U_{q,t}$ produce a
commuting square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.6cm, row sep=0.8cm]
 U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)
 \ar[r, "t \to 1"] \ar[d, "q \to 1"']
 & Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K) \otimes U(\C[v])
  \ar[d, "\hbar_1 \to 0"] \\
 U(\C[u]) \otimes Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K)
 \ar[r, "\hbar_2 \to 0"']
 & \mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
whose diagonal $\hbar_1 = \hbar_2 \to 0$ recovers the affine
Yangian as the common degeneration. The Koszul dual side has a
parallel identification: the double Yangian
$Y^{(2)}(\mathfrak{gl}_K)$ is the rational degeneration of
$U_{q^{-1}, t^{-1}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)$,
consistent with the parameter inversion
$(q,t) \mapsto (q^{-1}, t^{-1})$ of the Koszul dual toroidal
algebra. The modular characteristic is continuous across the
bridge:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K)) =
K(k+K)/2 = \lim_{\hbar_i \to 0}
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(U_{q,t})$.

\noindent\textbf{The Drinfeld centre as categorified center.}
Volume~I constructs the averaging map
$\mathrm{av} \colon \frakg^{E_1}_\cA \to
\frakg^{\mathrm{mod}}_\cA$ that projects the ordered
$R$-matrix to the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. This volume
constructs the categorical analogue (not a lift of averaging,
but the \emph{right adjoint to forgetful}): the Drinfeld centre
$Z \colon E_1\text{-}\Cat \to E_2\text{-}\Cat$. It takes the
$E_1$-monoidal representation category
$\Rep^{E_1}(A_\cC)$ to an $E_2$-braided monoidal category
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(A_\cC))$ by constructing universal
half-braidings $\sigma_M \colon M \otimes (-) \xrightarrow{\sim}
(-) \otimes M$. Direct restriction from $E_3$ to $E_2$
gives only symmetric braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^3)) = 0$); the Drinfeld centre is the sole
source of non-trivial braiding. For $\C^3$:
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))
\cong \Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))
\cong \Rep^{E_2}(\cW_{1+\infty})$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}). Five invariants match
at six levels: naive centre (dimension~$1$) versus derived centre
(dimension~$3$, capturing $\Ext^{1,2}$ invisible to the
commutant). The Drinfeld centre closes the $E_n$ circle
by recovering $E_2$-braiding from $E_1$-ordering.

\noindent\textbf{Wall-crossing as Maurer--Cartan gauge
equivalence.}\enspace
For a toric CY$_3$ with fan~$\Sigma$, Bridgeland stability
conditions carve the space of central charges into chambers.
Each chamber $\sigma$ determines a quiver presentation
$Q_\sigma$ and a critical CoHA
$\CoHA(Q_\sigma, W_\sigma)$. Wall-crossing (varying the
stability condition across a wall) changes the quiver
presentation but not the underlying CY category. The
conjecture: wall-crossing between chambers $\sigma$ and
$\sigma'$ is Maurer--Cartan gauge equivalence
$\Theta_\sigma \sim \Theta_{\sigma'}$ in the modular
convolution algebra of the CY chiral algebra. The global
chiral algebra $A_\cC$ would then be the homotopy colimit over
the chamber decomposition:
$A_\cC = \operatorname{hocolim}_\sigma A_{Q_\sigma}$.
For $\C^3$ (single chamber): the conjecture is vacuous. For
the resolved conifold $\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \P^1$
(two chambers): the wall-crossing formula is the flop relation
on DT partition functions (Bryan--Steinberg), and the MC
gauge equivalence is the bar-cobar transport across the flop.
This conjecture connects the algebraic programme of Volume~I
(Maurer--Cartan elements) to the enumerative programme
(DT/BPS wall-crossing) of this volume.

\noindent\textbf{From shadow tower to BPS degeneracies.}
The shadow obstruction tower of Volume~I produces scalar
invariants $S_r(\cA)$ at each degree~$r$. For CY$_3$ chiral
algebras, these invariants admit an enumerative interpretation:
the DT invariants $\Omega(\gamma)$ of the CY$_3$ manifold are
root multiplicities of the BKM superalgebra $\frakg_X$, and the
denominator identity
\[
\prod_{\alpha > 0} (1 - e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}
\;=\;
\sum_{w \in W} (-1)^{\ell(w)}\, e^{w(\rho) - \rho}
\]
is expected to match the bar Euler product when the relevant
chiral object exists: the product over positive roots is the
candidate graded Euler characteristic of the bar complex
$B(A_X)$. For $K3 \times E$:
the denominator is the Igusa cusp form
$\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$, and the root multiplicities are the
coefficients of the elliptic genus $\phi_{0,1}(\tau, z)$.

The identification operates at three levels:

(ii)~\emph{virtual}: the shadow coefficient $S_r$ at degree~$r$

(iii)~\emph{motivic}: the full chiral quantum group structure

This three-level correspondence is the connection traced here between
the algebraic-geometric programme of Volumes~I--II and the
enumerative geometry of this volume.

\noindent\textbf{Convergence and divergence.}
The shadow obstruction tower and the topological string free
energy produce genus-$g$ amplitudes with opposite analytic
behaviour. The shadow tower has Bernoulli decay
$|F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}| \sim (2\pi)^{-2g}/g$: convergent, with
radius~$2\pi$, Borel entire. The topological string has
factorial growth $|F_g^{\mathrm{top}}| \sim (2g)!$: divergent,
Gevrey-$1$, requiring non-perturbative completion. The
shadow is the algebraic soul: the part computable from OPE
structure constants alone, without worldsheet instantons.
For class~$\mathbf{G}$ algebras (Heisenberg, lattice VOAs),
the shadow is the full answer. For class~$\mathbf{M}$
(Virasoro, $\cW_{1+\infty}$), the shadow is a strict subtower,
and the instanton sum is infinite. Computing the instanton
corrections $F_g^{\mathrm{top}} - F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}$ requires
non-perturbative input: Borel resummation of the shadow tower,
BPS state counting, and (for $K3$-fibered CY$_3$) the Borcherds
product that packages the BKM root multiplicities into an
automorphic form.

The $\mathbf{G}$/$\mathbf{L}$/$\mathbf{C}$/$\mathbf{M}$
classification of Volume~I (shadow depth
$r = 2, 3, 4, \infty$) acquires a CY incarnation: the shadow
depth of $\Phi(\cC)$ is determined by the homological
complexity of~$\cC$, and the four classes reappear as
conditions on the Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence of
the CY category. Class~$\mathbf{G}$ corresponds to CY
categories with semisimple Hochschild cohomology
(e.g.\ $\Coh(E)$ for an elliptic curve); class~$\mathbf{M}$
to those with infinite algebraic depth (e.g.\ $D^b(K3)$,
whose chiral algebra $\Phi(K3)$ carries the full
Virasoro-type shadow tower).

\noindent\textbf{The $\Omega$-background and $E_1$ stabilization.}
The $E_1$ stabilization theorem: for CY$_d$ with
$d \ge 3$, the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has
degree $1 - d \leq -2$, too shifted to produce braiding. For
toric CY$_d$ ($\C^d$ with $\GL(d)$ action), the classical Lie
conformal algebra is abelian (SN brackets vanish by degree
overflow, Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}) and all
noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.
For non-toric CY$_3$, the bracket is nonzero but its shifted
degree still prevents braiding.
The $\Omega$-background (toric-specific) provides the concrete
mechanism: the equivariant parameters $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$
of the torus action on $\C^2 \subset Y$ break Dunn additivity
$E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$ by freezing one factor, producing
a single $E_1$ direction. At $\epsilon_1 = 0$, $\epsilon_2 \neq 0$
(the Nekrasov--Shatashvili limit): one $E_1$ factor survives and
the resulting algebra is the Yangian $Y(\fg)$, the quantum group
associated to the gauge theory. At generic
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$: the quantum toroidal algebra
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fg}})$ with
$q = e^{R\epsilon_1}$, $t = e^{-R\epsilon_2}$, and CY
condition $qt^{-1}(qt)^{1/2} = 1$ (for CY$_3$:
$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$). The $E_1$ stabilization at $d \geq 3$ is a general
consequence of CY dimension: the Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has degree $1 - d$, which for $d \geq 3$
is too negative to produce braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$, so ordered
configuration spaces carry no monodromy). For toric CY$_3$
manifolds, the $\Omega$-background provides a concrete
realization: the equivariant parameters
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3)$ break $U(3)$ to a
maximal torus $T^3$, restricting $E_3$ factorization on
$\C^3$ to $E_1$ factorization on each coordinate line. For
non-toric CY$_3$ (the quintic, generic $K3 \times E$), no
torus action exists, and the $E_1$ structure arises directly
from the operadic constraint on the bracket degree.

This connects all three volumes: Volume~I introduces
$E_1$-chiral quantum groups as abstract algebraic-geometric
objects; this volume constructs them from CY geometry (via the
$\Omega$-background for toric examples, via the CY-to-chiral
functor $\Phi_3$ in general); Volume~II

\begin{remark}[Kazhdan--Lusztig parameters for~K3: $q_{K3}$ is a

  \;=\;
  e^{2\pi i/8}
  \;=\;
  \text{primitive 8-th root of unity},
  \qquad
  \ell \;=\; 8,

specialisation~\cite{Lusztig1990,Lusztig1993}, the Hall--Drinfeld

\begin{remark}[The heterotic-on-$T^6$ decoupling limit isolates $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ as a stand-alone chiral bialgebra]
\label{rem:v3-preface-decoupling}
Four duality frames exhibit $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$: heterotic on
$K3\times T^2$, IIB D1--D5 on $K3\times S^1$, IIA DMVV on
$\mathrm{Sym}^N K3$, M-theory on $K3\times T^3$. Each frame
\emph{couples} the K3 chiral sector to a gravitational bulk, so the
Igusa form $\Delta_5$ appears only as a 1-loop anomaly
\emph{contribution} inside a larger generating function. A genuine
decoupling limit exists on the heterotic frame, in which
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ isolates as a stand-alone chiral bialgebra
with no residual gravitational bulk. The limit is
explicit: take the heterotic string on~$T^6$ with a distinguished
$T^2$ subfactor, and send
\[
 g_s \longrightarrow 0,
 \qquad
 R_{T^2} \longrightarrow R_{\mathrm{sd}}
 = \sqrt{\alpha'/2},
 \qquad
 R_{T^4} \longrightarrow \infty,
 \qquad
 \alpha' \cdot R_{T^4}^{-2} \longrightarrow 0,
\]
i.e.\ decouple the string coupling, fix the distinguished $T^2$ at the
self-dual radius, and send the complementary $T^4$ to infinite volume
so that the transverse Kaluza--Klein tower and the closed-string
gravitational modes decouple~\cite{Polchinski1998,KiritsisBook2007}.
At the self-dual $T^2$ radius the chiral left-movers of the
distinguished $T^2$ enhance to
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times \mathrm{SU}(2)_R$ at level~1, the Narain
lattice of signatures $(2,2)$ at self-dual radius embeds as
$\mathrm{II}_{2,2}^{\mathrm{sd}}$ (equivalently $D_4$-lattice in
orthogonal complement; see Nahm--Wendland~\cite{NahmWendland2001}); the
$(4,4)$ complement decouples with its gravity sector; and the
heterotic gauge lattice~$\mathrm{II}_{16}^{E_8\times E_8}$ together
with the residual $(1,1)$-signature left-over from
$T^6$/$T^4$-to-$T^2$ splitting combine into the signature
$(3,19)$ Narain lattice $\mathrm{II}_{3,19}$ of the dual
$K3\times T^2$ frame
\cite{NahmWendland2001,TaorminaWendland2011,Witten1995}. On this
limiting Narain lattice, the Borcherds singular theta lift of
the weight-$(2-24)/2 = -11$ vector-valued form
$\phi_{0,1}(K3)\cdot\theta_{\mathrm{II}_{3,19}}$ produces
$\Delta_5$ as the multiplicative Borcherds product associated
with the denominator~\cite{Borcherds1998,HarveyMoore1996}.
In the decoupled limit no bulk gravitational mode
contributes to the 1-loop threshold, because the transverse
$T^4$-volume factor has been sent to infinity. The limit is
\emph{rigorous} at the level of the BPS-counting generating
function (Harvey--Moore 1996 equation~(1.2)); the surviving
degrees of freedom are precisely the K3 chiral sector paired
with the self-dual-radius~$T^2$ chiral sector, i.e.\ the
chiral bialgebra~$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ uncoupled from any
ambient gravity. The decoupling is what makes $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$
a~\emph{quantum-group} object rather than an anomaly
contribution~\cite{Sen2007,CostelloGaiotto2018}.
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},
Theorem~\ref{thm:k3-1loop-output}, gives the non-decoupled statement
(1-loop-forced $\Delta_5$); this remark gives the stronger,
1-loop-isolated version.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[F-theory on elliptic K3: the 24 $I_1$ fibres as the 24 Ran-space base points]
\label{rem:v3-preface-ftheory-24-fibres}
F-theory on an elliptic K3 surface~$\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{P}^1$
identifies the K3 chiral sector with the 2d chiral algebra of
IIB~string theory compactified on a 6d~ambient geometry with 24
$(p,q)$-7-branes at the 24~locations of the~$I_1$~Kodaira fibres on
the base~$\mathbb{P}^1$~\cite{Vafa1996}. The monodromy around the
$i$-th $I_1$-fibre is an element of~$\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ of order~$12$

there are 24 such conjugacy data at the 24 fibres). The global
monodromy around all 24 fibres must be trivial in~$\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$

maps to a trivial element on encircling all punctures once), giving
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^{24}
 M_i
 \;=\;
 \mathbf{1}
 \quad\in\;
 \mathrm{SL}_2(\Z),
 \qquad
 M_i \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)
 \text{ of order~12,}
\]
the so-called \emph{24-product constraint} of Morrison--Vafa~II
\cite{MorrisonVafa1996II}. The 24 base points of the bi-based
Ran-space datum $\mathrm{Ran}(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24})$ are precisely
the 24 locations of the $I_1$ fibres, and the $\widetilde M_{24}$-action
on $M_{24}$ permutes these 24 points as the Steiner
system~$S(5,8,24)$ permits~\cite{ConwaySloane1988}. The F-theory
reading of the bi-based Ran space follows: the 24~nodes
of $E^{\mathrm{nod}}_{24}$ are 24~F-theory 7-branes, the
smooth-locus chiral bialgebra is the D3-brane probe 2d chiral
algebra of the residual $\mathcal{N}=4$ theory living transverse to
the 7-branes, and the $\Delta_5$-denominator is the 1-loop threshold
correction to the 7-brane sector. The $M_{24}$-quotient of the
monodromy group is exactly the~$\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)/$~$\Gamma(12)$~image,
and the identification of the bi-based Ran structure with the 7-brane
configuration makes the decoupling limit a direct descendant of the
Morrison--Vafa 24-brane rigidity.
See Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},
Remark~\ref{rem:v3-preface-six-charts}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[K3 as the unique simply-connected CY-$2$: Enriques, Kummer, and half-K3 comparanda]
\label{rem:v3-preface-cy2-scope}
The ``K3 chiral bialgebra $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$'' is the

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(T\arabic*)},leftmargin=2em,
 topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.3ex]

\item $h^{2,0}(X) = 1$, ensuring the Serre functor~$S_X = [2]$ picks
  up a single~$\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded~$A_\infty$-$4$-cycle rather
  than the $[3]$-shift of a CY-3~\cite{Kuznetsov2014}.
\end{enumerate}
Exactly four CY-2 surfaces exist: $K3$, abelian surface $T^4 = \mathbb{C}^2/\Lambda$,
Enriques surface $Y = K3/\langle\iota\rangle$, and bielliptic
surface~$T^4/G$ with~$G$~a finite group acting freely and
holomorphically~\cite{BarthHulekPetersVan2004}. Of these,
\begin{itemize}

  16 special exceptional $(-2)$-curves, so its~$\Phi$-image

  The automorphic datum sees
  $\Delta_5|_{\mathrm{Kum}} = \Delta_5(Z)|_{Z\in\mathcal{A}_2^{\mathrm{Kum}}}$,
  the restriction to the Kummer-specific $\mathcal{A}_2$-stratum.
\item Enriques surface $Y$ has $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 12$,
  $h^{2,0} = 0$, $c_1$ 2-torsion not zero (torsion
  canonical); it fails~(T2) and~(T3). The analogue
  $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}^{\mathrm{Enr}} = \mathfrak
  g_{\Delta_5}^{\mathrm{Enr}}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-gauging of

   \Delta_{5/2}^{\mathrm{Enr}}
   \;\in\;
   M_{5/2}\!\bigl(
    \widetilde{\Gamma_0(2)}_{\mathrm{paramod}}
   \bigr),
  \]
  on the level-$2$ paramodular metaplectic cover, not
  $\Delta_5$ on full $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$
  \cite{OguisoSakai2001, DolgachevKondo2002,
   GritsenkoNikulin1998}. The imaginary-root multiplicities

   \mathrm{mult}_{\mathrm{Enr}}(\alpha)
   \;=\;
   \frac{c_{\mathrm{K3}}(-\alpha^2/2)}{2}
   \qquad
   (\alpha \in L_{\mathrm{Enr}},\ \alpha^2 < 0),
