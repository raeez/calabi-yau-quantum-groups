\noindent
A chiral algebra is a solution. What is the problem it solves?

Volumes~I and~II develop the bar-cobar machine that extracts
modular invariants from any chiral algebra~$A$ on a curve~$X$:
the bar complex $B(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$ on the desuspended
augmentation ideal, with its deconcatenation coproduct; the
universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_A$; the modular
characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$; the five theorems
that control the genus tower; and the universal Koszul
conductor $K(\cA) = -c_{\mathrm{ghost}}(\BRST(\cA))$.
Volume~I constructs $E_1$-chiral quantum groups abstractly:
ordered bar complexes with spectral $R$-matrices, Drinfeld
coproducts, and Yang--Baxter equations, living on
configuration spaces of curves. Neither volume exhibits a
chiral algebra or a chiral quantum group; the input is
assumed.

\emph{This volume constructs the input.} The construction is
a $d$-indexed CY-to-chiral correspondence programme
$\{\Phi_d\}_{d \ge 1}$
\[
 \boxed{\;\Phi_d \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(\cM_d),
 \qquad
 n = n(d) = \begin{cases}\infty, & d = 1, \\ 2, & d = 2, \\
 1, & d \ge 3,\end{cases}\;}
\]
with target category varying with $d$; each $\Phi_d$ is
characterised by four universal properties:
\begin{description}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=2.0em,style=nextline]
\item[(U1) Hochschild pullback.]
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Phi(\cC)) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
as factorisation coalgebras: the ordered bar complex on the
output equals the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
of the input dg/$\Ainf$-category.
\item[(U2) CY-morphism functoriality.]
A wall-crossing in $\cC$ (mutation, Bridgeland traversal,
flop) maps to an $R$-matrix gauge transformation in
$\Phi(\cC)$.
\item[(U3) Drinfeld-centre compatibility.]
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC))) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\Phi(\cC)))$ at $d \ge 3$ (BZFN,
Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}); the $\Etwo$-braiding
emerges on the centre, not the algebra.
\item[(U4) Standard-input recovery.]
$\Phi(\Coh(E))$ is the lattice VOA of $\Lambda = H^*(E, \Z)$
at $d = 1$; $\Phi(D^b(K3))$ is the Mukai Heisenberg
$H_{\mathrm{Muk}} = \mathrm{Heis}(H^*(K3, \C), \omega_{\mathrm{Muk}})$
of rank~$24$ at $d = 2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-k3-explicit});
$\Phi(\CoHA(\C^3))$ is $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$,
the positive half of the affine Yangian
(Schiffmann--Vasserot), at $d = 3$.
\end{description}

The four-step recipe (cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal $\to$
factorisation envelope $\to$ $\mathbb{S}^d$-enhanced
quantisation) is the \emph{proof} of existence and uniqueness;
the four universal properties are the \emph{statement}.
\textbf{At $d \ge 3$, $\Phi(\cC)$ is $\Eone$-chiral; the
$\Etwo$-braiding lives on $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC)))$
only}, never on the chiral algebra itself
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \ge 3$ kills any
direct braiding). The crown of the volume is
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian}, the K3 Yangian: the first
non-trivial quantum group constructed from CY geometry via
$\Phi$.

The manuscript is supported by ${\sim}39{,}500$ tests across
${\sim}570$ compute engines, organised in seven parts.

\noindent\textbf{Three architectural theorems} anchor the construction.
\textit{Universal extension}: for
$\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^*$-generic $X$,
$M_{X \times E^k} = M_X$ for all $k \ge 0$.
\textit{$V_4$ four-phenotype classification}: every CY direction $Y$ belongs to
$\{\mathrm{P}_1, \mathrm{P}_2, \mathrm{P}_3, \mathrm{P}_4\}$ by Klein-four
Fourier support, closed under Künneth fusion
($\mathrm{P}_4$ absorbing, $\mathrm{P}_1$ identity).
\textit{CY-D tri-stratum}: the three strata above exhaust the
compact CY classification of Beauville--Bogomolov; they refine
the stratified characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC_X))$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}. Two
conventions coexist and must be distinguished: the
Hodge-supertrace
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(X) := \sum_q (-1)^q\,
h^{0,q}(X)$, which equals $\chi(\cO_X)$ and vanishes for every
odd-$d$ strict CY by Serre pairing; and the Künneth-additive
complex dimension $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X) :=
\dim_\C X$, which satisfies
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X \times Y) =
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(X) +
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(Y)$. The two coincide on
even-$d$ strict CY with $h^{0,0} = h^{0,d} = 1$ and intermediate
Hodge numbers contributing with alternating signs summing to
$d$ (e.g., K3 at $d = 2$: $1 - 0 + 1 = 2$); they diverge at
$d = 1$ (elliptic curve: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}
= 0$ vs $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}} = 1$) and at odd-$d$
strict CY (e.g., $K3 \times E$ at $d = 3$:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}} = 0$ vs
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}} = 3$). Throughout Vol~III the
operational convention is Künneth-additive
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}$;
the Hodge-supertrace identity appears as a theorem at even-$d$
strict CY only.

A terminological point: ``Hochschild'' in this volume means
categorical Hochschild cohomology: the cyclic bar complex
$\mathrm{CC}_*(\cC)$ of a dg or $\Ainf$-category, topological
in nature. The chiral upgrade $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^*(A)$ of
Volume~I (Theorem~H) is the holomorphic version; it is the
output of the correspondence $\Phi_d$, not the input. The two
are related by the correspondence itself: $\Phi_d$ sends
$\mathrm{CC}_*(\cC)$ to $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^*(A_\cC)$, lifting
topological Hochschild data to holomorphic chiral data on the
curve.

The four-step construction, in detail. A CY category $\cC$
of dimension~$d$ carries a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: a
non-degenerate trace $\Tr \colon \HC^-_d(\cC) \to k$ on
negative cyclic homology, encoding the CY condition as a
$d$-dimensional Frobenius structure at the chain level. The
cyclic $\Ainf$-structure determines a Lie conformal algebra
$\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ on~$X$ (anti-symmetrising the Hochschild bracket
via the cyclic symmetry); the factorization envelope
$U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$ on $\Ran(X)$ produces a
factorization algebra; the $\Sd$-framing upgrades
factorization to chiral; quantization of the Hochschild
Poisson structure completes the algebra $A_\cC$.
The Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has degree $1 - d$, fixing the native
$\En$ level: $d = 1$ gives an abelian bracket and an
$\Einf$-chiral algebra (commutative VOA); $d = 2$ gives the
$\mathbb{S}^2$-action of Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos, an
$\Etwo$-chiral algebra with braided monoidal representation
category (Theorem~CY-A$_2$); $d \ge 3$ gives $\Eone$-chiral
(ordered) output, with the residual $\Etwo$-braiding
constructed \emph{not on $A_\cC$ itself} but on the Drinfeld
centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$. The chain-level
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction is resolved by the
$\infty$-categorical vanishing
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ for smooth proper CY$_3$ categories
and Goodwillie contractibility of the space of $E_3$-liftings
(Theorem~CY-A$_3$).

\noindent\textbf{The Mukai Lagrangian.}
For a Calabi--Yau variety~$X$, the Mukai lattice
\[
 \Lambda_X \;\subset\; H^{\mathrm{even}}(X, \Q)\,,
 \qquad
 \langle v_1, v_2 \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}
 \;:=\;
 -\!\int_X v_1^\vee \cdot v_2 \cdot \mathrm{td}(X)\,,
\]
carries two structures simultaneously, and the interplay
between them is the inner music of this volume.

On the \emph{arithmetic} side, $\Lambda_X$ is the target of
the Borcherds singular theta lift
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}). For a lattice
$\Lambda$ of signature $(2, n+2)$ and a weakly holomorphic
modular form $F = \sum_m c(m)\,q^m$ of weight $(2 - n)/2$ with
integral Fourier coefficients, the lift $\Psi(F)$ is an
$\Aut(\Lambda)$-invariant meromorphic modular form on the
Grassmannian of positive-definite planes in $\Lambda$, with
product expansion
\[
 \Psi(F)(Z) \;=\;
 e^{2\pi i \langle \rho, Z \rangle}
 \prod_{\lambda > 0}
 \bigl(1 - e^{2\pi i \langle \lambda, Z \rangle}\bigr)^{c(-\langle \lambda, \lambda \rangle / 2)}
\]
and weight $\mathrm{wt}(\Psi(F)) = c(0)/2$, with Hecke
operators intertwining source and target. Two worked
instances pin the theory to its arithmetic skeleton:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.3ex]
 \item \emph{Fake monster} (Borcherds 1992, 1998). On
  $II_{2,26} = II_{1,1} \oplus II_{1,1}
  \oplus \Lambda_{\mathrm{Leech}}$ of signature $(2, 26)$,
  with input $F = J(\tau)$ where $J$ encodes the Fourier
  coefficients of $1/\Delta(\tau)
  = q^{-1} + 24 + 324\,q + \cdots$ (the unique Hauptmodul
  normalization on $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ with $c(-1) = 1$),
  the Borcherds product
  $\Psi_{12} \in M_{12}(\mathrm{O}^+(II_{2,26}))$ is the
  denominator function of the \emph{fake monster Lie algebra};
  its weight $12$ reflects
  $\dim(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Leech}})/2$.
 \item \emph{Igusa cusp form} (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998). On
  the signature-$(2, 3)$ even lattice
  $\Lambda^{2,3} = II_{1,1} \oplus II_{1,1}
  \oplus \langle -2 \rangle$, the accidental isomorphism
  $\mathrm{O}^+(\Lambda^{2,3}) \simeq \Sp_4(\Z)$ sends the
  Borcherds product of the K3 elliptic genus
  $\phi_{0,1}(\tau, z)$ of weight $0$ and index $1$ to the
  Igusa cusp form
  $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2 \in M_{10}(\Sp_4(\Z))$;
  equivalently $\Delta_5 \in M_5(\Sp_4(\Z))$ has weight~$5$,
  and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(K3 \times E)
  = \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5$.
\end{itemize}

On the \emph{Lie-theoretic} side, $\Lambda_X$ is the weight
lattice of the critical cohomological Hall algebra
\[
 \CoHA(Q_X, W_X) \;\cong\; Y^+\!\bigl(\widehat{\frakg}_X\bigr)
\]
(Schiffmann--Vasserot; Rap\v{c}\'ak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao),
the positive half of an affine super Yangian determined by
the quiver-with-potential $(Q_X, W_X)$ extracted from the CY
geometry. The universal $R$-matrix of
$Y(\widehat{\frakg}_X)$ arises from Maulik--Okounkov stable
envelopes on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(X)$; the ordered Hall
multiplication realises the $\Eone$-monoidal structure on
$\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA(Q_X, W_X))$. For the Jordan quiver
$Q_{\C^3}$:
$\CoHA(Q_{\C^3}) \cong Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$, with Drinfeld
double $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$. For the
K3 Mukai quiver: $\CoHA(Q_{K3}) \cong Y^+(\frakg_{K3})$, the
positive half of the K3 abelian Yangian
(Theorem~\ref{thm:k3-abelian-yangian-presentation}).

Both structures encode the same datum: the generating series
of BPS invariants of~$X$, once as an automorphic form
(Borcherds output with weight $c(0)/2$), once as the universal
$R$-matrix of a quantum group (Maulik--Okounkov stable
envelope). The CY-to-chiral correspondence programme
$\{\Phi_d\}$ (Theorem~CY-A) is the
\emph{arithmetic-to-Lie} bridge: $\Phi_d(D^b(\Coh(X)))$
produces a chiral algebra whose bar Euler product is a
Borcherds denominator in the cases where both sides are
constructed (for $d \leq 3$, by CY-A combined with the Vol~I
Borcherds-lift identification). The Borcherds lift is the
\emph{Lie-to-arithmetic} bridge: it converts
root-multiplicity data (encoded at $d = 3$ conjecturally in
the CoHA character) into an $\Aut(\Lambda_X)$-invariant
automorphic form on the Grassmannian of~$\Lambda_X$. Their
conjectural composition is the identity on $\Lambda_X$ up to
the Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller action on the chiral algebra: %
the same structural redundancy developed in Part~\ref{part:connections}
as the seven faces of $r_{\CY}(z)$, and the source of the
conjectural convergence of the six $K3 \times E$ constructions
to a single Hopf object (Conjecture~CY-C).

\noindent\textbf{The Platonic ensemble.}
Five theorems govern the volume. They are neither independent
nor sequential: each is an arithmetic-geometric-operadic
cross-section of the same underlying object, the Mukai-lattice
echo of a chiral algebra.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(P\arabic*)},leftmargin=2em,
 topsep=0.5ex,itemsep=0.5ex]
 \item \emph{Theorem~CY-A$_d$} (per-$d$ correspondence, all~$d$):
  each $\Phi_d$ exists and is uniquely characterised by (U1)--(U4)
  (Hochschild pullback, CY-morphism functoriality, Drinfeld
  centre compatibility, standard-input recovery), with
  $(n, d) \in \{(\infty, 1), (2, 2), (1, {\geq}3)\}$.
  Per-$d$ proofs are evaluations of the unified statement.
  At $d = 2$: $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing via
  Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos (CY-A$_2$). At $d = 3$ the
  $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction decomposes by CY class.
  For \emph{toric and formal} CY$_3$ targets ($\bC^3$, the
  conifold, local $\bP^2$, local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$), the
  $\infty$-categorical resolution is unconditional:
  $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ and the space of $E_3$-liftings is
  contractible (CY-A$_3$,
  Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}); statements
  formerly flagged ``conditional on CY-A$_3$'' are
  unconditional in this toric/formal regime. For
  \emph{compact non-formal} CY$_3$ targets (quintic, $K3 \times
  E$, generic complete-intersection CY$_3$), CY-A$_3$ remains
  \emph{conditional} on three genuine mathematical inputs:
  (i)~a Tradler-style strictification of the higher $A_\infty$
  tower on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$; (ii)~the chain-level
  identification $B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{TCFT}} \simeq
  B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{naive}}$ of the genus-one bulk copairing
  with its naive Hochschild counterpart; (iii)~connectivity of
  $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ in the range forced by the Yukawa
  curvature. The Costello TCFT moduli-boundary extension is
  stated here and not constructed; its compact-CY$_3$ upgrade
  is one of the programme's load-bearing open problems. The
  compact $d = 3$ case is precisely where strict formality
  fails and the curved picture below takes over.
 \item \emph{Theorem~CY-B} ($\En$-chiral Koszul duality,
  all~$d$ where $\Phi$ is constructed):
  $E_2$-Koszul on $\cA = \Phi_2(\cC)$ directly at $d = 2$;
  $E_1$-Koszul on $\cA = \Phi_3(\cC)$ at $d = 3$ via the
  Verdier spectral functor
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}), inducing
  $\Etwo$-braided equivalence on the Drinfeld centre
  $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))$ at every spectral sequence page.
 \item \emph{Conjecture~CY-C} (quantum group realization):
  conjectural in general. The abelian level is constructive,
  $C(\mathfrak{g}, q) = D(Y^+(\mathfrak{g}_{K3}))$; the full
  braided monoidal equivalence
  $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC)) \simeq
  \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\frakg}_X))$ is open.
 \item \emph{Theorem~CY-D} (modular CY characteristic):
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC)) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ at
  $d = 2$ (categorical CY Euler characteristic, computed
  via Serre duality $S_\cC = [2]$ which kills the one-loop
  correction), coincident at $d = 2$ with both the
  Hodge-supertrace
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ and
  the Künneth-additive
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}} = \dim_\C X$. At
  $d \notin 2\Z$ or at $d = 1$ the two conventions diverge:
  Hodge-supertrace vanishes on every odd-$d$ strict CY by
  Serre pairing $h^{0,q} = h^{0,d-q}$ and on the elliptic
  curve, while Künneth-additive retains
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(E) = 1$ at $d = 1$ and
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K}}(K3 \times E) = 3$ at
  $d = 3$. Throughout Vol~III we use the Künneth-additive
  convention; the Hodge-supertrace identity
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ is a theorem at even-$d$
  strict CY
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}), and the
  dimension-stratified programme at odd-$d$ is a frontier.
 \item \emph{Theorem~BKM-Universal}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}) combined with
  \emph{Proposition $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_N(0)/2$}

  Borcherds singular theta lift is universal on every
  signature-$(2, n+2)$ lattice (existence, product expansion,

  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ is the unique

  $\Z/N\Z$-orbifold CY$_3$'s, $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$. At

  paramodular form $\Delta_5$ of level $1$
  (\cite{Gritsenko1999}), giving
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_1) = 10/2 = 5$. The naive
  decomposition $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} =
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$ fails at

The five Platonic theorems form the arithmetic-to-operadic
master ensemble. CY-A supplies the functor that converts CY
geometry into chiral data. CY-B supplies the Koszul duality
relating $\cA$ to its $E_2$-braided centre, closing the
operadic spiral at $d = 3$. CY-D identifies
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ with $\chi^{\CY}$ at $d = 2$ and
flags the genuine obstruction at $d \geq 3$.
BKM-Universal supplies the arithmetic frontier with its
universal theta lift.
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_N(0)/2$ identifies the weight of
the Borcherds-lifted automorphic form with a single chiral
datum, extending the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum from
manifold invariants to algebraization invariants. Conjecture~CY-C
remains the last genuine frontier: the five non-source routes
assemble into a pentagon with named intertwiners
$\beta_{13}, \beta_{34}, \beta_{45}, \beta_{56}, \beta_{61}$,
and the Borcherds branch $R_2$ is the source; the pentagon
colimit is the conjectured universal Hopf object
$G(K3 \times E)$.

\noindent\textbf{The pentagon.}
The six routes do \emph{not} assemble into a common quotient,
and the obstruction is sharp. The chiral characteristic

\noindent\textbf{The curvature: Yukawa as BCOV datum.}
The pentagon's edge data is curved. For the compact quintic

 n_1^{(0)} = 2875,\;
 n_2^{(0)} = 609250,\;
 n_3^{(0)} = 317206375,\;

\]
(Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--Parkes 1991). The Kapranov

\noindent\textbf{The super-Riccati extension.}
Volume~I's bosonic Riccati recurrence for the shadow tower
$\{S_r(\cA)\}_{r \geq 2}$ of a class-$\mathbf{M}$ chiral
algebra extends to the super setting. For the super-Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m | n))$, the shadow tower satisfies the
\emph{super-Riccati master recurrence}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:super-riccati-master-recurrence})
\[
 S_r \;=\; -\frac{1}{2 r \kappa_\ell}
 \sum_{j + k = r}
 (-1)^{(j - 1)(k - 1) |\ell|}\, f(j, k)\, j k\, S_j S_k\,,
\]
where $|\ell| \in \{0, 1\}$ is the line parity. At $|\ell| = 0$
the recurrence recovers the Vol~I bosonic Riccati exactly
(verified at $S_6$); at $|\ell| = 1$ the parity sign factor
$(-1)^{(j-1)(k-1)}$ captures the super-convolution Koszul sign
on the fermionic boundary. The super-shadow complementarity,
on the \emph{sub-Sugawara line} and verified symbolically at
small rank, reads
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\!\bigl(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(m | n))\bigr)
 \;+\;
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\!\bigl(Y(\mathfrak{sl}(n | m))^!\bigr)
 \;=\; \max(m, n)\,,
\]
not zero as a naive Virasoro-style
``$c + (26 - c) = 26$''-analogy would suggest. Two distinct
pairings (super-trace and Berezinian) coexist without
programme-level canonicalisation; the central automorphism
$\sigma_{\mathrm{sBer}}$ intertwines them
(Lemma~\ref{lem:super-trace-berezinian-bridge}), and a
Verdier-pairing inscription is pending. At
$\mathfrak{psl}(2 | 2)$ the apparent coincidence with
$\max(2, 2) = 2$ reflects a \emph{centre-rank} match, not a
count of central elements: Beisert's 2007 analysis exhibits
three central charges $(P, K, C)$ in
$\mathfrak{psl}(2 | 2) \oplus \mathbb{C}^3$, of which the
two-dimensional centre rank survives under the shadow pairing.
Closed forms for $\mathfrak{sl}(1 | 1)$,
$\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 1)$, and $\mathfrak{sl}(2 | 2)$ realise

\noindent\textbf{What the CY perspective reveals.}
Volumes~I and~II see one chiral algebra at a time. The CY
functor sees a category $\cC$ that admits \emph{multiple}
chiral algebraizations, each with its own modular
characteristic. A single CY$_3$ manifold~$X$ produces a
spectrum of $\kappa_\bullet$-values, not a single number. For
$K3 \times E$:
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}_{\kappa_\bullet}(K3 \times E)
 = \{\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},\, \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}},\,
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},\, \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\}
 = \{0,\, 3,\, 5,\, 24\}\,.
\]
Two of these are \emph{manifold invariants}, fixed by the
geometry of $X$ and independent of any algebraization:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = 0 = \chi(\cO_{K3 \times E})$
(holomorphic Euler characteristic of the total space;
K\"unneth gives $\chi(\cO_{K3}) \cdot \chi(\cO_E) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0$)
and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$ (Mukai lattice rank).
The other two are \emph{algebraization invariants}, depending
on which chiral or BKM algebra is constructed from~$X$:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3 = \dim_\C X$ (modular
characteristic of the chiral de~Rham complex) and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ (weight of the Igusa cusp
form $\Delta_5$, so $2\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 10 = \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10})$).
The manifold invariants provide a fixed backdrop; the
algebraization invariants record which face of the geometry
a given construction captures.
The chiral-holographic pair
$(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}) = (3, 5)$ is
the one that sees the boundary--bulk axis of Volumes~I--II.
The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum is invisible from any single
chiral algebra; it is intrinsic to the CY category.

The $\En$-hierarchy provides the organizing spiral. A CY$_d$
category~$\cC$ carries a native $\Sd$-framing on its
Hochschild complex $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$, equipping
$\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ with an $E_d$-algebra structure. But the
\emph{chiral algebra} $\Phi(\cC)$ (a factorization algebra
on a curve) carries a \emph{lower} $\En$-level, determined
by the Gerstenhaber bracket degree $1 - d$: $\Einf$ at
$d = 1$, $\Etwo$ at $d = 2$, and $\Eone$ at $d \geq 3$.
At $d = 3$, the chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi_3(\cC)$ is
natively $\Eone$: ordered, not braided. The braiding is
\emph{constructed} (not recovered, not descended) via the
Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$. The mechanism is explicit
(Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}): the
Drinfeld center adjoins half-braidings
$\sigma_M \colon M \otimes (-) \xrightarrow{\sim} (-) \otimes M$
to each module $M$; the braiding on the center \emph{is}
the half-braiding,
$\beta_{M,N} = \sigma_M(N) \colon M \otimes N \to N \otimes M$;
evaluated on spectral-parameter families of modules
$V_u, V_v$, this gives the quantum group $R$-matrix
$R(z) = \sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$ with $z = u - v$.  The $R$-matrix
exists because $A$ is $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$: if $A$ were
already $\Etwo$, the half-braidings would be trivially given
and $R$ would reduce to the identity permutation.
The key arithmetic: $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$ (braid group,
native $\Etwo$, non-symmetric), while
$\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$
($S^{d-1}$ is simply connected): no topological braiding at all.
The \emph{unordered} configuration space
$\mathrm{UConf}_2(\R^d) = \Conf_2(\R^d)/S_2$ has
$\pi_1 = \Z/2$ (the symmetric group), but this permutation
braiding is trivially symmetric.
Dimension $d = 2$ is the sweet spot where the native
$\Etwo$-structure already carries a non-symmetric braiding.
For $d \geq 3$, the descent-and-center detour is mandatory:
one must pass to $\Eone$ and rebuild the braiding
categorically via the Drinfeld center.

The spiral, not circle, because the closing step (identifying
the derived $\Etwo$ with a genuine $\Etwo$-chiral algebra) is
the content of Theorem~CY-B, now proved at $d = 3$ via the
Verdier spectral functor. The trajectory
$E_d \to \Eone \to \Etwo$ (via the Drinfeld center) returns
to the same operadic level as $d = 2$, but the $\Etwo$
structure so obtained is \emph{derived}: it lives on the
representation category, not on the algebra itself. At $d = 2$
the spiral closes by construction: the native $\Etwo$-framing
and the Drinfeld center $\Etwo$ agree. At $d = 3$, the
functor $\Phi_3$ now exists (Theorem~CY-A$_3$), and
Theorem~CY-B$_3$ identifies the Koszul dual at every spectral
sequence page via the Verdier spectral functor
(Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}), so the
$\Eone$-chiral algebra, its Drinfeld center, and its Koszul
dual are all constructed. The spiral is the
honest shape of the $\En$-story: each CY dimension $d$ enters
at $E_d$, descends to $\Eone$, and ascends to $\Etwo$ through
the center; Theorem~CY-A guarantees the descent and ascent
exist at all~$d$; Theorem~CY-B closes the spiral at $d = 3$.

Three further structures emerge only through $\Phi$. First,
the bar complex converts enumerative geometry into homotopy
algebra: Donaldson--Thomas invariants become root
multiplicities of the Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra, and
the denominator identity appears as the bar Euler product
(proved for $d = 2$ via CY-A$_2$; for $K3 \times E$ via
CY-A$_3$ combined with the Vol~I Borcherds-lift identification).
Second, the $\Eone$-stabilization theorem: for $d \geq 3$,
the CY chiral output is natively $\Eone$. The Gerstenhaber
bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has degree $1 - d$; at
$d \geq 3$ this is $\leq -2$, too shifted to produce braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$). For toric
CY$_d$ ($\C^d$ with $\GL(d)$ action), the
$\GL(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets vanish
identically and all noncommutative structure arises from
the $\Omega$-deformation
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}); for non-toric CY$_3$
(the quintic, generic $K3 \times E$), the bracket is nonzero
but its shifted degree still prevents braiding. The braided
monoidal structure lives not in the algebra itself but in
the Drinfeld center of its $\Eone$-representation category;
the Drinfeld center is the right adjoint to the forgetful functor
$U \colon \Etwo\text{-}\Cat \to \Eone\text{-}\Cat$,
categorifying the algebraic center (commutant) of Volume~I. Third, the framing obstruction tower: the
effective obstruction
$\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \in \pi_d(BU)$ is $8$-periodic by
Bott periodicity and trivial precisely when
$d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$ (at $d \equiv 5 \bmod 8$, the
$\Sp$-refinement contributes a $\Z/2$-obstruction), giving a
rigid topological constraint on which CY dimensions admit
additional chiral structure beyond~$\Eone$.

\item \emph{Categorical}: the shadow tower produces numbers
 (Taylor coefficients of $\Theta_{A_\cC}$); the denominator
 identity is a function (an automorphic form on $\cH_2$).
\item \emph{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$--$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$
 mismatch}: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3 \neq 5 =
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$; the single-copy chiral algebra sees
 $\dim_\C X$, while the bulk reconstruction sees the
 Borcherds weight.
\item \emph{Second quantization}: the physical DT partition
 function is the DMVV symmetric product of single-copy data;
 the bar complex of a single algebra is the single copy.
\item \emph{Schottky}: at $g \geq 4$, the Torelli map
 $\cM_g \to \cA_g$ is no longer dominant, and the shadow
 tower is imprisoned in tautological classes on
 $\overline{\cM}_g$.
\end{enumerate}
Each obstruction points to a research programme. The
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$--$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ mismatch~(O2)
is the most telling: a single CY$_3$ admits multiple chiral
algebraizations, and the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum records
which face of the geometry each algebraization captures.

The functor $\Phi_2$ applied to $D^b(\Coh(K3))$ produces
\emph{one} chiral algebra: the Mukai-lattice Heisenberg
$H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-k3-explicit}).
This is the unique output of~$\Phi_2$; there is no ambiguity.
The invariant $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}}) = 2$
is the genus-$1$ bar coefficient of this specific algebra.

Separately, the K3 surface admits algebraic structures
from \emph{different constructions} that are not outputs
of~$\Phi$:
\begin{itemize}
\item The BKM superalgebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$, whose
 denominator identity gives the Igusa cusp form~$\Delta_5$
 of weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c(0)/2 = 5$; this is
 constructed from the Borcherds lift of the K3 elliptic
 genus, not from~$\Phi$.
\item The lattice VOA $V_\Lambda$ for each of the $24$
 Niemeier lattices; these arise from the lattice construction
 (Frenkel--Kac), not from~$\Phi$.
\item The Conway module from the Leech lattice; this arises
 from the FLM orbifold construction, not from~$\Phi$.
\end{itemize}
The manifold~$K3$ has topological invariants
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = \mathrm{rk}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}) = 24$
that are properties of the geometry alone, independent
of any construction. The \emph{construction-dependent}
invariants $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$
come from \emph{different} constructions:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$ from~$\Phi_2$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ from the Borcherds lift.
These are not ``competing algebraizations'' of a single
functor; they are distinct mathematical constructions
applied to the same manifold, producing distinct invariants.

$K3 \times E$. Together they constitute the most complete worked
example in the programme; we record all thirteen here, as the
pattern they form is itself a structural datum.

\noindent\textbf{The holomorphic Chern--Simons hierarchy.}
The theories studied in this volume form a hierarchy in real
dimension, each producing chiral algebras by restriction to
codimension-$2$ defects. At $d = 6$: holomorphic Chern--Simons
on a Calabi--Yau $3$-fold~$Y$ (Witten, Costello), the primary
source. A curve $C \subset Y$ of codimension~$2$ carries a defect
algebra: the $\Omega$-background equivariant parameters
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$ along the normal bundle $N_{C/Y}$
(of rank~$2$) supply two spectral parameters via equivariant
localization, and the defect algebra is a chiral algebra
on~$C$ with quantum group structure from the normal directions
(Proposition~\ref{prop:codim2-defect-ope}). For $Y = \C^3$ and
$C = \C$, the defect algebra is $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at
$\Psi = -\sigma_2(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ (the second elementary
symmetric polynomial in the $\Omega$-background parameters).
At $d = 5$: partially topological Chern--Simons on $\R \times Y$
for a CY$_2$ surface~$Y$; restriction to $\R \times C$ gives the
ordered bar with one spectral parameter. At $d = 4$: holomorphic
Chern--Simons on $\C^2$; the Costello--Witten--Yamazaki theory
that produces Yangians from holomorphic gauge fields. At $d = 3$:
the theories of Volume~II. The hierarchy
$6 \to 5 \to 4 \to 3$ is codimension-$2$ restriction at each
step; each restriction produces a chiral algebra from a gauge
theory, and the $E_n$ level decreases as the dimension drops.
The hierarchy is not limited to Chern--Simons: 6d little string
theory on K3 and M5 brane theory produce chiral algebras by the
same defect mechanism, with the specific gauge-theory content
replaced by stringy data.

\noindent\textbf{Concrete CY quantum groups.}
Volume~I introduces $E_1$-chiral quantum groups as abstract
algebraic-geometric objects: an ordered bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ with spectral $R$-matrix, Drinfeld
coproduct, and Yang--Baxter equation, all living on
configuration spaces of curves. This volume constructs the
concrete examples. The critical cohomological Hall algebra
$\CoHA(Q)$ of a quiver~$Q$ (with potential~$W$ from the CY
condition) is an $E_1$-chiral quantum group: its product is the
ordered Hall multiplication (graded by dimension vectors), its
$R$-matrix comes from the Ext-quiver, and its coproduct is the
CoHA vertex coproduct of Schiffmann--Vasserot. For the Jordan
quiver ($Q = \bullet \circlearrowleft$):
$\CoHA(Q) \cong Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (positive half),
with Drinfeld double
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \cong \cW_{1+\infty}$.
The Drinfeld center
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1))) \cong
\Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ recovers the full braided
structure (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}). For a general
toric CY$_3$ with fan~$\Sigma$: quiver charts indexed by
Bridgeland stability chambers give local chiral algebras;
wall-crossing is conjecturally MC gauge equivalence. The quantum
toroidal algebra $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ with CY
condition $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$ is the two-parameter deformation
carrying the full $\Z \times \Z$ bigrading from the double
current algebra. In the additive parametrization
$q_i = e^{h_i}$, the CY condition reads $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$:
two independent parameters survive, and the identification
$q_1 = q$, $q_2 = t^{-1}$, $q_3 = t/q$ recovers the standard
$(q,t)$-parametrization. The trigonometric structure function
\[
 G(x; q_1, q_2, q_3)
 = \frac{(1 - q_1 x)(1 - q_2 x)(1 - q_3 x)}
        {(1 - q_1^{-1} x)(1 - q_2^{-1} x)(1 - q_3^{-1} x)}
\]
satisfies the CY reflection identity
$G(x) \cdot G(1/x) = 1$ precisely when
$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$: the identity is equivalent to the CY
condition, and its failure measures the departure from
Calabi--Yau. The Miki automorphism (cyclic permutation of
$(q_1, q_2, q_3)$, generating the $S_3$-Weyl group of the CY
torus) is algebra-specific: it requires the concrete structure of
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ and does not follow from the
$E_3$-operad alone.

\noindent\textbf{Toric CY$_3$ and critical CoHAs.}
For a toric CY$_3$ determined by a fan~$\Sigma$, the toric
diagram fixes a quiver $Q_X$. The critical cohomological
Hall algebra $\CoHA(Q_X)$ is the $\Eone$-sector: the positive
half of an affine super Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\frakg}_{Q_X})$. The full braided structure is
recovered through the Drinfeld center. For $\C^3$: the
Jordan quiver gives
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$, and the chain
$\C^3 \to \cW_{1+\infty} \to
\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ is verified end-to-end.
The toric fan is the Dynkin data.

The CoHA is the natural $\Eone$-primitive: an associative
algebra whose Hall product provides the $m_2$ structure map
of the CY$_3$ chiral algebra. Its multiplication is encoded
in the bar differential of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$, but the
CoHA and the bar complex are different mathematical objects
(algebra vs coalgebra). The passage from CoHA to quantum group
is the CY incarnation of the $\Eone$-to-$\Etwo$ construction:
the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA))$ builds the
braided monoidal structure via half-braidings.
The algebraic underpinning of this passage (the integrable
structure of the ordered chiral bar complex, the R-matrix, and
the Yangian presentation) is developed in the companion paper
\emph{On the Integrable Theory of Chiral Yangians and Related
Chiral Quantum Groups} (Volume~I, standalone).

\noindent\textbf{The DDCA--toroidal bridge.}
The deformed double current algebra
$\mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K) = U(\mathfrak{gl}_K \otimes
\C[u,v])$ carries two spectral parameters $(u,v)$ from the two
holomorphic directions of $\C^2$ in the 5d noncommutative
Chern--Simons theory of Costello. The quantum toroidal algebra
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)$ carries two
deformation parameters $(q,t)$ from the two loop directions of
$\C^* \times \C^*$. Both degenerate to the affine Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K)$ in complementary limits: the DDCA
by specializing one spectral variable to zero (collapsing
$\C[u,v] \to \C[u]$); the toroidal algebra by sending $t \to 1$
(collapsing the outer loop). The conjecture
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:ddca-toroidal-bridge}) states the
bridge: writing $q = e^{\hbar_1}$, $t = e^{\hbar_2}$,
the DDCA is the rational ($\hbar_1, \hbar_2 \to 0$) degeneration
of the quantum toroidal algebra, in the category of filtered
algebras with the bidegree filtration on generators $J^a_{m,n}$.
The two one-parameter degenerations of $U_{q,t}$ produce a
commuting square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.6cm, row sep=0.8cm]
 U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)
 \ar[r, "t \to 1"] \ar[d, "q \to 1"']
 & Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K) \otimes U(\C[v])
  \ar[d, "\hbar_1 \to 0"] \\
 U(\C[u]) \otimes Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_K)
 \ar[r, "\hbar_2 \to 0"']
 & \mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
whose diagonal $\hbar_1 = \hbar_2 \to 0$ recovers the affine
Yangian as the common degeneration. The Koszul dual side has a
parallel identification: the double Yangian
$Y^{(2)}(\mathfrak{gl}_K)$ is the rational degeneration of
$U_{q^{-1}, t^{-1}}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_K)$,
consistent with the parameter inversion
$(q,t) \mapsto (q^{-1}, t^{-1})$ of the Koszul dual toroidal
algebra. The modular characteristic is continuous across the
bridge:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathrm{DDCA}_k(\mathfrak{gl}_K)) =
K(k+K)/2 = \lim_{\hbar_i \to 0}
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(U_{q,t})$.

\noindent\textbf{The Drinfeld centre as categorified center.}
Volume~I constructs the averaging map
$\mathrm{av} \colon \frakg^{E_1}_\cA \to
\frakg^{\mathrm{mod}}_\cA$ that projects the ordered
$R$-matrix to the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. This volume
constructs the categorical analogue (not a lift of averaging,
but the \emph{right adjoint to forgetful}): the Drinfeld centre
$Z \colon E_1\text{-}\Cat \to E_2\text{-}\Cat$. It takes the
$E_1$-monoidal representation category
$\Rep^{E_1}(A_\cC)$ to an $E_2$-braided monoidal category
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(A_\cC))$ by constructing universal
half-braidings $\sigma_M \colon M \otimes (-) \xrightarrow{\sim}
(-) \otimes M$. Direct restriction from $E_3$ to $E_2$
gives only symmetric braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^3)) = 0$); the Drinfeld centre is the sole
source of non-trivial braiding. For $\C^3$:
$Z(\Rep^{E_1}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))
\cong \Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))
\cong \Rep^{E_2}(\cW_{1+\infty})$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}). Five invariants match
at six levels: naive centre (dimension~$1$) versus derived centre
(dimension~$3$, capturing $\Ext^{1,2}$ invisible to the
commutant). The Drinfeld centre closes the $E_n$ circle
by recovering $E_2$-braiding from $E_1$-ordering.

\noindent\textbf{Wall-crossing as Maurer--Cartan gauge
equivalence.}\enspace
For a toric CY$_3$ with fan~$\Sigma$, Bridgeland stability
conditions carve the space of central charges into chambers.
Each chamber $\sigma$ determines a quiver presentation
$Q_\sigma$ and a critical CoHA
$\CoHA(Q_\sigma, W_\sigma)$. Wall-crossing (varying the
stability condition across a wall) changes the quiver
presentation but not the underlying CY category. The
conjecture: wall-crossing between chambers $\sigma$ and
$\sigma'$ is Maurer--Cartan gauge equivalence
$\Theta_\sigma \sim \Theta_{\sigma'}$ in the modular
convolution algebra of the CY chiral algebra. The global
chiral algebra $A_\cC$ would then be the homotopy colimit over
the chamber decomposition:
$A_\cC = \operatorname{hocolim}_\sigma A_{Q_\sigma}$.
For $\C^3$ (single chamber): the conjecture is vacuous. For
the resolved conifold $\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \P^1$
(two chambers): the wall-crossing formula is the flop relation
on DT partition functions (Bryan--Steinberg), and the MC
gauge equivalence is the bar-cobar transport across the flop.
This conjecture connects the algebraic programme of Volume~I
(Maurer--Cartan elements) to the enumerative programme
(DT/BPS wall-crossing) of this volume.

\;=\;

is expected to match the bar Euler product when the relevant

\noindent\textbf{Convergence and divergence.}
The shadow obstruction tower and the topological string free
energy produce genus-$g$ amplitudes with opposite analytic
behaviour. The shadow tower has Bernoulli decay
$|F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}| \sim (2\pi)^{-2g}/g$: convergent, with
radius~$2\pi$, Borel entire. The topological string has
factorial growth $|F_g^{\mathrm{top}}| \sim (2g)!$: divergent,
Gevrey-$1$, requiring non-perturbative completion. The
shadow is the algebraic soul: the part computable from OPE
structure constants alone, without worldsheet instantons.
For class~$\mathbf{G}$ algebras (Heisenberg, lattice VOAs),
the shadow is the full answer. For class~$\mathbf{M}$
(Virasoro, $\cW_{1+\infty}$), the shadow is a strict subtower,
and the instanton sum is infinite. Computing the instanton
corrections $F_g^{\mathrm{top}} - F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}$ requires
non-perturbative input: Borel resummation of the shadow tower,
BPS state counting, and (for $K3$-fibered CY$_3$) the Borcherds
product that packages the BKM root multiplicities into an
automorphic form.

The $\mathbf{G}$/$\mathbf{L}$/$\mathbf{C}$/$\mathbf{M}$
classification of Volume~I (shadow depth
$r = 2, 3, 4, \infty$) acquires a CY incarnation: the shadow
depth of $\Phi(\cC)$ is determined by the homological
complexity of~$\cC$, and the four classes reappear as
conditions on the Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence of
the CY category. Class~$\mathbf{G}$ corresponds to CY
categories with semisimple Hochschild cohomology
(e.g.\ $\Coh(E)$ for an elliptic curve); class~$\mathbf{M}$
to those with infinite algebraic depth (e.g.\ $D^b(K3)$,
whose chiral algebra $\Phi(K3)$ carries the full
Virasoro-type shadow tower).

\noindent\textbf{The $\Omega$-background and $E_1$ stabilization.}
The $E_1$ stabilization theorem: for CY$_d$ with
$d \ge 3$, the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has
degree $1 - d \leq -2$, too shifted to produce braiding. For
toric CY$_d$ ($\C^d$ with $\GL(d)$ action), the classical Lie
conformal algebra is abelian (SN brackets vanish by degree
overflow, Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}) and all
noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.
For non-toric CY$_3$, the bracket is nonzero but its shifted
degree still prevents braiding.
The $\Omega$-background (toric-specific) provides the concrete
mechanism: the equivariant parameters $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$
of the torus action on $\C^2 \subset Y$ break Dunn additivity
$E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$ by freezing one factor, producing
a single $E_1$ direction. At $\epsilon_1 = 0$, $\epsilon_2 \neq 0$
(the Nekrasov--Shatashvili limit): one $E_1$ factor survives and
the resulting algebra is the Yangian $Y(\fg)$, the quantum group
associated to the gauge theory. At generic
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$: the quantum toroidal algebra
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fg}})$ with
$q = e^{R\epsilon_1}$, $t = e^{-R\epsilon_2}$, and CY
condition $qt^{-1}(qt)^{1/2} = 1$ (for CY$_3$:
$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$). The $E_1$ stabilization at $d \geq 3$ is a general
consequence of CY dimension: the Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has degree $1 - d$, which for $d \geq 3$
is too negative to produce braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$, so ordered
configuration spaces carry no monodromy). For toric CY$_3$
manifolds, the $\Omega$-background provides a concrete
realization: the equivariant parameters
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3)$ break $U(3)$ to a
maximal torus $T^3$, restricting $E_3$ factorization on
$\C^3$ to $E_1$ factorization on each coordinate line. For
non-toric CY$_3$ (the quintic, generic $K3 \times E$), no
