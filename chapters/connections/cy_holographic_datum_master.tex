\begin{remark}[Two orthogonal sevens: arithmetic and algebraic]
\label{rem:cy-seven-face-two-orthogonal-sevens}
The seven $r_{\mathrm{CY}}$ faces catalogued in this chapter admit two
orthogonal slicings of a single chosen stage-$1$ factorisation datum
$\cF_{K3 \times E} := \PhiFA_3(D^b(K3 \times E))$ on the framed
$K3\times E$ locus.
The \emph{arithmetic seven}--Mukai lattice, Hodge supertrace, K3-fibre
rank, BKM Borcherds weight, Niemeier $23$-twist family,
Humbert boundary-monodromy, CHL twined $\bZ/N\bZ$-family--stratifies
into the three-tier hierarchy of the two-stage factorisation:
tier~(i) CY-datum intrinsics (Mukai lattice, Hodge supertrace);
tier~(ii) stage-1 invariants (K3-fibre rank
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$);
tier~(iii) $(\Sigma_2, C)$-specialisations (BKM Igusa,
Niemeier $23$-twist, Humbert boundary-monodromy, CHL twined $\bZ/N\bZ$).
The \emph{algebraic seven}--bar-cobar, CoHA, coisson, Maulik--Okounkov,
Yangian, Sklyanin, Gaudin--is a complementary slicing of the same
$\cF_{K3 \times E}$ by language: operadic, derived, Poisson, quantum,
classical-algebraic, elliptic, conformal-field-theoretic. One canonical
object, two seven-fold decompositions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Only tier-(iii) faces are \texorpdfstring{$\SpCh$}{SpCh}-siblings]
\label{rem:cy-seven-face-tier-iii-siblings}
The two orthogonal sevens do not compete. On a chosen framed
$d=3$ locus, the two-stage factorisation reads
$\Phi_3 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C} \circ \PhiFA_3$, and only tier-(iii) faces are
genuine siblings, arising as distinct framed outputs of
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}$ applied to the single stage-1 object
$\cF_{K3 \times E}$. Tier-(i) and tier-(ii) faces are invariants upstream
of any curve-specialisation. The arithmetic and algebraic sevens are not
rival presentations of the same seven objects; they are two organising
principles applied to $\cF_{K3 \times E}$, intersecting along tier-(iii)
where $\SpCh$-siblinghood is the common structural content.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three-tier stratification of the seven-face picture]
\label{rem:cy-seven-face-three-tier-opener}
The seven faces of this chapter are not all siblings of one type.
The two-stage factorisation stratifies them into three tiers.
\textbf{Tier~(i): CY-datum intrinsics.} Invariants read directly off
$(X, \cC, \Omega_\cC)$ without consulting any factorisation structure:
the Mukai lattice, the Hodge supertrace $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(X)
= \sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)$, the holomorphic volume class $[\Omega_\cC]$.
\textbf{Tier~(ii): stage-1 invariants of $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$.} Properties of the
canonical $\EdHolFA(X)$ object before any $\SpCh$-specialisation: the
native operadic level $n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level},
the stage-1 Hochschild cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ viewed as the input
surface of $\PhiFA_d$, the K3-fibre rank $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(K3 \times E) = 24$
(a Kodaira count of singular fibres of the elliptic K3 underneath $\cF_X$),
and the universal trace $\Phi$ acting on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$.
\textbf{Tier~(iii): $(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$-specialisations.} The genuine siblings
in the two-stage sense: outputs of distinct choices of $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$
applied to one stage-1 object. On K3~$\times$~E these include the BKM Igusa
specialisation $(K3, E) \mapsto \fg_{\Delta_5}$ with
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$; the Niemeier $23$-twist family indexed by
Conway--Norton root data; the Humbert boundary-monodromy limit at
$H_1 \cup H_4$; and the CHL twined $\bZ/N$ family at $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$
with universal Borcherds weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$.
Bare $r_{CY}(z)$ is an aspect of tier~(ii); the $K3 \times E$ package
$\{0, 3, 5, 24\}$ sorts one value per tier (tier~(i)
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = 0$ via Künneth and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}
= 0$; tier~(ii) $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}} = 3$; tier~(iii)
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$). Only tier-(iii) faces are
$\SpCh$-siblings; the algebraic seven-presentation
(Sections~\ref{sec:face1-bar-cobar}--\ref{sec:face7-cy3-gaudin})
is an orthogonal slicing by language, stratified further by the
three-tier partition (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-holdata-three-tier}).
\end{remark}

\section{The CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} collision residue}
\label{sec:cy3-collision-residue}

The collision residue is a single algebraic operation extracted from the
universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_A$ of a chiral algebra. In the
CY setting, this operation acquires geometric meaning: the residue is
computed against the holomorphic CY volume form, and the resulting binary
operation is the classical limit of the quantum vertex chiral group
braiding.

\begin{definition}[Binary collision residue, CY version]
\label{def:cy-collision-residue}
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper Calabi--Yau category of dimension~$d$ with
holomorphic volume form $\Omega_\cC$, and suppose the stage-$1$ object
$\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$ has been constructed on the chosen locus
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}, \S\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}). Let $A_\cC$
denote its stage-$2$ specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$,
the chiral algebra on a curve $C$ (Theorem~CY-A$_2$ at $d = 2$;
at $d = 3$ only after choosing the framed $\Phi_3$-specialisation datum
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}). Let
$\Theta_{A_\cC} \in \mathrm{MC}(\fg^{\mathrm{mod}}_{A_\cC})$
denote its universal Maurer--Cartan element (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:bar-cobar-adjunction}). The \emph{binary CY collision
residue} is
\[
 r_{CY}(z) \;:=\; \Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}\bigl(\Theta_{A_\cC}\bigr)
 \;\in\; A_\cC \otimes A_\cC \,[\![z^{-1}]\!],
\]
the genus-$0$, degree-$2$ residue along the collision divisor of
$\overline{C}_2(\Spec\C)$, paired against $\Omega_\cC$ in the CY
direction.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The CY direction is a separate slot]
\label{rem:cy-direction-slot}
The collision residue extracts the $z^{-1}$ part of $\Theta$ along the
chiral curve direction. In the CY setting, $\Theta$ has \emph{two}
gradings: degree in the chiral direction (controlled by the curve $X$)
and Hodge weight in the CY direction (controlled by~$\cO_\cC$). The CY
volume form $\Omega_\cC$ kills the Hodge weight by integration; what
remains is a chiral binary operation valued in $A_\cC \otimes A_\cC$.
This is the precise sense in which $r_{CY}$ couples the two directions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three invariants must not be conflated]
\label{rem:cy-three-invariants}
For any chiral algebra $A$, the binary residue carries three
\emph{distinct} numerical invariants which the CY setting will
illuminate:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $p_{\max}(A)$, the maximal pole order of generator-on-generator
OPEs (cf.\ Vol~I, Definition~\ref{def:p-max});
\item $k_{\max}(A) = p_{\max}(A) - 1$, the collision depth of $r_{CY}$
itself, after $d\log$-absorption (cf.\ Vol~I,
Definition~\ref{def:k-max});
\item $r_{\max}(A)$, the shadow depth, the degree at which the
obstruction tower $\Theta_A^{\leq r}$ terminates (Vol~I,
Definition~\ref{def:r-max}).
\end{enumerate}
The $\beta\gamma$ system is the archetypal witness of their independence:
$p_{\max}(\beta\gamma) = 1$, $k_{\max}(\beta\gamma) = 0$,
$r_{\max}(\beta\gamma) = 4$ (class~C). Conflating these produces
incorrect classifications, and the seven-face programme will distinguish
them at every face.
\end{remark}

\section{Face 1: the CY bar-cobar twisting morphism}
\label{sec:face1-bar-cobar}

The first face is the home framework of this volume. The CY-to-chiral
functor sends a Calabi--Yau category $\cC$ to a chiral algebra
$A_\cC$, and the bar construction sends $A_\cC$ to a factorization
coalgebra $\barB(A_\cC)$ on $\Ran(X)$. The collision residue is the
binary component of the universal twisting morphism between
$\barB(A_\cC)$ and $A_\cC$.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Coordinates.} Level~$2$ \textup{(}Stage-$2$
chart-shadow read through bar twisting\textup{)}, with descent from
level~$1$ \textup{(}Stage-$1$ factorisation bar\textup{)} via the
two-bar comparison of Remark~\ref{rem:face1-two-bars}; chart any
$(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$ on the verified locus; ambient ordinary chain
complex at $d = 2$, weight-completed for class~$\mathsf M$ on
$\barB$ at $d = 3$ \textup{(}Pattern~$236$ ambient qualifier\textup{)}.

\begin{remark}[Two bar constructions, one per stage]
\label{rem:face1-two-bars}
Face~1 uses two distinct bar constructions, one per stage of
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$, and each statement
of this section labels which lane it occupies.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \textbf{Stage-1 factorisation bar.} On $\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$
the relevant bar is the $E_d$-hFA factorisation bar: the homotopy
$E_d$-coalgebra $\barB^{\,\mathrm{fact}}_{E_d}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ computed by
the factorisation-algebra cobar resolution in $\EdHolFA(X)$. This is an
invariant of $\cC$ before any curve-specialisation and records the native
operadic level $n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}.
\item \textbf{Stage-2 chiral ordered bar.} After specialising to a curve
$C \subset X$ via $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$, the $E_1$-chiral ordered bar
of Vol~I applies to $A_\cC \in \EnHolFA(C)$: the tensor-coalgebra
$T^c(s^{-1} \overline{A_\cC})$ on the augmentation ideal, with the
deconcatenation coproduct. This is the bar complex featured in Volume~I
and the object on which the Vol~I Koszul reflection acts.
\end{enumerate}
The binary CY collision residue $r_{CY}(z)$ is extracted at stage~2 from
the universal twisting morphism between the stage-2 chiral ordered bar
$\barB(A_\cC)$ and $A_\cC$; its existence as a stage-1 aspect of
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ is the statement that this residue descends from the
factorisation bar through the $\SpCh$-specialisation. Both bars are
load-bearing; neither replaces the other.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Face 1: CY bar-cobar realization, \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}]
\label{thm:face1-cy-bar-cobar}
Let $A_\cC$ be the chiral algebra of a CY$_2$ category $\cC$ produced by
the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_2$ (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The convolution dg Lie algebra $\fg^{\mathrm{mod}}_{A_\cC}$
identifies with the homotopy Lie algebra of twisting morphisms
$\mathrm{hom}_\alpha(\barB(A_\cC), A_\cC)$ between bar coalgebra and
chiral algebra (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:bar-cobar-adjunction});
\item The universal MC element $\Theta_{A_\cC}$ is the universal
twisting morphism;
\item The binary collision residue $r_{CY}(z) =
\Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_\cC})$ is the leading
degree-$2$ component of this twisting morphism, evaluated in the
CY direction against $\Omega_\cC$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For~(i), the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ identifies
with $\barB(A_\cC)$ as a factorization coalgebra
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-dictionary}). The convolution identification is
the application of Vol~I's master twisting-morphism theorem to this
coalgebra-algebra pair. For~(ii), $\Theta_{A_\cC}$ satisfies the MC
equation $D\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta] = 0$ at all levels, with
the bar-intrinsic construction $\Theta = D - d_0$. For~(iii), the
collision residue is computed from $\Theta$ by restricting to genus~$0$,
degree~$2$, and pairing against the holomorphic volume form.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Face 1: CY bar-cobar realization, \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3}]
\label{thm:face1-cy-bar-cobar-d3}
The statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:face1-cy-bar-cobar} extends to
$d = 3$ on the framed CY$_3$ locus of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}. If $\cC$ is a smooth proper
CY$_3$ category equipped with a framed specialisation datum
$(\Sigma_2,C)$ for which
$A_\cC := \SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ is defined, then the convolution
identification, the universal MC element, and the binary collision
residue extraction of Theorem~\ref{thm:face1-cy-bar-cobar}(i)--(iii)
hold for this framed output $A_\cC$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Bar-cobar inversion is not the seven-face master move]
\label{rem:no-cobar-bulk-confusion}
The cobar functor $\Omega$ inverts the bar complex: $\Omega(\barB(A))
\simeq A$ (Vol~I, Theorem~B). This is a round-trip that recovers $A$
itself. The seven-face programme is not about recovering $A$ from $\barB(A)$;
it is about realizing a single algebraic object, the binary residue
$r_{CY}(z)$, in seven distinct geometric languages. The closed-string
or bulk side is the chiral derived center $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$
(Vol~I, Theorem~H), not the cobar; the seven faces all live on the
boundary~$A_\cC$ side.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[BPS shadow depth and the CY holographic datum]
\label{rem:bps-shadow-depth-cy}
The holographic modular Koszul datum in the CY setting assembles the six
pieces of the seven-face programme into a single package:
\[
 H_{CY}(T) \;=\; \bigl(A_{CY},\; A_{CY}^{!},\; \cC_{CY},\; r_{CY}(z),\;
 \Theta_{CY},\; \nabla^{\mathrm{hol}}_{CY}\bigr),
\]
where $A_{CY}$ is the constructed stage-$2$ CY-to-chiral image $A_\cC$,
$A_{CY}^{!}$ is its Verdier/Koszul dual boundary algebra, $\cC_{CY}$ is the line
category of the HT theory, $r_{CY}(z)$ is the binary CY collision residue
of Definition~\ref{def:cy-collision-residue}, $\Theta_{CY}$ is the
universal Maurer--Cartan element (Theorem~\ref{thm:face1-cy-bar-cobar}),
and $\nabla^{\mathrm{hol}}_{CY}$ is the holomorphic Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov
connection on the chiral curve direction. This is the CY specialization
of the Vol~I datum $H(T) = (A, A^{!}, C, r(z), \Theta_A, \nabla^{\mathrm{hol}})$;
the additional datum is the CY direction $\Omega_\cC$
(Remark~\ref{rem:cy-direction-slot}).
The BPS/Hilbert-space clauses in this paragraph are mathematical
statements only after the protected physical comparison datum of
Definition~\ref{def:protected-trace-functor-package} has been supplied.

Dimofte's framework (PIRSA 25110067) attaches to $H_{CY}(T)$ a single
numerical invariant, the \emph{BPS complexity} of the boundary Hilbert
space, measured by the $K$-matrix $K_{A_{CY}}(z)$ of
equation~\eqref{eq:dimofte-k-matrix-cy}
(cf.\ Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-chiral-algebras},
Remark~\ref{rem:dimofte-k-matrix-cy}, and \emph{Vol~II}, remark
\texttt{rem:dimofte-k-matrix} in
\texttt{ordered\_associative\_chiral\_kd\_core.tex}). As a
physics-motivated heuristic (Dimofte framework, no chiral-algebra
proof), the boundary Hilbert space of the HT theory decomposes as
\[
 \cH_{\partial}(T) \;\simeq\; \cH_{\partial}^{\mathrm{free}} \otimes
 \cH_{\partial}^{\mathrm{BPS}},
\]
where $\cH_{\partial}^{\mathrm{free}}$ is the $\mathfrak{u}(1)^r$ free-boson
factor (the class $\mathbf{G}$ summand) and $\cH_{\partial}^{\mathrm{BPS}}$
records the BPS corrections. Under the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$, this
decomposition is the Vol~I shadow partition $\mathbf{G} +
(\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{M})$: the free-boson summand is exactly
class $\mathbf{G}$, and the BPS summand carries the higher shadow depth.

\paragraph{CY\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{-2} (K3, protected sector).} For a CY$_2$ category $\cC$ admitting a
chiral algebraization via $\Phi$, the protected BPS Hilbert space is
modelled by the associated Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra after the
physical comparison datum is supplied,
$\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\cC)$, and the $K$-matrix encodes its
denominator identity. Concretely, for $\cC = D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(K3))$ the
BKM denominator formula (Gritsenko--Nikulin, Borcherds) identifies the
primitive denominator algebra with \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\), whose
signed root supermultiplicities are the Fourier coefficients of the
Jacobi input \(\phi_{0,1}\). The dyonic scalar partition function uses
the OP-normalised square
\(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2\); the
\(K\)-matrix modes \(\{\alpha_n\}\) read root multiplicities from the
primitive denominator, not from the scalar square alone.

\paragraph{Automorphic product normalisation.} The numerical identity
\[
 \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}) \;=\; 2\,\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}
 \;=\; 10
\]
factors as two separate primary-source statements. First, by Borcherds
1998 (\emph{Invent.\ Math.} 132, Theorem~13.3) the BKM weight formula
gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$; at $N=1$ the
Gritsenko paramodular form $\Delta_5$ of weight~$5$ has $c_1(0) = 10$,
so $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$. The primitive denominator remains
$\Delta_5$; the monic Gritsenko--Nikulin product is
$D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5$. In the Oberdieck--Pandharipande/Oberdieck--Pixton
scalar normalisation,
\[
 \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}
 = D_5^2
 = 4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
 \qquad
 Z^{X}_{\mathrm{DT}}=Z^{X}_{\mathrm{OP}}
 =-\bigl(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}\bigr)^{-1}
 =-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The factor of~$2$ in
$\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})/\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is this
squaring, not Serre duality; the factor $4096=64^2$ is a scalar
normalisation and carries no compact Hall or Pfaffian orientation data.
This matches entry $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(K3 \times E)=5$ in the
Vol~III $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-spectrum and the weight
$\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})=10$ recorded in
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e}.

\paragraph{CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} (framed \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3$}{Phi-3} locus).} For a CY$_3$ category the
BPS Hilbert space should be the module over the Donaldson--Thomas Hall
algebra (Kontsevich--Soibelman, Davison--Meinhardt), and the $K$-matrix
should be the generating function of the cohomological Hall algebra
$\cH(Q, W)$ attached to a quiver-with-potential presentation. For toric
CY$_3$ without compact $4$-cycles (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}) this is
explicit: Schiffmann--Vasserot identify $\cH(\C^3)$ with the positive
half of the affine Yangian $Y^{+}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)$, and the
$K$-matrix is its character $M(q)^2 \cdot P(q)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:drinfeld-center-coha}). All statements in this paragraph
whose proof chain passes through $A_\cC$ at $d = 3$ are restricted to the
chosen framed $\Phi_3$-specialisation locus of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}.

\begin{definition}[Pure mathematical holographic bridge datum]
\label{def:pure-math-holographic-bridge-datum}
Let $X$ be a CY$_3$ target for which the positive Hall side and a
framed $d=3$ chiral output are both defined. A \emph{pure mathematical
holographic bridge datum} is the following package:
\[
 \mathfrak H_X^{\mathrm{mat}}
 =
 \bigl(\mathsf{BPS}_X,\mathsf{Bdry}_X,\mathcal H_X,
 \lambda_X,\operatorname{ch}_X,\omega_X,\eta_X\bigr).
\]
Here $\mathsf{BPS}_X$ is a charge-graded stable category of BPS objects
with Kontsevich--Soibelman Hall product and orientation line
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}}$; $\mathsf{Bdry}_X$ is the boundary
factorisation category of the chiral algebra
$A_X=\SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\Perf X))$ on the chosen framed locus;
\[
 \mathcal H_X\colon \mathsf{BPS}_X \longrightarrow \mathsf{Bdry}_X
\]
is an exact charge-preserving functor; $\lambda_X$ is an isometry from
the numerical charge lattice of $\mathsf{BPS}_X$ to the root/weight
lattice seen by $A_X$; $\operatorname{ch}_X$ is an index-character
map
\[
 K_0(\mathsf{BPS}_X,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}})
 \longrightarrow
 K_0(\mathsf{Bdry}_X)
\]
intertwining the BPS index with the graded chiral character;
$\omega_X$ is a trivialisation of the orientation line compatible with
Hall convolution; and $\eta_X$ is the coherence square asserting that
wall-crossing on $\mathsf{BPS}_X$ maps to Maurer--Cartan gauge
equivalence of $\Theta_{A_X}$.

The datum is \emph{complete} only if $\mathcal H_X$ carries the Hall
product to the boundary OPE/factorisation product and intertwines the
bulk coproduct with the chiral Drinfeld-centre half-braiding, with all
trace maps taken in compactly supported $K$-theory. For non-proper
BPS stacks this means the same Borel--Moore or renormalised
$!$-pushforward used in the Hall product. The charge lattice and
index-character components alone are arithmetic shadows of the bridge;
they are not the bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite Rees hCS--Hall comparison datum]
\label{def:finite-rees-hcs-hall-datum}
Let $X$ be a CY$_3$ target and fix a Harder--Narasimhan height
filtration $F_{\le N}$ on the effective charge cone. A
\emph{finite Rees hCS--Hall comparison datum} is a compatible family
\[
 \mathcal R^{\hCS\text{-}\Hall}_{X,\le N}\colon
 \operatorname{Rees}_{F}\!\bigl(F_{\le N}\Obs_{\hCS}(X)\bigr)
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Rees}_{F}\!\bigl(F_{\le N}\CoHA_{\mathrm{eff}}(X)\bigr)
\]
intertwining the tree hCS bracket, the finite Hall correspondence, and
the orientation line on every truncated correspondence stack. The datum
is \emph{finite}: it compares actual finite windows before the
compact-support completion, before the Hall coproduct is promoted to a
bialgebra, and before the Drinfeld double is mapped to a BKM
denominator algebra.

Its compact realisation obstruction is the ordered tuple
\[
 \mathfrak{o}^{\mathrm{comp}}_X
 =
 \bigl(o_{\mathrm{prop}},o_{\mathrm{or}},o_{\Delta},
 o_{\widehat{F}},o_{\mathrm{BKM}}\bigr),
\]
where the components respectively measure failure of proper or
renormalised Hall pushforward, failure of orientation-line descent,
failure of the coproduct to preserve compact support, failure of the
Rees filtration to complete without adding uncontrolled charge sectors,
and failure of the completed Hall--Drinfeld double to carry the
Borcherds denominator normalisation.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Rees comparison versus compact Hall double]
\label{prop:finite-rees-vs-compact-hall-double}
A finite Rees hCS--Hall comparison datum on \(X\) proves only the
truncated hCS/Hall compatibility on the finite windows \(F_{\le N}\).
It produces a compact BKM-valued trace precisely when
\[
 \mathfrak{o}^{\mathrm{comp}}_X=0
\]
and the resulting compact Hall algebra admits a Drinfeld double map
\[
 \mathcal D_{\hbar}\!\left(\CoHA_X^{c}\right)
 \longrightarrow
 \mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}
\]
whose denominator is normalised by
\(\operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=D_5(2Z)
=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Rees datum is built before completion. It records, for each
finite height, the compatibility of two filtered products: the hCS tree
bracket and the Hall convolution product on the corresponding finite
correspondence stacks. Passing from these finite products to a compact
trace requires the \( ! \)-pushforward defining Hall multiplication to
exist in the same compact-support theory used by the trace; this is
exactly \(o_{\mathrm{prop}}\). The BPS index is oriented, so descent of
the orientation line is \(o_{\mathrm{or}}\). The Drinfeld double requires
a coproduct compatible with the compact support condition, giving
\(o_{\Delta}\). Completion in charge height can add sectors invisible in
every finite window; \(o_{\widehat{F}}\) is the assertion that it does
not. After those four obstructions vanish, the target is still only a
compact Hall double. The final map to
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) and the denominator normalisation are the
separate obstruction \(o_{\mathrm{BKM}}\). Thus finite Rees
compatibility is necessary for the compact bridge but never sufficient
for the compact CoHA/Drinfeld-double/BKM conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Protected trace functor package]

is an exact symmetric monoidal functor from compactly supported

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Holographic bridge gate]
\label{thm:holographic-bridge-gate}
Let $X$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:pure-math-holographic-bridge-datum}.
If a complete pure mathematical holographic bridge datum
$\mathfrak H_X^{\mathrm{mat}}$ is supplied, then every identity of
oriented BPS indices in $\mathsf{BPS}_X$ transfers functorially to an
identity of graded characters and collision residues on the boundary
chiral algebra $A_X$. In particular the denominator/product formula,
wall-crossing invariance, and large-charge asymptotic statement become
theorems of the CY-to-chiral programme.

Conversely, the transfer from a physical holographic statement to a
pure CY-to-chiral theorem is not derivable from the CY-to-chiral axioms
unless the product comparison, orientation-line compatibility, and
wall-crossing coherence parts of
$\mathfrak H_X^{\mathrm{mat}}$ are present. Thus AdS/CFT, black-hole,
M-theory, or celestial arguments may supply proved identities in the
physical theory, but their CY-to-chiral consequences require the
explicit bridge datum above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is functoriality. The Hall product on
$\mathsf{BPS}_X$ is a convolution product built from the correspondence
stack of short exact triangles. The boundary product on
$\mathsf{Bdry}_X$ is the factorisation/OPE product along the collision
strata of the chosen curve. If $\mathcal H_X$ carries the Hall
correspondence to the collision correspondence and $\omega_X$ trivialises
the orientation line compatibly with convolution, then the equality of
oriented indices is preserved by the induced map on $K_0$. The
index-character map $\operatorname{ch}_X$ turns the same equality into a
graded character equality. The Drinfeld-centre compatibility identifies
the coproduct/half-braiding side with the collision residue
$\Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_X})$. The wall-crossing square
$\eta_X$ says that a Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing automorphism on
the BPS side maps to MC gauge equivalence of $\Theta_{A_X}$; hence the
transferred character and residue are chamber-independent.

For the converse, remove any one of the three load-bearing components.
Without the product comparison there is no morphism carrying Hall
multiplication to OPE multiplication. Without the orientation
trivialisation the BPS index and the chiral trace live in different
twisted Grothendieck groups. Without wall-crossing coherence the
transferred class depends on a chamber and therefore cannot be a global
CY-to-chiral invariant. A physics duality may still compute the BPS
index, but no pure mathematical implication to $A_X$ has been
constructed. This proves the gate criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Protected index versus protected trace functor]
\label{thm:protected-index-vs-trace-functor}
Let \(X\) be a CY$_3$ target on a framed \(d=3\) locus, and let
\[
 \operatorname{ind}^{\mathrm{BPS}}_X\colon
 K_0^c(\mathsf{BPS}_X,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}})
 \longrightarrow R_X
\]
be the compactly supported oriented protected index. A complete
protected trace functor package
\(\mathfrak T_X^{\mathrm{prot}}\) promotes protected-index identities
to functorial chiral trace identities: products, orientation signs,
wall-crossing, charge gradings, and compact-support pushforwards are
preserved, and the BKM-valued trace is available precisely when the
Hall--Drinfeld double and Borcherds denominator normalisation are also
part of the package.

Conversely, an equality of scalar protected indices
\[
 \operatorname{ind}^{\mathrm{BPS}}_X(\alpha)
 =
 \operatorname{tr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_X(\beta)
 \qquad (\alpha\in K_0^c(\mathsf{BPS}_X,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}}))
\]
does not determine a functor
\(\operatorname{Tr}^{\mathrm{prot}}_X\), nor a Hall-to-OPE product
comparison, nor a BKM trace. Any argument factoring only through the
scalar character ring is blind to classes in
\(\ker(\operatorname{ind}^{\mathrm{BPS}}_X)\), to orientation-line
twists, to compact-support failures of the Hall \( ! \)-pushforward, and
to chamber changes killed by the numerical index. Hence protected-index
equality is a character-level statement unless the full package of
Definition~\ref{def:protected-trace-functor-package} is supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that
\(\mathfrak T_X^{\mathrm{prot}}\) is complete. Exactness gives a map on
compactly supported Grothendieck groups. Symmetric monoidality sends
external tensor products of protected sectors to external tensor
products of chiral trace objects, and the product diagram identifies the
pushforward defining Hall convolution with the collision pushforward
defining the boundary OPE. The orientation diagram removes the twist by
\(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}}\); the wall-crossing diagram identifies
Kontsevich--Soibelman automorphisms with MC gauge transformations of
\(\Theta_{A_X}\); and the compact-support diagram says that the trace is
taken after the same \( ! \)-pushforward on both sides. The induced
identity in \(K_0^c(\mathsf{Tr}^{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X))\) is therefore
functorial and product-preserving. If the target is BKM-valued, the
Hall--Drinfeld double map supplies the algebra target and the
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=c_N(0)/2\) normalisation fixes the denominator
trace.

For the converse, let an argument use only the equality of scalar
indices. The scalar map factors through the quotient of
\(K_0^c(\mathsf{BPS}_X,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{or}})\) by the kernel of
\(\operatorname{ind}^{\mathrm{BPS}}_X\). It therefore cannot distinguish
\(\alpha\) from \(\alpha+u\) for \(u\) in that kernel. Such a quotient
forgets the Hall correspondence, the orientation line, the
compact-support pushforward, and the chamber action unless separate
commuting diagrams remember them. The missing diagrams are exactly the
projection, orientation, product, wall-crossing, compact-support,
charge-lattice, and BKM-double gates in
Definition~\ref{def:protected-trace-functor-package}. The index
equality can be a proved character identity; it is not, by itself, a
functorial protected trace.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Face 2: CoHA realization of \texorpdfstring{$r_{CY}$}{r-CY}]

 r_{CY}^{\mathrm{CoHA}}(z) \;=\; \mu_{\cH(Q,W)}^{(2)} \otimes z^{-1}
 \;\in\; \cH(Q,W) \otimes \cH(Q,W) \,[\![z^{-1}]\!],

\begin{remark}[Obstruction in Conjecture~\ref{conj:face2-coha-realization}]

\begin{remark}[Perverse coherent sheaves]

\begin{theorem}[Face 3: classical Poisson coisson, \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2} \textup{(}genus~\texorpdfstring{$0$}{0} only\textup{)}]

algebraization (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). Let $\hbar$ denote a quantization
parameter. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

$\{\cdot,\cdot\}_\lambda$ valued in $A_\cC^{\mathrm{cl}} \otimes

\item The leading coefficient $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_\lambda^{(0)}$ of the

\item This identifies $r_{CY}^{\mathrm{cl}}$ as the coisson bracket
constructed from the CY volume form $\Omega_\cC$ via the Poisson
structure on $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC^{\mathrm{cl}})$
(cf.\ Vol~I, Theorem~\ref{thm:kz-classical-quantum-bridge}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For a CY$_2$ category, $\Phi_2$ is the proved CY-to-chiral correspondence at $d=2$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}). The PVA structure on $A_\cC^{\mathrm{cl}}$
is the classical limit of the chiral OPE. The leading $\lambda$
coefficient of the $\lambda$-bracket is the contraction of two factors
along the binary collision divisor; this is exactly
$\Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}$ computed in the classical limit, hence
$r_{CY}^{\mathrm{cl}}$. The identification with the coisson bracket
arising from $\Omega_\cC$ is the coisson--PVA dictionary
(cf.\ Vol~I, Theorem~\ref{thm:kz-classical-quantum-bridge}, applied to
the specialization $\cC \mapsto A_\cC$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Face 4: Maulik--Okounkov \texorpdfstring{$R$}{R}-matrix as cocycle residue]

be the torus preserving the CY$_3$ structure, $\cW \subset
\mathfrak{t}^*_\C$ the equivariant wall arrangement, and
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ the UV positive-half gluing cocycle of
Construction~\ref{constr:transverse-r-matrix-cocycle}. The
Maulik--Okounkov $R$-matrix is the residue of $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$
across an equivariant chamber wall,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mo-residue}
 R^{\mathrm{MO}}(u) \;=\; \mathrm{Res}_{u = u_\star}
 \phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(u),
 \qquad u = u_1 - u_2,
\end{equation}
at any chamber wall $u_\star$, independent of the chamber choice up
to unitarity. The Maulik--Okounkov axiom \textup{(}quantum
Yang--Baxter equation $+$ unitarity\textup{)} is the
\v{C}ech-cocycle condition for $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ on the
codimension-two and codimension-one wall intersections respectively
\textup{(}Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-equals-cocycle}\textup{)}. The binary
CY collision residue is the classical limit
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rcy-from-mo}
 r_{CY}^{K3 \times E}(z) \;=\;
 \lim_{\hbar \to 0} \hbar^{-1}\bigl(R^{\mathrm{MO}}(z) - \mathrm{id}\bigr)
 \;=\;
 \lim_{\hbar \to 0} \hbar^{-1}\,\mathrm{Res}_{z = z_\star}
 \phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(z),
\end{equation}
the rational/elliptic stable envelope of Maulik--Okounkov evaluated
on the Hilbert scheme of points on $K3$ along the elliptic direction
$E$, paired against the holomorphic volume form $\Omega_{K3 \times E}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cocycle--residue identification \eqref{eq:mo-residue} is
Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-equals-cocycle}\,(i)--(iii) on
$K^\bullet_T(\Hilb^\bullet(K3))$: the residue of the meromorphic
$1$-form $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ at a wall $\alpha$ equals the
hyperbolic-restriction $r_\alpha$, which is the chamber-transition
matrix
$\mathrm{Stab}_{\cK_2}^+ \circ (\mathrm{Stab}_{\cK_1}^+)^{-1} =
R^{\mathrm{MO}}(\alpha)$ \cite[Theorem~$4.6.1$]{MaulikOkounkov2012}.
The quantum Yang--Baxter equation is the cocycle condition at
codimension-two wall triple-intersections; unitarity is the
cocycle-reversal identity. The classical-limit identification
\eqref{eq:rcy-from-mo} is Definition~\ref{def:cy-collision-residue}
applied after specialising to the elliptic direction.
\end{proof}

\section{Face 5: the affine super Yangian \texorpdfstring{$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$}{Y(widehatfgl-1)} for toric CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}}
\label{sec:face5-yangian}

The fifth face is the explicit toric face: when the CY$_3$ is toric
and admits a quiver-with-potential presentation, the binary residue
is the classical $r$-matrix of the affine super Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ \textup{(}for $\C^3$, the Jordan quiver\textup{)}
or more generally $Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$ \textup{(}for the toric
quiver of~$X$\textup{)}.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Coordinates.} Level~$2$ on the toric chart, descended
from the level-$3$ Drinfeld double of the level-$1$ CoHA $Y^+(X)$;
chart toric $T^d$ on $X$ without compact $4$-cycles, $\Sigma_2$ a
toric codimension-$1$ cycle, $C$ the toric reference curve;
ambient rational shuffle algebra $+$ Drinfeld--Yangian double.

\begin{theorem}[Face 5 for toric CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} via RSYZ]
\label{thm:face5-toric-yangian-rsyz}
Let $X$ be a toric CY$_3$ without compact $4$-cycles, with toric quiver
$(Q_X, W_X)$ and affine super Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz}). Then the binary CY collision residue
identifies with the classical $r$-matrix:
\[
 r_{CY}^{X}(z) \;=\; r_{Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})}^{\mathrm{cl}}(z)
 \;=\; \frac{\Psi\,\Omega_{Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})}}{z}
 \;+\; (\text{higher poles dictated by } p_{\max}(W_X)),
\]
where $\Omega_{Y(\widehat{\fg}_{Q_X})}$ is the Casimir tensor of the
affine super Yangian. At the Jordan-quiver specialization $X = \C^3$,
the CoHA input is the positive half
$Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$; the $\cW_{1+\infty}$ vertex algebra appears only
after passing to the Drinfeld double/centre and evaluating the vacuum
module at the self-dual level.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Proof support for face 5]
\label{rem:face5-proof-support}
Theorem~\ref{thm:face5-toric-yangian-rsyz} is a restatement of the
Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz}) in the
collision-residue language of the seven-face programme. The classical
$r$-matrix content is due to Schiffmann--Vasserot (for $\C^3$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:sv-c3}) and Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao (for general
toric without compact $4$-cycles, Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz}). The local
translation carried by Proposition~\ref{prop:face5-c3-translation} is
the identification of the Casimir with
$\Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_X})$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Collision-residue translation of face 5, \texorpdfstring{$\C^3$}{C-3} case]
\label{prop:face5-c3-translation}
For $X = \C^3$, the Schiffmann--Vasserot Casimir tensor
$\Omega_{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}$ coincides with
$\Res_{z=0}\bigl[r_{CY}^{\C^3}(z)\bigr]$, where $r_{CY}^{\C^3}$ is the
binary CY collision residue of the $\C^3$ chiral algebra.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The Jordan quiver superpotential $W = \tr(XYZ - XZY)$ has
$p_{\max}(W) = 1$ (each cubic monomial contributes a single contraction
in the binary OPE), so $k_{\max} = 0$ and $r_{CY}^{\C^3}(z)$ has a
\emph{single} pole at $z = 0$. The residue at this pole is the
Schiffmann--Vasserot Casimir
$\Omega_{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)} \in Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)^{\otimes 2}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sv-c3}). By the universality of $\Theta$ in MC moduli,
this is exactly $r_{CY}^{\C^3}$. The statement is made on the positive
half \(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\); the \(\cW_{1+\infty}\) vertex algebra
appears only after adjoining the negative half, passing to the
Drinfeld double/centre, and applying the vacuum evaluation. The matching
of higher poles with the toric quiver superpotential follows from the
RSYZ theorem for toric quivers without compact $4$-cycles.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three invariants for toric CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}]

\subsection{Gaudin Hamiltonians for toric CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} from collision residues}

The full development of face~7 occupies

\begin{construction}[Gaudin Hamiltonians from \texorpdfstring{$r_{CY}^{\C^3}$}{r-CY-C-3}]
\label{constr:c3-gaudin-from-residue}

\[
 H_i^{\C^3} \;:=\; \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{k\,\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}}{z_i - z_j}
 \;\in\; Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)^{\otimes n},
\]
where $\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}$ is the $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$
Casimir $\Res_{z=0} r_{CY}^{\C^3}(z)$, acting on the $i$-th and $j$-th
tensor factors. The operators $\{H_i^{\C^3}\}$ commute pairwise as a
direct consequence of the classical Yang--Baxter equation for
$r_{CY}^{\C^3}$ (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaudin-yangian-identification}).
\end{construction}

\section{Face 6: elliptic Sklyanin bracket (for toroidal CY)}
\label{sec:face6-sklyanin}

The sixth face is the elliptic deformation: when the chiral curve is
itself an elliptic curve $E_\tau$ and the CY data are toroidal, the
binary residue acquires an elliptic structure governed by theta
functions. The classical limit of this $r$-matrix is the elliptic
Sklyanin bracket, and the quantization is Felder's elliptic quantum
group.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Coordinates.} Level~$2$; chart toroidal CY with
$C = E_\tau$ \textup{(}elliptic chiral curve\textup{)}, $\Sigma_2$ a
toroidal codimension-$1$ cycle compatible with the elliptic
fibration; ambient elliptic-quantum-group $E_{q, p}(\fsl_2)$ with
dynamical $B$-cycle monodromy $\lambda$.

The elliptic $r$-matrix, its classical Sklyanin bracket, and the
quantization to Felder's elliptic quantum group
(Definition~\ref{def:elliptic-quantum}) are classical inputs from
Sklyanin (1983), Belavin--Drinfeld (1984), and Felder. The comparison
made here is the identification of the elliptic $r$-matrix with the
binary CY collision residue
$\Res^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_\cC})$ via the chiral propagator
$d \log \theta_1(z\,|\,\tau)$.

\begin{theorem}[Face 6 for \texorpdfstring{$\fsl_2$}{fsl-2} Heisenberg on \texorpdfstring{$E_\tau$}{E-tau}]
\label{thm:face6-elliptic-sklyanin}
Let $E_\tau$ be an elliptic curve and let $A = H^{\fsl_2}_{E_\tau}$ be
the $\fsl_2$ Heisenberg chiral algebra realized on $E_\tau$. Then the
binary CY collision residue admits the elliptic representative
\[
 r_{CY}^{\tau}(z) \;=\;
 \frac{k\,\theta'_1(0\,|\,\tau)}{\theta_1(z\,|\,\tau)}\,\Omega_{\fsl_2},
\]
where $\theta_1$ is the odd Jacobi theta function and
$\Omega_{\fsl_2}$ is the Casimir tensor of~$\fsl_2$
(cf.\ Section~\ref{sec:elliptic-r-theta}). The classical limit of
$r_{CY}^{\tau}$ defines the Sklyanin bracket
$\{f,g\}_\tau = \langle r_{CY}^{\tau}, [\nabla f, \nabla g]\rangle$ on
$E_{q,p}(\fsl_2)^*$, and the quantization $\hbar \to q$ recovers
Felder's elliptic quantum group $E_{q,p}(\fsl_2)$ with dynamical
parameter $\lambda$ identified as the $B$-cycle monodromy of the
elliptic propagator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{conjecture}[Face 6 for general toroidal CY]
\label{conj:face6-general-toroidal}
The statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:face6-elliptic-sklyanin} extends to
general toroidal CY realizations $X$ on $E_\tau$: the binary residue
$r_{CY}^{\tau,X}(z)$ is the elliptic $r$-matrix of
$Y(\widehat{\fg}_X)$ acquiring dynamical theta-function corrections in

Theorem~\ref{thm:yangian-sklyanin-quantization}).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Proof support for face 6]
\label{rem:face6-proof-support}
The Sklyanin bracket and Belavin--Drinfeld classification are the
classical inputs. The collision-residue identification asserts that the
elliptic $r$-matrix equals

\end{remark}

\subsection{The three-parameter \texorpdfstring{$\hbar$}{hbar} identification}
\label{subsec:three-hbar}

A subtle but central observation is that three different $\hbar$
parameters appearing in the literature on elliptic quantum groups are
the same parameter under the seven-face dictionary.

\begin{construction}[Three-parameter \texorpdfstring{$\hbar$}{hbar} identification]
\label{constr:three-parameter-hbar}
The following three quantization parameters coincide:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the boundary coupling $\hbar^{\mathrm{KZ}}$ in the Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov
equation (Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov 1984; cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:kz-classical-quantum-bridge});
\item the loop parameter $\hbar^{\mathrm{DNP}}$ in the Dimofte--Niu--Py
realization of the dg-shifted Yangian (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:dnp-bar-cobar-identification});
\item the prefactor $\hbar^{\mathrm{coll}}$ of the binary CY collision
residue $r_{CY}(z)$.
\end{enumerate}
The identification $\hbar^{\mathrm{KZ}} = \hbar^{\mathrm{DNP}} =
\hbar^{\mathrm{coll}}$ holds canonically once the CY direction is fixed,
and follows from the universality of the binary residue in
$\fg^{\mathrm{mod}}_{A_\cC}$.
\end{construction}

\begin{remark}[Convention sanity check]
\label{rem:three-hbar-sanity}
The identification of Construction~\ref{constr:three-parameter-hbar}
fails if any of the three parameters carries a hidden $2\pi i$ or
$1/n!$. In particular, the KZ parameter is conventionally normalized
so that the connection $\nabla = d - \hbar^{-1}\sum_i r_{ij}\,d\log(z_i
- z_j)$ has integer monodromy on the configuration space; the DNP
parameter is normalized so that the loop algebra has bracket
$[a_m, b_n] = [a, b]_{m+n} + m\hbar\delta_{m+n,0}\langle a, b\rangle$;
the collision residue is normalized so that $\Omega = r_{CY}(z)\cdot z$
at $z = 0$. These normalizations are mutually compatible; the
identification is parameter-by-parameter, including scale.
\end{remark}

\section{Face 7: Gaudin model from CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} collision residues}
\label{sec:face7-cy3-gaudin}

In Vol~I, face~7 of the standard seven-face master is the Gaudin

\textup{(}Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaudin-yangian-identification}\textup{)}. In the CY
setting, the Gaudin model arises from CY$_3$ collision residues,
with Gaudin Hamiltonians constructed directly from the toric or
elliptic $r$-matrix of face~5 or face~6, and the Bethe ansatz read
through DT/PT counts.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Coordinates.} Level~$2$; chart $\C^3$
\textup{(}toric\textup{)} or toroidal CY with $C$ the chiral curve
carrying $n$ marked points $z_1, \ldots, z_n$, $\Sigma_2$ a
toric/toroidal codimension-$1$ cycle; ambient
$\End(V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n)$ acting on Fock
representations of $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ \textup{(}or its toroidal
analogue\textup{)}.

\begin{theorem}[Face 7 for \texorpdfstring{$\C^3$}{C-3}: Gaudin from the Jordan-quiver residue]
\label{thm:face7-cy3-gaudin}
Let $X = \C^3$. Fix $n$ marked points $z_1, \ldots, z_n$ on the chiral
curve and Fock representations $V_1, \ldots, V_n$ of
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$. Assume, for the Bethe-spectrum assertion only,
the Drinfeld-double/centre and Fock-vacuum evaluation that carry the
positive half \(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\) to the
\(\cW_{1+\infty}\) module. Define
\[
 H_i^{\C^3} \;:=\; \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{k\,\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}}{z_i - z_j},
\]
acting on $V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n$, where
$\Omega_{ij}^{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}$ is the Casimir tensor
$\Res_{z=0}\bigl[r_{CY}^{\C^3}(z)\bigr]$ acting on the $i$-th and
$j$-th factors. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The operators $\{H_i^{\C^3}\}_{i=1}^n$ commute pairwise:
$[H_i^{\C^3}, H_j^{\C^3}] = 0$;
\item Under the Drinfeld-double/centre and Fock-evaluation hypotheses,
the joint spectrum is governed by the $\cW_{1+\infty}$ Bethe
ansatz obtained from the Drinfeld-double/centre vacuum evaluation of
the affine-Yangian data of Litvinov and Maulik--Okounkov;
\item The DT counts of $\C^3$ (MacMahon plane-partition generating
function) provide character-level evidence for the integer
multiplicities of the Bethe roots; the Bethe-state/plane-partition
bijection is the representation-theoretic comparison datum.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{conjecture}[Face 7 for general toric/toroidal CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}]
\label{conj:face7-general-gaudin}
The statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:face7-cy3-gaudin} extends to any
CY$_3$ $X$ admitting a toric (face~5) or toroidal (face~6) chiral
realization, with Hamiltonians $H_i^{X} := \sum_{j \neq i} r_{CY}^X(z_i
- z_j)_{ij}$, commutativity following from the classical Yang--Baxter
equation for $r_{CY}^X$, and DT/PT counts of $X$ supplying the Bethe
root multiplicities.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Proof sketch for \texorpdfstring{$\C^3$}{C-3}]
For $X = \C^3$, $r_{CY}^{\C^3}(z) = k\,\Omega_{Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)}/z$ has
a single pole. The Hamiltonians $H_i^{\C^3} = \sum_{j \neq i}
k\,\Omega_{ij}/(z_i - z_j)$ are the standard Gaudin Hamiltonians for
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$, and their commutativity follows from the
classical Yang--Baxter equation for $r_{CY}^{\C^3}$
(Construction~\ref{constr:c3-gaudin-from-residue}; cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaudin-yangian-identification}). The Bethe ansatz
identification is the Litvinov $\cW_{1+\infty}$ vacuum-evaluation Bethe
ansatz of the Drinfeld-double/centre side, which identifies Bethe roots
with crystal melting configurations of $\C^3$ (equivalently, plane
partitions).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} supplies the Bethe roots]
\label{rem:cy3-bethe-content}
In the CY setting the Bethe roots are DT/PT counting data. For $\C^3$
the DT/PT counts are MacMahon-type partition functions (3D plane
partitions), and each plane partition contributes a single Bethe root.
For more general toric CY$_3$, the topological vertex provides the
partition counts and hence the Bethe-root populations. The CY$_3$
Gaudin model geometrises the Bethe ansatz in the same manner that the
toric quiver geometrises the affine Yangian.
\end{remark}

\section{The seven-face master theorem in the CY setting}
\label{sec:cy-seven-face-master}

The preceding seven sections each realize the binary CY collision residue
in one geometric language. The master theorem asserts that all seven
realizations are canonically equivalent, with the equivalences given by
explicit specialization or limit, and with the limit/specialization data
recorded by the seven-face commuting diagram of Vol~I.

\begin{conjecture}[Seven faces of \texorpdfstring{$r_{CY}(z)$}{r-CY(z)}]
\label{conj:cy-seven-face-master}
Let $\cC$ be a Calabi--Yau category of dimension $d \in \{2, 3\}$, and
let $A_\cC$ be its chiral algebraization (proved for $d = 2$; at
$d = 3$ restricted to a chosen framed $\Phi_3$-specialisation locus,
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}). Then the seven realizations
\[
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F1})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F2})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F3})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F4})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F5})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F6})},\;
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F7})}
\]
constructed in
Sections~\ref{sec:face1-bar-cobar}--\ref{sec:face7-cy3-gaudin}
all coincide, in the sense that
\[
 r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F}_i)} \;=\;
 \Phi_{i,j}\bigl(r_{CY}^{(\mathrm{F}_j)}\bigr)
 \qquad \forall\, i, j \in \{1, \ldots, 7\},
\]
for canonical change-of-language morphisms $\Phi_{i,j}$ given explicitly
by Theorem~CY-A$_2$ in the proved $d = 2$ case, and conditionally on the
framed CY$_3$ specialisation for face~1 to face~3, the
Schiffmann--Vasserot/RSYZ identification (face~2 to face~5), the MO
stable envelope (face~4), the elliptic specialization $\hbar \to 0$
(face~6 to face~3), and the Litvinov Bethe ansatz (face~5 to face~7).
The limits between faces are recorded in the seven-face commuting
diagram (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:vol1-seven-face-master}; Vol~II,
Theorem~\ref{thm:vol2-seven-face-master}).
\end{conjecture}

Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-seven-face-master} states the coincidence of
the seven CY-specific realizations built in
Sections~\ref{sec:face1-bar-cobar}--\ref{sec:face7-cy3-gaudin}. Its
cross-volume form is a Koszul-locus comparison: for $\Phi_d$-images,
the binary residue admits seven pairwise independent presentations
drawn from Vol~I / Vol~II / Vol~III primary sources, with equivalence
checked by six disjoint comparison maps.

\begin{conjecture}[Cross-volume seven-face agreement for \texorpdfstring{$r_{\CY}$}{r-CY},
modulo CY-C at edge (v)\texorpdfstring{$\Leftrightarrow$}{<=>}(vi)]
\label{thm:seven-faces-of-r-cy}
Let $A$ be a CY boundary chiral algebra in the image of the
dimension-stratified correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}_{d \ge 1}$, evaluated
on the Koszul locus. The following seven presentations of the binary
collision residue~$r_{\CY}(A) \in \End^{\mathrm{ch}}_A(2)[\![z^{-1}]\!]$
agree (the edge (v)$\Leftrightarrow$(vi) below is the Conjecture~CY-C
content; the remaining five edges, together with (vi)$\Leftrightarrow$(vii),
come from disjoint primary sources):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item \emph{Yangian coproduct twist} (Drinfeld~1985):
$r_{\CY}(A) = \Delta_u(x) - \Delta_u^{\mathrm{op}}(x)$
for $x \in \Phi_d(\cC)$ a Drinfeld primitive.
\item \emph{Drinfeld-centre half-braiding}:
$r_{\CY}(A) = \sigma_V\bigl(\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A))\bigr)$
for the canonical half-braiding on the $E_1$-chiral Drinfeld centre.
\item \emph{KZ monodromy} (Kohno~1987):
$r_{\CY}(A) = \lim_{z' \to z}\mathrm{Hol}_{\nabla_{\mathrm{KZ}}}(z' \to z)$
along a simple loop in $X \times X \setminus \Delta$.
\item \emph{BKM vertex-operator exchange} (Borcherds~1998):
$r_{\CY}(A) = V_\alpha(z)V_\beta(0) - (\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta)$
on the BKM lattice VOA attached to the Mukai lattice of $\cC$.
\item \emph{Maulik--Okounkov stable envelope} (Maulik--Okounkov~2012):
$r_{\CY}(A) = \mathrm{Stab}_\cC \circ \mathrm{Stab}_{\cC'}^{-1}$
for the stable envelope of the Nakajima quiver variety of $\Phi^{-1}(A)$.
\item \emph{$\Ainf$-coassociator}:
$r_{\CY}(A) = \delta^{(3)}$, the degree-$3$ term of the shadow tower
$\Theta_A^{\le 3}$.
\item \emph{Costello $5d$ hCS tree amplitude} (Costello~2017):
$r_{\CY}(A) = \mathrm{Tree}^{\mathrm{hCS}}_z[\fgl_1]$
in the holomorphic Chern--Simons theory on $\bC \times \bR^3$ with
spectral parameter~$z$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of the five proved edges; the sixth is
Conjecture~CY-C]
The commuting heptagon closes once six edges are fixed; five of the
six below are proved independently, and the sixth is
Conjecture~CY-C.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.8em]
\item[$(\mathrm{i}) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{ii})$]
The Drinfeld-centre half-braiding on $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A))$ is the
universal solution of the hexagon constraint; on the generating
primitive~$x$ it reduces to $\Delta_u(x) - \Delta_u^{\mathrm{op}}(x)$
by Drinfeld's quasi-triangular axiom. A second construction: the
$E_1$-chiral centre is constructed in Vol~II Chapter~``SC$^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$
heptagon'', face~(6) edge
\texttt{thm:sc-chtop-heptagon}, via chiral factorisation homology; this
is disjoint from any Yangian computation.
\item[$(\mathrm{ii}) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{iii})$]
KZ holonomy around the generic point of $X \times X \setminus \Delta$
realises the half-braiding up to Drinfeld-associator coherence. The
identification of monodromy with half-braiding is Kohno's theorem on
KZ monodromy. A second construction: direct computation of the
Arnold generating $1$-form $\eta = d\log(z_1 - z_2)$ on the pure braid
arrangement (Shelton--Yuzvinsky Koszulity) produces the same monodromy.
\item[$(\mathrm{iii}) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{iv})$]
On the BKM lattice VOA attached to $\cC$, the KZ connection coincides
with the Borcherds vertex-operator exchange, by the Frenkel--Lepowsky--Meurman
construction of the monster vertex algebra combined with Borcherds'
denominator identity. A second computation: the $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}
= c_N(0)/2$ universality (Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-weight-universal})
is derived from Borcherds weight without reference to KZ, yet agrees
numerically with the KZ monodromy eigenvalues on the Mukai lattice of
$K3 \times E$.
\item[$(\mathrm{iv}) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{v})$]
Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes for Nakajima quiver varieties of
ADE type reproduce BKM exchange on the Mukai-fibre: this is the
content of Maulik--Okounkov's Theorem~4.6.1 combined with Schiffmann--Vasserot
CoHA. A second derivation: RSYZ tree amplitudes on toric CY$_3$
give the same answer via a purely toric combinatorial argument,
disjoint from either the stable envelope or the BKM vertex
construction.
\item[$(\mathrm{v}) \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{vi})$ \textup{(Conjecture CY-C)}]
The assertion is that the MO $R$-matrix expands in $\hbar$ with leading
term equal to the $\Ainf$-coassociator~$\delta^{(3)}$, i.e.\ the
degree-$3$ shadow-tower term of $\Theta_A$. This leading-term

(Theorem~\ref{thm:rsyz}), but in the full $K3 \times E$ case it remains

algebra formality of hCS (Costello--Gwilliam). A second derivation:
Witten-diagram reduction to $4d$ Chern--Simons on

amplitude via a $1$-dimensional transverse integral.

Theorem~\ref{thm:vol1-seven-face-master} (Vol~I) and

\begin{remark}[Three invariants across the seven faces]

By Remark~\ref{rem:cy-three-invariants}, every realization of $r_{CY}$
carries the three invariants $(p_{\max}, k_{\max}, r_{\max})$.
These three invariants are independent; each face must state which
invariant it measures, and they must not be identified with one another.
\begin{itemize}
\item Face~5 (toric Yangian) is sensitive to $p_{\max}$: the pole
structure of $r_{Y(\widehat{\fg})}$ is the OPE pole structure of the
toric quiver superpotential.
\item Face~6 (elliptic) carries $k_{\max}$ in its theta-function
expansion: at $k_{\max} = 0$ the residue is purely a single-pole
elliptic term; at $k_{\max} \geq 1$ there are derivative terms.
\item Face~1 (bar-cobar) is sensitive to $r_{\max}$: the shadow tower
$\Theta_A^{\leq r}$ terminates at degree $r_{\max}$, and the binary
residue is the projection to $r = 2$.
\end{itemize}
The seven-face master statement is that the binary residue is the
\emph{same} object across all faces, but the sensitivity to the three
invariants differs face by face.
\end{remark}

(bar--cobar, CoHA, coisson, MO $R$-matrix, Yangian, Sklyanin, Gaudin)
is an \emph{orthogonal slicing} of the same $r_{CY}$: it stratifies by
algebraic language, whereas the three-tier partition stratifies by the
stage at which the datum is read. The two stratifications coexist; no
face of the algebraic seven-presentation is a competitor to the
three-tier partition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Attribution]
Tier~(i) is the Mukai--Hodge reading of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-modular-characteristic}(iii): the Hodge supertrace
$\chi(\cO_{K3 \times E}) = \chi(\cO_{K3}) \cdot \chi(\cO_E) = 2 \cdot 0
= 0$ is K\"unneth-multiplicative on $X$ and does not depend on the
factorisation algebra. Tier~(ii) is the K3-fibre content of
$\PhiFA_3(\cC)$: the Kodaira list of singular fibres
contributes $24 I_1$-type fibres on a generic elliptic K3, and this
count is a property of $\cF_X$ before $\SpCh$ is applied (cf.\
the K3$\times E$ Maulik--Okounkov fourth-face theorem and the class-$\cS$ realisation of
Remark~\ref{rem:holdata-class-s-parent}). Tier~(iii) is the
$(\Sigma_2, C)$-indexed fibre of specialisations: the BKM face is
Borcherds~$1995$/$1998$; the Niemeier and Humbert faces are indexed by
Conway--Norton and Gritsenko--Nikulin boundary arithmetic; the CHL
family is the $\bZ/N\bZ$-twisted Borcherds construction of
Persson--Volpato~$2018$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$
the universal identity of
\texttt{chapters/examples/cy\_d\_kappa\_stratification.tex}

orthogonality of the algebraic seven-presentation to the three-tier

datum and the specialisation fixed (it varies only the language in
which $r_{CY}$ is written).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Dimension-stratified BKM siblings: Monster, \texorpdfstring{$\fg_{\Delta_5}$}{g-Delta-5}, Fake Monster]
\label{rem:cy-holdata-bkm-dim-strat}
The three canonical BKM algebras of the programme are \emph{not} three
sibling tier-(iii) specialisations of one $\PhiFA_d$: they are
$\SpCh$-specialisations of \emph{different} $\PhiFA_d$'s at different
CY dimensions $d$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item \textbf{Monster $V^\natural$}: the Frenkel--Lepowsky--Meurman
moonshine module is a Vol.~I lattice-VOA / Moonshine boundary object,
not presently realised as a tier-(iii) \(K3\times E\) CY$_3$
specialisation. Any CY$_3$ realisation would require an explicit CY$_3$
category and lattice comparison; none is used here. The rank-$24$
Monster Lie algebra \(\mathfrak m\) remains a Borcherds/Moonshine
boundary cousin of the K3 story (Borcherds~1992).
\item \textbf{$\fg_{\Delta_5}$} at $d = 3$: the Gritsenko--Nikulin BKM
arising as the $\SpCh$-specialisation of $\PhiFA_3(D^b\Coh(K3 \times E))$
at the paramodular Humbert--Heegner boundary, rank $3$, weight $5$.
The Gritsenko paramodular generator $\Delta_5 \in M_5(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\bZ))$
has $c_1(0) = 10$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\fg_{\Delta_5}) = 5$.
\item \textbf{Fake Monster} at $d = 5$: Borcherds' Fake Monster Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{f}$ of rank $26$, arising as a $\SpCh$-specialisation of
$\PhiFA_5(\cC)$ for a CY$_5$ datum whose lattice is $\mathrm{II}_{25,1}$.
This is \emph{obstructed} from $d = 3$: no $\PhiFA_3$ input produces
a tier-(iii) specialisation of rank $26$, because the Mukai lattice of
any CY$_3$ has rank at most $24 \leq 25 < 26$. The Fake Monster is a
genuine $d = 5$ sibling, \emph{not} a tier-(iii) partner of
$\fg_{\Delta_5}$ at the same dimension.
\end{enumerate}
The confusion pattern: the three algebras all satisfy Borcherds
denominator identities and all admit Gritsenko-type paramodular lifts at
their respective ranks, which invites reading them as three coordinated
specialisations of one $\Phi$. They are not. Each lives on a different
$\EdHolFA(X)$ at a different CY dimension; only within a single $d$ do
tier-(iii) siblings admit a common stage-1 parent. The three-tier
stratification of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-holdata-three-tier} is a
statement \emph{per $d$}; across $d$ the stratification itself shifts.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Non-uniform twisted-twined \texorpdfstring{$H^3(C_{M_{24}}(g_N); U(1))$}{H-3(C-M-24(g-N); U(1))} across admissible cells]
\label{rem:cy-holdata-H3-nonuniform}
The $M_{24}$-centraliser cohomology that dresses the paramodular
multipliers in tier-(iii) is \emph{not} uniform across the nine
admissible $M_{24}$-conjugacy cells $g_N \in \{1A, 2A, 2B, 3B, 4A,
4B, 4C, 6A, 6B\}$ attached to the CHL family $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4,
6\}$. Seven cells; $1A, 3B, 4A, 4B, 4C, 6A, 6B$; have
$H^3(C_{M_{24}}(g_N); U(1)) = 0$ cleanly. The two cells $2A$ and
$2B$ carry residual discrete torsion:
$H^3(C_{M_{24}}(2A); U(1)) \supset \bZ/2$ and
$H^3(C_{M_{24}}(2B); U(1)) \supset \bZ/4$
(Milgram~$1995$ Sylow-detection at $p = 2$; Benson~$1998$
\emph{Cohomology of Sporadic Groups}). This residual torsion dresses
the paramodular \emph{multiplier} of the CHL Borcherds product at
$N = 2$; it does \emph{not} alter the paramodular \emph{weight}.
In particular the Borcherds weight identity
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_2) \;=\; c_2(0)/2 \;=\; 4
\]
is cocycle-free: the discrete torsion acts on the multiplier system
but commutes with the $c_N(0)/2$-weight projection. This is the precise
sense in which tier-(iii) faces share a common weight law
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-holdata-three-tier}(iii), final bullet) while
carrying non-uniform cocycle data; the two data types factor through
different projections of the Borcherds lift.
\end{remark}

\section{Cross-volume bridge}
\label{sec:cy-cross-volume-bridge}

Part~\ref{part:connections} of Vol~III mirrors the seven-face programme
developed in Vols.~I and II. The common skeleton is the seven-face
programme; the variation is in which face is most concrete.

\begin{remark}[The three seven-face masters]
\label{rem:three-seven-face-masters}
The three volumes each devote a part to the seven-face programme, with
the same architecture but different ground objects:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Vol~I, the seven-face part:} the binary collision residue of a chiral
algebra on a curve, in seven languages: bar-cobar twisting, primitive
generator, classical $r$-matrix, KZ connection, Gaudin model, Bethe
ansatz, dg-shifted Yangian (cf.\ Vol~I,
Theorem~\ref{thm:vol1-seven-face-master}).
\item \emph{Vol~II, the seven-face part:} the binary collision residue of a
holomorphic-topological quantum group, in seven languages: open-string
brace algebra, derived center, twisted holography boundary, line
defect, Wilson line $R$-matrix, soft graviton coupling, Yangian double
(cf.\ Vol~II, Theorem~\ref{thm:vol2-seven-face-master}).
\item \emph{Vol~III, this chapter:} the binary CY collision residue of a
Calabi--Yau chiral algebra, in seven CY-specific languages: CY
bar-cobar, CoHA / perverse coherent sheaves, classical CY Poisson
coisson, MO stable envelope, affine super Yangian for toric CY$_3$,
elliptic Sklyanin for toroidal CY, Gaudin from CY$_3$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-seven-face-master} above).
\end{enumerate}
The three master theorems are compatible through the installed
comparison maps: under the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme
$\{\Phi_d\}$, face~$i$ of Vol~III maps to a specialization of face~$i$
of Vol~I, and similarly for Vol~II, precisely at the level governed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-volume-comparison-gate}. The CY setting is the most
constrained: each face acquires geometric content that the abstract
chiral algebra setting does not see (DT counts, plane partitions,
crystal melting, MO stable envelopes).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The CY direction is the new slot]
\label{rem:cy-direction-new-slot}
Compared with Vol~I and Vol~II, the CY seven-face master contains the
additional CY direction $\Omega_\cC$. In Vol~I the
binary residue lives on $\overline{C}_2(X) \times \fg^{\otimes 2}$; in
Vol~II it lives on $\overline{C}_2(X) \times \mathrm{open\text{-}sector}^{\otimes 2}$;
in Vol~III it lives on $\overline{C}_2(X) \times \cC^{\otimes 2}$, with
the CY volume form supplying the pairing on $\cC^{\otimes 2}$. The CY
direction makes face~4 (MO) and face~7 (CY$_3$ Gaudin) geometric:
they live on quiver varieties whose definition requires the CY
structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)$}{kappa-ch(cA)} is a face-by-face invariant]
\label{rem:kappa-face-by-face}
The modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ depends on the construction,
not on the geometry alone. For $K3 \times E$, the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum
has four values from four distinct sources:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \chi(\cO_{K3 \times E}) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0$ (K\"unneth;
the K3-fiber value $\chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$ is a distinct \emph{fiber} invariant,
not $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ of the total space) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$ are
\emph{manifold invariants} (topological, independent of any construction),
while $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}} = 3$ (the Heisenberg-level
additive reading of the framed $\Phi_3$-image; the Hodge-supertrace reading
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{K3 \times E}) = \sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3 \times
E) = 0$ is the complementary manifold invariant of
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}
= 5$ (from the Borcherds weight $c_1(0)/2$ on the primitive denominator
$\Delta_5$, while
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2$ has weight $10$) are
\emph{construction-dependent invariants} arising from distinct
constructions, not from four applications of~$\Phi$. Each value sits on
a different face: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ on face~1 (bar-cobar of the
chiral de Rham complex), $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ on face~2 (holomorphic
Euler characteristic), $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ on face~3 (classical
lattice VOA limit), $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ on face~4 (MO/Igusa). The
$\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum is the imprint of the geometry on the
seven-face master: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)$ depends on the full
algebra (not on its Virasoro subalgebra alone), and in the CY setting
depends on the full construction (not on the underlying CY manifold
alone).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Outlook: an eighth face?]
\label{rem:outlook-eighth-face}
The standard seven-face master is closed: there are exactly seven
languages in which the binary residue lives, corresponding to the seven
edges of the Vol~I commuting diagram. The natural question in the CY setting: does the
deformation-quantization face (Khan--Zeng 3d PVA sigma model)
constitute an eighth? At present this face reduces to a
combination of face~3 (classical coisson) and face~7 (Gaudin from
CY$_3$), with the deformation-quantization parameter playing the role of
the loop parameter in face~7. An independent eighth face would require
a CY-specific structure not visible in the seven-face commuting diagram;
it remains open whether the
$3$d~$\cN = 2$ holomorphic-twisted theory provides such a structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three faces of \texorpdfstring{$8$}{8} and the universal \texorpdfstring{$\hbar^2 \cdot K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}} = -1$}{hbar-2 . K-kappa-ch = -1} identity]

\section{Twisted M-theory as the Stage-1 field-theory bulk of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3$}{Phi-3}}
\label{sec:twisted-m-theory-phi3-bulk-boundary}

The seven faces all live on the boundary chiral side $A_\cC$ or its
Stage-1 $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ predecessor. The present section reads the
same factorisation datum from the field-theoretic bulk side: as the
holomorphic factor of twisted $11$d supergravity on $X \times \R^5$ in the Omega-background,
compactified to $5$d holomorphic Chern--Simons on $X \times \R_t$ and
holographically expected to map to the $E_1$-chiral boundary on
$C \subset X$ via the Costello--Paquette extension of twisted holography
to $3$d HT gauge theory after the protected comparison datum is supplied.
Each arrow is chain-level; the $E_n$-level at every stage
is read off the factorisation-homology cochains. This field-theory bulk
is not the slot-$3$ chiral bulk object of the holographic datum; that
slot is always the chiral derived centre, equivalently chiral Hochschild
cochains, of the specialised boundary algebra.

\subsection{Perturbative reduction: twisted M-theory to \texorpdfstring{$5$}{5}d hCS}
\label{subsec:twisted-m-theory-reduction}

\begin{proposition}[Perturbative reduction of twisted \texorpdfstring{$11$}{11}d SUGRA to \texorpdfstring{$5$}{5}d hCS]
\label{prop:tw-m-theory-to-5d-hcs}
\textup{(Costello 2016, arXiv:1610.04144, Thm.~4.1.)}
The perturbative BV factorisation algebra of twisted $11$d supergravity
on $\C^3 \times \R^5$ with Omega-background on a $\R^2 \subset \R^5$
plane is equivalent, as a BV factorisation algebra on the surviving
slab $\C^3 \times \R_t$, to $5$d holomorphic Chern--Simons on
$\C^3 \times \R_t$ with gauge algebra $\widehat{\fgl}_\infty$. The
Omega-background localises the $\R^2$-rotation plane to its fixed
point; the residual $5$d hCS action on $\C^3 \times \R_t$ is
\[
 S_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}[\cA]
 \;=\;
 \int_{\C^3 \times \R_t} \Omega_{\C^3}
 \wedge \operatorname{tr}\!\left(
 \cA \wedge \bar{\partial}_{\C^3} \cA
 \;+\; \cA \wedge d_t \cA
 \;+\; \tfrac{2}{3}\, \cA \wedge \cA \wedge \cA
 \right),
\]
with gauge algebra $\widehat{\fgl}_\infty$ tracking the M$2$/M$5$
perturbative reductions of the $11$d parent. Restriction of the gauge
algebra to rank $r$ corresponds to compactifying on a $4$-manifold
carrying $r$ M$5$-branes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Compact CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3} reduction and paramodular arithmetic on \texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E}]
\label{rem:tw-m-theory-k3e-paramodular}
The compactification of Proposition~\ref{prop:tw-m-theory-to-5d-hcs} on
$X = K3 \times E$ replaces $\C^3$ by the compact CY$_3$ and introduces
$24$~M$5$-branes wrapping the $I_1$~Kodaira fibres of the elliptic
$K3$. The protected one-loop comparison target of the resulting $5$d
hCS theory on $(K3 \times E) \times \R_t$ contributes the paramodular weight
$\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)=\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=5$ as the one-loop forced output of
paramodular anomaly cancellation once the physical trace comparison is supplied
(Remark~\ref{rem:holdata-delta5-oneloop}). The number
$\chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$ remains the K3-fibre Hodge~Euler characteristic,
while the Kodaira residues belong to the anomaly calculation rather
than to an additive $\kappa_\bullet$ formula. Four independent duality frames
(heterotic $K3 \times T^2$ Harvey--Moore $1996$; Type IIB $K3 \times
S^1$ DVV $1996$--$1997$; Type IIA symmetric products DMVV $1997$;
$M$-theory $K3 \times T^3$ Witten $1995$) confirm the same arithmetic
numerically.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Chain-level \texorpdfstring{$E_3$}{E-3} bulk observables via holomorphic-topological factorisation}
\label{subsec:E3-chain-level-HT-factorisation}

\begin{theorem}[$E_3$~chain-level realisation of \texorpdfstring{$\PhiFA_3(\cC)$}{PhiFA-3(C)} via twisted-M-theory]
\label{thm:E3-chain-level-twisted-M-theory}
Let $X$ be a smooth CY$_3$ with holomorphic volume form $\Omega_X$ and
let $\frakg$ be a reductive gauge Lie algebra. Assume $X$ is restricted
to a formal/toric Costello--Gwilliam chart, or more generally that the
framed $\PhiFA_3$ chain witness has been constructed. This is a local
chain-level assertion on the stated chart; arbitrary compact non-formal
CY$_3$s require their own holomorphic-factorisation witnesses. Under the
holomorphic-topological tensor theorem of Costello--Gwilliam Vol.~II~\S$4.10$
\textup{(}\texttt{arXiv:2210.13036}\textup{),} the bulk local
observables of twisted M-theory on $X \times \R^2$ factor as a
chain-level tensor product
\[
 \Obs^{\mathrm{tw}\text{-}M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X \times \R^2)
 \;\simeq\;
 \Obs^{4\mathrm{d\,hol}}(X, \frakg)
 \;\boxtimes\;
 \Obs^{1\mathrm{d\,top}}(\R_t)
 \;\boxtimes\;
 \Obs^{1\mathrm{d\,top}}(\R_s),
\]
where the two topological $\R$-factors carry $E_1$-structure each and
the holomorphic $X$-factor carries $E_4$-structure via Kontsevich--
Tamarkin $4$d formality on the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $-3$ on
$\HH^\bullet(\Perf X)$. Restricting to the holomorphic $E_3$-subfactor
obtained by compactifying one $\R$-factor and reading the $\R_s$-
transverse slab gives
\[
 \PhiFA_3(\Perf X)
 \;\simeq\;
 \int_{\R_s} \Obs^{\mathrm{tw}\text{-}M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(X \times \R^2)
 \;\in\;
 \EdHolFA(X).
\]
On this stated locus, this identifies the constructed
$\PhiFA_3(\Perf X)$ witness with the holomorphic factor of the $5$d hCS
BV observable factorisation algebra on $X \times \R_t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The $5$d hCS action is linear in the $\R_t$-direction: $\cA \wedge d_t
\cA$ contributes a first-order $t$-derivative. The classical BV complex
on $X \times \R_t$ is therefore a tensor product
$(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X) \otimes \frakg[1], \bar{\partial}_X) \otimes
(\Omega^\bullet(\R_t), d_t)$ with the antibracket respecting the
factorisation. Costello--Gwilliam Vol.~II~\S$4.10$ promotes this tensor
decomposition at the cochain level to a tensor decomposition of
BV~factorisation algebras. The holomorphic factor is Costello--Li $4$d
hCS on $X$ \textup{(}\texttt{arXiv:1606.00365}\textup{)} with $E_4$-
structure from Kontsevich $E_d$-formality. The topological factor is
the $E_1$-algebra $C^*_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\frakg[1])$; factorisation homology
$\int_{\R_t}$ is the enveloping algebra $U(\frakg[\![\hbar]\!])$ by
Francis~$2013$ \textup{(}\texttt{arXiv:1303.0305}\textup{)}~Theorem~$2.29$.
Restriction to Stage-$1$ reads only the holomorphic factor; this is
$\PhiFA_3(\Perf X)$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}~\S$\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Costello--Paquette boundary: the \texorpdfstring{$E_1$}{E-1}-chiral algebra is the boundary of \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}d HT gauge theory}
\label{subsec:costello-paquette-E1-chiral-d3}

Why twisted holography at $d = 3$ requires the Costello--Paquette
extension rather than the Costello--Gaiotto $4$d-HT framework: the
sphere-OPE datum of a VOA lives in $H^\bullet(\mathrm{Ran}(\mathbb{A}^1),
\mathrm{Fact}_{E_2})$, whose one-dimensional $E$-factor contribution
$H^1(E, \C)$ is the Omega-deformation parameter rather than the
sphere-OPE structure. Direct transplantation would produce a VOA that
does not exist; the boundary of a $3$d HT theory is $E_1$-chiral
(locally constant on $C \subset X$), not $E_2$-chiral. This is the
chain-level reason why $\Phi_3(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3 \times E))$ is an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra, not a VOA.

\begin{proposition}[Costello--Paquette \texorpdfstring{$E_1$}{E-1}-chiral boundary at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}]
\label{prop:costello-paquette-E1-boundary-holdata}
Let $X = K3 \times E$, let $Y^{\mathrm{HT}}_3(X)$ denote the $3$d
holomorphic-topological gauge theory on $X \times \R_+$, and let
$\cA^{\Eone}_\partial$ denote its locally-constant boundary
factorisation algebra on $C = E \subset X$. Assuming the Stage-$1$
$\PhiFA_3$ witness, Costello--Paquette's boundary theorem
\textup{(}Costello--Paquette $2020$, \texttt{arXiv:2001.02177}
Thm.~$4.7$\textup{)}, and the protected hCS boundary comparison, then:
\[
 \Phi_3\bigl(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)\bigr)
 \;\simeq\;
 \cA^{\Eone}_\partial\bigl(Y^{\mathrm{HT}}_3(X)\bigr)
 \quad\text{on}\quad
 C = E \subset X,
\]
with $\Omega$-deformation parameter $\varepsilon$ set by the elliptic
period $\oint_\gamma \omega_E \in H^1(E, \C)$. The identification is
$E_1$-monoidal, not $E_2$-braided; the $r$-matrix $r_{\mathrm{CY}}$ is
an $E_1$-commutator shadow, not a braiding coefficient of a modular
tensor category. The mode algebra
$\mathrm{Modes}(\cA^{\Eone}_\partial)$ is an associative algebra (not
a vertex algebra); the pairing is the linearised $E_1$-commutator,
not the full Borcherds OPE.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-data hides on the derived centre]
\label{rem:E2-data-hides-derived-centre-twisted-M}
The $E_2$-chiral lift of $\Phi_3(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ lives on the
Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi_3(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X))))$, one
level up from the boundary algebra. The Borcherds product
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2$ is the
OP-normalised protected scalar target of \(Y^{\mathrm{HT}}_3(X)\) on
\(X \times S^1\), not the character of
\(\Phi_3(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X))\) itself. Under the same protected
comparison, the primitive genus-$2$ denominator
$\Delta_5(Z) \in M_5(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z))$ is the chiral-half genus-$2$
shadow of the $E_1$-mode character
\[
 \Delta_5(Z)
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{ch}^{\mathrm{modes}}_{g=2}(\cA^{\Eone}_\partial)(Z)
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{Tr}^{\Eone}_{\cA^{\Eone}_\partial}\!\left(
 e^{2\pi i(\tau L_0 + z J_0 + \sigma \bar L_0)}
 \right),
 \qquad
 Z = \begin{pmatrix}\tau & z \\ z & \sigma\end{pmatrix}
 \in \mathbb{H}_2,
\]
with period matrix $Z$ encoding the $\Omega_{K3 \times E}$-expansion
