\chapter{Geometric Langlands and CY Quantum Groups}
\label{ch:geometric-langlands}

The CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ of \ref{part:bridge} sends a Calabi--Yau category to a chiral algebra ($\Etwo$ at $d \leq 2$; $\Eone$ at $d \geq 3$); the bar complex of the output (Volume~I, Theorem~A) is the factorization invariant on which geometric Langlands is ultimately a statement. This chapter traces the thread. At the critical level the Feigin--Frenkel theorem identifies the chiral center with the algebra of $G^L$-opers; the Verdier intertwining of Volume~I Theorem~A then relates local geometric Langlands to the four-functor picture (bar, cobar, Verdier, derived center). For Calabi--Yau input, the analogue is conjectural: a Langlands dual of a CY $d$-category should realize the mirror of its $\Phi$-image. Every formal statement in this chapter is stated as a conjecture except for literal citations of Feigin--Frenkel (1992) or Frenkel--Gaitsgory (2006), which are tagged \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere.

\section{The Feigin--Frenkel center at the critical level}
\label{sec:feigin-frenkel}
% ============================================================

Let $\frakg$ be a simple finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and $\hat{\frakg}_k$ its affine Kac--Moody algebra at level $k$. The vacuum vertex algebra $V_k(\frakg)$ is the universal chiral algebra generated by the currents $J^a(z)$ with the Kac--Moody OPE. The \emph{critical level} is $k_c = -h^\vee$, where $h^\vee$ is the dual Coxeter number.

\begin{theorem}[Feigin--Frenkel, 1992]
\label{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere{}
At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the center of the vacuum vertex algebra is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of $G^L$-opers on the formal disk:
\[
 \mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \;:=\; Z(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \mathrm{Fun}\bigl(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)\bigr).
\]
Here $G^L$ is the Langlands dual group and $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)$ is the scheme of $G^L$-opers on the formal disk $D = \mathrm{Spec}\, \C[[t]]$.
\end{theorem}

The isomorphism is the content of Feigin--Frenkel (1992), with the full exposition in Frenkel's book \emph{Langlands Correspondence for Loop Groups} (2007). Off the critical level the center is trivial; at $k = -h^\vee$ it becomes a commutative vertex subalgebra of maximal size, and the resulting ind-scheme is $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)$.

\paragraph{Free-field resolution and the bar complex.}
The proof of the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism proceeds via the Wakimoto realization: a free-field embedding $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg) \hookrightarrow \Pi_{-h^\vee}$ into a $\beta\gamma$-system tensored with a Heisenberg algebra, followed by a BRST reduction. The bar complex $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) = T^c(s^{-1}\overline{V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)})$ of the critical-level vacuum algebra carries the deconcatenation coproduct of Volume~I. The Wakimoto free-field embedding induces a map $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \to B(\Pi_{-h^\vee})$ of factorization coalgebras. Since $\Pi_{-h^\vee}$ is a tensor product of free-field algebras, its bar complex is computed by the abelian (class G) shadow tower, where all operations above degree two vanish. The nontrivial content of the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism, from the bar-complex perspective, is that the BRST cohomology of the Wakimoto complex computes $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ as the Verdier-dual of the primitive summand of $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg))$ (the summand generated by single-letter words in the ordered bar).

\paragraph{Locating Feigin--Frenkel among the four functors.} Volume~I Theorem~A organizes four distinct operations on chiral algebras over $\mathrm{Ran}(X)$. They must never be conflated (Volume~I).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item The bar functor $B$, producing a factorization coalgebra.
 \item Verdier duality $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}$ applied to the bar, producing the linear-dual algebra denoted $A^!$.
 \item Inversion, returning the original algebra up to quasi-isomorphism on the Koszul locus.
 \item The derived chiral center $Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}$, computed as chiral Hochschild cochains; this is the bulk.
\end{enumerate}
Feigin--Frenkel is a statement about the chiral center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \subset V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$. The relevant legs of the four-functor picture are the derived center of item~(d), and the Verdier leg of item~(b). It is not an instance of inversion (item~(c)). In particular one should not describe Feigin--Frenkel as ``bar followed by cobar produces the spectral side.'' For $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$, the bar complex $B(A)$ carries the deconcatenation coproduct of $T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$ (Volume~I); the Verdier-dual complex is the habitat in which $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L})$ should be located.

At the critical level $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) = \dim(\frakg) \cdot (k + h^\vee) / (2 h^\vee) = 0$. In the trace-form convention the classical $r$-matrix carries an explicit level prefix, $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$, so at $k = -h^\vee$ it is $-h^\vee\,\Omega/z$, not $0$. The critical phenomenon is the vanishing of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the resulting shadow/center degeneration encoded by $\Theta_A$, not a level-stripped disappearance of the affine collision residue.

\begin{conjecture}[Critical-level Verdier-intertwining; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:critical-self-dual}
Let $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$ and write $A^!$ for the Verdier-dual chiral algebra $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B(A))$ of Volume~I Theorem~A. At the critical level the chiral-algebra inclusion $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \hookrightarrow A$, combined with the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}, implies (does not iff) the existence of a factorization-coalgebra map $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)) \to B(A^!)$ on $\mathrm{Ran}(X)$. The conjecture is that this map is a quasi-isomorphism on the Volume~I Koszul locus. This is a statement about the Verdier leg of the four-functor picture, not about the inversion leg $\Omega \circ B$.
\end{conjecture}

The forward implication (Feigin--Frenkel implies a bar-coalgebra map) follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center} and the factorization construction of $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}$. The converse (that the map is a quasi-isomorphism) is open.

\paragraph{Remark on the Koszul locus.} Volume~I defines the Koszul locus as the set of chiral algebras $A$ for which the inversion map $\Omega(B(A)) \to A$ is a quasi-isomorphism. For $V_k(\frakg)$ with $\frakg$ simple and $k$ generic, membership in the Koszul locus is a theorem of Volume~I (the Kac--Moody family is class L, shadow depth $r = 3$). At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$ the chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ vanishes, and membership requires a separate argument because the shadow metric degenerates there even though the trace-form $r$-matrix $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$ remains nonzero. The status of $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$ on the Koszul locus is therefore itself conjectural and feeds into Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}.

\paragraph{Remark on the opposite level.} Vol~I records that the Koszul conductor for the Kac--Moody family is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ where $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'}(\frakg))$ with $k' = -k - 2 h^\vee$ (the Feigin--Frenkel reflection). The two critical-level points $k = -h^\vee$ and $k' = -h^\vee$ coincide: the critical level is the fixed point of the Feigin--Frenkel reflection. This is the algebraic reason the critical level is distinguished, and the source of the self-duality language that must not be transposed to universal form.

\paragraph{D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ and chiral localization.}
The Frenkel--Gaitsgory programme (2006--) globalizes the Feigin--Frenkel center from the formal disk to an algebraic curve $X$. For each smooth projective curve $X$ and reductive group $G$, the category $\hat{\frakg}_{\mathrm{crit}}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}^{\mathrm{Ran}(X)}$ of chiral modules for the critical-level vacuum algebra over $\Ran(X)$ admits a localization functor
\[
  \Delta_X \colon \hat{\frakg}_{\mathrm{crit}}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}^{\Ran(X)} \;\longrightarrow\; D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_G(X))
\]
whose essential image at the abelian level is the category of Hecke eigensheaves. The localization $\Delta_X$ is a chiral-algebraic shadow of the Beilinson--Drinfeld construction: the factorization structure of the vacuum module on $\Ran(X)$ produces, upon localization, the D-module structure on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$. The bar complex $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg))$ on $\Ran(X)$ maps, under the Verdier leg of Theorem~A, to the factorization algebra that controls the spectral side. The passage from factorization coalgebra to D-module on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ is the content of $\Delta_X$; its compatibility with the four-functor picture of Volume~I is the subject of Conjecture~\ref{conj:d-mod-factorization}.

\begin{conjecture}[Factorization-to-D-module compatibility; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:d-mod-factorization}
The localization functor $\Delta_X$ intertwines the Verdier leg $D_{\Ran} \circ B$ of Volume~I Theorem~A with the de Rham functor on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$. Concretely: for $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$, the D-module $\Delta_X(A^!)$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$ is the Hecke eigensheaf associated to the trivial $G^L$-local system, and the factorization-coalgebra structure of $B(A)$ on $\Ran(X)$ determines the Hecke property via the chiral Verdier intertwining. This is an ``implies'' statement: the factorization structure determines the Hecke property, but the converse (recovering the factorization structure from the Hecke property alone) is open.
\end{conjecture}

\section{CY Langlands for $d = 1, 2, 3$}
\label{sec:cy-langlands}
% ============================================================

The statement of Feigin--Frenkel is that the \emph{center} of a chiral algebra at a distinguished level encodes opers for the Langlands dual group. For a CY category $\cC$, the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ produces a chiral algebra $A_\cC$ whose bar complex is the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}); a CY analogue of Langlands duality should therefore manifest as a pairing $\cC \leftrightarrow \cC^L$ on the source of the correspondence.

\begin{conjecture}[CY Langlands; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:cy-langlands}
For each dimension $d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ there exists an involution $\cC \mapsto \cC^L$ on smooth proper CY $d$-categories, the \emph{Langlands dual}, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The Verdier-dual chiral algebra $\Phi(\cC)^! := D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B(\Phi(\cC)))$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\Phi(\cC^L)$ (compatibility of the Langlands involution with the Verdier leg of Vol~I Theorem~A); equivalently, the modular tensor categories $\Phi(\cC)$ and $\Phi(\cC^L)$ are related by the geometric Langlands equivalence of Frenkel--Gaitsgory (2006).
 \item The chiral modular characteristics $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC^L))$ satisfy a family-dependent Koszul conductor relation. For input giving rise to Kac--Moody output, the conductor is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ (the KM/free-field case of Volume~I); for Virasoro-type output it is $13$ (the self-dual point $c = 13$). The general CY Koszul conductor $\rho_K(\cC)$ is family-dependent and takes the archetype values $\{0, 13, 250/3, 98/3\}$ across the four archetypes $\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{M}$; there is no single universal value.

\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The case $d = 1$.} Smooth proper CY $1$-categories are, up to Morita equivalence, the derived category of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective curve of genus $g = 1$. The correspondence $\Phi_1$ on $d = 1$ input is not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral} (which is stated at $d = 2$); the expected output is the Heisenberg-like current algebra of the elliptic curve. The involution $\cC \mapsto \cC^L$ at $d = 1$ is the identity. This is consistent with geometric Langlands on an elliptic curve being the Fourier--Mukai self-duality (Polishchuk--Rothstein, 2001).

\paragraph{The case $d = 2$.} Smooth proper CY $2$-categories include $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ for $X$ a K3 or abelian surface, and the Fukaya category of the same. The conjectured $d = 2$ Langlands is the \emph{K3 mirror map}: Mukai's Hodge-theoretic mirror $X \leftrightarrow X^\vee$ for K3 surfaces should induce an involution on CY $2$-categories which, via $\Phi$, corresponds to the $d = 2$ Langlands equivalence. This is not proved. A first milestone is to verify Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}(i) on the sublocus of lattice-polarized K3 categories, where the mirror map is the Dolgachev--Nikulin involution on the N\'eron--Severi lattice.

\paragraph{The case $d = 3$.}
At $d = 3$ the functor $\Phi_3$ exists (Theorem~CY-A$_3$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).
The Langlands conjecture at $d = 3$ remains the most speculative, not because the functor is missing but because the quantum vertex chiral group $G(X)$ is unconstructed in general (Conjecture~CY-C).
Because BPS invariants are the primary output of the $d = 3$ functor (via CoHA), the expected statement is that Langlands duality exchanges \emph{mirror BPS algebras}.
Concretely: for a CY $3$-fold $X$ with mirror $X^\vee$, the conjectural quantum vertex chiral groups $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$ (CY-C) should become Langlands dual as $\Eone$-chiral algebras.
The CoHA of $X$ is an associative algebra, providing the $\Eone$-ordered half of the conjectural $G(X)$.
Every statement of this form passes through the unconstructed object $G(X)$ (CY-C) and must remain a conjecture.

\paragraph{The Hitchin realization at $d = 2$.}
Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve and $\mathbf{G}$ a reductive group.
The Hitchin moduli space $\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})$ is a smooth hyperk\"ahler manifold whose derived category of coherent sheaves $D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})))$ is a CY $2$-category (in the sense of the Mukai pairing on K3-type derived categories; the precise CY dimension depends on $\dim \mathcal{M}_H$).
The Hausel--Thaddeus and Donagi--Pantev mirror symmetry for Hitchin systems identifies
\[
 \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})^\vee \;\simeq\; \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G}^L)
\]
at the level of generic Hitchin fibers (dual abelian varieties) and, conjecturally, at the derived-categorical level via homological mirror symmetry: $D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))) \simeq \Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L))$.
Applying $\Phi$ to both sides produces a pair of $\Etwo$-chiral algebras.

\begin{conjecture}[Hitchin-system CY Langlands; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:cy-langlands-hitchin}
The CY Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}, restricted to Hitchin-type CY $2$-categories, is the Donagi--Pantev mirror map: $\Phi(D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))))^! \simeq \Phi(\Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L)))$ as $\Etwo$-chiral algebras on $\Ran(X)$.
The chiral modular characteristics satisfy $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L))) = 0$ (the KM-type Koszul conductor, not the Virasoro conductor $13$).
This is the $d = 2$ Hitchin specialization; it implies (does not iff) the general $d = 2$ CY Langlands.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The Kapustin--Witten picture (physical heuristic).}
The following paragraph is a physical heuristic, not a mathematical derivation; each identification is conjectural unless independently proved. A physical interpretation of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands} comes from the Kapustin--Witten realization of geometric Langlands as S-duality of $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super-Yang--Mills on a product $\Sigma \times C$ (Kapustin--Witten, 2007).
In that picture, the Langlands dual group arises as the S-dual gauge group and the category of D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ arises from the category of branes of an A-model or B-model twist.
The CY category entering the construction is the Fukaya category of $\mathrm{Hit}(C, G)$ (the Hitchin moduli space) on one side and $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Hit}(C, G^L)))$ on the other.
The mirror symmetry of Hitchin moduli spaces under hyperk\"ahler rotation (Hausel--Thaddeus, 2003; Donagi--Pantev, 2012) provides a concrete CY $2$-category instance where the $d = 2$ Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands} is testable.
The $\Phi$-image on each side should recover, via Volume~I, the spectral and automorphic sides of the Beilinson--Drinfeld construction.

\begin{remark}[4d class-$S$ parent of the K3 chiral bialgebra]
\label{rem:gl-DNA-class-S-parent}
The K3 chiral bialgebra admits a 4d $\cN=2$ parent in the class-$S$ framework of Gaiotto (2012). Compactify the $(2,0)$ $A_1$ theory on the 24-punctured sphere $\Sigma_{0,24}$: this produces a 4d $\cN=2$ superconformal theory with canonical charges
\[
  c_{4d} \;=\; \tfrac{107}{6}, \qquad a_{4d} \;=\; \tfrac{403}{24}, \qquad
  \text{Coulomb rank} \;=\; 21, \qquad
  \text{flavour} \;=\; \mathfrak{su}(2)^{24}
\]
(intermediate evaluation $(c_{4d}, a_{4d}, c_{2d}) = (26, 49/2, -312)$ retracted;
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}
Remark~\ref{rem:k3-classS-numerical-caveat}).
The Beem--Lemos--Liendo--Peelaers--Rastelli--van~Rees chiral algebra functor $\chi_{4d}$ (BLLPRvR 2013) sends this theory to a 2d chiral algebra with central charge
\[
  c_{2d} \;=\; -12 c_{4d} \;=\; -214
\]
carrying $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}_{-2}^{\otimes 24}$ current symmetry (flavour level $k_{2d} = -h^\vee = -2$ for each $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factor), precisely the critical-level affine symmetry of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center} applied $24$ times. The 24-puncture rigidity is enforced by the Steiner system $S(5,8,24)$: the $24$ punctures of $\Sigma_{0,24}$ are the $24$ points permuted faithfully by the Mathieu group $M_{24}$, with the $759$ octads of $S(5,8,24)$ indexing the maximal parabolic degenerations of $\Sigma_{0,24}$. The Langlands content: under $\Phi_2$ applied to the K3 category and Gaiotto duality applied to the class-$S$ parent, the QGL involution $k \mapsto k^L$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:qgl-involution} (anticipated below) is the $S$-duality $\Psi \leftrightarrow -1/\Psi$ of the 4d theory restricted to the critical-level 24-fold tensor (Gaiotto~2012, Beem et al.~2015, Moore--Tachikawa~2012 for the Higgs-branch interpretation).
\end{remark}

\section{Quantum geometric Langlands and the shadow parameter}
\label{sec:quantum-geom-langlands}
% ============================================================

Quantum geometric Langlands (QGL), introduced by Feigin--Frenkel and developed by Frenkel--Gaitsgory (2006), is a one-parameter deformation of classical geometric Langlands. The parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = k + h^\vee$ is a complex number. The two limits recover known correspondences:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} \to 0$ (the critical level $k = -h^\vee$): the classical geometric Langlands correspondence of Beilinson--Drinfeld, with Feigin--Frenkel centers as the spectral side.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} \to \infty$: the classical de Rham geometric Langlands in Gaitsgory's formulation, the semi-classical limit of the deformation.
\end{itemize}

At finite $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ the spectral side is a category of \emph{twisted D-modules} on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ at level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, and the automorphic side is the corresponding category at level $-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ for the Langlands dual group. The quantum correspondence is stated as an equivalence of DG categories (Frenkel, \emph{Lectures on Quantum Geometric Langlands}, 2005; Gaitsgory's ongoing programme).

\paragraph{The identification conjecture.} The parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ acts on the $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $V_{k}(\frakg)$ via $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = k + h^\vee$. Volume~I defines a distinct parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, the chiral modular characteristic (Volume~I, Theorem~D), which for $V_k(\frakg)$ evaluates to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \dim(\frakg)(k + h^\vee)/(2 h^\vee)$. The two are proportional:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\frakg)) \;=\; \frac{\dim(\frakg)}{2 h^\vee} \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}.
\]
At the critical level both vanish. This proportionality is already unconditional for $V_k(\frakg)$; the conjectural extension is to general CY input.

\begin{conjecture}[QGL parameter equals shadow obstruction; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}
For every CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d = 2$, the quantum geometric Langlands parameter of the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence applied to $\Phi(\cC)$ coincides, up to the dimensional normalization $\dim(\frakg)/(2 h^\vee)$ in the Kac--Moody case, with the Vol~I chiral shadow parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ of the CY $2$-category. Equivalently, the Frenkel--Gaitsgory QGL deformation is the shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I (Volume~I, Theorem~D) evaluated along the critical-level family.
\end{conjecture}

Consequence of Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}:
the G/L/C/M shadow class of $\Phi(\cC)$ controls the convergence of the QGL deformation series in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$.
Class G (finite tower, $r = 2$) would correspond to polynomial deformations;
class L ($r = 3$, the Kac--Moody generic class) to rational-in-$\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ deformations;
class C ($r = 4$, $\beta\gamma$-type) to deformations with finite transcendence degree;
and class M (infinite tower, Virasoro and $W_N$) to formal power series with genuine transcendental content.
The critical-level limit $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \to 0$ is then the degeneration point of Volume~I at $k = -h^\vee$, consistent with Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}.

The conjecture implies (does not iff): the QGL duality exchanges $(\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}, G) \leftrightarrow (-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}, G^L)$, which under Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} should correspond to an involution on the space of CY $2$-categories with a distinguished $\frakg$-action. This is the $d = 2$ specialization of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}.

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow convergence class determines QGL analytic type; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl}
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY $2$-category with $\Phi(\cC)$ in shadow class $\mathcal{X} \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ (Volume~I classification).  The QGL deformation series of the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence applied to $\Phi(\cC)$, viewed as a formal power series in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, has the following analytic type:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item class G ($r = 2$): the series is polynomial in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, terminating at degree $1$;
 \item class L ($r = 3$): the series is a rational function of $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ with poles at roots of the Kac--Moody Kac determinant;
 \item class C ($r = 4$): the series converges in a disk $|\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}| < R_C$ with $R_C$ determined by the $\beta\gamma$-type spectral radius;
 \item class M ($r = \infty$): the series is Gevrey-$1$ divergent and requires Borel resummation, with Stokes data encoding the shadow tower.
\end{enumerate}
Conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}.  At the critical level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = 0$ all four types agree: the deformation is trivial and classical geometric Langlands holds.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The Costello--Gaiotto perspective (physical heuristic).}
The identifications in this paragraph are physical heuristics, not mathematical inputs: each is conjectural unless independently proved. Costello and Gaiotto derive quantum geometric Langlands from a twisted compactification of 4d $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super-Yang--Mills on a Riemann surface $C$ (Costello, 2013; Costello--Gaiotto, 2020).  In their framework the QGL parameter is the complexified gauge coupling $\Psi = k + h^\vee$, and the duality $\Psi \leftrightarrow -1/\Psi$ is S-duality of the 4d theory.  The chiral algebra on $C$ is produced by the holomorphic-topological twist of Volume~II; the bar complex of this chiral algebra (Volume~I, Theorem~A) encodes the factorization structure that Costello--Gaiotto identify with the Hecke action.  For CY $2$-category input, the expected statement is that the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_2$ applied to the B-model category of the Hitchin system reproduces the Costello--Gaiotto chiral algebra at the corresponding value of $\Psi$.  This identification is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands-hitchin} and the CY-A$_2$ correspondence (proved).

\section{K3 with ADE enhancement: the Feigin--Frenkel map}
\label{sec:k3-ade-langlands}
% ============================================================

At a du Val singularity of type $\frakg$ on a K3 surface $S$, the exceptional divisors of the minimal resolution generate a root lattice $\Lambda_\frakg \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Pic}(S) \hookrightarrow \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$.  The Mukai pairing restricts to the Cartan matrix on $\Lambda_\frakg$, normalised so that long roots have length-squared $2$.  Under the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_2$ (CY-A$_2$, proved at $d = 2$), the resulting chiral algebra $A_S := \Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(S)))$ contains $\hat{\frakg}_1$ (affine Kac--Moody at level $k = 1$) as a vertex subalgebra.  The level is \emph{forced}: the Mukai normalisation gives $\langle \alpha_i, \alpha_j \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}} = C_{ij}(\frakg)$, which is the level-$1$ OPE.

\paragraph{Level-$1$ central charge and the rank identity.}
For simply-laced $\frakg$ (types $A$, $D$, $E$), the identity $\dim(\frakg) = \mathrm{rank}(\frakg) \cdot (1 + h^\vee)$ implies
\[
 c(\hat{\frakg}_1) = \frac{\dim(\frakg) \cdot 1}{1 + h^\vee} = \mathrm{rank}(\frakg).
\]
Thus $c(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_1) = 1$, $c(\hat{\mathfrak{so}}_8{}_1) = 4$, $c(\hat{e}_{8,1}) = 8$.  The chiral modular characteristic is
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\hat{\frakg}_1) = \frac{\dim(\frakg)(1 + h^\vee)}{2 h^\vee}.
\]
For $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 9/4$.  For $\hat{e}_{8}$ at level $1$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1922/15$.  The ADE root lattice uses $\mathrm{rank}(\frakg)$ of the $24$ Mukai directions; the orthogonal complement $\Lambda_\frakg^\perp \subset \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ has rank $24 - \mathrm{rank}(\frakg)$ and carries the ``spectator'' Heisenberg currents.

\begin{remark}[Bi-based Ran-space factorisation site and the averaging morphism]
\label{rem:gl-DNA-bi-based-ran}
The K3 chiral bialgebra $A_S = \Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(S)))$ does not live on $\Ran(S)$ directly: its natural factorisation site is the \emph{bi-based Ran space}
\[
  (\Ran(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}),\; \overline{\cA}_2),
\]
where $E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}$ is the relative nodal elliptic curve over the base $\overline{\cA}_2$ of the Igusa compactification of the Siegel modular threefold $\cA_2 = \mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)\backslash\mathfrak{H}_2$, and the $24$ base points are the $24$ singular fibres of an elliptic K3 $\pi\colon S \to \bP^1$ (Kas--Kodaira, 1963; Miranda, 1989). The averaging map $\mathrm{av}\colon \frakg^{E_1} \to \frakg^{\mathrm{mod}}$ of Vol~I, in this setting, factors as a three-step composite
\[
  \mathrm{av} \;=\; \mathrm{Torelli}_{K3} \circ \mathrm{KugaSatake} \circ j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}},
\]
where $j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}}$ is the Kodaira embedding of the nodal $24$-curve into the universal elliptic surface, $\mathrm{KugaSatake}$ is the Kuga--Satake correspondence $H^2(S) \hookrightarrow H^1(A_{\mathrm{KS}})^{\otimes 2}$ to the Kuga--Satake abelian variety of dimension $2^{20}$ (Kuga--Satake, 1967; Deligne, 1972), and $\mathrm{Torelli}_{K3}$ is the global Torelli theorem sending period points to isomorphism classes of marked K3 surfaces (Pjatecki\v{\i}-\v{S}apiro--\v{S}afarevi\v{c}, 1971). The composite is the geometric-Langlands incarnation of the Vol~I averaging principle: the ordered chiral bialgebra on $(\Ran(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}), \overline{\cA}_2)$ becomes the symmetric chiral bialgebra on $\Ran(S)$ under the pushforward along $\mathrm{av}$. The $24$ base points are not accidental: they are the rank of $H^2(K3,\Z) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$, and their permutation action is by $M_{24}$ via the Steiner system $S(5,8,24)$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The critical-level deformation.}
The level-$1$ embedding $\hat{\frakg}_1 \hookrightarrow A_S$ deforms along the KM moduli $k \in \C$.  At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the Feigin--Frenkel theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}) produces the center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \cong \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$.  The deformation $k\colon 1 \to -h^\vee$ tracks a path in the shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I along which $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ decreases linearly from $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\hat{\frakg}_1)$ to $0$, at rate $d\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}/dk = \dim(\frakg)/(2 h^\vee)$.  The Sugawara central charge $c(k) = \dim(\frakg) k / (k + h^\vee)$ has a pole at $k = -h^\vee$ with residue $-\dim(\frakg) h^\vee$.  Conjecturally, the oper connection emerges from the shadow metric degeneration at $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$: the shadow metric $Q_L(0) = 4\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2 \to 0$ acquires a double zero, and the surviving connection data is the projective connection (oper) for the Langlands dual group $G^L$ (this ``degeneration-to-oper'' mechanism is the Vol~I shadow reading of Feigin--Frenkel, distinct from the Wakimoto proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}, and is the shadow-side content of Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}).

\paragraph{Opers for $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$ on K3.}
For $\frakg = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, the Langlands dual is $G^L = \mathrm{PGL}_2$ (same Lie algebra, type $A_1$).  A $\mathrm{PGL}_2$-oper on a curve $C$ of genus $g \geq 2$ is a projective connection $d^2/dt^2 + q(t)$, where $q \in H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes 2})$ is a quadratic differential.  The space of opers has dimension $\dim H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes 2}) = 3(g - 1)$.

The K3 connection: for a polarisation $L$ on $S$ with $L^2 = 2g - 2$, the generic curve $C \in |L|$ has genus $g$.  The restriction of the K3 holomorphic $2$-form $\omega_S$ to a tubular neighbourhood of $C$ determines, conjecturally, a quadratic differential on $C$ and hence an oper.  The oper moduli at genus $g$ are summarised by the Casimir grading: for general ADE type $\frakg$ with Langlands dual $\frakg^L$ of rank $r$, the Casimir degrees $d_1, \ldots, d_r$ give $\dim H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes d_i}) = (2d_i - 1)(g - 1)$, summing to $\dim(\frakg^L)(g - 1)$.  This sum equals the Hitchin base dimension (Hitchin--Beilinson--Drinfeld correspondence).

\section{K3 Yangian and quantum geometric Langlands duality}
\label{sec:k3-yangian-langlands}
% ============================================================

The K3 Yangian $Y(\frakg_{K3})$ of Section~\ref{sec:k3-yangian-quantization} (status: \ClaimStatusConjectured{}) has, at each ADE enhancement, a subalgebra $Y(\hat{\frakg}_k)$.  The quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (Frenkel--Gaitsgory) acts on this subalgebra via the level duality $k \mapsto k^L$.

\paragraph{The QGL and FF involutions.}
For simply-laced types, two involutions act on the level parameter $k$:
\begin{align}
 \text{QGL:}\quad k &\mapsto k^L = -k - h^\vee, \label{eq:qgl-involution} \\
 \text{FF:}\quad k &\mapsto k' = -k - 2h^\vee. \label{eq:ff-involution}
\end{align}
These are \emph{different}: the QGL involution exchanges category $\mathcal{O}_k(\hat{\frakg})$ and $\mathcal{O}_{k^L}(\hat{\frakg}^L)$ (quantum Langlands duality), while the FF involution exchanges $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k)$ and $-\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k')$ (Koszul duality of Vol~I Theorem~A).  Their distinguished points:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
 & QGL fixed point & FF fixed point \\
\hline
$k$ value & $-h^\vee/2$ & $-h^\vee$ (critical level) \\
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ value & $\frac{\dim(\frakg)}{4}$ & $0$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both are involutions ($k^{LL} = k$ and $k'' = k$).  The QGL involution satisfies the reciprocal identity
\[
 \Psi(k) \cdot \Psi(k^L) = 1, \qquad \Psi := \frac{k}{k + h^\vee},
\]
for the chapter's coupling $\Psi$; this is a reflection of the coupling through $\Psi = 1$, formally analogous to but distinct from the Kapustin--Witten S-duality $\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}} \to -1/\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}}$ with $\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}} = k + h^\vee$.  The FF involution satisfies $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k') = 0$.  These two properties are \emph{not} simultaneously satisfied by any single involution.

\paragraph{Application to K3 with $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$.}
For $\frakg = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ ($h^\vee = 2$) at $k = 1$:
\begin{itemize}
 \item QGL dual level: $k^L = -1 - 2 = -3$.  Then $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-3) = 3(-3 + 2)/4 = -3/4$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(1) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-3) = 9/4 - 3/4 = 3/2 \neq 0$.
 \item FF dual level: $k' = -1 - 4 = -5$.  Then $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-5) = 3(-5 + 2)/4 = -9/4$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(1) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-5) = 0$.
 \item Critical level: $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = -2$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ and $c$ undefined (pole).  The QGL dual of $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is $k^L = 0$ (classical limit of the dual); the FF dual is $k' = -2$ (fixed point).
\end{itemize}
The Langlands dual of the K3 $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$ in the QGL sense is $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $-3$.  This is a \emph{negative} level, corresponding to the ``wrong sign'' category $\mathcal{O}$, which is the correct home for the spectral side of quantum Langlands.

\begin{conjecture}[K3 Yangian QGL duality; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:k3-yangian-qgl}
For a K3 surface $S$ with ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$, the subalgebra $Y(\hat{\frakg}_1) \subset Y(\frakg_{K3})$ and the QGL-dual subalgebra $Y(\hat{\frakg}_{k^L}) \subset Y(\frakg_{K3^L})$ (where $K3^L$ is the Mukai mirror) are related by the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence: the category $\mathcal{O}$ of $Y(\hat{\frakg}_1)$ at level $1$ is equivalent to the category $\mathcal{O}$ of $Y(\hat{\frakg}_{k^L})$ at the dual level $k^L = -1 - h^\vee$. This is conditional on the existence of $Y(\frakg_{K3})$ and on CY-A$_2$ for the correspondence $\Phi_2$.
\end{conjecture}

\section{K3 $\times$ E and D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(E)$}
\label{sec:k3xe-dmodules}
% ============================================================

For an elliptically fibered K3 surface $\pi\colon S \to \bP^1$ with section, the product $S \times E$ (where $E$ is an elliptic curve) is a Calabi--Yau $3$-fold.  The chiral algebra $\Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(S \times E)))$ exists by CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}); its factored structure $\Phi(S) \otimes \Phi(E)$ is conditionally accessible via CY-A$_2$ on each factor (the factorisation itself is not established; see Conjecture~\ref{conj:k3xe-hecke}).

\paragraph{The family over $E$.}
At an ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$ on $S$, the chiral subalgebra $\hat{\frakg}_1 \subset \Phi(S)$ produces, upon tensor with the chiral structure on $E$, a family of chiral modules parametrised by $z \in E$.  The resulting object over $\Ran(E)$ is a factorization sheaf.  The Frenkel--Gaitsgory localisation functor $\Delta_E$ of Section~\ref{sec:feigin-frenkel} sends this to a D-module on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(E)$.

\paragraph{$\mathrm{Bun}_G$ for an elliptic curve.}
For a reductive group $G$, the moduli stack of semi-stable $G$-bundles on an elliptic curve $E$ has the concrete description
\[
 \mathrm{Bun}_G(E) \;=\; (E \otimes_\Z \Lambda_{\mathrm{cochar}}) \,/\, W,
\]
where $\Lambda_{\mathrm{cochar}}$ is the cocharacter lattice and $W$ is the Weyl group.  For $G = \mathrm{SL}_2$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_2}(E) = E / (\Z/2\Z) \cong \bP^1$ (the Kummer quotient, with $4$ ramification points at the $2$-torsion of $E$).  For $G = \mathrm{SL}_3$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_3}(E) = (E \times E) / S_3$.  For $G = E_8$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{E_8}(E) = E^8 / W(E_8)$, coarse moduli of dimension $8$.

\begin{conjecture}[K3 $\times$ E Hecke eigensheaf; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:k3xe-hecke}
Let $S$ be a K3 surface with $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ enhancement at level $1$, and let $E$ be an elliptic curve.  The D-module $\Delta_E(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2,1})$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_2}(E) \cong \bP^1$ is a Hecke eigensheaf for the $\mathrm{PGL}_2$-local system on $E$ determined by the K3 periods.  By CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}), $A_{S \times E} = \Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(S \times E)))$ exists; the remaining conditionality is the factorisation $\Phi(S \times E) \simeq \Phi(S) \otimes \Phi(E)$ (not established) and the identification of the D-module with a Hecke eigensheaf.
\end{conjecture}

The factorisation hypothesis $\Phi_3(S \times E) \simeq \Phi_2(S) \otimes \Phi_1(E)$ is not established: the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ is constructed for individual CY categories, and the tensor product of chiral algebras over $\Ran$ is a nontrivial operation.  For the K3 factor alone, $\Phi_2(S)$ is accessible via CY-A$_2$ (proved); for the elliptic curve $E$, the chiral theory is the rank-$1$ Heisenberg (also proved).  The difficulty is in the interaction terms.

\section{Shadow depth and Langlands parameter type}
\label{sec:shadow-langlands-type}
% ============================================================

The shadow class $\mathcal{X} \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ of Volume~I classifies chiral algebras by the depth of their shadow obstruction tower.  Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl} identifies this classification with the analytic type of the QGL deformation series.  We now refine this identification to a correspondence with types of Langlands parameters.

\paragraph{The conjectured dictionary.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
Shadow class & Shadow depth & QGL analytic type & Langlands parameter \\
\hline
G & $2$ & polynomial & unramified \\
L & $3$ & rational & tamely ramified \\
C & $4$ & convergent & wildly ramified (regular) \\
M & $\infty$ & Gevrey-$1$ divergent & irregular \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The logic: in the QGL correspondence of Frenkel--Gaitsgory, the spectral side consists of twisted D-modules at level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$, and the automorphic side of twisted D-modules at level $-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ for $G^L$.  The singularity structure of D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ is classified by the ramification type of the $G^L$-local system on $X$: unramified (smooth), tamely ramified (regular singular), and wildly ramified (irregular singular).  If $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ is identified with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}), then the convergence type of the $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-series, which is controlled by the shadow depth (Volume~I, Theorem~D), determines the singularity type of the Langlands parameter.

\paragraph{K3 ADE enhancement: class L.}
At an ADE enhancement on K3, the Kac--Moody subalgebra $\hat{\frakg}_1$ has shadow class L (proved, Volume~I).  The conjectured dictionary maps class L to tamely ramified Langlands parameters: the D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ arising from the K3 chiral algebra should have regular singularities, controlled by the cubic shadow $\alpha$ of the KM family.  The poles of the rational QGL series at roots of the Kac determinant would then correspond to the reducibility loci of tamely ramified local systems.

\paragraph{Generic K3: class G.}
For a generic K3 surface without ADE enhancement, the chiral algebra $\Phi(S)$ is a free-field theory (Heisenberg of rank $24$), shadow class G.  The QGL series is polynomial (degree $1$), and the conjectured Langlands parameter is unramified.  This is consistent with the Frenkel--Gaitsgory expectation: for free-field (commutative) chiral algebras, the Langlands correspondence reduces to the classical abelian case (Fourier--Mukai for the Heisenberg, which is the abelian geometric Langlands).

\paragraph{W-algebras: class M.}
The Virasoro and $W_N$-algebras have shadow class M (infinite depth, $r = \infty$).  If a CY category $\cC$ has $\Phi(\cC)$ of class M (as happens for the local $\bP^2$ chiral algebra), the QGL series is Gevrey-$1$ divergent.  The Borel resummation required to extract finite answers corresponds, conjecturally, to the Stokes phenomenon for irregular Langlands parameters.  The Stokes data would encode the shadow tower of Volume~I.

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow depth equals ramification depth; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:shadow-ramification}
For a smooth proper CY $2$-category $\cC$, the shadow depth $r(\Phi(\cC))$ of Volume~I equals the ramification depth of the associated Langlands parameter: $r = 2$ is unramified, $r = 3$ is tame, $r = 4$ is regular wild, and $r = \infty$ is irregular.  This is a refinement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl}, replacing ``analytic type'' with ``ramification type.''  Conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} and on CY-A$_2$ for the correspondence $\Phi_2$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Igusa $\Delta_5$ as the classifying invariant]
\label{rem:gl-DNA-igusa-invariant}
The ramification-type classification refined in Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-ramification} admits a modular incarnation. The $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$-equivariant cohomology of the chiral bialgebra deformation Lie algebra~$\frakg_{\Delta_5}$ controlling K3 period variation carries a canonical class:
\[
  H^2(\frakg_{\Delta_5})^{\Z/2,\,K(1)} \;\cong\; \C \cdot \Delta_5,
\]
where $\Delta_5 \in M_5(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z))$ is the Igusa scalar-valued cusp form of weight~$5$ (Igusa, 1964, \emph{On Siegel modular forms of genus two. II}) — the unique generator of its weight space modulo the $\Z/2$-Atkin--Lehner involution and $K(1)$-level structure. The classifying-space content: $\Delta_5$ is \emph{the} invariant separating ramification classes of the $\Phi$-image, with its vanishing locus
\[
  V(\Delta_5) \;=\; \{\text{Humbert surfaces}\} \;\subset\; \cA_2
\]
detecting the loci where class~L degenerates to class~G (cf.\ van der Geer, 1987, \emph{Siegel modular forms and their applications}; Freitag~1983, \emph{Siegel's modular forms and Dirichlet series}). Under the Langlands-dictionary refinement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-ramification}, Humbert loci correspond to reducibility loci of tamely ramified $G^L$-local systems, and $\Delta_5$ is their defining equation. This is the modular incarnation of Volume~I's shadow classification: the shadow depth $r(\Phi(\cC))$ is read off algebraically from the vanishing order of $\Delta_5$ at the period point $\tau(\cC) \in \cA_2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Chiral quantization of the Hitchin system on K3 curves}
\label{sec:chiral-hitchin-k3}
% ============================================================

The preceding sections construct geometric Langlands data from the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ applied to abstract CY categories.  This section specializes to a concrete geometric source: curves $C$ inside a K3 surface~$S$.  The Hitchin system $\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})$ on such a curve, combined with the K3 adjunction $K_C = L|_C$ (where $K_S = \mathcal{O}_S$), connects the Hitchin spectral parameter to the K3 Yangian spectral parameter~$z$.  All new identifications in this section are \textbf{conjectural} unless stated otherwise.

\paragraph{K3 linear systems.}
Let $S$ be a K3 surface and $L$ a line bundle on~$S$ with $L^2 = 2g - 2 > 0$.  By Riemann--Roch on~K3, $\chi(L) = L^2/2 + 2 = g + 1$, and for $L$ nef and big, $h^0(S, L) = g + 1$ (Kodaira vanishing gives $h^1 = h^2 = 0$).  The complete linear system $|L| \cong \bP^g$ parametrises a family of curves of genus~$g$.  A generic member $C \in |L|$ is smooth and connected.

The adjunction formula gives
\[
  K_C \;=\; (K_S \otimes L)\big|_C \;=\; L\big|_C,
\]
since $K_S = \mathcal{O}_S$ for K3.  This identification $K_C = L|_C$ is the structural input from the K3 embedding: the canonical bundle of~$C$ is the \emph{restriction of the K3 polarisation}.

\paragraph{Hitchin system on $C \subset S$.}
For a reductive group~$\mathbf{G}$ with Lie algebra $\frakg$, the Hitchin moduli space
\[
  \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G}) \;=\; T^*\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C)
\]
is a hyperk\"ahler manifold of complex dimension $2\dim(\frakg)(g-1)$, with Hitchin fibration $\pi\colon \mathcal{M}_H \to B_H$ where
\[
  B_H \;=\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r} H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes d_i}), \qquad \dim B_H = \dim(\frakg)(g-1),
\]
with $d_1, \ldots, d_r$ the Casimir degrees of~$\frakg$.  The fibration is Lagrangian: $\dim \mathcal{M}_H = 2\dim B_H$.

For $\mathbf{G} = \mathrm{SL}_2$ and $g = 2$: $\dim B_H = 3$ and $\dim \mathcal{M}_H = 6$.  This is the unique ``CY$_3$-type'' Hitchin system, where the Lagrangian fibration has $3$-dimensional base and fibre (cf.~\texttt{hitchin\_sl2\_genus2.py}).

\paragraph{The spectral parameter from K3 geometry.}
The spectral curve $\Sigma \subset \mathrm{Tot}(K_C)$ is defined by $\det(\lambda \cdot I - \varphi) = 0$ for the Higgs field~$\varphi$.  The K3 identification $K_C = L|_C$ embeds the spectral curve into $\mathrm{Tot}(L|_C)$, which is a tubular neighbourhood of~$C$ in~$S$.  The tautological section $\lambda \in H^0(\Sigma, \pi^*K_C)$ is then a coordinate on the total space of~$L$ restricted to~$C$: the \emph{Hitchin spectral parameter is a local coordinate on K3 transverse to~$C$}.

\begin{conjecture}[Hitchin spectral parameter = K3 Yangian parameter; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian}
For a K3 surface~$S$ with ADE enhancement of type~$\frakg$, the Hitchin spectral parameter $\lambda \in \mathrm{Tot}(L|_C)$ for a curve $C \in |L|$ identifies with the K3 Yangian spectral parameter~$z$ entering the Drinfeld coproduct $\Delta_z$. The identification is:
\[
  z \;=\; \lambda,
\]
where both live in the fibres of $L|_C$. This is conditional on CY-A$_2$ (proved at $d = 2$) and on the existence of $Y(\frakg_{K3})$.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Chiral differential operators and the three-step functor.}
The Beilinson--Drinfeld quantization of the Hitchin system produces chiral differential operators (CDO) on $\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C)$, which are modules for $\hat{\frakg}_{-h^\vee}$ at the critical level.  The Frenkel--Gaitsgory localization functor $\Delta_C$ sends these to D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C)$.  For $C \subset S$ with ADE enhancement, the K3 structure conjecturally promotes the D-modules to $Y(\frakg_{K3})$-modules:
\[
  \mathrm{CDO}_{\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C), \mathrm{crit}}
  \;\xrightarrow{\text{BD}}{}
  \hat{\frakg}_{-h^\vee}\text{-mod}
  \;\xrightarrow{\Delta_C}{}
  D\text{-mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C))
  \;\xrightarrow{\text{K3}}{}
  Y(\frakg_{K3})\text{-mod}.
\]
The first two arrows are \ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere{} (Beilinson--Drinfeld; Frenkel--Gaitsgory).  The third arrow is the content of the following conjecture.

\begin{conjecture}[Hitchin-to-Yangian module functor; \ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:hitchin-yangian-module}
For a K3 surface~$S$ with ADE enhancement of type~$\frakg$ and a smooth curve $C \in |L|$ of genus $g \geq 2$, there exists a functor
\[
  D\text{-mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C)) \;\longrightarrow\; Y(\frakg_{K3})\text{-mod}
\]
induced by the spectral parameter identification of Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian} and the variation of~$C$ in the linear system~$|L|$.  As $C$ varies in $|L| \cong \bP^g$, the D-modules assemble into a factorization sheaf on $\mathrm{Ran}(|L|)$ valued in $Y(\frakg_{K3})$-modules.

This is conditional on: (i) the existence of $Y(\frakg_{K3})$; (ii) CY-A$_2$ for the functor~$\Phi$; (iii) Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian}.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Kappa analysis.}

\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\hat{\frakg}_1) = \frac{\dim(\frakg)(1 + h^\vee)}{2h^\vee}.
\]
The Koszul conductor is verified: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k') = 0$ where $k' = -k - 2h^\vee$ is the Feigin--Frenkel reflection.  For $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at $k = 1$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 9/4$, $k' = -5$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-5) = -9/4$.

A key observation: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ depends on the group~$\frakg$ and the level~$k$ but is \emph{independent of the genus}~$g$ of~$C$.  As $C$ varies in~$|L|$, the anomaly is constant across the family.  The genus enters only the dimensional data ($\dim\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}$, $\dim B_H$, $\dim\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}$).

\section{Derived Satake for chiral quantum groups}
\label{sec:derived-satake-chiral}
% ============================================================

The geometric Satake equivalence is the foundational theorem of the geometric Langlands programme.
The chiral extension of Satake, placed in the CY--chiral dictionary, expands on open problem~(v) below.
Every new formal statement is a \textbf{conjecture}: the arrows that compose the chiral Satake pass through the unconstructed quantum vertex chiral group $G(X)$ at $d = 3$ (CY-C); the chiral algebra $A_\cC$ itself exists by CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).

\paragraph{Classical and derived Satake.}
The Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen geometric Satake (2007) identifies the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian~$\mathrm{Gr}_G = G(\!(t)\!)/G[\![t]\!]$ with representations of the Langlands dual group:
\[
  \mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \;\simeq\; \Rep(G^L),
\]
as \emph{symmetric} monoidal categories, where the monoidal structure on the left is the convolution product of Lusztig and on the right the tensor product of representations.
The derived refinement of Bezrukavnikov--Finkelberg (2008) lifts this to DG~categories:
\[
  D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{N}^L / G^L),
\]
where $\mathcal{N}^L$ is the nilpotent cone in~$\frakg^L$.
Both statements are theorems (\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere{}).
The commutativity constraint on $\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$ (the natural isomorphism $\mathcal{F}_1 * \mathcal{F}_2 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{F}_2 * \mathcal{F}_1$ satisfying $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$) is the geometric source of the \emph{symmetric} monoidal structure on $\Rep(G^L)$.

\paragraph{The chiral affine Grassmannian.}
The Beilinson--Drinfeld construction (\emph{Chiral Algebras}, Ch.~5) promotes $\mathrm{Gr}_G$ to a \emph{factorization ind-scheme} $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G$ over the Ran space $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ of a curve~$C$.
The fiber over a finite subset $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\} \in \mathrm{Ran}(C)$ parametrises $G$-bundles on the formal neighbourhood of $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ with trivialization outside.
The key property:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Separated points}: $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G(\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}) \simeq \prod_i \mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G(\{x_i\})$ when the $x_i$ are disjoint (the fiber splits, encoding the \emph{tensor product} of representations).
 \item \emph{Collision}: as $x_1 \to x_2$, the fiber does \emph{not} split; the non-splitting encodes the OPE/fusion product and, dually, the Drinfeld coproduct~$\Delta_z$ on the quantum group side.
\end{itemize}
The chiral Grassmannian exists as a mathematical object (\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere{}: Beilinson--Drinfeld).
What is \emph{conjectural} is the identification of D-modules on it with chiral quantum group representations.

\paragraph{Convolution as the geometric coproduct.}
The convolution product on $\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$,
\[
  \mathcal{F}_1 * \mathcal{F}_2 \;=\; m_*(\mathcal{F}_1 \boxtimes \mathcal{F}_2),
\]
where $m\colon \mathrm{Gr}_G \tilde{\times}^{G[\![t]\!]} \mathrm{Gr}_G \to \mathrm{Gr}_G$ is the convolution Grassmannian, becomes in the chiral setting the \emph{factorization product}:
\[
  \mathcal{F}_1 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_2 \;=\; \text{factorization product over } \mathrm{Ran}(C).
\]
Under Satake duality, convolution on sheaves corresponds to the tensor product on representations; dually, it corresponds to the coproduct on the algebra.
For the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ with structure function~$g(u)$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
  Convolution of charge-$n$ and charge-$m$ sheaves & $\longleftrightarrow$ & tensor product $\cF_n \otimes \cF_m$ \\
  Factorization product as $x_1 \to x_2$ & $\longleftrightarrow$ & Drinfeld coproduct $\Delta_z(T(u)) = T_L(u)\,T_R(u - z)$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The spectral parameter $z$ is the separation in $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ between the two factorization points.

\paragraph{The geometric $R$-matrix.}
In the classical Satake, the commutativity constraint $c_{\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2}$ with $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$ makes the category \emph{symmetric} monoidal.
In the chiral setting, the commutativity constraint is replaced by an $\Etwo$-\emph{braiding}:
\[
  \beta_{\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2}(z) \colon \mathcal{F}_1 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_2 \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{F}_2 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_1,
\]
which is the geometric $R$-matrix.
The braiding arises from the holonomy of the factorization connection along a path in $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ that exchanges $x_1$ and $x_2$, and it depends on $z = x_1 - x_2$ (the spectral separation).
On the charge-$1$ Fock representation for $G = \mathrm{GL}_1$ on $\C^3$ with Omega-background $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$, $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$:
\[
  R(z) \;=\; g(z) \;=\; \frac{(z - h_1)(z - h_2)(z - h_3)}{(z + h_1)(z + h_2)(z + h_3)},
\]
which is the Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope $R$-matrix (Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-chiral-rmatrix}).
The unitarity $R(z)\,R(-z) = 1$ follows algebraically from the CY condition (each factor $(z-h_i)/(z+h_i)$ paired with $(-z-h_i)/(-z+h_i) = (z+h_i)/(z-h_i)$ gives $1$).

The braiding is \emph{not symmetric}: $R(z) \neq \mathrm{id}$ for generic $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$.
This non-symmetry is the geometric manifestation of the $\Etwo$ (braided, not symmetric) structure on chiral quantum group representations, distinguishing the chiral Satake from its classical predecessor.
The $R$-matrix is characterized via the half-braiding on the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+))$, not by applying $\Delta$ to the vacuum.

\section{The Langlands functor from chiral quantum groups}
\label{sec:langlands-functor-chiral-qg}
% ============================================================

The preceding sections develop individual aspects of the CY--Langlands correspondence: the Feigin--Frenkel center (Section~\ref{sec:feigin-frenkel}), QGL duality (Section~\ref{sec:quantum-geom-langlands}), K3 Yangian--oper bridge (Section~\ref{sec:k3-ade-langlands}), and shadow--ramification (Section~\ref{sec:shadow-langlands-type}).  This section assembles them into a unified five-step \emph{Langlands functor} from CY categories to Langlands data, and records the three involutions that act on the level parameter.

\paragraph{The five-step pipeline.}
For a CY $2$-category $\cC$ with ADE enhancement of type~$\frakg$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step~\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{CY-to-chiral} (CY-A$_2$, proved at $d = 2$): the correspondence $\Phi_2$ sends $\cC = D^b(\Coh(S))$ to an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $A_S$ containing $\hat{\frakg}_1$.
  \item \textbf{Bar complex} (Vol~I Theorem~A, proved): the bar functor $B$ sends $A_S$ to a factorization coalgebra $T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_S)$ with deconcatenation coproduct.
  \item \textbf{Verdier dual} (Vol~I Theorem~A, proved): the Ran-space Verdier dual $D_{\Ran}$ applied to $B(A_S)$ gives the Koszul dual $A_S^! = D_{\Ran}(B(A_S))$.
  \item \textbf{Chiral center} (Feigin--Frenkel 1992, proved elsewhere): at the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) = Z(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \cong \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$.
  \item \textbf{Oper identification} (\textbf{conjectural}): the CY chiral center at critical level, via the Verdier leg, is quasi-isomorphic to $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ for CY input.
\end{enumerate}
Steps 1--4 are proved (for KM input); Step~5 is the content of Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}. \section{Open problems}
\label{sec:open-problems}
% ============================================================

The following six problems are the concrete frontier of CY Langlands. Each is stated as an open problem, not a conjecture, because the evidence is not yet sufficient to commit to a definite equivalence.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] \textbf{K3 mirror map as $d = 2$ Langlands.} Is the Mukai--Dolgachev--Nikulin mirror map on lattice-polarized K3 categories the $d = 2$ Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}? A first test: verify that $\Phi$ sends mirror K3 pairs to Koszul-dual $\Etwo$-chiral algebras in the Volume~I sense.
 \item[(ii)] \textbf{BPS Langlands for $d = 3$.} For a CY $3$-fold $X$ with mirror $X^\vee$, are the conjectural quantum vertex chiral groups $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$ (CY-C) Langlands dual as $\Eone$-chiral algebras? By CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}) the chiral algebras $A_X$ and $A_{X^\vee}$ exist; the open content is the construction of $G(X)$ (CY-C) and the Langlands duality relation itself. The CoHAs of $X$ and $X^\vee$ are associative algebras, not chiral algebras; they provide the $\Eone$-ordered halves of the conjectural $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$.
 \item[(iii)] \textbf{QGL parameter equals shadow $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.} Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} for the Kac--Moody family as a theorem (it is currently only a proportionality of definitions), then extend to $\Phi(\cC)$ for CY $2$-categories $\cC$. The key step is to identify the shadow tower of $\Phi(\cC)$ with the Gaitsgory deformation at finite $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$.
 \item[(iv)] \textbf{Categorified Feigin--Frenkel.} Does there exist a CY $2$-category $\cC_{\mathrm{FF}}$ whose image $\Phi(\cC_{\mathrm{FF}})$ is, at the critical level, equivalent to the Feigin--Frenkel center $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ viewed as a constant $\Etwo$-chiral algebra? If so, $\cC_{\mathrm{FF}}$ would be a geometric realization of opers at the categorical level.
 \item[(v)] \textbf{Geometric Satake from CY.} The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen, 2007) identifies the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with $\Rep(G^L)$. Can the equivalence be derived from a CY $3$-category $\cC_{\mathrm{Sat}}$ via $\Phi$, so that $\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Phi(\cC_{\mathrm{Sat}})) \simeq \mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$? Supporting evidence: both sides carry braided monoidal structures; both admit fusion constructions; both are controlled by an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple at the critical level.
 \item[(vi)] \textbf{Hitchin-K3 family and K3 Yangian modules.} For a K3 surface $S$ with ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$ and a linear system $|L| \cong \bP^g$ of genus-$g$ curves, does the family of Beilinson--Drinfeld quantizations $\{\mathrm{CDO}_{\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C_t), \mathrm{crit}}\}_{t \in |L|}$ produce a factorization sheaf on $\mathrm{Ran}(|L|)$ valued in $Y(\frakg_{K3})$-modules (Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-yangian-module})? The first test case is $\mathbf{G} = \mathrm{SL}_2$, $g = 2$: the Hitchin base is $3$-dimensional, the spectral curve has genus~$5$, and the linear system is~$\bP^2$.  The Hitchin spectral parameter identification $\lambda = z$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian}) is the mechanism; evidence would require computing the factorization structure explicitly for one ADE type.
\end{enumerate}

Problems (i)--(iii) are the $d = 2$ core and can in principle be attacked with the technology of Volume~I and the $d = 2$ functor of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}.
Problems (iv)--(v) lie beyond the current reach:
(iv) asks to reverse-engineer a CY $2$-category from a target chiral algebra, and
(v) asks to place the geometric Satake equivalence, which is the foundational theorem of the geometric Langlands programme, inside the CY--chiral dictionary.
Problem~(vi) is the K3-specific instantiation of the Hitchin-K3 programme of Section~\ref{sec:chiral-hitchin-k3}; it is the most concrete of the six problems, with explicit numerical data computed by \texttt{chiral\_hitchin\_k3.py}.
The payoff would be a derivation of local geometric Langlands from Calabi--Yau geometry alone.
