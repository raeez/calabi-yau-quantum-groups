\begin{remark}[Gaiotto curve correction]
\label{rem:gaiotto-curve-correction}
The class-$\mathcal{S}$ construction used here has Gaiotto curve $\Sigma_{0,24}$, the $24$-punctured sphere, rather than the genus-$2$ closed surface $\Sigma_{2,0}$. Chacaltana--Distler \cite{ChacaltanaDistler2010}, Table~3, row~1: at $n = 24$ full punctures the $4$-dimensional $\cN = 2$ central charge is
\[
c_{4d} \;=\; \frac{5n - 13}{6} \;=\; \frac{107}{6},
\qquad c_{2d} \;=\; -12\, c_{4d} \;=\; -214.
\]
The vertex operator algebra $\mathcal{V}_{24}$ coinciding with the Beem--Rastelli \cite{BeemRastelli2014} chiral-algebra image $\mathrm{VOA}[T_{24}]$ is the iterated Drinfeld--Sokolov reduction
\[
\mathcal{V}_{24} \;=\; H^0_{DS}\!\left( L_{-2 + 1/22}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)^{\otimes 22} \right)
\]
per Arakawa \cite{Arakawa2017} together with Creutzig--Linshaw \cite{CreutzigLinshaw2017}, \emph{not} $L_{-6}(\mathfrak{e}_8)$. The distinguishing computation: the affine $\mathfrak{e}_8$ candidate gives
\[
c\!\left(L_{-6}(\mathfrak{e}_8)\right) \;=\; -\frac{6 \cdot 248}{24} \;=\; -62 \;\neq\; -214 \;=\; c_{2d},
\]
which rules $L_{-6}(\mathfrak{e}_8)$ out. The iterated $H^0_{DS}$ construction matches $c_{2d} = -214$ by Arakawa's central-charge formula for principal $W$-algebras composed with Creutzig--Linshaw coset reduction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Humbert locus match at the Heisenberg--Mukai level]
\label{rem:humbert-heisenberg-mukai}
The Humbert-locus identification with $\Delta_5^{-2}$ is a statement at the Heisenberg--Mukai $\eta^{-48}$ level, \emph{not} at the bare $\Delta_5$ level. The governing identity, following Gritsenko--Nikulin \cite{GritsenkoNikulin1996} with the Mukai-doubled Heisenberg character, is
\[
\chi_{\mathrm{Heis}(\mathrm{Muk}^{\oplus 2})}(q)
\;=\; \prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^n)^{-48}
\;=\; -\, q^{-2}\, \bigl[ (2\pi i z)^2\, \Delta_5^{-2} \bigr]_{H_1,\, z = 0},
\]
where $[\,\cdot\,]_{H_1, z=0}$ extracts the Humbert-$1$ Fourier--Jacobi coefficient in the elliptic variable $z$. The weight-$10$ denominator $\Delta_5^2$ (equivalently the unnormalised Igusa square $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}$) pairs with the Mukai-doubled Heisenberg character $\eta^{-48}$ through the $(2\pi i z)^2$ Jacobi prefactor; the Humbert locus match is therefore a \emph{Heisenberg--Mukai-enhanced} identity, and any ``match to $\Delta_5^{-2}$'' at the scalar level is shorthand for the displayed $\eta^{-48}$ identity at Humbert~$1$.
\end{remark}


The two-stage factorisation $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}, Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}) sends a Calabi--Yau category to a holomorphic factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the CY$_d$ variety and then specialises to a chiral algebra on a reference curve~$C$ ($\Etwo$ at $d \leq 2$ via Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}; $\Eone$ at $d \geq 3$); the bar complex of the output (Volume~I, Theorem~A) is the factorization invariant on which geometric Langlands is stated. Geometric Langlands connections in this chapter operate at the canonical $\PhiFA_d$ level: the factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the curve or surface is the primary geometric object, and specialisation cycles $\Sigma_{d-1}$ select the specific chiral shadow (Remark~\ref{rem:multiple-e1-specialisations}). At the critical level the Feigin--Frenkel theorem identifies the chiral center with the algebra of $G^L$-opers; the Verdier intertwining of Volume~I Theorem~A then relates local geometric Langlands to the four-functor picture (bar, cobar, Verdier, derived center). For Calabi--Yau input, the analogue is conjectural: a Langlands dual of a CY $d$-category should realize the mirror of its $\Phi$-image. Literal Feigin--Frenkel and Frenkel--Gaitsgory inputs are cited as theorems; CY analogues are formulated as conjectures with their hypotheses explicit.

\begin{remark}[Stage discipline for geometric Langlands connections]
\label{rem:gl-stage-discipline}
The three Langlands data streams that enter this chapter live on
different stages of the two-stage factorisation, and each theorem is
labelled with its stage below.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item \textbf{Feigin--Frenkel centre at the critical level:} stage-$1$.
The centre $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) = Z(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg))$ and the
oper identification $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \simeq
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ are properties of the canonical
$\PhiFA_d$-side factorisation algebra on curves; the factorisation
structure on $\Ran(X)$ is the native home of the Feigin--Frenkel
algebra.
\item \textbf{D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ and derived Satake:}
$(\infty, 1)$-categorical / $\PhiFA_d$-level. The Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen and
Bezrukavnikov--Finkelberg equivalences operate on
$D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$ and $D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_G)$;
both are $(\infty, 1)$-categorical objects living on the stage-$1$ side
of the factorisation, and the chiral algebraic inputs to them
(Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral localisation, factorisation envelopes) are
properties of $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ before any $(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$-specialisation.
\item \textbf{Koszul duality to quantum groups:} stage-$2$ shadow. The
Koszul reflection on a chiral algebra $A$, the Verdier dual
$A^! = D_{\Ran}(\barB(A))$, and the Vol~I Koszul conductor $K(A) + K(A^!)$
all live on the stage-$2$ chiral output $A_\cC \in \EnHolFA(C)$ on the
reference curve. The shadow-depth classification
$\{G, L, C, M\}$ is a stage-$2$ invariant. The Frenkel--Gaitsgory QGL
parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ is a stage-$2$ quantity whose
identification with the Vol~I $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}) sits on the stage-$2$ side.
\end{enumerate}
The CY side of Langlands is the $(\infty, 1)$-categorical stage-$1$
input $\cC$ itself; the chiral side is the stage-$2$ image $A_\cC$; the
bridge through $\Phi_d$ factors through the stage-$1$ intermediate
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$. Each conjecture below records the stage on which its
hypothesis lives.
\end{remark}

\section{The Feigin--Frenkel center at the critical level}
\label{sec:feigin-frenkel}
% ============================================================

Let $\frakg$ be a simple finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and $\hat{\frakg}_k$ its affine Kac--Moody algebra at level $k$. The vacuum vertex algebra $V_k(\frakg)$ is the universal chiral algebra generated by the currents $J^a(z)$ with the Kac--Moody OPE. The \emph{critical level} is $k_c = -h^\vee$, where $h^\vee$ is the dual Coxeter number.

\begin{theorem}[Feigin--Frenkel, 1992]
\label{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}
At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the center of the vacuum vertex algebra is canonically isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of $G^L$-opers on the formal disk:
\[
 \mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \;:=\; Z(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \mathrm{Fun}\bigl(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)\bigr).
\]
Here $G^L$ is the Langlands dual group and $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)$ is the scheme of $G^L$-opers on the formal disk $D = \mathrm{Spec}\, \C[[t]]$.
\end{theorem}

The isomorphism is the content of Feigin--Frenkel (1992), with the full exposition in Frenkel's book \emph{Langlands Correspondence for Loop Groups} (2007). Off the critical level the center is trivial; at $k = -h^\vee$ it becomes a commutative vertex subalgebra of maximal size, and the resulting ind-scheme is $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)$.

\paragraph{Free-field resolution and the bar complex.}
The proof of the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism proceeds via the Wakimoto realization: a free-field embedding $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg) \hookrightarrow \Pi_{-h^\vee}$ into a $\beta\gamma$-system tensored with a Heisenberg algebra, followed by a BRST reduction. The bar complex $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) = T^c(s^{-1}\overline{V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)})$ of the critical-level vacuum algebra carries the deconcatenation coproduct of Volume~I. The Wakimoto free-field embedding induces a map $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \to B(\Pi_{-h^\vee})$ of factorization coalgebras. Since $\Pi_{-h^\vee}$ is a tensor product of free-field algebras, its bar complex is computed by the abelian (class G) shadow tower, where all operations above degree two vanish. The nontrivial content of the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism, from the bar-complex perspective, is that the BRST cohomology of the Wakimoto complex computes $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ as the Verdier-dual of the primitive summand of $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg))$ (the summand generated by single-letter words in the ordered bar).

\paragraph{Locating Feigin--Frenkel among the four functors.} Volume~I Theorem~A organizes four distinct operations on chiral algebras over $\mathrm{Ran}(X)$. They must never be conflated (Volume~I).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item The bar functor $B$, producing a factorization coalgebra.
 \item Verdier duality $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}$ applied to the bar, producing the linear-dual algebra denoted $A^!$.
 \item Inversion, returning the original algebra up to quasi-isomorphism on the Koszul locus.
 \item The derived chiral center $Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}$, computed as chiral Hochschild cochains; this is the bulk.
\end{enumerate}
Feigin--Frenkel is a statement about the chiral center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \subset V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$. The relevant legs of the four-functor picture are the derived center of item~(d), and the Verdier leg of item~(b). It is not an instance of inversion (item~(c)). In particular one should not describe Feigin--Frenkel as ``bar followed by cobar produces the spectral side.'' For $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$, the bar complex $B(A)$ carries the deconcatenation coproduct of $T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$ (Volume~I); the Verdier-dual complex is the habitat in which $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L})$ should be located.

At the critical level $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) = \dim(\frakg) \cdot (k + h^\vee) / (2 h^\vee) = 0$. In the trace-form convention the classical $r$-matrix carries an explicit level prefix, $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$, so at $k = -h^\vee$ it is $-h^\vee\,\Omega/z$, not $0$. The critical phenomenon is the vanishing of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the resulting shadow/center degeneration encoded by $\Theta_A$, not a level-stripped disappearance of the affine collision residue.

\begin{conjecture}[Critical-level Verdier-intertwining]
\label{conj:critical-self-dual}
Let $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$ and write $A^!$ for the Verdier-dual chiral algebra $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B(A))$ of Volume~I Theorem~A. At the critical level the chiral-algebra inclusion $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \hookrightarrow A$, combined with the Feigin--Frenkel isomorphism of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}, implies (does not iff) the existence of a factorization-coalgebra map $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D)) \to B(A^!)$ on $\mathrm{Ran}(X)$. The conjecture is that this map is a quasi-isomorphism on the Volume~I Koszul locus. This is a statement about the Verdier leg of the four-functor picture, not about the inversion leg $\Omega \circ B$.
\end{conjecture}

The forward implication (Feigin--Frenkel implies a bar-coalgebra map) follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center} and the factorization construction of $\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}$. The converse (that the map is a quasi-isomorphism) is open.

\paragraph{Remark on the Koszul locus.} Volume~I defines the Koszul locus as the set of chiral algebras $A$ for which the inversion map $\Omega(B(A)) \to A$ is a quasi-isomorphism. For $V_k(\frakg)$ with $\frakg$ simple and $k$ generic, membership in the Koszul locus is a theorem of Volume~I (the Kac--Moody family is class L, shadow depth $r = 3$). At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$ the chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ vanishes, and membership requires a separate argument because the shadow metric degenerates there even though the trace-form $r$-matrix $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$ remains nonzero. The missing input is the critical-level Koszul quasi-isomorphism for $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$, and it is exactly the hypothesis feeding Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}.

\paragraph{Remark on the opposite level.} Vol~I records that the Koszul conductor for the Kac--Moody family is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ where $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'}(\frakg))$ with $k' = -k - 2 h^\vee$ (the Feigin--Frenkel reflection). The two critical-level points $k = -h^\vee$ and $k' = -h^\vee$ coincide: the critical level is the fixed point of the Feigin--Frenkel reflection. This is the algebraic reason the critical level is distinguished, and the source of the self-duality language that is distinct from the universal form.

\paragraph{D-modules on \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Bun}_G$}{Bun-G} and chiral localization.}
The Frenkel--Gaitsgory construction (2006--) globalizes the Feigin--Frenkel center from the formal disk to an algebraic curve $X$. For each smooth projective curve $X$ and reductive group $G$, the category $\hat{\frakg}_{\mathrm{crit}}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}^{\mathrm{Ran}(X)}$ of chiral modules for the critical-level vacuum algebra over $\Ran(X)$ admits a localization functor
\[
 \Delta_X \colon \hat{\frakg}_{\mathrm{crit}}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}^{\Ran(X)} \;\longrightarrow\; D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Bun}_G(X))
\]
whose essential image at the abelian level is the category of Hecke eigensheaves. The localization $\Delta_X$ is a chiral-algebraic shadow of the Beilinson--Drinfeld construction: the factorization structure of the vacuum module on $\Ran(X)$ produces, upon localization, the D-module structure on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$. The bar complex $B(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg))$ on $\Ran(X)$ maps, under the Verdier leg of Theorem~A, to the factorization algebra that controls the spectral side. The passage from factorization coalgebra to D-module on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ is the content of $\Delta_X$; its compatibility with the four-functor picture of Volume~I is the subject of Conjecture~\ref{conj:d-mod-factorization}.

\begin{conjecture}[Factorization-to-D-module compatibility]
\label{conj:d-mod-factorization}
The localization functor $\Delta_X$ intertwines the Verdier leg $D_{\Ran} \circ B$ of Volume~I Theorem~A with the de Rham functor on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$. Concretely: for $A = V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$, the D-module $\Delta_X(A^!)$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$ is the Hecke eigensheaf associated to the trivial $G^L$-local system, and the factorization-coalgebra structure of $B(A)$ on $\Ran(X)$ determines the Hecke property via the chiral Verdier intertwining. This is an ``implies'' statement: the factorization structure determines the Hecke property, but the converse (recovering the factorization structure from the Hecke property alone) is open.
\end{conjecture}

\section{CY Langlands for \texorpdfstring{$d = 1, 2, 3$}{d = 1, 2, 3}}
\label{sec:cy-langlands}
% ============================================================

The statement of Feigin--Frenkel is that the \emph{center} of a chiral algebra at a distinguished level encodes opers for the Langlands dual group. For a CY category $\cC$, the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}$ produces a chiral algebra $A_\cC$ whose bar complex is the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}); a CY analogue of Langlands duality should therefore manifest as a pairing $\cC \leftrightarrow \cC^L$ on the source of the correspondence.

\begin{conjecture}[CY Langlands]
\label{conj:cy-langlands}
For each dimension $d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ there exists an involution $\cC \mapsto \cC^L$ on smooth proper CY $d$-categories, the \emph{Langlands dual}, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The Verdier-dual chiral algebra $\Phi(\cC)^! := D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B(\Phi(\cC)))$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\Phi(\cC^L)$ (compatibility of the Langlands involution with the Verdier leg of Vol~I Theorem~A); equivalently, the modular tensor categories $\Phi(\cC)$ and $\Phi(\cC^L)$ are related by the geometric Langlands equivalence of Frenkel--Gaitsgory (2006).
 \item The chiral modular characteristics $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC^L))$ satisfy a family-dependent Koszul conductor relation. For input giving rise to Kac--Moody output, the conductor is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ (the KM/free-field case of Volume~I); for Virasoro-type output it is $13$ (the self-dual point $c = 13$). The general CY Koszul conductor $\rho_K(\cC)$ is family-dependent and takes the archetype values $\{0, 13, 250/3, 98/3\}$ across the classical four archetypes $\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{M}$, enlarged to the canonical five-element bucket $\{0, 8, 13, 250/3, 98/3\}$ on $\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{L}/\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{M}/\mathbf{B}$ by the Lorentzian-lattice-parametric $\mathbf{B}$-family (K3 Mukai-doubling witness at $K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}} = 2\,c_+(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3)) = 8$); there is no single universal value.

\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The case \texorpdfstring{$d = 1$}{d = 1}.} Smooth proper CY $1$-categories are, up to Morita equivalence, the derived category of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective curve of genus $g = 1$. The correspondence $\Phi_1$ on $d = 1$ input is not covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral} (which is stated at $d = 2$); the expected output is the Heisenberg-like current algebra of the elliptic curve. The involution $\cC \mapsto \cC^L$ at $d = 1$ is the identity. This is consistent with geometric Langlands on an elliptic curve being the Fourier--Mukai self-duality (Polishchuk--Rothstein, 2001).

\paragraph{The case \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}.} Smooth proper CY $2$-categories include $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ for $X$ a K3 or abelian surface, and the Fukaya category of the same. The conjectured $d = 2$ Langlands is the \emph{K3 mirror map}: Mukai's Hodge-theoretic mirror $X \leftrightarrow X^\vee$ for K3 surfaces should induce an involution on CY $2$-categories which, via $\Phi$, corresponds to the $d = 2$ Langlands equivalence. This is not proved. A first milestone is to verify Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}(i) on the sublocus of lattice-polarized K3 categories, where the mirror map is the Dolgachev--Nikulin involution on the N\'eron--Severi lattice.

\paragraph{The case \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3}.}
At $d = 3$ the functor $\Phi_3$ exists (Theorem~CY-A$_3$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).
The Langlands conjecture at $d = 3$ remains the most speculative, not because the functor is missing but because the quantum vertex chiral group $G(X)$ is unconstructed in general (Conjecture~CY-C).
Because BPS invariants are the primary output of the $d = 3$ functor (via CoHA), the expected statement is that Langlands duality exchanges \emph{mirror BPS algebras}.
Concretely: for a CY $3$-fold $X$ with mirror $X^\vee$, the conjectural quantum vertex chiral groups $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$ (CY-C) should become Langlands dual as $\Eone$-chiral algebras.
The CoHA of $X$ is an associative algebra, providing the $\Eone$-ordered half of the conjectural $G(X)$.
Every statement of this form passes through the unconstructed object $G(X)$ (CY-C) and must remain a conjecture.

\paragraph{The Hitchin realization at \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}.}
Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve and $\mathbf{G}$ a reductive group.
The Hitchin moduli space $\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})$ is a smooth hyperk\"ahler manifold whose derived category of coherent sheaves $D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})))$ is a CY $2$-category (in the sense of the Mukai pairing on K3-type derived categories; the precise CY dimension depends on $\dim \mathcal{M}_H$).
The Hausel--Thaddeus and Donagi--Pantev mirror symmetry for Hitchin systems identifies
\[
 \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})^\vee \;\simeq\; \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G}^L)
\]
at the level of generic Hitchin fibers (dual abelian varieties) and, conjecturally, at the derived-categorical level via homological mirror symmetry: $D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))) \simeq \Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L))$.
Applying $\Phi$ to both sides produces a pair of $\Etwo$-chiral algebras.

\begin{conjecture}[Hitchin-system CY Langlands]
\label{conj:cy-langlands-hitchin}
The CY Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}, restricted to Hitchin-type CY $2$-categories, is the Donagi--Pantev mirror map: $\Phi(D^b(\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))))^! \simeq \Phi(\Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L)))$ as $\Etwo$-chiral algebras on $\Ran(X)$.
The chiral modular characteristics satisfy $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}))) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\mathcal{M}_H(\mathbf{G}^L))) = 0$ (the KM-type Koszul conductor, not the Virasoro conductor $13$).
This is the $d = 2$ Hitchin specialization; it implies (does not iff) the general $d = 2$ CY Langlands.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The Kapustin--Witten picture (physical heuristic).}
The following paragraph is a physical heuristic, not a mathematical derivation; each identification is conjectural unless independently proved. A physical interpretation of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands} comes from the Kapustin--Witten realization of geometric Langlands as S-duality of $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super-Yang--Mills on a product $\Sigma \times C$ (Kapustin--Witten, 2007).
In that picture, the Langlands dual group arises as the S-dual gauge group and the category of D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ arises from the category of branes of an A-model or B-model twist.
The CY category entering the construction is the Fukaya category of $\mathrm{Hit}(C, G)$ (the Hitchin moduli space) on one side and $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{Hit}(C, G^L)))$ on the other.
The mirror symmetry of Hitchin moduli spaces under hyperk\"ahler rotation (Hausel--Thaddeus, 2003; Donagi--Pantev, 2012) provides a concrete CY $2$-category instance where the $d = 2$ Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands} is testable.
The $\Phi$-image on each side should recover, via Volume~I, the spectral and automorphic sides of the Beilinson--Drinfeld construction.

\begin{remark}[4d class-\texorpdfstring{$S$}{S} parent of the K3 chiral bialgebra]
\label{rem:gl-class-s-parent}
The K3 chiral bialgebra admits a 4d $\cN=2$ parent in the class-$S$ framework of Gaiotto (2012). Compactify the $(2,0)$ $A_1$ theory on the 24-punctured sphere $\Sigma_{0,24}$: this produces a 4d $\cN=2$ superconformal theory with canonical charges
\[
 c_{4d} \;=\; \tfrac{107}{6}, \qquad a_{4d} \;=\; \tfrac{403}{24}, \qquad
 \text{Coulomb rank} \;=\; 21, \qquad
 \text{flavour} \;=\; \mathfrak{su}(2)^{24}
\]
(Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic},
Remark~\ref{rem:k3-classS-numerical-caveat}).
The Beem--Lemos--Liendo--Peelaers--Rastelli--van~Rees chiral algebra functor $\chi_{4d}$ (BLLPRvR 2013) sends this theory to a 2d chiral algebra with central charge
\[
 c_{2d} \;=\; -12 c_{4d} \;=\; -214
\]
carrying $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}_{-2}^{\otimes 24}$ current symmetry (flavour level $k_{2d} = -h^\vee = -2$ for each $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factor), precisely the critical-level affine symmetry of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center} applied $24$ times. The 24-puncture rigidity is enforced by the Steiner system $S(5,8,24)$: the $24$ punctures of $\Sigma_{0,24}$ are the $24$ points permuted faithfully by the Mathieu group $M_{24}$, with the $759$ octads of $S(5,8,24)$ indexing the maximal parabolic degenerations of $\Sigma_{0,24}$. The Langlands content: under $\Phi_2$ applied to the K3 category and Gaiotto duality applied to the class-$S$ parent, the QGL involution $k \mapsto k^L$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:qgl-involution} (anticipated below) is the $S$-duality $\Psi \leftrightarrow -1/\Psi$ of the 4d theory restricted to the critical-level 24-fold tensor (Gaiotto~2012, Beem et al.~2015, Moore--Tachikawa~2012 for the Higgs-branch interpretation).
\end{remark}

\section{Quantum geometric Langlands and the shadow parameter}
\label{sec:quantum-geom-langlands}
% ============================================================

Quantum geometric Langlands (QGL), introduced by Feigin--Frenkel and developed by Frenkel--Gaitsgory (2006), is a one-parameter deformation of classical geometric Langlands. The parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = k + h^\vee$ is a complex number. The two limits recover known correspondences:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} \to 0$ (the critical level $k = -h^\vee$): the classical geometric Langlands correspondence of Beilinson--Drinfeld, with Feigin--Frenkel centers as the spectral side.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} \to \infty$: the classical de Rham geometric Langlands in Gaitsgory's formulation, the semi-classical limit of the deformation.
\end{itemize}

At finite $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ the spectral side is a category of \emph{twisted D-modules} on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ at level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, and the automorphic side is the corresponding category at level $-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ for the Langlands dual group. The quantum correspondence is stated as an equivalence of DG categories (Frenkel, \emph{Lectures on Quantum Geometric Langlands}, 2005; Gaitsgory's framework).

\paragraph{The identification conjecture.} The parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ acts on the $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $V_{k}(\frakg)$ via $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = k + h^\vee$. Volume~I defines a distinct parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, the chiral modular characteristic (Volume~I, Theorem~D), which for $V_k(\frakg)$ evaluates to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \dim(\frakg)(k + h^\vee)/(2 h^\vee)$. The two are proportional:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\frakg)) \;=\; \frac{\dim(\frakg)}{2 h^\vee} \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}.
\]
At the critical level both vanish. This proportionality is already unconditional for $V_k(\frakg)$; the conjectural extension is to general CY input.

\begin{conjecture}[QGL parameter equals shadow obstruction]
\label{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}
For every CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d = 2$, the quantum geometric Langlands parameter of the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence applied to $\Phi(\cC)$ coincides, up to the dimensional normalization $\dim(\frakg)/(2 h^\vee)$ in the Kac--Moody case, with the Vol~I chiral shadow parameter $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ of the CY $2$-category. Equivalently, the Frenkel--Gaitsgory QGL deformation is the shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I (Volume~I, Theorem~D) evaluated along the critical-level family.
\end{conjecture}

Consequence of Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}:
the G/L/C/M shadow class of $\Phi(\cC)$ controls the convergence of the QGL deformation series in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$.
Class G (finite tower, $r = 2$) would correspond to polynomial deformations;
class L ($r = 3$, the Kac--Moody generic class) to rational-in-$\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ deformations;
class C ($r = 4$, $\beta\gamma$-type) to deformations with finite transcendence degree;
and class M (infinite tower, Virasoro and $W_N$) to formal power series with genuine transcendental content.
The critical-level limit $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \to 0$ is then the degeneration point of Volume~I at $k = -h^\vee$, consistent with Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}.

The conjecture implies (does not iff): the QGL duality exchanges $(\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}, G) \leftrightarrow (-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}, G^L)$, which under Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} should correspond to an involution on the space of CY $2$-categories with a distinguished $\frakg$-action. This is the $d = 2$ specialization of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}.

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow convergence class determines QGL analytic type]
\label{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl}
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY $2$-category with $\Phi(\cC)$ in shadow class $\mathcal{X} \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ (Volume~I classification). The QGL deformation series of the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence applied to $\Phi(\cC)$, viewed as a formal power series in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, has the following analytic type:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item class G ($r = 2$): the series is polynomial in $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$, terminating at degree $1$;
 \item class L ($r = 3$): the series is a rational function of $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ with poles at roots of the Kac--Moody Kac determinant;
 \item class C ($r = 4$): the series converges in a disk $|\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}| < R_C$ with $R_C$ determined by the $\beta\gamma$-type spectral radius;
 \item class M ($r = \infty$): the series is Gevrey-$1$ divergent and requires Borel resummation, with Stokes data encoding the shadow tower.
\end{enumerate}
Conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}. At the critical level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = 0$ all four types agree: the deformation is trivial and classical geometric Langlands holds.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The Costello--Gaiotto perspective (physical heuristic).}
The identifications in this paragraph are physical heuristics, not mathematical inputs: each is conjectural unless independently proved. Costello and Gaiotto derive quantum geometric Langlands from a twisted compactification of 4d $\mathcal{N} = 4$ super-Yang--Mills on a Riemann surface $C$ (Costello, 2013; Costello--Gaiotto, 2020). In their framework the QGL parameter is the complexified gauge coupling $\Psi = k + h^\vee$, and the duality $\Psi \leftrightarrow -1/\Psi$ is S-duality of the 4d theory. The chiral algebra on $C$ is produced by the holomorphic-topological twist of Volume~II; the bar complex of this chiral algebra (Volume~I, Theorem~A) encodes the factorization structure that Costello--Gaiotto identify with the Hecke action. For CY $2$-category input, the expected statement is that the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_2$ applied to the B-model category of the Hitchin system reproduces the Costello--Gaiotto chiral algebra at the corresponding value of $\Psi$. This identification is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands-hitchin} and the CY-A$_2$ correspondence (proved).

\section{K3 with ADE enhancement: the Feigin--Frenkel map}
\label{sec:k3-ade-langlands}
% ============================================================

At a du Val singularity of type $\frakg$ on a K3 surface $S$, the exceptional divisors of the minimal resolution generate a root lattice $\Lambda_\frakg \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Pic}(S) \hookrightarrow \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$. The Mukai pairing restricts to the Cartan matrix on $\Lambda_\frakg$, normalised so that long roots have length-squared $2$. Under the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_2$ (CY-A$_2$, proved at $d = 2$), the resulting chiral algebra $A_S := \Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(S)))$ contains $\hat{\frakg}_1$ (affine Kac--Moody at level $k = 1$) as a vertex subalgebra. The level is \emph{forced}: the Mukai normalisation gives $\langle \alpha_i, \alpha_j \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}} = C_{ij}(\frakg)$, which is the level-$1$ OPE.

\paragraph{Level-\texorpdfstring{$1$}{1} central charge and the rank identity.}
For simply-laced $\frakg$ (types $A$, $D$, $E$), the identity $\dim(\frakg) = \mathrm{rank}(\frakg) \cdot (1 + h^\vee)$ implies
\[
 c(\hat{\frakg}_1) = \frac{\dim(\frakg) \cdot 1}{1 + h^\vee} = \mathrm{rank}(\frakg).
\]
Thus $c(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2{}_1) = 1$, $c(\hat{\mathfrak{so}}_8{}_1) = 4$, $c(\hat{e}_{8,1}) = 8$. The chiral modular characteristic is
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\hat{\frakg}_1) = \frac{\dim(\frakg)(1 + h^\vee)}{2 h^\vee}.
\]
For $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 9/4$. For $\hat{e}_{8}$ at level $1$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1922/15$. The ADE root lattice uses $\mathrm{rank}(\frakg)$ of the $24$ Mukai directions; the orthogonal complement $\Lambda_\frakg^\perp \subset \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ has rank $24 - \mathrm{rank}(\frakg)$ and carries the ``spectator'' Heisenberg currents.

\begin{remark}[Bi-based Ran-space factorisation site and the averaging morphism]
\label{rem:gl-bi-based-ran}
The K3 chiral bialgebra $A_S = \Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(S)))$ does not live on $\Ran(S)$ directly: its natural factorisation site is the \emph{bi-based Ran space}
\[
 (\Ran(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}),\; \overline{\cA}_2),
\]
where $E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}$ is the relative nodal elliptic curve over the base $\overline{\cA}_2$ of the Igusa compactification of the Siegel modular threefold $\cA_2 = \mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)\backslash\mathfrak{H}_2$, and the $24$ base points are the $24$ singular fibres of an elliptic K3 $\pi\colon S \to \bP^1$ (Kas--Kodaira, 1963; Miranda, 1989). The averaging map $\mathrm{av}\colon \frakg^{E_1} \to \frakg^{\mathrm{mod}}$ of Vol~I, in this setting, factors as a three-step composite
\[
 \mathrm{av} \;=\; \mathrm{Torelli}_{K3} \circ \mathrm{KugaSatake} \circ j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}},
\]
where $j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}}$ is the Kodaira embedding of the nodal $24$-curve into the universal elliptic surface, $\mathrm{KugaSatake}$ is the Kuga--Satake correspondence $H^2(S) \hookrightarrow H^1(A_{\mathrm{KS}})^{\otimes 2}$ to the Kuga--Satake abelian variety of dimension $2^{20}$ (Kuga--Satake, 1967; Deligne, 1972), and $\mathrm{Torelli}_{K3}$ is the global Torelli theorem sending period points to isomorphism classes of marked K3 surfaces (Pjatecki\v{\i}-\v{S}apiro--\v{S}afarevi\v{c}, 1971). The composite is the geometric-Langlands incarnation of the Vol~I averaging principle: the ordered chiral bialgebra on $(\Ran(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}), \overline{\cA}_2)$ becomes the symmetric chiral bialgebra on $\Ran(S)$ under the pushforward along $\mathrm{av}$. The $24$ base points are not accidental: they are the rank of $H^2(K3,\Z) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$, and their permutation action is by $M_{24}$ via the Steiner system $S(5,8,24)$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The critical-level deformation.}
The level-$1$ embedding $\hat{\frakg}_1 \hookrightarrow A_S$ deforms along the KM moduli $k \in \C$. At the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the Feigin--Frenkel theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}) produces the center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \cong \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$. The deformation $k\colon 1 \to -h^\vee$ tracks a path in the shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I along which $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ decreases linearly from $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\hat{\frakg}_1)$ to $0$, at rate $d\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}/dk = \dim(\frakg)/(2 h^\vee)$. The Sugawara central charge $c(k) = \dim(\frakg) k / (k + h^\vee)$ has a pole at $k = -h^\vee$ with residue $-\dim(\frakg) h^\vee$. Conjecturally, the oper connection emerges from the shadow metric degeneration at $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$: the shadow metric $Q_L(0) = 4\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2 \to 0$ acquires a double zero, and the surviving connection data is the projective connection (oper) for the Langlands dual group $G^L$ (this ``degeneration-to-oper'' mechanism is the Vol~I shadow reading of Feigin--Frenkel, distinct from the Wakimoto proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}, and is the shadow-side content of Conjecture~\ref{conj:critical-self-dual}).

\paragraph{Opers for \texorpdfstring{$\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$}{hatsl-2} at level \texorpdfstring{$1$}{1} on K3.}
For $\frakg = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, the Langlands dual is $G^L = \mathrm{PGL}_2$ (same Lie algebra, type $A_1$). A $\mathrm{PGL}_2$-oper on a curve $C$ of genus $g \geq 2$ is a projective connection $d^2/dt^2 + q(t)$, where $q \in H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes 2})$ is a quadratic differential. The space of opers has dimension $\dim H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes 2}) = 3(g - 1)$.

The K3 connection: for a polarisation $L$ on $S$ with $L^2 = 2g - 2$, the generic curve $C \in |L|$ has genus $g$. The restriction of the K3 holomorphic $2$-form $\omega_S$ to a tubular neighbourhood of $C$ determines, conjecturally, a quadratic differential on $C$ and hence an oper. The oper moduli at genus $g$ are summarised by the Casimir grading: for general ADE type $\frakg$ with Langlands dual $\frakg^L$ of rank $r$, the Casimir degrees $d_1, \ldots, d_r$ give $\dim H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes d_i}) = (2d_i - 1)(g - 1)$, summing to $\dim(\frakg^L)(g - 1)$. This sum equals the Hitchin base dimension (Hitchin--Beilinson--Drinfeld correspondence).

\section{K3 Yangian and quantum geometric Langlands duality}
\label{sec:k3-yangian-langlands}
% ============================================================

Assume the non-abelian K3 Yangian of
Section~\ref{sec:k3-yangian-quantization} exists with ADE enhancement
embeddings \(Y(\hat{\frakg}_k)\hookrightarrow Y(\frakg_{K3})\). The
quantum geometric Langlands correspondence (Frenkel--Gaitsgory) acts on
each ADE subalgebra by the level duality \(k \mapsto k^L\).

\paragraph{The QGL and FF involutions.}
For simply-laced types, two involutions act on the level parameter $k$:
\begin{align}
 \text{QGL:}\quad k &\mapsto k^L = -k - h^\vee, \label{eq:qgl-involution} \\
 \text{FF:}\quad k &\mapsto k' = -k - 2h^\vee. \label{eq:ff-involution}
\end{align}
These are \emph{different}: the QGL involution exchanges category $\mathcal{O}_k(\hat{\frakg})$ and $\mathcal{O}_{k^L}(\hat{\frakg}^L)$ (quantum Langlands duality), while the FF involution exchanges $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k)$ and $-\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k')$ (Koszul duality of Vol~I Theorem~A). Their distinguished points:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
 & QGL fixed point & FF fixed point \\
\hline
$k$ value & $-h^\vee/2$ & $-h^\vee$ (critical level) \\
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ value & $\frac{\dim(\frakg)}{4}$ & $0$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both are involutions ($k^{LL} = k$ and $k'' = k$). The QGL involution satisfies the reciprocal identity
\[
 \Psi(k) \cdot \Psi(k^L) = 1, \qquad \Psi := \frac{k}{k + h^\vee},
\]
for the chapter's coupling $\Psi$; this is a reflection of the coupling through $\Psi = 1$, formally analogous to but distinct from the Kapustin--Witten S-duality $\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}} \to -1/\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}}$ with $\Psi_{\mathrm{KW}} = k + h^\vee$. The FF involution satisfies $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(k') = 0$. These two properties are \emph{not} simultaneously satisfied by any single involution.

\paragraph{Application to K3 with \texorpdfstring{$\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$}{hatsl-2} at level \texorpdfstring{$1$}{1}.}
For $\frakg = \mathfrak{sl}_2$ ($h^\vee = 2$) at $k = 1$:
\begin{itemize}
 \item QGL dual level: $k^L = -1 - 2 = -3$. Then $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-3) = 3(-3 + 2)/4 = -3/4$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(1) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-3) = 9/4 - 3/4 = 3/2 \neq 0$.
 \item FF dual level: $k' = -1 - 4 = -5$. Then $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-5) = 3(-5 + 2)/4 = -9/4$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(1) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(-5) = 0$.
 \item Critical level: $k_{\mathrm{crit}} = -2$, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$ and $c$ undefined (pole). The QGL dual of $k_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is $k^L = 0$ (classical limit of the dual); the FF dual is $k' = -2$ (fixed point).
\end{itemize}
The Langlands dual of the K3 $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $1$ in the QGL sense is $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at level $-3$. This is a \emph{negative} level, corresponding to the ``wrong sign'' category $\mathcal{O}$, which is the correct home for the spectral side of quantum Langlands.

\begin{conjecture}[K3 Yangian QGL duality]
\label{conj:k3-yangian-qgl}
For a K3 surface $S$ with ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$, the subalgebra $Y(\hat{\frakg}_1) \subset Y(\frakg_{K3})$ and the QGL-dual subalgebra $Y(\hat{\frakg}_{k^L}) \subset Y(\frakg_{K3^L})$ (where $K3^L$ is the Mukai mirror) are related by the Frenkel--Gaitsgory equivalence: the category $\mathcal{O}$ of $Y(\hat{\frakg}_1)$ at level $1$ is equivalent to the category $\mathcal{O}$ of $Y(\hat{\frakg}_{k^L})$ at the dual level $k^L = -1 - h^\vee$. This is conditional on the existence of $Y(\frakg_{K3})$ and on CY-A$_2$ for the correspondence $\Phi_2$.
\end{conjecture}

\section{K3 \texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x} E and D-modules on \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Bun}_G(E)$}{Bun-G(E)}}
\label{sec:k3xe-dmodules}
% ============================================================

For an elliptically fibered K3 surface $\pi\colon S \to \bP^1$ with section, the product $S \times E$ (where $E$ is an elliptic curve) is a Calabi--Yau $3$-fold. The chiral algebra $\Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(S \times E)))$ exists by CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}); its factored structure $\Phi(S) \otimes \Phi(E)$ is conditionally accessible via CY-A$_2$ on each factor (the factorisation itself is not established; see Conjecture~\ref{conj:k3xe-hecke}).

\paragraph{The family over \texorpdfstring{$E$}{E}.}
At an ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$ on $S$, the chiral subalgebra $\hat{\frakg}_1 \subset \Phi(S)$ produces, upon tensor with the chiral structure on $E$, a family of chiral modules parametrised by $z \in E$. The resulting object over $\Ran(E)$ is a factorization sheaf. The Frenkel--Gaitsgory localisation functor $\Delta_E$ of Section~\ref{sec:feigin-frenkel} sends this to a D-module on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(E)$.

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Bun}_G$}{Bun-G} for an elliptic curve.}
For a reductive group $G$, the moduli stack of semi-stable $G$-bundles on an elliptic curve $E$ has the concrete description
\[
 \mathrm{Bun}_G(E) \;=\; (E \otimes_\Z \Lambda_{\mathrm{cochar}}) \,/\, W,
\]
where $\Lambda_{\mathrm{cochar}}$ is the cocharacter lattice and $W$ is the Weyl group. For $G = \mathrm{SL}_2$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_2}(E) = E / (\Z/2\Z) \cong \bP^1$ (the Kummer quotient, with $4$ ramification points at the $2$-torsion of $E$). For $G = \mathrm{SL}_3$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_3}(E) = (E \times E) / S_3$. For $G = E_8$: $\mathrm{Bun}_{E_8}(E) = E^8 / W(E_8)$, coarse moduli of dimension $8$.

\begin{conjecture}[K3 \texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x} E Hecke eigensheaf]
\label{conj:k3xe-hecke}
Let $S$ be a K3 surface with $\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ enhancement at level $1$, and let $E$ be an elliptic curve. The D-module $\Delta_E(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2,1})$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_{\mathrm{SL}_2}(E) \cong \bP^1$ is a Hecke eigensheaf for the $\mathrm{PGL}_2$-local system on $E$ determined by the K3 periods. By CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}), $A_{S \times E} = \Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(S \times E)))$ exists; the remaining conditionality is the factorisation $\Phi(S \times E) \simeq \Phi(S) \otimes \Phi(E)$ (not established) and the identification of the D-module with a Hecke eigensheaf.
\end{conjecture}

The factorisation hypothesis $\Phi_3(S \times E) \simeq \Phi_2(S) \otimes \Phi_1(E)$ is not established: the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}$ is constructed for individual CY categories, and the tensor product of chiral algebras over $\Ran$ is a nontrivial operation. For the K3 factor alone, $\Phi_2(S)$ is accessible via CY-A$_2$ (proved); for the elliptic curve $E$, the chiral theory is the rank-$1$ Heisenberg (also proved). The difficulty is in the interaction terms.

\section{Shadow depth and Langlands parameter type}
\label{sec:shadow-langlands-type}
% ============================================================

The shadow class $\mathcal{X} \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ of Volume~I classifies chiral algebras by the depth of their shadow obstruction tower. Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl} identifies this classification with the analytic type of the QGL deformation series. We refine this identification to a correspondence with types of Langlands parameters.

\paragraph{The conjectured dictionary.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
Shadow class & Shadow depth & QGL analytic type & Langlands parameter \\
\hline
G & $2$ & polynomial & unramified \\
L & $3$ & rational & tamely ramified \\
C & $4$ & convergent & wildly ramified (regular) \\
M & $\infty$ & Gevrey-$1$ divergent & irregular \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The logic: in the QGL correspondence of Frenkel--Gaitsgory, the spectral side consists of twisted D-modules at level $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$, and the automorphic side of twisted D-modules at level $-1/\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ for $G^L$. The singularity structure of D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ is classified by the ramification type of the $G^L$-local system on $X$: unramified (smooth), tamely ramified (regular singular), and wildly ramified (irregular singular). If $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$ is identified with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}), then the convergence type of the $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-series, which is controlled by the shadow depth (Volume~I, Theorem~D), determines the singularity type of the Langlands parameter.

\paragraph{K3 ADE enhancement: class L.}
At an ADE enhancement on K3, the Kac--Moody subalgebra $\hat{\frakg}_1$ has shadow class L (proved, Volume~I). The conjectured dictionary maps class L to tamely ramified Langlands parameters: the D-modules on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$ arising from the K3 chiral algebra should have regular singularities, controlled by the cubic shadow $\alpha$ of the KM family. The poles of the rational QGL series at roots of the Kac determinant would then correspond to the reducibility loci of tamely ramified local systems.

\paragraph{Generic K3: class G.}
For a generic K3 surface without ADE enhancement, the chiral algebra $\Phi(S)$ is a free-field theory (Heisenberg of rank $24$), shadow class G. The QGL series is polynomial (degree $1$), and the conjectured Langlands parameter is unramified. This is consistent with the Frenkel--Gaitsgory expectation: for free-field (commutative) chiral algebras, the Langlands correspondence reduces to the classical abelian case (Fourier--Mukai for the Heisenberg, which is the abelian geometric Langlands).

\paragraph{W-algebras: class M.}
The Virasoro and $W_N$-algebras have shadow class M (infinite depth, $r = \infty$). If a CY category $\cC$ has $\Phi(\cC)$ of class M (as happens for the local $\bP^2$ chiral algebra), the QGL series is Gevrey-$1$ divergent. The Borel resummation required to extract finite answers corresponds, conjecturally, to the Stokes phenomenon for irregular Langlands parameters. The Stokes data would encode the shadow tower of Volume~I.

\begin{conjecture}[Shadow depth equals ramification depth]
\label{conj:shadow-ramification}
For a smooth proper CY $2$-category $\cC$, the shadow depth $r(\Phi(\cC))$ of Volume~I equals the ramification depth of the associated Langlands parameter: $r = 2$ is unramified, $r = 3$ is tame, $r = 4$ is regular wild, and $r = \infty$ is irregular. This is a refinement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl}, replacing ``analytic type'' with ``ramification type.'' Conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} and on CY-A$_2$ for the correspondence $\Phi_2$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Igusa \texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta-5} as conditional comparison characteristic]
\label{rem:gl-igusa-invariant}
The ramification-type classification refined in Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-ramification} admits a modular incarnation after the finite Hall--Borcherds recognition gates and the Heegner comparison have been supplied. The $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$-equivariant cohomology of the chiral bialgebra deformation Lie algebra~$\frakg_{\Delta_5}$ controlling K3 period variation carries a canonical comparison line:
\[
 H^2(\frakg_{\Delta_5})^{\Z/2,\,K(1)} \;\cong\; \C \cdot \Delta_5,
\]
where $\Delta_5 \in M_5(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z))$ is the Igusa scalar-valued cusp form of weight~$5$ (Igusa, 1964, \emph{On Siegel modular forms of genus two. II}) -- the unique generator of its weight space modulo the $\Z/2$-Atkin--Lehner involution and $K(1)$-level structure. The comparison-space content: $\Delta_5$ separates the ramification classes of the $\Phi$-image only on this recognised Heegner locus, with its vanishing locus
\[
 V(\Delta_5) \;=\; \{\text{Humbert surfaces}\} \;\subset\; \cA_2
\]
detecting the loci where class~L degenerates to class~G (cf.\ van der Geer, 1987, \emph{Siegel modular forms and their applications}; Freitag~1983, \emph{Siegel's modular forms and Dirichlet series}). Under the Langlands-dictionary refinement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-ramification}, Humbert loci correspond to reducibility loci of tamely ramified $G^L$-local systems, and $\Delta_5$ is their defining equation. This is the modular incarnation of Volume~I's shadow classification on the recognised comparison locus: the shadow depth $r(\Phi(\cC))$ is read off algebraically from the vanishing order of $\Delta_5$ at the period point $\tau(\cC) \in \cA_2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Chiral quantization of the Hitchin system on K3 curves}
\label{sec:chiral-hitchin-k3}
% ============================================================

The preceding sections construct geometric Langlands data from the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}$ applied to abstract CY categories. This section specializes to a concrete geometric source: curves $C$ inside a K3 surface~$S$. The Hitchin system $\mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G})$ on such a curve, combined with the K3 adjunction $K_C = L|_C$ (where $K_S = \mathcal{O}_S$), connects the Hitchin spectral parameter to the K3 Yangian spectral parameter~$z$. All new identifications in this section are \textbf{conjectural} unless stated otherwise.

\paragraph{K3 linear systems.}
Let $S$ be a K3 surface and $L$ a line bundle on~$S$ with $L^2 = 2g - 2 > 0$. By Riemann--Roch on~K3, $\chi(L) = L^2/2 + 2 = g + 1$, and for $L$ nef and big, $h^0(S, L) = g + 1$ (Kodaira vanishing gives $h^1 = h^2 = 0$). The complete linear system $|L| \cong \bP^g$ parametrises a family of curves of genus~$g$. A generic member $C \in |L|$ is smooth and connected.

The adjunction formula gives
\[
 K_C \;=\; (K_S \otimes L)\big|_C \;=\; L\big|_C,
\]
since $K_S = \mathcal{O}_S$ for K3. This identification $K_C = L|_C$ is the structural input from the K3 embedding: the canonical bundle of~$C$ is the \emph{restriction of the K3 polarisation}.

\paragraph{Hitchin system on \texorpdfstring{$C \subset S$}{C subset S}.}
For a reductive group~$\mathbf{G}$ with Lie algebra $\frakg$, the Hitchin moduli space
\[
 \mathcal{M}_H(C, \mathbf{G}) \;=\; T^*\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C)
\]
is a hyperk\"ahler manifold of complex dimension $2\dim(\frakg)(g-1)$, with Hitchin fibration $\pi\colon \mathcal{M}_H \to B_H$ where
\[
 B_H \;=\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r} H^0(C, K_C^{\otimes d_i}), \qquad \dim B_H = \dim(\frakg)(g-1),
\]
with $d_1, \ldots, d_r$ the Casimir degrees of~$\frakg$. The fibration is Lagrangian: $\dim \mathcal{M}_H = 2\dim B_H$.

For $\mathbf{G} = \mathrm{SL}_2$ and $g = 2$: $\dim B_H = 3$ and $\dim \mathcal{M}_H = 6$. This is the unique ``CY$_3$-type'' Hitchin system, where the Lagrangian fibration has $3$-dimensional base and fibre (cf.~\texttt{hitchin\_sl2\_genus2.py}).

\paragraph{The spectral parameter from K3 geometry.}
The spectral curve $\Sigma \subset \mathrm{Tot}(K_C)$ is defined by $\det(\lambda \cdot I - \varphi) = 0$ for the Higgs field~$\varphi$. The K3 identification $K_C = L|_C$ embeds the spectral curve into $\mathrm{Tot}(L|_C)$, which is a tubular neighbourhood of~$C$ in~$S$. The tautological section $\lambda \in H^0(\Sigma, \pi^*K_C)$ is then a coordinate on the total space of~$L$ restricted to~$C$: the \emph{Hitchin spectral parameter is a local coordinate on K3 transverse to~$C$}.

\begin{conjecture}[Hitchin spectral parameter = K3 Yangian parameter]
\label{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian}

\section{Derived Satake for chiral quantum groups}
\label{sec:derived-satake-chiral}
% ============================================================

The geometric Satake equivalence is the foundational theorem of geometric Langlands.
The chiral extension of Satake, placed in the CY--chiral dictionary, expands on open problem~(v) below.
Every new formal statement is a \textbf{conjecture}: the arrows that compose the chiral Satake pass through the unconstructed quantum vertex chiral group $G(X)$ at $d = 3$ (CY-C); the chiral algebra $A_\cC$ itself exists by CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).

\paragraph{Classical and derived Satake.}
The Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen geometric Satake (2007) identifies the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian~$\mathrm{Gr}_G = G(\!(t)\!)/G[\![t]\!]$ with representations of the Langlands dual group:
\[
 \mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \;\simeq\; \Rep(G^L),
\]
as \emph{symmetric} monoidal categories, where the monoidal structure on the left is the convolution product of Lusztig and on the right the tensor product of representations.
The derived refinement of Bezrukavnikov--Finkelberg (2008) lifts this to DG~categories:
\[
 D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{IndCoh}(\mathcal{N}^L / G^L),
\]
where $\mathcal{N}^L$ is the nilpotent cone in~$\frakg^L$.
Both statements are theorems of Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen and Bezrukavnikov--Finkelberg.
The commutativity constraint on $\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$ (the natural isomorphism $\mathcal{F}_1 * \mathcal{F}_2 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{F}_2 * \mathcal{F}_1$ satisfying $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$) is the geometric source of the \emph{symmetric} monoidal structure on $\Rep(G^L)$.

\paragraph{The chiral affine Grassmannian.}
The Beilinson--Drinfeld construction (\emph{Chiral Algebras}, Ch.~5) promotes $\mathrm{Gr}_G$ to a \emph{factorization ind-scheme} $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G$ over the Ran space $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ of a curve~$C$.
The fiber over a finite subset $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\} \in \mathrm{Ran}(C)$ parametrises $G$-bundles on the formal neighbourhood of $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ with trivialization outside.
The key property:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Separated points}: $\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G(\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}) \simeq \prod_i \mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G(\{x_i\})$ when the $x_i$ are disjoint (the fiber splits, encoding the \emph{tensor product} of representations).
 \item \emph{Collision}: as $x_1 \to x_2$, the fiber does \emph{not} split; the non-splitting encodes the OPE/fusion product and, dually, the Drinfeld coproduct~$\Delta_z$ on the quantum group side.
\end{itemize}
The chiral Grassmannian exists by Beilinson--Drinfeld.
What is \emph{conjectural} is the identification of D-modules on it with chiral quantum group representations.

\paragraph{Convolution as the geometric coproduct.}
The convolution product on $\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$,
\[
 \mathcal{F}_1 * \mathcal{F}_2 \;=\; m_*(\mathcal{F}_1 \boxtimes \mathcal{F}_2),
\]
where $m\colon \mathrm{Gr}_G \tilde{\times}^{G[\![t]\!]} \mathrm{Gr}_G \to \mathrm{Gr}_G$ is the convolution Grassmannian, becomes in the chiral setting the \emph{factorization product}:
\[
 \mathcal{F}_1 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_2 \;=\; \text{factorization product over } \mathrm{Ran}(C).
\]
Under Satake duality, convolution on sheaves corresponds to the tensor product on representations; dually, it corresponds to the coproduct on the algebra.
For the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ with structure function~$g(u)$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
 Convolution of charge-$n$ and charge-$m$ sheaves & $\longleftrightarrow$ & tensor product $\cF_n \otimes \cF_m$ \\
 Factorization product as $x_1 \to x_2$ & $\longleftrightarrow$ & Drinfeld coproduct $\Delta_z(T(u)) = T_L(u)\,T_R(u - z)$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The spectral parameter $z$ is the separation in $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ between the two factorization points.

\paragraph{The geometric \texorpdfstring{$R$}{R}-matrix.}
In the classical Satake, the commutativity constraint $c_{\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2}$ with $c^2 = \mathrm{id}$ makes the category \emph{symmetric} monoidal.
In the chiral setting, the commutativity constraint is replaced by an $\Etwo$-\emph{braiding}:
\[
 \beta_{\mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2}(z) \colon \mathcal{F}_1 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_2 \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{F}_2 *^{\mathrm{ch}} \mathcal{F}_1,
\]
which is the geometric $R$-matrix.
The braiding arises from the holonomy of the factorization connection along a path in $\mathrm{Ran}(C)$ that exchanges $x_1$ and $x_2$, and it depends on $z = x_1 - x_2$ (the spectral separation).
On the charge-$1$ Fock representation for $G = \mathrm{GL}_1$ on $\C^3$ with Omega-background $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$, $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$:
\[
 R(z) \;=\; g(z) \;=\; \frac{(z - h_1)(z - h_2)(z - h_3)}{(z + h_1)(z + h_2)(z + h_3)},
\]
which is the Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope $R$-matrix (Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-chiral-rmatrix}).
The unitarity $R(z)\,R(-z) = 1$ follows algebraically from the CY condition (each factor $(z-h_i)/(z+h_i)$ paired with $(-z-h_i)/(-z+h_i) = (z+h_i)/(z-h_i)$ gives $1$).

The braiding is \emph{not symmetric}: $R(z) \neq \mathrm{id}$ for generic $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$.
This non-symmetry is the geometric manifestation of the $\Etwo$ (braided, not symmetric) structure on chiral quantum group representations, distinguishing the chiral Satake from its classical predecessor.
The $R$-matrix is characterized via the half-braiding on the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+))$, not by applying $\Delta$ to the vacuum.

\paragraph{The Iwahori reduction and the \texorpdfstring{$\Eone \to \Etwo$}{E1 -> E2} passage.}
The Iwahori subgroup $I \subset G[\![t]\!]$ defines the affine flag variety $\mathrm{Fl}_G = G(\!(t)\!)/I$.
The category $D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_I(\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G)$ of D-modules with Iwahori level structure carries an $\Eone$-monoidal structure (the ordered convolution with level structure).
The passage to the full (spherical) Satake category recovers the $\Etwo$-braided monoidal structure via the Drinfeld center:
\[
 \cZ\bigl(D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}_I(\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G)\bigr)
 \;\simeq\;
 D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\mathrm{Gr}^{\mathrm{ch}}_G)
 \;\simeq\;
 \Rep^{\Etwo}(\text{chiral QG}).
\]
This is the \emph{geometric} realization of the algebraic $\Eone \to \Etwo$ passage of Theorem~\ref{thm:drinfeld-center-coha}:
\[
 \cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1))) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)),
\]
where $Y^+$ is the positive half (the CoHA, which is associative and carries $\Eone$ but not $\Etwo$ structure) and $Y$ is the full affine Yangian.
The Drinfeld center $\cZ(\cdot)$ is a category-theoretic operation on the monoidal representation category; it is \emph{not} the derived center $Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$ of the chiral algebra.

\paragraph{Weight functors and MV cycles.}
The Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen weight functors $F_\mu\colon \mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G) \to \mathrm{Vect}$, defined by
\[

\]
(where $S_\mu = N^-(\!(t)\!) \cdot t^\mu$ is the semi-infinite orbit), extract the $\mu$-weight space of the representation~$V_\lambda$ under Satake.

For $G = \mathrm{GL}_1$ on K3, the group is abelian and all orbits are points: $\mathrm{Gr}^{(\lambda)}_{\mathrm{GL}_1} = \{t^\lambda\}$ for $\lambda$ in the Mukai lattice.
The IC sheaves are skyscraper sheaves at lattice points.

For $G = \mathrm{SL}_2$: the orbits $\mathrm{Gr}^{(n)}_{\mathrm{SL}_2}$ have dimension~$2n$, and the IC sheaf $\mathrm{IC}_n$ maps under Satake to the irreducible representation $V_n$ of $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ of dimension~$n+1$.

\paragraph{The five-step construction.}
For a CY $2$-category $\cC$ with ADE enhancement of type~$\frakg$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step~\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
 \item \textbf{CY-to-chiral} (CY-A$_2$, proved at $d = 2$): the correspondence $\Phi_2$ sends $\cC = D^b(\Coh(S))$ to an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $A_S$ containing $\hat{\frakg}_1$.
 \item \textbf{Bar complex} (Vol~I Theorem~A, proved): the bar functor $B$ sends $A_S$ to a factorization coalgebra $T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_S)$ with deconcatenation coproduct.

 \item \textbf{Chiral center} (Feigin--Frenkel 1992): at the critical level $k = -h^\vee$, the center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) = Z(V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)) \cong \mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$.
 \item \textbf{Oper identification} (\textbf{conjectural}): the CY (non-derived, Feigin--Frenkel) chiral centre at critical level $Z(A_S)$ (concordance T7($\beta$)(i)), via the Verdier leg, is quasi-isomorphic to $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ for CY input.

\section{S-duality, the Montonen--Olive conjecture, and Koszul duality}
\label{sec:phys-sduality-koszul}
% ============================================================

The Kapustin--Witten derivation of geometric Langlands rests on a
non-perturbative duality of four-dimensional gauge theories. We record the
mathematical content of that duality and its triple identification with
Koszul duality of quantum vertex chiral groups. Every physical statement in
this section is labelled as a mathematical conjecture or an established theorem;
physical heuristics are identified as such.

\subsection{The Montonen--Olive duality}
\label{subsec:phys-montonen-olive}

Let $G$ be a compact simple Lie group. The $\mathcal{N}=4$ super Yang--Mills
theory with gauge group $G$ on a four-manifold $M^4$ depends on a complexified
coupling constant
\[
 \tau = \frac{\theta}{2\pi} + \frac{4\pi i}{g^2} \in \mathbb{H}.
\]

\begin{conjecture}[Montonen--Olive S-duality]
\label{conj:phys-montonen-olive}
The $\mathcal{N}=4$ super Yang--Mills theory with gauge group $G$ at coupling
$\tau$ is equivalent to the $\mathcal{N}=4$ theory with gauge group $G^L$
(the Langlands dual group) at coupling $-1/n_\frakg\tau$, where $n_\frakg = 1$
for simply-laced $\frakg$ and $n_\frakg = 2, 3$ for $B_n/C_n$ and $G_2$
respectively. For simply-laced groups, the S-duality group is
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ acting on $\tau$ by M\"{o}bius transformations,
generated by $T\colon \tau \mapsto \tau + 1$ and $S\colon \tau \mapsto -1/\tau$.
\end{conjecture}

The Langlands dual group $G^L$ is defined by interchanging root and coroot
lattices:
\[
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{root}}(G^L) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{coroot}}(G), \qquad
 \Lambda_{\mathrm{coroot}}(G^L) = \Lambda_{\mathrm{root}}(G).
\]
For simply-laced groups, $G^L \simeq G/Z(G)$ or $\widetilde{G}$ depending on
whether $G$ is adjoint or simply connected. The non-simply-laced examples are
$B_n^L = C_n$, $C_n^L = B_n$, $G_2^L = G_2$ with long and short roots
exchanged.

\subsection{The Kapustin--Witten twist and geometric Langlands}
\label{subsec:phys-kw-twist-langlands}

The $\mathcal{N}=4$ theory admits a one-parameter family of topological twists
parametrised by $t \in \mathbb{CP}^1$, constructed by Kapustin and Witten.
The twist combines two supercharges $Q_\ell$ and $Q_r$ into a nilpotent scalar
supercharge $Q_t = Q_\ell + t\,Q_r$, $Q_t^2 = 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Kapustin--Witten]
\label{prop:phys-kw-4dtft}
The GL-twist ($t = 1$) of $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM on $M^4 = \Sigma \times C$,
where $\Sigma$ is a surface and $C$ is a smooth projective curve, produces a
$4$d topological field theory whose reduction on $C$ gives a $2$d $\sigma$-model
into the Hitchin moduli space $\mathcal{M}_H(C, G)$. Under S-duality, the
A-model on $\mathcal{M}_H(C, G)$ is exchanged with the B-model on
$\mathcal{M}_H(C, G^L)$:
\[
 \mathrm{A\text{-}model\ on}\ \mathcal{M}_H(C,G)
 \;\xleftrightarrow{\quad S \quad}\;
 \mathrm{B\text{-}model\ on}\ \mathcal{M}_H(C,G^L).
\]
This is the physics origin of the geometric Langlands correspondence: the A-model
produces the Fukaya category $\Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(C,G))$ and the B-model produces
$D^b\Coh(\mathcal{M}_H(C,G^L))$; homological mirror symmetry for the Hitchin
system relates these to the two sides of the Beilinson--Drinfeld construction.
\end{proposition}

The three KW twist parameters are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $t = 0$: the A-twist, with BPS equations $F_A = 0$ (flat connections),
 category of branes $D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\Bun_G)$;
\item $t = \infty$: the B-twist, with BPS equations the Hitchin equations,
 category of branes $\Fuk(\mathcal{M}_H(C,G))$;
\item $t = 1$: the GL-twist (geometric Langlands twist), relevant for the
 geometric Langlands correspondence. At this value, the braided monoidal
 structure on line operators is the $\Etwo$-structure of the quantum vertex
 chiral group $G(C, \frakg)$.
\end{enumerate}

Under S-duality, the twist parameter transforms as $t \mapsto t/|t|^2$, so the
A-twist at coupling $\tau$ for $G$ is exchanged with the B-twist at coupling
$-1/\tau$ for $G^L$.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric Langlands; Gaitsgory et al.\ 2024]
\label{thm:phys-geom-langlands-gaitsgory}
There is an equivalence of DG categories
\[
 \mathbb{L} \colon D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\Bun_G(C)) \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;
 \mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{LocSys}_{G^L}(C)),
\]
where $\Bun_G(C)$ is the moduli stack of principal $G$-bundles on $C$,
$\mathrm{LocSys}_{G^L}(C)$ is the derived stack of $G^L$-local systems, and
$\mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}$ denotes ind-coherent sheaves with nilpotent
singular support. The na\"ive equivalence with $\mathrm{QCoh}$ on the smooth
locus is corrected at singular points by the nilpotent singular support
condition.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Koszul duality at the critical level and the Langlands dual}
\label{subsec:phys-koszul-critical-langlands}

The Vol~I bar-cobar machine applied to the affine Kac--Moody vertex algebra
$V_k(\frakg)$ involves the dual level
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-dual-level}
 k' = -k - 2h^\vee.
\end{equation}
The self-dual level satisfying $k' = k$ is $k_{\mathrm{sd}} = -h^\vee$, the
critical level. In the physical dictionary, this is the vertex-algebraic
shadow of S-duality at the self-dual coupling $\tau = i$.

\begin{conjecture}[Koszul duality at the critical level]
\label{prop:phys-koszul-critical}
Assume that $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$ lies on the Volume~I Koszul locus and
that Beilinson--Drinfeld localization is compatible with the Verdier leg
$D_{\Ran}\circ B$. Then the Verdier dual $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)^!$ contains a
commutative central factor whose global sections are the Feigin--Frenkel
oper algebra:
\[
 V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)^! \;\longleftrightarrow\; \mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg})
 \;\simeq\; \mathrm{Fun}\,\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(C).
\]
After localization, this factor maps to the nilpotent-singular-support
spectral category on $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G^L}(C)$. The conjecture does not
identify the whole Koszul dual with all functions on $\mathrm{LocSys}_{G^L}(C)$;
the proved input is the oper centre, and the open input is the Verdier-localization
comparison.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Self-dual level and S-duality coupling]
\label{rem:phys-self-dual-level}
The coincidence $k_{\mathrm{sd}} = -h^\vee$ (critical level = self-dual level)
is read physically as the vertex-algebraic shadow of the S-duality involution
$\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$ at the self-dual coupling $|\tau| = 1$. At this point
S-duality, Langlands duality, and Koszul duality have the same fixed-level
numerology, while their categorical identifications remain those stated in
Conjectures~\ref{prop:phys-koszul-critical} and
\ref{conj:phys-triple-sduality-langlands-koszul}. The quantum deformation away
from the critical level is accommodated by the $\Etwo$-chiral
Koszul duality of Volume~III, with the deformation parameter
$\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}} = k + h^\vee$ identified (up to the KM normalisation)
with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ of Volume~I (Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The triple identification: S-duality = Langlands duality = Koszul duality}
\label{subsec:phys-triple-id}

\begin{conjecture}[Triple identification]
\label{conj:phys-triple-sduality-langlands-koszul}
For a simple Lie algebra $\frakg$ and a smooth projective curve $C$, the
following three dualities coincide on the quantum vertex chiral group $G(C,\frakg)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{S-duality}: the Montonen--Olive duality of $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM at
 $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, exchanging gauge group $G$ with $G^L$;
\item \textbf{Langlands duality}: the geometric Langlands equivalence
 $D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\Bun_G(C)) \simeq \mathrm{IndCoh}_{\mathrm{Nilp}}(\mathrm{LocSys}_{G^L}(C))$,
 with $V_{-h^\vee}(\frakg)$ on the left and $G^L$ on the right;
\item \textbf{Koszul duality}: the chiral bar-cobar duality of Volume~I,
 which exchanges
 \[
 G(C,\frakg) \;\longleftrightarrow\; G(C,\frakg^L)
 \]
 as quantum vertex chiral groups, with the dual level formula
 $k' = -k - 2h^\vee$ specialising at the critical level to the
 Feigin--Frenkel center.
\end{enumerate}
The evidence: (i) S-duality exchanges W-bosons (roots of $\frakg$) with
magnetic monopoles (coroots of $\frakg$ = roots of $\frakg^L$), which is
exactly the root/coroot exchange of Koszul duality; (ii) the Feigin--Frenkel
isomorphism $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg}) \simeq \mathrm{Fun}\,\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}$
identifies the Koszul-dual datum with the Langlands dual group at the critical
level; (iii) the dual level $k' = -k - 2h^\vee$ at $k = -h^\vee$ gives
$k' = -h^\vee$ (self-dual), matching the self-dual coupling $|\tau| = 1$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Root datum exchange under S/Langlands/Koszul duality]
\label{prop:phys-root-datum-exchange}
The triple duality of Conjecture~\ref{conj:phys-triple-sduality-langlands-koszul}
acts on the generalised root datum of $G(C,\frakg)$ by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item exchanging roots and coroots: $\alpha \leftrightarrow \alpha^\vee$;
\item inverting the Cartan matrix: $A \leftrightarrow A^T$;
\item preserving the Weyl group: $W(\frakg) \simeq W(\frakg^L)$;
\item exchanging long and short roots (for non-simply-laced $\frakg$).
\end{enumerate}
This is identical to the action of Langlands duality on root data and to the
action of S-duality on the BPS spectrum (W-bosons $\leftrightarrow$ monopoles).
For simply-laced types, the root datum is self-dual: $G(C,\frakg)^! \cong G(C,\frakg)$.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Type IIB on CY3: 4d \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{N}=2$}{N=2} theories and the Hitchin system}
\label{sec:phys-iib-cy3-hitchin}
% ============================================================

For local (non-compact) CY3 geometries, the Hitchin system encodes the exact
low-energy data of 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ gauge theories. This section records the
precise identifications in mathematical terms; every physical claim is either
a theorem (cited) or a conjecture (labelled). The \emph{physical heuristic}
label marks passages where the identification is suggested by physics but not
yet established mathematically.

\subsection{Type IIB on a CY3 and the Coulomb branch}
\label{subsec:phys-iib-coulomb}

Let $X$ be a compact CY3. Type IIB string theory compactified on $X$ yields a
4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ supergravity theory. The massless spectrum is determined by
the Hodge numbers: $h^{2,1}(X)$ vector multiplets and $h^{1,1}(X)$ hypermultiplets.
The moduli space factorises as
\[
 \mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_V \times \mathcal{M}_H,
\]
with $\mathcal{M}_V$ a special K\"{a}hler manifold of complex dimension
$h^{2,1}(X)$ (the Coulomb branch, identified with the complex structure moduli
of $X$) and $\mathcal{M}_H$ a quaternionic K\"{a}hler manifold.

The BPS states arise from D3-branes wrapping special Lagrangian 3-cycles in
$X$. For a class $\gamma \in H_3(X, \mathbb{Z})$, the central charge is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-bps-central-charge}
 Z_\gamma(u) = \oint_\gamma \Omega_u, \qquad u \in \mathcal{M}_V,
\end{equation}
and the mass of the BPS state is $|Z_\gamma(u)|$. The BPS index
\[
 \Omega(\gamma; u) = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_\gamma}(-1)^{2J_3}(2J_3)^2
\]
is locally constant but jumps across walls of marginal stability
$\mathcal{W}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) = \{u : Z_{\gamma_1}/Z_{\gamma_2} \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}\}$.

In the Vol~III framework: the charge lattice $\Gamma = H_3(X, \mathbb{Z})$
with its symplectic intersection form is the lattice $\Lambda(X)$ of the
generalised root datum $\mathcal{R}(X)$, and the BPS indices $\Omega(\gamma; u)$
are the root multiplicities (Section~\ref{sec:cy-langlands}).

\subsection{The Seiberg--Witten curve as spectral curve}
\label{subsec:phys-sw-spectral}

For local CY3 geometries engineered from a curve $C$ and a group $G$ via
class-$\mathcal{S}$ compactification of the 6d $(2,0)$ theory, the Coulomb
branch is the Hitchin base and the Seiberg--Witten fibration is the Hitchin
fibration.

\begin{theorem}[Donagi--Witten; Gorsky--Krichever--Marshakov--Mironov--Morozov]
\label{thm:phys-sw-hitchin}
The Seiberg--Witten curve $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SW}}$ of the 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$
gauge theory of class $\mathcal{S}[G, C]$ is the spectral curve of the Hitchin
system on $(C, G)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The SW curve is
 \[
 \Sigma_a = \bigl\{(x, p) \in T^*C \;\big|\; \det(p\cdot\mathrm{id} - \varphi(x)) = 0\bigr\}
 \;\subset\; T^*C,
 \]
 a branched cover $\Sigma_a \to C$ of degree $\mathrm{rk}(G)$, parametrised
 by $a \in \mathcal{A}_H$ (the Coulomb branch coordinates);
\item The SW differential is $\lambda_{\mathrm{SW}} = p\,dx$, the tautological
 $1$-form on $T^*C$ restricted to $\Sigma_a$;
\item The periods $a_I = \oint_{\alpha_I} \lambda_{\mathrm{SW}}$ and
 $a_{D,J} = \oint_{\beta_J} \lambda_{\mathrm{SW}} = \partial\mathcal{F}/\partial a_J$
 give the special coordinates and their duals;
\item The Hitchin base $\mathcal{A}_H$ is the Coulomb branch
 $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$, and the Hitchin fibration parametrises the
 family of SW curves.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{example}[Pure \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{SU}(2)$}{SU(2)}]
\label{ex:phys-pure-su2}
For $G = \mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $C = \mathbb{P}^1$, the Hitchin base is
$\mathcal{A}_H = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1, K^{\otimes 2}) \cong \mathbb{C}$ (one
Coulomb branch parameter $u$). The spectral curve in $T^*\mathbb{P}^1$ is
$\Sigma_u\colon p^2 = u + \Lambda^2 x + \Lambda^{-2} x^{-1}$, recovering the
Seiberg--Witten curve. The discriminant locus gives the monopole and dyon
singularities in the Coulomb branch, where BPS states become massless.
\end{example}

\subsection{Hitchin system as CY geometry}
\label{subsec:phys-hitchin-cy-framework}

The Hitchin moduli space $\mathcal{M}_H(C, G)$ is a non-compact holomorphic
symplectic manifold, hence a non-compact Calabi--Yau manifold of complex
dimension $2\dim(G)(g-1)$. In the Vol~III framework (Section~\ref{sec:cy-langlands}),
this provides the following data for the quantum vertex chiral group:
\begin{itemize}
\item The lattice $\Lambda(X) = H_1(\Sigma_a, \mathbb{Z})$ of the spectral
 curve is the charge lattice of the 4d theory;
\item The Hitchin base $\mathcal{A}_H$ parametrises the complex structure
 deformations (the Coulomb branch);
\item The Hitchin fibres are the abelian varieties on which the BPS states live;
\item The BPS indices $\Omega(\gamma; a)$ determine the root multiplicities of
 the quantum vertex chiral group.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Argyres--Douglas theories and irregular Langlands parameters}
\label{subsec:phys-argyres-douglas}

Argyres--Douglas (AD) theories are 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ SCFTs arising at special
points $u_{\mathrm{AD}} \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$ where mutually
non-local BPS states simultaneously become massless. These points correspond to
the intersection of walls of marginal stability.

In the Hitchin language, an Argyres--Douglas point occurs when the spectral
curve $\Sigma_a$ acquires a singularity of higher order than a simple node.
For the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ theory, the AD point at $u = \pm \Lambda^2$ (where
$\Sigma_u$ has a cusp) gives the simplest AD theory of type $H_0$ (or $(A_1, A_1)$).

\begin{remark}[Argyres--Douglas and irregular Langlands parameters]
\label{rem:phys-ad-irregular}
In the shadow-ramification dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:shadow-langlands-type}
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-ramification}), Argyres--Douglas theories
correspond to irregular Langlands parameters (shadow class M, depth $r = \infty$).
The cusp singularity of the spectral curve produces an irregular singular point
of the corresponding $D$-module on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$, and the Stokes data at
the irregular singular point encodes the AD spectrum. The Borel resummation
required to extract finite answers from the class-M shadow tower (Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-convergence-qgl}) is the mathematical incarnation of the
AD wall-crossing phenomenon: the resurgent series produced by the Nekrasov
partition function near the AD point has Stokes data controlled by the BPS
indices $\Omega(\gamma; u)$ of the AD theory.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Coulomb branch as complex structure moduli}
\label{subsec:phys-coulomb-cx-structure}

For Type IIB on $X$, the Coulomb branch is $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cx}}(X)$, the
complex structure moduli space. The special K\"{a}hler geometry on
$\mathcal{M}_V$ is determined by the periods of $\Omega_u\in H^{3,0}(X_u)$:
choose a symplectic basis $\{A^I, B_J\}$ of $H_3(X, \mathbb{Z})$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-periods}
 z^I(u) = \oint_{A^I} \Omega_u, \qquad \mathcal{F}_J(u) = \oint_{B_J} \Omega_u.
\end{equation}
The $z^I$ serve as special coordinates and $\mathcal{F}_J = \partial\mathcal{F}/\partial z^J$
for a holomorphic prepotential $\mathcal{F}(z)$ (homogeneous of degree~2).
Under mirror symmetry $X \leftrightarrow X^\vee$, the roles of complex structure
and K\"{a}hler moduli are exchanged: for Type IIA on $X^\vee$, the Coulomb
branch is $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{K\ddot{a}h}}(X^\vee)$ and the prepotential is
computed by genus-0 Gromov--Witten invariants.

\section{Wall-crossing as Maurer--Cartan gauge equivalence}
\label{sec:phys-wall-crossing-mc}
% ============================================================

The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula, governing the jumps in BPS
indices across walls of marginal stability, admits a natural interpretation in
the Vol~I/$A_\infty$ framework as gauge equivalence of Maurer--Cartan elements.

\subsection{The Kontsevich--Soibelman formula}
\label{subsec:phys-ks-formula}

Fix a charge lattice $\Gamma$ with antisymmetric pairing $\langle -, -\rangle$
and a central charge function $Z\colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{C}$ holomorphic over
$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$.

\begin{definition}[BPS automorphisms]
\label{def:phys-bps-auto}
For each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ with $\Omega(\gamma) \neq 0$, define the
automorphism $\mathcal{K}_\gamma$ of the quantum torus algebra $\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]$
(with generators $X_\gamma$ satisfying
$X_{\gamma_1} X_{\gamma_2} = (-1)^{\langle\gamma_1,\gamma_2\rangle} X_{\gamma_1+\gamma_2}$)
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-ks-automorphism}
 \mathcal{K}_\gamma = \prod_{n \geq 1}(1 - (-1)^n X_{n\gamma})^{n\,\Omega(n\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
For the primitive case $\Omega(\gamma) = 1$, this reduces to the
Kontsevich--Soibelman symplectomorphism
$\mathcal{K}_\gamma(X_{\gamma'}) = X_{\gamma'}(1 + X_\gamma)^{\langle\gamma,\gamma'\rangle}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kontsevich--Soibelman]
\label{thm:phys-ks-wcf}
The ordered product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-ks-product}
 \mathcal{A}(u) = \prod_{\gamma:\,\arg Z_\gamma(u)\,\downarrow}^{\curvearrowleft}
 \mathcal{K}_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma;\,u)}
\end{equation}
taken over all BPS charges $\gamma$ in decreasing order of phase $\arg Z_\gamma(u)$
is invariant under continuous deformation of $u$ across walls of marginal stability.
Although the individual $\Omega(\gamma; u)$ jump and the phase ordering changes,
the total product $\mathcal{A}(u)$ is constant.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Wall-crossing as gauge equivalence in the shadow obstruction tower}
\label{subsec:phys-wc-mc-id}

In the Vol~I/$A_\infty$ framework, the KS wall-crossing formula is gauge
equivalence of Maurer--Cartan elements.

\begin{construction}[MC interpretation of wall-crossing]
\label{constr:phys-mc-wall-crossing}
The key identifications:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The $L_\infty$-algebra controlling the deformation problem is the
 \emph{scattering diagram $L_\infty$-algebra} $\mathfrak{L}_\Gamma$, built
 from the charge lattice $\Gamma$ with its antisymmetric form. Its
 Maurer--Cartan equation governs consistent scattering diagrams on
 $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$ (the $Z$-plane);
\item A BPS spectrum $\{\Omega(\gamma; u)\}_{\gamma \in \Gamma}$ at a point
 $u \in \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$ determines a Maurer--Cartan element
 \[
 \Theta_u = \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \Omega(\gamma; u)\cdot e_\gamma
 \;\in\; \mathfrak{L}_\Gamma,
 \]
 where $e_\gamma$ are the generators associated to charges $\gamma$;
\item Crossing a wall $\mathcal{W}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ corresponds to a
 gauge transformation $\Theta_u \mapsto \Theta_{u'} = g\cdot\Theta_u$ in
 $\mathfrak{L}_\Gamma$, where $g$ is determined by the KS symplectomorphism
 $\mathcal{K}_\gamma$;
\item The KS invariant $\mathcal{A}(u)$ is the gauge equivalence class
 $[\Theta_u] \in \mathrm{MC}(\mathfrak{L}_\Gamma)/\mathrm{gauge}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

\begin{conjecture}[Wall-crossing = shadow tower gauge equivalence]
\label{prop:phys-wc-shadow}
The KS wall-crossing formula for the 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ theory from CY3 $X$ is
equivalent to the statement that the MC elements $\Theta_u$, as $u$ varies over
$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}} = \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cx}}(X)$, are
gauge-equivalent in $\mathfrak{L}_{\Gamma(X)}$. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The gauge equivalence class $[\Theta_u]$ is a topological invariant of
 the 4d theory;
\item The individual $\Omega(\gamma; u)$ are the coordinates of $\Theta_u$ in
 a particular gauge (a choice of chamber in $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}$);
\item Wall-crossing is a change of gauge; the KS product $\mathcal{A}(u)$ is
 the gauge-invariant content.
\end{enumerate}
This is the precise connection to the universal MC element $\Theta_A$ of
Volume~I: the shadow obstruction tower $\Theta_{A_X}^{\leq r}$ at successive
degrees recovers the BPS spectrum at successive levels of complexity.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Scattering diagrams and tropical geometry]
\label{rem:phys-scattering-diagrams}
The scattering diagram technology of Gross--Siebert and Kontsevich--Soibelman
provides the combinatorial framework for encoding the MC element $\Theta_u$. A
scattering diagram $\mathfrak{D}$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ consists of rays (walls)
emanating from the origin, decorated with automorphisms of the quantum torus.
The consistency condition (trivial monodromy around the origin) is exactly the
MC equation for $\Theta_u$. This is made precise by Bridgeland (Stokes data)
and by Gaiotto--Moore--Neitzke (spectral network formalism).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Motivic DT invariants and the Hall algebra]
\label{rem:phys-motivic-dt}
The BPS indices $\Omega(\gamma; u)$ are numerical shadows of the motivic
Donaldson--Thomas invariants $\Omega^{\mathrm{mot}}(\gamma; u) \in K_0(\mathrm{Var}/\mathbb{C})[\mathbb{L}^{-1/2}]$. The motivic Hall algebra $H(\mathcal{C})$
of a CY3 category $\mathcal{C}$ provides the natural home for the motivic
wall-crossing formula; the integration map
$H(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathrm{quantum\, torus}$ descends to the KS symplectomorphisms.
In the Vol~III language: the passage
\[
 H^{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{C}) \xrightarrow{\text{cohomological}} \mathcal{H}(\mathcal{C}) = \mathrm{CoHA} \xrightarrow{\text{positive half}} G^+(X)
\]
recovers the $\Eone$-sector (positive half) of $G(X)$, recovering the
identification of the CoHA as $Y^+$ established in the CY-A$_3$ proof.
\end{remark}

\section{The Nekrasov partition function and quantum curves}
\label{sec:phys-nekrasov-quantum-curves}
% ============================================================

The Nekrasov partition function computes the exact prepotential of 4d
$\mathcal{N}=2$ theories and, in the NS limit, quantises the Hitchin spectral
curve into an oper. This connects the four-dimensional physics directly to the
geometric Langlands via the identification of quantum curves with
$G^L$-opers.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\Omega$}{Omega}-background and the Nekrasov partition function}
\label{subsec:phys-omega-background}

Let $T = U(1)_{\epsilon_1} \times U(1)_{\epsilon_2}$ act on $\mathbb{R}^4 \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ by $(z_1, z_2) \mapsto (e^{i\epsilon_1}z_1, e^{i\epsilon_2}z_2)$. The
\emph{Nekrasov partition function} for a 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ theory with gauge
group $G$ and Coulomb branch parameters $\vec{a}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-nekrasov-pf}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}; \mathfrak{q}) = \sum_{\vec{\lambda}}
 \mathfrak{q}^{|\vec{\lambda}|} z_{\vec{\lambda}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}),
\end{equation}
where the sum is over tuples of Young diagrams (fixed points of the $T$-action
on the instanton moduli space) and $z_{\vec{\lambda}}$ are equivariant weights.

\begin{theorem}[Nekrasov]
\label{thm:phys-nekrasov}
The Seiberg--Witten prepotential is recovered as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-nekrasov-limit}
 \mathcal{F}(\vec{a}) = \lim_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2 \to 0}
 \epsilon_1\epsilon_2 \log Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}; \mathfrak{q}).
\end{equation}
The genus expansion $\log Z_{\mathrm{top}} = \sum_{g \geq 0} g_s^{2g-2}\mathcal{F}_g(t)$
gives $\mathcal{F}_0$ as the prepotential, $\mathcal{F}_1$ as the genus-1
free energy (related to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the Ray--Singer torsion),
and $\mathcal{F}_g$ for $g \geq 2$ as the higher-genus Gromov--Witten/DT
invariants: in the Vol~I language, $\mathcal{F}_g$ is the genus-$g$ obstruction
$\mathrm{obs}_g = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \lambda_g$ on the uniform-weight lane.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The Nekrasov--Shatashvili limit and quantum curves}
\label{subsec:phys-ns-limit}

The Nekrasov--Shatashvili (NS) limit is $\epsilon_2 \to 0$ with
$\epsilon_1 = \hbar$ fixed. In this limit, the partition function develops
an essential singularity
\[
 \log Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\hbar, \epsilon_2, \vec{a})
 \xrightarrow{\epsilon_2 \to 0}
 \frac{1}{\epsilon_2}\mathcal{W}(\hbar, \vec{a}) + O(1),
\]
where $\mathcal{W}$ is the twisted effective superpotential.

\begin{theorem}[Nekrasov--Shatashvili]
\label{thm:phys-ns-quantization}
In the NS limit:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The saddle-point equation $\exp(\partial\mathcal{W}/\partial a_I) = 1$
 determines the quantum Coulomb branch parameters $a_I(\hbar)$;
\item The Seiberg--Witten curve $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SW}}$ is quantised: the
 classical equation $\det(p\cdot\mathrm{id} - \varphi(x)) = 0$ is promoted
 to a \emph{quantum curve} (a differential equation)
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-quantum-curve}
 \widehat{\Sigma}_\hbar\colon\qquad
 \det\!\left(\hbar\frac{d}{dx}\cdot\mathrm{id} - \varphi(x)\right)\psi(x) = 0,
 \end{equation}
 obtained by the canonical quantisation $p \mapsto \hbar\,d/dx$;
\item The NS partition function $\psi(x) = Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}^{\mathrm{NS}}(\hbar, x)$
 is a solution of the quantum curve equation~\eqref{eq:phys-quantum-curve}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Quantum curves are opers]
\label{rem:phys-quantum-curve-oper}
The quantum spectral curve~\eqref{eq:phys-quantum-curve} is a $G^L$-oper on
$C$: a flat $G^L$-connection with a specific reduction to a Borel subgroup.
For $G = \mathrm{SU}(N)$, the quantum curve is an $N$-th order ODE, the
null-vector equation of the $\mathcal{W}_N$-algebra at level
$k = -N + \hbar^{-1}$. The critical level $k = -h^\vee = -N$ is
$\hbar \to \infty$; the NS parameter $\hbar$ provides the deformation from
critical. The Feigin--Frenkel center $\mathfrak{z}(\hat{\frakg})$ at the critical
level parametrises $G^L$-opers, and the NS partition function is a section of
the line bundle on the space of opers determined by the $\mathcal{W}$-algebra.
In the Vol~III framework: the passage from the spectral curve (classical) to
the oper (quantum) is the passage from the tree-level $R$-matrix $r(z)$ to
the full quantum $R$-matrix $R(z, \hbar)$ of $G(X)$. The three regimes
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$ correspond to three Langlands regimes:
$\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_2 = 0$ (classical geometric Langlands),
$\epsilon_2 = 0$, $\epsilon_1 = \hbar \neq 0$ (quantum geometric Langlands,
opers), and both nonzero (doubly quantum, $K$-theoretic Langlands).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The AGT correspondence}
\label{subsec:phys-agt}

The Alday--Gaiotto--Tachikawa correspondence provides the most direct link
between Nekrasov partition functions and vertex algebras.

\begin{theorem}[AGT correspondence]
\label{thm:phys-agt}
For the 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ gauge theory of class
$\mathcal{S}[\mathrm{SU}(N), C_{g,n}]$ on a genus-$g$ curve with $n$
punctures:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-agt}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}^{\mathrm{SU}(N)}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}, \vec{m}; \mathfrak{q})
 = \Bigl\langle \prod_{i=1}^n V_{\alpha_i}(z_i) \Bigr\rangle_{\mathcal{W}_N,\,c(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)},
\end{equation}
where the right side is a conformal block of the $\mathcal{W}_N$-algebra at
central charge
$c = (N-1)\bigl(1 + N(N+1)(\epsilon_1/\epsilon_2 + \epsilon_2/\epsilon_1 + 2)\bigr)$,
and vertex operators $V_{\alpha_i}$ are determined by the puncture data.
\end{theorem}

In the Vol~III framework, the AGT correspondence is the statement that the
Nekrasov partition function is a genus-$g$ $n$-point function of the quantum
chiral algebra $A_X$ ($\mathcal{W}_N$ sector of $G(X)$). The genus expansion
of $Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}$ is the shadow obstruction tower: genus-0, no punctures
gives the prepotential $\mathcal{F}_0$ (degree-2 shadow, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$);
genus-0, $n$ punctures gives the $n$-point tree-level amplitude (degree-$n$
shadow); genus $g$ gives the free energy $\mathcal{F}_g$ ($\mathrm{obs}_g$).

\begin{conjecture}[Nekrasov partition function as character of \texorpdfstring{$G(X)$}{G(X)}]
\label{conj:phys-nekrasov-character}
For any CY3 $X$ engineering a 4d $\mathcal{N}=2$ theory, the Nekrasov partition
function $Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}; \mathfrak{q})$ is the
graded character of the vacuum module of the quantum vertex chiral group $G(X)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-nekrasov-character}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \vec{a}; \mathfrak{q})
 = \chi_{G(X)}(\mathcal{F}_{\vec{a}};\, q_1, q_2, \mathfrak{q}),
\end{equation}
where $q_i = e^{i\epsilon_i}$ are the equivariant fugacities. In the
unrefined limit $\epsilon_1 = -\epsilon_2 = g_s$, this reduces to the
topological string partition function, which is the denominator identity of
$G(X)$. Evidence:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item For $X = \mathbb{C}^3$: $Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}$ is the MacMahon function
 $M(q) = \prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-n}$, which is the character of the MacMahon
 module for the positive half $Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)$; the full
 $\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$ or affine-Yangian object requires adjoining the
 Cartan/completion data (Schiffmann--Vasserot, Maulik--Okounkov);
\item The AGT correspondence identifies $Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}$ for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$
 theories with conformal blocks of the Virasoro algebra at $c = 1 + 6(\sqrt{\beta} - 1/\sqrt{\beta})^2$, $\beta = \epsilon_1/\epsilon_2$, which is a subalgebra
 of the completed $\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$ side of $G(\mathbb{C}^3)$, not of
 the CoHA positive half alone;
 \item For $K3 \times E$: the topological string partition function involves
 $\Delta_5^{-1}$ (the inverse primitive Gritsenko denominator), which is the Fock space
 character of the BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ (the denominator
 formula inverted).
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Quantum vertex chiral Langlands from the NS limit]
\label{conj:phys-qvcg-langlands-ns}
The geometric Langlands correspondence for a curve $C$ and gauge group $G$,
in the quantum ($\hbar \neq 0$) regime, is the equivalence of representation
categories obtained from the Drinfeld centre of the $\Eone$ positive half:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phys-qvcg-langlands}
 \cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(G^+(X_{G,C}))) \;\simeq\; D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\Bun_{G^L}(C)),
\end{equation}
where $X_{G,C}$ is the local CY3 engineering the class-$\mathcal{S}$ theory
and $D\text{-}\mathrm{mod}(\Bun_{G^L}(C))$ is the category of $D$-modules on
the moduli of $G^L$-bundles on $C$, with the quantum parameter $\hbar = \epsilon_1$
playing the role of the level of the $D$-module category. Here $G^+(X_{G,C})$
denotes the $\Eone$ positive half supplied by the CY3/CoHA construction; a full
$G(X_{G,C})$ notation is justified only after the centre/double/completion is
constructed. This is the NS-limit version of the QGL equivalence of
Section~\ref{sec:quantum-geom-langlands}, with the quantum spectral
curve~\eqref{eq:phys-quantum-curve} serving as the oper on the spectral side.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Open problems}
\label{sec:open-problems}
% ============================================================

The following six problems are the concrete frontier of CY Langlands. Each is stated as an open problem, not a conjecture, because the evidence is not yet sufficient to commit to a definite equivalence.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] \textbf{K3 mirror map as $d = 2$ Langlands.} Is the Mukai--Dolgachev--Nikulin mirror map on lattice-polarized K3 categories the $d = 2$ Langlands involution of Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-langlands}? A first test: verify that $\Phi$ sends mirror K3 pairs to Koszul-dual $\Etwo$-chiral algebras in the Volume~I sense.
 \item[(ii)] \textbf{BPS Langlands for $d = 3$.} For a CY $3$-fold $X$ with mirror $X^\vee$, are the conjectural quantum vertex chiral groups $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$ (CY-C) Langlands dual as $\Eone$-chiral algebras? By CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}) the chiral algebras $A_X$ and $A_{X^\vee}$ exist; the open content is the construction of $G(X)$ (CY-C) and the Langlands duality relation itself. The CoHAs of $X$ and $X^\vee$ are associative algebras, not chiral algebras; they provide the $\Eone$-ordered halves of the conjectural $G(X)$ and $G(X^\vee)$.
 \item[(iii)] \textbf{QGL parameter equals shadow $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.} Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:qgl-equals-shadow} for the Kac--Moody family as a theorem (the available identity is the proportionality of definitions), then extend to $\Phi(\cC)$ for CY $2$-categories $\cC$. The key step is to identify the shadow tower of $\Phi(\cC)$ with the Gaitsgory deformation at finite $\kappa_{\mathrm{QGL}}$.
 \item[(iv)] \textbf{Categorified Feigin--Frenkel.} Does there exist a CY $2$-category $\cC_{\mathrm{FF}}$ whose image $\Phi(\cC_{\mathrm{FF}})$ is, at the critical level, equivalent to the Feigin--Frenkel center $\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Op}_{G^L}(D))$ viewed as a constant $\Etwo$-chiral algebra? If so, $\cC_{\mathrm{FF}}$ would be a geometric realization of opers at the categorical level.
 \item[(v)] \textbf{Geometric Satake from CY.} The geometric Satake equivalence (Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen, 2007) identifies the category of perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with $\Rep(G^L)$. Can the equivalence be derived from a CY $3$-category $\cC_{\mathrm{Sat}}$ via $\Phi$, so that the Drinfeld centre of the $\Eone$ representation category of $\Phi_3(\cC_{\mathrm{Sat}})$ recovers $\mathrm{Perv}(\mathrm{Gr}_G)$? Supporting evidence: both sides carry braided monoidal structures; both admit fusion constructions; both are controlled by an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple at the critical level.
 \item[(vi)] \textbf{Hitchin-K3 family and K3 Yangian modules.} For a K3 surface $S$ with ADE enhancement of type $\frakg$ and a linear system $|L| \cong \bP^g$ of genus-$g$ curves, does the family of Beilinson--Drinfeld quantizations $\{\mathrm{CDO}_{\mathrm{Bun}_\mathbf{G}(C_t), \mathrm{crit}}\}_{t \in |L|}$ produce a factorization sheaf on $\mathrm{Ran}(|L|)$ valued in $Y(\frakg_{K3})$-modules (Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-yangian-module})? The first instance is $\mathbf{G} = \mathrm{SL}_2$, $g = 2$: the Hitchin base is $3$-dimensional, the spectral curve has genus~$5$, and the linear system is~$\bP^2$. The Hitchin spectral parameter identification $\lambda = z$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:hitchin-spectral-yangian}) is the mechanism; evidence would require computing the factorization structure explicitly for one ADE type.
\end{enumerate}

Problems (i)--(iii) are the $d = 2$ core and can in principle be attacked with the technology of Volume~I and the $d = 2$ functor of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}.
Problems (iv)--(v) require two missing constructions:
(iv) asks to reverse-engineer a CY $2$-category from a target chiral algebra, and
(v) asks to place the geometric Satake equivalence, the foundational theorem of geometric Langlands, inside the CY--chiral dictionary.
Problem~(vi) is the K3-specific instantiation of the Hitchin-K3 construction problem of Section~\ref{sec:chiral-hitchin-k3}; explicit numerical data are recorded by \texttt{chiral\_hitchin\_k3.py}.
A solution would derive local geometric Langlands from Calabi--Yau geometry alone.

\paragraph{Feigin--Frenkel input and CY extension data.}
The unconditional input is Theorem~\ref{thm:feigin-frenkel-center}, cited
from Feigin--Frenkel (1992) and Frenkel (2007), together with the
standard level-$1$ identity
\(c(\widehat{\frakg}_1)=\mathrm{rank}(\frakg)\) and the quantum
geometric Langlands relation \(\Psi(k)\Psi(k^L)=1\). The CY extensions
require the named comparison maps in their statements: the
critical-level Verdier intertwiner, the CY Langlands mirror functor, the
Hitchin spectral specialisation, the QGL-shadow parameter map, the K3
Yangian QGL duality, the \(K3\times E\) Hecke eigensheaf, and the
ramification-depth comparison. The six problems of
Section~\ref{sec:open-problems} ask for constructions rather than
asserting expected equivalences.

The chapter uses four inputs from the earlier volumes.
From Volume~I: the bar functor $B$ and the Verdier leg $D_{\mathrm{Ran}} \circ B$ of Theorem~A (four-functor picture), the shadow tower $\Theta_A$ and its G/L/C/M classification (Theorem~D), the Koszul locus definition, and the Kac--Moody chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\frakg)) = \dim(\frakg)(k + h^\vee)/(2 h^\vee)$.

\begin{remark}[Evidence and proof obligation]
\label{rem:gl-unified-grt1-torsor-evidence}
The proposed comparison has four independent inputs. The first is the
torsor structure on $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EK}}$ by
Etingof--Kazhdan's Theorem on uniqueness of associator-dependence
(Etingof--Kazhdan 1996, Part~I, Thm~1.2 bosonic; 2008, Part~V, \S5
super). The second is the motivic Galois action on the mixed-Tate
subquotient of $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{mot}}$ by Deligne--Goncharov
(\emph{Ann.\ Sci.\ ENS}~38, 2005, Thm~4.11) and Brown (2012, Thm~1.2).
The third is the Kuga--Satake correspondence from the primitive K3 Hodge
structure to the associated abelian variety, which provides an
$\mathrm{O}^+(\mathrm{II}_{4,20})$-equivariant period map compatible with
the Mukai lattice Hodge structure (Kuga--Satake 1967
\emph{J.\ Math.\ Kyoto Univ.}~7). The fourth is Willwacher's bijection
$\mathrm{GRT}_1 \simeq H^0(\mathrm{GC}_2)$, which transports via the K3
Kuga--Satake map to a common graph-cohomological comparison target. The
missing proof obligation is the construction of the super extension and the
canonicality of $\Upsilon_{\mathrm{K3}}$ beyond the mixed-Tate subquotient
(Furusho 2011 Thm~2.1 for super-associator compatibility;
Willwacher--Zivkovic 2014 \emph{Adv.\ Math.}~266 for graph-complex
super-extensions).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Four comparison paths]
\label{rem:gl-grt1-four-paths}
\index{GRT1 torsor!four verification paths}
The $\mathrm{GRT}_1^{\mathrm{super}}$-torsor comparison has four independent
consistency paths:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Drinfeld-associator side}: the freedom in $\Phi \in
 \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EK}}$ matches the $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-gauge
 freedom in the Knizhnik--Zamolodchikov connection for the
 K3 OPE algebra (Kazhdan--Lusztig 1993 \emph{DMJ}~71; Drinfeld 1989).
 \item \emph{Motivic-period side}: the freedom in a period
 realisation $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{mot}}$ for K3 matches the
 $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-action on mixed Tate motives over $\mathbb{Z}$
 (Brown 2012 \emph{Ann.\ Math.}~175 Thm~1.2).
 \item \emph{Graph-cohomology side}: Willwacher's bijection
 $\mathrm{GRT}_1 \simeq H^0(\mathrm{GC}_2)$ transports both
 torsors to a common home in graph cohomology (Willwacher 2015
 \emph{Invent.}~200).
 \item \emph{Kuga--Satake side}: the K3 embedding into a Shimura
 variety of orthogonal type $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{KS}}$
 intertwines the Mukai weight-$0$ Hodge structure with the
 Kuga--Satake abelian-variety period matrix, providing an
 arithmetic realisation (Kuga--Satake 1967; Morrison 1985
 \emph{Duke Math.\ J.}~52).
\end{enumerate}
All four paths point to the same $\mathrm{GRT}_1^{\mathrm{super}}$-action.
The equality of the actions, including the bounded higher-homotopy corrections
tracked by the $A_\infty$-quasi-Hopf structure, is precisely the content of
Conjecture~\ref{thm:gl-unified-grt1-torsor-k3}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Mixed Tate motive from weight-\texorpdfstring{$n$}{n} \texorpdfstring{$\zeta(n)$}{zeta(n)}-periods]
\label{rem:gl-mixed-tate-zeta-periods}
\index{mixed Tate motive!K3 CE cohomology}
\index{motivic zeta values on K3}
The mixed Tate sub-motive of $H^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}
(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cycat-ce-mixed-tate}})
is functorially compatible with the CY Langlands prediction.
Specifically, the weight-$n$ graded piece
$\mathrm{gr}^W_n H^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})$
carries an action of the Galois group
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{MT}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbb{Q})$ of the Tannakian
category of mixed Tate motives unramified over $\mathbb{Z}$
(Deligne--Goncharov 2005; Brown 2012), with Lie algebra
\[
 \mathrm{Lie}\bigl(\mathrm{Gal}(\mathrm{MT}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbb{Q})\bigr)
 \;=\; \mathbb{Q} \cdot \mathrm{id} \ \oplus\
 \bigoplus_{n \geq 3,\, n\,\text{odd}} \mathbb{Q} \cdot f_n,
\]
where each $f_n$ is a free generator realising the period $\zeta(n)/
(2\pi i)^n$. The \emph{chiral Langlands pairing} on this sub-motive
sends $f_n \mapsto f_n^\vee$, where $f_n^\vee$ is the
co-generator in $\mathrm{Lie}(U^{\mathrm{MT}})^\vee$ detecting the
same $\zeta(n)$-period functional; equivalently, chiral Langlands
restricted to the $\mathrm{gr}^W_n$-graded coincides with the
zeta-motivic Tannakian duality. This gives a concrete prediction:
$\mathrm{gr}^W_n$ pieces of $H^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}(\Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(K3))))$

(\Phi_2(\mathrm{Fuk}(\mathrm{K3}^\vee)))$ in their $\zeta(n)$-period

The prediction is verifiable at weights $n = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11$ using
the Padovan-dimension $\dim \phi^{(n)}$ sequence, which gives the
bracket-weight multiplicities $1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, \ldots$ for
$n = 3, 4, 5, \ldots, 12$. At weight $12$ the first depth-$4$ MZV
$\zeta(3,3,3,3)$ enters irreducibly; matching this across the
Langlands exchange is a sharp test of
Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:gl-cy-langlands-grt-torsor-exchange}}.
Primary: Deligne--Goncharov 2005 \emph{Ann.\ Sci.\ ENS}~38;
Brown 2012 \emph{Ann.\ Math.}~175;

\end{remark}

\section{Tempered Hecke on Mukai-graded
\texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5} as Wilson lines on Fricke self-duality}
\label{sec:gl-kazhdan-fricke-wilson-section}

The Vol~I derived-Langlands side (\S\cite{VolI} (Vol~I))
proves that the Andrianov--Ikeda spinor Hecke $T_p$ acts on every
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$-graded piece of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ as the
single tempered scalar $\lambda_p = a_p(\Delta_{E_6}) + p^8 + p^9$.
Translated through the chiral-CY functor $\Phi$, this eigenvalue is the
trace of a Wilson line on the Fricke self-dual $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$: the
spectral-side incarnation of the automorphic identity
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2 = \Delta_{10} = \mathrm{Ik}(\Delta_{E_6})$.

\begin{remark}[Fricke-Wilson trace of the Mukai-graded
Hecke action]
\label{rem:gl-kazhdan-fricke-wilson}
\index{Kazhdan!Fricke-Wilson trace|textbf}
\index{Hecke!Wilson line!Mukai grading}
\index{Fricke self-duality!Saito--Kurokawa decomposition}
On the geometric Langlands side of the K3-BKM self-duality
(Remark~\ref{rem:gl-fricke-self-duality}), the tempered
Mukai-graded eigenvalue $\lambda_p(v) = a_p(\Delta_{E_6}) + p^8 + p^9$
of Vol~I \cite{VolI}
incarnates as the Wilson-line trace
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gl-kazhdan-wilson-trace}
 \bigl\langle W_p\bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)^{\mathrm{Fricke}}}
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{tr}_{W_p^{\mathrm{std}}(\Delta_{E_6})}
 \;+\;
 p^8\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{W_p^{\mathrm{triv}}}
 \;+\;
 p^9\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{W_p^{\mathrm{triv}}},
\end{equation}
with $W_p^{\mathrm{std}}(\Delta_{E_6})$ the weight-$15/2$ Wilson line
attached to the elliptic factor and $W_p^{\mathrm{triv}}$ the trivial
Wilson line contributing the two Saito--Kurokawa CAP pieces at Langlands
parameters $p^{\pm 1/2}$. \emph{Fricke symmetry.} The $w_8$-involution
fixes $W_p^{\mathrm{std}}(\Delta_{E_6})$ (since $\Delta_{E_6}$ is
$w_1$-invariant on $S_{16}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}))$, the trivial
level) and swaps the two CAP pieces $p^8 \leftrightarrow p^9$ (weight
reflection $k \mapsto 2h^\vee - k$ with $h^\vee = 9$ in the symplectic
case), giving the net Fricke-equivariant decomposition
\[
 W_p\;=\;W_p^{w_8\text{-fixed}}\oplus
 \bigl(W_p^{p^8} \oplus W_p^{p^9}\bigr)^{w_8\text{-swap}}.
\]
\emph{Mukai-grading universality.} The Wilson-line trace
\eqref{eq:gl-kazhdan-wilson-trace} is a scalar, not a matrix, on
each Mukai-graded piece $(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})_v$; this is the
C1 universality of Vol~I
\cite{VolI} translated to
the spectral side. The universality is a non-trivial prediction: generic
Wilson lines on $\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$ would act non-trivially across Mukai
weight sectors; the Saito--Kurokawa structure \emph{forces} the action
through the scalar central character, which is the geometric
manifestation of the Arthur CAP.
Primary: Arthur 2013 \emph{Endoscopic classification}, Thm.~1.5.2;
Andrianov 1974 \S 2--3; Vol~I
\cite{VolI};
Arinkin--Gaitsgory 2015 Conj.~1.1.5 (categorical trace compatibility
with Fricke self-duality).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Mukai-graded Chriss--Ginzburg
convolution and Beilinson--Drinfeld factorisation compatibility]
\label{rem:gl-kazhdan-CG-convolution}
\index{Kazhdan!Chriss--Ginzburg convolution}
\index{Beilinson--Drinfeld factorisation!Mukai-graded Hecke}
The Mukai-graded Hecke eigenvalue of Vol~I
(the $C_1$-eigenvalue identity for the Kazhdan Wilson trace) is the \emph{categorical trace}
of the Hecke correspondence
$\mathrm{Hecke}_p\subset\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)\times\mathrm{Bun}_G(X)$
on the Mukai-graded factorisation algebra $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$; the
Chriss--Ginzburg convolution formula computes this trace as
\[
 T_p \star v
 \;=\;
 \pi_{2,*}\bigl(\pi_1^*v \cdot [\mathrm{Hecke}_p]\bigr)
 \;=\;
 \lambda_p(v)\cdot v,
\]
where $\pi_1, \pi_2$ are the two projections and $[\mathrm{Hecke}_p]$
is the fundamental class of the $p$-th Hecke correspondence. The scalar
$\lambda_p(v) = a_p(\Delta_{E_6}) + p^8 + p^9$ reads off the
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$-equivariant structure of the Hecke stack:
the three Euler-product factors decompose $[\mathrm{Hecke}_p]$ into the
elliptic-standard factor and two CAP factors, each realising a different
character of the Mukai torus, but with \emph{identical weights}~$0$ on
every $v \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ (because each is Mukai-invariant,
by the centrality established in Vol~I C1).

\emph{Beilinson--Drinfeld compatibility.} The BD chiral factorisation
of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ over $\mathrm{Ran}(X)$ transports the
Mukai-graded Hecke action compatibly across the Ran insertion structure:
the two-point factorisation morphism
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5,x_1}\otimes\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5,x_2}\to
\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5,(x_1,x_2)}$
(with $x_i\in X$) intertwines $T_p$ on each side, giving
$\lambda_p(v_1+v_2) = \lambda_p(v_1) = \lambda_p(v_2)$ by C1
universality; a finite-dimensional consistency check that
$\lambda_p$ is \emph{constant} on each Mukai-torus orbit, never just
equivariant. Primary: Chriss--Ginzburg 1997 \emph{Representation theory
and complex geometry}, \S 2.7 (convolution formula);
Beilinson--Drinfeld 2004 \emph{Chiral algebras}, \S 3.4
(factorisation compatibility of Hecke correspondences);
Mirkovi\'c--Vilonen 2007 \emph{Ann.~Math.}~166 (geometric Satake
as categorical trace).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[genus-\texorpdfstring{$2$}{2} Siegel Wilson line at the
\texorpdfstring{$\mu_8$}{mu-8}-gerbe-twisted Fricke-fixed locus]
\label{rem:gl-kazhdan-genus2-siegel-wilson}
\index{Kazhdan!Siegel Wilson line}
\index{genus-$2$!Siegel upper half space!Fricke-fixed locus}
\index{$\mu_8$-gerbe!twisted Tannakian fibre}
At the $\zeta_8$-specialisation of $\mathrm{Rep}(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$
(Vol~I the derived-Langlands Fricke self-duality remark (Vol~I, \cite{VolI})), the
Fricke-fixed locus inside $\mathcal{A}_2$ is the $w_8$-fixed divisor
$\mathcal{A}_2^{w_8}\subset\mathcal{A}_2$, cut out by
$\Delta_5|_{\mathcal{A}_2^{w_8}} = \Delta_5$ (the Fricke-symmetric part
of the cusp form). On this locus, the Siegel Wilson line
$W_p^{\mathrm{Siegel}}$ at the $\mu_8$-gerbe-twisted Tannakian fibre
computes the tempered spinor eigenvalue directly:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gl-kazhdan-siegel-wilson-trace}
 \bigl\langle
 W_p^{\mathrm{Siegel}}
 \bigr\rangle_{(\mathcal{A}_2^{w_8},\,\mu_8\text{-gerbe})}
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{spin}(\Delta_{10})}\bigl(
 \mathrm{Frob}_p
 \bigr)
 \;=\;
 \lambda_p(v),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Frob}_p\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$ is
the Frobenius at $p$ and $\mathrm{spin}(\Delta_{10})$ is the $4$-dim
spinor Galois representation of $\Delta_{10}$ (Weissauer 2005;
Mok 2015). This gives the geometric content of the universal
eigenvalue: \emph{every} Mukai-graded $T_p$-eigenvalue on
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ is a \emph{Frobenius trace}
(T17 arithmetic sibling, Vol~I concordance: the character
$a_p = \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p\,|\,H^\bullet_\ell)$ on
$\ell$-adic cohomology, i.e.\ T17~(i) applied to the Galois
representation; never the chiral trace T17~(viii) on
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ itself, nor the categorical trace
T17~(ii)) on the same $4$-dim
Galois representation $\mathrm{spin}(\Delta_{10})$, regardless of the
Mukai weight $v$. The uniqueness of the spinor Galois representation
(Weissauer~2005 Thm.~B; Mok~2015 Thm.~5.1) verifies the C1 universality
from the Galois side. Primary: Weissauer 2005 \emph{Lect.~Notes
Math.}~1968, Thm.~B on spinor $\mathrm{GSp}(4)$ Galois representations;
Mok 2015 \emph{Mem.~AMS}~235, Thm.~5.1 on quasi-split unitary-group
Arthur packets; Arthur 2013 \S 1.5 (Arthur parameter); Vol~I
Theorem~\cite{VolI} and
Vol~I~\S\cite{VolI}.
\end{remark}
