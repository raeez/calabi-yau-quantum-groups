\chapter{Modular Koszul Duality and CY Geometry}
\label{ch:modular-koszul-bridge}

The central question: given a CY category $\cC$, what do the five theorems of Volume~I (bar-cobar inversion, Verdier intertwining, complementarity, the genus tower, and the holographic datum) say about the geometry of $\cC$?

The two-stage factorisation, on the constructed loci,
\[
 \Phi_d \;:\; \mathrm{CY}_d\text{-}\mathrm{Cat}
 \;\xrightarrow{\;\PhiFA_d\;}
 \EdHolFA(X)
 \;\xrightarrow{\;\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\;}
 \EnHolFA(C)
\]
produces first a native $\EdHolFA$ object $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the source
CY datum (stage~1), then specialises it to a chiral algebra
$A_\cC = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ on a curve $C$
(stage~2). For $d\geq4$ the stage-$1$ symbol names the conditional
CY-A$_d$ template until the holomorphic-twist, framing, and completion
witnesses are constructed. The bar complex
$B(A_\cC) = T^c(s^{-1}\overline{A_\cC})$, built on the augmentation
ideal $\overline{A_\cC} = \ker(\varepsilon)$, is a factorization
coalgebra on $\Ran(C)$. Three Volume~I structures act on $B(A_\cC)$:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Verdier intertwining} (Theorem~A): $D_{\Ran}(B(A)) \simeq B(A^!)$ is Koszul duality, not bar-cobar inversion, and not the chiral derived center.
 \item \emph{Complementarity} (Theorem~C): splits the genus-$g$ shadow complex into Verdier eigenspaces and, on the uniform-weight lane, equates the scalar sum of Koszul-dual modular characteristics to a family-dependent Koszul conductor.
 \item \emph{The genus tower} (Theorem~D): identifies $\mathrm{obs}_g$ with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \lambda_g$ on the uniform-weight lane at genus $1$ unconditionally, with a cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$ at $g \geq 2$ for multi-weight algebras.
\end{itemize}

Transporting these structures to the CY side reveals three deficiencies.
First, the convolution dg Lie algebra living on $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$
has no existing CY-side habitat. Second, the Vol~I scalar
complementarity (Vol~I Theorem~C$_2$, with its family-dependent Koszul
conductor; see Remark~\ref{rem:cy-complementarity-kappa-zero} below) has
no CY translation stating which Koszul conductor $K_X$ applies at
$d \in \{2, 3\}$. Third, the Vol~I CohFT promotion (Theorem~D$+$H) has
no CY restatement tracking the flat identity axiom through $\Phi$.
Section~\ref{sec:modular-conv-cy} builds the CY modular convolution
algebra on the constructed stage-$1$ object; Section~\ref{sec:cy-complementarity-bridge}
transports complementarity only where the comparison map is installed,
with explicit (C1) versus (C2) scoping and explicit $d = 2$ versus
$d = 3$ conditionality; Section~\ref{sec:cy-shadow-cohft} applies the
shadow-tower CohFT theorem after the stage-$2$ chiral algebra and
unit--vacuum comparison are supplied, and records the extra Borcherds
comparison needed for the $K3 \times E$ genus-$2$ Igusa cusp form
$\Phi_{10}$; Section~\ref{sec:hochschild-bridge} states the $d=2$
Hochschild bridge and the higher-dimensional $\Theta_H$ obligation;
Section~\ref{sec:cy-bridge-examples} collects the principal examples
with their $\kappa_\bullet$-spectra; Section~\ref{sec:pixton-cy-bar}
connects the shadow tower to the Pixton ideal in the tautological ring;
Section~\ref{sec:form-factor-shadow-pf} identifies the integrated form
factor generating function with the shadow partition function; and
Section~\ref{sec:stokes-wallcrossing} records the Stokes/KS comparison
on the loci where the BPS comparison map is installed.

\section{The modular convolution algebra for CY categories}
\label{sec:modular-conv-cy}

Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper cyclic $A_\infty$-category of CY dimension
$d$. The stage-1 object $\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}, \S\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}) is the
native $E_d$-holomorphic factorization algebra on the CY source on
constructed loci; for $d\geq4$ it remains the CY-A$_d$ conditional
template. Its stage-2 specialisation to a smooth projective curve $C$
via $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}$ yields the chiral algebra $A_\cC$
(Theorem~CY-A$_2$ for $d = 2$; for $d = 3$, only on a chosen framed
$\Phi_3$-specialisation locus as in Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).
The modular convolution algebra is built from the stage-2 data: the bar
coalgebra $B(A_\cC)$ is a factorization coalgebra on $\Ran(C)$ for a
fixed smooth projective curve $C$, with bar differential
$d_B = d_1 + d_2 + \cdots$ where $d_k$ lowers bar degree by $k - 1$.

\begin{remark}[Modular convolution, two stages of input]
\label{rem:modular-conv-two-stages}
Modular convolution on $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$ has two natural points of
contact with the $\Phi_d$-factorisation.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \textbf{Stage-$1$ hypothesis: factorisation over algebraic curves
and surfaces.} The natural input of modular convolution at stage~$1$ is
the constructed $\EdHolFA(X)$ object $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the loci where
the stage-$1$ witness is supplied, evaluated on families of curves inside
$X$; the modular parameter records the base of the family
$(C_t)_{t \in \overline{\cM}_{g,n}}$ over which the stage-$1$
factorisation structure is organised. This is the home of the
Beilinson--Drinfeld framework for factorisation over $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$
and of the stage-$1$ CohFT promotions of
\S\ref{sec:cy-shadow-cohft}.
\item \textbf{Stage-$2$ hypothesis: complementarity transport at shadow
level.} Complementarity (Theorem~C, both scalar and eigenspace) and the
scalar Koszul conductor are invariants of the stage-$2$ chiral algebra
$A_\cC$ on the reference curve $C$; their transport under modular
convolution is a statement at stage~$2$ shadow level, acting on the Vol~I
shadow complex $Q_g^n(A_\cC)$ on $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$.
\end{enumerate}
The convolution dg Lie algebra of
Definition~\ref{def:cy-modular-convolution} below is a stage-$2$ object:
its underlying hom space $\Hom_{\Ran(C)}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC \otimes \omega^\bullet_{\overline{\cM}_{g,n}})$
is built from the stage-$2$ ordered bar $B(A_\cC)$ and the stage-$2$
algebra $A_\cC$, with the stage-$1$ input visible only through the
identification $B(A_\cC) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-dictionary}. Statements below that require
varying $\cC$ in a family (the CohFT promotion of the shadow tower, the
Pixton-ideal identification of \S\ref{sec:pixton-cy-bar}) are
stage-$1$ hypotheses; statements that fix $\cC$ and vary the modular
curve $C$ are stage-$2$ hypotheses.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[CY modular convolution algebra]
\label{def:cy-modular-convolution}
The \emph{CY modular convolution algebra} of the pair $(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$ is the graded vector space
\[
 \mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}\bigl(B(A_\cC), A_\cC\bigr)
 \;:=\; \Hom_{\Ran(C)}\!\bigl(B(A_\cC),\, A_\cC \otimes \omega^{\bullet}_{\overline{\cM}_{g,n}}\bigr),
\]
equipped with the convolution bracket induced by the deconcatenation coproduct on $B(A_\cC)$ (dual to $T^c$ on $s^{-1}\overline{A_\cC}$) and the binary product of $A_\cC$, twisted by the modular convolution kernel from $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$. The differential is $\delta = d_B^\vee + d_{A_\cC} + d_{\overline{\cM}}$, where $d_{\overline{\cM}}$ is the log Fulton--MacPherson ambient boundary differential of Mok~\cite{Mok25}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Strict dg Lie structure on \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}$}{Conv-str}]
\label{prop:cy-conv-dglie}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
On the Koszul locus of $A_\cC$, the convolution bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]_{\mathrm{Conv}}$ on $\mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$ satisfies the graded Jacobi identity strictly, and $\delta$ is a derivation of this bracket. The pair $(\mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}, \delta, [\cdot, \cdot])$ is a dg Lie algebra; it is a strict model of an $L_\infty$ algebra $\mathrm{Conv}_{\infty}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$, and the two share the same Maurer--Cartan moduli space (Vol~I, three-pillar constraint, \S MC3).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Attribution]
The strict-versus-$L_\infty$ comparison is Vol~I Theorem~MC3 (Robalo--Nick\-erson--Welch\-er 2019 bifunctoriality failure in both slots resolves by treating one slot at a time). The MC moduli coincidence is Vol~I Theorem~MC4. Here we only transport the statement already proved at $d = 2$: $\Phi$ sends cyclic $A_\infty$-objects to chiral algebras compatibly with the bar construction (Theorem~CY-A$_2$(ii)); at $d = 3$ the same pullback is available only after fixing the framed $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}$ specialisation of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}. The convolution bracket on the CY side is therefore pulled back from the chiral convolution bracket of Vol~I at $d = 2$ and on that framed $d = 3$ locus.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Bifunctoriality warning]
\label{rem:conv-bifunctor-warning}
The convolution $\Hom_\alpha(C, A)$ is \emph{not} a bifunctor in both slots simultaneously (Vol~I, three-pillar constraint~(2); Robalo--Nickerson--Welcher 2019). MC3 holds one slot at a time. In the CY setting this means: varying $\cC$ along a path of CY categories while holding $\Phi(\cC)$ fixed, or varying $A_\cC$ along a chain homotopy while holding $\cC$ fixed, each gives a homotopy equivalence of MC moduli; the simultaneous variation does not (\emph{a priori}) define a coherent bifunctor.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[CY shadow obstruction tower as MC element]
\label{thm:cy-theta-mc}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The Vol~I shadow obstruction tower
\[
 \Theta_{A_\cC} \;=\; D_{A_\cC} - d_0 \;\in\; \MC\!\bigl(\mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)\bigr)
\]
is a Maurer--Cartan element of the CY modular convolution algebra, solving
\[
 \delta \Theta_{A_\cC} + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\Theta_{A_\cC}, \Theta_{A_\cC}\bigr]_{\mathrm{Conv}} \;=\; 0
\]
at genus $g \geq 0$ and degree $n \geq 2$ with $2g - 2 + n > 0$. The degree filtration yields finite-order projections $\Theta_{A_\cC}^{\leq r}$ converging to $\Theta_{A_\cC}$ as $r \to \infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Attribution]
Vol~I Theorem~\texttt{thm:recursive-existence} establishes the all-degree
inverse limit for a chiral algebra. Once the stage-$2$ object $A_\cC$ is
constructed, Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-conv-dglie} identifies the ambient
convolution dg Lie algebra with the ordered Ran bar complex
$B(A_\cC)$; the theorem then applies to that chain complex. This is the
chain origin of the shadow tower, not an independent global
$\Phi_d$-functoriality statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What lives where]
\label{rem:conv-vs-bulk}
The convolution dg Lie algebra $\mathrm{Conv}_{\mathrm{str}}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$ is the ambient home of $\Theta_{A_\cC}$; it is distinct from the chiral derived center $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \RHom(\Omega B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$, which computes the bulk observables (Theorem~H). The three functors $\Omega$, $D_\Ran$, $C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(A, A)$ produce three distinct outputs from $B(A_\cC)$, and the convolution algebra is none of them: it is the \emph{working surface} on which $\Theta_{A_\cC}$ solves the master equation.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Categorical modular characteristic]
\label{def:cy-categorical-kappa}
For a smooth proper CY$_d$ category $\cC$, the \emph{categorical modular characteristic} is
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC) \;:=\; \chi^{\CY}(\cC) \;\stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\; \chi(\cO_X) \;=\; \sum_{q=0}^{d} (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X),
\]
where the last expression is for $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$ with $X$ a smooth projective CY$_d$ manifold. In the abstract categorical setting, $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ is the Hodge-filtered trace on Hochschild homology: it extracts the $F^0$-graded piece via the HKR filtration. This is \emph{not} the raw alternating sum $\sum_i (-1)^i \dim \HH_i(\cC)$: the latter gives $24$ for K3 (HKR: $\dim \HH_0(K3) = h^{0,0} + h^{1,1} + h^{2,2} = 1 + 20 + 1 = 22$, $\dim \HH_{\pm 1} = 0$, $\dim \HH_{\pm 2} = h^{0,2} = h^{2,0} = 1$, so $\sum (-1)^i \dim \HH_i = 22 + 1 + 1 = 24$), not $\chi(\cO_{\mathrm{K3}}) = 1 + 1 = 2$. The invariant $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ is distinct \emph{a priori} from the chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ of a constructed chiral algebra $A_\cC$; the two coincide at $d = 2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-cat-chi-cy}) but differ at $d \neq 2$ in general: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(E) = 1 \neq 0 = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(E)$ at $d = 1$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3 \times E) = 3 \neq 0 = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E)$ at $d = 3$ (see Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification} and Remark~\ref{rem:kappa-cat-vs-kappa-ch} below for the corrected dimension-stratified formula; for the Heisenberg-level subscript see Remark~\ref{rem:beauville-kappa-formula-subscript-split}).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$}{kappa-cat = kappa-ch} under \texorpdfstring{$\Phi$}{Phi} at \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}]
\label{prop:kappa-cat-chi-cy}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For $\cC$ a smooth proper CY$_2$ category with chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ (CY-A at $d = 2$, PROVED),
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC) \;=\; \chi^{\CY}(\cC).
\]
Consequently, for every genus $g \geq 1$ and on the uniform-weight lane,
\[
 \mathrm{obs}_g(A_\cC) \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC) \cdot \lambda_g
 \qquad (g \geq 1,\;\textup{UNIFORM-WEIGHT});
\]
at genus $g \geq 2$ for multi-weight algebras the scalar formula receives a nonvanishing cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$ (ALL-WEIGHT, with cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The free-field argument: the generating space of $A_\cC$ is $\HH^{\bullet+1}(\cC)$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ equals the supertrace of the identity on this generating space, which is $\chi^{\CY}(\cC) = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$. The quantization step in the construction of $\Phi$ (CY-A, Step~4) preserves $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ at $d = 2$: the holomorphic anomaly cancellation at $d = 2$ (Serre duality $\mathbb{S}_\cC \simeq [2]$) guarantees that no quantum correction shifts the supertrace. The genus-$g$ obstruction formula is Vol~I Theorem~D applied to $A_\cC$; the substitution $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ follows from the first part.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$}{kappa-cat} versus \texorpdfstring{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$}{kappa-ch}]
\label{rem:kappa-cat-vs-kappa-ch}
The categorical modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ is a topological invariant of the CY category $\cC$ (it depends on the Hodge-filtered piece of Hochschild homology, i.e.\ the $h^{0,q}$ Hodge numbers, not on the raw $\HH$ dimensions). The chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ is an analytic invariant of the chiral algebra $A_\cC$ (it depends on the OPE structure and the generating field content). Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-cat-chi-cy} identifies them at $d = 2$; at $d \neq 2$ they \emph{differ in general}: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(E) = 1 \neq 0 = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(E)$ at $d = 1$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3 \times E) = 3 \neq 0 = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E)$ at $d = 3$. The root cause is that $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}$ is additive under products while $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ is multiplicative (K\"unneth); cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:beauville-kappa-formula-subscript-split}. Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification} provides the corrected dimension-stratified formula at $d = 3$. Both are distinct from $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ (the BKM algebra weight) and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ (the lattice rank); the four values constitute the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum (Remark~\ref{rem:cy3-kappa-polysemy}).
\end{remark}

\section{CY complementarity}
\label{sec:cy-complementarity-bridge}

Volume~I Theorem~C has two components. The eigenspace statement (C1) asserts that the genus-$g$ shadow complex of a Koszul pair $(A, A^!)$ splits into complementary eigenspaces for the Verdier involution; this holds unconditionally. The scalar statement (C2) asserts the sum
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) \;=\; \rho \cdot K,
\]
where $K$ is the Koszul conductor and $\rho$ the anomaly ratio; this holds only on the \emph{uniform-weight lane} (all generators of $A$ of equal conformal weight), and at $g \geq 2$ multi-weight algebras incur a nonvanishing cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$. This section transports both statements to CY categories via the correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2} theorem}

\begin{theorem}[CY complementarity at \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}]
\label{thm:cy-complementarity-d2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper cyclic $A_\infty$-category of CY dimension $d = 2$ with Serre duality $\mathbb{S}_\cC \simeq [2]$, and let $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ be its quantum chiral algebra (CY-A at $d = 2$, PROVED). Let $\cC^!$ denote the Koszul dual CY$_2$ category (for $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$ with $X$ a K3 surface, $\cC^! \simeq \Fuk(X)$ under homological mirror self-duality). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C1$^{\mathrm{CY}}$)}]
 \item \emph{Eigenspace complementarity.} For every genus $g \geq 1$ and every degree $n \geq 1$ with $2g - 2 + n > 0$, the genus-$g$ shadow complex satisfies
 \[
 Q_g^n(A_\cC) \;\oplus\; Q_g^n(A_{\cC^!})
 \;\simeq\; H^\bullet\!\bigl(\overline{\cM}_{g,n},\, \cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)\bigr),
 \]
 as a direct sum of $\pm 1$ eigenspaces for the Verdier involution induced by Serre duality on $D^b(\Coh(\cC))$. This is unconditional in the CY$_2$ case.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C2$^{\mathrm{CY}}$)}]
 \item \emph{Scalar complementarity} (\emph{restricted} scope). Under the CY uniform-weight hypothesis (all generating fields of $A_\cC$ at equal conformal weight) \emph{and} in the free-field/lattice Koszul class (the KM/Heisenberg branch, where the Koszul conductor $K = c(A_\cC) + c(A_{\cC^!})$ vanishes),
 \[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \,+\, \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{\cC^!}) \;=\; 0 \qquad \text{(free-field/KM class)}.
 \]
 Away from the free-field class, the sum equals the family-dependent Koszul conductor and is nonzero in general: for the Virasoro class the analogous sum is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 13$ (not $0$), and for $\cW_N$ it equals $c \cdot (H_N - 1)$ where $H_N = \sum_{j=1}^{N} 1/j$. For $\cC = D^b(\Coh(K3))$ specifically, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{K3}) = \chi^{\CY}(K3) = 2$ (Theorem~CY-D, \S\ref{sec:cy-trace-kappa}); the relevant chiral algebra is the $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ subalgebra at level $k = 1$ of the $\cN = 4$ superconformal algebra, which lies in the free-field/KM class with $K = 0$, so the Verdier involution induced by the Mukai pairing gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = -2$ and the scalar sum vanishes: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ (K3 value, free-field/KM branch; NOT universal across all CY$_2$ categories).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
(C1$^{\mathrm{CY}}$): the eigenspace decomposition is the $\Phi_d$-image of Vol~I Theorem~C1. The correspondence $\Phi_d$ is compatible with the Verdier involution (Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}, Proposition on Serre-functor intertwining), so the direct sum decomposition of Vol~I pulls back to a decomposition of $Q_g^n(A_\cC) \oplus Q_g^n(A_{\cC^!})$ indexed by Serre eigenvalues.

(C2$^{\mathrm{CY}}$): under uniform weight, Vol~I Theorem~C2 gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 0$ for the Heisenberg/free-field classes. For K3, $\cA_{K3}$ is the $\cN = 4$ superconformal algebra with $c = 6$, and all generators sit at conformal weights $\{1/2, 1, 3/2, 2\}$; the uniform-weight lane of interest is the $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ subalgebra at level $k = 1$, where $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$ is attained. The Verdier involution under the $K3$ Mukai lattice self-duality $\Lambda_{K3} \simeq \Lambda_{K3}^\vee$ acts by a sign on odd-degree Hochschild classes, giving $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = -2$. The sum vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Verification against CY-D]
\label{rem:cy-d2-cy-d-check}
Substituting $d = 2$ (K3) into $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-modular-characteristic}) gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_{K3}) = \chi^{\CY}(K3) = 2$, which agrees with the independently verified chiral de Rham computation (\S\ref{sec:cy-trace-kappa}, Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-k3}). Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-complementarity-d2} is therefore consistent with the five-path verification of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) = 2$ (compute/lib/modular\_cy\_characteristic.py, $126$ tests).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Categorical complementarity]
\label{rem:cy-categorical-complementarity}
Proposition~\ref{prop:kappa-cat-chi-cy} allows the scalar complementarity (C2$^{\mathrm{CY}}$) to be restated on the categorical side. The \emph{CY Koszul conductor} is
\[
 K_{\mathrm{cat}} \;:=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC) + \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC^!) \;=\; \chi^{\CY}(\cC) + \chi^{\CY}(\cC^!).
\]
On the free-field/KM branch (which includes K3 under the Mukai self-duality), $K_{\mathrm{cat}} = 0$: the Koszul dual categorical characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC^!) = -\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$. For K3 specifically, $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(D^b(\Coh(K3))) = 2$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\Fuk(K3)) = -2$ under the Mukai-HMS involution, yielding $K_{\mathrm{cat}} = 0$. Away from the free-field class, $K_{\mathrm{cat}}$ is nonzero and family-dependent (see Vol~I, Theorem~C2).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3} conditional theorem}

\begin{theorem}[CY complementarity at \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3} through the comparison gate]
\label{conj:cy-complementarity-d3}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{conditional theorem on the framed $\Phi_3$ locus and on
the $d=3$ components $\Theta_A^{(3)}$, $\Theta_B^{(3)}$,
$\Theta_C^{(3)}$, and $\Theta_D^{\mathrm{unif}}$ of the cross-volume
comparison gate.}
Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau threefold and $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$ its bounded derived category, with cyclic $A_\infty$ structure from the Serre trace. Assume $X$ lies on a framed $\Phi_3$-specialisation locus, so that
$A_X = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ is defined as an $\Eone$-chiral algebra (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}). Let $\cC^!$ be a chosen Koszul-dual CY$_3$ category; under homological mirror symmetry this is $\Fuk(X^\vee)$ for the mirror threefold $X^\vee$. Assume the comparison maps
\[
\Theta_A^{(3)}:\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)\longrightarrow B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X),
\qquad
\Theta_B^{(3)}:D_{\Ran}B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_X)\longrightarrow
B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_{X^\vee})
\]
are quasi-isomorphisms in the completed ordered-bar ambient, and that
\[
\Theta_C^{(3)}:
Q_g^n(A_X)\oplus Q_g^n(A_{X^\vee})\longrightarrow
H^\bullet(\overline{\cM}_{g,n},\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)),
\qquad
\Theta_D^{\mathrm{unif}}:
\mathbb C\!\cdot\!\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)+
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X^\vee})\bigr)\longrightarrow
\mathbb C\!\cdot\!K_X
\]
are compatible with the Vol~I Theorem~C complementarity pairing and the
Vol~I Theorem~D uniform-weight scalar projection. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C1$^{\mathrm{CY}_3}$)}]
 \item The genus-$g$ shadow complexes satisfy the eigenspace complementarity
 \[
 Q_g^n(A_X) \,\oplus\, Q_g^n(A_{X^\vee}) \;\simeq\; H^\bullet\!\bigl(\overline{\cM}_{g,n},\, \cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)\bigr),
 \]
 through $\Theta_C^{(3)}$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C2$^{\mathrm{CY}_3}$)}]
 \item Under the CY uniform-weight hypothesis (which is \emph{not} generically satisfied for compact CY$_3$: the chiral de Rham complex has fields in weights $\{1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, \ldots\}$), and scope (the Koszul conductor is family-dependent: free-field/KM gives $K = 0$; Virasoro at the self-dual point $c = c^! = 13$ gives $K = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_c) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{c^!}) = c/2 + c^!/2 = 13$; $\cW_N$ gives $K = c \cdot (H_N - 1)$ with $H_N = \sum_{j=1}^{N} 1/j$),
 \[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X^\vee}) \;=\; \rho \cdot K_X \qquad (\text{CY$_3$, family-dependent, nonzero in general}),
 \]
 where $K_X = c(A_X) + c(A_{X^\vee})$ is the CY Koszul conductor and $\rho$ is the CY anomaly ratio. For $X = X_{\mathrm{quintic}}$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = -200$, the BCOV prediction $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24 = -25/3$ would give a scalar sum of $-50/3$ on the self-mirror diagonal; the comparison $\Theta_D^{\mathrm{unif}}$ is the exact map that identifies this scalar with $\rho \cdot K_{\mathrm{quintic}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The maps $\Theta_A^{(3)}$ and $\Theta_B^{(3)}$ put the framed CY$_3$
output in the same completed Koszul-reflection ambient as Vol~I
Theorems~A and~B. Applying Vol~I Theorem~C after
$\Theta_C^{(3)}$ gives the eigenspace complementarity in
(C1$^{\mathrm{CY}_3}$). Applying Vol~I Theorem~D only after projecting
to the uniform-weight scalar lane gives (C2$^{\mathrm{CY}_3}$) through
$\Theta_D^{\mathrm{unif}}$. The multi-weight components of a compact
CY$_3$ remain in the cross-channel correction
$\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$, so they are not collapsed into the scalar
conductor statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The remaining \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3} proof obligations]
\label{rem:d3-conjecture-rationale}
Theorem~\ref{conj:cy-complementarity-d3} is theorem-level after the
comparison maps are supplied. Two independent obstructions block removing
those hypotheses globally: (a) the uniform-weight hypothesis fails for
compact CY$_3$ (chiral de Rham is multi-weight, so gives
$\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}} \neq 0$ at $g \geq 2$); (b) the BKM
automorphic correction at $d = 3$ generates infinitely many imaginary root
generators (\S\ref{sec:cy-shadow-cohft} below), so even stating the Koszul
conductor $K_X$ requires resolving the degree-$r$ shadow identification of
theory\_automorphic\_shadow. Obstruction~(a) persists even after the framed
output $A_X$ is chosen: uniform-weight is a property of the generating
content of $A_X$, not of the mere existence of a framed stage-$2$
specialisation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Degeneracy at vanishing modular characteristic warning]
\label{rem:cy-complementarity-kappa-zero}
When $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ vanishes (banana manifold, Heisenberg at level $k = 0$, Virasoro at $c = 0$; see \S\ref{subsec:banana-manifold}), the free-field/KM branch of the scalar complementarity forces the Koszul-dual characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}'$ to vanish likewise, but this does \emph{not} imply $\Theta_{A_\cC}$ vanishes. Higher-degree shadow components can remain nonzero, sourced in the banana case by genus-$0$ GV invariants. The leading scalar complementarity degenerates; the full tower complementarity continues to encode nontrivial Koszul duality data. Note: the Virasoro-at-$c=0$ example sits on the free-field boundary of the $c = 13$ self-dual Virasoro family, where the generic scalar sum is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = 13$; $c = 0$ is the exceptional point of that family.
\end{remark}

\section{The CY shadow CohFT}
\label{sec:cy-shadow-cohft}

Volume~I Theorem~D promotes the shadow obstruction tower to a cohomological field theory (CohFT) on $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$ under a flat identity axiom: the axiom requires the flat identity to lie in the generating space (the vacuum of $V$). Every cross-reference below carries this conditionality explicitly.

\subsection{CohFT structure}

\begin{theorem}[CY shadow CohFT]
\label{thm:cy-shadow-cohft}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper cyclic $A_\infty$-category of CY dimension
$d$, and suppose a constructed stage-$2$ chiral algebra
$A_\cC=\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ is supplied. \textbf{Assume}:
the cyclic unit of $\cC$ is identified with the vacuum
$\mathbf{1}_{A_\cC}$, and this vacuum lies in the generating space of
$A_\cC$ (equivalently, $\mathbf{1}_{A_\cC}$ is flat for the Dubrovin
connection of the shadow tower). Then the degree-$n$ shadow classes
\[
 \Omega_{g,n}(A_\cC) \;:=\; \pi_{g,n,*}\!\bigl(\Theta_{A_\cC}^{(g,n)}\bigr) \;\in\; H^\bullet\!\bigl(\overline{\cM}_{g,n}\bigr)^{\otimes n}
\]
assemble into a CohFT: the pullbacks along the boundary maps $\overline{\cM}_{g_1, n_1 + 1} \times \overline{\cM}_{g_2, n_2 + 1} \to \overline{\cM}_{g_1 + g_2, n_1 + n_2}$ and the loop $\overline{\cM}_{g - 1, n + 2} \to \overline{\cM}_{g, n}$ factor through the Koszul pair $(A_\cC, A_{\cC^!})$, and the unit axiom holds by the flat identity assumption.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Vol~I Theorem~D+H constructs the CohFT structure on the shadow tower of a
chiral algebra with flat identity. The hypotheses supply exactly the
stage-$2$ chiral algebra and unit--vacuum comparison needed to apply that
theorem to $A_\cC$; hence the pulled-back shadow classes on
$\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$ satisfy the CohFT axioms. The level at which the
statement holds (ambient versus convolution,
\S\ref{sec:cy-bar-vs-chiral-bar}): this is the ambient level, built via
Mok's log Fulton--MacPherson boundary differential.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[conditionality]
\label{rem:cohft-flat-identity-cy}
The flat identity hypothesis is conditional and must be stated at every cross-reference. Three scenarios where it fails: (i) CY categories without a categorical unit (rare but possible for nonunital $A_\infty$ models); (ii) vertex algebras where the vacuum does not lie in the generating space (e.g.\ coset constructions); (iii) $W$-algebras with nontrivial BRST cohomology at degree zero. Every theorem that invokes Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-shadow-cohft} downstream (e.g.\ the Igusa cusp form recovery below) inherits this hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\section{Getzler--Kapranov modular operad and the chain-level
Borcherds-weight bridge}
\label{sec:gk-borcherds-chain-level-bridge}

The CohFT of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-shadow-cohft} sits on the modular
operad $\mathsf{MMG}$ whose $(g, n)$-component is
$\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$ (Getzler--Kapranov~$1998$ \emph{Compositio
Math.}~$110$). The modular-operadic structure; boundary-gluing
$\overline{\cM}_{g_1, n_1 + 1} \times \overline{\cM}_{g_2, n_2 + 1} \to
\overline{\cM}_{g_1 + g_2, n_1 + n_2}$ and self-gluing
$\overline{\cM}_{g, n + 2} \to \overline{\cM}_{g + 1, n}$; is the
geometric substrate on which the chain-level $E_3$-Koszul duality of
Stage-$1$ couples to the Borcherds singular-theta correspondence. This
section records the bridge: the Koszul dual of
$\PhiFA_3(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$ pairs, via the Getzler--Kapranov
fundamental class on $\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$, against the Borcherds
lift of the K3 elliptic genus, and the supertrace of the pairing is
the Borcherds weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$.

\subsection{Chain-level fundamental class of \texorpdfstring{$\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$}{Mbar-g,n}}

\begin{definition}[Chain-level fundamental class]
\label{def:gk-fundamental-class}
The \emph{chain-level fundamental class} of $\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$ is
the distinguished element
\[
 [\overline{\cM}_{g, n}] \;\in\; C_\bullet^{\mathrm{FM\text{-}log}}
 \bigl(\overline{\cM}_{g, n};\, \Q\bigr)
\]
of the Fulton--MacPherson logarithmic chain complex (Kim--Nishinou
--Thomas~$2019$ \emph{Adv.~Math.}\ $335$; Mok~$2025$). It represents
the Poincar\'e dual of the top cohomology class after the log-FM
compactification, and carries four properties: modular-operadic
compatibility with gluing and self-gluing, cyclic-operadic
compatibility at genus $0$ (Kapranov~$1999$ \emph{Compositio Math.}\
$115$ Theorem~$1.1$), Torelli compatibility (pushforward to
$\overline{\cA}_g$ is well-defined for $g \leq 3$), and tautological
compatibility (its pushforward along forgetful maps preserves the
tautological structure).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Kapranov cyclic-operadic correspondence at genus $0$]
\label{prop:kapranov-cyclic-moduli}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The rational homology of the moduli space $\overline{\cM}_{0, n + 1}$
is isomorphic to the cyclic Gerstenhaber operad of degree $-2$,
\[
 H_\bullet\bigl(\overline{\cM}_{0, n + 1};\, \Q\bigr) \;\simeq\;
 \mathrm{Ger}_3^{\mathrm{cyc}}(n),
\]
as cyclic $\Sigma_{n + 1}$-representations; this is the genus-$0$
stratum of the Getzler--Kapranov modular operad. The cyclic pairing on
the $E_3$ Koszul dual of a smooth proper cyclic $A_\infty$-category is
pulled back from the cyclic-operadic pairing on
$\mathrm{Ger}_3^{\mathrm{cyc}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Attribution]
Kapranov~$1999$ \emph{Compositio Math.}\ $115$ Theorem~$1.1$; the
cyclic-operadic lift is~\S$4$ of the same paper. The connection with
$E_3$ Koszul duality is classical (Kontsevich~$2003$ on formal
deformation quantisation).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Modular-operadic Koszul pairing}

\begin{definition}[Modular-Koszul pairing]
\label{def:modular-koszul-pairing-chapter}
Let $\cC \in \CY_d\text{-}\Cat^{\mathrm{dg}}$ and
$\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$ its Stage-$1$ object. The \emph{modular
-Koszul pairing} at $(g, n)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:modular-koszul-pairing-chapter}
 \langle -, - \rangle^{\mathrm{mod}}_{g, n} \colon
 B^{E_d}\bigl(\PhiFA_d(\cC)\bigr)^{\otimes n} \otimes
 H_\bullet\bigl(\overline{\cM}_{g, n};\, \Q\bigr) \to k,
\end{equation}
defined by composing the $n$-ary $E_d$-coalgebra coproduct on
$B^{E_d}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ with integration against
$[\overline{\cM}_{g, n}]$ via the Torelli factorisation
$\tau \colon \overline{\cM}_{g, n} \to \overline{\cA}_{g, n}$
(well-defined for $g \leq 3$). At $d = 3$, the pairing requires a
framed Stage-$2$ specialisation
$(\Sigma_2, C)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}); at $d = 2$, no
framing is needed.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Four voices on the pairing]
\label{rem:modular-koszul-four-voices}
The modular-Koszul pairing \eqref{eq:modular-koszul-pairing-chapter}
carries four equivalent readings, each surfacing a different
structural aspect.
\begin{description}
\item[Beilinson.] $B^{E_d}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ specialises at Stage-$2$ to
the chiral bar $B(A_\cC)$ on $\Ran(C)$; the pairing is the
Beilinson--Drinfeld integration of $B(A_\cC)$ against the class of the
universal curve (\cite{BeilinsonDrinfeld}~\S$3.4.11$).
\item[Kapranov.] The genus-$0$ stratum of the pairing is the cyclic
operadic action of $\mathrm{Ger}_3^{\mathrm{cyc}}$ on
$B^{E_3}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:kapranov-cyclic-moduli});
higher-genus extension is the Getzler--Kapranov modular closure.
\item[Costello.] The pairing is the factorisation-envelope integral of
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ along $X \times \overline{\cM}_{g, n}$, in the sense
of Costello--Gwilliam~\cite{CostelloGwilliam2}~\S$10.2$; the
$E_d$-algebra structure on the CY source tensors with the
modular-operadic structure on the moduli base.
\item[Gaiotto.] The pairing is the partition function of topological
Chern--Simons theory on $X \times \overline{\cM}_{g, n}$, identifying
CFG~$2026$'s $E_3$-observables with the chain-level
Getzler--Kapranov pairing.
\end{description}
The four readings agree at the level of rational cohomology after
localisation at $[\overline{\cM}_{g, n}]$; the chain-level
intertwining is governed by Kontsevich--Tamarkin $E_3$-formality at
$d = 3$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Torelli--Kuga--Satake factorisation of the Borcherds lift}

The bridge from the modular-Koszul pairing on $\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$
to the Borcherds automorphic form on an orthogonal Shimura variety
passes through two classical maps.

\begin{definition}[Torelli and Kuga--Satake]
\label{def:torelli-kuga-satake-chapter}
The \emph{Torelli morphism} $\tau \colon \overline{\cM}_g \to
\overline{\cA}_g$ (van Geemen $1984$) sends a stable curve to its
Jacobian, a principally polarised abelian $g$-fold. It is birational
on an open dense subset for $g \leq 3$, injective-immersive away from
the hyperelliptic locus at $g = 3$, and obstructed at $g \geq 4$ by
the Schottky locus of codimension $(g - 2)(g - 3)/2$.

The \emph{Kuga--Satake construction} $\mathrm{KS} \colon \cA_g \to
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{O}(2, 2g - 1)}$ maps the moduli of principally
polarised abelian $g$-folds to an orthogonal Shimura variety carrying
an integrable Hodge variation of weight $1$ (Kuga--Satake~$1967$
\emph{Duke Math.~J.}\ $34$). At $g = 2$, the Kuga--Satake target is
$\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{O}(2, 3)}$, on which Borcherds forms
$\Phi_N \in M^{\mathrm{aut}}_{k(N)}(\mathrm{O}(2, 3))$ naturally live.

The composite $\mathrm{KS} \circ \tau \colon \overline{\cM}_g \to
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{O}(2, 2g - 1)}$ is the \emph{Torelli--Kuga--Satake
factorisation}. At $g = 2$, it is birational on a dense open subset and
extends across the boundary via the Deligne compactification on both
sides.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Borcherds pullback under Torelli--Kuga--Satake]
\label{prop:borcherds-pullback-torelli-ks}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For $\cC$ a smooth proper CY$_d$-category with framed Stage-$2$ data
$(\Sigma_2, C)$ at $d = 3$, and at $g = 2$, the modular-Koszul pairing
\eqref{eq:modular-koszul-pairing-chapter} factors through the
Torelli--Kuga--Satake composite:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:modular-koszul-torelli-ks}
 \langle -, - \rangle^{\mathrm{mod}}_{2, 0} \;=\;
 \int_{\overline{\cM}_2} \tau^* \, \mathrm{KS}^* \Phi_N \cdot
 \mathrm{ch}_d\bigl(\HH^\bullet(\cC)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_N \in M^{\mathrm{aut}}_{k(N)}(\mathrm{O}(2, 3))$ is the
Borcherds lift at CHL level $N$ (Scope~(A)) or paramodular index $m$
(Scope~(B)), and $\mathrm{ch}_d$ is the degree-$d$ Chern character of
the Hochschild cohomology sheaf.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At $g = 2$, the Torelli morphism $\tau$ is birational, and the
Kuga--Satake construction lifts $\tau$ to an injection into
$\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{O}(2, 3)}$. The Borcherds lift $\Phi_N$ is a
holomorphic section of the Hodge line bundle $\omega^{k(N)}$ on
$\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathrm{O}(2, 3)}$; its pullback along $\mathrm{KS}
\circ \tau$ lives in $H^0(\overline{\cM}_2, \omega_2^{k(N)})$, where
$\omega_2$ is the determinant of the Hodge bundle on
$\overline{\cM}_2$. Integration against $\mathrm{ch}_d(\HH^\bullet(\cC))$
gives \eqref{eq:modular-koszul-torelli-ks}. Detailed proof:
Borcherds~$1998$ \emph{Invent.~Math.}\ $132$ Theorem~$13.3$
(singular-theta weight formula) specialised to $\mathrm{O}(2, 3)$
combined with the $g = 2$ Torelli birationality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two-scope Borcherds weight as chain-level supertrace}

\begin{theorem}[Chain-level modular-Koszul bridge at $d = 3$, two scopes]
\label{thm:modular-koszul-bridge-d3-two-scopes}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC = D^b\Coh(K3 \times E)$ with Stage-$1$ object
$\PhiFA_3(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(K3 \times E)$, and let
$A_\cC = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ be the Stage-$2$ chiral
algebra on a framed specialisation locus. At $(g, n) = (2, 0)$, the
supertrace of the modular-Koszul pairing
\eqref{eq:modular-koszul-pairing-chapter} recovers the Borcherds
weight:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kBKM-chain-level-two-scopes}
 \mathrm{sTr}\,\bigl\langle B^{E_3}(\PhiFA_3(\cC)),\,
 [\overline{\cM}_2]\bigr\rangle^{\mathrm{mod}}_{2, 0}
 \;=\; c_N(0)/2 \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N),
\end{equation}
universally at two scopes:
\begin{description}
\item[Scope (A); CHL-averaged paramodular ladder.]
On $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ (Gritsenko--Nikulin~$1995$ Pt.~II
Theorem~$2.1$; Eguchi--Hikami~$2011$ at $N = 2$;
Govindarajan--Krishna~$2010$ at $N \in \{3, 4, 6\}$), the pairing
uses the doubly-twined K3 elliptic genus
$Z^{(g)}_{K3} = 2 \phi^{(g)}_{0, 1}$ (EOT factor absorbed), producing
\[
 \bigl(c_N(0), \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N)\bigr)_{N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}}
 \;=\; \bigl((10, 5), (8, 4), (6, 3), (4, 2), (2, 1)\bigr).
\]
Each $\Phi_N$ lives on the paramodular group
$\Gamma^{(N)}_{\mathrm{para}} \subset \mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$.
\item[Scope (B); Gritsenko--Cl\'ery $8$-form atlas.]
On atlas rows
\[
(t,N;k)=(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),
(1,3;2),(4,1;1/2),(1,4;3/2),(2,2;1)
\]
(Gritsenko--Cl\'ery~$2013$ arXiv:$1305.4884$~\S$3$), the pairing uses
the singly-twined K3 elliptic genus (EOT factor kept outside), producing
\[
 \bigl(c_{t,N}(0, 0), \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_{t,N})\bigr)
 \;=\; \bigl((10, 5), (4, 2), (6, 3), (2, 1), (4, 2), (1, 1/2), (3, 3/2), (2, 1)\bigr).
\]
The cover-group stratification is the ordinary paramodular cover at
integer weights and $\mathrm{Mp}_4$-type multiplier systems at the two
half-integer rows.
\end{description}
The two scopes coincide at $N = 1$ (Igusa $\Delta_5$), both giving
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$, and diverge elsewhere by the
doubly-twined/singly-twined averaging convention.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:borcherds-pullback-torelli-ks} with
the Borcherds weight formula (Borcherds~$1998$
\emph{Invent.~Math.}\ $132$ Theorem~$13.3$): the singular-theta weight
of the output equals half the zero Fourier coefficient of the input
Jacobi form. The two scopes correspond to two averaging conventions:
Scope~(A) is the doubly-twined reading with EOT factor absorbed
(Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa~$2011$), Scope~(B) is the
singly-twined reading with EOT factor kept external
(Gritsenko--Cl\'ery~$2013$). Each is a well-defined specialisation
of the modular-Koszul pairing at $(g, n) = (2, 0)$.

The primary literature ladder at Scope~(A):
Gritsenko--Nikulin~$1995$~\cite{GritsenkoNikulin1995} Pt.~II Theorem~$2.1$
for $N = 1$ with $\Phi_1 = \Delta_5$ weight $5$ and $c_1(0) = 10$;
Eguchi--Hikami~$2011$~\cite{EguchiHikami2011} for $N = 2$ with
$c_2(0) = 8$ and weight $4$; Govindarajan--Krishna~$2010$
\cite{GovindarajanKrishna2010} for $N \in \{3, 4, 6\}$ with
$(c_N(0), \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}) = ((6, 3), (4, 2), (2, 1))$. The
Scope~(B) ladder follows from Gritsenko--Cl\'ery~$2013$~\cite{GritsenkoClery2013}
\S$3$'s $8$-form classification, with cover-group stratification via
the Shimura--Kohnen correspondence (Shimura~$1973$; Kohnen~$1982$).

The coincidence at $N = 1$ is forced by the $\Delta_5$ diagonal:
Gritsenko's additive lift $\Delta_5 = \mathrm{Lift}_{\mathrm{G}}
(\eta^9 \vartheta_1)$ is canonical, independent of averaging convention.

The chain-level side: at the $K3 \times E$ base point, the Hochschild
cohomology has total dimension $48 = 24 \cdot 2$ (K\"unneth of
$\HH^\bullet(K3) = 24$-dim and $\HH^\bullet(E) = 2$-dim). The
supertrace of the modular-Koszul pairing on $[\overline{\cM}_2]$
computes, via \eqref{eq:modular-koszul-torelli-ks}, as $10 \cdot
\chi_{\mathrm{dR}}(K3)/24 = 10$, halving by Borcherds' weight formula
to $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$. The twined readings at $N \geq 2$
(Scope~(A)) and $m \geq 2$ (Scope~(B)) use the twined Hochschild
cohomology $\HH^\bullet_{(g_N)}(K3 \times E)$, with twisted Euler
characteristic $\chi^{(g_N)}_{\mathrm{dR}}(K3)$ matching the twined
Fourier coefficient $c_N(0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum at $K3 \times E$]
\label{rem:kBKM-chain-level-spectrum}
The chain-level modular-Koszul bridge recovers only
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ among the four construction values in the
$K3 \times E$ spectrum $\{\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\} = \{0, 3, 5, 24\}$:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E) = 0$ is the K\"unneth-multiplicative
Euler characteristic $\chi(\cO_{K3}) \chi(\cO_E) = 2 \cdot 0$;
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3 \times E) = 3$ is the Heisenberg
--Cartan rank $2 + 1$; $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}) = 5$
is the Borcherds weight derived here; $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(K3) = 24$
is the Mukai-lattice rank. The chain-level bridge does NOT identify
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$:
this additive split fails at every $N \in \{2, 3, 4, 6\}$ and is an
$N = 1$ coincidence only. The universal identity is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The modular operad assembles local data globally]
\label{rem:gk-local-to-global}
The modular operad $\mathsf{MMG}$ assembles the genus-$0$ $E_3$-Koszul

\begin{itemize}
 \item Boundary-gluing $\overline{\cM}_{g_1, n_1 + 1} \times
 \overline{\cM}_{g_2, n_2 + 1} \to \overline{\cM}_{g_1 + g_2, n_1 + n_2}$,
 corresponding to the tensor product of $E_3$-modules.
 \item Self-gluing $\overline{\cM}_{g, n + 2} \to \overline{\cM}_{g + 1, n}$,

\subsection{Obstructions and scope}

\begin{remark}[Four obstructions limiting the bridge]
\label{rem:modular-koszul-bridge-obstructions}
Four obstructions limit the chain-level modular-Koszul bridge:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(O\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Schottky at $g \geq 4$.} The Torelli morphism
$\tau \colon \overline{\cM}_g \to \overline{\cA}_g$ is not birational
for $g \geq 4$ (Schottky locus of codimension $(g - 2)(g - 3)/2$). The
bridge is clean at $g \leq 3$; at $g \geq 4$, a Schottky lift is
required. For the Igusa $\Phi_{10}$ at $g = 2$, this is not an
obstruction.
\item \emph{Cover-group at Scope~(B) weights $1/2, 3/2$.} The
metaplectic weights require the Shimura--Kohnen tower. The bridge
extends via the metaplectic lift of the Torelli morphism.
\item \emph{Hodge filtration off pure weight.} The pairing factors
through the Hodge filtration; the Borcherds weight is the supertrace
of the pure-weight sector. Off the pure-weight sector, a correction
$\delta \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{mix}}$ appears.
\item \emph{Framed Stage-$2$ conditionality.} The bridge requires
$A_\cC = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ on a framed specialisation
locus (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}). Compact CY$_3$ without a
framed specialisation does not support the bridge.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Lorgat~$2020$ Conjecture~$1$ as two-scope sharpening]
\label{rem:lorgat-conj1-two-scope-chapter}
Lorgat~$2020$ arXiv:$2004.09030$~\S$4$ conjectures (Conjecture~$1$)
that the $8$ Gritsenko--Cl\'ery forms correspond to $8$ distinct DT
zeta-function specialisations of reduced $K3 \times E$-type partition
functions, twined by $8$ distinct Mathieu $M_{24}$ traces. The
chain-level modular-Koszul bridge of
Theorem~\ref{thm:modular-koszul-bridge-d3-two-scopes} provides the
Scope~(B) side of this conjecture: each paramodular index
$(t,N)\in\{(1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(3,1),(1,3),(4,1),(1,4),(2,2)\}$
corresponds to a distinct framing
$(\Sigma_2^{(t,N)}, C^{(t,N)})$ of the Stage-$2$ specialisation locus;
the modular-Koszul pairing at each framing recovers the Borcherds
weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_{t,N}) = c_{t,N}(0, 0)/2$. The
chain-level identification of each framing with an $M_{24}$ twist is
the content of Lorgat's Conjecture.
\end{remark}

\subsection{CFG~$2026$ identification of the physical side}

The chain-level modular-Koszul bridge pairs

\section{The Hochschild bridge}
\label{sec:hochschild-bridge}

A persistent source of confusion in the CY programme is the multiplicity
of ``Hochschild'' objects: the categorical Hochschild homology of $\cC$,
the chiral Hochschild cohomology of $A_\cC$, and the derived center of
$A_\cC$ are three distinct mathematical objects that agree only in
special cases and compute different invariants. The categorical
Hochschild controls deformation theory; the chiral Hochschild controls
the shadow tower; the derived center is the bulk algebra. A constructed
comparison $\Theta_H$ is what relates these theories; without it they are
not automatically identified by the notation $\Phi_d$. Three Hochschild
theories act on a CY category $\cC$ with constructed stage-$2$ chiral
algebra $A_\cC$, and distinguishing them is essential for the bridge to
Volume~I.

\begin{definition}[Three Hochschild theories]
\label{def:three-hochschild}
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY$_d$ category with a constructed
stage-$2$ chiral algebra $A_\cC$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Categorical Hochschild.} The cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ with Hochschild differential $b$ and Connes operator $B$. This is the topological invariant of $\cC$ as a dg category: it carries the Gerstenhaber bracket and the $(2-d)$-shifted Poisson structure from the Serre pairing (Chapter~\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}).
 \item \emph{Chiral Hochschild.} The chiral Hochschild cohomology $\ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$ of Volume~I (Theorem~H; stated on the curve-ambient, i.e.\ $d=1$ ambient on the Ran space): concentrated in cohomological degrees $\{0, 1, 2\}$ with polynomial Hilbert series on the curve-ambient reading. When the $\Theta_H$ comparison is installed for $\Phi_d$ with $d \ge 2$, the CY$_d$-enlarged ambient contributes the additional degree-$d$ cohomological lane; chain-level is infinite for class-M inputs at $d \ge 3$. This is the obstruction-theoretic invariant governing the shadow tower of $A_\cC$.
 \item \emph{Derived center.} The chiral derived center $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \RHom(\Omega B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$: the universal bulk algebra, computed by chiral Hochschild \emph{cochains} (not cohomology). This is the target of the holographic datum (Vol~II).
\end{enumerate}
These are distinct invariants. The categorical Hochschild lives on $\cC$ (no curve geometry); the chiral Hochschild lives on $A_\cC$ (curve geometry through the OPE); the derived center is the endomorphism algebra of the identity functor in the factorization category (curve geometry through $\Ran(X)$).
\end{definition}

At $d=2$ the bridge theorem identifies how $\Phi$ intertwines the
categorical and chiral sides.

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild bridge]
\label{thm:hochschild-bridge}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY$_2$ category and $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ (CY-A at $d = 2$, PROVED). Then $\Phi$ induces:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item A quasi-isomorphism of factorization coalgebras $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC) \xrightarrow{\sim} B(A_\cC)$ on $\Ran(X)$ (this is CY-A(ii), Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-dictionary}).
 \item A map on cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\cC) \to \ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$ that sends the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ to the convolution bracket on $\ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$, and sends the Connes $B$-operator to the modular differential.
 \item A map $\HH^\bullet(\cC, \cC) \to \cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ from the categorical Hochschild cochains (the endomorphism algebra of the identity bimodule) to the chiral derived center, compatible with the Gerstenhaber product on the source and the chiral bracket on the target.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Part (i) is Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-dictionary}. For (ii): the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is a Lie bracket of degree $-1$. Under $\Phi$, this bracket becomes the convolution bracket of Vol~I by functoriality of the bar construction: the deconcatenation coproduct on $B(A_\cC)$ and the binary product of $A_\cC$ assemble into a bracket on $\Hom_{\Ran}(B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$, which restricts to $\ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$ on the cohomology. The Connes operator $B$ on $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ corresponds, under the bar dictionary, to the degree-preserving component of the bar differential (Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-dictionary}(ii)), which is the modular differential on the chiral side. For (iii): the categorical Hochschild cochains $\mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC, \cC)$ map to $\RHom(\Omega B(A_\cC), A_\cC)$ by applying $\Phi$ to each hom-space and using the bar-cobar quasi-isomorphism $\Omega B(A_\cC) \simeq A_\cC$ (Vol~I Theorem~B on the Koszul locus).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Which Hochschild controls which invariant]
\label{rem:hochschild-controls}
The categorical Hochschild $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ controls the deformation
theory of $\cC$ as a CY category: $\HH^2(\cC)$ parametrizes first-order
deformations, $\HH^1(\cC)$ the infinitesimal automorphisms. The chiral
Hochschild $\ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$ controls the shadow tower of $A_\cC$:
the obstruction classes $\mathrm{obs}_g$ live in
$\ChirHoch^2(A_\cC) \otimes H^\bullet(\overline{\cM}_{g,n})$. The
derived center $\cZ^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ is the bulk
algebra of the holographic datum: its elements are the operators that
commute with all boundary insertions. Theorem~\ref{thm:hochschild-bridge}
gives the $d=2$ transmission; for $d\geq3$ the corresponding statement is
the $\Theta_H$ comparison obligation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-volume-comparison-gate}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild bridge at \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3} through \texorpdfstring{$\Theta_H$}{Theta-H}]
\label{conj:hochschild-bridge-d3}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{conditional theorem on the $d=3$ Hochschild comparison
$\Theta_H^{(3)}$, the bar comparison $\Theta_A^{(3)}$, and the centre
comparison $\Theta_Z^{(3)}$.}
For $\cC$ a smooth proper CY$_3$ category with framed output
$A_\cC = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}), assume there are chain maps
\[
\Theta_H^{(3)}:\HC^-_\bullet(\cC)\longrightarrow \ChirHoch^*(A_\cC),
\qquad
\Theta_A^{(3)}:\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)\longrightarrow B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC),
\]
and
\[
\Theta_Z^{(3)}:\mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC,\cC)\longrightarrow
\RHom\!\left(\Omega B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC),A_\cC\right)
\]
compatible with the Connes operator versus the modular differential, with
the Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi $(-1)$-shifted Poisson bracket
versus the genus-$0$ convolution bracket, and with the Vol~I Theorem~B
bar-cobar counit in the weight-completed / coderived ambient. Then the
maps (i)--(iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:hochschild-bridge} extend to $d = 3$.
The $(-1)$-shifted Poisson structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ maps to the
genus-$0$ contribution of the convolution bracket on
$\ChirHoch^*(A_\cC)$; the genus-$g \geq 1$ components of the convolution
bracket have no direct categorical-Hochschild source and arise from the
curve geometry of $\Ran(X)$ through $\Phi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The comparison $\Theta_A^{(3)}$ gives the factorization-coalgebra part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hochschild-bridge}(i). The map $\Theta_H^{(3)}$ carries
the mixed complex $(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC),b,B)$ to the chiral
Hochschild complex and identifies $B$ with the modular differential, so it
gives (ii) after cohomology. The map $\Theta_Z^{(3)}$, together with the
Vol~I Theorem~B counit $\Omega B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)\to A_\cC$ in the
completed ambient, gives the derived-centre map in (iii). The remaining
obstruction to an unconditional statement is therefore the construction of
these three compatible chain maps, not a change in the Vol~I Hochschild
theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{Principal examples}
\label{sec:cy-bridge-examples}

The modular Koszul bridge produces concrete numerical data for each CY category: the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum, the shadow class, and the genus-$g$ obstruction tower. This section collects the principal examples.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E}}

\section{The Pixton ideal and the CY bar complex}

This section connects the two.

\begin{definition}[Miller--Morita--Mumford classes versus modular characteristic]
\label{def:kappa-taut-vs-kappa-ch}
\index{kappa-class!tautological vs modular}
Two distinct symbol conventions coexist in this section, with disjoint meanings:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Tautological classes.} The Miller--Morita--Mumford classes $\kappa_j^{\mathrm{taut}} \in R^j(\overline{\cM}_g)$, $j \geq 1$, are intersection-theoretic invariants of the moduli space. They generate $R^*(\overline{\cM}_g)$ together with the $\lambda$-classes and the boundary strata.
 \item \emph{Modular characteristic.} The subscripted $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$, etc.\ drawn from the closed $\kappa_\bullet$ menu $\{\mathrm{ch}, \mathrm{cat}, \mathrm{BKM}, \mathrm{fiber}\}$ are invariants of the chiral algebra, not of the moduli space.
\end{enumerate}
The two are related by the shadow CohFT: the genus-$g$ amplitude $F_g = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \hat{A}_g$ is an intersection number against tautological classes, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ \emph{scales} the tautological class $\lambda_g \in R^g(\overline{\cM}_g)$.
\end{definition}

 A_{\mathrm{bulk}} \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{bar}\;}

\section{The Stokes--wall-crossing identification}
\label{sec:stokes-wallcrossing}
\index{Stokes automorphism!wall-crossing identification}%
\index{wall-crossing!Stokes identification}%
\index{resurgence!BPS}%

The shadow invariants $\{S_k\}_{k \geq 2}$ of a chiral algebra $A$ encode genus-$g$ geometric data through the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = S_2$, the cubic shadow $S_3$, the quartic contact $S_4$, and the infinite tail. Assemble them into a formal power series in a resurgence parameter $t$:
\[
 \Theta_A(t) \;:=\; \sum_{k \geq 2} S_k\, t^k \;\in\; \C\llbracket t \rrbracket
\]
(Definition~\ref{def:shadow-generating-gevrey} below; distinct from the Vol~I partition function $\Theta_A(q)$ of \S\ref{sec:form-factor-shadow-pf}, whose log is the $q$-expansion whose degree-$r$ coefficient is $S_r(q)$). For class~$G$ (Gaussian) algebras, only $S_2 = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is nonzero and $\Theta_A(t)$ is a quadratic polynomial: convergent, exact, unproblematic. For class~$M$ (mixed) algebras, every $S_k$ is nonzero and $\Theta_A(t)$ is of \emph{Gevrey-$1$} type: the coefficients satisfy $|S_k| \sim C^k\, k!$, so the radius of convergence is zero. This factorial divergence is not a defect: the Borel transform $\hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)$ has positive radius of convergence, its analytic continuation has singularities exactly at the BPS central-charge values $\zeta = Z(\gamma)$, the discontinuities of the Laplace sum across each Stokes ray are the Donaldson--Thomas invariants $\Omega(\gamma)$, and the Stokes automorphism of the Borel-resummed formal series equals the Kontsevich--Soibelman (KS) wall-crossing automorphism of $D^b\Coh(X)$.

The shadow tower diverges because the Borel plane has singularities; the singularities lie at the BPS masses; the discontinuity around each singularity is the BPS index; the trans-series completion is the BPS partition function. The identification has two components of different logical status. The \emph{Stokes side} (Borel transform, singularity analysis, alien derivatives) is intrinsic to the chiral algebra $A$: it requires only the shadow invariants $S_k$ and the Vol~I resurgence theory. The \emph{wall-crossing side} (KS formula, BPS spectrum, walls of marginal stability) is intrinsic to the CY category $\cC$: it requires DT invariants and Bridgeland stability conditions. The identification is unconditional for the conifold (Theorem~\ref{thm:stokes-wc-conifold}); for $K3 \times E$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:stokes-wc-k3e}), $A_{K3 \times E}$ is available only on the chosen framed $\Phi_3$ specialisation, and the remaining conditionality includes the Vol~I Borcherds-lift identification.

\subsection{The Borel side: resurgence of the shadow tower}
\label{subsec:borel-side}
\index{Borel transform!shadow tower}%

The analytic side treats $\Theta_A(t)$ as a resurgent formal power series: the Borel transform $\hat{\Theta}_A$, its analytic continuation, the alien derivative $\Delta_\zeta$ at each singularity, and the Stokes automorphism $\mathfrak{S}_\ell$ across each ray are the four objects that encode the non-perturbative data of the tower.

\begin{definition}[Shadow generating function and Gevrey-\texorpdfstring{$1$}{1} type]
\label{def:shadow-generating-gevrey}
Let $A$ be a chiral algebra with shadow invariants $\{S_k\}_{k \geq 2}$ (Vol~I, Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}). The \emph{shadow generating function} is the formal power series
\[
 \Theta_A(t) \;:=\; \sum_{k \geq 2} S_k\, t^k \;\in\; \C\llbracket t \rrbracket.
\]
We say $\Theta_A$ is of \emph{Gevrey-$1$} type if there exist constants $C, M > 0$ such that $|S_k| \leq C \cdot M^k \cdot k!$ for all $k \geq 2$. Equivalently, the formal Borel transform (Definition~\ref{def:borel-transform} below) has a positive radius of convergence. The shadow class determines the Gevrey type:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Tail behaviour} & \textbf{Borel convergence} \\
\midrule
$G$ (Gaussian) & $S_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$ & $\hat{\Theta}(\zeta)$ is linear (trivial) \\
$L$ (Lie) & $S_k = 0$ for $k \geq 4$ & $\hat{\Theta}(\zeta)$ is quadratic (trivial) \\
$C$ (contact) & $S_k = 0$ for $k \gg 0$ & $\hat{\Theta}(\zeta)$ is polynomial (trivial) \\
$M$ (mixed) & $|S_k| \sim C^k\, k!$ & $\hat{\Theta}(\zeta)$ has finite radius (nontrivial) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For classes $G$, $L$, $C$: the shadow generating function converges, and the Borel analysis is trivial. The entire theory of this section is operative only for class~$M$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Borel transform]
\label{def:borel-transform}
The \emph{formal Borel transform} of $\Theta_A(t) = \sum_{k \geq 2} S_k\, t^k$ is the formal power series
\[
 \hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta) \;:=\; \sum_{k \geq 2} \frac{S_k}{k!}\, \zeta^k \;\in\; \C\llbracket \zeta \rrbracket.
\]
Since $|S_k| \leq C \cdot M^k \cdot k!$ (Gevrey-$1$), we have $|S_k / k!| \leq C \cdot M^k$, so $\hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)$ converges for $|\zeta| < 1/M$. The Borel transform converts a factorially divergent series into a convergent function on a disc in the $\zeta$-plane (the \emph{Borel plane}).

The \emph{Laplace integral} inverts the Borel transform: the \emph{Borel sum} along a direction $\theta$ is
\[
 \cS_\theta[\Theta_A](t) \;:=\; \int_0^{e^{i\theta} \cdot \infty} e^{-\zeta/t}\, \hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)\, d\zeta,
\]
defined provided $\hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)$ admits analytic continuation along the ray $\arg(\zeta) = \theta$ without obstruction. If $\hat{\Theta}_A$ has a singularity on the ray, the integral is obstructed and one must use \emph{lateral Borel summation}: the integral slightly above ($\theta + \epsilon$) and slightly below ($\theta - \epsilon$) the ray give different results, and the discrepancy is the \emph{Stokes discontinuity}.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Virasoro at \texorpdfstring{$c = 1$}{c = 1}: explicit Borel analysis]
\label{ex:virasoro-borel}
The Virasoro algebra at $c = 1$ has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1/2$, cubic shadow $\alpha = 2$, and quartic shadow $S_4 = 10/27$. The shadow generating function begins
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{Vir}}(t) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\, t^2 \;+\; 2\, t^3 \;+\; \tfrac{10}{27}\, t^4 \;+\; \cdots,
\]
with $|S_k| \sim k!$ for large $k$ (class~$M$, infinite shadow depth). The formal Borel transform is
\[
 \hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{Vir}}(\zeta) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot \tfrac{\zeta^2}{2!} \;+\; 2 \cdot \tfrac{\zeta^3}{3!} \;+\; \tfrac{10}{27} \cdot \tfrac{\zeta^4}{4!} \;+\; \cdots \;=\; \tfrac{\zeta^2}{4} \;+\; \tfrac{\zeta^3}{3} \;+\; \tfrac{10\,\zeta^4}{648} \;+\; \cdots,
\]
which converges on a disc of radius $R > 0$. The shadow quadratic $Q_L(\zeta) = q_0 + q_1\zeta + q_2\zeta^2$ has coefficients $q_0 = 4\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2 = 1$, $q_1 = 12\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\alpha = 12$, $q_2 = 9\alpha^2 + 16\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4 = 36 + 80/27 = 1052/27$; the discriminant $\Delta(Q_L) = q_1^2 - 4 q_0 q_2 = -256\,\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3 S_4 = -320/27 < 0$. The Borel singularities are the complex-conjugate roots $\omega_\pm = t_c \pm i\delta$ with
\[
 t_c \;=\; -\frac{q_1}{2q_2} \;=\; -\frac{81}{526} \;\approx\; -0.154, \qquad \delta^2 \;=\; -\frac{\Delta(Q_L)}{4q_2^2} \;=\; \frac{64\,\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3 S_4}{(9\alpha^2 + 16\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4)^2} \;>\; 0.
\]
Both satisfy $\Re(\omega_\pm) = t_c < 0$ (left half-plane). The positive real axis is singularity-free, so $\cS_0[\Theta_{\mathrm{Vir}}]$ is unambiguous; the Stokes lines at $\theta_\pm = \arg(\omega_\pm)$ lie in the second and third quadrants.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Borel summability of class~\texorpdfstring{$M$}{M} shadow towers]
\label{prop:class-m-borel-summability-bridge}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $A$ be a chiral algebra of class~$M$ with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} > 0$, cubic shadow $\alpha > 0$, and quartic shadow $S_4 > 0$ \textup{(}e.g.\ the Virasoro at $c = 1$; see Example~\textup{\ref{ex:virasoro-borel}}\textup{)}. The Borel transform $\hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)$ extends to an analytic function on $\C \setminus \{\omega_+, \omega_-\}$, where $\omega_\pm = t_c \pm i\delta$ are complex-conjugate Borel singularities, the roots of the shadow quadratic $Q_L(\zeta) = q_0 + q_1 \zeta + q_2 \zeta^2$ with
\[
 q_0 = 4\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2, \qquad q_1 = 12\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\alpha, \qquad q_2 = 9\alpha^2 + 16\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4
\]
\textup{(}Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}} Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability}}, Step~1\textup{)}. The discriminant satisfies the shadow identity $\Delta(Q_L) = q_1^2 - 4 q_0 q_2 = -256\,\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3\, S_4$: under $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, S_4 > 0$ it is strictly negative, so the roots are complex-conjugate with $\Re(\omega_\pm) = t_c = -q_1/(2q_2) = -3\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\alpha/(9\alpha^2 + 16\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} S_4) < 0$.

\medskip
The crucial consequence: since $t_c < 0$, \emph{both singularities lie in the left half-plane}, and the positive real axis $\R_+ \subset \C$ is free of obstructions. The Borel sum along $\theta = 0$ \textup{(}the physical direction\textup{)} is well-defined and unambiguous:
\[
 \cS_0[\Theta_A](t) \;=\; \int_0^{+\infty} e^{-\zeta/t}\, \hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)\, d\zeta
\]
converges for $\Re(t) > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Alien derivative and Stokes constant]
\label{def:alien-derivative}
\index{alien derivative}%
\index{Stokes constant}%
Let $\omega \in \C^*$ be a singularity of $\hat{\Theta}_A(\zeta)$. The \emph{alien derivative} (Ecalle, 1981) of $\Theta_A$ at $\omega$ is the formal power series $\Delta_\omega \Theta_A \in \C\llbracket t \rrbracket$ defined by the discontinuity of the Borel sum across the Stokes line at angle $\theta = \arg(\omega)$:
\[
 \cS_{\theta^+}[\Theta_A](t) - \cS_{\theta^-}[\Theta_A](t) \;=\; e^{-\omega/t}\, \cS_\theta[\Delta_\omega \Theta_A](t).
\]
The alien derivative measures how much the Borel sum ``jumps'' when the integration contour crosses the singularity.

For \emph{simple resurgent} functions (Ecalle), the alien derivative at $\omega$ takes the factored form
\[
 \Delta_\omega \Theta_A \;=\; \cS_\omega \cdot \Theta_A^{(\omega)},
\]
where $\cS_\omega \in \C$ is the \emph{Stokes constant} and $\Theta_A^{(\omega)}$ is the \emph{perturbative tail} of the one-instanton sector at action $\omega$. The Stokes constant encodes the strength of the non-perturbative contribution: it is the single number that converts a Borel singularity into a trans-series coefficient.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trans-series completion]
\label{def:trans-series}
\index{trans-series}%
The \emph{trans-series completion} of $\Theta_A$ is the formal object
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trans-series-bridge}
 \Theta_A^{\mathrm{full}}(t) \;=\; \Theta_A^{\mathrm{pert}}(t) \;+\; \sum_{\substack{n_+, n_- \geq 0 \\ (n_+, n_-) \neq (0,0)}} C_+^{n_+}\, C_-^{n_-}\, e^{-(n_+ \omega_+ + n_- \omega_-)/t}\, \Theta_A^{(n_+, n_-)}(t),
\end{equation}
where $C_\pm$ are the Stokes constants at $\omega_\pm$, the exponentials $e^{-\omega/t}$ are the \emph{instanton weights}, and $\Theta_A^{(n_+, n_-)}(t)$ are perturbative tails (formal power series in $t$) determined recursively by iterated alien derivatives.

The trans-series organises the non-perturbative content into \emph{sectors} labelled by pairs $(n_+, n_-)$: the sector $(0,0)$ is the original perturbative series; $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ are one-instanton sectors; $(n_+, n_-)$ is the sector with $n_+$ instantons of action $\omega_+$ and $n_-$ instantons of action $\omega_-$. Each sector carries an exponential suppression $e^{-(n_+ \omega_+ + n_- \omega_-)/t}$ that makes it invisible to perturbation theory (all Taylor coefficients in $t$ vanish).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Stokes automorphism]
\label{def:stokes-automorphism}
\index{Stokes automorphism}%
Let $\theta_0 = \arg(\omega_+)$ be a Stokes direction \textup{(}i.e.\ a direction in the $\zeta$-plane along which a singularity of $\hat{\Theta}_A$ lies\textup{)}. The \emph{Stokes automorphism} $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\theta_0}$ is the automorphism of the trans-series space defined by
\[
 \underline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\theta_0} \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl(\sum_{\omega:\, \arg(\omega) = \theta_0} \cS_\omega\, \Delta_\omega\Bigr),
\]
where the sum runs over all Borel singularities on the ray at angle $\theta_0$. The Stokes automorphism acts on the trans-series by mixing sectors: crossing the Stokes line at angle $\theta_0$ replaces the lateral Borel sum $\cS_{\theta_0^-}$ by $\cS_{\theta_0^+} = \underline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\theta_0} \circ \cS_{\theta_0^-}$.

Three structural properties of $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\theta_0}$, which will reappear on the wall-crossing side:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \emph{Upper-triangularity}: in the instanton-number filtration (ordered by $|n_+ \omega_+ + n_- \omega_-|$), $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\theta_0}$ is upper-triangular with ones on the diagonal.
 \item \emph{Integer data}: for simple resurgent functions, the Stokes constants $\cS_\omega$ determine the entire automorphism.
 \item \emph{Factorisation}: the automorphism factorises as a product of elementary Stokes factors, one per singularity, labeled-ordered (i.e.\ indexed by the singularity labels, not in a time-ordered/OPE sense) by the angle $\arg(\omega)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Stokes lines in the left half-plane]
\label{rem:stokes-lhp}
For class~$M$ algebras with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} > 0$, $\alpha > 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability-bridge}), all Borel singularities lie in the left half-plane ($\Re(\omega_\pm) < 0$). Consequently, all Stokes lines avoid the positive real axis $\R_+$, and the physical Borel sum $\cS_0[\Theta_A]$ is unambiguous. The Stokes phenomenon is present but does not afflict the physical direction: it describes what happens when one analytically continues the Borel sum past $\R_+$ into the left half-plane. In the wall-crossing interpretation below, this corresponds to being in a \emph{stable chamber}: the physical moduli do not sit on a wall.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The BPS/KS side: wall-crossing in CY categories}
\label{subsec:ks-side}
\index{Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing}%
\index{BPS spectrum}%

The other side of the identification is the Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula for BPS states in CY categories. The tools are Bridgeland stability conditions, BPS indices, walls of marginal stability, and the KS automorphism.

\begin{definition}[BPS spectrum and central charge]
\label{def:bps-spectrum}
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category with charge lattice $\Gamma \simeq \Z^r$ equipped with an antisymmetric Euler pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle \colon \Gamma \times \Gamma \to \Z$. A \emph{stability condition} $\sigma = (Z, \cP)$ on $\cC$ (Bridgeland, 2007) consists of a group homomorphism $Z \colon \Gamma \to \C$ (the \emph{central charge}) and a slicing $\cP$ of the derived category. The \emph{BPS index} $\Omega_\sigma(\gamma) \in \Z$ counts (with signs) the $\sigma$-stable objects of charge $\gamma \in \Gamma$.

The BPS mass of a state of charge $\gamma$ is $|Z(\gamma)|$, and the BPS phase is $\phi(\gamma) = (1/\pi) \arg Z(\gamma)$. A BPS state $\gamma$ is stable if no decomposition $\gamma = \gamma_1 + \gamma_2$ with $\phi(\gamma_1) = \phi(\gamma_2)$ and $\Omega(\gamma_i) \neq 0$ exists; when such a decomposition does exist, the state is \emph{marginally stable}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Walls of marginal stability]
\label{def:walls-marginal-stability}
\index{wall of marginal stability}%
A \emph{wall of marginal stability} $\cW(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) \subset \Stab(\cC)$ for charges $\gamma_1, \gamma_2 \in \Gamma$ with $\langle \gamma_1, \gamma_2 \rangle \neq 0$ is the codimension-$1$ locus in the stability manifold where $\phi_\sigma(\gamma_1) = \phi_\sigma(\gamma_2)$, equivalently where $Z(\gamma_1)/Z(\gamma_2) \in \R_{>0}$. As $\sigma$ crosses $\cW(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$, the BPS spectrum changes: the bound state $\gamma_1 + \gamma_2$ may appear or disappear, and the BPS indices jump.

Walls are real codimension-$1$ submanifolds of $\Stab(\cC)$; they partition the stability manifold into \emph{chambers}, within each of which the BPS spectrum is constant.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[KS wall-crossing automorphism]
\label{def:ks-wall-crossing}
\index{KS automorphism}%
The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula (Kontsevich--Soibelman, 2008) associates to each wall $\cW$ the product
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ks-product}
 \cA_\cW \;:=\; \prod_{\substack{\gamma \in \Gamma^+:\\ \phi_\sigma(\gamma) = \theta_\cW}}^{\curvearrowleft} \cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)},
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma^+ \subset \Gamma$ is the positive cone, $\theta_\cW$ is the phase at the wall, the product is taken in the \emph{clockwise} order of $\arg Z(\gamma)$ (a labeled-ordered product indexed by the charge lattice, not a time-ordered product in the OPE sense), and the elementary KS symplectomorphism $\cK_\gamma$ acts on the quantum torus algebra $\C[\Gamma]$ by
\[
 \cK_\gamma(X^\beta) \;=\; X^\beta \cdot (1 - X^\gamma)^{\langle \gamma, \beta \rangle}.
\]
The \textbf{KS wall-crossing formula} asserts that the product $\cA_\cW$ is \emph{wall-crossing invariant}: as $\sigma$ crosses $\cW$, the individual factors $\cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$ change (because $\Omega(\gamma)$ jumps), but their ordered product remains the same.

Three structural properties of $\cA_\cW$ (compare with Definition~\ref{def:stokes-automorphism}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \emph{Upper-triangularity}: in the charge filtration (ordered by $|\gamma|$), $\cA_\cW$ is upper-triangular with ones on the diagonal.
 \item \emph{Integer data}: the KS automorphism is determined by the integer BPS indices $\Omega(\gamma)$.
 \item \emph{Factorisation}: $\cA_\cW$ factorises as a product of elementary symplectomorphisms $\cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$, labeled-ordered by the phase $\phi(\gamma)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The bar differential and wall-crossing]
\label{rem:bar-diff-wc}
The connection between the KS formula and the bar complex of $A_\cC$ passes through the $E_1$ bar differential $d_B$ on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$. The deconcatenation coproduct on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ is \emph{not} wall-crossing invariant: it changes at walls. But the bar Euler product $\prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^n)^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}$ (the generating function of the bar cohomology) IS wall-crossing invariant (Conjecture~\ref{conj:k3e-kswcf-bar} in Chapter~\ref{ch:k3e-bkm}). The shadow coefficients $S_k$ change at walls (Proposition~\ref{prop:k3e-shadow-jump}), but their generating function is invariant; precisely because the Stokes automorphism is the KS automorphism.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The identification}
\label{subsec:stokes-wc-identification}
\index{Stokes--wall-crossing identification}%

The identification between Stokes and KS sides is not analogy but equality of automorphisms: both act on the \emph{same object} (the shadow generating function $\Theta_A$).

\begin{theorem}[Six-fold correspondence table]
\label{thm:six-fold-correspondence}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The following correspondence holds between the resurgent structure of the shadow tower \textup{(}Borel side\textup{)} and the BPS wall-crossing data \textup{(}KS side\textup{)}:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Borel/Stokes side} & \textbf{BPS/KS side} & \textbf{Identification} \\
\midrule
Stokes line at $\theta = \arg(\omega)$ & Wall $\cW(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ & $\arg(\omega) = \arg(Z(\gamma))$ \\
Borel singularity at $\omega$ & BPS state of charge $\gamma$ & $|\omega| = |Z(\gamma)|$ \\
Trans-series sector $(n_+, n_-)$ & BPS charge sector $n_+ \gamma_+ + n_- \gamma_-$ & Instanton number $=$ charge \\
Stokes constant $\cS_\omega$ & BPS index $\Omega(\gamma)$ & $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma)$ \\
Stokes auto.\ $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_\theta$ & KS symplecto.\ $\cA_\cW$ & Same automorphism \\
Lateral Borel sum $\cS_{\theta^\pm}$ & Chamber DT invariants & Same generating function \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each row is a matching at a different level of structure: the first row matches the geometry \textup{(}rays in $\C$\textup{)}, the second matches the data carried by each ray \textup{(}singularity type $=$ BPS mass\textup{)}, the third matches the grading \textup{(}instanton sectors $=$ charge sectors\textup{)}, the fourth matches the numerical invariants \textup{(}Stokes constants $=$ BPS indices\textup{)}, the fifth matches the automorphisms \textup{(}Stokes $=$ KS\textup{)}, and the sixth matches the result of summation \textup{(}lateral Borel sum $=$ chamber-dependent DT partition function\textup{)}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Why it works}: both the Stokes automorphism and the KS automorphism act on the \emph{same} generating function $\Theta_A(t) = \sum S_k\, t^k$. The shadow coefficients $S_k$ are Borel-resummable via the Borel side and wall-crossing invariant via the KS side. The generating function is the bridge: it lives simultaneously in the resurgent world \textup{(}where it diverges and requires Borel summation\textup{)} and in the stability world \textup{(}where it jumps at walls and requires KS correction\textup{)}. The divergence and the jumping are two aspects of the same phenomenon.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof consists of verifying the five structural properties shared by both sides (Section~\ref{subsubsec:five-checks} below), together with the explicit verifications for the conifold and $K3 \times E$ (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:conifold-verification}--\ref{subsubsec:k3e-verification}).

\smallskip
\noindent(1) \emph{Ray indexing.} Stokes lines are rays in the Borel plane at angles $\arg(\omega)$ for each singularity $\omega$. Walls are loci in the stability manifold where $Z(\gamma_1)/Z(\gamma_2) \in \R_{>0}$, i.e.\ where the central charges align. Under the identification $|\omega| = |Z(\gamma)|$, a Stokes line at angle $\theta$ corresponds to a wall where BPS phases align at $\theta$.

\smallskip
\noindent(2) \emph{Factorisation.} The Stokes automorphism factorises as $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_\theta = \prod \exp(\cS_\omega \Delta_\omega)$, a labeled-ordered product of elementary Stokes factors. The KS automorphism factorises as $\cA_\cW = \prod \cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$, a labeled-ordered product of elementary symplectomorphisms. Under $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma)$ and $\Delta_\omega \leftrightarrow \cK_\gamma$, the two factorisations match.

\smallskip
\noindent(3) \emph{Upper-triangularity.} Both sides are upper-triangular: the Stokes automorphism in the instanton filtration, the KS automorphism in the charge filtration. These filtrations are identified by instanton number $=$ charge height.

\smallskip
\noindent(4) \emph{Integer data.} The Stokes constants $\cS_\omega$ are integers for simple resurgent functions. The BPS indices $\Omega(\gamma)$ are integers by definition. The identification $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma)$ is integer $=$ integer.

\smallskip
\noindent(5) \emph{Gauge invariance.} The physical Borel sum $\cS_0[\Theta_A]$ is independent of the choice of lateral direction (when no Stokes line lies on $\R_+$). The bar Euler product is independent of the stability chamber (wall-crossing invariance). Both express the same gauge-invariant quantity.

\medskip\noindent Verification: all five structural checks implemented in \texttt{stokes\_wallcrossing\_identification.py} ($84$~tests, function \texttt{five\_point\_structural\_check}).
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The Bridgeland Riemann--Hilbert bridge}
\label{subsubsec:bridgeland-rh}
\index{Riemann--Hilbert problem!Bridgeland}%

The identification between Stokes data and BPS data is not an ad hoc analogy: it is underwritten by a precise mathematical result of Bridgeland.

\begin{theorem}[Bridgeland, 2019]
\label{thm:bridgeland-rh}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
\textup{(}Bridgeland, \textup{\cite{Bridgeland2019RH}}, Theorem~$1.1$.\textup{)} Let $(\Gamma, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle, Z, \Omega)$ be a BPS structure: a lattice with pairing, central charge, and BPS indices. There exists a Riemann--Hilbert problem whose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Stokes rays} are the rays $\ell_\gamma = \R_{>0} \cdot Z(\gamma) \subset \C$ for each $\gamma$ with $\Omega(\gamma) \neq 0$;
 \item \emph{Stokes factors} are $\cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$;
 \item \emph{Connection} is a flat connection on the complement of the Stokes rays;
 \item \emph{Jump matrices} across Stokes rays are the KS wall-crossing factors.
\end{enumerate}
The solution of the RH problem exists and is unique under appropriate convergence conditions on the BPS spectrum. The DT invariants $\Omega(\gamma)$ ARE the Stokes data of the RH problem.
\end{theorem}

\noindent The shadow tower layer refines Bridgeland's framework: the Borel transform of $\Theta_A$ is the connection of the Bridgeland RH problem, the Borel singularities are the Stokes rays, and the alien derivatives are the Stokes factors. The chain reads:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:identification-chain}
 \underbrace{\Theta_A}_{\text{Shadow tower}} \xrightarrow{\;\;\text{Borel}\;\;} \underbrace{\hat{\Theta}_A}_{\text{Borel plane}} \xrightarrow{\;\text{singularities}\;} \underbrace{\omega = Z(\gamma)}_{\text{Stokes rays}} \xrightarrow{\;\text{Bridgeland RH}\;} \underbrace{\Omega(\gamma)}_{\text{DT invariants}} \xrightarrow{\;\;\text{KS}\;\;} \underbrace{\cA_\cW}_{\text{Wall-crossing}}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Five structural checks}
\label{subsubsec:five-checks}

The five shared structural properties of the Stokes and KS automorphisms are verified computationally:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
 \item \textbf{Ray indexing.} Stokes: lines at $\arg(\omega)$. KS: walls at $\arg(Z(\gamma))$. Match: $\arg(\omega) = \arg(Z(\gamma))$.
 \item \textbf{Factorisation.} Stokes: $\underline{\mathfrak{S}}_\theta = \prod \exp(\cS_\omega \Delta_\omega)$. KS: $\cA_\cW = \prod \cK_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$. Match: elementary factor $\leftrightarrow$ elementary factor.
 \item \textbf{Upper-triangularity.} Stokes: in instanton filtration. KS: in charge filtration. Match: $n_{\mathrm{inst}} = |\gamma|$.
 \item \textbf{Integer data.} Stokes: $\cS_\omega \in \Z$. KS: $\Omega(\gamma) \in \Z$. Match: $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma)$.
 \item \textbf{Gauge invariance.} Stokes: Borel sum independent of lateral direction. KS: bar Euler product independent of chamber. Match: same gauge-invariant generating function.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Verified cases}
\label{subsec:verified-cases}

\subsubsection{The conifold}
\label{subsubsec:conifold-verification}
\index{conifold!Stokes--wall-crossing}%

The resolved conifold $X = \cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1$ is the simplest CY$_3$ with nontrivial wall-crossing. It has charge lattice $\Gamma = \Z^2$ with $\langle \gamma_1, \gamma_2 \rangle = 1$, and a single wall of marginal stability (the flop wall).

\begin{theorem}[Stokes \texorpdfstring{$=$}{=} WC for the conifold]
\label{thm:stokes-wc-conifold}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For the resolved conifold, the Stokes automorphism of the resurgent DT partition function is the KS wall-crossing automorphism. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item There is one wall $\cW(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ and one Stokes line at $\theta = \pi$ \textup{(}real negative $Q$-axis\textup{)}.
 \item The BPS spectrum has $\Omega(\gamma_1) = \Omega(\gamma_2) = -1$ in Chamber~I \textup{(}large volume\textup{)} and $\Omega(\gamma_1 + \gamma_2) = -1$ appearing in Chamber~II.
 \item The Stokes constant is $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma_1 + \gamma_2) = -1$.
 \item The Stokes factorisation identity IS the Faddeev--Kashaev pentagon identity in the quantum torus:
 \begin{equation}
 \label{eq:pentagon-stokes}
 \underbrace{E(X)\, E(Y)}_{\text{Chamber I: 2 BPS states}} \;=\; \underbrace{E(Y)\, E(XY)\, E(X)}_{\text{Chamber II: 3 BPS states}},
 \end{equation}
 where $E(Z) = \prod_{n \geq 0} (1 + q^{n+1/2} Z)^{-1}$ is the compact quantum dilogarithm.
 \item The Stokes matrix and the KS symplectomorphism matrix agree:
 \[
 \underline{\mathfrak{S}}_\theta \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \;=\; \cA_\cW \qquad \text{\textup{(}in the 3-sector basis\textup{)}}.
 \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conifold has finitely many BPS states (two per chamber plus one bound state), so the local wall-crossing identification is exact and does not use a global $d=3$ chiral-algebra existence theorem. The pentagon identity is verified exactly in the quantum torus algebra at each charge sector (\texttt{conifold\_wall\_crossing.py}). The matrix identity is verified by direct computation (\texttt{stokes\_wallcrossing\_identification.py}, function \texttt{conifold\_stokes\_matrix}). The Bridgeland RH problem (Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgeland-rh}) specialises to a single Stokes ray with a single jump matrix, and the jump is the pentagon identity. The factor $E(XY)$ on the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:pentagon-stokes} IS the Stokes discontinuity: the trans-series sector that appears when the Borel integration contour crosses the singularity.

\smallskip\noindent Verification: $84$~tests in \texttt{stokes\_wallcrossing\_identification.py}, covering pentagon $=$ Stokes factorisation, matrix matching, Borel singularity $=$ conifold point, and cross-checks with \texttt{conifold\_wall\_crossing.py} and \texttt{shadow\_resurgence\_nonpert.py}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The pentagon identity as resurgence]
\label{rem:pentagon-resurgence}
The pentagon identity $E(X)E(Y) = E(Y)E(XY)E(X)$ has appeared in three different contexts in these volumes: as a wall-crossing identity (KS, 2008), as a Stokes factorisation (this section), and as a cluster mutation relation (Vol~I, \S bar-cobar involution). The identification of this section explains why the same identity recurs: the pentagon identity is the \emph{unique} wall-crossing/Stokes/mutation identity for a two-dimensional charge lattice with a single wall. Any CY$_3$ with exactly one wall must exhibit the pentagon identity in all three guises.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E}}
\label{subsubsec:k3e-verification}
\index{K3 x E@$K3 \times E$!Stokes--wall-crossing}%

For $K3 \times E$, the BKM root system of $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ produces infinitely many walls (one per positive root) and infinitely many Stokes lines (one per Borel singularity of the shadow tower). The identification is stratified by the BKM discriminant $D = 4nm - l^2$.

\begin{conjecture}[Stokes \texorpdfstring{$=$}{=} WC for \texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E}]
\label{conj:stokes-wc-k3e}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
For $K3 \times E$ with the BKM root system of $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$, the Stokes automorphism of the shadow trans-series \textup{(}Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:shadow-resurgence})} is the KS wall-crossing automorphism. The identification is stratified by discriminant:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Discriminant} & $c(D)$ & \textbf{Wall type} & \textbf{Stokes type} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
$D = -1$ & $1$ & Real root (flop) & Real axis & THEOREM (local conifold) \\
$D = 0$ & $10$ & Lightlike & Imaginary axis & Conjectural (framed $\Phi_3$ and CY-D) \\
$D > 0$ & $c(D)$ & Imaginary (bound state) & Conjugate pair & Conjectural (framed $\Phi_3$ and CY-D) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At each discriminant $D$, the BKM root multiplicity $c(D)$ \textup{(}the Fourier coefficient of the weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$\textup{)} simultaneously counts:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item The number of BPS states at discriminant $D$;
 \item The number of walls of marginal stability at those BPS masses;
 \item The number of Stokes lines in the Borel plane at action $|Z_D|$;

\begin{proof}[Partial proof and evidence]
At $D = -1$ \textup{(}real roots\textup{)}: each real root $\alpha$ of $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ with $\alpha^2 = 2$ decomposes as $\alpha = \gamma_1 + \gamma_2$ with $\langle \gamma_1, \gamma_2 \rangle = 1$ (the Euler pairing of the conifold). The local model at each real root wall is therefore the conifold, and the identification reduces to Theorem~\ref{thm:stokes-wc-conifold}. Since $c(-1) = 1$ (one real root orbit per discriminant class), each real root wall carries exactly one Stokes line, a mini-conifold. The $K3 \times E$ identification is thus a ``stitching'' of infinitely many conifold-type identifications (one per positive real root), together with the bound-state identifications at $D \geq 0$.
At $D > 0$ \textup{(}imaginary roots\textup{)}: the BKM multiplicities $c(D)$ grow exponentially ($|c(D)| \sim e^{4\pi\sqrt{D}}$; Rademacher), producing infinitely many walls and Stokes lines. These imaginary-root walls are the \emph{source} of shadow class~$M$: infinitely many Borel singularities produce infinite shadow depth ($S_k \neq 0$ for all $k$). The shadow contribution from discriminant $D$ at degree $k$ is $\Delta S_k = c(D) \cdot D^{k-2}/k!$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:k3e-shadow-jump}).

The global stitching of these infinitely many local identifications requires the full shadow tower of $A_{K3 \times E}$. The framed $K3\times E$ specialisation of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3} supplies the stage-2 output $A_{K3 \times E}$; the remaining conditionality includes the Vol~I Borcherds-lift identification of the bar Euler product with $\Phi_{10}$.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The instanton gap is computable]
\label{rem:instanton-gap-computable}
For a class~$M$ chiral algebra $A$, let $F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}$ denote the genus-$g$ free energy computed from the perturbative shadow tower $\Theta_A(t)$ and let $F_g^{\mathrm{top}}$ denote the exact topological string free energy (the Borel-resummed value). The difference $F_g^{\mathrm{top}} - F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}$ (the \emph{instanton gap}) is not merely a label for missing non-perturbative data: it is a computable quantity. The shadow tower is Gevrey-$1$ divergent and Borel summable (Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability-bridge}), so the physical Borel sum $\cS_0[\Theta_A]$ exists and is unambiguous along $\R_+$. The instanton gap is then encoded in the Stokes automorphism: the lateral Borel sums $\cS_{\theta^\pm}$ differ by $\exp(\cS_\omega \cdot \Delta_\omega)$, and the identification $\cS_\omega = \Omega(\gamma)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:six-fold-correspondence}) converts the Stokes discontinuity into BPS data. Since the Stokes automorphism IS the KS wall-crossing automorphism, the instanton gap is controlled by the wall-crossing formula: crossing a Stokes line shifts $F_g$ by a sum over BPS charges weighted by $\Omega(\gamma)$, and the resulting trans-series gives $F_g^{\mathrm{top}}$ exactly. This makes the instanton gap a \emph{derived} quantity, determined by the perturbative shadow coefficients $\{S_k\}$ alone through the Borel--KS pipeline of~\eqref{eq:identification-chain}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-volume comparison gate]
\label{thm:cross-volume-comparison-gate}
\ClaimStatusConditional
Let
\[
 A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}
 =
 \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\bigl(\PhiFA_d(\cC)\bigr)
\]
be a constructed stage-$2$ shadow. A Vol~I or Vol~II theorem
pushes forward to Vol~III as a theorem precisely on the part of the
following comparison diagram that has been supplied by an explicit
chain-level map:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{A. Bar--cobar.}
The required map is
\[
 \Theta_A\colon
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}\bigl(A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}\bigr)
 \longrightarrow
 \Bigl(\int_{\Sigma_{d-1}}
 B_{E_d}\bigl(\PhiFA_d(\cC)\bigr)\Bigr)\bigm|_C .
\]
At $d=2$ this is the CY-A$_2$ bar dictionary. At $d=3$ it is available
only on the witnessed framed locus of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}.
At $d\geq4$ the map is a construction obligation, not a consequence of
the notation $\PhiFA_d$.

\item \emph{B. Koszul/Verdier duality.}
The required map is the completed Verdier comparison
\[
 \Theta_B\colon
 D_{\Ran}B^{\mathrm{ord}}\bigl(A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}\bigr)
 \longrightarrow
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}\bigl(D_{\Ran}A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}\bigr),
\]
with finite graded pieces before taking products in the completed bar
construction. This is proved on the CY$_2$ shadows and on the framed
CY$_3$ Verdier spectral pages used in this chapter; for $d\geq4$ the
finite-piece and completion hypotheses must be verified anew.

\item \emph{C. Quantum group realisation.}
The required map is a braided comparison
\[
 \Theta_C\colon
 \Rep(G(X))
 \longrightarrow
 \cZ\!\left(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)})\right).
\]
For $K3\times E$ the proved core is the generator-level
rank stratification and the pentagon colimit of the named intertwiners.
The universal object $G(X)$ and the braided equivalence above are not
constructed in general; the positive-half Hall algebra $Y^+(X)$ and its

\item \emph{D. Modular characteristic.}
The Hodge branch is the supertrace
\[
 \Theta_D^{\mathrm{Hodge}}\colon
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(X)
 =
 \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X).
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai and Borcherds branches require separate maps
\[

\item \emph{H. Hochschild comparison.}

\[
 \Theta_H\colon
 HC^-_\bullet(\cC)
 \longrightarrow
 \ChirHoch\bigl(A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}\bigr).

On the chirally Koszul generic locus it identifies the
$\tau_{\le2}$ piece with Vol~I Theorem~H. At $d\geq3$ the residual

\]
compatible with the elliptic fibre, the DMVV second quantisation, and
the Borcherds lift. Without this map, the $d=2$ mock theorem supplies
evidence for the $K3$ fibre, not a theorem for the full CY$_3$ target.

\item \emph{ZTE correction.}
The exact rational matrix $T$ solves the Yangian deformation-complex
problem
\[
 \ell_1(T)+\ell_3(r,r,r)=0
\]
for the Yang $R$-matrix at the parameter $\hbar_{\mathrm{Y}}$. Its

 \Theta_{\mathrm{ZTE}\to\Phi}\colon
 R_{\mathrm{Yang}}(z)
 \longrightarrow

 \in

 \Theta_{A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}} =
 D_{A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma,C)}}-d_0

 H^\bullet_{\mathrm{van}}\!\left(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{DT}}(X)\right)
\]

\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}
The statement is a type check of the three ambient categories. Vol~I
Theorems A--D and H are theorems of chiral algebras on a curve and their
ordered Ran bar complexes. Vol~II supplies holographic or
Swiss-cheese structures on boundary--bulk pairs. Vol~III begins with a
CY category and reaches a curve only after the two-stage construction
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ\PhiFA_d$. Hence every cross-volume
transport factors through an explicit map from the CY or physical source
to the stage-$2$ ordered bar, Hochschild, centre, or representation
category. Items \textup{(a)}--\textup{(h)} list exactly those target
objects. Where the named map is installed by CY-A$_2$, the framed
CY-A$_3$ theorem, the Verdier spectral functor, or direct exact
computation, the pushforward is theorem-level. Where the map is not
installed, the formula is a proof obligation. No additional
cross-volume theorem can bypass this gate, because it would have to
identify objects in different categories without a functor between them.
\end{proof}

\section{Humbert monodromy and the universal identity}

\subsection{The Humbert divisors and their Heegner structure}

\begin{definition}[Humbert divisors in \texorpdfstring{$\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$}{overlineA-2}]
\label{def:humbert-divisors}
The Humbert surfaces $H_D\subset\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$, indexed by positive discriminants $D>0$, are the Heegner loci parametrising principally polarised abelian surfaces admitting real multiplication by the real quadratic order $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}]$ for $D\equiv 1\pmod 4$, resp.\ $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{D}]$ for $D\equiv 0\pmod 4$ (Humbert, \emph{Sur les surfaces algébriques dans l'espace de Hilbert}, J.~Math.~Pures Appl.~9, 1899; modern treatment: van der Geer, \emph{Hilbert modular surfaces}, Springer 1988, Ch.~IX). Each $H_D$ is a codimension-one Heegner divisor in $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$; the low-discriminant cases $H_1$ (product of two elliptic curves) and $H_4$ (product of CM elliptic curves) are both load-bearing for $\Delta_5$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Vanishing orders of \texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta-5} on Humbert divisors]
\label{fact:delta5-vanishing-humbert}
By Gritsenko--Nikulin, \emph{Automorphic forms and Lorentzian Kac--Moody algebras~II}, Int.~J.~Math.~9, 1998, Theorem~2.1: the Borcherds form $\Delta_5 = \mathrm{Grit}(\eta^9\vartheta_1)$ vanishes on $H_1$ to order $1$ and on $H_4$ to order $2$. Its square $\Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2$ therefore vanishes to orders $2$ and $4$ respectively, and $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_{10}|_{\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})}$ (Igusa's genus-two cusp form) to the same orders.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The monodromy identities}

\begin{proposition}[Humbert monodromy identities]
\label{prop:humbert-monodromy-K-kappa}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The local monodromy of the regular holonomic $\mathcal{D}$-module $\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}$ on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$ around the Humbert divisors has orders
\[
 \mathrm{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{monodromy}\;\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}\big|_{H_1}\bigr)
 \;=\; 8,
 \qquad
 \mathrm{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{monodromy}\;\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}\big|_{H_4}\bigr)
 \;=\; 16.
\]
The order-$8$ Humbert-$H_1$ monodromy equals the Koszul conductor of the Mukai-enhanced $K3$ category,
\[
 \mathrm{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{monodromy}\;H_1\bigr)
 \;=\;
 K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}
 \;=\;
 2\,c_+\bigl(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3)\bigr)
 \;=\;
 8,
\]
universally on the Lorentzian-lattice-parametric $\mathcal{B}$-family (Mukai signature $(4,20)$, positive part $c_+=4$, doubling $K=2c_+=8$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch via Bruinier, Heegner-divisor Chern-class reciprocity]
\label{proof:humbert-monodromy}
The key input is Bruinier, \emph{Borcherds products on $O(2,l)$ and Chern classes of Heegner divisors}, Springer LNM~1780, 2002, Proposition~5.1. Specialised to the orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{2,3})$ that acts on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$ (via the exceptional isomorphism $\mathrm{Sp}_4\simeq \mathrm{Spin}(2,3)$), Bruinier's proposition asserts: the first Chern class
\[
 c_1\bigl(\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}|_{H_D}\bigr)
 \;\in\;
 \mathrm{CH}^1\bigl(H_D\bigr)
\]
of the line bundle restricted to a Heegner divisor $H_D$ is a torsion class of order equal to $(2/\varepsilon_D)\cdot\mathrm{lcm}(\text{denominators in local Fourier expansion})$, where $\varepsilon_D\in\{1,2\}$ depends on the parity of $D$.

For $H_1$ (discriminant $1$, so $\varepsilon_1=2$), the Fourier expansion of $\Delta_5$ near $H_1$ has denominator $8$ (Bruinier 2002, Ex.~5.3, computed from the singular theta correspondence for the weight-$5$ half-integral form), giving Chern-class order $\gcd$-free equal to $8$. For $H_4$ ($\varepsilon_4=1$), the analogous computation yields order~$16$.

The regular-singular holonomic $\mathcal{D}$-module $\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}$ is constructed via the Riemann--Hilbert correspondence from the locally constant sheaf of rank-one solutions of the Picard--Fuchs type equation satisfied by log$(\Delta_5)$; regular singularity along $H_D$ follows from $\Delta_5$ being a section of a line bundle in good position, and the local monodromy matrix around $H_D$ is multiplication by a root of unity of order equal to the Chern-class order of the restriction (Deligne, \emph{Équations différentielles à points singuliers réguliers}, Springer LNM~163, 1970, Thm.~II.1.9; Kashiwara, \emph{The Riemann--Hilbert problem for holonomic systems}, Publ.~RIMS~20, 1984). Therefore the monodromy orders are $8$ and $16$ on $H_1$ and $H_4$.

The Koszul-conductor identity $K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=2c_+(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3))=8$ is the Mukai-lattice computation of the Mukai-doubling section: Mukai$(K3)$ has signature $(4,20)$, positive part $c_+=4$, and the Koszul conductor on a CY-2 category doubles this to $K=8$ by the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-Serre-duality symmetrisation of the bar differential across the Mukai pairing (Theorem~CY-C$_2$, Section~\ref{sec:cy-complementarity-bridge}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Lusztig specialisation \texorpdfstring{$\ell=8$}{l=8} and the universal identity]
\label{prop:lusztig-l8-universal-identity}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The Lusztig specialisation at order $\ell=8$ of the Hall--Drinfeld double

 \mathcal{D}_\hbar\bigl(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{Hall}}_\hbar(\mathrm{CoHA}_{K3\times E})\bigr)

 \;=\;
 -1

\subsection{One identity, three faces}

\begin{remark}[The trinity of faces of the number \texorpdfstring{$8$}{8}]
\label{rem:trinity-of-eight}
The universal identity $\hbar^2\cdot K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=-1$ arises because the integer $8$ appears simultaneously in three a-priori unrelated mathematical settings.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{(Face 1, Mukai.)} $8 = 2c_+(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3))$ is the doubled positive-signature rank of the Mukai lattice of K3 (signature $(4,20)$, positive part $4$), arising from the CY-$2$ Serre-duality symmetrisation of the bar differential. This is the Koszul-conductor reading.
\item \emph{(Face 2, Bruinier.)} $8 = \mathrm{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{monodromy}\,\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}|_{H_1}\bigr)$ is the order of the local monodromy of the $\Delta_5$-automorphic $\mathcal{D}$-module on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$, equal by Bruinier's Proposition~5.1 to the order of the Chern class $c_1(\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}|_{H_1})\in\mathrm{CH}^1(H_1)$. This is the holonomic-$\mathcal{D}$-module reading.
\item \emph{(Face 3, Lusztig.)} $8 = \ell$ is the order of the root of unity at which the Hall--Drinfeld double $\mathcal{D}_\hbar(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{Hall}}_\hbar(\mathrm{CoHA}_{K3\times E}))$ specialises to the small quantum group $u_\zeta$. This is the quantum-group reading.
\end{itemize}
Bruinier's Chern-class reciprocity is the single categorical identity that makes Face~1 $\leftrightarrow$ Face~2, and Drinfeld's 1990 scaling makes Face~2 $\leftrightarrow$ Face~3. The bi-based Ran-space architecture of Chapter~\ref{ch:bar-cobar-bridge}, Section~\ref{sec:bar-cobar-bi-based-k3e}, is exactly the geometric substrate on which all three faces live together.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of universality]
\label{rem:universal-identity-scope}
The identity $\hbar^2\cdot K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=-1$ is established on the Lorentzian-lattice-parametric $\mathcal{B}$-family; its scope is determined by the hypothesis that the chiral algebra arises from a CY-$2$ category via $\Phi_2$ with Mukai lattice of fixed signature, and that the monodromy-order computation of Bruinier specialises to $8$ at the distinguished Heegner divisor (here $H_1$). For other lattice inputs the triplet (Mukai-$c_+$, Bruinier-monodromy-order, Lusztig-$\ell$) may shift in parallel, preserving the product identity. The scope is \emph{not} to be interpreted as ``universal across all chiral algebras''; it is ``universal across the $\mathcal{B}$-family of Mukai-lattice-parametric CY-$2$ inputs''. Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}, Theorem~\ref{thm:humbert-order-K-kappa}, records the precise scope.
\end{remark}
