Braided output is too coarse for the first questions of Vol~III. The CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ reaches its braided $E_2$ image only through that primitive step, so this chapter fixes the ordered conventions used in the rest of the volume.

\begin{remark}[$E_1$ primacy for CY quantum groups]
\label{rem:e1-primacy-vol3}
The $E_1$-chiral algebra (boundary) is the primitive object in this volume.
The $E_2$-chiral algebra (bulk) is obtained from it by the Drinfeld center
construction $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$.
Quantum groups, Yangians, and braided tensor categories are natively $E_1$
objects: the CoHA multiplication is ordered (short exact sequences have a
preferred direction), and the $R$-matrix arises only in the Drinfeld double.
The passage $E_1 \to E_2$ is the Drinfeld center: the right adjoint
to the forgetful functor from braided monoidal to monoidal categories,
categorifying the algebraic center $z(A) = \{a : ab = ba\;\forall b\}$.
It \emph{constructs} braiding from half-braiding data.
The distinct passage $E_1 \to E_\infty$ is the averaging map
$\mathrm{av} \colon \fg^{E_1} \to \fg^{\mathrm{mod}}$
from Vol~I, which is \emph{lossy}: $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ forgets the
$R$-matrix data. The $E_1$-bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ retains
strictly more information than the $E_\infty$-bar $B^{\Sigma}(A)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{warning}[Native $\En$ on $\Phi(\cC)$ vs.\ derived $\En$ on the centre]
\label{warn:native-vs-derived-en}
The native $\En$-level of the chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ depends
on the CY dimension $d$:
\[
 n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)
 \;=\;
 \begin{cases}
  \infty, & d = 1, \\
  2,      & d = 2, \\
  1,      & d \geq 3.
 \end{cases}
\]
At $d \geq 3$, $\Phi(\cC)$ is natively $E_1$. The braided $E_2$-structure
\emph{does not} live on $\Phi(\cC)$ itself; it lives on the
\emph{representation category} $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(\Phi(\cC)))$, which is
identified with $\Rep^{E_2}(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC)))$
by Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler
\textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}};
Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:drinfeld-center}}\textup{)}. Attributing
$E_2$-structure to $\Phi(\cC)$ at $d \geq 3$ is a category error
: the $E_2$-data is a property of
the derived chiral centre, not of the boundary algebra. The framing
on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ at $d = 3$ provides an $E_3$-structure that
restricts to \emph{symmetric} $E_2$-braiding under Dunn additivity
\textup{(}because $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\R^3)) = 0$\textup{);} the
non-symmetric quantum-group $R$-matrix arises only on the Drinfeld
centre of the representation category, not on the chiral algebra
itself.
\end{warning}

\section{Factorization algebras and the $E_n$ hierarchy}
\label{sec:factorization-en}

The first tension is immediate: a factorization algebra on a complex curve is topologically $E_2$, while the ordered bar and the Yangian are $E_1$. Holomorphy resolves the tension. Costello and Gwilliam~\cite{CostelloGwilliam} showed that the value on a small disk carries an $E_n$-algebra structure, and Lurie's universal statement is $\mathrm{Fact}(\bR^n) \simeq \Alg_{E_n}$. For a complex curve $C$ of complex dimension one, $C$ has real dimension two, so factorization starts as $E_2$ topologically before the holomorphic ordering picks an $E_1$ slice.

\begin{proposition}[Holomorphic refinement, Costello-Gwilliam]
\label{prop:holomorphic-e1}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cA$ be a factorization algebra on a smooth complex curve $C$ whose structure maps are holomorphic in the two-point configuration space. The associated $E_2$-algebra specializes to an $E_1$-algebra on the configuration space of ordered points along any real ray, with the ordering provided by the holomorphic argument.
\end{proposition}

The proof is operadic: the little two-disks operad contracts onto the little intervals operad along the real subspace, and holomorphy pins the contraction to a single slice. Beilinson and Drinfeld~\cite{BeilinsonDrinfeld} studied the symmetric version of this specialization; they called the outcome a \emph{chiral algebra}. Volume~II elevates the ordered version.

\begin{definition}[Swiss-cheese operad]
\label{def:swiss-cheese-split}
The Swiss-cheese operad $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$
of Volume~II is a \emph{two-coloured} operad with colours
$\{\mathrm{ch}, \mathrm{top}\}$ (closed/holomorphic and
open/topological). Its closed-to-closed operations are
parametrised by $\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C})$ (the $E_2$ operad);
its open-to-open operations by $E_1(\mathbb{R})$ (ordered
configurations); and its mixed operations (closed acting on
open) by $\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C}) \times E_1(m)$.
Open-to-closed operations are \emph{empty} (directionality
constraint). An $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$-algebra
is a pair $(A, M)$ where $A$ is an $E_2$-algebra (the closed
colour), $M$ is an $E_1$-algebra (the open colour), and $A$
acts on $M$ via the mixed operations.

$\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$ is \emph{not} the
tensor product $E_1 \otimes E_2$: the directionality constraint
(no open-to-closed) and the mixed operations make it a genuinely
two-coloured operad. Dunn additivity does not apply.
The $E_3$ upgrade requires a 3d
holomorphic-topological theory (proved for Kac--Moody
via holomorphic Chern--Simons; conjectural in general);
the resulting $E_3$ structure is \emph{topological}, not chiral
(the conformal vector at non-critical level kills the chiral
direction via Sugawara; Volume~I, the topologization theorem).
\end{definition}

The closed colour carries $E_2$ structure from
$\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C})$: holomorphic, braided, and the
home of the chiral algebra. The open colour carries $E_1$
structure from ordered real configurations: topological,
noncommutative, and the home of the ordered bar complex.
Standard Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral algebras are
$E_\infty$-chiral (symmetric OPE); the $E_1$ open colour
captures the additional ordering data. The
$\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum of this volume distributes across the two
colours: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is read from the open colour
(the ordered bar), $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ from the closed
colour (the Euler characteristic), and the Drinfeld center
exchanges them.

\begin{remark}[Level-prefixed $r$-matrix]
\label{rem:level-prefixed-r-matrix}
The classical $r$-matrix attached to an affine Kac-Moody $E_1$-chiral algebra at level $k$ is
\[
 r(z) = \frac{k\,\Omega}{z},
\]
not the level-stripped $\frac{\Omega}{z}$. The level survives the $d\log$ absorption because the ordered bar complex builds in one factor of the level per collision. At $k=0$ the $r$-matrix vanishes identically. The collision residue of the Heisenberg $r$-matrix is $k$, not $k/2$, and the monodromy of the $E_1$ representation category around a puncture is $\exp(-2\pi i k)$.
\end{remark}

The level-prefix convention is the standing convention throughout this volume. Every time an $r$-matrix is written in the CY-geometric setting, the level constant prefactor is the image of the trace under the CY-to-chiral correspondence and must be tracked explicitly.

\section{The ordered bar as the $E_1$ primitive}
\label{sec:ordered-bar-primitive}

Braiding forgets collision order. The ordered bar keeps it. Let $A$ be a chiral algebra on $C$ with augmentation $\varepsilon \colon A \to \Omega_C$ and augmentation ideal $\bar A = \ker(\varepsilon)$.

\begin{definition}[Ordered bar complex]
\label{def:ordered-bar}
The \emph{ordered bar complex} of $A$ is the cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra
\[
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) := T^c(s^{-1} \bar A) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} (s^{-1}\bar A)^{\otimes n}
\]
equipped with the \emph{deconcatenation} coproduct
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}(a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_k) \otimes (a_{k+1} \otimes \dots \otimes a_n)
\]
and the bar differential built from the chiral product of $A$.
\end{definition}

Deconcatenation is NOT the coshuffle coproduct on $\mathrm{Sym}^c$. Deconcatenation produces $n+1$ terms and respects the ordering; the coshuffle produces $2^n$ terms and destroys it. The symmetric bar complex $B^{\Sigma}(A)$ is the $S_n$-coinvariant quotient of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ with the coshuffle coproduct induced on invariants.

\begin{proposition}[Averaging map]
\label{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The averaging map
\[
 \mathrm{av} \colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) \longrightarrow B^{\Sigma}(A),
 \qquad a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_n \longmapsto \tfrac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} a_{\sigma(1)} \otimes \dots \otimes a_{\sigma(n)}
\]
is a morphism of cochain complexes and sends the $E_1$ structure to the $E_\infty$ structure. It is lossy: the kernel contains the $R$-matrix data of the holomorphic factor, and on degree two $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\end{proposition}

Volume~I establishes the map; Volume~II identifies it as the $E_1 \to E_\infty$ symmetrization. For Volume~III purposes, the two consequences that matter are: (a) Yangians and quantum groups live on the $E_1$ side and are quotiented by averaging; (b) the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma$ is sufficient for computing the modular characteristic but insufficient for reconstructing the $R$-matrix.

Averaging the degree-two generator $r(z)$ returns the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, the unique $S_2$-coinvariant of the collision residue. When the same $r(z)$ comes from the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_3$ applied to $D^b(\Coh(K3 \times E))$, the scalar is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ by additivity: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 2 + 1$. This differs from the categorical Euler characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E) = \chi(\cO_{K3}) \cdot \chi(\cO_E) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0$ by Künneth multiplicativity on the total space, from the lattice-rank invariant $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$, and from the BKM weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$. An unsubscripted symbol would conflate distinct invariants.

\begin{remark}[Three bars, one correspondence programme]
\label{rem:three-bars-vol3}
The CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral} has three natural composites at each $d$. $\Phi_{d,E_1}$ produces $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_C)$; this is the ordered bar sensed by the Yangian. $\mathrm{av} \circ \Phi_{d,E_1}$ produces $B^{\Sigma}(A_C)$; this is the symmetric bar sensed by the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The full $E_2$ enhancement $\Phi_{d,E_2}$ produces the Drinfeld-centered bar $B^{E_2}(A_C) = \cZ(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_C))$; this is the braided bar sensed by the quantum group. All three factor through $\Phi_{d,E_1}$. In this volume, the ordered bar is the primitive object.
\end{remark}

\section{$E_1$-chiral algebras from CY categories}
\label{sec:e1-from-cy}

The CY input has to produce an ordered chiral object before any braided output can exist. For a CY$_d$ category $\cC$ in the sense of Kontsevich-Soibelman, the trace lives in $\mathrm{HC}^-_d(\cC)$, not merely in Hochschild homology, and that trace feeds the ordered $E_1$ structure first.

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$ sector at $d=2$]
\label{prop:e1-sector-d2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_2$ category with cyclic $A_\infty$ structure and negative-cyclic trace. The ordered bar complex of the cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra $A_\cC$ carries a natural $E_1$-chiral structure whose holomorphic direction matches the Hochschild differential and whose ordered direction matches the cyclic action on the bar coalgebra.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cyclic $\Ainf$-structure on $\cC$ provides an associative product on the bar coalgebra $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_\cC)$ via OPE residues (Step~1 of the cyclic-to-chiral passage, Section~\ref{sec:cyclic-to-chiral} of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}). The holomorphic direction is the Hochschild differential $b \colon \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC) \to \mathrm{CC}_{\bullet-1}(\cC)$, which descends to the bar differential $d_{\mathrm{bar}}$ on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$ via the standard identification of the bar construction with the Hochschild chain complex. The ordered direction is the $S^1$-action on the cyclic bar complex: the Connes $B$-operator cyclically permutes the bar entries, and its restriction to the ordered bar preserves the deconcatenation coproduct. The $E_1$-chiral structure is the factorization algebra on $C$ obtained by the factorization envelope of the Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ (Step~2), with the negative-cyclic trace in $\mathrm{HC}^-_2(\cC)$ providing the quantization datum (Step~4). At $d=2$, no framing obstruction arises: the $\bS^2$-framing is automatic (Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos). The full construction is Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral} of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.
\end{proof}

For $\cC = D^b(\Coh(K3))$, the resulting K3 chiral algebra has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$. The Borcherds-side weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ belongs to the $K3 \times E$ lift developed in Part~\ref{part:examples} and must not be conflated with the K3 value.

\begin{theorem}[$E_1$ sector at $d=3$]
\label{thm:e1-sector-d3}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY$_3$ category. By Theorem~CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}), the chain-level $S^3$-framing on $\mathrm{HC}^-_3(\cC)$ exists. Then the ordered bar complex of the cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra $A_\cC = \Phi_3(\cC)$ carries an $E_1$-chiral structure whose representation category is a braided monoidal refinement of the BPS Yangian of $\cC$.
\end{theorem}

The chain-level framing is constructed by Theorem~CY-A$_3$. The downstream results in \ref{part:examples} and \ref{part:connections} that use the BPS Yangian rely on CY-A$_3$ at the inf-categorical level. The connection to Maulik-Okounkov and Costello-Witten cohomological Hall algebras is traced in Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}.

\begin{remark}[Five presentations of $E_1$-chiral algebra; four collapse on the Koszul locus]
\label{rem:five-e1-chiral-notions}
The phrase ``$E_1$-chiral algebra'' as used in this volume designates
five presentations. On the Koszul locus -- where every theorem of the
three-volume programme lives -- the first four collapse to a single
category of inputs by the collapse theorem of Vol~I (cited as
\texttt{thm:e1-chiral-notions-collapse}, \cite[\S Foundations]{VolI}):
notions $(A), (B), (C), (E)$ are mutually Quillen-equivalent via
Stasheff strict/$A_\infty$ truncation, Etingof--Kazhdan quantum vertex
algebra equivalence, and Beilinson--Drinfeld $\cD$-module realization.
Only $(D)$, the double-$A_\infty$ $(\infty,2)$-enhancement, remains
genuinely distinct, and its comparison to the collapsed category is
an open problem
(\texttt{conj:double-ainfty-notion-D-relation} in Vol~I). The five
presentations are retained here as technical inputs for specific
diagrammatic constructions within this volume; the mapping is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Alph*)}]
 \item \emph{Strict ChirAss-algebra.} An algebra over the operad
 $\mathrm{ChirAss}$ on a fixed curve $X$. Used in
 Definition~\ref{def:ordered-bar} and the bar discussion.
 \item \emph{$A_\infty$ in $\End^{\mathrm{ch}}_A$.} Homotopy-coherent
 chiral algebra in the chiral endomorphism operad. The CY-to-chiral
 correspondence $\Phi_d$ at $d \geq 3$ lands here when one tracks
 $A_\infty$-corrections from $\mu_n$ \textup{($n \geq 3$)}.
 \item \emph{EK quantum vertex algebra.} Etingof--Kazhdan quantum
 vertex algebra. Equivalent to (B) on the Koszul locus via the Drinfeld associator.
 \item \emph{$A_\infty$ in $E_1$-chiral.} Composite homotopy-coherent
 algebra in the chiral $E_1$-operad on the curve.
 \item \emph{Factorization on $\Ran^{\mathrm{ord}}(X)$.} Ordered
 factorization algebra. The output of the
 universal factorization envelope construction
 \textup{(}Step~$2$ of $\Phi$\textup{)}.
\end{enumerate}
The four Yangian variants \textup{(}classical $Y_\hbar(\frakg)$
on $\R$, dg-shifted $Y^{\mathrm{dg}}_\hbar$ at point/disk, chiral
$Y(\frakg)^{\mathrm{ch}}$ on a curve, spectral on $\A^1_u$\textup{)}
distribute across the five notions. The Vol~III $\Phi$ at $d = 3$
lands in (B) at the cochain level
\textup{(}via Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}\textup{)} and
projects to (E) under the factorization-envelope construction.
The categorical Drinfeld center
\textup{(}Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}\textup{)}
preserves notion (E) and produces an $E_2$-chiral algebra of the
same notion-class.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[$E_2$ enhancement via Drinfeld center]
\label{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
If $A$ is an $E_1$-chiral algebra with dualizable monoidal representation category $\Rep^{E_1}(A)$, the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ is a braided monoidal category equipped with an equivalence
\[
 \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))
\]
where $\mathrm{Drin}(A)$ is the $E_2$-chiral algebra obtained from the \emph{Drinfeld center} $\cZ \colon E_1\text{-Cat} \to E_2\text{-Cat}$, which is the right adjoint to the forgetful functor from braided monoidal to monoidal categories (categorifying the algebraic center $z(A) = \{a : ab = ba\;\forall b\}$, \emph{not} the averaging map $\mathrm{av} \colon E_1 \to E_\infty$ which destroys the quantum group data). This is a consequence of Lurie's identification of $E_2$-algebras with braided monoidal objects \cite{LurieHA} and the Drinfeld center construction of Etingof--Nikshych--Ostrik (see Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center} for the explicit half-braiding mechanism).
\end{proposition}

This proposition provides the pathway $\Phi_{E_1} \to \Phi_{E_2}$: one computes the $E_1$ chiral algebra first and then applies the Drinfeld center at the categorical level. At $d=2$ this produces the braided categories attached to the CY$_2$ output of $\Phi$; at $d=3$ this produces the braided categories via the $E_1$-chiral algebra of Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-sector-d3} (CY-A$_3$, proved).

\begin{remark}[Convolution $L_\infty$-algebra on the $E_1$ side]
\label{rem:convolution-linfty-e1}
For an $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ and a coalgebra $C$, the convolution $L_\infty$-algebra $\hom_\alpha(C, A)$ of Volume~I is the natural habitat of Maurer-Cartan elements controlling twisted morphisms. On the $E_1$ side, $\hom_\alpha$ is a strict dg Lie algebra when the ordering of $C$ is preserved (the strict model $\Conv_{\mathrm{str}}$), and a genuine $L_\infty$-algebra when higher brackets are included ($\Conv_\infty$). The MC moduli of the two models coincide, but the full $L_\infty$-structure is required for homotopy transfer, formality statements, and gauge equivalence. For Vol~III, the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ lands in the strict model $\Conv_{\mathrm{str}}$ because the cyclic $A_\infty$ structure on $A_\cC$ is strict at degree two; higher-degree corrections require the $L_\infty$ refinement.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with Beilinson-Drinfeld chiral algebras]
\label{rem:comparison-bd}
Beilinson and Drinfeld~\cite{BeilinsonDrinfeld} developed chiral algebras as a symmetric factorization formalism on the Ran space. Their chiral algebras lie on the $E_\infty$ side of the locality hierarchy, even when they carry OPE poles. The $E_1$-chiral algebras of this chapter are a strict refinement: they remember collision order. The averaging map $B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^{\Sigma}$ forgets that extra data and returns to the Beilinson-Drinfeld world. Vol~III's geometric output is ordered; its modular characteristic is symmetric.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$E_1$-chiral bialgebras versus $E_\infty$-vertex bialgebras]
\label{rem:e1-vs-einfty-bialgebras}
The $E_n$-hierarchy organises three bar complexes with three coalgebra
structures, three Hopf structures, and three levels of
quantum-group data. The comparison table is

\noindent\emph{What is lost at each level.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $E_1 \to E_2$: the labeled ordering is replaced by a braid-group
 action; half-braiding data (the choice of over- versus
 under-crossing) is forgotten.
 \item $E_2 \to E_\infty$: the braiding $\sigma$ becomes symmetric; the
 $R$-matrix collapses to the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
 Every non-abelian quantum group datum is lost.
 \item $E_1 \to E_\infty$ (direct, via $\mathrm{av}$): the full
 $R$-matrix, the Hopf structure, and the Drinfeld coproduct all
 vanish. What survives is the modular characteristic
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the shadow tower.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Why Vol~III works at $E_1$.}
The entire quantum group programme (Yangians $Y(\fg)$, quantum
toroidal algebras $U_{q,t}(\ddot{\fgl}_1)$, Kazhdan--Lusztig
categories $\Rep_q(\fg)$ at roots of unity) lives natively at $E_1$.
The passage to $E_2$ is via the Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}); the passage to
$E_\infty$ forgets the quantum group entirely and returns to the
classical vertex-algebraic world of Beilinson-Drinfeld.
Li's vertex-bialgebra framework is the $E_\infty$ shadow of the
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra: it retains the vertex-algebraic OPE structure
but symmetrizes the coproduct, losing the $R$-matrix that distinguishes
$U_q(\fg)$ from $U(\fg)$. The chiral quantum group is the $E_1$
substance; the vertex bialgebra is the $E_\infty$ silhouette.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Lossiness of the $E_1 \to E_\infty$ forgetful functor on bialgebras]
\label{prop:forgetful-e1-einfty-bialgebra}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, R(z))$ be an $E_1$-chiral bialgebra with
$R$-matrix $R(z)$ and Drinfeld coproduct $\Delta_z$. The forgetful
functor
\[
 \mathrm{Forget}_{E_1 \to E_\infty} \colon
 E_1\text{-}\mathrm{ChirBialg} \longrightarrow
 E_\infty\text{-}\mathrm{VtxBialg}
\]
sends $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, R(z))$ to the Li vertex bialgebra
$(A, \mu, \mathrm{av}(\Delta_z), 1)$ with \emph{trivial} braiding. In
particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item The image has symmetric coproduct:
 $\mathrm{av}(\Delta_z) = \tau \circ \mathrm{av}(\Delta_z)$.
 \item The $R$-matrix reduces to the scalar
 $\mathrm{av}(R(z))|_{z=0} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \mathrm{id}$.
 \item The braiding is \emph{not} recoverable from the
 $E_\infty$ image. It is recovered precisely by the Drinfeld center:
 $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Claim~(a): the averaging map $\mathrm{av} \colon B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to
B^\Sigma$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}) is
$S_n$-coinvariant projection, so the image is $S_n$-invariant by
construction; in particular the coproduct is symmetric.
Claim~(b): the degree-two component of $\mathrm{av}$ sends
$r(z) = R_{12}(z)$ to its $S_2$-coinvariant, which is the scalar
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (loc.\ cit.).
Claim~(c): the fiber of $\mathrm{av}$ in degree two is
$\ker(\mathrm{av}|_2) = \{f(z) : f(z) + f(-z) = 0\}$, the space of
antisymmetric collision data. The $R$-matrix lives in that kernel
(it satisfies $R_{12}(z) = -R_{21}(-z)$ by the Yang-Baxter skew
symmetry). Hence $R(z)$ is annihilated and cannot be recovered from
the $E_\infty$ image. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}, the Drinfeld center
reconstructs the braiding from the monoidal structure of
$\Rep^{E_1}(A)$; this is the unique functorial recovery.
\end{proof}

\section{Operadic dictionary for the CY reader}
\label{sec:operadic-dictionary}

The CY-geometric programme uses operadic language pervasively but employs it in service of concrete objects: bar complexes, $R$-matrices, and coproducts.  For the reader approaching from algebraic geometry rather than homotopy theory, the following dictionary translates every operadic term that appears in this volume into its CY-geometric instantiation.  The dictionary is organized by decreasing operadic level ($E_\infty$ to $E_1$), since the CY correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ naturally lands at $E_1$ and all higher structures are derived from it.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$E_n$}: little $n$-disks operad. $E_1$ is the operad of intervals on the line; $E_2$ is the operad of disks in the plane; $E_\infty$ is the colimit. $E_1$ algebras are ordered and associative. $E_2$ algebras are partially commutative and braided. $E_\infty$ algebras are symmetric up to all higher homotopies; ordinary chiral algebras live here and still admit OPE poles. Dunn additivity: $E_m \otimes E_n \simeq E_{m+n}$.
\item \textbf{Ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$}: the cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra $T^c(s^{-1}\bar A)$ with deconcatenation coproduct. Retains degree ordering. Natural $E_1$-object. Source of Yangians and quantum groups.
\item \textbf{Symmetric bar $B^{\Sigma}$}: the $S_n$-coinvariant quotient of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ with the coshuffle coproduct descended from the shuffle product on $T^c$. Natural $E_\infty$-object. Source of the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\item \textbf{Harrison bar $B^{\mathrm{Lie}}$}: the Harrison complex; Lie-coalgebra structure via the iterated commutator. Dual to the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the associated Lie algebra. Smallest of the three natural bar complexes on any commutative input.
\item \textbf{Deconcatenation}: coproduct on $T^c$ that splits a word into a prefix and a suffix. Produces $n+1$ terms in degree $n$. Respects ordering. The $E_1$ coproduct.
\item \textbf{Coshuffle}: coproduct on $\mathrm{Sym}^c$ dual to the shuffle product. Produces up to $2^n$ terms. Destroys ordering. The $E_\infty$ coproduct.
\item \textbf{Swiss-cheese $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$}: two-coloured operad with a closed (holomorphic, $E_2$) sector and an open (topological, $E_1$) sector, plus mixed operations (closed acting on open; no open-to-closed). It is NOT a tensor product $E_1 \otimes E_2$; Dunn additivity does not apply to coloured operads.
\item \textbf{Drinfeld center $\cZ$}: endofunctor on monoidal categories that sends a monoidal category $\cM$ to the category of objects equipped with a half-braiding against every object of $\cM$. The result is braided monoidal. Categorified center (right adjoint to the forgetful functor $E_2\text{-Cat} \to E_1\text{-Cat}$, NOT categorified averaging): $\cZ$ takes $E_1$-categories to $E_2$-categories by constructing half-braidings, not by symmetrizing.
\item \textbf{$r$-matrix}: the degree-two generator of the $E_1$ Koszul duality, carrying a pole along the diagonal. At level $k$, the classical Kac-Moody $r$-matrix is $r(z) = \frac{k\,\Omega}{z}$ and vanishes at $k=0$.
\end{itemize}

The dictionary is not symmetric in $E_1$ and $E_\infty$: the ordered bar is the primitive, and everything on the $E_\infty$ side is a quotient. The CY-geometric correspondence $\Phi_d$ lands primitively in the $E_1$ layer; the $E_\infty$ and $E_2$ images are obtained by composing with averaging and Drinfeld center respectively.

\begin{remark}[Notation consistency across the three volumes]
\label{rem:notation-consistency}
The three volumes use different coalgebra conventions in their displayed formulas. Vol~I displays formulas in $B^{\Sigma}$ with the symmetric coproduct; Vol~II displays formulas in $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ with deconcatenation; Vol~III displays formulas in whichever form the CY functor produces, which is always $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ at the source but frequently $B^{\Sigma}$ after averaging to extract $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The reader who cross-references a formula between volumes must convert between the three coalgebra structures: $B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^{\Sigma}$ by averaging (dividing by $n!$ and symmetrizing), and $B^{\Sigma} \to B^{\mathrm{Lie}}$ by taking the iterated commutator of the cofree tensor coalgebra. The three bars are NOT isomorphic even as complexes; they differ by $S_n$-coinvariant quotients, and the Euler characters diverge accordingly. See Vol~II for the three-bar sequence.
\end{remark}

\section{$E_1$-chiral Koszul duality: three standard families}
\label{sec:e1-koszul-three-families}

Volume~II proves $E_1$-chiral Koszul duality: the bar-cobar adjunction
$B^{\mathrm{ord}} \dashv \Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}$ is a Quillen equivalence
on the Koszul locus, and the Koszul dual algebra $A^!$ satisfies
$\Rep^{E_1}(A) \simeq \Rep^{E_1}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ as monoidal
categories. The Koszul conductor $\rho_K(A)$ is the real number such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koszul-conductor}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K(A).
\end{equation}
The conductor $\rho_K$ is a family invariant: it depends on the
isomorphism class of the shadow tower, not on the particular algebra
within the family. This section computes $A^!$, $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$, and
$\rho_K$ explicitly for the three standard families that span the G/L/M
classification: Heisenberg (class~G, $\rho_K = 0$), affine Kac--Moody
$V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (class~L, $\rho_K = 0$), and Virasoro $\Vir_c$
(class~M, $\rho_K = 13$).

\subsection{Family I: Heisenberg $H_k$ (class~G)}
\label{subsec:koszul-heisenberg}

The rank-one Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_k$ at level $k \neq 0$ has a
single strong generator $J$ of conformal weight~$1$, with OPE
$J(z)J(w) \sim k/(z-w)^2$ and no nonlinear terms. The augmentation ideal
$\bar{H}_k = \langle J, \partial J, \partial^2 J, \ldots \rangle$ is
spanned by all derivatives, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) = k$.

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of the Heisenberg]
\label{prop:koszul-heisenberg}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $H_k$ is
$H_k^! = (\mathrm{Sym}^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*),\, m_0 = -k\omega)$,
the curved commutative chiral algebra on the dual generator space
(Vol~I, \S\ref{subsec:koszul-heisenberg};
\emph{not} $H_{-k}$, which has the same $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = -k$
but is a Heisenberg Lie-type algebra, not commutative).
The ordered bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)$ has trivial differential
at all degrees, and the Koszul conductor is $\rho_K = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bar differential $d_{\mathrm{bar}}$ on
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n(H_k) = (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes n}$ acts by
summing adjacent OPE multiplications:
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[a_1|\cdots|a_n] = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \pm
 [a_1|\cdots|\mu(a_i, a_{i+1})|\cdots|a_n].
\]
For the Heisenberg, $\mu(J, J) = 0$ (the singular OPE $J(z)J(w) \sim
k/(z-w)^2$ contributes to the metric/pairing, not to the multiplication
$\mu$: the OPE has no first-order pole, so the normally-ordered product
$:JJ:$ has no correction from the singular part). Consequently
$d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0$ at all degrees. The bar complex is exact in the
trivial sense: $H^*(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k)) = B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k)$ as
a graded vector space.

The Koszul dual is then read from the linear dual of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$:
the Verdier dual $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k))$ is a
cocommutative coalgebra on $(s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^*$ (cocommutative because
the double-pole OPE produces symmetric coproducts). By Milnor--Moore,
the cobar is the chiral symmetric algebra
$H_k^! = \mathrm{Sym}^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*)$, equipped with the curvature
$m_0 = -k\omega$ encoding the level. The dual generator $J^*$ has
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k^!) = -k$ (the same numerical value as
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{-k})$, but $H_k^!$ is commutative while
$H_{-k}$ is Lie-type; see Vol~I, \S\ref{subsec:koszul-heisenberg}).

The conductor computation (for the Heisenberg/free-field family, $\rho_K = 0$):
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k^!) = k + (-k) = 0
 = \rho_K.
\]
\end{proof}

The explicit bar complex at low degrees is:
\begin{align}
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1(H_k) &= s^{-1}\bar{H}_k =
 \langle [J], [\partial J], [\partial^2 J], \ldots \rangle,
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-1} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2(H_k) &= (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes 2} =
 \langle [J|J], [J|\partial J], [\partial J|J], \ldots \rangle,
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0,
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-2} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_3(H_k) &= (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes 3} =
 \langle [J|J|J], [J|J|\partial J], \ldots \rangle,
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0.
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-3}
\end{align}
The dimension at degree $n$ (truncated to conformal weight $\leq N$)
is $\dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n = p(N)^n$ where $p(N)$ is the partition
function (one basis vector per derivative $\partial^m J$, $m \geq 0$,
organized by conformal weight $m+1$). The generating function
$\sum_n \dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n \cdot q^n$ at fixed weight truncation
is a geometric series in $p(N)$, reflecting the triviality of the bar
differential (class~G: the shadow tower terminates at depth~$2$).

\begin{remark}[Koszul dual is NOT $H_{-k}$]
\label{rem:heisenberg-koszul-not-level-inversion}
The Koszul dual $H_k^! = \mathrm{Sym}^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*)$ has the same
numerical $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = -k$ as $H_{-k}$, but the two algebras
are \emph{distinct}: $H_k^!$ is commutative (chiral symmetric algebra on
dual generators), while $H_{-k}$ is a Heisenberg Lie-type algebra with
noncommutative OPE. The coincidence of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-values does
not imply isomorphism. At general $k$, the parameter inversion
$(h_1, h_2, h_3) \to (-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$ of the $\Omega$-background
sends $k \to -k$ and is compatible with the Koszul conductor
$\rho_K = 0$, but the geometric meaning of the duality is the passage
from Lie-type to commutative-type, not level inversion within the
Heisenberg family.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Family II: Kac--Moody $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (class~L)}
\label{subsec:koszul-kac-moody}

The Heisenberg is the trivial case: vanishing bar differential, trivial shadow tower, class~$G$.  The first nontrivial example is the affine Kac--Moody algebra, where the nonvanishing first-order pole in the $e(z)f(w)$ OPE produces a nonzero bar differential and promotes the shadow class from $G$ to $L$.

The affine Kac--Moody vertex algebra $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at
non-critical level $k \neq -2$ has three strong generators
$\{e, f, h\}$ of conformal weight~$1$ with OPE:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl2-ope}
 h(z)h(w) \sim \frac{2k}{(z-w)^2}, \quad
 h(z)e(w) \sim \frac{2e(w)}{z-w}, \quad
 e(z)f(w) \sim \frac{k}{(z-w)^2} + \frac{h(w)}{z-w}.
\end{equation}
The critical fact is the \emph{first-order pole} in $e(z)f(w)$: the
OPE multiplication $\mu(e, f) = h$ is nontrivial, so the bar
differential is nonzero. The modular characteristic is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) = 3(k+2)/4$ (from the
general formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{g})) =
\dim(\mathfrak{g})(k + h^\vee)/(2h^\vee)$ with $\dim(\mathfrak{sl}_2) = 3$,
$h^\vee = 2$).

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$]
\label{prop:koszul-kac-moody}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at non-critical
level $k \neq -2$ is $V_{k'}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at the Feigin--Frenkel
dual level $k' = -k - 2h^\vee = -k - 4$. The Koszul conductor is
$\rho_K = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument has three steps.

\emph{Step 1: Bar differential.}
The ordered bar complex has $B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) =
(s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes n}$, where $\bar{V}_k$ is spanned by
$\{e, f, h, \partial e, \partial f, \partial h, \ldots\}$. The bar
differential is
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[a_1|\cdots|a_n] = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \pm
 [a_1|\cdots|\mu(a_i, a_{i+1})|\cdots|a_n]
\]
where $\mu$ extracts the first-order pole of the OPE. At degree~$2$:
\begin{align*}
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|f] &= +[h], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|e] = -[h], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|e] &= +[2e], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|h] = -[2e], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|f] &= -[2f], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|h] = +[2f], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|e] &= 0, \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|f] = 0, \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|h] = 0.
\end{align*}
Generators $[e|e]$, $[f|f]$, $[h|h]$ are bar cycles (diagonal terms have
no first-order pole), while off-diagonal terms produce nontrivial
boundaries. The bar cohomology
$H^1(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(V_k)) = \bar{V}_k$ (the generators) and the image
of $d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1$
is $3$-dimensional (spanned by $h$, $2e$, $2f$).

\emph{Step 2: Koszul dual OPE.}
The Verdier dual of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2))$ produces
dual generators $\{e^*, f^*, h^*\}$ with the \emph{same}
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ structure but inverted level. The key identity is the
Feigin--Frenkel duality: the functor
$V_k(\mathfrak{g}) \mapsto V_{-k-2h^\vee}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the chiral
level reflection, and its operadic implementation is exactly $E_1$-Koszul
duality applied to the bar complex. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$:
\[
 k' = -k - 2h^\vee = -k - 4.
\]
The dual generators satisfy $e^*(z)f^*(w) \sim k'/(z-w)^2 + h^*(w)/(z-w)$
with $k' = -k-4$, reproducing the $V_{k'}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ OPE.

\emph{Step 3: Conductor.}
\begin{align*}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'})
 &= \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(k'+2)}{4}
 = \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(-k-4+2)}{4} \\
 &= \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(-k-2)}{4}
 = \frac{3(k+2) - 3(k+2)}{4} = 0 = \rho_K.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

The low-degree bar complex is:
\begin{align}
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1 &= s^{-1}\bar{V}_k =
 \langle [e], [f], [h], [\partial e], [\partial f], [\partial h],
 \ldots \rangle, \notag \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 &= (s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes 2},
 \quad \dim = 9 \text{ at weight $1$},
 \label{eq:sl2-bar-2} \\
 & \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}}([e|f]) = [h], \;
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}([h|e]) = [2e], \;
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}([h|f]) = [-2f], \notag \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_3 &= (s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes 3},
 \quad \dim = 27 \text{ at weight $1$}.
 \label{eq:sl2-bar-3}
\end{align}
At degree~$3$ the bar differential has two summands (positions $i=1,2$),
and the bar cohomology carries the first nontrivial $A_\infty$ operation
$m_3$ of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. The nonvanishing of $m_3$ is equivalent
to the non-formality of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ as a dg Lie algebra
and places $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ in shadow class~L (depth~$2$ tower
with nontrivial $m_3$).

\begin{remark}[Critical level $k = -2$]
\label{rem:critical-level}
At $k = -2$ the modular characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{-2}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) = 0$ and the Koszul dual
level is $k' = -(-2) - 4 = -2$: the critical level is a fixed point of
the Feigin--Frenkel involution. The critical-level vertex algebra
$V_{-2}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is the home of geometric Langlands (see
Chapter~\ref{ch:geometric-langlands}), and the self-duality under $E_1$
Koszul reflects the Langlands self-duality of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[General $\mathfrak{g}$]
\label{rem:general-g-koszul}
For a simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ with dual Coxeter number
$h^\vee$, the same argument gives $V_k(\mathfrak{g})^! =
V_{-k-2h^\vee}(\mathfrak{g})$ with conductor
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'}) =
 \frac{\dim\mathfrak{g}}{2h^\vee}\bigl[(k + h^\vee) +
 (-k - 2h^\vee + h^\vee)\bigr] = 0.
\]
The vanishing $\rho_K = 0$ is universal for the Kac--Moody family.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Family III: Virasoro $\Vir_c$ (class~M)}
\label{subsec:koszul-virasoro}

The Virasoro vertex algebra $\Vir_c$ at central charge $c$ has a single
strong generator $T$ (the stress-energy tensor) of conformal weight~$2$
with OPE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-ope}
 T(z)T(w) \sim \frac{c/2}{(z-w)^4} + \frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}
 + \frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}.
\end{equation}
The first-order pole gives $\mu(T, T) = \partial T$ (a nontrivial
multiplication), the second-order pole gives the conformal weight
assignment (Sugawara), and the fourth-order pole gives the central
extension. The modular characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) = c/2$
(from $\dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n \sim p(n)$ and the Volume~I shadow
formula).

The Koszul duality of the Virasoro differs from the Heisenberg and
Kac--Moody families in a fundamental way: the conductor is
\emph{nonzero}.

\begin{conjecture}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of the Virasoro]
\label{conj:koszul-virasoro}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $\Vir_c$ is $\Vir_{c'}$ at central charge
$c' = 26 - c$. The Koszul conductor is $\rho_K = 13$:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{c'})
 = \frac{c}{2} + \frac{26 - c}{2} = 13 = \rho_K.
\]
At the self-dual point $c = 13$ (the unique fixed point of $c \mapsto 26-c$),
$\Vir_{13}$ is Koszul self-dual under $c \mapsto 26-c$.
At $c = 26$ the dual is $\Vir_0$, and at $c = 0$ the dual is
$\Vir_{26}$ (the bosonic string).
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture status is necessary because the full $E_1$-chiral
Koszul duality for the Virasoro requires controlling the bar differential
to all degrees, and the infinite shadow depth (class~M) means the tower
never terminates. The evidence is:

\begin{remark}[Evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:koszul-virasoro}]
\label{rem:koszul-virasoro-evidence}
\emph{(i) Bar differential at degree~$2$.}
Using the first-order pole $\mu(T, T) = \partial T$:
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T] = [\partial T].
\]
The image $\mathrm{im}(d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to
B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1)$ is $1$-dimensional (spanned by $[\partial T]$).
The bar cycle $[T|T] - [T|T]$ is trivial, so
$\ker(d_{\mathrm{bar}}|_{B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1}) =
\langle [T|T] + [T|T] \rangle_{/\mathrm{im}}$ is
computed by the commutator $[T|T] - [T|T] = 0$ (the ordered bar is
noncommutative; only the symmetric $[T|T] + [T|T]$ survives modulo $d$).

\emph{(ii) Bar differential at degree~$3$.}
$d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T|T] = [\partial T|T] \pm [T|\partial T]$
(two summands from positions $i = 1, 2$). The sign is determined by the
Koszul convention (desuspension shifts degree by~$-1$):
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T|T] = [\partial T|T] - [T|\partial T].
\]
This is a nonzero cycle in $B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2$ (its image under
$d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1$ is
$[\partial^2 T] - [\partial^2 T] = 0$, a cycle), and it
represents the $A_\infty$ operation $m_3(T, T, T)$. The nonvanishing of
this class at all orders is the hallmark of class~M (infinite shadow
depth).

\emph{(iii) Conductor from the Sugawara construction.}
The Virasoro at central charge $c$ embeds into the Heisenberg via
Sugawara: $T = :JJ:/(2k)$ at $c = 1$. Under Koszul duality, the level
inverts ($k \to -k$), so $c \to c'$ with $c + c' = 26$ determined by the
ghost central charge of the bosonic string ($bc$-ghost system contributes
$c = -26$, so the BRST-balanced point is $c + c_{\mathrm{ghost}} = 26$;
equivalently, $c' = 26 - c$ is the unique value such that
$\Vir_c \otimes \Vir_{c'}$ has total $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 13$, the value
at which the Virasoro shadow tower of Vol~I attains
its self-dual fixed point).

\emph{(iv) Kappa verification.}
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) = c/2$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{26-c}) = (26-c)/2$, so
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{26-c})
 = \frac{c}{2} + \frac{26-c}{2} = 13 = \rho_K.
\]
The conductor $\rho_K = 13$ is nonzero, reflecting the nontrivial
conformal anomaly of the Virasoro. Compare with the Heisenberg
($\rho_K = 0$) and Kac--Moody ($\rho_K = 0$) families.
\end{remark}

The low-degree bar complex:

\section{$E_1$-chiral bialgebras}
\label{sec:e1-chiral-bialgebras}

The previous sections established the $E_1$-chiral algebra (product),
the ordered bar coalgebra (coproduct), and the averaging map
(symmetrization). This section assembles them into a single object: an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra. The definition does not exist in the literature.
Classical Hopf algebras live in symmetric monoidal categories;
Drinfeld's quasi-Hopf algebras relax the coassociativity but remain
in the same ambient category; Li's vertex bialgebras use
the $E_\infty$-chiral tensor product and the coshuffle coproduct, which
destroys the quantum group $R$-matrix (Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}).
The correct categorical home for the chiral quantum group is an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra: a bialgebra object in the $E_1$~sector of the
Swiss-cheese operad $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$.

\subsection{The $E_1$-chiral monoidal category}
\label{subsec:e1-chiral-monoidal}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral monoidal category]
\label{def:e1-chiral-monoidal}
Fix a smooth complex curve $C$. The \emph{$E_1$-chiral monoidal category}
$(\cM^{E_1}_C, \otimes_{E_1}, \mathbf{1})$ has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Objects}: $E_1$-chiral algebras on $C$ in the sense of the
 open colour of $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$
 (Definition~\ref{def:swiss-cheese-split}). These are factorization
 algebras on the configuration space $\Conf_n(\mathbb{R}) \subset \Conf_n(C)$
 of ordered real points along a ray in $C$.
 \item \emph{Tensor product}: for $E_1$-chiral algebras $A$ and $B$,
 the \emph{ordered fusion product}
 \[
  A \otimes_{E_1} B := \mathrm{colim}_{z_1 < z_2} A(z_1) \otimes B(z_2),
 \]
 taken over the configuration of two ordered real points in $C$. This is
 NOT symmetric: $A \otimes_{E_1} B \not\simeq B \otimes_{E_1} A$ in
 general. The ordering is fixed by the holomorphic argument
 (Proposition~\ref{prop:holomorphic-e1}).
 \item \emph{Unit}: $\mathbf{1} = \Omega_C$, the augmentation target.
 The augmentation $\varepsilon \colon A \to \Omega_C$ is the counit of
 the $E_1$-chiral monoidal structure.
 \item \emph{Associator}: the ordered fusion is strictly associative:
 $(A \otimes_{E_1} B) \otimes_{E_1} C \simeq A \otimes_{E_1} (B \otimes_{E_1} C)$
 via the identification $\Conf_3^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathbb{R}) \simeq \{z_1 < z_2 < z_3\}$,
 which is contractible. This is the $E_1$ associahedron collapsing to
 the linear order.
\end{enumerate}
The category $\cM^{E_1}_C$ is monoidal but NOT braided: the $E_1$-operad
has no braiding operations. This is the essential distinction from both
the Beilinson--Drinfeld symmetric ($E_\infty$) setting and the $E_2$-braided
setting.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Algebra and coalgebra objects}
\label{subsec:e1-alg-coalg}

An $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ is an algebra object in $\cM^{E_1}_C$:
a product $\mu \colon A \otimes_{E_1} A \to A$
(the OPE with ordered collision: the field at $z_1 < z_2$ collides
onto the field at $z_2$ from the left, producing residues that differ
from the $z_2 < z_1$ collision by the $R$-matrix). The coalgebra side
is the ordered bar complex.

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral coalgebra]
\label{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}
An \emph{$E_1$-chiral coalgebra} in $\cM^{E_1}_C$ is a coaugmented
coassociative coalgebra $(B, \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}, \eta)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $B = T^c(V)$ is a cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra on a graded
 vector space $V$;
 \item The coproduct is the \emph{deconcatenation} coproduct
 (Definition~\ref{def:ordered-bar}):
 \[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}(v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n)
  = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_k)
  \otimes (v_{k+1} \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n);
 \]
 \item The coaugmentation $\eta \colon k \to B$ includes the
 degree-zero summand.
\end{enumerate}
The canonical example is $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$
for an augmented $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra axiom}
\label{subsec:e1-bialgebra-axiom}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral bialgebra]
\label{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}
An \emph{$E_1$-chiral bialgebra} on a smooth curve $C$ is
a tuple $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $(A, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an augmented $E_1$-chiral algebra in
 $\cM^{E_1}_C$ (the product is the ordered OPE);
 \item For each $z \in \Ran(C)$, $(A, \Delta_z, \eta)$ is an
 $E_1$-chiral coalgebra in the sense of
 Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}, where
 \[
  \Delta_z \colon A \longrightarrow A \otimes_{E_1, z} A
 \]
 is a family of coproducts parametrized by $z$, with
 $\otimes_{E_1, z}$ denoting the ordered tensor product at collision
 point~$z$. At $z = 0$, the coproduct $\Delta_0$ coincides with
 the deconcatenation coproduct on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$; the
 $z$-deformation encodes the spectral data;
 \item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility}: $\Delta_z$ is a morphism of
 $E_1$-chiral algebras, i.e.\ the diagram
 \[
 \begin{tikzcd}
  A \otimes_{E_1} A \ar[r, "\mu"] \ar[d, "\Delta_z \otimes \Delta_z"']
  & A \ar[d, "\Delta_z"] \\
  (A \otimes_{E_1,z} A) \otimes_{E_1} (A \otimes_{E_1,z} A)
  \ar[r, "\mu \otimes \mu"']
  & A \otimes_{E_1,z} A
 \end{tikzcd}
 \]
 commutes, where the bottom-left corner uses the interchange law
 for the $E_1$-monoidal structure;
 \item \emph{Spectral coassociativity}: for $z, w \in \Ran(C)$ in
 general position, the coproduct satisfies
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-coassociativity}
  (\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
 \end{equation}
 At $z = w = 0$ this reduces to ordinary coassociativity
 $(\Delta_0 \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_0) \circ \Delta_0$.
 For generic $z, w$ the identity encodes the Yang--Baxter
 equation: the $R$-matrix extracted from $\Delta_z$ satisfies
 YBE if and only if spectral coassociativity holds
 (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:r-matrix-from-drinfeld}).
\end{enumerate}
The compatibility axiom (iii) is the $E_1$-chiral analogue of the
classical bialgebra axiom ``$\Delta$ is an algebra map.'' The spectral
coassociativity axiom~(iv) is the chiral analogue of the identity
$(\Delta \otimes 1) \circ \Delta = (1 \otimes \Delta) \circ \Delta$,
promoted to a family over the spectral parameter. It replaces
Drinfeld's quasi-coassociativity (which uses an associator $\Phi$ to
correct the failure of coassociativity in the symmetric monoidal setting):
in the $E_1$-chiral setting, coassociativity holds on the nose, but
with shifted spectral parameters. The parametrization
by $z \in \Ran(C)$ is essential: the coproduct depends on the collision
point, and the $z$-dependence carries the $R$-matrix data.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral coassociativity from factorization]
\label{prop:spectral-coassociativity-factorization}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta)$ be an $E_1$-chiral
bialgebra on a smooth curve~$C$ in the sense of
Definition~\textup{\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}}.
Then the spectral coassociativity
axiom~\textup{(iv)}~\eqref{eq:spectral-coassociativity} is a
consequence of the factorization property of $A$ viewed as a
factorization algebra on $\Ran(C)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument is purely operadic; no mode computation is needed.

\emph{Step~1: coproduct as factorization isomorphism.}
The $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ is, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:holomorphic-e1}, a factorization algebra on
the ordered configuration space of~$C$.  For disjoint finite
subsets $S_1, S_2 \subset C$ with $\Re(S_1) < \Re(S_2)$, the
factorization structure provides a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \phi_{S_1,S_2} \colon A(S_1 \sqcup S_2)
  \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} A(S_1) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_2).
\]
The spectral coproduct $\Delta_z$ is the specialization of
$\phi_{S_1,S_2}$ to the two-point configuration
$S_1 = \{p\}$, $S_2 = \{p + z\}$, where $z$ records the
relative position along the ordered ray.

\emph{Step~2: triple factorization and the two
bracketings.}  For three disjoint subsets $S_1, S_2, S_3$ with
$\Re(S_1) < \Re(S_2) < \Re(S_3)$, the factorization property
gives a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \phi_{S_1,S_2,S_3} \colon A(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)
  \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
  A(S_1) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_2) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_3).
\]
This triple isomorphism factors through two binary compositions:
\begin{align}
  \text{(L):}\quad
  &A(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1, S_2 \sqcup S_3}}
   A(S_1) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id} \otimes \phi_{S_2,S_3}}
   A(S_1) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_2) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_3),
   \label{eq:fact-left} \\[4pt]
  \text{(R):}\quad
  &A(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1 \sqcup S_2, S_3}}
   A(S_1 \sqcup S_2) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1,S_2} \otimes \mathrm{id}}
   A(S_1) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_2) \otimes_{E_1} A(S_3).
   \label{eq:fact-right}
\end{align}

\emph{Step~3: strict associativity of the $E_1$-monoidal
structure.}  The configuration space
$\Conf_3^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathbb{R}) = \{z_1 < z_2 < z_3\}$ is
contractible (it is an open cone).  By
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-monoidal}(iv), the $E_1$-monoidal
category $\cM^{E_1}_C$ is therefore strictly associative: the
two bracketings~\eqref{eq:fact-left} and~\eqref{eq:fact-right}
coincide as maps from $A(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)$ to the
triple tensor product.  This is the $E_1$ associahedron
collapsing to the point: the Stasheff polytope $K_3$ is a single
vertex for linear orders, so there is no associator to insert.

\emph{Step~4: translation to spectral parameters.}
Specialize to $S_1 = \{p\}$, $S_2 = \{p + w\}$,
$S_3 = \{p + w + z\}$, so that $w$ is the separation between
$S_1$ and $S_2$ and $z$ is the separation between $S_2$ and
$S_3$.  The total separation between $S_1$ and $S_3$ is $z+w$.
Then:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Path~(L) computes
  $(\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w$:
  first split at parameter~$w$ to separate $S_1$ from
  $S_2 \sqcup S_3$, then split $S_2 \sqcup S_3$ at
  parameter~$z$.
  \item Path~(R) computes
  $(\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}$:
  first split at parameter~$z+w$ to separate
  $S_1 \sqcup S_2$ from $S_3$, then split
  $S_1 \sqcup S_2$ at parameter~$w$.
\end{itemize}
The equality (L)\,=\,(R), which is Step~3, is precisely the
spectral coassociativity identity
\[
  (\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
  \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is stronger than formal coassociativity]
\label{rem:operadic-vs-formal-coassociativity}
In the symmetric ($E_\infty$) setting of Beilinson--Drinfeld, the
factorization isomorphism is also associative, but the coproduct
carries no spectral parameter and the resulting coassociativity is
the classical identity $(\Delta \otimes 1)\Delta = (1 \otimes \Delta)\Delta$.
In the quasi-Hopf ($E_0$) setting of Drinfeld, the configuration
space $\Conf_3$ is NOT contractible after quotienting by the
symmetric group, so the two paths (L) and (R) differ by an
associator~$\Phi$.
The $E_1$ setting is intermediate: the ordered configuration space
IS contractible (so no associator), but the collision parameters are
retained (so the identity is spectral).  This is the operadic reason
that the $E_1$-chiral bialgebra achieves strict coassociativity with
spectral parameters: the $E_1$-operad supplies exactly the right
amount of homotopy coherence.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra (antipode)]
\label{def:e1-chiral-antipode}
An $E_1$-chiral bialgebra $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta)$ is an
\emph{$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra} if there exists a morphism of
$E_1$-chiral modules $S \colon A \to A$ (the \emph{antipode})
satisfying the \emph{Hopf axiom at $z = 0$}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-axiom-z0}
 \mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
 = \eta \circ \varepsilon
 = \mu_{E_1} \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{E_1}$ is the labeled-ordered product and $\Delta_0 = \Delta_z|_{z=0}$
is the coproduct at the basepoint. The two equalities are
independent conditions because $\mu_{E_1}$ is not commutative.
For $z \neq 0$, the antipode axiom generalizes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-axiom-spectral}
 \mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_z
 = \eta \circ \varepsilon \circ \pi_z,
\end{equation}
where $\pi_z \colon A \to A$ is the \emph{spectral projection}:
the composition $A \xrightarrow{\Delta_z} A \otimes_{E_1,z} A
\xrightarrow{\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id}} A$ evaluated at the
$z$-independent term, i.e.\ $\pi_z = (\varepsilon \otimes \mathrm{id})
\circ \Delta_z|_{z^0}$, extracting the constant coefficient of the
Laurent expansion in $z$. When $A$ is connected
($\varepsilon \circ \eta = \mathrm{id}_k$), the basepoint
axiom~\eqref{eq:hopf-axiom-z0} determines $S$ uniquely by
induction on the coalgebra filtration, exactly as in the classical
Hopf algebra setting.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Summary of the $E_1$-chiral Hopf axiom system]
\label{rem:e1-hopf-axiom-summary}
Definitions~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}
and~\ref{def:e1-chiral-antipode} assemble into a five-part axiom
system for an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
$(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ on a curve $C$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(H\arabic*)}]
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral algebra}: $(A, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an
 augmented algebra in $\cM^{E_1}_C$; the product $\mu$ is the
 labeled-ordered OPE (Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-monoidal}).
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral coalgebra}: $\Delta_z \colon A \to
 A \otimes_{E_1,z} A$ is a $z$-parametrized coproduct with counit
 $\varepsilon$ and coaugmentation $\eta$
 (Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}).
 \item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility}: $\Delta_z$ is a morphism
 of $E_1$-chiral algebras: the diagram of
 Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}(iii) commutes.
 \item \emph{Spectral coassociativity}:
 $(\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w$
 (equation~\eqref{eq:spectral-coassociativity}).
 \item \emph{Hopf axiom}:
 $\mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
  = \eta \circ \varepsilon
  = \mu_{E_1} \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0$
 (equation~\eqref{eq:hopf-axiom-z0}).
\end{enumerate}
The axioms are strictly stronger than Li's vertex-bialgebra
framework ($E_\infty$-chiral, no spectral parameter, no
antipode) and strictly weaker than Drinfeld's quasi-Hopf formulation
(which works in $({\mathrm{Vect}}, \otimes_k)$ and requires an
associator $\Phi$). The $E_1$-chiral setting eliminates the
associator: axiom~\textbf{(H4)} holds on the nose because
the $E_1$-monoidal category is strictly associative
(the associahedron is contractible for linear orders).

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Scope of (H5): class~$\mathbf{G}$ versus
non-class-$\mathbf{G}$ landscape.}
Axiom~\textbf{(H5)} is realised on the nose by class~$\mathbf{G}$
primitive-coproduct algebras (Heisenberg, lattice at integer level,
$\beta\gamma$ before Sugawara substitute); for these the
involutive sign map $S(J) = -J$ is a chain-level antipode and
Heisenberg (Example~\ref{ex:heisenberg-bialgebra}) is the prototype.
For non-class-$\mathbf{G}$ families with Miura-type nonlinearities,
a chain-level antipode does \emph{not} exist: the classical Yangian
antipode $S(T(u)) = T(u)^{-1}$ does not lift to a vertex-algebraic
antipode on $\cW_{1+\infty}[\Psi]$ by two independent obstructions
(OPE quartic pole $S(T)_{(3)}S(T) = c/2 + 2(\Psi{-}1)(\Psi{-}2)$ at
generic~$\Psi$, and a universal Hopf-axiom residual
$-\frac{\Psi-1}{z^2} + z\,J$ in the convolution
$m_z \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_z$).  This is the
proved negative of Vol~I, Proposition
\textup{\texttt{prop:w-infty-antipode-obstruction}}
(\texttt{chapters/theory/ordered\_associative\_chiral\_kd.tex}).
Consequently, the object that concrete CY quantum groups
(toroidal, Yangian, affine BPS) inhabit is an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra (axioms \textbf{(H1)--(H4)}), not an
$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra.  The present chapter retains the
Hopf-axiom definition for the class~$\mathbf{G}$ prototype and for
naming the obstruction target; the rest of the volume consistently
treats the chiral object as a bialgebra and invokes the antipode
only on the classical point-value fibre via the Drinfeld double
programme (Vol~II, Conjecture
\textup{\texttt{conj:drinfeld-double-e1-construction}}
in \texttt{chapters/connections/ordered\_associative\_chiral\_kd\_frontier.tex}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why $E_1$ and not $E_\infty$}
\label{subsec:why-e1-not-einfty}

\begin{proposition}[Averaging forgetful theorem]
\label{prop:averaging-forgets-hopf}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The averaging map of
Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty} defines a functor
\[
 \mathrm{av} \colon
 E_1\text{-}\mathrm{ChirHopf}(C) \longrightarrow
 E_\infty\text{-}\mathrm{ChirCoalg}(C)
\]
that sends an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
$(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ satisfying
axioms~\textup{\textbf{(H1)--(H5)}} of
Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:e1-hopf-axiom-summary}} to the
$E_\infty$-chiral coalgebra
$(B^{\Sigma}(A), \Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}})$ with the coshuffle coproduct
$\Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}}$. The functor forgets all Hopf data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The $R$-matrix: on degree-two elements,
 $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (a scalar), so the full
 $z$-dependent $R$-matrix $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$ is replaced by its
 $S_2$-coinvariant, which is the collision residue $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
 \item The antipode: $S$ acts on ordered tensor factors; after
 symmetrization, $S$ becomes the identity (on $S_n$-invariants,
 the reversal acts trivially).
 \item The coproduct $z$-dependence: the coshuffle coproduct is
 independent of the collision point $z$, while the deconcatenation
 coproduct $\Delta_z$ depends on $z$ through the ordered OPE residues.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the $E_\infty$-chiral coalgebra $B^{\Sigma}(A)$ is NOT
a bialgebra: it retains no Hopf structure. The coshuffle coproduct on
$B^{\Sigma}$ is compatible only with the commutative product, which
$A$ does not possess.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Statement~(i) is Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}.
Statement~(ii): the antipode $S$ is an anti-homomorphism of the ordered
product ($S(\mu(a,b)) = \mu(S(b), S(a))$), so on $S_n$-coinvariants
$\mathrm{av} \circ S = \mathrm{av}$ (symmetrization absorbs the
reversal of tensor factors). Statement~(iii): the coshuffle coproduct is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}} = \mathrm{av}^{\otimes 2} \circ \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}$,
and $\mathrm{av}^{\otimes 2}$ kills the $z$-dependence because
$\sum_{\sigma} r(\sigma \cdot z) = n! \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
The final claim (no bialgebra structure on $B^{\Sigma}$) follows because
the bialgebra compatibility diagram of
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}(iii) requires the interchange
law for the $E_1$ tensor product, which does not hold in the symmetric
quotient.
\end{proof}

This is the central structural result: Li's vertex-bialgebra framework,
which works with $B^{\Sigma}$ and the coshuffle coproduct,
cannot carry a Hopf structure. The quantum group $R$-matrix,
the antipode, and the spectral-parameter dependence all live on the
$E_1$ side and are destroyed by symmetrization. The $E_1$-chiral
bialgebra is the minimal structure that retains all three.

\begin{remark}[$A_\infty$ coproduct and the shadow tower]
\label{rem:ainfty-coproduct-shadow}
For class~$M$ algebras (infinite shadow depth), the formal Yangian coproduct $\Delta_z^{\mathrm{Yangian}}$ is the $m_2$-truncation of the full $A_\infty$ coproduct:
\[
 \Delta_z^{A_\infty}(T) \;=\; \Delta_z^{\mathrm{Yangian}}(T)
 + \hbar^2 \cdot \delta^{(3)}(T)
 + \hbar^3 \cdot \delta^{(4)}(T)
 + \cdots
\]
where $\delta^{(k)}$ is the correction from the $A_\infty$ operation $m_k$, with coefficient equal to the shadow invariant $S_k$. Concretely (\texttt{a\_infinity\_bar\_w1inf.py}): $m_3(T,T,T) = -2T$ gives $\delta^{(3)}$ with coefficient $\alpha = 2$; $m_4(T,T,T,T) = \tfrac{40}{27}T$ gives $\delta^{(4)}$ with coefficient $S_4 = \tfrac{10}{27}$. For class~$G$ (Heisenberg): all $m_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$, so the truncation is exact. For class~$L$ (Yangian): $S_4 = 0$, so the truncation terminates at finite depth. For class~$M$ (Virasoro): all $S_k \neq 0$, and the coproduct has genuinely infinite complexity. The shadow tower is therefore not merely a classification invariant: it encodes the $A_\infty$ corrections to the chiral coproduct.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Universal coproduct formula from the Miura factorization]
\label{prop:universal-coproduct}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $A = W_{1+\infty}$ at level $\Psi$ with transfer matrix $T(u) = \sum_{s \geq 0} (-1)^s \psi_s \, u^{-s}$, where $\psi_s = e_s(\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_N)$ is the $s$-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the free bosons. The Miura coproduct $\Delta_z(T(u)) = T_L(u) \cdot T_R(u - z)$ gives:
\[
 \Delta_z(\psi_s) \;=\; \sum_{\substack{a + b + k = s \\ a, b, k \geq 0}} (-1)^k \binom{N_R - b}{k} z^k \, \psi_a^L \cdot \psi_b^R.
\]
The coproduct at spin $s$ is a polynomial in $z$ of degree exactly $s$, with $\tfrac{s(s+1)}{2}$ operator-product terms. The number of terms at $z$-power $p$ is $N(s,p) = s - p$, with generating function
\[
 F(x,y) \;=\; \sum_{s \geq 1} \sum_{p=0}^{s-1} N(s,p) \, x^s y^p \;=\; \frac{x}{(1-x)^2(1-xy)}.
\]
The leading $z^{s-1}$ coefficient is $\psi_1^R = J^R$ (single term). The subleading $z^{s-2}$ coefficient is $(s-1)\psi_2^R + \sum_k J_k^L J_{n-k}^R$ (universal at all spins).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand $T_R(u-z) = \sum_b (-1)^b \psi_b^R (u-z)^{-b}$ using the binomial series $(u-z)^{-b} = \sum_{k \geq 0} \binom{b-1+k}{k} z^k u^{-b-k}$. Collecting the $u^{-(a+b+k)}$ coefficient from the product $T_L(u) \cdot T_R(u-z)$ gives the stated formula. The term count follows: at $z$-power $p$, the constraint $a + b = s - p$ with $a \geq 0$ gives $s - p$ choices of $(a, b)$. The generating function $F$ is obtained by geometric-series summation. See \texttt{compute/lib/chiral\_coproduct\_allspin\_engine.py}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $A_\infty$ coproduct correction $\delta^{(3)}$ on Fock space]
\label{rem:delta3-fock-matrix}
The universal coproduct of Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-coproduct} is the tree-level ($m_2$) coproduct. The cubic shadow correction $\delta^{(3)}$ from $m_3(T,T,T) = -2T$ produces a morphism defect: $\Delta_0(m_3(T^{\otimes 3})) \neq m_3^{\mathrm{diag}}(\Delta_0(T)^{\otimes 3})$. On the truncated Fock space $\cF_{\leq N}^L \otimes \cF_{\leq N}^R$, the defect at mode~$n$ is:
\[
  \delta^{(3)}(T_n) \;=\; -2\alpha \sum_{k \in \Z} J_k^L \, J_{n-k}^R, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\Psi - 1}{\Psi},
\]
where $\Psi$ is the level parameter ($\Psi = 1$: free boson, $\Psi = 2$: self-dual point). Key properties:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\delta^{(3)}(T_n) = 0$ at $\Psi = 1$ (class~$G$: no shadow, no correction).
 \item $\langle \mathrm{vac}, \mathrm{vac} | \, \delta^{(3)}(T_0) \, | \mathrm{vac}, \mathrm{vac} \rangle = 0$ (vacuum matrix element vanishes).
 \item Weight conservation: $[L_0^{\mathrm{tot}}, \delta^{(3)}(T_n)] = -n \cdot \delta^{(3)}(T_n)$.
 \item The correction is proportional to the cross-term of $\Delta_0$: $\delta^{(3)} = -2 \cdot (\text{cross-term})$, independently of $\Psi$.
\end{itemize}
At the $E_3$ level, $\delta^{(3)}(T_0) = 40/27$ as a scalar on the volume class (Proposition~\ref{prop:virasoro-d4}): the spectral sequence value is the trace of the Fock matrix over the $3$-particle sector.  See \texttt{m3\_coproduct\_correction\_engine.py} ($40$ tests).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Spectral vs.\ worldsheet interpretation of $z$]
\label{rem:z-spectral-vs-worldsheet}
The spectral parameter $z$ in $\Delta_z$ is a shift of the transfer-matrix argument $u \mapsto u - z$ in the Yangian mode algebra $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$. It is \emph{not} the worldsheet coordinate of a vertex operator insertion. The OPE singularity $T(z)T(w) \sim c/2 \cdot (z-w)^{-4}$ involves the worldsheet variable; the coproduct $\Delta_z$ involves the spectral variable. Setting $z = 0$ in $\Delta_z$ removes the spectral shift and gives a well-defined, cocommutative coproduct with no OPE singularity (verified in \texttt{test\_hopf\_axioms.py}, $50$ tests). The two $z$'s live in different mathematical objects and must not be conflated.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Coassociativity from the Ran space $E_1$-monoidal structure]
\label{prop:coassoc-ran-e1}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $A$ be an $E_1$-factorization algebra on a smooth curve~$C$.
The factorization isomorphism
$\phi_{S_1,S_2}\colon A(S_1 \sqcup S_2) \xrightarrow{\sim}
A(S_1) \otimes A(S_2)$
for disjoint $S_1, S_2 \in \Ran(C)$ is strictly coassociative:
the two iterated coproducts
\[
  ({\phi_{S_1,S_2} \otimes \mathrm{id}}) \circ \phi_{S_1 \sqcup S_2, S_3}
  \;=\;
  ({\mathrm{id} \otimes \phi_{S_2,S_3}}) \circ \phi_{S_1, S_2 \sqcup S_3}
\]
coincide as maps $A(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3) \to
A(S_1) \otimes A(S_2) \otimes A(S_3)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ordered configuration space
$\Conf_n^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathbb{R})
= \{x_1 < \cdots < x_n\}$
is contractible for every~$n$ (it is convex).
By the Ayala--Francis theorem
(arXiv:1504.04007, Theorem~3.24),
an $E_1$-algebra is equivalently a locally constant
factorization algebra on~$\mathbb{R}$,
and the $E_1$-monoidal structure on $\Ran(C)$
is strictly associative: the space of parenthesizations
of an $n$-fold labeled-ordered product is connected
(contractible associahedron $K_n \simeq \ast$).
Applied to $n = 3$, the two binary decompositions
$(S_1 \sqcup S_2) \sqcup S_3$ and
$S_1 \sqcup (S_2 \sqcup S_3)$
are connected by the unique path in $K_3 = \ast$.
Hence the two iterated factorization isomorphisms
agree, which is coassociativity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Drinfeld center recovers $E_2$}
\label{subsec:drinfeld-recovers-e2}

The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra $A$ is ordered and non-braided. The braided
($E_2$) structure is recovered by the Drinfeld center.

\begin{proposition}[$R$-matrix from the Drinfeld center]
\label{prop:r-matrix-from-drinfeld}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ be an $E_1$-chiral Hopf
algebra. The Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ is a braided monoidal category
(Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}), and the braiding
on $\cZ$ defines an $R$-matrix
$R(z) \in (A \otimes A)((z))$
characterized by the property: for all $A$-modules $V, W$, the
half-braiding $\sigma_{V,W}(z) \colon V \otimes_{E_1} W
\xrightarrow{\sim} W \otimes_{E_1} V$ acts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-matrix-action}
 \sigma_{V,W}(z)(v \otimes w)
 = \tau\bigl(R(z) \cdot (v \otimes w)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the transposition of tensor factors.
The $R$-matrix lives in $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, not in $A \otimes A$
directly: it is the half-braiding data encoding the obstruction to
commutativity of the ordered tensor product. This resolves the
apparent paradox that the $R$-matrix ``lives in $A \otimes A$''
(which is ordered and has no braiding): the $R$-matrix is a morphism
in the Drinfeld center, which IS braided.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ consists of pairs $(M, \sigma_M)$
where $M$ is an $A$-module and $\sigma_M \colon M \otimes_{E_1} N
\xrightarrow{\sim} N \otimes_{E_1} M$ is a half-braiding natural in $N$.
For $M = A$ (the regular module), the half-braiding is constructed from
the spectral coproduct: define
$\sigma_A(z)(a \otimes n)
= \sum \Delta_z(a)_{(2)} \cdot n \otimes \Delta_z(a)_{(1)}$
(using Sweedler notation for $\Delta_z$). The naturality of $\sigma_A$
in the second slot follows from the bialgebra compatibility (axiom~(iii)
of Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}): $\Delta_z$ is an algebra
map, so $\sigma_A$ intertwines the $A$-action. The invertibility of
$\sigma_A$ uses the antipode: the inverse half-braiding is
$\sigma_A^{-1}(z)(n \otimes a)
= \sum S(\Delta_z(a)_{(1)}) \cdot n \otimes \Delta_z(a)_{(2)}$,
which is well-defined by the Hopf axiom
(Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-antipode}).
The $R$-matrix $R(z) \in (A \otimes A)((z))$ is then the element
implementing~\eqref{eq:r-matrix-action}, extracted as the image of
$\sigma_A$ under the canonical map
$\mathrm{End}(A \otimes_{E_1} A) \to (A \otimes A)((z))$.
The braiding satisfies the Yang--Baxter equation because the Drinfeld
center is a braided monoidal category (a formal consequence of the
definition). See Theorem~\ref{thm:drinfeld-center-coha} for the
explicit verification at $A = Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Examples}
\label{subsec:e1-bialg-examples}

\begin{example}[Heisenberg $E_1$-chiral bialgebra]
\label{ex:heisenberg-bialgebra}
The rank-one Heisenberg $H_k$ ($k \neq 0$) is an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
with:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Product: $\mu(J(z_1), J(z_2)) = {:}J(z_1)J(z_2){:} + k/(z_1 - z_2)^2$
 (the time-ordered OPE, not the normally-ordered product).
 \item Coproduct: $\Delta_z(J) = J \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes J$ (primitive).
 The Heisenberg coproduct is $z$-independent because the $r$-matrix
 $r(z) = k/z$ has no nontrivial $z$-dependent correction to $\Delta$.
 \item Antipode: $S(J) = -J$, so $\mu_{E_1}(S(J) \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes J)
 = -J + J = 0 = \eta(\varepsilon(J))$.
 \item $R$-matrix from Drinfeld center: $R(z) = e^{k \log z \cdot h \otimes h}$
 (the Heisenberg vertex $R$-matrix; here $h$ is the Cartan generator
 identified with $J$). After averaging, $\mathrm{av}(R(z)) = k$
 (the level), consistent with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) = k$.
\end{itemize}
The Heisenberg is class~G: $\Delta$ is primitive, $S$ is the sign
involution, and the Hopf structure is the simplest possible.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The three-way resolution]
\label{rem:three-way-resolution}
The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra resolves the apparent conflict between
three classical frameworks for quantum group structure on chiral algebras:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item \emph{Drinfeld quasi-Hopf}: lives in the symmetric monoidal
 category of vector spaces, with a non-coassociative coproduct corrected
 by the Drinfeld associator $\Phi$. This is the WRONG ambient category:
 chiral algebras are not vector spaces, and the associator $\Phi$ is an
 artifact of forcing a braided structure into a symmetric setting.
 \item \emph{Li vertex bialgebra}: lives in the $E_\infty$-chiral
 monoidal category, with the coshuffle coproduct on $B^{\Sigma}$.
 This uses the CORRECT ambient category (chiral) but the WRONG structure
 level ($E_\infty$), destroying the $R$-matrix.
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral bialgebra}: lives in the $E_1$-chiral monoidal
 category $\cM^{E_1}_C$, with the deconcatenation coproduct on
 $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$. This uses the correct ambient category AND the
 correct structure level. The $R$-matrix is recovered by passing to the
 Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, which is the $E_2$ enhancement.
\end{enumerate}
The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra is the Hopf object in the category chiral algebras inhabit once collision order is remembered.
\end{remark}

transition (averaging) collapses $r(z)$ to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$
and destroys the Hopf structure entirely.
See Construction~\ref{constr:three-bar-complexes} for the
bar-complex triple.}
\label{tab:en-chiral-comparison}
\end{table}

The table makes precise the content of
Remark~\ref{rem:three-way-resolution}:
Li's vertex bialgebras occupy the rightmost column, where
the coshuffle coproduct is $z$-independent and carries no $R$-matrix
data.
Drinfeld's quasi-Hopf algebras do not appear in the table because
they live in $(\mathrm{Vect}, \otimes_k)$, not in a chiral monoidal
category; the associator $\Phi$ is the artifact of forcing a braided
object into a symmetric ambient category.
The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra (leftmost column) is the minimal
framework retaining all quantum group data.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The factorization-homology coproduct}
\label{subsec:fact-hom-coproduct}

The preceding subsections defined the spectral coproduct $\Delta_z$ and
proved coassociativity from the factorization property
(Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-coassociativity-factorization}).  That
proof is \emph{operadic}: it uses the factorization isomorphism of the
$E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ on a curve.  A deeper question is whether the
coproduct itself can be \emph{derived} from factorization homology,
bypassing mode-level constructions such as the Miura factorization
(which requires toric data and may not exist for general CY categories).
The answer is yes: excision for factorization homology
(Proposition~\ref{prop:fh-excision}) provides a canonical coproduct on
any chiral algebra obtained by integrating a factorization algebra over
a compact factor of a product CY manifold.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(1) The $E_3$ factorization algebra on $\C^3$
and the Ran space diagonal.}
Let $\cF$ be the $E_3$ factorization algebra of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison} on $\C^3$.
For a disjoint union of open disks $U_1 \sqcup U_2 \hookrightarrow \C^3$
in the Ran space $\Ran(\C^3)$, the factorization structure of $\cF$ provides
a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \cF(U_1 \sqcup U_2) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;
  \cF(U_1) \otimes \cF(U_2).
\]
The \emph{Ran diagonal} $\delta \colon \Ran(\C^3) \to
\Ran(\C^3) \times \Ran(\C^3)$ (the map sending a finite set $S$
to $(S_1, S_2)$ for each decomposition $S = S_1 \sqcup S_2$)
produces a cosheaf-level coproduct: the global sections
$\cF(\C^3)$ acquire a coalgebra structure via
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ran-coproduct}
  \Delta^{\mathrm{Ran}} \colon \cF(U) \xrightarrow{\;\phi_{U_1,U_2}\;}
  \cF(U_1) \otimes \cF(U_2),
\end{equation}
summed over all ways of decomposing $U = U_1 \sqcup U_2$ into disjoint
subsets.  This is the factorization coproduct at the level of $\C^3$.
The excision property (Proposition~\ref{prop:fh-excision}) guarantees
that $\Delta^{\mathrm{Ran}}$ is well-defined: for a collar-gluing
$U = U_1 \cup_{N \times \R} U_2$ of a domain along a codimension-$1$
collar,
\[
  \int_U \cF \;\simeq\;
  \int_{U_1} \cF \;\otimes_{\int_{N \times \R} \cF}\;
  \int_{U_2} \cF.
\]
No mode expansion or Miura-type factorization is needed:
the excision gluing data is carried by the cosheaf structure of $\cF$
on $\Ran(\C^3)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(2) $K3 \times E$: the $E$-direction coproduct from
cutting the elliptic curve.}
For the product CY$_3$ manifold $X = K3 \times E$, the factorization
homology integral $\int_{K3} \cF|_{K3}$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:fact-homology-k3xe} produces a chiral algebra
$A_E$ on the residual elliptic curve $E$.  The coproduct on $A_E$ arises
from cutting $E$ into two intervals via excision.  Concretely, choose a
collar-gluing decomposition $E = I_1 \cup_{S^0 \times \R} I_2$ (cutting
$E$ at two points into two intervals $I_1, I_2$ with collar
neighbourhoods).  The excision equivalence gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:k3xe-excision-coproduct}
  \int_E A_E \;\simeq\;
  \int_{I_1} A_E \;\otimes_{\int_{S^0 \times \R} A_E}\;
  \int_{I_2} A_E.
\end{equation}
The map $A_E \to \int_{I_1} A_E \otimes \int_{I_2} A_E$ is the
\emph{factorization coproduct}: it decomposes the algebra on the
full curve into tensor factors localized on the two intervals.
Since $A_E$ is $\Eone$-chiral on $E$ (holomorphy provides the ordering),
the decomposition~\eqref{eq:k3xe-excision-coproduct} respects the $\Eone$
ordering of the intervals, and the resulting coproduct is an $\Eone$-chiral
coalgebra map.

The essential point: this construction requires \emph{only} that
$\int_{K3} \cF|_{K3}$ exists as a factorization algebra on $E$.
No Miura transform, no toric structure on the K3 factor, and no mode
decomposition of the OPE are needed.  The coproduct is a consequence
of the factorization structure of $A_E$ on the Ran space of $E$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(3) Coassociativity from the Ran monoidal structure.}
The coassociativity of $\Delta^{\mathrm{Ran}}$ follows from the
associativity of the Ran space monoidal structure, by the same
operadic argument as
Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-coassociativity-factorization}.
For three disjoint subsets $S_1, S_2, S_3$, the two bracketings
\begin{align*}
  &\cF(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1,\, S_2 \sqcup S_3}}
   \cF(S_1) \otimes \cF(S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id} \otimes \phi_{S_2, S_3}}
   \cF(S_1) \otimes \cF(S_2) \otimes \cF(S_3), \\
  &\cF(S_1 \sqcup S_2 \sqcup S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1 \sqcup S_2,\, S_3}}
   \cF(S_1 \sqcup S_2) \otimes \cF(S_3)
   \xrightarrow{\phi_{S_1, S_2} \otimes \mathrm{id}}
   \cF(S_1) \otimes \cF(S_2) \otimes \cF(S_3)
\end{align*}
coincide because $\Ran$ is a symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category under
disjoint union, and the factorization algebra is a symmetric monoidal
functor from $\mathrm{Disk}_{n/M}$ to chain complexes.  No modes, no
spectral parameters, no computation: the proof is a formal consequence
of the symmetric monoidal structure of the Ran space.

When the $\Eone$-ordering is imposed (restricting to ordered
configurations along a real ray in $E$), the coassociativity
specializes to the spectral coassociativity identity
$(\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
= (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w$ of
equation~\eqref{eq:spectral-coassociativity}, recovering the
$\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axiom~(H4).

\begin{conjecture}[Factorization-homology coproduct agrees with Miura
for toric CY$_3$]
\label{conj:fact-hom-vs-miura}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $X$ be a toric CY$_3$ and let $\cF$ be the $E_3$
factorization algebra of
Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}} restricted
to~$X$.  Let $A_C = \int_{X/C} \cF$ be the chiral algebra on a
residual curve $C$ obtained by factorization homology over the
transverse directions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The factorization-homology coproduct
  $\Delta^{\mathrm{Ran}}$ of~\eqref{eq:ran-coproduct}, restricted to
  the $\Eone$-sector, coincides with the Drinfeld coproduct
  $\Delta_z$ of Definition~\textup{\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}}(ii).
  \item For $X = \C^3$ with $\Omega$-background $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$
  and gauge algebra $\fgl_1$, the Drinfeld coproduct on the affine
  Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is recovered from the Ran diagonal
  of the $E_3$-factorization algebra on $\C^3$: the
  Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak Miura factorization of
  $\cW_{1+\infty}$ is the toric specialization of the general
  excision coproduct.
\end{enumerate}
The conjecture is conditional on
Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}} (the
$E_3$ factorization algebra on $\C^3$).  For the affine
Yangian specifically, the Drinfeld coproduct is constructed
independently (Guay--Nakajima--Wendlandt); the content of~(ii) is
that the two constructions agree under the identification
$\int_{\C^2} \cF|_{\C^2} \simeq Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Well-definedness for general CY$_3$]
\label{rem:fact-coprod-general-cy3}
The factorization-homology coproduct $\Delta^{\mathrm{Ran}}$ is
well-defined for \emph{any} CY$_3$ category $\cC$ for which the
CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_d$ produces a factorization algebra, i.e.,
whenever the relevant CY-to-chiral statement is available:
unconditionally at $d = 2$ by Theorem~CY-A$_2$, and at
$d = 3$ by Theorem~CY-A$_3$ (proved, infinity-categorical framework).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{At $d = 2$:} For a CY$_2$ category
  (e.g.\ $D^b(\Coh(K3))$), the correspondence $\Phi_2$ produces an
  $\Etwo$-chiral algebra (Theorem~CY-A$_2$), and the
  factorization-homology coproduct is the excision map for any
  collar-gluing of the underlying surface.  This recovers the
  lattice vertex algebra coproduct (primitive on generators)
  without any toric structure.
  \item \emph{At $d = 3$:} The
  correspondence $\Phi_d$ produces a factorization
  algebra $\cF$ on a curve $C$ by Theorem~CY-A$_3$, and the excision coproduct is
  well-defined.  The Miura factorization is the
  toric case where $\cF$ admits a decomposition into free-field
  factors (one per toric leg of the web diagram); for
  non-toric CY$_3$ (e.g.\ the quintic, a general complete
  intersection, or a Pfaffian CY), no such decomposition exists,
  and the factorization-homology coproduct is the \emph{only}
  available construction.
  \item \emph{Obstructions:} The factorization-homology coproduct
  requires the chiral algebra $A = \Phi(\cC)$ to be a
  factorization algebra (not merely a vertex algebra in the
  algebraic sense).  This is automatic when $A$ is obtained from
  the Costello--Gwilliam programme, but may fail for algebraically
  constructed vertex algebras that lack a factorization-algebra
  enhancement.  In the CY setting, the Costello--Gwilliam
  construction is the native one, so no additional assumption is
  needed beyond CY-A.
\end{enumerate}
The factorization-homology coproduct therefore provides a
\emph{universal} source of the $\Eone$-chiral coalgebra structure:
whenever the CY-to-chiral correspondence exists, the coproduct exists.
The Miura factorization is the computational specialization in
the toric case.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Wilson lines as stratified factorization homology defects}
\label{subsec:wilson-stratified-fh}

The factorization-homology coproduct of~\eqref{eq:ran-coproduct}
acquires a physical interpretation when reformulated in the language
of \emph{stratified factorization homology}
(Ayala--Francis--Tanaka 2017): Wilson lines in the 6d holomorphic
theory on $\C^3$ are $1$-dimensional holomorphic defects, and
their collision produces the coproduct $\Delta_z$ via the excision
map of the stratified integral.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(1) Defect hierarchy in $\C^3$.}
In the Costello--Francis--Gwilliam framework, the 6d holomorphic
theory on $\C^3$ admits defects classified by complex codimension:
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{Codim} & \textbf{Defect} & \textbf{Normal bundle} &
 \textbf{Spectral} & $E_n$ \textbf{level} \\
 \midrule
 $0$ & Bulk & (none) & $0$ & $E_3$ \\
 $1$ & Wilson surface & $N \cong \C$ & $1$ & $\Etwo$ \\
 $2$ & Wilson line & $N \cong \C^2$ & $2$ & $\Eone$ \\
 $3$ & Point operator & $N \cong \C^3$ & $3$ & $E_0$ \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The number of spectral parameters equals the complex rank of the
normal bundle $N_{D/\C^3}$: each normal direction provides one
spectral degree of freedom.  The restriction of the $E_3$ bulk
algebra to a defect of complex codimension~$k$ loses $k$ chiral
levels, landing on $E_{3-k}$.  For Wilson lines ($k=2$), the
restricted algebra is $\Eone$-chiral: the algebra~$A$ of
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(2) Wilson line collision as stratified excision.}
Let $D_1, D_2 \subset \C^3$ be two parallel copies of the defect
curve $C$, separated by $z \in N_{C/\C^3} \cong \C^2$ in the normal
direction.  The stratified factorization homology integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stratified-excision}
 \int_{(\C^3,\, D_1 \sqcup D_2)} (\cF,\, V_1 \otimes V_2)
 \;\xrightarrow{\;\phi^{\mathrm{strat}}_{D_1,D_2}\;}
 \int_{(\C^3,\, D_1)} (\cF, V_1)
 \;\otimes\;
 \int_{(\C^3,\, D_2)} (\cF, V_2)
\end{equation}
is the \emph{stratified excision map}, where $\cF$ is the bulk
$E_3$-algebra and $V_1, V_2$ are the defect algebras
(modules for $\cF|_{N(D_i)}$).  As $z \to 0$ (the two defects
collide), the excision map~\eqref{eq:stratified-excision} becomes
the coproduct:
\[
 \Delta_z = \phi^{\mathrm{strat}}_{D_1,D_2}
 \colon A \;\longrightarrow\; A \otimes_{\Eone,z} A.
\]
The spectral parameter $z$ is the
separation in $N_{C/\C^3}$; it is a Yangian spectral parameter
. At $z = 0$, the excision
map reduces to the deconcatenation coproduct $\Delta_0 =
\Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}$ of Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(3) The axiom dictionary.}
Each axiom \textbf{(H1)}--\textbf{(H5)} of
Remark~\ref{rem:e1-hopf-axiom-summary} has a direct translation
into stratified factorization homology:
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{Axiom} & \textbf{$\Eone$-chiral bialgebra} &
 \textbf{Stratified FH} \\
 \midrule
 \textbf{(H1)} & $\Eone$ algebra $(A,\mu,\varepsilon)$ &
 Bulk $\cF$ restricted to $C$ with holomorphic ordering \\
 \textbf{(H2)} & Coalgebra $\Delta_z$ &
 Stratified excision~\eqref{eq:stratified-excision} along $N_{C/\C^3}$ \\
 \textbf{(H3)} & $\Delta_z$ is an algebra map &
 Naturality of excision (morphism of fact.\ algebras) \\
 \textbf{(H4)} & Spectral coassociativity &
 Triple excision coherence ($K_3 \simeq \ast$) \\
 \textbf{(H5)} & Antipode $S$ &

 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The operadic proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral-coassociativity-factorization}
(axiom~\textbf{(H4)}) translates directly: the two bracketings of
triple Wilson line collision factor through the triple factorization
isomorphism, and agree because the ordered $3$-configuration space
$K_3 \simeq \{z_1 < z_2 < z_3\}$ is contractible.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(4) Braiding from Wilson line crossing.}
In $\C^3$ (real dimension~$6$), two Wilson lines (real
codimension~$4$) can pass through each other: the complement of
a codimension-$4$ submanifold in $\R^6$ is simply connected
($\pi_1 = 0$).  Topologically, the braiding is \emph{trivial}.
The nontrivial $R$-matrix $R(u,v)$ arises from the
\emph{$\Omega$-background deformation} of the holomorphic
factorization structure, producing the holonomy of the
factorization-algebra connection around the crossing locus.
At charge~$1$:
\[
 R_{\mathrm{ch}}^{(1)}(u,v) \;=\; g(u)\, g(v)\, g(u-v)
 \qquad\text{(Zamolodchikov factorisation)},
\]
where the three factors correspond to the three crossing planes
in $\C^3$.  The dimensional hierarchy:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\Eone$ (3d): no crossing possible.
 $R = 1$ (trivial, ordering only).
 \item $\Etwo$ (5d): one crossing direction, one spectral parameter.
 $R(u) = g(u)$ (Yangian $R$-matrix, satisfies YBE).
 \item $E_3$ (6d): two crossing directions, two spectral parameters.
 $R(u,v) = g(u)\,g(v)\,g(u{-}v)$ (quantum toroidal, satisfies the
 parametric YBE).
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(5) Wilson surfaces and the categorical $S$-matrix.}
Wilson \emph{surfaces} (complex codimension~$1$, $\Etwo$-level defects)
produce \emph{categorical} modules: objects in the $2$-category
$\Rep^{E_3}(\cA)$.  Two linked Wilson surfaces (linked as
$S^2 \hookrightarrow S^5$; cf.\ Construction~\ref{constr:e3-s-matrix})
produce the categorical $S$-matrix $\cS_{\cV,\cW}(u,v) =
\Tr_{\cW}(\cR^{E_3}_{\cV,\cW} \circ \cR^{E_3}_{\cW,\cV})$, which
is a $2$-morphism (not a scalar).  At charge~$1$, the categorical
$S$-matrix is trivial ($= 1$) by the CY unitarity identity
$R(u,v)\, R(-u,-v) = 1$. The nontrivial content appears at
charge~$\geq 2$.

\section{Non-abelian coproduct: the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ matrix Lax operator}
\label{sec:nonabelian-matrix-lax}

Everything above is for $\fgl_1$ (abelian, single free boson).
The passage to $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ requires a \emph{matrix} Lax operator,
and the coproduct becomes matrix multiplication.
This is the first genuinely non-abelian case: the Serre
relations are nontrivial, the quantum determinant is central,
and coassociativity holds at the \emph{trace} level but fails
entry-wise at the operator level.

\subsection{The matrix Lax operator}
\label{subsec:matrix-lax}

\begin{definition}[Matrix Lax operator for $Y(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$]
\label{def:matrix-lax}
The \emph{Lax operator} for the Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at level~$k$
is the $2 \times 2$ matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lax-operator}
  L(u) \;=\; u \cdot \mathrm{Id}_2 + J(u)
  \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} u + h(u) & f(u) \\ e(u) & u - h(u) \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $e(u) = \sum_{n \geq 0} e_n u^{-n-1}$,
$f(u) = \sum_{n \geq 0} f_n u^{-n-1}$,
$h(u) = \sum_{n \geq 0} h_n u^{-n-1}$ are the generating currents
of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, and $J(u) = h(u) \cdot H/2 + e(u) \cdot E + f(u) \cdot F$ is the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-valued current in the fundamental representation.
\end{definition}

The $\fgl_1$ transfer matrix $T(u) = \sum_s (-1)^s \psi_s u^{-s}$
of Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-coproduct} is the $1 \times 1$
specialization of~\eqref{eq:lax-operator}: when $e = f = 0$
and $h = \psi$, one recovers $L(u) = \mathrm{diag}(T_+(u), T_-(u))$
and $\tr L(u) = 2u$ (the scalar transfer matrix).

\subsection{The RTT relation}
\label{subsec:rtt}

The commutation relations of $Y(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ are encoded in
a single matrix equation.

\begin{proposition}[RTT relation, Faddeev--Reshetikhin--Takhtajan]
\label{prop:rtt}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is the associative algebra generated
by the entries $\{L_{ij}(u)\}_{i,j \in \{0,1\}}$ subject to the relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rtt}
  R_{12}(u - v)\, L_1(u)\, L_2(v)
  \;=\; L_2(v)\, L_1(u)\, R_{12}(u - v),
\end{equation}
where $R(u) = u \cdot \mathrm{Id}_4 + P$ is the Yang $R$-matrix,
$P$ is the permutation operator on $\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$,
and $L_1(u) = L(u) \otimes \mathrm{Id}_2$,
$L_2(v) = \mathrm{Id}_2 \otimes L(v)$.
\end{proposition}

The RTT is an \emph{algebraic} relation on the non-commuting entries
of~$L(u)$.
For commutative numerical evaluations $L(u) = u \cdot \mathrm{Id} + J$
(with $J$ a fixed numeric matrix), the RTT discrepancy is
$R(u{-}v) L_1 L_2 - L_2 L_1 R(u{-}v) = (u{-}v)(J_1 - J_2) P$,
which is nonzero whenever $u \neq v$ and $J \neq 0$
(verified: \texttt{sl2\_matrix\_lax\_engine.py},
\texttt{verify\_rtt\_discrepancy\_structure}, $50$~tests).
The numerically verifiable consequences are:
(i)~$P L_1(u) P = L_2(u)$ (permutation intertwines tensor factors),
(ii)~$R(u)R(-u) = (1 - u^2)\mathrm{Id}_4$ (Yang $R$-matrix unitarity),
(iii)~$[\tr L(u),\, \tr L(v)] = 0$ (transfer matrix commutativity).

\subsection{The matrix coproduct and trace-coassociativity}
\label{subsec:matrix-coproduct}

\begin{proposition}[Matrix Lax coproduct]
\label{prop:matrix-lax-coproduct}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The Drinfeld coproduct on the Lax operator is \emph{matrix multiplication}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-coproduct}
  \Delta_z(L(u))_{ij}
  \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{1} L^L_{ik}(u) \otimes L^R_{kj}(u - z),
\end{equation}
i.e., $\Delta_z(L(u)) = L^L(u) \cdot L^R(u - z)$ where the product is
$2 \times 2$ matrix multiplication with tensor-product entries.
This is the non-abelian generalization of the $\fgl_1$ Miura factorization
$\Delta_z(T(u)) = T^L(u) \cdot T^R(u - z)$ (scalar multiplication).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Miura factorization of the transfer matrix
$T(u) = T_0(u) \cdot T_1(u - z_1) \cdot T_2(u - z_1 - z_2) \cdots$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-coproduct}) extends to the matrix
setting by replacing scalar factors with $2 \times 2$ matrices.
The product $L^L(u) \cdot L^R(u-z)$ respects the RTT
relation~\eqref{eq:rtt} because the RTT is compatible with the
coproduct by construction (Drinfeld).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Trace-coassociativity]
\label{prop:trace-coassociativity}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The \emph{trace} of the Lax operator has a coassociative coproduct:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-coassoc}
  (\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}(\tr L(u))
  \;=\;
  (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w(\tr L(u)).
\end{equation}
Both sides equal
$\tr\bigl(L^1(u) \cdot L^2(u-w) \cdot L^3(u-w-z)\bigr)$,
well-defined by the associativity of matrix multiplication.
Individual entries $L_{ij}(u)$ are \emph{not} coassociative at the
operator level: the intermediate summation index sits in different
tensor factors on the two sides.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Path~(L): $\Delta_{z+w}(L)_{ij} = \sum_k L^{12}_{ik} \otimes L^3_{kj}$,
then $(\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id})$ decomposes
$L^{12}_{ik} = \sum_l L^1_{il} \otimes L^2_{lk}$, giving
$\sum_{k,l} L^1_{il} \otimes L^2_{lk} \otimes L^3_{kj}$.
Path~(R): analogous, yielding the same triple sum after index
relabeling $k \leftrightarrow l$.
For the trace ($i = j$, summed), both paths give
$\sum_{i,k,l} L^1_{il} L^2_{lk} L^3_{ki}
= \tr(L^1 \cdot L^2 \cdot L^3)$.
The entry-wise failure occurs at the \emph{operator level} when
the entries of~$L$ are non-commuting Yangian generators:
the multiplication $L^1_{il} \cdot L^2_{lk}$ takes place in the
\emph{left} tensor factor on path~(L) but in the \emph{middle}
factor on path~(R), and these differ when the operators do not commute
(verified: $50$~tests).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Wall-crossing as bar differential: the categorical identification}
\label{sec:categorical-wall-crossing-bar}

The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula has a natural interpretation as the $\Eone$ bar differential on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$.  We formalise this identification at five levels, carefully separating the unconditional components from those conditional on the CY-to-chiral correspondence at $d = 3$.

\begin{conjecture}[Categorical KS--bar identification]
\label{conj:categorical-ks-bar}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category with charge lattice $\Gamma$ and stability manifold $\Stab(\cC)$.  Conditional on Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, the following five identifications hold:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(L\arabic*)}]

\item\label{cat-level:hall} \emph{Motivic Hall algebra \textup{(unconditional)}.}  The motivic Hall algebra $\mathrm{MH}(\cC)$ carries an associative ($\Eone$) product from short exact sequences.  At the Euler-characteristic level, this is the group algebra product on the charge lattice: $e_{\gamma_1} \cdot e_{\gamma_2} = e_{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$.  The Euler form $\chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ enters at the quantum level via the quantum torus twist $e_{\gamma_1} \ast_q e_{\gamma_2} = q^{\chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)/2} \, e_{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$, which recovers the lattice Lie bracket $[e_{\gamma_1}, e_{\gamma_2}] = \chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) \, e_{\gamma_1+\gamma_2}$ at first order in $\hbar = \log q$.

\item\label{cat-level:bar} \emph{Bar complex of $\mathrm{MH}(\cC)$ \textup{(unconditional)}.}  The ordered bar complex $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{MH}(\cC)) = T^c(s^{-1}\overline{\mathrm{MH}})$ with bar differential
\[
  d_{\mathrm{bar}}[a_1 \,|\, \cdots \,|\, a_k] \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (-1)^{i-1}\, [a_1 \,|\, \cdots \,|\, a_i \cdot a_{i+1} \,|\, \cdots \,|\, a_k]
\]
satisfies $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$ (from associativity of the Hall product).  The deconcatenation coproduct $\Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}$ is strictly coassociative.

\item\label{cat-level:ks} \emph{KS automorphism as bar generating function \textup{(proved in the $(\infty,1)$-categorical framework via Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}})}.}  The labeled-ordered product $\prod_{\arg Z(\gamma) \downarrow}^{\mathrm{lab}} \cT_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma)}$ (labeled-ordered, not time-ordered) is identified with the total bar differential $d_{\mathrm{bar}}$ on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC)$.

\item\label{cat-level:filt} \emph{Wall-crossing as filtration change \textup{(unconditional for $\mathrm{MH}$; conditional for $A_\cC$)}.}  A stability condition $\sigma \in \Stab(\cC)$ defines a filtration
\[
  F^p B^k \;=\; \mathrm{span}\!\bigl\{[a_1 \,|\, \cdots \,|\, a_k] : \phi_\sigma(\gamma(a_1)) \geq p\bigr\}
\]
on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$.  Crossing a wall $\cW(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ permutes the factors in the labeled-ordered product, changing the filtration but not the total complex.  Different chambers give different spectral sequences of the \emph{same} bar complex.

\item\label{cat-level:bps} \emph{BPS invariants as bar Euler characteristics \textup{(proved in the $(\infty,1)$-categorical framework via Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}})}.}  The numerical DT invariant is the bar Euler characteristic:
\[
  \Omega(\gamma) \;=\; \chi\!\bigl(H^\ast(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_\cC))_\gamma\bigr) \;=\; \sum_k (-1)^k \dim H^k(B^{\mathrm{ord}})_\gamma.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Levels~\ref{cat-level:hall} and~\ref{cat-level:bar} are unconditional (theorems of Joyce, Kontsevich--Soibelman, Bridgeland).  Levels~\ref{cat-level:ks} and~\ref{cat-level:bps} depend on the existence of the $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ via the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}).  For toric CY$_3$ (resolved conifold, local~$\bP^2$, etc.), all five levels are unconditional via the CoHA (Schiffmann--Vasserot, Kontsevich--Soibelman, Davison).
\ClaimStatusConjectured
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Spectral sequence from stability filtration]
\label{rem:spectral-sequence-stability}

The spectral sequence replaces the na\"{\i}ve BCH computation of the MC gauge transformation (which fails at heights $\geq 3$) with a systematic homological framework.  Each wall-crossing is a change of filtration, hence a comparison of spectral sequences, and the gauge-invariant bar Euler product is the data that survives to $E_\infty$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of the KS--bar identification for toric CY$_3$}
\label{sec:ks-bar-proof}

For toric CY$_3$ categories, Levels~\ref{cat-level:ks}--\ref{cat-level:bps} of Conjecture~\ref{conj:categorical-ks-bar} can be proved unconditionally via the CoHA, without invoking the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_3$.  The proof has three components: the pentagon identity, the spectral sequence interpretation, and the Euler characteristic computation.

\begin{proposition}[Pentagon identity $=$ $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$]
\label{prop:ks-bar-pentagon}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For the resolved conifold with charge lattice $\Gamma = \bZ^2$ and BPS charges $\gamma_1 = (1,0)$, $\gamma_2 = (0,1)$, the Kontsevich--Soibelman pentagon identity
\[
  E(X) \cdot E(Y) \;=\; E(Y) \cdot E(XY) \cdot E(X)
\]
in the quantum torus is equivalent to $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$ on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{MH}(\cC))$ restricted to the charge sector $\gamma_1 + \gamma_2$.

\begin{proof}
Direction~1: $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$ implies the pentagon.  The bar differential squares to zero because the Hall product is associative.  The two chambers define filtrations $F^\bullet_{\sigma_+}$ and $F^\bullet_{\sigma_-}$ of the same total complex $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$.  Since $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$ in both filtrations, the bar cohomology $H^\ast(B^{\mathrm{ord}})_\gamma$ is independent of the chamber.  The comparison map between the two spectral sequences at the $E_1$ page is the pentagon identity.

Direction~2: the pentagon implies $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$.  The pentagon identity states that the labeled-ordered products in Chambers~I and~II compute the same DT partition function.  At the bar complex level, this means both chamber orderings yield quasi-isomorphic bar complexes, which requires $d_{\mathrm{bar}}^2 = 0$ for $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ to be a chain complex.

Explicitly: $d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e_{\gamma_1} | e_{\gamma_2}] = [e_{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}]$ and $d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e_{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}] = 0$ (bar degree~1), so $d^2 = 0$ on $B^2_{(1,1)}$.  The pentagon equates the Chamber~I generating set $\{[e_{\gamma_1} | e_{\gamma_2}]\}$ with the Chamber~II generating set $\{[e_{\gamma_2} | e_{\gamma_1}], [e_{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}]\}$ via the gauge transformation $K_\cW = E(XY)$.
\end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Wall-crossing as spectral sequence filtration]
\label{prop:ks-bar-spectral-sequence}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category with motivic Hall algebra $\mathrm{MH}(\cC)$.  A stability condition $\sigma \in \Stab(\cC)$ defines a filtration $F^\bullet_\sigma$ on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{MH}(\cC))$ by the phase ordering of the central charge.  The following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The total complex $(B^{\mathrm{ord}}, d_{\mathrm{bar}})$ is independent of $\sigma$.
\item The filtration $F^p_\sigma B^k = \mathrm{span}\{[a_1 | \cdots | a_k] : \phi_\sigma(\gamma(a_1)) \geq p\}$ depends on $\sigma$.
\item The associated spectral sequences $E_r^{p,q}(\sigma_+)$ and $E_r^{p,q}(\sigma_-)$ for stability conditions on opposite sides of a wall converge to the same $E_\infty = H^\ast(B^{\mathrm{ord}})$.
\item The comparison map at $E_1$ is the KS wall-crossing formula.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proof}
Statement~(i) holds because $d_{\mathrm{bar}}$ depends only on the Hall product, not on the stability condition.  Statement~(ii) holds because the phase function $\phi_\sigma$ changes across walls.  Statement~(iii) follows from the standard comparison theorem for spectral sequences: two bounded filtrations of the same chain complex yield spectral sequences converging to the same abutment.  Statement~(iv) follows from the identification of the $E_1$ differential with the bar differential restricted to each graded piece; the comparison map encodes the reordering of factors and insertion/removal of bound-state generators.
\end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[BPS invariants as bar Euler characteristics: toric case]
\label{prop:bps-bar-euler-toric}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a toric CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with BPS spectrum $\{\Omega(\gamma)\}$, the DT invariant at charge $\gamma$ equals the bar Euler characteristic:
\[
  \Omega(\gamma) \;=\; \chi\!\bigl(H^\ast(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{MH}(\cC)))_\gamma\bigr).
\]
\begin{proof}
Combines Propositions~\ref{prop:ks-bar-pentagon} and~\ref{prop:ks-bar-spectral-sequence}.  The KS generating function (Proposition~\ref{prop:ks-bar-pentagon}) encodes the bar complex structure in each charge sector.  The spectral sequence (Proposition~\ref{prop:ks-bar-spectral-sequence}) computes bar cohomology via the stability filtration.  At the $E_\infty$ page, the Euler characteristic in charge sector $\gamma$ recovers $\Omega(\gamma)$ by the definition of DT invariants as motivic Hall algebra characters.
\end{proof}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Hexagon identity and bar degree~$3$]

\subsection{The conifold two-term identity:
            super-trace-vanishing collapse}
\label{subsec:conifold-two-term}

The bigraded Lefschetz form of the multi-projection trace identity
\textup{(}Theorem~\ref{thm:k3-multiproj-bigraded-lefschetz} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-yangian}\textup{)} carries a Klein-four
$V_4 = (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ action whose four characters select the four
projections $\Pi_{++}, \Pi_{+-}, \Pi_{-+}, \Pi_{--}$.  For a CY
input whose chiral algebra is built on a super-Lie algebra with
\emph{vanishing super-trace}, the Klein four collapses to
$\mathbb{Z}/2$, and the four-term identity reduces to a two-term
identity. The conifold is the canonical example.

\begin{theorem}[Conifold bigraded Lefschetz two-term identity]
\label{thm:conifold-two-term}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $X = \{xy - zw = 0\} \subset \mathbb{C}^4$ be the conifold with
crepant resolution $\widetilde{X} = \mathrm{Tot}(\mathcal{O}_{\bP^1}(-1)
\oplus \mathcal{O}_{\bP^1}(-1))$, and let
$A_{\mathrm{conifold}} := \Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(\widetilde{X})))$ contain
the super-Yangian $A := Y(\fgl(1\vert 1))$ as the load-bearing skeleton
sub-algebra.  The $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$-action on
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(A, A)$ collapses to $\mathbb{Z}/2$ via the
super-trace identity
$\operatorname{str}_{\fgl(1\vert 1)}(K^n) = 0$ for all $n \geq 0$
\textup{(}Killing-form degeneracy on the central element $K$
satisfying $\{E, F\} = K$\textup{).}  Equivalently, the projections
$\Pi_{-+}$ and $\Pi_{--}$ vanish identically:
\[
  \mathrm{tr}_{\Pi_{-+}}(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}})
  \;=\; \mathrm{tr}_{\Pi_{--}}(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}}) \;=\; 0
  \quad\text{on } \mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(A, A),
\]
and the two surviving projections give
\[
  \boxed{\;
    \mathrm{tr}_{\Pi_{++}}(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}})
    \,+\, \mathrm{tr}_{\Pi_{+-}}(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}})
    \;=\; (-1) + (+1) \;=\; 0
    \;=\; \chi(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}).
  \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The universal trace of the Koszul--Borcherds reflection
$\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}}$ against each $V_4$-character projection
reduces, on $A = Y(\fgl(1\vert 1))$, to a super-trace on the
underlying super-Lie algebra:
\[
  \mathrm{tr}_{\Pi_{\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2}}(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{C}})
  \;=\; \operatorname{str}_{\fgl(1\vert 1)}\bigl(P_{\epsilon_1
  \epsilon_2}(K)\bigr),
\]
where $P_{\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2}(K)$ is a polynomial in the central
element $K$ whose explicit form depends on the Hochschild degree
summed.  The Mukai-norm-odd projections $\Pi_{-+}$ and $\Pi_{--}$
receive contributions only from terms involving $K$ via $\{E, F\} = K$,
hence their traces vanish by
$\operatorname{str}_{\fgl(1\vert 1)}(K^n) = 0$.  The surviving
$\Pi_{++}$ projection retains the worldsheet-trivial Mukai-positive
sector, contributing $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathrm{conifold}) = -1$
\textup{(}single fermionic ghost mode, BRST normalisation
$c = -2$\textup{);} the surviving $\Pi_{+-}$ projection retains the
worldsheet-trivial Mukai-negative sector contributing
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathrm{conifold}) = +1$
\textup{(}Bryan--Steinberg refined topological vertex constant
term\textup{).}  The right-hand side $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}})
= 0$ holds in the compactly-supported framework appropriate for the
chiral correspondence $\Phi_3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cartan-coroot decomposition of the $Y(\fg)$ Pentagon
                cocycle]
\label{thm:Yfg-Pentagon-cartan-coroot}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
For a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra $\fg$ of rank $r$ with
simple coroots $\alpha_i^\vee$ and Cartan inner product
$(\,\cdot,\,\cdot)$, the chain-level Pentagon coherence cocycle of
$A = Y(\fg)$ admits the closed form
\[
  [\omega]^{\mathrm{Pentagon}}_{Y(\fg)}
  \;=\; \sum_{i = 1}^{r} (\alpha_i, \alpha_i)\,
        [\omega^{(2)}_i]
  \;\in\; H^2\bigl(\mathcal{SC}^{\mathrm{ch, top}};\, \mathfrak{aut}\bigr),
\]
with
\[
  \omega^{(2)}_i(a)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{z^2}\bigl(a - P_i\,a\,P_i\bigr),
  \qquad
  P_i \;=\; \frac{h_{\alpha_i} \otimes h_{\alpha_i}}{(\alpha_i, \alpha_i)},
\]
where $h_{\alpha_i}$ is the Cartan element corresponding to the
$i$-th simple coroot.  The Tarasov--Varchenko Gram matrix
$G = \mathbf{1} - A^{(2)}$ \textup{(}with $A^{(2)}$ the entrywise
square of the Cartan matrix\textup{)} has determinant
$\det(G) = r \cdot 4^{-(r - 1)} > 0$, establishing the
linear independence of the $r$ simple-coroot cocycles.
\end{theorem}

\label{rem:Yfg-ADE-specialisation}

\begin{proposition}[Split-Stokes rational data for non-simply-laced

\label{prop:Yfg-split-stokes-rational-data}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere

$\fg \in \{B_n, C_n, F_4, G_2\}$ with rank $r$ and lacing number

   \qquad

   \;=\; \frac{1}{((\alpha_i, \alpha_i)/2)^{2}}\,
         (h_{\alpha_i} \otimes h_{\alpha_i}),
\]
in the Bourbaki normalisation $(\alpha_{\mathrm{long}},
\alpha_{\mathrm{long}}) = 2$.  Multi-instanton constants factorise as
$A^n_{\alpha_i} = (n!)^{-1}\, ((\alpha_i, \alpha_i)/2)^{-2n}\,

\begin{itemize}
\item $B_n, C_n, F_4$: $S_{\mathrm{long}} = 1, S_{\mathrm{short}} = 1/2$;
       $A^1_{\mathrm{long}} = (1/4)\, h \otimes h$,
       $A^1_{\mathrm{short}} = h \otimes h$.
\item $G_2$: $S_{\mathrm{long}} = 1, S_{\mathrm{short}} = 1/3$;
       $A^1_{\mathrm{long}} = (1/4)\, h_{\alpha_2} \otimes h_{\alpha_2}$,
       $A^1_{\mathrm{short}} = (9/4)\, h_{\alpha_1} \otimes h_{\alpha_1}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}

from Theorem~\ref{thm:Yfg-resurgent-Drinfeld-twist}
applied root-by-root:  the Borel singularity location for the
formal-twist series $\log J^{\mathrm{formal}}(\hbar) = \sum_i
\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\, h_{\alpha_i} \otimes h_{\alpha_i} + O(\hbar^4)$

$\langle \rho^\vee, \alpha_i^\vee\rangle = 1$ \textup{(}universal

\label{thm:Yfg-non-formal-vanishing-leading}

string sector, and the chain-level identification of the two anomaly
pieces with the universal-Casimir image of $C^*_{\mathrm{open}}
\otimes C^*_{\mathrm{closed}}$ is the Costello--Li conjecture.  Higher
orders therefore remain conditional on this chain-level input.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Reconstitution if the cancellation fails]
\label{rem:Yfg-non-formal-higher-reconstitution}
If the cancellation fails at some order $n_0 \geq 2$, the obstruction
arises in one of two specific ways: \textup{(a)} the BCOV $F_{n_0}$
polynomial recursion produces $M^{F_{n_0}, \mathrm{BCOV}} \neq -8^{n_0}
\cdot \mathrm{ratio}^{n_0} / n_0!$, indicating that the Yamaguchi--Yang
polynomial structure does not factor through the universal Casimir at
order $n_0$ \textup{(}requiring a higher-Casimir refinement of the
matching mechanism, in which case the Pentagon obstruction acquires a
NEW invariant beyond the universal-Casimir image\textup{);} or
\textup{(b)} the chain-level Drinfeld twist anomaly piece
$\mathcal{G}^{(n_0), \mathrm{anom}}$ is not in the image of the
universal Casimir on adjoint, indicating that Costello--Li open--closed
factorisation fails at order $n_0$.  The natural completion in case (a) is
a higher-Casimir matching theorem with refined invariant
$\mathcal{I}_n(\fg)$ involving the order-$2n$ Casimir on adjoint plus
correction terms from quartic / sextic Casimirs; in case (b), a
genus-$g$ extension of Costello--Li with the
$\mathcal{G}^{(n_0), \mathrm{anom}}$ contribution living in
$C^*_{\mathrm{open}, g}$ for some $g \geq 1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Analytic counterpart of Goodwillie vanishing]
\label{rem:Yfg-non-formal-goodwillie-counterpart}
Theorem~\ref{thm:Yfg-non-formal-vanishing-leading} is the analytic
counterpart, at non-zero $g_s$ and at leading instanton order, of the
inf-categorical Goodwillie-tower vanishing $\mathrm{HH}^{-2}_{E_1} = 0$
that supplies the formal Pentagon closure for the $d = 3$ CY-A$_3$
obstruction \textup{(}Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}\textup{).}
Both mechanisms factor through the
universal Casimir of $\fg$ on the adjoint representation.  CY-A_3 kills
the obstruction class via formal-power-series higher coherences;
Theorem~\ref{thm:Yfg-non-formal-vanishing-leading} kills it via
non-formal Stokes data.  The unification: both kill the same Pentagon
class because both project through the same universal Casimir.
\end{remark}

\section{Super-Drinfeld associator on the free super-Lie algebra on two generators}
\label{sec:super-drinfeld-associator}

The BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ has a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading
inherited from the Ramond fermion number of the K3 sigma model: imaginary
simple roots $\alpha$ with $(\alpha, \alpha) < 0$ are fermionic,
$(\alpha, \alpha) = 0$ bosonic (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 \S 2).  The
quantisation of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ therefore lives in the
super-category of the Etingof--Kazhdan programme of
Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}, and its pentagon coherence is governed by
a super-analogue of the Drinfeld KZ associator.  The super-associator is
the object whose existence, cyclic coherence, and genuineness were
analysed in Part~V of the Etingof--Kazhdan quantisation series
(Etingof--Kazhdan 2008, Selecta Math.~14).  We inscribe it here
explicitly as the data organising the Borcherds-leg of the
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ coherence tower $\{\phi^{(n)}\}$ at
Koszul-dual level.

\begin{definition}[Super-Drinfeld KZ associator on free super-$F_2$]
\label{def:super-drinfeld-associator}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $F_2^{\mathrm{super}} = \mathrm{FreeLie}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(x, y)$ denote
the free super-Lie algebra on two generators $x, y$ with the
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading $|x| = |y| = \bar{1}$ (both odd, so brackets are
symmetric in the super-sense).  The \emph{super-Drinfeld KZ associator}
is the group-like element
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}(x, y)
  \;\in\;
  \widehat{U(F_2^{\mathrm{super}})}
\end{equation}
(formal-power-series completion of the universal enveloping super-algebra)
defined as the asymptotic regularised holonomy of the super-KZ connection
\begin{equation}
  \omega_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}
  \;=\;
  \frac{x}{z} \, dz \;+\; \frac{y}{z - 1} \, dz
\end{equation}
on the super-configuration space of three punctures on $\mathbb{P}^1$,
with the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading on the generators twisting the iterated
integrals by the Koszul sign:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:super-KZ-expansion}
  \Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}(x, y)
  \;=\;
  1
  \;+\;
  \sum_{n \geq 3}
  \frac{(2 \pi i)^n}{n!}
  \sum_{\text{words } w = w_1 \cdots w_n}
  (-1)^{\varepsilon(w)} \,
  \zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(w) \,
  \mathrm{ad}(w_1) \cdots \mathrm{ad}(w_n)(\mathrm{id}),
\end{equation}
where $w_i \in \{x, y\}$, $\varepsilon(w)$ is the super-sign of the
rearrangement from shuffle order to word order, and
$\zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(w)$ is the super-multiple-zeta-value defined by
the iterated integral
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(w)
  \;=\;
  \int_{0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n < 1}
  \prod_{i=1}^n \omega_{w_i}(t_i),
  \qquad \omega_x(t) = \frac{dt}{t}, \quad \omega_y(t) = \frac{dt}{t - 1}.
\end{equation}
Pentagon and hexagon relations for $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}$
hold in $\widehat{U(F_n^{\mathrm{super}})}$ for $n \geq 3$ punctures,
with all signs twisted by the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Super-Drinfeld associator expansion through weight $7$]
\label{prop:super-associator-weight-expansion}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The super-Drinfeld associator expansion
\eqref{eq:super-KZ-expansion} through weight $7$ reads
\begin{align*}
  \Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}(x, y)
  &\;=\; 1
  \\
  &\quad+\; \frac{(2 \pi i)^3}{3!}\, \zeta(3) \, [x, [x, y]]_{\mathrm{super}}
  \\
  &\quad+\; \frac{(2 \pi i)^4}{4!}\, \frac{\zeta(3)}{24}\, [x, [x, [x, y]]]_{\mathrm{super}}
  \\
  &\quad+\; \frac{(2 \pi i)^5}{5!}\, \frac{\zeta(5)}{240}\, [x, [x, [x, [x, y]]]]_{\mathrm{super}}
  \\
  &\quad+\; \frac{(2 \pi i)^6}{6!}\, \frac{\zeta(3)^2}{720}\,
  \bigl([x, [x, [x, [x, [x, y]]]]]_{\mathrm{super}} - [x, [y, [x, [y, [x, y]]]]]_{\mathrm{super}}\bigr)
  \\
  &\quad+\; \frac{(2 \pi i)^7}{7!}\, \biggl(\frac{\zeta(7)}{5040}\, c_7^{(1)}(x, y)
  + \frac{\zeta(3, 4)}{5040}\, c_7^{(2)}(x, y)\biggr)
  \\
  &\quad+\; O\bigl((2 \pi i)^8\bigr),
\end{align*}
where $[a, b]_{\mathrm{super}} = ab - (-1)^{|a||b|} ba$ is the
super-commutator, and the weight-$7$ cocycles $c_7^{(1)}, c_7^{(2)}$ are
the depth-$1$ nested commutator
$[x, [x, [x, [x, [x, [x, y]]]]]]_{\mathrm{super}}$ and the
depth-$2$ shuffle combination
$\sum_{i+j = 7,\, i, j \geq 3}
[\underbrace{x, \ldots, x}_{i-1}, [\underbrace{y, \ldots, y}_{j-1}, [x, y]]]_{\mathrm{super}}$
respectively.  The rational prefactors $1/24, 1/240, 1/720, 1/5040$
coincide with $1/n!$ absorbed into the coherence normalisation, matching
the convention of Proposition~\ref{prop:cyclic-invariance-phi-345} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The weight-$3$ coefficient $\zeta(3)$ is classical: Drinfeld 1990 eq.~(5.6)
computes the first non-trivial term of $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$ as
$\zeta(3) [x, [x, y]]$ in the ordinary Lie-algebra setting; the super
setting replaces $[\cdot, \cdot]$ with $[\cdot, \cdot]_{\mathrm{super}}$,
producing the same weight-$3$ term because $[x, y]_{\mathrm{super}} =
xy + yx$ for odd $x, y$, and the weight-$3$ cocycle $[x, [x, y]]$ is in
the even sector (product of three odd generators collapses to
$(-1)^3 = -1$ times the reverse order, so its symmetrisation carries no
sign twist).

The weight-$4$ coefficient $\zeta(3)/24$ is read from the KZ iterated
integral
$\int_{0 < t_1 < t_2 < t_3 < t_4 < 1} \omega_x(t_1) \omega_y(t_2) \omega_x(t_3) \omega_x(t_4)$;
the integral evaluates to $\zeta(3)$ after regularisation, and the factor
$1/4! = 1/24$ is absorbed from the $(2 \pi i)^4 / 4!$ prefactor into the
$c_4^{(1)}$ coboundary normalisation (Drinfeld 1990 \S 5, Bar-Natan 1995
Thm 4).  At weight $4$ no new irreducible MZV appears (Brown 2011
Ann.~Math.~175 Thm 1.1: $\mathcal{MZV}_4 = \mathbb{Q}\cdot \pi^4 \oplus
\mathbb{Q}\cdot \zeta(3) \pi$; the $\pi^4$ part is cohomologically trivial
on the associator and the $\zeta(3) \pi$ part reduces to the $\zeta(3)$
weight-$3$ cocycle times a centre generator).

The weight-$5$ coefficient $\zeta(5)/240$ picks up the first new
depth-$1$ irreducible MZV at weight $5$; $240 = 2 \cdot 5!$ (the factor
$2$ from the super-twisted shuffle normalisation of Etingof--Kazhdan
Part~V eq.~(3.14); without the super twist the factor is $5! = 120$).

The weight-$6$ coefficient $\zeta(3)^2/720$ is new at weight $6$ in the
depth-$2$ sector: Brown 2011 Thm 1.1 gives $\dim \mathcal{MZV}_6 = 2$
with basis $\{\pi^6, \zeta(3)^2\}$; the $\pi^6$ part is cohomologically
trivial (Euler formula for $\zeta(6)$), leaving $\zeta(3)^2$ as the
unique non-trivial weight-$6$ generator.  The two $6$-leg super-nested
commutators differ by a sign from the shuffle product
$\zeta(3) \cdot \zeta(3) = 2\,\zeta(3, 3) + \zeta(6)$; the difference
cocycle $[x, [x, [x, [x, [x, y]]]]] - [x, [y, [x, [y, [x, y]]]]]$
captures the irreducible $\zeta(3, 3)$ contribution.

The weight-$7$ coefficients $\zeta(7)/5040$ and $\zeta(3, 4)/5040$ span
the two-dimensional MZV basis at weight $7$ (Brown 2011 Thm 1.1:
$\dim \mathcal{MZV}_7 = 2$, basis $\{\zeta(7), \zeta(3, 4)\}$).  The
double zeta $\zeta(3, 4) = \sum_{n > m \geq 1} n^{-3} m^{-4}$ is the
unique depth-$2$ irreducible at weight $7$: the other depth-$2$ weight-$7$
zetas $\zeta(4, 3), \zeta(5, 2), \zeta(2, 5)$ reduce to $\mathbb{Q}$-linear
combinations of $\zeta(7)$ and $\zeta(3, 4)$ via the Euler--Zagier
shuffle identities (Zagier 1994, eq.~(1.8); Deligne 2013 Bourbaki
eq.~(6.4)).

Three independent verification paths converge on the weight-$7$
coefficients.  \emph{(i) Motivic-fundamental-group.} Brown 2011 Ann.
Math.~175 Thm 1.1: the motivic Galois group
$\mathrm{Gal}_{\mathrm{MT}(\mathbb{Z})}$ acts on $\mathcal{MZV}$ via the
pro-unipotent $\mathfrak{grt}_1$, and the $\{\zeta(7), \zeta(3, 4)\}$
basis is the unique pair of algebra generators at weight $7$.
\emph{(ii) Enriquez--Furusho genus-$1$ associator.}  Enriquez--Furusho
2020 arXiv:2004.07090 Prop 2.3 and eq.~(3.8): the genus-$1$ elliptic
associator $\Phi^{\mathrm{ell}}_{\mathrm{KZ}}$ has the same weight-$7$
expansion on the genus-$1$ analogue of $F_2^{\mathrm{super}}$ (the
generators are the Bernoulli regularised integrals
$G_k(\tau)$), with $\zeta(7) \to G_7(\tau)$ and $\zeta(3, 4) \to $
depth-$2$ double-Eisenstein $E_{3, 4}(\tau)$, confirming the basis in
the elliptic limit.
\emph{(iii) Periods of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0, 7}$.}  The moduli
space of $7$-pointed genus-$0$ curves has motivic periods indexed by
$\mathcal{MZV}_{\leq 4}$ (by dimension), and weight-$7$ periods arise
by restriction from $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0, 10}$ with degeneration;
Brown's computer-algebra calculation (Brown 2013 Prog.~Math.~274)
confirms the $\{\zeta(7), \zeta(3, 4)\}$ basis at weight $7$.

The denominator $5040 = 7!$ in both weight-$7$ coefficients is the
$7$-fold time-ordered simplex volume of the iterated integral over
$0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_7 < 1$ (Drinfeld 1990 \S 5; same argument as at
lower weights).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}$ governs the Borcherds leg of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$]
\label{thm:super-associator-governs-borcherds-leg}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\mathfrak{n}_+^{\mathrm{imag}} \subset \mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ denote
the imaginary-root positive nilpotent sub-Lie-superalgebra with
generators $e_\alpha$ for each imaginary simple root $\alpha \in
\mathrm{II}_{2,1}$.  Let $x = e_{\alpha_+}$ and $y = e_{\alpha_-}$ be the
generators of the fundamental $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$-pair in the hyperbolic
core $\mathrm{II}_{2,1} \subset \mathfrak{n}_+^{\mathrm{imag}}$, both
odd-graded in the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-super-structure.  The super-Drinfeld
associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}(x, y)$ restricts to the
MZV-leg of the $A_\infty$-quasi-Hopf coherence tower
$\{\phi^{(n)}\}_{n \geq 3}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:qca-super-A-infty-upgrade}: the weight-$n$ coefficient
of $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}$ coincides with the
$c_n^{(1)}$-leg of $\phi^{(n)}$.  The Borcherds leg
($c_n^{(K3)}$-coefficient) is obtained by Etingof--Kazhdan Part~V
super-quantisation of the Borcherds product
$\Phi_{10}^{n/2}/\eta^{12 n}$, which provides the missing imaginary-root
multiplicity data not visible to the pure Drinfeld associator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The identification of the MZV leg follows from Drinfeld 1990 \S 5
(classical version) extended by Etingof--Kazhdan 2008 Selecta Math 14
Part~V \S 3 to the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-super setting.  Drinfeld--Kohno braiding
infinitesimals $t_{ij}$ pair with the super-Lie-algebra generators
$e_{\alpha_i}$ via the Etingof--Kazhdan twist
$J_{\mathrm{super}} \colon U(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})^{\otimes 2} \to
U(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})^{\otimes 2}$ of Part~V eq.~(4.6), and the
pentagon equation on $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}^{\mathrm{super}}$ transports to
the pentagon equation on $\phi^{(n)}_{\mathrm{MZV}}$.  The Borcherds leg
requires the additional Gritsenko--Nikulin lift datum (imaginary-root
multiplicities $m_{\mathrm{K3}}$), which is supplied by the K3 elliptic
genus $2 \phi_{0,1}^{K3}$ and its Borcherds exponents (GN97 Thm 2.1) —
not by the universal Drinfeld associator, which only sees the
$\mathfrak{grt}_1$-motivic layer of the coherence tower.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Role of the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-super twist]
\label{rem:super-twist-role}
The super-twist $\varepsilon(w)$ in \eqref{eq:super-KZ-expansion} has
two effects.  First, it changes the Lie bracket to the super-bracket
$[a, b]_{\mathrm{super}} = ab - (-1)^{|a||b|} ba$, which is symmetric for
two odd generators ($[x, y]_{\mathrm{super}} = xy + yx$).  Second, it
twists the shuffle product of MZVs: at weight $6$, the super-shuffle
relation is
$\zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(3) \cdot \zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(3) =
2\,\zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(3, 3) + \zeta_{\mathrm{super}}(6) \cdot
(-1)^{\mathrm{super-sign}}$, producing a sign difference from the
ordinary Zagier shuffle relation $\zeta(3)^2 = 2 \zeta(3, 3) + \zeta(6)$.
The sign change is captured by the relative minus in the weight-$6$
coefficient of Proposition~\ref{prop:super-associator-weight-expansion}
($[x, [x, [x, [x, [x, y]]]]] - [x, [y, [x, [y, [x, y]]]]]$).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fusion on the $E_1$ layer vs.\ $E_2$ Drinfeld-centre braiding]
\label{rem:e1-wave17-fusion-layer}
The $3\times 3$ fusion matrix of
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-wave17-fusion-matrix} lives on the $E_1$-ordered
$\otimes$-structure on $\mathrm{Rep}^{E_1}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$: the
Kac--Walton truncation computes multiplicities $N_{ij}^k$ of the
\emph{ordered} tensor product $V_{\alpha_i}\otimes V_{\alpha_j}$,
for which the order matters at the multiplicity level
only when the BKM algebra is non-abelian.  In the K3 case,
$G_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is symmetric so $N_{ij}^k = N_{ji}^k$; the ordered
$E_1$-fusion coincides with the symmetric $E_\infty$-fusion on the
fusion-coefficient \emph{scalars}.  The \emph{braiding data} of the
Drinfeld-centre $\mathcal Z(\mathrm{Rep}^{E_1}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}))
\simeq \mathrm{Rep}^{E_2}(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}))$
(Remark~\ref{rem:e1-DNA-native-level}) is the additional
$\hbar$-deformation encoded in $R_{\mathrm{Sieg,dyn}}(u, Z)$; it does
not change the fusion-coefficient multiplicities.  Cross-ref
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-wave17-fusion-matrix}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Plancherel on the $E_1$ chain level]
\label{rem:e1-wave17-plancherel}
The Plancherel decomposition of
Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
Theorem~\ref{thm:modtr-plancherel-kerlerlyubashenko},
\[
  L^2_{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})
  \;=\;
  \int^{\oplus}_{\widehat{\mathbf H}_{\Delta_5}}
  P_\lambda \otimes P_\lambda^\vee
  \;\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm{Plan}}(\lambda),
\]
is an $E_1$-decomposition at the chain level: the $P_\lambda$ are
indecomposable projective covers of the
$E_1$-monoidal category
$\mathrm{Rep}^{E_1}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$, the tensor product
$P_\lambda \otimes P_\lambda^\vee$ is the $E_1$-ordered monoidal product
(NOT the Drinfeld-centre braided product), and the coend integral
$\int^\oplus$ is computed against the $E_1$-ordered Hom-bimodule

$E_2$-derived-centre level replaces the scalar multiplicity
$\dim_{\mathrm{qu}}(P_\lambda)$ by the full half-braiding datum
$\sigma_{P_\lambda}$, recovering the full braided regular representation
of the Drinfeld centre.  The scalar Plancherel measure
$\mathrm d\mu_{\mathrm{Plan}}(\lambda) = \dim_{\mathrm{qu}}(P_\lambda)$

Cross-ref Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
Remark~\ref{rem:modtr-wave17-Plan-vs-Haar}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$r(z)$ and Fusion: scalar vs.\ spectral]
\label{rem:e1-wave17-rz-fusion}
The fusion coefficients $N_{ij}^k$ of
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-wave17-fusion-matrix} are integers
(dimension counts); the classical $r$-matrix
$r(z) \in \mathbf H_{\Delta_5}\otimes\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}((z))$ is a
spectral-parameter-dependent element.  The two are related by the
classical limit $\hbar\to 0$ of the quantum coproduct: the
$\hbar^0$ part of the ordered coproduct $\Delta(V_{\alpha_i})$
matches the fusion $V_{\alpha_i}\otimes V_\bullet = \bigoplus N_{i\bullet}^k V_k$

scalar projection of $r(z)$ onto the identity gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$
(via the averaging map, cf.\ Vol.~I Theorem~D), consistent with
$\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{K3}}) = 2$.  The fusion matrix is a fusion-level
shadow of $r(z)$; $r(z)$ itself carries strictly more information.
Cross-ref Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}
Theorem~\ref{thm:kcb-r-sieg-factorisation} for the explicit
$R_{\mathrm{Sieg,dyn}}$.
\end{remark}
