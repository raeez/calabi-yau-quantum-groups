\chapter{$E_1$-Chiral Algebras}
\label{ch:e1-chiral}

Braided output is too coarse for the first questions of Vol~III. The quantum group, the Yangian, and the collision residue all live on an ordered $E_1$ layer that remembers the direction of collisions. The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ reaches its braided $E_2$ image only through that primitive step, so this chapter fixes the ordered conventions used in the rest of the volume.

\begin{remark}[$E_1$ primacy for CY quantum groups]
\label{rem:e1-primacy-vol3}
The $E_1$-chiral algebra (boundary) is the primitive object in this volume.
The $E_2$-chiral algebra (bulk) is obtained from it by the Drinfeld center
construction $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$.
Quantum groups, Yangians, and braided tensor categories are natively $E_1$
objects: the CoHA multiplication is ordered (short exact sequences have a
preferred direction), and the $R$-matrix arises only in the Drinfeld double.
The passage $E_1 \to E_2$ is the higher-categorical analogue of the
averaging map $\mathrm{av} \colon \fg^{E_1} \to \fg^{\mathrm{mod}}$
from Vol~I.
This averaging is \emph{lossy}: $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ forgets the
$R$-matrix data. The $E_1$-bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ retains
strictly more information than the $E_\infty$-bar $B^{\Sigma}(A)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Factorization algebras and the $E_n$ hierarchy}
\label{sec:factorization-en}

The first tension is immediate: a factorization algebra on a complex curve is topologically $E_2$, while the ordered bar and the Yangian are $E_1$. Holomorphy resolves the tension. Costello and Gwilliam~\cite{CostelloGwilliam} showed that the value on a small disk carries an $E_n$-algebra structure, and Lurie's universal statement is $\mathrm{Fact}(\bR^n) \simeq \Alg_{E_n}$. For a complex curve $C$ of complex dimension one, $C$ has real dimension two, so factorization starts as $E_2$ topologically before the holomorphic ordering picks an $E_1$ slice.

\begin{proposition}[Holomorphic refinement, Costello-Gwilliam]
\label{prop:holomorphic-e1}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cA$ be a factorization algebra on a smooth complex curve $C$ whose structure maps are holomorphic in the two-point configuration space. The associated $E_2$-algebra specializes to an $E_1$-algebra on the configuration space of ordered points along any real ray, with the ordering provided by the holomorphic argument.
\end{proposition}

The proof is operadic: the little two-disks operad contracts onto the little intervals operad along the real subspace, and holomorphy pins the contraction to a single slice. Beilinson and Drinfeld~\cite{BeilinsonDrinfeld} studied the symmetric version of this specialization; they called the outcome a \emph{chiral algebra}. Volume~II elevates the ordered version.

\begin{definition}[Swiss-cheese operad]
\label{def:swiss-cheese-split}
The Swiss-cheese operad $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$
of Volume~II is a \emph{two-coloured} operad with colours
$\{\mathrm{ch}, \mathrm{top}\}$ (closed/holomorphic and
open/topological). Its closed-to-closed operations are
parametrised by $\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C})$ (the $E_2$ operad);
its open-to-open operations by $E_1(\mathbb{R})$ (ordered
configurations); and its mixed operations (closed acting on
open) by $\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C}) \times E_1(m)$.
Open-to-closed operations are \emph{empty} (directionality
constraint). An $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$-algebra
is a pair $(A, M)$ where $A$ is an $E_2$-algebra (the closed
colour), $M$ is an $E_1$-algebra (the open colour), and $A$
acts on $M$ via the mixed operations.

$\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$ is \emph{not} the
tensor product $E_1 \otimes E_2$: the directionality constraint
(no open-to-closed) and the mixed operations make it a genuinely
two-coloured operad. Dunn additivity does not apply.
The $E_3$-chiral upgrade requires a 3d
holomorphic-topological theory (proved for Kac--Moody
via holomorphic Chern--Simons; conjectural in general).
The further $E_3$-topological upgrade requires a conformal
vector at non-critical level
(Volume~I, Theorem~\textup{\ref*{thm:topologization}}).
\end{definition}

The closed colour carries $E_2$ structure from
$\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C})$: holomorphic, braided, and the
home of the chiral algebra. The open colour carries $E_1$
structure from ordered real configurations: topological,
noncommutative, and the home of the ordered bar complex.
Standard Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral algebras are
$E_\infty$-chiral (symmetric OPE); the $E_1$ open colour
captures the additional ordering data. The
$\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum of this volume distributes across the two
colours: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is read from the open colour
(the ordered bar), $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ from the closed
colour (the Euler characteristic), and the Drinfeld center
exchanges them.

\begin{remark}[Level-prefixed $r$-matrix]
\label{rem:level-prefixed-r-matrix}
The classical $r$-matrix attached to an affine Kac-Moody $E_1$-chiral algebra at level $k$ is
\[
 r(z) = \frac{k\,\Omega}{z},
\]
not the level-stripped $\frac{\Omega}{z}$. The level survives the $d\log$ absorption because the ordered bar complex builds in one factor of the level per collision. At $k=0$ the $r$-matrix vanishes identically. The collision residue of the Heisenberg $r$-matrix is $k$, not $k/2$, and the monodromy of the $E_1$ representation category around a puncture is $\exp(-2\pi i k)$.
\end{remark}

The level-prefix convention is the standing convention throughout this volume. Every time an $r$-matrix is written in the CY-geometric setting, the level constant prefactor is the image of the trace under the CY-to-chiral functor and must be tracked explicitly.

\section{The ordered bar as the $E_1$ primitive}
\label{sec:ordered-bar-primitive}

Braiding forgets collision order. The ordered bar keeps it. Let $A$ be a chiral algebra on $C$ with augmentation $\varepsilon \colon A \to \Omega_C$ and augmentation ideal $\bar A = \ker(\varepsilon)$.

\begin{definition}[Ordered bar complex]
\label{def:ordered-bar}
The \emph{ordered bar complex} of $A$ is the cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra
\[
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) := T^c(s^{-1} \bar A) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} (s^{-1}\bar A)^{\otimes n}
\]
equipped with the \emph{deconcatenation} coproduct
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}(a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_k) \otimes (a_{k+1} \otimes \dots \otimes a_n)
\]
and the bar differential built from the chiral product of $A$.
\end{definition}

Deconcatenation is NOT the coshuffle coproduct on $\mathrm{Sym}^c$. Deconcatenation produces $n+1$ terms and respects the ordering; the coshuffle produces $2^n$ terms and destroys it. The symmetric bar complex $B^{\Sigma}(A)$ is the $S_n$-coinvariant quotient of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ with the coshuffle coproduct induced on invariants.

\begin{proposition}[Averaging map]
\label{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The averaging map
\[
 \mathrm{av} \colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) \longrightarrow B^{\Sigma}(A),
 \qquad a_1 \otimes \dots \otimes a_n \longmapsto \tfrac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma \in S_n} a_{\sigma(1)} \otimes \dots \otimes a_{\sigma(n)}
\]
is a morphism of cochain complexes and sends the $E_1$ structure to the $E_\infty$ structure. It is lossy: the kernel contains the $R$-matrix data of the holomorphic factor, and on degree two $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\end{proposition}

Volume~I establishes the map; Volume~II identifies it as the $E_1 \to E_\infty$ symmetrization. For Volume~III purposes, the two consequences that matter are: (a) Yangians and quantum groups live on the $E_1$ side and are quotiented by averaging; (b) the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma$ is sufficient for computing the modular characteristic but insufficient for reconstructing the $R$-matrix.

Averaging the degree-two generator $r(z)$ returns the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, the unique $S_2$-coinvariant of the collision residue. When the same $r(z)$ comes from the CY-to-chiral functor applied to $D^b(\Coh(K3 \times E))$, the scalar is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ by additivity: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 2 + 1$. This differs from the categorical Euler characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = 2$, the lattice-rank invariant $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$, and the BKM weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$. An unsubscripted symbol would conflate distinct invariants.

\begin{remark}[Three bars, one functor]
\label{rem:three-bars-vol3}
The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-chiral-functor} has three natural composites. $\Phi_{E_1}$ produces $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_C)$; this is the ordered bar sensed by the Yangian. $\mathrm{av} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$ produces $B^{\Sigma}(A_C)$; this is the symmetric bar sensed by the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The full $E_2$ enhancement $\Phi_{E_2}$ produces the Drinfeld-centered bar $B^{E_2}(A_C) = \cZ(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_C))$; this is the braided bar sensed by the quantum group. All three factor through $\Phi_{E_1}$. This is the volume's motto: the ordered bar is the primitive.
\end{remark}

\section{$E_1$-chiral algebras from CY categories}
\label{sec:e1-from-cy}

The CY input has to produce an ordered chiral object before any braided output can exist. For a CY$_d$ category $\cC$ in the sense of Kontsevich-Soibelman, the trace lives in $\mathrm{HC}^-_d(\cC)$, not merely in Hochschild homology, and that trace feeds the ordered $E_1$ structure first.

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$ sector at $d=2$]
\label{prop:e1-sector-d2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_2$ category with cyclic $A_\infty$ structure and negative-cyclic trace. The ordered bar complex of the cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra $A_\cC$ carries a natural $E_1$-chiral structure whose holomorphic direction matches the Hochschild differential and whose ordered direction matches the cyclic action on the bar coalgebra.
\end{proposition}

The proof unfolds in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-chiral-functor}. For $\cC = D^b(\Coh(K3))$ the output is the positive half of the BKM superalgebra studied in \ref{part:examples}: the Borcherds denominator arises as the Euler character of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A_{K3})$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ is distinct from $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$.

\begin{conjecture}[$E_1$ sector at $d=3$]
\label{conj:e1-sector-d3}
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category with a chain-level $S^3$-framing on $\mathrm{HC}^-_3(\cC)$ (the condition of Conjecture~CY-A$_3$). Then the ordered bar complex of the cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra $A_\cC$ carries an $E_1$-chiral structure whose representation category is a braided monoidal refinement of the BPS Yangian of $\cC$.
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture is conditional on the chain-level framing; the unconditional statement requires the full CY-A$_3$ programme. Every downstream result in \ref{part:examples} and \ref{part:connections} that uses the BPS Yangian inherits this conditionality. The connection to Maulik-Okounkov and Costello-Witten cohomological Hall algebras is traced in Chapter~\ref{ch:cha-yangian}.

\begin{proposition}[$E_2$ enhancement via Drinfeld center]
\label{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
If $A$ is an $E_1$-chiral algebra with monoidal representation category $\Rep^{E_1}(A)$, the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ is a braided monoidal category equipped with an equivalence
\[
 \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))
\]
where $\mathrm{Drin}(A)$ is the $E_2$-chiral algebra obtained by the categorified averaging map $E_1\text{-Cat} \to E_2\text{-Cat}$.
\end{proposition}

This proposition provides the pathway $\Phi_{E_1} \to \Phi_{E_2}$: one computes the $E_1$ chiral algebra first and then applies the Drinfeld center at the categorical level. At $d=2$ the construction produces the braided vertex tensor category of the BKM superalgebra; at $d=3$ the construction is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:e1-sector-d3}.

\begin{remark}[Convolution $L_\infty$-algebra on the $E_1$ side]
\label{rem:convolution-linfty-e1}
For an $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ and a coalgebra $C$, the convolution $L_\infty$-algebra $\hom_\alpha(C, A)$ of Volume~I is the natural habitat of Maurer-Cartan elements controlling twisted morphisms. On the $E_1$ side, $\hom_\alpha$ is a strict dg Lie algebra when the ordering of $C$ is preserved (the strict model $\Conv_{\mathrm{str}}$), and a genuine $L_\infty$-algebra when higher brackets are included ($\Conv_\infty$). The MC moduli of the two models coincide, but the full $L_\infty$-structure is required for homotopy transfer, formality statements, and gauge equivalence. For Vol~III purposes, the key point is that the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ lands in the strict model $\Conv_{\mathrm{str}}$ because the cyclic $A_\infty$ structure on $A_\cC$ is strict at degree two; higher-degree corrections require the $L_\infty$ refinement.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with Beilinson-Drinfeld chiral algebras]
\label{rem:comparison-bd}
Beilinson and Drinfeld~\cite{BeilinsonDrinfeld} developed chiral algebras as a symmetric factorization formalism on the Ran space. Their chiral algebras lie on the $E_\infty$ side of the locality hierarchy, even when they carry OPE poles. The $E_1$-chiral algebras of this chapter are a strict refinement: they remember collision order. The averaging map $B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^{\Sigma}$ forgets that extra data and returns to the Beilinson-Drinfeld world. Vol~III's geometric output is ordered; its modular characteristic is symmetric.
\end{remark}

\noindent\emph{What is lost at each level.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $E_1 \to E_2$: the labeled ordering is replaced by a braid-group
 action; half-braiding data (the choice of over- versus
 under-crossing) is forgotten.
 \item $E_2 \to E_\infty$: the braiding $\sigma$ becomes symmetric; the
 $R$-matrix collapses to the scalar $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
 Every non-abelian quantum group datum is lost.
 \item $E_1 \to E_\infty$ (direct, via $\mathrm{av}$): the full
 $R$-matrix, the Hopf structure, and the Drinfeld coproduct all
 vanish. What survives is the modular characteristic
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the shadow tower.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\emph{Why Vol~III works at $E_1$.}
The entire quantum group programme---Yangians ($Y(\fg)$), quantum
toroidal algebras ($U_{q,t}(\ddot{\fgl}_1)$), Kazhdan-Lusztig
categories ($\Rep_q(\fg)$ at roots of unity)---lives natively at $E_1$.
The passage to $E_2$ is via the Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}); the passage to
$E_\infty$ forgets the quantum group entirely and returns to the
classical vertex-algebraic world of Beilinson-Drinfeld.
Li's vertex bialgebra~\cite{Li} is the $E_\infty$ shadow of the
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra: it retains the vertex-algebraic OPE structure
but symmetrizes the coproduct, losing the $R$-matrix that distinguishes
$U_q(\fg)$ from $U(\fg)$. The chiral quantum group is the $E_1$
substance; the vertex bialgebra is the $E_\infty$ silhouette.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Lossiness of the $E_1 \to E_\infty$ forgetful functor on bialgebras]
\label{prop:forgetful-e1-einfty-bialgebra}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, R(z))$ be an $E_1$-chiral bialgebra with
$R$-matrix $R(z)$ and Drinfeld coproduct $\Delta_z$. The forgetful
functor
\[
 \mathrm{Forget}_{E_1 \to E_\infty} \colon
 E_1\text{-}\mathrm{ChirBialg} \longrightarrow
 E_\infty\text{-}\mathrm{VtxBialg}
\]
sends $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, R(z))$ to the Li vertex bialgebra
$(A, \mu, \mathrm{av}(\Delta_z), 1)$ with \emph{trivial} braiding. In
particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item The image has symmetric coproduct:
 $\mathrm{av}(\Delta_z) = \tau \circ \mathrm{av}(\Delta_z)$.
 \item The $R$-matrix reduces to the scalar
 $\mathrm{av}(R(z))|_{z=0} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \mathrm{id}$.
 \item The braiding is \emph{not} recoverable from the
 $E_\infty$ image. It is recovered precisely by the Drinfeld center:
 $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\mathrm{Drin}(A))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Claim~(a): the averaging map $\mathrm{av} \colon B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to
B^\Sigma$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}) is
$S_n$-coinvariant projection, so the image is $S_n$-invariant by
construction; in particular the coproduct is symmetric.
Claim~(b): the degree-two component of $\mathrm{av}$ sends
$r(z) = R_{12}(z)$ to its $S_2$-coinvariant, which is the scalar
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (loc.\ cit.).
Claim~(c): the fiber of $\mathrm{av}$ in degree two is
$\ker(\mathrm{av}|_2) = \{f(z) : f(z) + f(-z) = 0\}$, the space of
antisymmetric collision data. The $R$-matrix lives in that kernel
(it satisfies $R_{12}(z) = -R_{21}(-z)$ by the Yang-Baxter skew
symmetry). Hence $R(z)$ is annihilated and cannot be recovered from
the $E_\infty$ image. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}, the Drinfeld center
reconstructs the braiding from the monoidal structure of
$\Rep^{E_1}(A)$; this is the unique functorial recovery.
\end{proof}

\section{Connection to Volume~II}
\label{sec:vol2-connection}

Volume~II resolves the ordered side of the programme. Its seven parts develop the Swiss-cheese operad, the ordered bar complex, the classical $r(z)$-matrix at level $k$, the seven faces of $r(z)$, and the derived center of an $E_1$-chiral algebra. Vol~III uses that machinery through the CY interface fixed here.

\begin{proposition}[Vol~III composes with Vol~II]
\label{prop:vol3-composes-vol2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ of \ref{part:cy-categories} factors as $\Phi = \Phi^{\mathrm{Vol\ II}}_{E_1} \circ \Phi^{\mathrm{Vol\ III}}_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, where $\Phi^{\mathrm{Vol\ III}}_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ takes a CY$_d$ category to its cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra and $\Phi^{\mathrm{Vol\ II}}_{E_1}$ takes a cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra to an $E_1$-chiral algebra via the Swiss-cheese promotion.
\end{proposition}

The detailed operadic content of $\Phi^{\mathrm{Vol\ II}}_{E_1}$ involves the three coalgebra structures, the difference between coshuffle and deconcatenation, the promotion from one-colour to two-colour, the mixed-sector dimension formula, the curved factor of two at positive genus, the averaging map lossiness, the bound on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\ast(\Vir_c)$, and the distinction between generating depth and algebraic depth.

\begin{remark}[Why the $E_1$ layer cannot be skipped]
\label{rem:why-e1-layer}
The CY geometric data can be packaged directly into a braided factorization algebra (the $E_2$ form) via Kontsevich-Soibelman COHA technology. The temptation is to skip the $E_1$ intermediate and go from $\cC$ straight to the braided output. Three obstructions block that route. First, the convolution $L_\infty$-algebra $\hom_\alpha(C,A)$ of Volume~II fails to be a bifunctor in both slots simultaneously (RNW19); MC3 must be proved one slot at a time, and the slot that admits a proof is the $E_1$ slot. Second, the $r$-matrix $r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$ cannot be extracted without seeing the ordered collisions, since the braiding of the $E_2$ picture only remembers $r(z)$ up to $S_2$-coinvariance. Third, the CoHA itself is an $E_1$-associative multiplication; writing it as an $E_2$ product forgets the preferred direction on short exact sequences that is central to wall-crossing. These three obstructions are the same obstruction seen from three angles: the ordered bar is the only model that remembers the direction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[lambda-bracket convention in Vol~III]
\label{rem:lambda-bracket-vol3}
Vol~III writes classical shadow operations in lambda-bracket notation with divided powers: $\{T_\lambda T\} = (c/12)\,\lambda^3$. The divided-power prefactor $1/3! = 1/6$ absorbs the OPE mode coefficient into the lambda-bracket rewrite: starting from the OPE mode $T_{(3)}T$ and dividing by $3!$ yields the stated $c/12$ at order $\lambda^3$. Every formula imported from Vol~I (which uses OPE mode notation) must be converted before appearing in Vol~III. The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ is agnostic to the choice of convention, but its computed values of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ are convention-dependent at the level of integral prefactors, and a Vol~I formula transplanted without conversion will produce a wrong $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ by exactly a factor of $6$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three-volume thesis]
\label{rem:three-volume-thesis}
The three volumes are three faces of a single $E_1$-$E_1$ operadic Koszul duality. Volume~I is the symmetric modular face: it develops $B^{\Sigma}$, the five theorems A-D+H, and the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ in the uniform-weight setting. Volume~II is the $E_1$ open-colour face: it develops $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$, the Swiss-cheese operad, the $r(z)$-matrix with its seven faces, and the three-dimensional holomorphic-topological bridge to quantum gravity. Volume~III is the CY-geometric face: it develops the functor $\Phi$ that produces the input algebra from a Calabi-Yau category, identifies $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ within the kappa-spectrum, and traces the quantum group back to its geometric origin in BPS state counts.
\end{remark}

\section{Operadic dictionary for the CY reader}
\label{sec:operadic-dictionary}

The operadic vocabulary of Volume~II can be compressed into the following dictionary, which the CY reader can keep on a single page.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$E_n$}: little $n$-disks operad. $E_1$ is the operad of intervals on the line; $E_2$ is the operad of disks in the plane; $E_\infty$ is the colimit. $E_1$ algebras are ordered and associative. $E_2$ algebras are partially commutative and braided. $E_\infty$ algebras are symmetric up to all higher homotopies; ordinary chiral algebras live here and still admit OPE poles. Dunn additivity: $E_m \otimes E_n \simeq E_{m+n}$.
\item \textbf{Ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$}: the cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra $T^c(s^{-1}\bar A)$ with deconcatenation coproduct. Retains degree ordering. Natural $E_1$-object. Source of Yangians and quantum groups.
\item \textbf{Symmetric bar $B^{\Sigma}$}: the $S_n$-coinvariant quotient of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ with the coshuffle coproduct descended from the shuffle product on $T^c$. Natural $E_\infty$-object. Source of the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
\item \textbf{Harrison bar $B^{\mathrm{Lie}}$}: the Harrison complex; Lie-coalgebra structure via the iterated commutator. Dual to the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the associated Lie algebra. Smallest of the three natural bar complexes on any commutative input.
\item \textbf{Deconcatenation}: coproduct on $T^c$ that splits a word into a prefix and a suffix. Produces $n+1$ terms in degree $n$. Respects ordering. The $E_1$ coproduct.
\item \textbf{Coshuffle}: coproduct on $\mathrm{Sym}^c$ dual to the shuffle product. Produces up to $2^n$ terms. Destroys ordering. The $E_\infty$ coproduct.
\item \textbf{Swiss-cheese $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$}: two-coloured operad combining a chiral (holomorphic, closed) sector with an ordered (topological, open) sector. Product decomposition $E_1(C) \times E_2(\mathrm{top})$ isolates the $E_1$ chiral factor.
\item \textbf{Drinfeld center $\cZ$}: endofunctor on monoidal categories that sends a monoidal category $\cM$ to the category of objects equipped with a half-braiding against every object of $\cM$. The result is braided monoidal. Categorified averaging: $\cZ$ takes $E_1$-categories to $E_2$-categories.
\item \textbf{$r$-matrix}: the degree-two generator of the $E_1$ Koszul duality, carrying a pole along the diagonal. At level $k$, the classical Kac-Moody $r$-matrix is $r(z) = \frac{k\,\Omega}{z}$ and vanishes at $k=0$.
\end{itemize}

The dictionary is not symmetric in $E_1$ and $E_\infty$: the ordered bar is the primitive, and everything on the $E_\infty$ side is a quotient. The CY-geometric functor $\Phi$ lands primitively in the $E_1$ layer; the $E_\infty$ and $E_2$ images are obtained by composing with averaging and Drinfeld center respectively.

\begin{remark}[Notation consistency across the three volumes]
\label{rem:notation-consistency}
The three volumes use different coalgebra conventions in their displayed formulas. Vol~I displays formulas in $B^{\Sigma}$ with the symmetric coproduct; Vol~II displays formulas in $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ with deconcatenation; Vol~III displays formulas in whichever form the CY functor produces, which is always $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$ at the source but frequently $B^{\Sigma}$ after averaging to extract $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The reader who cross-references a formula between volumes must convert between the three coalgebra structures: $B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^{\Sigma}$ by averaging (dividing by $n!$ and symmetrizing), and $B^{\Sigma} \to B^{\mathrm{Lie}}$ by taking the iterated commutator of the cofree tensor coalgebra. The three bars are NOT isomorphic even as complexes; they differ by $S_n$-coinvariant quotients, and the Euler characters diverge accordingly. See Vol~II for the three-bar sequence.
\end{remark}

\section{$E_1$-chiral Koszul duality: three standard families}
\label{sec:e1-koszul-three-families}

Volume~II proves $E_1$-chiral Koszul duality: the bar-cobar adjunction
$B^{\mathrm{ord}} \dashv \Omega^{\mathrm{ord}}$ is a Quillen equivalence
on the Koszul locus, and the Koszul dual algebra $A^!$ satisfies
$\Rep^{E_1}(A) \simeq \Rep^{E_1}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ as monoidal
categories. The Koszul conductor $\rho_K(A)$ is the real number such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koszul-conductor}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K(A).
\end{equation}
The conductor $\rho_K$ is a family invariant: it depends on the
isomorphism class of the shadow tower, not on the particular algebra
within the family. This section computes $A^!$, $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$, and
$\rho_K$ explicitly for the three standard families that span the G/L/M
classification: Heisenberg (class~G, $\rho_K = 0$), affine Kac--Moody
$V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (class~L, $\rho_K = 0$), and Virasoro $\Vir_c$
(class~M, $\rho_K = 13$).

\subsection{Family I: Heisenberg $H_k$ (class~G)}
\label{subsec:koszul-heisenberg}

The rank-one Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_k$ at level $k \neq 0$ has a
single strong generator $J$ of conformal weight~$1$, with OPE
$J(z)J(w) \sim k/(z-w)^2$ and no nonlinear terms. The augmentation ideal
$\bar{H}_k = \langle J, \partial J, \partial^2 J, \ldots \rangle$ is
spanned by all derivatives, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) = k$.

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of the Heisenberg]
\label{prop:koszul-heisenberg}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $H_k$ is $H_k^! = H_{-k}$, the
Heisenberg at level $-k$. The ordered bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)$ has trivial differential
at all arities, and the Koszul conductor is $\rho_K = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bar differential $d_{\mathrm{bar}}$ on
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n(H_k) = (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes n}$ acts by
summing adjacent OPE multiplications:
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[a_1|\cdots|a_n] = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \pm
 [a_1|\cdots|\mu(a_i, a_{i+1})|\cdots|a_n].
\]
For the Heisenberg, $\mu(J, J) = 0$ (the singular OPE $J(z)J(w) \sim
k/(z-w)^2$ contributes to the metric/pairing, not to the multiplication
$\mu$: the OPE has no first-order pole, so the normally-ordered product
$:JJ:$ has no correction from the singular part). Consequently
$d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0$ at all arities. The bar complex is exact in the
trivial sense: $H^*(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k)) = B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k)$ as
a graded vector space.

The Koszul dual is then read from the linear dual of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$:
the Verdier dual $D_{\mathrm{Ran}}(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(H_k))$ is a tensor
coalgebra on $(s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^*$ with inverted level. Explicitly, the
dual generator $J^*$ satisfies $J^*(z)J^*(w) \sim (-k)/(z-w)^2$, so
$H_k^! \cong H_{-k}$.

The conductor computation:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{-k}) = k + (-k) = 0
 = \rho_K.
\]
\end{proof}

The explicit bar complex at low arities is:
\begin{align}
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1(H_k) &= s^{-1}\bar{H}_k =
 \langle [J], [\partial J], [\partial^2 J], \ldots \rangle,
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-1} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2(H_k) &= (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes 2} =
 \langle [J|J], [J|\partial J], [\partial J|J], \ldots \rangle,
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0,
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-2} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_3(H_k) &= (s^{-1}\bar{H}_k)^{\otimes 3} =
 \langle [J|J|J], [J|J|\partial J], \ldots \rangle,
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}} = 0.
 \label{eq:heisenberg-bar-3}
\end{align}
The dimension at arity $n$ (truncated to conformal weight $\leq N$)
is $\dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n = p(N)^n$ where $p(N)$ is the partition
function (one basis vector per derivative $\partial^m J$, $m \geq 0$,
organized by conformal weight $m+1$). The generating function
$\sum_n \dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n \cdot q^n$ at fixed weight truncation
is a geometric series in $p(N)$, reflecting the triviality of the bar
differential (class~G: the shadow tower terminates at depth~$2$).

\begin{remark}[Self-duality at $k=1$]
\label{rem:heisenberg-self-dual}
At $k = 1$ the Koszul dual is $H_{-1}$, which is isomorphic to $H_1$
after reversing the ordering of the bar (the level sign is absorbed by
the $\mathrm{rev}$ functor on $\Rep^{E_1}$). At general $k$, the duality
$H_k \leftrightarrow H_{-k}$ is the chiral reflection of the parameter
inversion $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \to (-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$ of the
$\Omega$-background.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Family II: Kac--Moody $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ (class~L)}
\label{subsec:koszul-kac-moody}

The affine Kac--Moody vertex algebra $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at
non-critical level $k \neq -2$ has three strong generators
$\{e, f, h\}$ of conformal weight~$1$ with OPE:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sl2-ope}
 h(z)h(w) \sim \frac{2k}{(z-w)^2}, \quad
 h(z)e(w) \sim \frac{2e(w)}{z-w}, \quad
 e(z)f(w) \sim \frac{k}{(z-w)^2} + \frac{h(w)}{z-w}.
\end{equation}
The critical fact is the \emph{first-order pole} in $e(z)f(w)$: the
OPE multiplication $\mu(e, f) = h$ is nontrivial, so the bar
differential is nonzero. The modular characteristic is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) = 3(k+2)/4$ (from the
general formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\mathfrak{g})) =
\dim(\mathfrak{g})(k + h^\vee)/(2h^\vee)$ with $\dim(\mathfrak{sl}_2) = 3$,
$h^\vee = 2$).

\begin{proposition}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$]
\label{prop:koszul-kac-moody}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at non-critical
level $k \neq -2$ is $V_{k'}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ at the Feigin--Frenkel
dual level $k' = -k - 2h^\vee = -k - 4$. The Koszul conductor is
$\rho_K = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The argument has three steps.

\emph{Step 1: Bar differential.}
The ordered bar complex has $B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) =
(s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes n}$, where $\bar{V}_k$ is spanned by
$\{e, f, h, \partial e, \partial f, \partial h, \ldots\}$. The bar
differential is
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[a_1|\cdots|a_n] = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} \pm
 [a_1|\cdots|\mu(a_i, a_{i+1})|\cdots|a_n]
\]
where $\mu$ extracts the first-order pole of the OPE. At arity~$2$:
\begin{align*}
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|f] &= +[h], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|e] = -[h], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|e] &= +[2e], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|h] = -[2e], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|f] &= -[2f], \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|h] = +[2f], \\
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[e|e] &= 0, \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[f|f] = 0, \quad
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[h|h] = 0.
\end{align*}
Generators $[e|e]$, $[f|f]$, $[h|h]$ are bar cycles (diagonal terms have
no first-order pole), while off-diagonal terms produce nontrivial
boundaries. The bar cohomology
$H^1(B^{\mathrm{ord}}(V_k)) = \bar{V}_k$ (the generators) and the image
of $d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1$
is $3$-dimensional (spanned by $h$, $2e$, $2f$).

\emph{Step 2: Koszul dual OPE.}
The Verdier dual of $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2))$ produces
dual generators $\{e^*, f^*, h^*\}$ with the \emph{same}
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ structure but inverted level. The key identity is the
Feigin--Frenkel duality: the functor
$V_k(\mathfrak{g}) \mapsto V_{-k-2h^\vee}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the chiral
level reflection, and its operadic implementation is exactly $E_1$-Koszul
duality applied to the bar complex. For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$:
\[
 k' = -k - 2h^\vee = -k - 4.
\]
The dual generators satisfy $e^*(z)f^*(w) \sim k'/(z-w)^2 + h^*(w)/(z-w)$
with $k' = -k-4$, reproducing the $V_{k'}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ OPE.

\emph{Step 3: Conductor.}
\begin{align*}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'})
 &= \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(k'+2)}{4}
 = \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(-k-4+2)}{4} \\
 &= \frac{3(k+2)}{4} + \frac{3(-k-2)}{4}
 = \frac{3(k+2) - 3(k+2)}{4} = 0 = \rho_K.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

The low-arity bar complex is:
\begin{align}
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1 &= s^{-1}\bar{V}_k =
 \langle [e], [f], [h], [\partial e], [\partial f], [\partial h],
 \ldots \rangle, \notag \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 &= (s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes 2},
 \quad \dim = 9 \text{ at weight $1$},
 \label{eq:sl2-bar-2} \\
 & \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}}([e|f]) = [h], \;
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}([h|e]) = [2e], \;
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}([h|f]) = [-2f], \notag \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_3 &= (s^{-1}\bar{V}_k)^{\otimes 3},
 \quad \dim = 27 \text{ at weight $1$}.
 \label{eq:sl2-bar-3}
\end{align}
At arity~$3$ the bar differential has two summands (positions $i=1,2$),
and the bar cohomology carries the first nontrivial $A_\infty$ operation
$m_3$ of $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. The nonvanishing of $m_3$ is equivalent
to the non-formality of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ as a dg Lie algebra
and places $V_k(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ in shadow class~L (depth~$2$ tower
with nontrivial $m_3$).

\begin{remark}[Critical level $k = -2$]
\label{rem:critical-level}
At $k = -2$ the modular characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{-2}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)) = 0$ and the Koszul dual
level is $k' = -(-2) - 4 = -2$: the critical level is a fixed point of
the Feigin--Frenkel involution. The critical-level vertex algebra
$V_{-2}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ is the home of geometric Langlands (see
Chapter~\ref{ch:geometric-langlands}), and the self-duality under $E_1$
Koszul reflects the Langlands self-duality of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[General $\mathfrak{g}$]
\label{rem:general-g-koszul}
For a simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ with dual Coxeter number
$h^\vee$, the same argument gives $V_k(\mathfrak{g})^! =
V_{-k-2h^\vee}(\mathfrak{g})$ with conductor
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{k'}) =
 \frac{\dim\mathfrak{g}}{2h^\vee}\bigl[(k + h^\vee) +
 (-k - 2h^\vee + h^\vee)\bigr] = 0.
\]
The vanishing $\rho_K = 0$ is universal for the Kac--Moody family.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Family III: Virasoro $\Vir_c$ (class~M)}
\label{subsec:koszul-virasoro}

The Virasoro vertex algebra $\Vir_c$ at central charge $c$ has a single
strong generator $T$ (the stress-energy tensor) of conformal weight~$2$
with OPE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virasoro-ope}
 T(z)T(w) \sim \frac{c/2}{(z-w)^4} + \frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}
 + \frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}.
\end{equation}
The first-order pole gives $\mu(T, T) = \partial T$ (a nontrivial
multiplication), the second-order pole gives the conformal weight
assignment (Sugawara), and the fourth-order pole gives the central
extension. The modular characteristic is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) = c/2$
(from $\dim B^{\mathrm{ord}}_n \sim p(n)$ and the Volume~I shadow
formula).

The Koszul duality of the Virasoro differs from the Heisenberg and
Kac--Moody families in a fundamental way: the conductor is
\emph{nonzero}.

\begin{conjecture}[$E_1$-Koszul dual of the Virasoro]
\label{conj:koszul-virasoro}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
The $E_1$-chiral Koszul dual of $\Vir_c$ is $\Vir_{c'}$ at central charge
$c' = 26 - c$. The Koszul conductor is $\rho_K = 13$:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{c'})
 = \frac{c}{2} + \frac{26 - c}{2} = 13 = \rho_K.
\]
At the self-dual point $c = 13$, $\Vir_{13}$ is Koszul
self-dual. At $c = 26$ the dual is $\Vir_0$, and at $c = 0$ the dual is
$\Vir_{26}$ (the bosonic string).
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture status is necessary because the full $E_1$-chiral
Koszul duality for the Virasoro requires controlling the bar differential
to all arities, and the infinite shadow depth (class~M) means the tower
never terminates. The evidence is:

\begin{proof}[Evidence]
\emph{(i) Bar differential at arity~$2$.}
Using the first-order pole $\mu(T, T) = \partial T$:
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T] = [\partial T].
\]
The image $\mathrm{im}(d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to
B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1)$ is $1$-dimensional (spanned by $[\partial T]$).
The bar cycle $[T|T] - [T|T]$ is trivial, so
$\ker(d_{\mathrm{bar}}|_{B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1}) =
\langle [T|T] + [T|T] \rangle_{/\mathrm{im}}$ is
computed by the commutator $[T|T] - [T|T] = 0$ (the ordered bar is
noncommutative; only the symmetric $[T|T] + [T|T]$ survives modulo $d$).

\emph{(ii) Bar differential at arity~$3$.}
$d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T|T] = [\partial T|T] \pm [T|\partial T]$
(two summands from positions $i = 1, 2$). The sign is determined by the
Koszul convention (desuspension shifts degree by~$-1$):
\[
 d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T|T] = [\partial T|T] - [T|\partial T].
\]
This is a nonzero cycle in $B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2$ (its image under
$d_{\mathrm{bar}}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2 \to B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1$ is
$[\partial^2 T] - [\partial^2 T] = 0$, so it is indeed a cycle), and it
represents the $A_\infty$ operation $m_3(T, T, T)$. The nonvanishing of
this class at all orders is the hallmark of class~M (infinite shadow
depth).

\emph{(iii) Conductor from the Sugawara construction.}
The Virasoro at central charge $c$ embeds into the Heisenberg via
Sugawara: $T = :JJ:/(2k)$ at $c = 1$. Under Koszul duality, the level
inverts ($k \to -k$), so $c \to c'$ with $c + c' = 26$ determined by the
ghost central charge of the bosonic string ($bc$-ghost system contributes
$c = -26$, so the BRST-balanced point is $c + c_{\mathrm{ghost}} = 26$;
equivalently, $c' = 26 - c$ is the unique value such that
$\Vir_c \otimes \Vir_{c'}$ has total $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 13$, the value
at which the Virasoro shadow tower of Vol~I attains
its self-dual fixed point).

\emph{(iv) Kappa verification.}
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) = c/2$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{26-c}) = (26-c)/2$, so
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_c) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Vir_{26-c})
 = \frac{c}{2} + \frac{26-c}{2} = 13 = \rho_K.
\]
The conductor $\rho_K = 13$ is nonzero, reflecting the nontrivial
conformal anomaly of the Virasoro. Compare with the Heisenberg
($\rho_K = 0$) and Kac--Moody ($\rho_K = 0$) families.
\end{proof}

The low-arity bar complex:
\begin{align}
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_1(\Vir_c) &= s^{-1}\overline{\Vir}_c =
 \langle [T], [\partial T], [\partial^2 T], \ldots \rangle,
 \label{eq:vir-bar-1} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_2(\Vir_c) &= (s^{-1}\overline{\Vir}_c)^{\otimes 2},
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T] = [\partial T],
 \label{eq:vir-bar-2} \\
 B^{\mathrm{ord}}_3(\Vir_c) &= (s^{-1}\overline{\Vir}_c)^{\otimes 3},
 \quad d_{\mathrm{bar}}[T|T|T] = [\partial T|T] - [T|\partial T].
 \label{eq:vir-bar-3}
\end{align}

\section{$E_1$-chiral bialgebras}
\label{sec:e1-chiral-bialgebras}

The previous sections established the $E_1$-chiral algebra (product),
the ordered bar coalgebra (coproduct), and the averaging map
(symmetrization). This section assembles them into a single object: an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra. The definition does not exist in the literature.
Classical Hopf algebras live in symmetric monoidal categories;
Drinfeld's quasi-Hopf algebras relax the coassociativity but remain
in the same ambient category; Li's vertex bialgebras~\cite{Li} use
the $E_\infty$-chiral tensor product and the coshuffle coproduct, which
destroys the quantum group $R$-matrix (Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}).
The correct categorical home for the chiral quantum group is an
$E_1$-chiral bialgebra: a bialgebra object in the $E_1$~sector of the
Swiss-cheese operad $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$.

\subsection{The $E_1$-chiral monoidal category}
\label{subsec:e1-chiral-monoidal}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral monoidal category]
\label{def:e1-chiral-monoidal}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Fix a smooth complex curve $C$. The \emph{$E_1$-chiral monoidal category}
$(\cM^{E_1}_C, \otimes_{E_1}, \mathbf{1})$ has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Objects}: $E_1$-chiral algebras on $C$ in the sense of the
 open colour of $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch},\mathrm{top}}$
 (Definition~\ref{def:swiss-cheese-split}). These are factorization
 algebras on the configuration space $\Conf_n(\mathbb{R}) \subset \Conf_n(C)$
 of ordered real points along a ray in $C$.
 \item \emph{Tensor product}: for $E_1$-chiral algebras $A$ and $B$,
 the \emph{ordered fusion product}
 \[
  A \otimes_{E_1} B := \mathrm{colim}_{z_1 < z_2} A(z_1) \otimes B(z_2),
 \]
 taken over the configuration of two ordered real points in $C$. This is
 NOT symmetric: $A \otimes_{E_1} B \not\simeq B \otimes_{E_1} A$ in
 general. The ordering is fixed by the holomorphic argument
 (Proposition~\ref{prop:holomorphic-e1}).
 \item \emph{Unit}: $\mathbf{1} = \Omega_C$, the augmentation target.
 The augmentation $\varepsilon \colon A \to \Omega_C$ is the counit of
 the $E_1$-chiral monoidal structure.
 \item \emph{Associator}: the ordered fusion is strictly associative:
 $(A \otimes_{E_1} B) \otimes_{E_1} C \simeq A \otimes_{E_1} (B \otimes_{E_1} C)$
 via the identification $\Conf_3^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathbb{R}) \simeq \{z_1 < z_2 < z_3\}$,
 which is contractible. This is the $E_1$ associahedron collapsing to
 the linear order.
\end{enumerate}
The category $\cM^{E_1}_C$ is monoidal but NOT braided: the $E_1$-operad
has no braiding operations. This is the essential distinction from both
the Beilinson--Drinfeld symmetric ($E_\infty$) setting and the $E_2$-braided
setting.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Algebra and coalgebra objects}
\label{subsec:e1-alg-coalg}

An $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$ is an algebra object in $\cM^{E_1}_C$:
a product $\mu \colon A \otimes_{E_1} A \to A$
(the OPE with ordered collision: the field at $z_1 < z_2$ collides
onto the field at $z_2$ from the left, producing residues that differ
from the $z_2 < z_1$ collision by the $R$-matrix). The coalgebra side
is the ordered bar complex.

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral coalgebra]
\label{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
An \emph{$E_1$-chiral coalgebra} in $\cM^{E_1}_C$ is a coaugmented
coassociative coalgebra $(B, \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}, \eta)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $B = T^c(V)$ is a cofree conilpotent tensor coalgebra on a graded
 vector space $V$;
 \item The coproduct is the \emph{deconcatenation} coproduct
 (Definition~\ref{def:ordered-bar}):
 \[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}(v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n)
  = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_k)
  \otimes (v_{k+1} \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n);
 \]
 \item The coaugmentation $\eta \colon k \to B$ includes the
 degree-zero summand.
\end{enumerate}
The canonical example is $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$
for an augmented $E_1$-chiral algebra $A$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra axiom}
\label{subsec:e1-bialgebra-axiom}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral bialgebra]
\label{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
An \emph{$E_1$-chiral bialgebra} on a smooth curve $C$ is
a tuple $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $(A, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an augmented $E_1$-chiral algebra in
 $\cM^{E_1}_C$ (the product is the ordered OPE);
 \item $(A, \Delta_z, \eta)$ is an $E_1$-chiral coalgebra, where
 \[
  \Delta_z \colon A \longrightarrow A \otimes_{E_1, z} A
 \]
 is a family of coproducts parametrized by $z \in \Ran(C)$, with
 $\otimes_{E_1, z}$ denoting the ordered tensor product at collision
 point~$z$;
 \item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility}: $\Delta_z$ is a morphism of
 $E_1$-chiral algebras, i.e.\ the diagram
 \[
 \begin{tikzcd}
  A \otimes_{E_1} A \ar[r, "\mu"] \ar[d, "\Delta_z \otimes \Delta_z"']
  & A \ar[d, "\Delta_z"] \\
  (A \otimes_{E_1,z} A) \otimes_{E_1} (A \otimes_{E_1,z} A)
  \ar[r, "\mu \otimes \mu"']
  & A \otimes_{E_1,z} A
 \end{tikzcd}
 \]
 commutes, where the bottom-left corner uses the interchange law
 for the $E_1$-monoidal structure;
 \item \emph{Spectral coassociativity}: for $z, w \in \Ran(C)$ in
 general position, the coproduct satisfies
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-coassociativity}
  (\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
 \end{equation}
 At $z = w = 0$ this reduces to ordinary coassociativity
 $(\Delta_0 \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_0) \circ \Delta_0$.
 For generic $z, w$ the identity encodes the Yang--Baxter
 equation: the $R$-matrix extracted from $\Delta_z$ satisfies
 YBE if and only if spectral coassociativity holds
 (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:r-matrix-from-drinfeld}).
\end{enumerate}
The compatibility axiom (iii) is the $E_1$-chiral analogue of the
classical bialgebra axiom ``$\Delta$ is an algebra map.'' The spectral
coassociativity axiom~(iv) is the chiral analogue of the identity
$(\Delta \otimes 1) \circ \Delta = (1 \otimes \Delta) \circ \Delta$,
promoted to a family over the spectral parameter. It replaces
Drinfeld's quasi-coassociativity (which uses an associator $\Phi$ to
correct the failure of coassociativity in the symmetric monoidal setting):
in the $E_1$-chiral setting, coassociativity holds on the nose, but
with shifted spectral parameters. The parametrization
by $z \in \Ran(C)$ is essential: the coproduct depends on the collision
point, and the $z$-dependence carries the $R$-matrix data.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra (antipode)]
\label{def:e1-chiral-antipode}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
An $E_1$-chiral bialgebra $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta)$ is an
\emph{$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra} if there exists a morphism of
$E_1$-chiral modules $S \colon A \to A$ (the \emph{antipode})
satisfying the \emph{Hopf axiom at $z = 0$}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-axiom-z0}
 \mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
 = \eta \circ \varepsilon
 = \mu_{E_1} \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{E_1}$ is the ordered product and $\Delta_0 = \Delta_z|_{z=0}$
is the coproduct at the basepoint. The two equalities are
independent conditions because $\mu_{E_1}$ is not commutative.
For $z \neq 0$, the antipode axiom generalizes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hopf-axiom-spectral}
 \mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_z
 = \eta \circ \varepsilon \circ \pi_z,
\end{equation}
where $\pi_z \colon A \to A$ is the spectral projection that extracts
the $z$-independent component. When $A$ is connected
($\varepsilon \circ \eta = \mathrm{id}_k$), the basepoint
axiom~\eqref{eq:hopf-axiom-z0} determines $S$ uniquely by
induction on the coalgebra filtration, exactly as in the classical
Hopf algebra setting.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Summary of the $E_1$-chiral Hopf axiom system]
\label{rem:e1-hopf-axiom-summary}
Definitions~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}
and~\ref{def:e1-chiral-antipode} assemble into a five-part axiom
system for an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
$(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ on a curve $C$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(H\arabic*)}]
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral algebra}: $(A, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an
 augmented algebra in $\cM^{E_1}_C$; the product $\mu$ is the
 labeled-ordered OPE (Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-monoidal}).
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral coalgebra}: $\Delta_z \colon A \to
 A \otimes_{E_1,z} A$ is a $z$-parametrized coproduct with counit
 $\varepsilon$ and coaugmentation $\eta$
 (Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-coalgebra}).
 \item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility}: $\Delta_z$ is a morphism
 of $E_1$-chiral algebras---the diagram of
 Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}(iii) commutes.
 \item \emph{Spectral coassociativity}:
 $(\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w$
 (equation~\eqref{eq:spectral-coassociativity}).
 \item \emph{Hopf axiom}:
 $\mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
  = \eta \circ \varepsilon
  = \mu_{E_1} \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0$
 (equation~\eqref{eq:hopf-axiom-z0}).
\end{enumerate}
The axioms are strictly stronger than Li's vertex
bialgebra~\cite{Li} ($E_\infty$-chiral, no spectral parameter, no
antipode) and strictly weaker than Drinfeld's quasi-Hopf formulation
(which works in $({\mathrm{Vect}}, \otimes_k)$ and requires an
associator $\Phi$). The $E_1$-chiral setting eliminates the
associator: axiom~\textbf{(H4)} holds on the nose because
the $E_1$-monoidal category is strictly associative
(the associahedron is contractible for linear orders).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why $E_1$ and not $E_\infty$}
\label{subsec:why-e1-not-einfty}

\begin{proposition}[Averaging forgetful theorem]
\label{prop:averaging-forgets-hopf}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
The averaging map of
Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty} defines a functor
\[
 \mathrm{av} \colon
 E_1\text{-}\mathrm{ChirHopf}(C) \longrightarrow
 E_\infty\text{-}\mathrm{ChirCoalg}(C)
\]
that sends an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
$(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ satisfying
axioms~\textup{\textbf{(H1)--(H5)}} of
Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:e1-hopf-axiom-summary}} to the
$E_\infty$-chiral coalgebra
$(B^{\Sigma}(A), \Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}})$ with the coshuffle coproduct
$\Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}}$. The functor forgets all Hopf data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The $R$-matrix: on degree-two elements,
 $\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (a scalar), so the full
 $z$-dependent $R$-matrix $r(z) = k\,\Omega/z$ is replaced by its
 $S_2$-coinvariant, which is the collision residue $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
 \item The antipode: $S$ acts on ordered tensor factors; after
 symmetrization, $S$ becomes the identity (on $S_n$-invariants,
 the reversal acts trivially).
 \item The coproduct $z$-dependence: the coshuffle coproduct is
 independent of the collision point $z$, while the deconcatenation
 coproduct $\Delta_z$ depends on $z$ through the ordered OPE residues.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the $E_\infty$-chiral coalgebra $B^{\Sigma}(A)$ is NOT
a bialgebra: it retains no Hopf structure. The coshuffle coproduct on
$B^{\Sigma}$ is compatible only with the commutative product, which
$A$ does not possess.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Statement~(i) is Proposition~\ref{prop:averaging-e1-einfty}.
Statement~(ii): the antipode $S$ reverses the ordered product, so
$\mathrm{av} \circ S = \mathrm{av}$ (symmetrization absorbs the
reversal). Statement~(iii): the coshuffle coproduct is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}} = \mathrm{av}^{\otimes 2} \circ \Delta_{\mathrm{dec}}$,
and $\mathrm{av}^{\otimes 2}$ kills the $z$-dependence because
$\sum_{\sigma} r(\sigma \cdot z) = n! \cdot \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.
The final claim (no bialgebra structure on $B^{\Sigma}$) follows because
the bialgebra compatibility diagram of
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}(iii) requires the interchange
law for the $E_1$ tensor product, which does not hold in the symmetric
quotient.
\end{proof}

This is the central structural result: the vertex bialgebra framework
of Li~\cite{Li}, which works with $B^{\Sigma}$ and the coshuffle coproduct,
cannot carry a Hopf structure. The quantum group $R$-matrix,
the antipode, and the spectral-parameter dependence all live on the
$E_1$ side and are destroyed by symmetrization. The $E_1$-chiral
bialgebra is the minimal structure that retains all three.

\subsection{The Drinfeld center recovers $E_2$}
\label{subsec:drinfeld-recovers-e2}

The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra $A$ is ordered and non-braided. The braided
($E_2$) structure is recovered by the Drinfeld center.

\begin{proposition}[$R$-matrix from the Drinfeld center]
\label{prop:r-matrix-from-drinfeld}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Let $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ be an $E_1$-chiral Hopf
algebra. The Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ is a braided monoidal category
(Proposition~\ref{prop:e2-enhancement-drinfeld}), and the universal
$R$-matrix of the braided structure is
\[
 R(z) = (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_z(1_A) \in A \otimes A
 \otimes k((z)),
\]
where $1_A = \eta(1)$ is the vacuum and $S$ is the antipode. The
$R$-matrix lives in $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, not in $A \otimes A$
directly: it is the half-braiding data of the vacuum module against
all other modules. This resolves the apparent paradox that the
$R$-matrix ``lives in $A \otimes A$'' (which is ordered and has no
braiding): the $R$-matrix is a morphism in the Drinfeld center, which
IS braided.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ consists of pairs $(M, \sigma_M)$
where $M$ is an $A$-module and $\sigma_M \colon M \otimes_{E_1} N
\xrightarrow{\sim} N \otimes_{E_1} M$ is a half-braiding natural in $N$.
For $M = A$ (the regular module) with the half-braiding
$\sigma_A(a \otimes n) = \sum \Delta_z(a)_{(2)} \cdot n \otimes S(\Delta_z(a)_{(1)})$
(using Sweedler notation for $\Delta_z$), the half-braiding condition is
exactly the antipode axiom of
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-antipode}, and the resulting braiding
on $\cZ$ extracts $R(z) = (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_z(1_A)$.
The braiding satisfies the Yang--Baxter equation because the Drinfeld
center is a braided monoidal category (a formal consequence of the
definition). See Theorem~\ref{thm:drinfeld-center-coha} for the
explicit verification at $A = Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Examples}
\label{subsec:e1-bialg-examples}

\begin{example}[Heisenberg $E_1$-chiral bialgebra]
\label{ex:heisenberg-bialgebra}
The rank-one Heisenberg $H_k$ ($k \neq 0$) is an $E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra
with:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Product: $\mu(J(z_1), J(z_2)) = {:}J(z_1)J(z_2){:} + k/(z_1 - z_2)^2$
 (the time-ordered OPE, not the normally-ordered product).
 \item Coproduct: $\Delta_z(J) = J \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes J$ (primitive).
 The Heisenberg coproduct is $z$-independent because the $r$-matrix
 $r(z) = k/z$ has no nontrivial $z$-dependent correction to $\Delta$.
 \item Antipode: $S(J) = -J$, so $\mu_{E_1}(S(J) \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes J)
 = -J + J = 0 = \eta(\varepsilon(J))$.
 \item $R$-matrix from Drinfeld center: $R(z) = e^{k \log z \cdot h \otimes h}$
 (the Heisenberg vertex $R$-matrix; here $h$ is the Cartan generator
 identified with $J$). After averaging, $\mathrm{av}(R(z)) = k$
 (the level), consistent with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) = k$.
\end{itemize}
The Heisenberg is class~G: $\Delta$ is primitive, $S$ is the sign
involution, and the Hopf structure is the simplest possible.
\end{example}

\begin{conjecture}[$W_{1+\infty}$ $E_1$-chiral bialgebra]
\label{conj:w1inf-e1-bialgebra}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
The $W_{1+\infty}$ algebra at $c = 1$ (equivalently, the affine
Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ via Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak) carries an
$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra structure with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Coproduct}: for the generating field $T(u)$ (the Yangian

 for the higher spin fields $W_n$.
 \item \emph{Antipode}: $S(T(u)) = T(u)^{-1}$ (formal inverse of the

 \item \emph{$R$-matrix}: via the Drinfeld center,
 the universal $R$-matrix is the Yangian $R$-matrix
 of Theorem~\ref{thm:yang-r-matrix-ybe} with structure function $g(u)$.
 \item \emph{Averaging}: $\mathrm{av}(\Delta_z) = \Delta_{\mathrm{cosh}}$
 (the coshuffle coproduct on $B^{\Sigma}$), and

\end{enumerate}

Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}; the individual ingredients
(Yangian coproduct, $R$-matrix, antipode) are established
(Schiffmann--Vasserot, Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak), but their assembly into
an $E_1$-chiral bialgebra in the Swiss-cheese framework requires
verifying the compatibility diagram (iii) of
Definition~\ref{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra}.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[The three-way resolution]
\label{rem:three-way-resolution}
The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra resolves the apparent conflict between
three classical frameworks for quantum group structure on chiral algebras:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item \emph{Drinfeld quasi-Hopf}: lives in the symmetric monoidal
 category of vector spaces, with a non-coassociative coproduct corrected
 by the Drinfeld associator $\Phi$. This is the WRONG ambient category:
 chiral algebras are not vector spaces, and the associator $\Phi$ is an
 artifact of forcing a braided structure into a symmetric setting.
 \item \emph{Li vertex bialgebra}: lives in the $E_\infty$-chiral
 monoidal category, with the coshuffle coproduct on $B^{\Sigma}$.
 This uses the CORRECT ambient category (chiral) but the WRONG structure
 level ($E_\infty$), destroying the $R$-matrix.
 \item \emph{$E_1$-chiral bialgebra}: lives in the $E_1$-chiral monoidal
 category $\cM^{E_1}_C$, with the deconcatenation coproduct on
 $B^{\mathrm{ord}}$. This uses the correct ambient category AND the
 correct structure level. The $R$-matrix is recovered by passing to the
 Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, which is the $E_2$ enhancement.
\end{enumerate}
The slogan: the $E_1$-chiral bialgebra is the Hopf algebra object in the
category that chiral algebras actually inhabit when collision order is
remembered.
\end{remark}

\section{Closing: the ordered bar as the primitive of Vol~III}
\label{sec:closing-primitive}

Four facts control the later parts of Vol~III. First, factorization algebras on a complex curve are $E_2$ topologically but specialize to $E_1$ when holomorphy fixes an ordering, and the Swiss-cheese splitting $E_1(C) \times E_2(\mathrm{top})$ makes that specialization precise. Second, the ordered bar complex $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar A)$ with deconcatenation coproduct is the natural $E_1$ primitive, and averaging to the symmetric bar $B^{\Sigma}(A)$ is lossy because it kills the ordered $R$-matrix data. Third, the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ at $d=2$ produces an ordered bar whose Euler character is the Borcherds denominator and whose first shadow invariant is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, distinct from $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$. Fourth, the $E_2$ enhancement requires the Drinfeld center construction, the categorified averaging map $E_1\text{-Cat} \to E_2\text{-Cat}$; at $d=3$ the enhancement is conditional on the CY-A$_3$ programme.

This is the version of the $E_1$-$E_1$ operadic Koszul duality adapted to the CY setting: one form $\eta = d\log(z_1 - z_2)$, one relation (Arnold), one object $\Theta_A$, one equation $D_\ast \Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta] = 0$, all on the ordered bar of the cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra $A_\cC$ attached to the Calabi-Yau category $\cC$. The symmetric bar is the modular shadow.

The remaining parts use these facts directly. \ref{part:bridge} computes the modular trace $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ for each CY family, using the ordered bar as the computational model. \ref{part:examples} catalogues the families $K3$, $K3 \times E$, toric CY$_3$, Fukaya, and quantum-group, and identifies the corresponding point in the kappa-spectrum. \ref{part:connections} reads the seven faces of $r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$ from the $E_1$ structure of Proposition~\ref{prop:e1-sector-d2}. \ref{part:frontier} collects the open problems, most of them variants of Conjecture~\ref{conj:e1-sector-d3} or its downstream consequences. Throughout, the ordered bar is the primitive, and the averaging map, Drinfeld center, and modular characteristic are all computed on the $E_1$ side before any symmetric or braided image is taken.
