\section{The question}
\label{sec:the-question}

A Calabi--Yau category $\cC$ of dimension $d$ carries a canonical cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: a non-degenerate trace
\[
 \Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \longrightarrow k[-d]
\]
on its Hochschild homology, encoding the CY condition as a $d$-dimensional Frobenius structure at the chain level. The cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ is the primary invariant; it carries an $\Sd$-action from the $d$-sphere framing of the trace, and its $\Sd$-equivariant structure governs higher-genus amplitudes.

A chiral algebra $A$ on a curve $X$ carries a bar complex $B(A)$, a factorization coalgebra on $\Ran(X)$ whose differential encodes holomorphic OPE residues and whose coproduct encodes topological interval-cutting. At genus $g \geq 1$, the bar complex acquires curvature $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) \cdot \omega_g$ from the Hodge bundle, and the full modular structure is controlled by the universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_A := D_A - d_0$ (Volume~I). Together with the $\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ structure on the derived center pair $(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(A,A),\, A)$ (Volume~II), these data form the complete modular invariant.

That these two structures admit a common description is the organizing theorem of the volume. Both the CY cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ and the chiral bar complex $B(A)$ are instances of the same homotopical object: a cyclic $\Ainf$-coalgebra equipped with a compatible trace. The cyclic $\Ainf$-condition on the CY side (the non-degenerate pairing $\Tr$ satisfying the higher homotopy Frobenius relations) and the Maurer--Cartan equation $D\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta] = 0$ on the chiral side are two presentations of one structure. Homological algebra supplies the common language in which CY geometry and chiral algebra can be compared; the functor $\Phi$ is the translation, proved here for all~$d$.

The bridge is the functor $\Phi$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
 \left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{CY categories with}\\\text{appropriate $E_n$-structure}\end{array}\right\}
 \arrow[r, "\Phi"]
 &
 \left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{quantum chiral algebras}\\\text{with modular trace}\end{array}\right\}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
For $d = 2$, the functor $\Phi$ sends a CY category $\cC$ (Fukaya, derived, matrix factorization, or more general) to an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ whose bar complex $B(A_\cC)$ encodes the CY cyclic homology, with the CY trace realized as the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). For $d = 3$, the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction is resolved by an $\infty$-categorical argument: $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ for any smooth proper CY$_3$ category, and the space of $E_3$-liftings is contractible, producing an $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ (Theorem~CY-A$_3$). The braided monoidal structure is recovered via the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category.

\section{The $E_1$/$E_2$ chiral hierarchy}
\label{sec:e1-e2-hierarchy}

Three levels of chiral structure organize the theory:

\begin{itemize}
 \item $E_1$-chiral algebras (Volume~I): ordered factorization on $\Ran^{\mathrm{ord}}(X)$, carrying ordered bar complexes with $R$-matrices and Yangian structures. Introduced as algebraic-geometric objects in Volume~I; their derived centres are physically realised as 3d gauge theories in Volume~II. Representation categories are monoidal.
 \item $E_2$-chiral algebras (this work): braided factorization encoding the holomorphic-holomorphic sector. Representation categories are braided monoidal: the natural habitat of quantum groups. The CY quantum groups of this volume are concrete $E_1$-chiral quantum groups (Volume~I) whose Drinfeld centres carry $E_2$-braiding.
 \item The passage $E_1 \to E_2$ via Dunn additivity: $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$. An $E_2$-chiral algebra is an $E_1$-algebra in $E_1$-algebras.
\end{itemize}

The CY condition enters through Kontsevich's identification: a $d$-dimensional CY structure on $\cC$ determines an $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing of the Hochschild complex, hence an $\mathbb{S}^1$-action on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$. For $d = 2$ (CY surfaces), this $\mathbb{S}^1$-action is exactly the data of an $E_2$-algebra structure on the cyclic homology: the braiding.

\subsection{The $E_1$ stabilization theorem}
\label{subsec:e1-stabilization-intro}

For $d \geq 3$, the expected CY chiral output stabilizes at $E_1$, not $E_d$. The reason is structural: the $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket on polyvector fields $\mathrm{PV}^*(\mathbb{C}^d)$ vanishes identically for $d \geq 3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket} and its generalization), so the classical Lie conformal algebra is abelian and all noncommutative structure arises from quantization. The CoHA construction then gives an ordered (associative) product: the extension correspondence $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ has a preferred direction (sub before quotient), which is $E_1$, not $E_2$. The braided monoidal structure is recovered through the Drinfeld center of the $E_1$-monoidal representation category (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}) when the $E_1$ input exists.

The additional shifted structure beyond the base $E_1$ level is controlled by the \emph{framing obstruction} $\pi_d(BU)$, which measures the failure of the $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing on Hochschild homology to trivialize. Two independent computations of this obstruction, the unitary path (Bott periodicity for $BU$, period~2) and the symplectic/orthogonal path (Bott periodicity for $\Sp$ and $O$, period~8), produce the following landscape:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 $d$ & Native $E_n$ & $\pi_d(BU)$ & $\mathrm{Obs}_{\mathrm{eff}}(d)$ & Shifted structure \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $E_\infty$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (commutative) \\
 $2$ & $E_2$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & braiding parameter ($c_1$) \\
 $3$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (topological obstruction $0$) \\
 $4$ & $E_1$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$-shifted symplectic ($p_1$) \\
 $5$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $\mathbb{Z}_2$ & $\mathbb{Z}_2$-shifted ($\Sp$ refinement) \\
 $6$ & $E_1$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$-shifted ($c_3$) \\
 $7$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (trivial, mirrors $d = 3$) \\
 $8$ & $E_1$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$ & $\mathbb{Z}$-shifted (Bott-periodic) \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern repeats with period~$8$ by Bott periodicity of $\Sp$ and $O$. The effective obstruction is trivial precisely at $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$. All even $d \geq 2$ have $\mathbb{Z}$-valued obstruction from $\pi_d(BU) = \mathbb{Z}$. For CY$_4$, the obstruction is the first Pontryagin class $p_1 \in \pi_4(BU) = \mathbb{Z}$: it provides a level (analogous to the level of a Kac--Moody algebra) that governs the shifted symplectic structure on the moduli of objects. At $d = 3$, the vanishing of this topological obstruction does not by itself construct the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing required by CY-A$_3$. For $K3 \times K3$ ($d = 4$), the Hopf decomposition $\mathbb{S}^4 = \mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$ recovers $E_2$ by Dunn additivity.

The full proof of the stabilization theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) is in Chapter~\ref{ch:en-factorization}.

\begin{remark}[The five-object chain in the CY context]
\label{rem:v3-five-object-chain}
%: four functors, five distinct objects. Never conflate.
%: all kappa subscripted (kappa_ch).
Volume~I constructs five objects from a chiral algebra $A$ on a
curve~$X$, each produced by a distinct functor (Theorems~A--D$+$H).
Under the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi \colon \cC \mapsto A_\cC$,
these become CY invariants:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $A_\cC$ (the chiral algebra): the factorization envelope of the
 Lie conformal algebra extracted from the cyclic $\Ainf$-structure
 of~$\cC$.
 \item $B(A_\cC) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A}_\cC)$ (the bar coalgebra):
 a factorization coalgebra on $\Ran(X)$ with deconcatenation
 coproduct, encoding the CY cyclic bar complex
 $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$. The bar differential carries the
 holomorphic OPE data; at genus $g \geq 1$ the fiberwise
 differential satisfies $d_{\mathrm{fib}}^2 =
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \cdot \omega_g$.
 \item $A_\cC^i = H^*(B(A_\cC))$ (the dual coalgebra): the Koszul
 cohomology of the bar complex, a cooperad-algebra whose
 coalgebra structure encodes the BPS spectrum of~$\cC$.
 \item $A_\cC^! = ((A_\cC^i)^\vee)$ (the Koszul dual algebra):
 obtained by applying Verdier duality $D_{\Ran}$ to the bar
 coalgebra. For CY$_2$ categories, $A_\cC^!$ is the chiral
 algebra of the mirror CY$_2$.
 \item $\Zder(A_\cC)$ (the derived chiral center): the chiral
 Hochschild cochain complex, encoding the bulk sector. For a
 CY category of dimension~$d$, $\Zder(A_\cC)$ carries the
 $\mathbb{S}^d$-equivariant structure that governs higher-genus
 amplitudes.
\end{enumerate}
The five objects are connected by four functors (bar $B$,
cohomology $H^*$, linear duality $(-)^\vee$, Hochschild cochains),
none of which should be conflated. In particular: bar-cobar
$\Omega(B(A_\cC)) \xrightarrow{\sim} A_\cC$ is \emph{inversion}
(recovering the algebra), not Koszul duality; and $\Zder(A_\cC)$
is the Hochschild construction, not the output of bar-cobar.
The modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ governs
the curvature of $B(A_\cC)$ at genus $g \geq 1$ and equals the
CY Euler characteristic $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ at $d = 2$
(Theorem~CY-D).
\end{remark}


\begin{remark}[The three-volume hierarchy]
\label{rem:vol3-dimensional-reduction}
Volume~I constructs $E_n$-chiral algebras at all operadic levels
($n = 1, 2, 3, \infty$) as algebraic-geometric objects on curves
and their configuration spaces: the five theorems A--D$+$H are
the $E_\infty$-invariants that survive $\Sigma_n$-coinvariance,
while the $E_1$ data ($R$-matrices, Yangians, chiral quantum
groups, derived centres) lives in the ordered bar
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ and is developed in Volume~I.
Volume~II interprets these algebraic-geometric constructions
physically: the derived chiral centre IS the bulk of a real 3d
HT gauge theory on $\C_z \times \R_t$; the
$\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ pair IS a boundary-bulk system; 3d quantum
gravity is the climax.
This volume constructs the concrete $E_1$-chiral quantum groups
from CY geometry: Calabi--Yau categories of dimension $d \geq 3$
produce $\Eone$-chiral algebras via the functor $\Phi$, and the
CY quantum groups (CoHA, quantum toroidal algebras) are the
concrete examples of Volume~I's abstract framework. The Drinfeld
center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))$ recovers $E_2$-braiding. The
passage between volumes: Vol~I constructs and proves;
Vol~II interprets physically; Vol~III constructs concrete examples
from higher-dimensional geometry.
\end{remark}

\label{rem:v3-cy-glcm-shadow-classification}

(Volume~I) organizes the resulting landscape:
\begin{itemize}

\item Resolved conifold: class~$\mathbf{G}$ (shadow depth~$2$,

\item Local $\bP^2$: class~$\mathbf{M}$ (shadow depth~$\infty$,

\item $K3 \times E$: class~$\mathbf{M}$ (shadow depth~$\infty$,
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$). The
 infinite BPS spectrum drives the tower.
\end{itemize}
Two structural patterns emerge from the grand atlas
(Table~\ref{tab:cy3-grand-atlas}). First, compact CY$_3$ with
$\chi \neq 0$ are generically class~$\mathbf{M}$: the infinite BPS

\section{The analytic gap and the \v{C}ech resolution}
\label{sec:analytic-gap}

The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ at $d = 3$ requires a chain-level contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault complex of the CY threefold. For noncompact targets ($\mathbb{C}^3$, the conifold), the $T^3$-equivariant structure provides the necessary data. For compact CY$_3$, such as the quintic threefold $X_5 \subset \mathbb{P}^4$, the analytic construction of a contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault resolution would require solving a $\bar\partial$-equation with estimates; this is a PDE problem foreign to the algebraic framework of this volume.

The algebraic \v{C}ech resolution closes this gap at the perturbative level. The standard affine cover $\{U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}\}_{i=0}^4$ of $\mathbb{P}^4$ restricts to the quintic, giving five affine open sets whose finite intersections are all affine. Leray's theorem guarantees acyclicity of the \v{C}ech complex, and the explicit contracting homotopy $s^q(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) := f_{0 i_0 \cdots i_q}$ (prepend the distinguished index~$0$) satisfies the strong deformation retract conditions
\[
 \delta s + s \delta = \mathrm{Id} - i \circ p, \qquad
 s^2 = 0, \qquad
 s \circ i = 0, \qquad
 p \circ s = 0.
\]
The SDR data $(i, p, s)$ interfaces directly with the homotopy transfer theorem (HTT), transferring the $A_\infty$-structure from \v{C}ech cochains $\check{C}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E}))$ to its cohomology $\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{E})$. The transferred operations $\mu_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ are given by sums over planar trees whose internal edges are decorated by the homotopy~$s$; each application of~$s$ introduces rational functions on the affine intersections, and the resulting chiral operations are well-defined as formal power series.

The scope is perturbative: the \v{C}ech homotopy produces an algebraic $A_\infty$-structure on the Ext algebra, but convergence of the resulting series to holomorphic functions (the non-perturbative statement) requires separate input from the analytic completion programme of Volume~I (MC5, the analytic sewing section there). The perturbative statement suffices for the formal CY-to-chiral functor and for all combinatorial applications in this volume. The full construction, including the proof that the SDR conditions hold and the scope qualification, is Theorem~\ref{thm:cech-contracting-homotopy} in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.

For the quintic $X_5 = \{x_0^5 + \cdots + x_4^5 = 0\} \subset
\bP^4$, the computation proceeds as follows. The Hodge diamond
gives $h^{1,1} = 1$, $h^{2,1} = 101$, hence
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5) = 2(h^{1,1} - h^{2,1}) = -200$, so the
MacMahon-normalized topological quantity is
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 = -100$. The \v{C}ech cover
$\{U_i\}_{i=0}^4$ has $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ double intersections,
$\binom{5}{3} = 10$ triple intersections, $\binom{5}{4} = 5$
quadruple intersections, and one quintuple intersection
$U_{01234} = X_5 \cap \{x_i \neq 0 \text{ for all } i\}$. Each
$U_{i_0 \cdots i_q}$ is affine (intersection of an affine variety
with $X_5$), so the \v{C}ech complex computes sheaf cohomology by
Leray. The contracting homotopy $s$ prepends the distinguished
index~$0$: the operation
$s^q(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) = f_{0 i_0 \cdots i_q}$ introduces
rational functions with poles along $\{x_0 = 0\} \cap X_5$. The
transferred $\Ainf$-operations are finite sums: at degree~$k$,
the sum is over planar trees with $k$ leaves, and the number
of terms is the Catalan number $C_{k-1}$. At $k = 2$, there is a
single binary tree; at $k = 3$, two trees; the operations stabilize
to the formal CY-to-chiral data. The mirror quintic
$X_5^\vee$ has $h^{1,1} = 101$, $h^{2,1} = 1$, and
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5^\vee)/2 = +100$;
the sign flip
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 + \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5^\vee)/2
= 0$ is the CY analogue of the Koszul complementarity
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = K$ at $K = 0$,
consistent with the vanishing Koszul conductor for KM/free
families in Volume~I.

Three independent consistency checks confirm the resulting quintic normalization: the Gepner model matrix factorization (via mirror symmetry, $\mathrm{MF}(x_1^5 + \cdots + x_5^5)$), the large-volume Hodge--Kodaira--Rosenberg limit ($\mathrm{HKR}$ degeneration of $\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet$ to Dolbeault cohomology), and the Gopakumar--Vafa integrality of the resulting genus-$g$ amplitudes. All three are consistent with the same topological quantity $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 = -100$, which is distinct from $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ in the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum used in this volume.

\section{CY3 combinatorics as generalized root data}
\label{sec:cy3-root-data}

%: G(X) is defined via Phi for toric CY3, d=2, and now d=3 (CY-A_3 proved).
%: Conjecture CY-C (quantum group realization) remains open.
The combinatorics of a Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ (lattice, intersection form, BPS spectrum) constitute the \emph{generalized root datum} of a \emph{quantum vertex chiral group} $G(X)$. For toric CY3 (via the CoHA and Drinfeld double), for CY$_2$ categories (via Theorem~CY-A$_2$), and now for CY$_3$ categories (via Theorem~CY-A$_3$), the chiral algebra $A_\cC$ is constructed. The further identification of the representation category $\Rep^{E_2}(G(X))$ with classical quantum group categories is the content of Conjecture~CY-C.

Given a CY3 $X$, the generalized root datum $\mathcal{R}(X)$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item A lattice $\Lambda(X)$ extracted from $K(X)$ or $H^*(X)$ with its Euler form;
 \item Real simple roots $\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}$ from the geometry (hyperbolic sublattice for $K3 \times E$; toric diagram for toric CY3);
 \item Imaginary roots $\Delta^{\mathrm{im}} = \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_0 \sqcup \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_1$ (even and odd) with multiplicities $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ from BPS counting (Donaldson--Thomas invariants for $X$, or equivalently the Fourier coefficients of the appropriate automorphic form);
 \item A Weyl group $W(X)$ acting on the positive cone of $\Lambda(X)$;
 \item A Weyl vector $\rho \in \Lambda(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$.
\end{enumerate}

Two families of examples illustrate the construction.

\emph{The $K3 \times E$ tower.} For $X = (S \times E)/(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$ with $S$ a K3 surface and $E$ an elliptic curve, the lattice is $\Lambda^{3,2} \simeq \Lambda^{1,1} \oplus \Lambda^{1,1} \oplus [2]$ of signature $(3,2)$. The hyperbolic sublattice $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ with Gram matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ provides the real roots. The root multiplicities are the Fourier coefficients $f(n,l)$ of the weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$, the K3 elliptic genus. The resulting generalized Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ has the Igusa cusp form $\Delta_5$ as its denominator identity. The single-copy chiral modular characteristic satisfies $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3 \times E) = 3 = \dim_\C$ (from additivity: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 2 + 1$), verified by six independent paths; the BKM automorphic weight is the distinct quantity $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ (the weight of $\Delta_5$; see the Shadow--Siegel gap theorem below); the factor~$2$ in $Z_{K3} = 2\,\phi_{0,1}$ is the bar-complex moonshine multiplier $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cA_{K3})$; and the shadow obstruction tower does not produce $\Delta_5$ directly (four structural obstructions: categorical, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-mismatch, second quantization, Schottky; Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-siegel-gap}).

\emph{Toric CY3 and critical CoHAs.} For a toric CY3 determined by a fan $\Sigma$, the toric diagram determines a quiver $Q_X$. The critical cohomological Hall algebra $\mathrm{CoHA}(Q_X)$ is the positive half of an affine super Yangian $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q_X})$. For $\mathbb{C}^3$: the Jordan quiver gives $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1) \simeq \mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$. The toric fan is the Dynkin data.

\section{Automorphic correction as shadow obstruction tower}
\label{sec:automorphic-shadow}

The passage from the naive Kac--Moody algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ (built from the real root Gram matrix alone) to the full generalized BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_X$ (with all imaginary roots and their multiplicities) is the \emph{automorphic correction}. In the language of Volume~I, this passage is the \emph{shadow obstruction tower}:

\begin{tabular}{lll}
 \toprule

 \midrule

The denominator identity of $\mathfrak{g}_X$ ($\Delta_5$ for $K3 \times E$, the DT partition function for toric CY3) is the bar-complex Euler product of the quantum vertex chiral group. For toric CY3 the full identification with classical quantum group categories is part of Conjecture~CY-C. \section{Main results}
\label{sec:main-results}

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Theorem CY-A} (CY-to-chiral functor; \emph{proved at all~$d$}): Construction of $\Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \to E_n\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$ via the factorization envelope and $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing. For $d = 2$: the $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing gives $\Etwo$ via Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). For $d = 3$: the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction is resolved by the $\infty$-categorical proof ($\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$, the space of $E_3$-liftings contractible), producing an $\Eone$-chiral algebra (Theorem~CY-A$_3$). Since $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{R}^3))$ is trivial, the native $E_3$-braiding is symmetric; the quantum group braiding is recovered through the Drinfeld center of $E_1$-monoidal categories.
 \item \textbf{Theorem CY-B} ($E_2$-chiral Koszul duality; \emph{programme}): Bar-cobar adjunction in the $E_2$-chiral setting. Items (i)--(ii) follow from the Volume~I machine applied to each $E_1$-direction via Dunn additivity; the full $E_2$-equivariant refinement (coherent intertwining by the $R$-matrix) is established as a rigorous proof sketch, with detailed verification of higher coherences deferred. The further identification of automorphic correction with the shadow obstruction tower and of the denominator identity with the bar-complex Euler product remains a programme rather than a proved consequence.
 \item \textbf{Conjecture CY-C} (Quantum group realization): For toric CY3, $\Rep^{E_2}(G(X))$ should be braided monoidal equivalent to $\Rep(Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q_X}))$; for $K3 \times E$, the $\Sp_4(\mathbb{Z})$-module structure on the denominator identity should recover the braided structure. The critical CoHA is the $E_1$-sector (positive half). The CY category $\cC(\frakg, q)$ is not yet constructed in general.
 \item \textbf{Theorem CY-D} (Modular CY characteristic; \emph{proved at $d = 2$, programme at $d \geq 3$}): For $d = 2$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ equals the CY Euler characteristic $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ (proved via Serre duality $S_\cC = [2]$). For $K3 \times E$ ($d = 3$), $\Phi_3$ now constructs $A_{K3 \times E}$, and the Borcherds weight gives the distinct automorphic invariant $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$; the relation between $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{K3 \times E})$ and the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum is a programme. The general $d = 3$ formula is conjectural.
 \item \textbf{Theorem (K3 Yangian)}: The K3 double current algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{K3}$ quantizes to an abelian Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$ with 24 Heisenberg generators $J_i$, Mukai-signature Serre relations, and degree-$(24,24)$ structure function. The super-Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{gl}(4|20))$ provides the natural envelope. For $K3 \times E$, six independent routes (BKM, lattice VOA, Kummer, CY-to-chiral, sigma model, BLLPR) converge on $G(K3 \times E)$, whose denominator identity is $\Phi_{10}$.
 \item \textbf{Theorem (Shadow--Siegel gap)}: The shadow obstruction tower of $K3 \times E$ does not produce the Igusa cusp form $\Phi_{10}$. Four structural obstructions: categorical (number vs function), modular characteristic ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3 \neq 5 = \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$), second quantization (single-copy vs DMVV symmetric product), and Schottky at $g \geq 4$ ($\mathrm{codim} = (g{-}2)(g{-}3)/2$). \section{The $E_1$-chiral bialgebra and Hopf axioms}
\label{sec:e1-bialgebra-intro}

Volume~I introduces $E_1$-chiral quantum groups as abstract
algebraic-geometric objects. This volume constructs them
concretely from CY geometry. The axioms, formalized in
\S\ref{sec:e1-chiral-bialgebras}, are recorded here for
reference; they constitute the algebraic framework into which
all CY quantum groups of this volume are placed.

\begin{definition}[$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra; preview]
\label{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra-intro}
An \emph{$E_1$-chiral Hopf algebra} on a smooth curve $C$ is a
tuple $(A, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ satisfying five
axioms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)},nosep]
\item \emph{$E_1$-chiral algebra.}
 $(A, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an augmented $E_1$-chiral algebra
 on~$C$: the product $\mu$ is the ordered OPE, and the
 operations live on configuration spaces
 $\mathrm{Conf}_k^{\mathrm{ord}}(C)$ of ordered points.
\item \emph{Spectral coproduct.}
 For each $z \in \Ran(C)$,
 $\Delta_z \colon A \to A \otimes_{E_1, z} A$
 is an $E_1$-chiral coalgebra structure. At $z = 0$, the
 coproduct $\Delta_0$ coincides with the deconcatenation
 coproduct on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$; the $z$-deformation
 encodes the spectral data.
\item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility.}
 The coproduct $\Delta_z$ is a morphism of $E_1$-chiral
 algebras: the diagram expressing ``$\Delta_z$ is an algebra
 map'' commutes, using the interchange law for the
 $E_1$-monoidal structure. This is the chiral analogue of the
 classical bialgebra axiom.
\item \emph{Spectral coassociativity.}
 For $z, w \in \Ran(C)$ in general position:
 \[
  (\Delta_w \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  = (\mathrm{id} \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
 \]
 At $z = w = 0$ this reduces to ordinary coassociativity.
\item \emph{Hopf axiom.}
 The antipode $S$ satisfies
 \[
  \mu_{E_1} \circ (S \otimes \mathrm{id}) \circ \Delta_0
  = \eta \circ \varepsilon
  = \mu_{E_1} \circ (\mathrm{id} \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0.
 \]
 The first four axioms are the bialgebra data; this fifth axiom
 upgrades the ordered object to the Hopf framework used for the
 quantum-group applications.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ preserves the $R$-matrix;
the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(A)$ of Volume~I kills the Hopf
structure via averaging
$\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The $E_1$-chiral
bialgebra (not the $E_\infty$-vertex bialgebra of Li) is the
correct Hopf framework: the coproduct $\Delta_z$ lives on the
$E_1$ (ordered) side, and the $E_\infty$-averaging map destroys
the Hopf data. The associated $R$-matrix and Yang--Baxter equation
are recovered from the ordered Hopf data rather than postulated as
separate axioms. Two concrete instances are verified:
the Heisenberg algebra $H_k$ (where $\Delta_z$ is the constant
coproduct and $R(z) = k/z$) and $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at generic
$\Psi$ (where the universal Miura coefficient
$(\Psi{-}1)/\Psi$ governs the leading cross-term at every spin
$s \geq 2$). The full development is in
Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}.

\section{The ten research programmes}
\label{sec:ten-programmes}

The $K3 \times E$ prototype generates ten research programmes, each with formal conjectures grounded in the 90+ compute engines. The formal development is in Chapter~\ref{chap:toroidal-elliptic}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(Prog \Alph*)}]
\item \emph{Constructive CY gluing.}
 Reconstruct $D^b(K3 \times E)$ from quiver charts indexed by
 Bridgeland stability conditions. The bar functor commutes
 with homotopy colimits (Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-hocolim}),
 so the global chiral algebra is the hocolim of local CoHAs.
 The minimum chart number, the Brauer obstruction, and the
 extension to non-toric K3 surfaces are open.
\item \emph{$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ as universal moonshine multiplier.}
 The factor $2$ in $Z_{K3} = 2\,\phi_{0,1}$ is $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3)
 = \chi(\cO_{K3})$. The BPS degeneracies factorise as
 $A_n = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} \cdot \dim(\rho_n)$, separating the homological
 part ($\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$, from the bar complex) from the
 group-theoretic part ($\rho_n$, from the sporadic symmetry).
 The programme: extend this factorisation to the Monster module,
 the Conway module, and all holomorphic VOAs.
\item \emph{Second-quantization bridge.}
 The ratio $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}/\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}
 = 5/3$ encodes the first-to-second-quantization passage:
 the single-copy chiral algebra sees $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$,
 while the Borcherds lift sees $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$.
 The conjectural formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(S \times E)
 = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(S) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(S \times E)$ expresses the weight
 of the automorphic form as a sum of fiber and global
 modular characteristics. The Hilbert--Chow exceptional
 divisor accounts for
 $\chi(\operatorname{Hilb}^2) - \chi(\operatorname{Sym}^2)
 = 24 = \chi(K3)$, and the DMVV symmetric product formula
 implements the passage from single-copy bar complex to
 second-quantized partition function.
\item \emph{Schottky shadow programme.}
 At $g \leq 3$, the Torelli map
 $\cM_g \to \cA_g$ is dominant and the shadow obstruction
 tower sees nearly all of the moduli. At $g \geq 4$, the
 Schottky locus has positive codimension
 $\operatorname{codim} = (g{-}2)(g{-}3)/2$ and the shadow is
 imprisoned in tautological classes on
 $\overline{\cM}_g$. The programme: characterise the
 tautological projection of Siegel modular forms, and
 determine whether the shadow tower generates the image of
 $S_*(\Sp_{2g}(\Z)) \to R^*(\overline{\cM}_g)$.
 The first nontrivial case $g = 4$ (codimension~$1$)
 already constrains the genus-$4$ shadow coefficient.
\item \emph{Mock modularity and the bar complex.}
 The 1/4-BPS counting function is a mock modular form with
 shadow $24\,\eta^3$. The factor $24 = \chi(K3)$ is
 the surface announcing its existence. The programme:
 construct a half-integral-weight metaplectic bar complex
 whose shadow obstruction tower produces the mock modular
 completion.
\item \emph{Modular factorization envelope.}
 Construct the universal modular factorization envelope
 $U_X^{\mathrm{mod}}(L)$ for cyclically admissible Lie
 conformal algebras, carrying the full shadow obstruction
 tower. This would give the left adjoint of the modular
 primitive-current functor. The construction requires the
 modular operad structure on the collection of $\Ainf$-operations
 parametrised by $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$; for CY input, the
 cyclic $\Ainf$-structure of $\cC$ provides exactly the data
 needed for cyclical admissibility.
\item \emph{BKM algebras and scattering diagrams.}
 The Gross--Siebert scattering diagram consistency condition
 IS the $E_1$ Maurer--Cartan equation
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:scattering-mc}). The programme: lift
 this identification to the full motivic Hall algebra level
 (where naive BCH is insufficient).
\item \emph{Descent theorem.}
 \v{C}ech descent for $E_1$-algebras over the Bridgeland
 stability manifold: the global $E_1$-chiral algebra is the
 homotopy colimit of local CoHAs indexed by stability chambers,
 with transition maps given by MC gauge equivalence across walls.
 Proved for toric CY$_3$
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}); open for
 compact CY$_3$ beyond the chart-atlas regime, where
 the number of charts may be infinite and the hocolim
 requires a convergence argument.
\item \emph{Higher-dimensional CY shadows.}
 The $E_n$ stabilization theorem
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) gives a Bott-periodic
 8-fold pattern: the effective obstruction
 $\mathrm{Obs}_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \in \pi_d(BU)$ is trivial at
 $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$ and $\Z$-valued at all even~$d$.
 For CY$_4$: the $p_1$ Pontryagin class controls
 the shifted symplectic structure via the obstruction
 $\pi_4(BU) = \Z$.
 For $K3 \times K3$: the Hopf decomposition
 $\mathbb{S}^4 = \mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$
 gives $E_2$ by Dunn additivity.
\item \emph{Convergence vs divergence.}
 The shadow partition function converges with radius $2\pi$
 (Bernoulli decay: $|F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}| \sim (2\pi)^{-2g}/g$);
 the topological string diverges
 (Gevrey-$1$: $|F_g^{\mathrm{top}}| \sim (2g)!$). For
 class~$\mathbf{G}$ algebras (Heisenberg, lattice VOAs), the
 shadow is the full answer; for class~$\mathbf{M}$
 ($\cW_{1+\infty}$, Virasoro), the instanton sum is infinite.
 The programme: quantify the instanton gap
 $F_g^{\mathrm{top}} - F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}$ at each genus, and
 determine whether the shadow tower is Borel-summable to the
 full amplitude.
\end{enumerate}

All ten programmes are computationally testable: the 90+ compute engines provide a laboratory for each conjecture.

\section{The landscape of examples}
\label{sec:landscape-examples}

The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ operates on any CY category with cyclic $A_\infty$-structure. Four families of CY categories provide the standard landscape; each family contributes distinct structural features to the theory.

\emph{Fukaya categories} (Chapter~\ref{ch:fukaya}). The symplectic input. For an elliptic curve $E_\tau$, the Fukaya category $\Fuk(E_\tau)$ is CY of dimension~$1$ and $\Phi$ produces the Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_k$ at level $k = \mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$. For a K3 surface~$S$, $\Fuk(S)$ is CY of dimension~$2$ and $\Phi$ produces an $E_2$-chiral algebra with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$. For compact CY threefolds, the Fukaya-side functor is now available via Theorem~CY-A$_3$; the open-string sector ($\Fuk(X)$ with Lagrangian boundary conditions) connects to the Volume~II Swiss-cheese structure. Wrapped Fukaya categories $\mathcal{W}(X)$ of Liouville manifolds provide the non-compact analogue: for cotangent bundles $T^*M$, Abouzaid's equivalence $\mathcal{W}(T^*M) \simeq \mathrm{Mod}(C_*(\Omega M))$ reduces the CY-to-chiral functor to the based loop space.

\emph{Derived categories of coherent sheaves}. The algebraic input. For a smooth projective CY manifold $X$, the bounded derived category $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ is CY of dimension~$d$ with the CY trace induced by Serre duality. Homological mirror symmetry identifies $\Fuk(X) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X^\vee))$; under $\Phi$, this becomes an equivalence $A_{\Fuk(X)} \simeq A_{D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X^\vee))}$ of quantum chiral algebras, providing a consistency check between the symplectic and algebraic sides. When $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ admits a full exceptional collection, the CY-to-chiral functor reduces to a quiver-with-potential computation: the critical CoHA of the quiver (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}). Bridgeland stability conditions parametrize the space of t-structures on $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$; wall-crossing in the stability manifold corresponds to mutations of the bar complex, and the global chiral algebra is assembled as a homotopy colimit over stability chambers (Programme~A).

\emph{Matrix factorizations}. The Landau--Ginzburg input. A polynomial $W \colon \mathbb{C}^n \to \mathbb{C}$ gives a CY category $\mathrm{MF}(W)$ of dimension $n - 2$. For ADE singularities $W = x^N + y^2 + z^2 + w^2$ in four variables, $\mathrm{MF}(W)$ is CY of dimension~$2$ and $\Phi$ (Theorem~CY-A$_2$) produces chiral algebras related to $\mathcal{W}_N$-algebras. The LG/CY correspondence $\mathrm{MF}(W) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X_W))$ provides a further consistency check against the derived-category side. For non-ADE singularities (unimodal, bimodal), the resulting chiral algebras are expected to be new objects not realized by the standard Lie-theoretic landscape of Volume~I.

\emph{Quantum group representations} (Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-group-reps}). The representation-theoretic target. The braided monoidal category $\mathrm{Rep}_q(\mathfrak{g})$ is what the CY programme aims to realize: Conjecture~CY-C predicts that $\mathrm{Rep}_q(\mathfrak{g}) \simeq \mathrm{Rep}^{E_2}(A_\cC)$ for a CY category $\cC(\mathfrak{g}, q)$. The Kazhdan--Lusztig equivalence $\mathrm{Rep}_q(\mathfrak{g}) \simeq \mathrm{KL}_k(\mathfrak{g})$ at roots of unity is the prototype: the Volume~I bar complex $B(V_k(\mathfrak{g}))$ encodes the quantum group $R$-matrix in its degree-$(1,1)$ component, and the DK bridge (Volume~II, MC3 on the evaluation-generated core for all simple types) recovers the quantum group categorical structure used here. The affine Yangian realization (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}) provides the $E_1$-sector; the Drinfeld center (Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center}) provides the $E_2$-enhancement.

In addition to the four standard families, the toroidal and elliptic algebras chapter (Chapter~\ref{chap:toroidal-elliptic}) develops the $K3 \times E$ programme in full: the generalized root datum, the BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ with its Igusa cusp form denominator identity, the shadow--Siegel gap theorem, thirteen structural results (K3-1 through K3-13), all ten research programmes (A through J), and the DDCA--toroidal bridge identifying the deformed double current algebra as the rational degeneration of the quantum toroidal algebra (Conjecture~\ref{conj:ddca-toroidal-bridge}). The K3 double current algebra $\fg_{K3}$, discussed in the DDCA--toroidal bridge of Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e}, provides the K3 analogue of the DDCA, replacing $\bC[u,v]$ with $H^*(S,\bC)$. The toric CY$_3$ CoHA chapter (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}) develops the affine Yangian and $\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$ side: the Jordan quiver gives $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1) \simeq \mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$, the toric fan is the Dynkin data, and the shuffle algebra presentation connects to the KZ equations of Volume~I.

\section{Conventions and notation}
\label{sec:conventions-intro}

This volume inherits the conventions of Volumes~I and~II; we record here the points where the CY setting introduces additional notation or where conventions differ across the literature. A comprehensive conventions appendix is Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}.

\emph{Grading.} All grading is cohomological: differentials have degree $+1$. The bar construction uses desuspension $s^{-1}$, with $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$ (matching Volume~I; see signs\_and\_shifts.tex therein). The CY dimension~$d$ enters as a cohomological shift: the CY trace $\mathrm{Tr} \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$ is a map of degree $-d$.

\emph{$E_n$ notation.} We write $E_n$ for the little $n$-disks operad (Boardman--Vogt) and $E_\infty$ for the commutative operad. Dunn additivity: $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$. An $E_2$-chiral algebra (Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra}) factorizes along two holomorphic directions; its representation category is braided monoidal (Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}). We write $\mathrm{FM}_k(\mathbb{C})$ for the Fulton--MacPherson compactification of $\mathrm{Conf}_k(\mathbb{C})$; this is a blowup along diagonals, not the complement of the diagonal.

\emph{Divided powers and the $\lambda$-bracket.} The translation between OPE modes $a_{(n)}b$ and the $\lambda$-bracket uses the divided-power convention $\lambda^{(n)} = \lambda^n / n!$, so $\{a_\lambda b\} = \sum_n \lambda^{(n)} a_{(n)}b$. The $\lambda$-bracket coefficient at order~$n$ is $a_{(n)}b / n!$, not $a_{(n)}b$ (Volume~I). This convention is consistent with Volumes~I and~II; when consulting references that use the Borel transform $B(K)(\lambda) = \sum \lambda^{(n)} c_n$, the $1/n!$ is already absorbed into $\lambda^{(n)}$.

\emph{CY categories.} We write $\CY_d$-$\Cat$ for the $\infty$-category of smooth $d$-dimensional CY categories. Tensor products of dg categories are derived unless stated otherwise. ``Equivalence'' means quasi-equivalence of dg categories (equivalence of associated $\infty$-categories). The Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg isomorphism $\HH_\bullet(D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))) \simeq H^*(X, \Omega^\bullet_X)$ is used freely for smooth projective varieties.

\emph{The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum.}
A single CY manifold admits multiple modular characteristics,
each measuring a different face of the geometry. This volume uses
subscripted $\kappa_\bullet$ throughout:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (the chiral algebra modular characteristic
of Volume~I, defined as the degree-$2$ projection of $\Theta_A$),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ (the holomorphic Euler characteristic
$\chi(\cO_X)$, a categorical invariant),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ (the half-weight of the Borcherds
automorphic form, an automorphic invariant), and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ (the lattice rank of the fiber,
relevant for fibered CY manifolds). Unsubscripted use is
forbidden in this volume; every occurrence carries an explicit
qualifier.

\emph{Operadic terminology.}
Following Volume~I, the word ``degree'' is used universally
for: bar complex grading, operadic input count, tree vertex
valence, Stasheff identity level, Swiss-cheese mixed sector
parameters, and all index-counting contexts. The word ``arity''
does not appear.

\emph{Bar complex conventions.}
The bar complex is $B(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$ with $\bar{A}
= \ker(\varepsilon)$ the augmentation ideal. The tensor coalgebra
$T^c$ carries the deconcatenation coproduct. The ordered bar
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ is the primitive object; the symmetric bar
$B^\Sigma(A)$ is the $\Sigma_n$-coinvariant quotient. The
desuspension satisfies $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$.

\emph{Cross-volume references.} Results from Volume~I are cited as ``Vol~I, Theorem~X'' or by their Volume~I label (e.g., thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic). Results from Volume~II are cited analogously. When a formula appears in multiple volumes, the conventions may differ: Volume~I uses OPE modes, Volume~II uses $\lambda$-brackets with divided powers, and this volume uses both depending on context. Every cross-volume formula citation in this work has been independently verified against the source.
