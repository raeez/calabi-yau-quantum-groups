\section{The question}
\label{sec:the-question}
\label{sec:the-problem}

A Calabi--Yau category~$\cC$ of dimension~$d$ carries a canonical
cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: a non-degenerate trace
\[
 \Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \longrightarrow k[-d]
\]
on its Hochschild homology, encoding the CY condition as a
$d$-dimensional Frobenius structure at the chain level. The
cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ is the primary
invariant; it carries an $\Sd$-action from the $d$-sphere
framing of the trace, and its $\Sd$-equivariant structure
governs higher-genus amplitudes.

A chiral algebra~$\cA$ on a curve~$X$ carries a bar complex
$B(\cA)$, a factorization coalgebra on $\Ran(X)$ whose
differential encodes holomorphic OPE residues and whose
coproduct encodes topological interval-cutting. At genus
$g \geq 1$, the bar complex acquires curvature
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA) \cdot \omega_g$ from the Hodge
bundle, and the full modular structure is controlled by the
universal Maurer--Cartan element
$\Theta_\cA := D_\cA - d_0$ (Volume~I). Together with the
$\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ structure on the derived centre pair
$(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA, \cA),\, \cA)$ (Volume~II), these
data form the complete modular invariant.

Both $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ and $B(\cA)$ are instances of
the same homotopical object: a cyclic $\Ainf$-coalgebra
equipped with a compatible trace. The cyclic $\Ainf$-condition
on the CY side (non-degenerate pairing $\Tr$ satisfying the
higher homotopy Frobenius relations) and the Maurer--Cartan
equation
$D\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$
on the chiral side are two presentations of one structure.
Homological algebra supplies the common language in which CY
geometry and chiral algebra can be compared; the translation
is \emph{one} symmetric monoidal functor
\[
 \boxed{\;\Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(\cM_d),
 \qquad
 n = \begin{cases}\infty, & d = 1, \\ 2, & d = 2, \\
 1, & d \ge 3,\end{cases}\;}
\]
characterised by four universal properties:

\begin{description}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=1.8em,style=nextline]
\item[(U1) Hochschild pullback.]
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Phi(\cC)) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
as factorisation coalgebras on $\Ran(X)$. The categorical
Hochschild data is the ordered-bar data of the chiral
output.
\item[(U2) CY-morphism functoriality.]
A wall-crossing in $\cC$ (mutation, Bridgeland traversal,
flop) maps to an $R$-matrix gauge transformation on
$\Phi(\cC)$. Wall-crossing $=$ MC gauge equivalence in the
modular convolution algebra of $\Phi(\cC)$.
\item[(U3) Drinfeld-centre compatibility.]
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC))) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\Phi(\cC)))$ at $d \ge 3$
(Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler,
Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}). The half-braiding
$\sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$ \emph{is} the quantum group $R$-matrix
$R(z)$ with $z = u - v$
(Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}).
\item[(U4) Standard-input recovery.]
$\Phi(\Coh(E_\tau)) = $ lattice VOA at $d = 1$
(Heisenberg at level $\mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$);
$\Phi(D^b(K3)) = H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ at $d = 2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-k3-explicit});
$\Phi(\CoHA(\C^3)) = Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ at $d = 3$
(positive half, Drinfeld-double envelope is
$\cW_{1+\infty}$).
\end{description}

The four-step recipe (cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal $\to$
factorization envelope $\to$ $\Sd$-enhanced quantization)
is the \emph{proof} of existence and uniqueness; the four
universal properties (U1)--(U4) are the \emph{statement}.
The per-$d$ theorems CY-A$_1$, CY-A$_2$, CY-A$_3$,
CY-A$_{d \ge 4}$ are evaluations of the unified $\Phi$,
not parallel theorems.

\textbf{Native operadic level dispatching.} The CY dimension
fixes the native chiral level: $n = \infty$ at $d = 1$
(commutative VOA), $n = 2$ at $d = 2$ (braided VOA), $n = 1$
at $d \ge 3$ (ordered chiral). \emph{At $d \ge 3$,
$\Phi(\cC)$ is $\Eone$-chiral; the $\Etwo$-braided structure
lives on the Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi(\cC)))$
only}, never on the chiral algebra itself
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \ge 3$ kills any direct
braiding). Direct restriction $E_3 \to E_2$ would give only
symmetric braiding; the Drinfeld-centre mechanism is the
sole source of non-trivial braiding at higher $d$.

For $d = 2$, $\Phi$ sends a CY category~$\cC$ (Fukaya, derived,
matrix factorization, or more general) to an $\Etwo$-chiral
algebra $\cA_\cC$ whose bar complex $B(\cA_\cC)$ encodes the CY
cyclic homology, with the CY trace realized as the modular
characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_\cC)$
(Theorem~CY-A$_2$). For $d = 3$, the chain-level
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction is resolved by an
$\infty$-categorical argument: $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ for
every smooth proper CY$_3$ category and the space of
$E_3$-liftings is contractible, producing an $\Eone$-chiral
algebra $\cA_\cC$ (Theorem~CY-A$_3$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}). The braided
monoidal structure is constructed via the Drinfeld centre of
the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category: the centre
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC))$ adjoins half-braidings
$\sigma_M \colon M \otimes (-) \xrightarrow{\sim} (-) \otimes M$
to each module~$M$, and the quantum group $R$-matrix is
\[
 R(z) \;=\; \sigma_{V_u}(V_v)
 \qquad (z = u - v)
\]
evaluated on spectral-parameter families of evaluation modules
(Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}). The construction
is unconditional via the $\infty$-categorical closure
(Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}).

\section{The $\En$ chiral hierarchy}
\label{sec:e1-e2-hierarchy}

The CY dimension~$d$ determines the native $\En$ level of the
chiral algebra $\Phi(\cC)$ through the Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC, \cC)$, which has degree $1 - d$:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.4ex]
 \item $d = 1$: $E_\infty$-chiral (commutative). The degree-$0$
  bracket is a Poisson bracket; the associated Lie conformal
  algebra is abelian and the factorization envelope is
  commutative. Representation categories are symmetric monoidal.
  Example: $\Fuk(E_\tau)$ produces the Heisenberg VOA, which is
  $E_\infty$ as a vertex algebra.
 \item $d = 2$: $E_2$-chiral (braided). The degree-$(-1)$
  bracket is a Lie bracket (the $\lambda$-bracket). The
  $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing of $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ gives $E_2$
  directly via Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos. Representation
  categories are braided monoidal: the natural habitat of
  quantum groups at this dimension.
 \item $d = 3$: $E_1$-chiral (ordered) \emph{natively}. The
  degree-$(-2)$ bracket is a shifted Lie bracket. The CoHA
  multiplication is ordered (short exact sequences have a
  preferred direction), giving an associative but \emph{not}
  braided product. The braided $E_2$ structure is
  \emph{derived}, not native: it lives on the Drinfeld centre
  $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(\cA))$, a braided monoidal \emph{category},
  not a property of the chiral algebra itself.
 \item $d \geq 4$: $E_1$-stabilized. The $\pi_d(BU)$ obstruction
  governs shifted structure but the native level remains $E_1$.
\end{itemize}

The passage $E_1 \to E_2$ is always through the Drinfeld centre:
\[
 \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(\cA))
 \;\simeq\; \Rep^{E_2}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Drin}(\cA)\bigr)\,.
\]
By Dunn additivity, $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$, so an
$E_2$-algebra is an $E_1$-algebra in $E_1$-algebras. The CY
quantum groups of this volume are concrete $E_1$-chiral quantum
groups (Volume~I) whose Drinfeld centres carry $E_2$-braiding.

The CY condition enters through Kontsevich's identification: a
$d$-dimensional CY structure on $\cC$ determines an
$\mathbb{S}^d$-framing of the Hochschild complex. For $d = 2$
(CY surfaces), this framing gives $E_2$ directly. For $d = 3$,
the $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing resolves the $E_3$-lifting obstruction
but the native chiral algebra is $E_1$; the $E_2$-braiding on
representation categories is recovered via the Drinfeld centre.

\subsection{The $\Eone$ stabilization theorem}
\label{subsec:e1-stabilization-intro}

For $d \geq 3$, the CY chiral output stabilizes at $\Eone$, not
$E_d$. The reason is the degree of the Gerstenhaber bracket: on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$, the bracket has degree $1 - d$, which for
$d \geq 3$ is $\leq -2$, too shifted to produce braiding.
Equivalently,
\[
 \pi_1\bigl(\Conf_2^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R^d)\bigr)
 \;=\; \pi_1(S^{d-1}) \;=\; 0
 \qquad (d \geq 3)\,.
\]
For toric CY$_d$ ($\C^d$ with $\GL(d)$-action), the
$\GL(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets vanish
identically (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}): the
classical Lie conformal algebra is abelian and all
noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.
For non-toric CY$_3$, the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket is
generically nonzero, but its shifted degree still prevents
braiding. The CoHA construction gives an ordered (associative)
product: the extension correspondence
$0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ has a preferred direction
(sub before quotient), which is $E_1$, not $E_2$. The braided
monoidal structure is constructed via the Drinfeld centre of
the $E_1$-monoidal representation category
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center},
Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}): the half-braiding
$\sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$ on evaluation modules is the quantum group
$R$-matrix.

The additional shifted structure beyond the base $\Eone$ level
is controlled by the framing obstruction $\pi_d(BU)$. Two
independent computations of this obstruction (the unitary path
via Bott periodicity for $BU$, period~$2$, and the
symplectic/orthogonal path via Bott periodicity for $\Sp$ and $O$,
period~$8$) produce the following landscape:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 $d$ & Native $\En$ & $\pi_d(BU)$
  & $\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d)$ & Shifted structure \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $E_\infty$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (commutative) \\
 $2$ & $E_2$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & braiding parameter ($c_1$) \\
 $3$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (topological obstruction $0$) \\
 $4$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted symplectic ($p_1$) \\
 $5$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$-shifted ($\Sp$ refinement) \\
 $6$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted ($c_3$) \\
 $7$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (trivial, mirrors $d = 3$) \\
 $8$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted (Bott-periodic) \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern repeats with period~$8$ by Bott periodicity of
$\Sp$ and $O$. The effective obstruction is trivial precisely
at $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$. All even $d \geq 2$ have
$\Z$-valued obstruction from $\pi_d(BU) = \Z$. For CY$_4$, the
obstruction is the first Pontryagin class
$p_1 \in \pi_4(BU) = \Z$: it provides a level (analogous to the
level of a Kac--Moody algebra) that governs the shifted
symplectic structure on the moduli of objects. At $d = 3$, the
vanishing of this topological obstruction does not by itself
construct the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing required by
CY-A$_3$. For $K3 \times K3$ ($d = 4$), the $E_4$-framing of
$\Phi_4(\cC_{K3 \times K3})$ splits as the K\"unneth product
of two $E_2$-framings, one per $K3$ factor, compatible with
the Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos $\mathbb{S}^2$-action on each
factor's Hochschild homology; by Dunn additivity
$E_2 \otimes E_2 = E_4$, so the product carrier recovers $E_2$
per factor without invoking any (false) sphere identification
$\mathbb{S}^4 \cong \mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$.

The full proof of the stabilization theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) is in
Chapter~\ref{ch:en-factorization}.

\section{The Mukai Lagrangian and the Platonic ensemble}
\label{sec:mukai-lagrangian-platonic}

For a Calabi--Yau variety~$X$, the even-graded cohomology
\[
 \Lambda_X \;\subset\; H^{\mathrm{even}}(X,\Q)\,,
 \qquad
 \langle v, w \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}
 \;=\; -\!\int_X v^\vee \cdot w \cdot \mathrm{td}(X)
\]
is the Mukai Lagrangian. It is the site of a structural
redundancy: on the arithmetic side, $\Lambda_X$ is the target
of the Borcherds singular theta lift; on the Lie-theoretic
side, $\Lambda_X$ is the weight lattice of the critical
cohomological Hall algebra $\CoHA(Q_X, W_X) \cong
Y^+(\widehat{\frakg}_X)$ and of the Maulik--Okounkov stable
envelope on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(X)$. Both sides encode the same
datum (the generating series of BPS invariants of~$X$): once
as an automorphic form, once as the universal $R$-matrix of a
quantum group.

Five Platonic theorems govern the volume. They are not five
parallel results: they are five evaluations of one universal
correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ together with one universal trace identity.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(P\arabic*)},leftmargin=2em,
 topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.4ex]
 \item \emph{Theorem CY-A$_d$} (functor existence, all~$d$):
  $\Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \to E_n\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$
  exists and is uniquely characterised by the four universal
  properties (U1)--(U4) above, with $n = \infty$ at $d = 1$,
  $n = 2$ at $d = 2$, $n = 1$ at $d \geq 3$. Proved via
  $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing (CY-A$_2$), $\infty$-categorical
  resolution (CY-A$_3$,
  Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}), and
  $\Eone$-stabilisation
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) at $d \ge 4$.
 \item \emph{Theorem CY-B} ($\En$-chiral Koszul duality):
  $E_2$-Koszul on $\cA$ at $d = 2$; $E_1$-Koszul on $\cA$ at
  $d = 3$ via the Verdier spectral functor
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}), inducing
  $\Etwo$-braided equivalence on
  $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))$ at every spectral sequence page.
 \item \emph{Theorem CY-D} (modular CY characteristic):
  at $d = 2$,
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC)) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$,
  proved via Serre duality $S_\cC = [2]$ which kills the
  one-loop correction. At $d \geq 3$, the topological identity
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ fails: Serre's pairing
  $h^{0,q} = h^{0,d-q}$ produces pairwise cancellation giving
  $\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ at every odd $d$, while
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ remains nonzero by additivity
  under products
  ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 1$,
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3 \times E) = 3$). The dimension
  stratification beyond $d = 2$ is the content of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}, which records
  for each CY stratum a distinct mechanism (strict-CY sextic,
  holomorphic-symplectic, Hodge-supertrace on $\cO_X$ when
  the target is on the strict-CY branch); the direct identity
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC_X)) = \sum_q (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X)$
  of the stratification theorem holds in the restricted scope
  where categorical $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and the $\cO_X$
  Hodge supertrace coincide, and diverges from
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ on the additive-product class (see
  the Beauville--Bogomolov tri-stratum in
  Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-d-kappa-stratification}).
 \item \emph{Theorem BKM-Universal}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}): for every
  weakly holomorphic modular form~$F$ of weight $(2-n)/2$
  with integral Fourier coefficients $c(m)$ on a lattice
  $\Lambda$ of signature $(2, n+2)$, the singular theta lift
  $\Psi(F)$ is a meromorphic $\Aut(\Lambda)$-invariant modular
  form on the Grassmannian with product expansion
  $\Psi(F)(Z) = e^{2\pi i \langle \rho, Z \rangle}
  \prod_{\lambda > 0}(1 - e^{2\pi i \langle \lambda, Z \rangle})^{c(-\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle/2)}$,
  weight $\mathrm{wt}(\Psi(F)) = c(0)/2$, and Hecke-intertwining
  functoriality.
 \item \emph{Proposition} ($\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ universality,
  Proposition~\ref{prop:bkm-weight-universal}):
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(X) = c_N(0)/2$ universal for every
  K3-fibered diagonal $\Z/N\Z$-orbifold CY$_3$ with
  $N \in \{1, \ldots, 6\}$ (eight diagonal orbifolds plus the
  STU model). \emph{Undefined for Class~B}:
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ does not apply to quintic, $\C^3$,
  conifold, local $\bP^2$, where the replacement invariant is
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BCOV}} = \chi(X)/24$ together with shadow
  depth (conditional on CY-A). The naive decomposition
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}
  + \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})$ is the $N = 1$
  $K3 \times E$ numerical coincidence; it FAILS for all
  $N \geq 2$
  (Remark~\ref{rem:bkm-decomposition-adversarial}, $62$
  adversarial tests).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Conjecture CY-C} (quantum group realization) remains
conjectural in general; the quantum vertex chiral group
$G(X)$ is unconstructed. Its abelian level,
$C(\mathfrak{g}, q) = D(Y^+(\mathfrak{g}_{K3}))$, is
constructive. For $K3 \times E$, the naive six-way
isomorphism fails; the routes form a pentagon
colimit over five intertwiners (see the pentagon stratification
below in \S\ref{sec:main-results}).

\begin{remark}[The five-object chain in the CY context]
\label{rem:v3-five-object-chain}
Volume~I constructs five objects from a chiral algebra~$\cA$
on a curve~$X$, each produced by a distinct functor
(Theorems~A--D$+$H). Under the CY-to-chiral correspondence
$\Phi \colon \cC \mapsto \cA_\cC$, these become CY invariants:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\cA_\cC$ (the chiral algebra): the factorization
  envelope of the Lie conformal algebra extracted from the
  cyclic $\Ainf$-structure of~$\cC$.
 \item $B(\cA_\cC) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{\cA}_\cC)$ (the bar
  coalgebra): a factorization coalgebra on $\Ran(X)$ with
  deconcatenation coproduct, encoding the CY cyclic bar
  complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$. The bar differential
  carries the holomorphic OPE data; at genus $g \geq 1$ the
  fiberwise differential satisfies
  $d_{\mathrm{fib}}^2 = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_\cC) \cdot
  \omega_g$.
 \item $\cA_\cC^i = H^*(B(\cA_\cC))$ (the dual coalgebra):
  the Koszul cohomology of the bar complex, a cooperad-algebra
  whose coalgebra structure encodes the BPS spectrum of~$\cC$.
 \item $\cA_\cC^! = ((\cA_\cC^i)^\vee)$ (the Koszul dual
  algebra): obtained by applying Verdier duality $D_{\Ran}$
  to the bar coalgebra. For CY$_2$ categories, $\cA_\cC^!$ is
  the chiral algebra of the mirror CY$_2$.
 \item $\Zder(\cA_\cC)$ (the derived chiral centre): the
  chiral Hochschild cochain complex, encoding the bulk sector.
  For a CY category of dimension~$d$, $\Zder(\cA_\cC)$ carries
  the $\mathbb{S}^d$-equivariant structure that governs
  higher-genus amplitudes.
\end{enumerate}
The five objects are connected by four functors (bar $B$,
cohomology $H^*$, linear duality $(-)^\vee$, Hochschild
cochains), none of which should be conflated. Bar--cobar
$\Omega(B(\cA_\cC)) \xrightarrow{\sim} \cA_\cC$ is
\emph{inversion} (recovering the algebra), not Koszul duality;
$\Zder(\cA_\cC)$ is the Hochschild construction, not the
output of bar--cobar. The modular characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA_\cC)$ governs the curvature of
$B(\cA_\cC)$ at genus $g \geq 1$ and equals the CY Euler
characteristic $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ at $d = 2$ (Theorem~CY-D).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The three-volume hierarchy]
\label{rem:vol3-dimensional-reduction}
Volume~I constructs $\En$-chiral algebras at all operadic
levels ($n = 1, 2, 3, \infty$) as algebraic-geometric objects
on curves and their configuration spaces: the five theorems
A--D$+$H are the $E_\infty$-invariants that survive
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariance, while the $\Eone$ data
($R$-matrices, Yangians, chiral quantum groups, derived
centres) lives in the ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$.
Volume~II interprets these algebraic-geometric constructions
physically: the derived chiral centre \emph{is} the bulk of a
real 3d holomorphic-topological gauge theory on
$\C_z \times \R_t$; the $\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ pair
\emph{is} a boundary--bulk system; 3d quantum gravity is the
climax. This volume constructs the concrete $\Eone$-chiral
quantum groups from CY geometry through one symmetric
monoidal correspondence $\Phi_d$ with four universal properties; the
per-$d$ theorems CY-A$_1$, CY-A$_2$, CY-A$_3$, CY-A$_{d \ge 4}$
are evaluations. Calabi--Yau categories of dimension
$d \geq 3$ produce $\Eone$-chiral algebras via $\Phi$, and
the CY quantum groups (CoHA, quantum toroidal algebras) are
the concrete examples of Volume~I's abstract framework. The
Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))$ constructs the
$\Etwo$-braiding: the half-braiding $\sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$
\emph{is} the $R$-matrix
(Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}). The passage
between volumes: Vol~I constructs and proves; Vol~II
interprets physically; Vol~III constructs concrete examples
from higher-dimensional geometry, and the universal trace
identity (Vol~I conductor $K = -c_{\mathrm{ghost}}(\BRST)$
$\;\leftrightarrow\;$ Vol~III $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} =
c_N(0)/2$, mediated by $\Phi$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$,
K3-fibered Class~A only) is the cross-volume seam.
\end{remark}

 \item $K3 \times E$: class~$\mathbf{M}$, shadow

\section{The analytic gap and the \v{C}ech resolution}
\label{sec:analytic-gap}

The CY-to-chiral correspondence $\Phi_3$ at $d = 3$ requires a
chain-level contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault complex of
the CY threefold. For noncompact targets ($\C^3$, the
conifold), the $T^3$-equivariant structure provides the
necessary data. For compact CY$_3$, such as the quintic
threefold $X_5 \subset \bP^4$, the analytic construction of a
contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault resolution would require
solving a $\bar\partial$-equation with estimates: a PDE
problem foreign to the algebraic framework.

The algebraic \v{C}ech resolution closes this gap at the
perturbative level. The standard affine cover
$\{U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}\}_{i=0}^{4}$ of $\bP^4$ restricts to
the quintic, giving five affine open sets whose finite
intersections are all affine. Leray's theorem guarantees
acyclicity of the \v{C}ech complex, and the explicit
contracting homotopy
\[
 s^q\bigl(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}\bigr)
 \;:=\; f_{0\, i_0 \cdots i_q}
 \quad(\text{prepend the distinguished index }0)
\]
satisfies the strong deformation retract conditions
\[
 \delta s + s \delta \;=\; \Id - i \circ p,
 \qquad s^2 \;=\; 0,
 \qquad s \circ i \;=\; 0,
 \qquad p \circ s \;=\; 0.
\]
The SDR data $(i, p, s)$ interfaces directly with the
homotopy transfer theorem (HTT), transferring the
$\Ainf$-structure from \v{C}ech cochains
$\check{C}^\bullet(\cE nd(\cE))$ to its cohomology
$\Ext^\bullet(\cE, \cE)$. The transferred operations
$\mu_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ are given by sums over planar trees
whose internal edges are decorated by the homotopy~$s$; each
application of~$s$ introduces rational functions on the
affine intersections, and the resulting chiral operations
are well-defined as formal power series.

The scope is perturbative: the \v{C}ech homotopy produces an
algebraic $\Ainf$-structure on the Ext algebra, but convergence
of the resulting series to holomorphic functions (the
non-perturbative statement) requires separate input from the
analytic completion programme of Volume~I (MC5, the analytic
sewing section there). The perturbative statement suffices for
the formal CY-to-chiral correspondence and for all combinatorial
applications in this volume. The full construction, including
the proof that the SDR conditions hold and the scope
qualification, is Theorem~\ref{thm:cech-contracting-homotopy}
in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.

For the quintic $X_5 = \{x_0^5 + \cdots + x_4^5 = 0\} \subset
\bP^4$, the computation proceeds as follows. The Hodge
diamond gives
\[
 h^{1,1} \;=\; 1,
 \qquad h^{2,1} \;=\; 101,
 \qquad \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5) \;=\; 2(h^{1,1} - h^{2,1})
 \;=\; -200,
\]
so the MacMahon-normalized topological quantity is
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 = -100$. The \v{C}ech cover
$\{U_i\}_{i=0}^{4}$ has
$\binom{5}{2} = 10$ double intersections,
$\binom{5}{3} = 10$ triple intersections,
$\binom{5}{4} = 5$ quadruple intersections, and one quintuple
intersection
$U_{01234} = X_5 \cap \{x_i \neq 0 \text{ for all } i\}$. Each
$U_{i_0 \cdots i_q}$ is affine (intersection of an affine
variety with $X_5$), so the \v{C}ech complex computes sheaf
cohomology by Leray. The contracting homotopy $s$ prepends the
distinguished index~$0$: the operation
$s^q(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) = f_{0\, i_0 \cdots i_q}$ introduces
rational functions with poles along $\{x_0 = 0\} \cap X_5$.
The transferred $\Ainf$-operations are finite sums: at
degree~$k$, the sum is over planar trees with $k$ leaves, and
the number of terms is the Catalan number $C_{k-1}$. At
$k = 2$, there is a single binary tree; at $k = 3$, two trees;
the operations stabilize to the formal CY-to-chiral data.

The mirror quintic $X_5^\vee$ has $h^{1,1} = 101$, $h^{2,1} = 1$,
and $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5^\vee)/2 = +100$; the sign flip
\[
 \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 + \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5^\vee)/2
 \;=\; 0
\]
is the CY analogue of the Koszul complementarity
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = K$ at $K = 0$,
consistent with the vanishing Koszul conductor for KM/free
families in Volume~I.

Three independent consistency checks confirm the resulting
quintic normalization: the Gepner model matrix factorization
(via mirror symmetry, $\MF(x_1^5 + \cdots + x_5^5)$), the
large-volume Hodge--Kodaira--Rosenberg limit (HKR degeneration
of $\Ext^\bullet$ to Dolbeault cohomology), and the
Gopakumar--Vafa integrality of the resulting genus-$g$
amplitudes. All three are consistent with the same topological
quantity $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 = -100$, distinct from
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ in the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum used
in this volume.

\section{CY$_3$ combinatorics as generalized root data}
\label{sec:cy3-root-data}

The combinatorics of a Calabi--Yau threefold~$X$ (lattice,
intersection form, BPS spectrum) constitute the
\emph{generalized root datum} of a \emph{quantum vertex chiral
group} attached to~$X$. For toric CY$_3$ (via the CoHA and
Drinfeld double), for CY$_2$ categories (via Theorem~CY-A$_2$),
and for CY$_3$ categories (via Theorem~CY-A$_3$), the chiral
algebra $\cA_\cC$ is constructed. The further identification of
the $\Etwo$-braided representation category
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC))$ with classical quantum group
categories is the content of Conjecture~CY-C.

Given a CY$_3$ $X$, the generalized root datum $\cR(X)$
consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item a lattice $\Lambda(X)$ extracted from $K(X)$ or
  $H^*(X)$ with its Euler form;
 \item real simple roots $\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}$ from the
  geometry (hyperbolic sublattice for $K3 \times E$; toric
  diagram for toric CY$_3$);
 \item imaginary roots
  $\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}
  = \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_0 \sqcup \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_1$
  (even and odd) with multiplicities $\mult(\alpha)$ from
  BPS counting (Donaldson--Thomas invariants for~$X$, or
  equivalently the Fourier coefficients of the appropriate
  automorphic form);
 \item a Weyl group $W(X)$ acting on the positive cone of
  $\Lambda(X)$;
 \item a Weyl vector $\rho \in \Lambda(X) \otimes \Q$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{The $K3 \times E$ tower.} For $X = (S \times E)/(\Z/N\Z)$
with $S$ a K3 surface and $E$ an elliptic curve, the lattice
is
$\Lambda^{3,2} \simeq \Lambda^{1,1} \oplus \Lambda^{1,1}
\oplus [2]$ of signature $(3, 2)$. The hyperbolic sublattice
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ with Gram matrix
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  2 & -2 & -2 \\
  -2 & 2 & -2 \\
  -2 & -2 & 2
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
provides the real roots. The root multiplicities are the
Fourier coefficients $f(n, \ell)$ of the weak Jacobi form
$\phi_{0,1}$, the K3 elliptic genus. The resulting generalized
Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra
$\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ has the Igusa cusp form $\Delta_5$ as
its denominator identity. The single-copy chiral modular
characteristic satisfies
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3 \times E) \;=\; 3 \;=\; \dim_\C
 \qquad (\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 2 + 1)\,,
\]
verified by six independent paths; the BKM automorphic weight
is the distinct quantity $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ (the
weight of $\Delta_5$, Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-siegel-gap}).
The factor~$2$ in $Z_{K3} = 2\,\phi_{0,1}$ is the moonshine
multiplier
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3) = \chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$ (a manifold
invariant, independent of algebraization). The shadow
obstruction tower does not produce $\Delta_5$ directly (four
structural obstructions: categorical,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-mismatch, second quantization, Schottky;
Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-siegel-gap}).

\emph{Toric CY$_3$ and critical CoHAs.} For a toric CY$_3$
determined by a fan $\Sigma$, the toric diagram determines a
quiver $Q_X$. The critical cohomological Hall algebra
$\CoHA(Q_X)$ is the positive half of an affine super Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q_X})$. For $\C^3$: the Jordan
quiver gives
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$. The toric fan is
the Dynkin data.

\section{Automorphic correction as shadow obstruction tower}

from the real root Gram matrix alone) to the full generalized

multiplicities) is the \emph{automorphic correction}. In the
language of Volume~I, this passage is the \emph{shadow
obstruction tower}:

\section{The $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra and Hopf axioms}
\label{sec:e1-bialgebra-intro}

Volume~I introduces $\Eone$-chiral quantum groups as abstract
algebraic-geometric objects. This volume constructs them
concretely from CY geometry. The axioms, formalized in
\S\ref{sec:e1-chiral-bialgebras}, are recorded here for
reference; they constitute the algebraic framework into which
all CY quantum groups of this volume are placed.

\begin{definition}[$\Eone$-chiral Hopf algebra; preview]
\label{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra-intro}
An \emph{$\Eone$-chiral Hopf algebra} on a smooth curve~$C$
is a tuple
$(\cA, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ satisfying five
axioms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)},topsep=0.3ex,
 itemsep=0.3ex]
\item \emph{$\Eone$-chiral algebra.}
 $(\cA, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an augmented $\Eone$-chiral
 algebra on $C$: the product $\mu$ is the ordered OPE, and
 the operations live on configuration spaces
 $\Conf_k^{\mathrm{ord}}(C)$ of ordered points.
\item \emph{Spectral coproduct.} For each $z \in \Ran(C)$,
 $\Delta_z \colon \cA \to \cA \otimes_{\Eone, z} \cA$ is an
 $\Eone$-chiral coalgebra structure. At $z = 0$, the coproduct
 $\Delta_0$ coincides with the deconcatenation coproduct on
 $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$; the $z$-deformation encodes the
 spectral data.
\item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility.} The coproduct $\Delta_z$
 is a morphism of $\Eone$-chiral algebras: the diagram
 expressing ``$\Delta_z$ is an algebra map'' commutes, using
 the interchange law for the $\Eone$-monoidal structure.
\item \emph{Spectral coassociativity.} For $z, w \in \Ran(C)$
 in general position:
 \[
  (\Delta_w \otimes \id) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  \;=\; (\id \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
 \]
 At $z = w = 0$ this reduces to ordinary coassociativity.
\item \emph{Hopf axiom.} The antipode $S$ satisfies
 \[
  \mu_{\Eone} \circ (S \otimes \id) \circ \Delta_0
  \;=\; \eta \circ \varepsilon
  \;=\; \mu_{\Eone} \circ (\id \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0.
 \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$ preserves the
$R$-matrix; the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(\cA)$ of Volume~I
kills the Hopf structure via averaging
$\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The
$\Eone$-chiral bialgebra (not the $E_\infty$-vertex bialgebra
of Li) is the correct Hopf framework: the coproduct $\Delta_z$
lives on the $\Eone$ (ordered) side, and the
$E_\infty$-averaging map destroys the Hopf data. The
associated $R$-matrix and Yang--Baxter equation are recovered
from the ordered Hopf data rather than postulated as separate
axioms. Two concrete instances are verified: the Heisenberg
algebra $H_k$ (where $\Delta_z$ is the constant coproduct and
$R(z) = k/z$) and $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at generic $\Psi$ (where
the universal Miura coefficient $(\Psi - 1)/\Psi$ governs the
leading cross-term at every spin $s \geq 2$). The full
development is in Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}.

\emph{Fukaya categories} (Chapter~\ref{ch:fukaya}). The
symplectic input. For an elliptic curve $E_\tau$, the Fukaya
category $\Fuk(E_\tau)$ is CY of dimension~$1$ and $\Phi$
produces the Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_k$ at level
$k = \mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$, an $E_\infty$-chiral algebra
(commutative vertex algebra). For a K3 surface~$S$, $\Fuk(S)$
is CY of dimension~$2$ and $\Phi$ produces an $\Etwo$-chiral
algebra with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_S) = 2$. For
compact CY threefolds, the Fukaya-side functor is available
via Theorem~CY-A$_3$; the open-string sector ($\Fuk(X)$ with
Lagrangian boundary conditions) connects to the Volume~II
Swiss-cheese structure. Wrapped Fukaya categories
$\mathcal{W}(X)$ of Liouville manifolds provide the
non-compact analogue: for cotangent bundles $T^*M$, Abouzaid's
equivalence
$\mathcal{W}(T^*M) \simeq \mathrm{Mod}(C_*(\Omega M))$
reduces the CY-to-chiral correspondence to the based loop space.

\emph{Derived categories of coherent sheaves.} The algebraic
input. For a smooth projective CY manifold $X$, the bounded
derived category $D^b(\Coh(X))$ is CY of dimension~$d$ with
the CY trace induced by Serre duality. Homological mirror
symmetry identifies $\Fuk(X) \simeq D^b(\Coh(X^\vee))$; under
$\Phi$, this becomes an equivalence
$\cA_{\Fuk(X)} \simeq \cA_{D^b(\Coh(X^\vee))}$ of quantum
chiral algebras, providing a consistency check between the
symplectic and algebraic sides. When $D^b(\Coh(X))$ admits a
full exceptional collection, the CY-to-chiral correspondence reduces
to a quiver-with-potential computation: the critical CoHA of
the quiver (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}). Bridgeland stability
conditions parametrize the space of t-structures on
$D^b(\Coh(X))$; wall-crossing in the stability manifold
corresponds to mutations of the bar complex, and the global
chiral algebra is assembled as a homotopy colimit over
stability chambers (Programme~A).

\emph{Matrix factorizations.} The Landau--Ginzburg input. A
polynomial $W \colon \C^n \to \C$ gives a CY category
$\MF(W)$ of dimension $n - 2$. For ADE singularities
$W = x^N + y^2 + z^2 + w^2$ in four variables, $\MF(W)$ is
CY of dimension~$2$ and $\Phi$ (Theorem~CY-A$_2$) produces
chiral algebras related to $\cW_N$-algebras. The LG/CY
correspondence $\MF(W) \simeq D^b(\Coh(X_W))$ provides a
further consistency check against the derived-category side.
For non-ADE singularities (unimodal, bimodal), the resulting
chiral algebras are expected to be new objects not realized
by the standard Lie-theoretic landscape of Volume~I.

(Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-group-reps}). The
representation-theoretic target. The braided monoidal

realize: Conjecture~CY-C predicts that
$\Rep_q(\frakg) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\cA_\cC)$ for a CY category
$\cC(\frakg, q)$. The Kazhdan--Lusztig equivalence
$\Rep_q(\frakg) \simeq \mathrm{KL}_k(\frakg)$ at roots of
unity is the prototype: the Volume~I bar complex
$B(V_k(\frakg))$ encodes the quantum group $R$-matrix in its
degree-$(1, 1)$ component, and the DK bridge (Volume~II, MC3
on the evaluation-generated core for all simple types)
recovers the quantum group categorical structure used here.
The affine Yangian realization
(Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}) provides the $\Eone$-sector;
the Drinfeld centre (Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center})
provides the $E_2$-enhancement.

elliptic algebras chapter (Chapter~\ref{chap:toroidal-elliptic})

the K3 analogue of the DDCA, replacing $\bC[u, v]$ with
$H^*(S, \bC)$. The toric CY$_3$ CoHA chapter
(Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}) develops the affine Yangian and
$\cW_{1+\infty}$ side: the Jordan quiver gives
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$, the toric fan is
the Dynkin data, and the shuffle algebra presentation
connects to the KZ equations of Volume~I.

\section{Guide for the reader}
\label{sec:guide}

The volume has seven parts. The logical dependencies are:
\[
 \text{I} \longrightarrow \text{II} \longrightarrow \text{III}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
  \text{IV (K3 Yangian)} \\
  \text{V (CY Landscape)}
 \end{cases}
 \longrightarrow \text{VI} \longrightarrow \text{VII}
\]
Parts~\ref{part:k3-yangian} and~\ref{part:examples} are
independent of each other: either may be read first.
Part~\ref{part:connections} draws on both.
Part~\ref{part:frontier} is largely self-contained but
references all prior parts. We recommend three reading paths,
depending on the reader's background and goals.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~1: The CY-to-chiral correspondence}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:bridge};
approximately 8 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
This is the shortest path to the main theorems.
Part~\ref{part:cy-categories} establishes the categorical
foundations: CY categories (Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories}),
cyclic $\Ainf$-structures (Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}), and
the Hochschild calculus (Chapter~\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}).
Part~\ref{part:bridge} constructs the correspondence~$\Phi_d$ and
proves Theorems~CY-A$_2$ and CY-A$_3$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}), recounts the
$[m_3, B^{(2)}]$ saga that led to the resolution
(Chapter~\ref{ch:m3-b2-saga}), and develops the holomorphic
Chern--Simons route as an independent construction
(Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-chiral-algebras}).
Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} includes the proof of
Theorem~CY-B$_3$ (the $\Etwo$-chiral Koszul dual identified
via the Verdier spectral functor).

A reader who wants only the construction of $\Phi$ and its
proof can stop after Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}; the
remaining chapters of Part~\ref{part:bridge} provide context
and alternative routes. Prerequisites: familiarity with dg
categories and basic homotopical algebra at the level
of~\cite{LurieHA}; the bar--cobar formalism of Volume~I is
used freely but the essential definitions are recalled in
Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~2: The K3 Yangian}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}, \ref{part:bridge}, and
\ref{part:k3-yangian}; approximately 15 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
This path follows Path~1 through the functor construction,
then jumps directly to Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} for the
central worked example. The K3 surface is the simplest
compact CY manifold on which all structures are explicit: the
Mukai lattice $\Lambda^{4,20}$ provides the root datum, the
correspondence $\Phi_2$ produces the lattice Heisenberg VOA
$H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$, and the K3 double current algebra
$\frakg_{K3}$ quantizes to the abelian Yangian
$Y(\frakg_{K3})$ with $24$ generators and degree-$(24, 24)$
structure function.

For the threefold $K3 \times E$, six independent routes (BKM,
lattice VOA, Kummer, CY-to-chiral, sigma model, BLLPR)
converge on the quantum vertex chiral group whose denominator
identity is the Igusa cusp form~$\Phi_{10}$. The mock modular
structure of the K3 elliptic genus is proved at $d = 2$
(shadow $24\,\eta^3$, Zwegers completion, Borcherds lift), and
the Stokes--wall-crossing identification connects Borel
singularities to walls of marginal stability. At
root-of-unity level $N = 3$, the K3 quantum group produces
the first non-abelian MTC from 6d geometry ($3200$ modules,
Ising fusion). Readers from vertex algebras, BPS state
counting, or Borcherds--Kac--Moody algebras will find this
path self-contained once the correspondence~$\Phi_d$ is in hand.
Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} may be consulted as needed for
the $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axioms
(Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}) and the Drinfeld centre
construction (Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center}), but the K3
material does not depend on the full operadic development.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~3: The full programme}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:frontier};
all 25 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
The seven parts are written to be read in sequence.
Part~\ref{part:cy-categories} lays the categorical and
homological foundations: CY categories, cyclic
$\Ainf$-structures, and the Hochschild calculus that extracts
the Lie conformal algebra~$\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ from the
categorical data. Part~\ref{part:bridge} constructs and proves
the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme~$\{\Phi_d\}$ at all~$d$, including the
$\infty$-categorical resolution of the
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction and the holomorphic
Chern--Simons route. Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} develops
the $\En$-hierarchy: $\Eone$-chiral bialgebras with their
Hopf axioms, $\Etwo$-chiral algebras with braided monoidal
representation categories, the $\Eone$-stabilization theorem,
classical quantum group theory (Drinfeld--Jimbo, RTT,
Kazhdan--Lusztig), and the Drinfeld centre passage from
$\Eone$ to $\Etwo$.

Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} works out the K3 Yangian as the
principal example: derived categories, the K3 chiral algebra,
the abelian Yangian, the BKM superalgebra, quantum toroidal
algebras, and the full $K3 \times E$ CY$_3$ programme.
Part~\ref{part:examples} surveys the broader CY landscape:
toric CY$_3$ CoHAs, matrix factorizations, Fukaya categories,
quantum group representations, and the modular trace.
Part~\ref{part:connections} develops the seven faces of
$r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$: the modular Koszul bridge
($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi^{\CY}$ at $d = 2$), the
bar--cobar bridge to Volume~I (shadow--Feynman dictionary,
$\Ainf$-coproduct tower), the CY holographic datum, the
Stokes--wall-crossing identification, and the BCOV--shadow
tower dictionary. Part~\ref{part:frontier} addresses the open
frontiers: the geometric Langlands programme, the nonabelian
Yangian, root-of-unity phenomena (including the $N = 3$
non-abelian MTC), the Zamolodchikov tetrahedron correction
(exact rational~$T$ matrix), the chiral volume conjecture,
the chiral Khovanov complex, and the Conway--Leech--Niemeier
rigidity connection. This path is recommended for readers
who intend to work with the full CY-to-chiral-to-modular
pipeline across all three volumes.

\section{Conventions and notation}
\label{sec:conventions-intro}

This volume inherits the conventions of Volumes~I and~II; the
points where the CY setting introduces additional notation or
where conventions differ across the literature are recorded
here. A comprehensive conventions appendix is
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}.

\emph{Grading.} All grading is cohomological: differentials
have degree $+1$. The bar construction uses desuspension
$s^{-1}$, with $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$ (matching Volume~I; see
signs\_and\_shifts.tex therein). The CY dimension~$d$ enters
as a cohomological shift: the CY trace
$\Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$ is a map of
degree~$-d$.

\emph{$E_n$ notation.} $\En$ denotes the little $n$-disks
operad (Boardman--Vogt); $E_\infty$ denotes the commutative
operad. Dunn additivity: $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$. An
$E_2$-chiral algebra (Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra})
factorizes along two holomorphic directions; its
representation category is braided monoidal
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}). $\FM_k(\C)$ denotes the
Fulton--MacPherson compactification of $\Conf_k(\C)$; this is
a blowup along diagonals, not the complement of the diagonal.

\emph{Divided powers and the $\lambda$-bracket.} The
translation between OPE modes $a_{(n)} b$ and the
$\lambda$-bracket uses the divided-power convention
$\lambda^{(n)} = \lambda^n / n!$, so
$\{a_\lambda b\} = \sum_n \lambda^{(n)} a_{(n)} b$. The
$\lambda$-bracket coefficient at order~$n$ is
$a_{(n)} b / n!$, not $a_{(n)} b$ (Volume~I). This convention
is consistent with Volumes~I and~II; when consulting
references that use the Borel transform
$B(K)(\lambda) = \sum \lambda^{(n)} c_n$, the $1/n!$ is
already absorbed into $\lambda^{(n)}$.

\emph{CY categories.} $\CY_d$-$\Cat$ denotes the
$\infty$-category of smooth $d$-dimensional CY categories.
Tensor products of dg categories are derived unless stated
otherwise. ``Equivalence'' means quasi-equivalence of dg
categories (equivalence of associated $\infty$-categories).
The Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg isomorphism
$\HH_\bullet(D^b(\Coh(X))) \simeq H^*(X, \Omega^\bullet_X)$
is used freely for smooth projective varieties.

\emph{The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum.} A single CY manifold
admits multiple modular characteristics, each measuring a
different face of the geometry. This volume uses subscripted
$\kappa_\bullet$ throughout: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (the
chiral algebra modular characteristic of Volume~I, defined as
the degree-$2$ projection of $\Theta_\cA$),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ (the holomorphic Euler characteristic
$\chi(\cO_X)$, a categorical invariant),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ (the half-weight of the Borcherds
automorphic form, an automorphic invariant), and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ (the lattice rank of the fiber,
relevant for fibered CY manifolds). Unsubscripted use is
forbidden in this volume; every occurrence carries an
explicit qualifier.

\emph{Operadic terminology.} Following Volume~I, the word
``degree'' is used universally for: bar complex grading,
operadic input count, tree vertex valence, Stasheff identity
level, Swiss-cheese mixed sector parameters, and all
index-counting contexts. The word ``arity'' does not appear.

\emph{Bar complex conventions.} The bar complex is
$B(\cA) = T^c(s^{-1} \bar{\cA})$ with
$\bar{\cA} = \ker(\varepsilon)$ the augmentation ideal. The
tensor coalgebra $T^c$ carries the deconcatenation coproduct.
The ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$ is the primitive
object; the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(\cA)$ is the
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant quotient. The desuspension satisfies
$|s^{-1} v| = |v| - 1$.

\emph{Cross-volume references.} Results from Volume~I are
cited as ``Vol~I, Theorem~X'' or by their Volume~I label
(e.g., thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic). Results from Volume~II are
cited analogously. When a formula appears in multiple
volumes, the conventions may differ: Volume~I uses OPE modes,
Volume~II uses $\lambda$-brackets with divided powers, and
this volume uses both depending on context. Every cross-volume
formula citation in this work has been independently verified
against the source.
