\section{The positive-geometry grammar: the object of study}
\label{wn:sec:platonic-intro-object}

A single Calabi--Yau input does not carry a single numerical shadow.
At $X = K3\times E$ the four invariants
\[
 \kcat(X)=0,\qquad
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(X)=3,\qquad
 \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
 \kfib(K3)=24
\]
are all correct and mutually incommensurable.  The first is the
K\"unneth-multiplicative holomorphic Euler characteristic of the total
space.  The second is the Heisenberg--Mukai chiral reading of the
$K3$ and elliptic factors.  The third is the Borcherds weight
$c_1(0)/2$ of the Gritsenko denominator.  The fourth is the rank of
the Mukai lattice of the fibre.  The equality to be proved is never
``four values of one $\kappa_\bullet$-invariant'' and never ``six
applications of one functor''.  The object of study is the mechanism that explains why
these different measurements are read from one Calabi--Yau datum.

Let $\cA$ be a Calabi--Yau category of complex dimension $d$ in the
Kontsevich--Soibelman sense: a pretriangulated dg category equipped
with a non-degenerate cyclic pairing of degree $-d$, compatible with
the $\Ainf$-structure up to coherent homotopy.  For a smooth
projective Calabi--Yau variety $X$, the model example is
$\Perf(X)$ with its Serre-duality trace.  For a Landau--Ginzburg
chart $(Q,W)$, the model is the matrix-factorisation or critical-CoHA
category attached to $W$.  Van~den Bergh non-commutative crepant
resolutions and Morita tilting charts bridge these models where such
charts exist; no global NCCR or finite quiver chart is assumed for an
arbitrary compact Calabi--Yau threefold.

Write $\Omega_X\in H^{d,0}(X)$ for the holomorphic volume form.  It
rigidifies the cyclic trace into the holomorphic BV datum used in the
native factorisation algebra.

\subsection{The functor}
\label{wn:sec:platonic-intro-functor}

The Calabi--Yau-to-chiral functor
\[
\Phi_d: \CYcat_d \longrightarrow \ChirAlg
\]
is a two-stage construction.  Stage~$1$ produces a native
$E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra on the Calabi--Yau target.
Stage~$2$ evaluates that native object on a geometric datum
$(\Sigma_{d-1},C)$, with $\Sigma_{d-1}\subset X$ a closed
$(d-1)$-cycle and $C\subset X$ a reference curve.  The notation
$\Phi_d(\cA)$ abbreviates this composite only after the specialisation
datum has been fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Stage 1: the holomorphic factorisation algebra]
\label{wn:thm:platonic-intro-stage-1}
\ClaimStatusConditional\
On the verified loci there is a canonical assignment
\[
\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_d: \CYcat_d \longrightarrow E_d\text{-}\HolFA(X)
\]
sending a Calabi--Yau category $\cA$ over $X$ to the holomorphic
factorisation algebra of observables on $X$ with gauge data read from
$\cA$.  The assignment is pinned, up to contractible choice, by the
conjunction of Kontsevich--Tamarkin $E_d$-formality for the
degree-$(1-d)$ Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cA)$,
Costello--Gwilliam locality, and the Costello--Li holomorphic BV
twist determined by $\Omega_X$.  It is theorem-grade in the loci
proved in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}: unconditionally at
$d\leq 2$, at $d=3$ on the smooth proper object-level locus by the
three obstruction vanishings of Theorem~\ref{thm:cya3-existence-rigidity},
and on explicit toric/formal charts.  Functoriality on all
Calabi--Yau morphisms and compact non-formal chain models beyond the
verified loci remain part of Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-d-functoriality}
and the compact CY$_3$ strictification programme.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Stage 2: specialisation to a curve]
\label{wn:thm:platonic-intro-stage-2}
\ClaimStatusTheorem\
For any admissible specialisation datum
$(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$, with $\Sigma_{d-1}\subset X$ a closed
$(d{-}1)$-cycle for which the factorisation-homology pushforward is
defined and $C\subset X$ a reference curve, factorisation homology
along $\Sigma_{d-1}$ followed by restriction to $C$ yields a functor
\[
\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}: E_d\text{-}\HolFA(X) \longrightarrow
E_1\text{-}\ChirAlg(C).
\]
The composite $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_d$
depends on the pair $(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$.  Different choices produce
different chiral shadows of the same Stage-$1$ output.  At
$d\geq 3$ these shadows are natively $\Eone$-chiral; the
non-symmetric $\Etwo$ braiding is recovered from the Drinfeld centre
of the $\Eone$ representation category, not from an $\Etwo$ structure
on the shadow itself.
\end{theorem}

The six routes to $G(K3 \times E)$
(Mukai-lattice, Igusa $\Phi_{10}$ via Gritsenko $\Delta_5$, BKM
Borcherds weight, K3 fibre rank, Niemeier lattice, Humbert divisor,
CHL twisted orbifold) are \emph{not} six applications of $\Phi_3$
to one object: they are six different kinds of measurement.
Four of them (Mukai, Hodge, fibre, Igusa-weight) are Stage-1
invariants of $\cF_\cA$ or of the underlying Calabi--Yau datum; the
others are $(\Sigma_2, C)$-specialisations of a single canonical
$\cF_\cA \in E_3\text{-}\HolFA(K3 \times E)$ at different cycle
choices. Reading the six faces through this grammar dissolves
their apparent conflict.

\subsection{The quantum group}
\label{wn:sec:platonic-intro-quantum-group}

Inside the Stage-$1$ object lies the positive effective moduli stack
$\Meff(X)$ carrying the Kontsevich--Soibelman critical CoHA structure
whenever the required critical atlas is available.  Its equivariant
vanishing-cycle cohomology is the positive half:
\[
Y^+(X) \;:=\; H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{eq}}\!\bigl(\Meff(X),\,\phi_W\bigr).
\]
When the PBW integrality and Hopf pairing are constructed, the
Drinfeld double
\[
G(X) \;:=\; D(Y^+(X)) \;=\; Y^+(X) \bowtie Y^0(X) \bowtie Y^-(X)
\]
is the quantum group attached to the datum.  This last sentence is a
theorem for the toric and explicitly constructed Hall loci and a
conjectural envelope for general compact CY$_3$ targets.  The Hopf
pairing comes from Serre duality on the compact locus, supplemented
by Davison--Meinhardt PBW integration; the spectral structure comes
from Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes transported by positive-half
gluing cocycles.

The equivariance label stratifies by geometric type: full toric $T^d$
on $\CC^3$ and toric three-folds, reduced virtual class with residual
$\CC^\times$ on compact non-toric Calabi--Yau threefolds, discrete
orbifold inertia on quotient geometries, period-domain equivariance
on lattice-polarised families. The algebra $Y^+(X)$ is the same
construction read through four local languages.

\subsection{The chiral shadow on a curve}
\label{wn:sec:platonic-intro-chiral-shadow}

Specialising Stage 1 at $(\Sigma_2, C) = (K3, E)$ sends
$\cF_\cA \in E_3\text{-}\HolFA(K3 \times E)$ to an $E_1$-chiral
algebra on the elliptic curve $E$. Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope
transport identifies this chiral algebra with the chiral bialgebra
$\bH_{\Delta_5}$ supported on the Humbert divisor $H_1$ inside the
Siegel paramodular space $\cH_{3,2}$: its character is $\Delta_5^{-2}$,
its root-multiplicity table is the Fourier coefficients of the
Gritsenko weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$, and its Weyl--Kac--Borcherds
denominator identity is the singular theta lift of $\phi_{0,1}$ in
the sense of Borcherds 1998.

The generalised Kac--Moody superalgebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ of
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 is the automorphic correction.  A
programme-level conjecture identifies it with the bracket completion
of the BPS Lie algebra extracted from
$\Meff(K3 \times E)$ by Davison--Meinhardt integrality. The
graded dimensions coincide unconditionally; the bracket-level
identification is the canonical frontier, outlined in
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e}.

\subsection{The eight-form class}
\label{wn:sec:platonic-intro-eight-form}

Gritsenko and Cl\'ery classified the diagonal-divisor paramodular
cusp forms of Hecke type.  This volume uses the resulting eight-form
class as a controlled testing ground for $\kBKM$: one can separate
the CHL slice, where elliptic automorphisms force
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, from the full Borcherds-weight reading on the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery list.  In the normalisation used for the
Borcherds denominator scope, the weight-ordered list is
\[
\Delta_5, \quad \Delta_2^{(2)}, \quad \Delta_1^{(3)}, \quad
\Delta_1^{(4)}, \quad \Delta_{1/2}^{(5)}, \quad \Delta_1^{(6)},
\quad \Delta_{1/4}^{(7)}, \quad \Delta_0^{(8)}.
\]
The universal weight identity $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$ holds
uniformly in its Borcherds-weight scope, with cover selection
$\{\Sp_4,\Mp_4,\widetilde{\Mp}_4\}$ determined by weight
integrality.  The Chaudhuri--Hockney--Lykken subset
$N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ --- precisely those $N$ with
$\varphi(N) \mid 2$ --- realises each $\Phi_N$ as the denominator
of a generalised Kac--Moody superalgebra on the twisted-reduced
Donaldson--Thomas moduli of the Calabi--Yau orbifold
$(K3 \times E)/(\ZZ/N\ZZ)$; the three remaining $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$
fall outside the CHL slice and find their geometric hosts in
the Borcea--Voisin threefold $(S_5 \times E_5)/(\iota_S \times \iota_E)$
at $N = 5$ with metaplectic fourth-root character, an
order-$4$ Cheeger--Simons gerbe on the singular
$(K3 \times E)/\ZZ_7$ at $N = 7$, and the Mongardi--Tari--Wandel
Kummer-$3$ hyperk\"ahler fourfold at $N = 8$.

Each form supplies a four-corner data: a Calabi--Yau geometric host,
a cohomological Hall algebra extracted from its positive effective
moduli, a BPS Lie algebra extracted by Davison--Meinhardt
integrality, and an automorphic correction realising the
generalised Kac--Moody as a Borcherds singular theta lift.  At
$N=1$ the automorphic and character-level corners are theorem-grade,
while the bracket completion remains conditional.  At
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ the primitive-class statements are theorem-grade on
the Bryan--Oberdieck locus and the imprimitive completion is
conditional.  At $N\in\{5,7,8\}$ the host construction itself is
conditional.

\section{The two-stage factorisation \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$}{Phi-d = SpCh-Sigma-d-1, CoPhiFA-d}}
\label{sec:two-stage-factorisation}

The organising principle of this volume is the two-stage factorisation
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \Phi_d
 \;=\;
 \CY_d\text{-}\Cat
 \xrightarrow{\;\PhiFA_d\;}
 \EdHolFA(X)
 \xrightarrow{\;\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\;}
 \EnHolFA(C),
 \qquad
 n_{\mathrm{native}}(d) = \begin{cases}\infty, & d = 1, \\ 2, & d = 2, \\
 1, & d \ge 3\end{cases}\;}
\]
(Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp},
Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation},
Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}). Stage~1, the
\emph{canonical holomorphic factorisation algebra}
$\PhiFA_d \colon \cC \mapsto \PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$,
is pinned up to contractible choice by the Gerstenhaber bracket
of degree $1-d$ on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ through Kontsevich--Tamarkin
$E_d$-formality and Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality on the CY$_d$
variety~$X$. Stage~2, the \emph{chiral specialisation}
\(
 \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}(\cF)
 = \bigl(\intSigma \cF\bigr)\!\mid_C,
\)
pushes a native $E_d$-hFA forward along a $(d{-}1)$-dimensional
closed cycle $\Sigma_{d-1}\subset X$ via factorisation homology
(Lurie, Francis--Gaitsgory) and restricts to a reference
curve~$C\subset X$. The image $\EnHolFA(C)$ jumps in operadic
level between dimensions; the uniform presentation lives on
the native stage. A given CY$_d$ category therefore admits a
\emph{family} of $E_n$-chiral shadows on~$C$, one per choice of
$(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$
	(Remark~\ref{rem:multiple-e1-specialisations}); the six routes
	to $G(K3\times E)$ catalogued below are six distinct
	constructions compared through named intertwiners, not six
	specialisations of one $\PhiFA_3$ and not six applications of
	$\Phi_3$ to one object.

A Calabi--Yau category~$\cC$ of dimension~$d$ carries a
canonical cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: a non-degenerate trace
$\Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$ on its Hochschild
homology, encoding the CY condition as a $d$-dimensional
Frobenius structure at the chain level. The cyclic bar complex
$\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ carries an $\Sd$-action from the
$d$-sphere framing of the trace; its $\Sd$-equivariant
structure governs higher-genus amplitudes. A chiral algebra~$\cA$
on a curve~$C$ carries a bar complex $B(\cA)$, a factorisation
coalgebra on $\Ran(C)$ whose differential encodes holomorphic OPE
residues and whose coproduct encodes topological interval-cutting.
At genus $g \geq 1$, the bar complex acquires curvature
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA) \cdot \omega_g$ from the Hodge bundle,
and the full modular-Koszul structure (Vol~I, genus-$g$-enhanced
bar--cobar over $\overline{M}_{g,n}$) is controlled by the
universal Maurer--Cartan element $\Theta_\cA := D_\cA - d_0$
(Volume~I). Together with the $\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ structure
on the derived centre pair $(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA, \cA),\,
\cA)$ (Volume~II), these data form the complete modular-Koszul
invariant. Both $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ and $B(\cA)$ are
instances of the same homotopical object: a cyclic
$\Ainf$-coalgebra equipped with a compatible trace. The cyclic
$\Ainf$-condition on the CY side and the Maurer--Cartan equation
$D\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ on the chiral side
are two presentations of one structure. $\Phi_d$ transports one
presentation to the other through stage~1 and stage~2.

Four universal properties (U1)--(U4) characterise the combined
construction.

\begin{description}[itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=1.8em,style=nextline]
\item[(U1) Hochschild pullback.]
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC)))
\simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ as factorisation coalgebras
on $\Ran(C)$. The categorical Hochschild data is the
ordered-bar data of the chiral shadow on the reference curve.
\item[(U2) CY-morphism functoriality.]
A wall-crossing in $\cC$ (mutation, Bridgeland traversal,
flop) maps to an $R$-matrix gauge transformation on
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ and descends under $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$.
Wall-crossing $=$ MC gauge equivalence in the modular
convolution algebra of the chiral shadow.
\item[(U3) Drinfeld-centre compatibility.]
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi_d(\cC))) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\Phi_d(\cC)))$ at $d \ge 3$
(Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler,
Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}). The half-braiding
$\sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$ \emph{is} the quantum group $R$-matrix
$R(z)$ with $z = u - v$
(Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center});
the braided $E_2$-datum is carried natively by
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on pairs of $2$-disks in $X$ and descends
to $C$ via $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$
(Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre}).
\item[(U4) Standard-input recovery.]
$\Phi_1(\Coh(E_\tau)) = $ lattice VOA at $d = 1$
(Heisenberg at level $\mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$) via the
$\SpCh_{\mathrm{pt}, E_\tau}$ specialisation;
$\Phi_2(D^b(K3)) = H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ at $d = 2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-k3-explicit}) via
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}$ on a K3 curve class;
$\Phi_3(D^b(\Coh(\C^3)))|_{\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}} = Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1) = \CoHA(\C^3)$ at $d = 3$
(positive half, Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013 direct identification;
Drinfeld-double envelope is
$\cW_{1+\infty}$) via $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}$ with
$\Sigma_2 \subset \C^3$ a coordinate plane; $\CoHA(\C^3)$ is not a
$\Phi$-input (neither CY-category nor chiral-algebra), so
``$\Phi_3(\CoHA(\C^3))$'' would be a type error.
\end{description}

The four-step computational recipe of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}
--- cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal algebra $\to$ factorisation
envelope $\to$ $\Sd$-enhanced quantisation --- is the explicit
realisation of the composite $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$:
the Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ is the one-dimensional
shadow of $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ under pushforward along a codimension-one
cycle, and the factorisation envelope $\Fact_C(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$
restores the chiral structure on~$C$. The per-$d$ theorems
CY-A$_1$, CY-A$_2$, CY-A$_3$, CY-A$_{d \ge 4}$ are parallel
evaluations of Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}
sharing the (U1)--(U4) skeleton; they differ only in the stage-1
obstruction they resolve and in the stage-2 geometric datum
$(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$ they contract.

At $d = 2$, $\PhiFA_2(\cC)$ is a canonical $E_2$-hFA on a CY$_2$
surface $X$; the $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing of $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$
(Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos) supplies stage~1 unconditionally, and
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}$ along a curve class $\Sigma_1\subset X$
restricted to a reference curve~$C$ yields an $\Etwo$-chiral
algebra $\cA_\cC$ on~$C$ whose bar complex $B(\cA_\cC)$ encodes
the CY cyclic homology (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). At $d = 3$, the
chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction at stage~1 is
resolved on the connective, unit-connected framed locus by an
$\infty$-categorical argument
($\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ and the space of $E_3$-liftings is
contractible; Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}), so
$\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ is available on that locus as an $E_3$-hFA on the
CY$_3$ variety~$X$. The stage-2
specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}$ along a real surface
$\Sigma_2\subset X$ produces an $\Eone$-chiral shadow on~$C$
(Theorem~CY-A$_3$); different $\Sigma_2$-data produce different
shadows of one native $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$. Direct restriction
$E_3 \to E_2$ of the shadow on~$C$ gives only symmetric braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$); the non-symmetric
$R$-matrix is read from the Drinfeld centre
\[
 \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC)\bigr)
 \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\Etwo}\bigl(\Zder(\cA_\cC)\bigr),
 \qquad
 R(z) \;=\; \sigma_{V_u}(V_v),\ \ z = u - v,
\]
(Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler, Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre};
Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}). The braided
$E_2$-datum is carried natively by $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ on pairs of
$2$-disks in~$X$ and descends to $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC))$
under $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}$; the chiral shadow on~$C$ is itself
$\Eone$-ordered.

The K3 data admit two ambient-precise presentations of the
composite. The CY$_2$ specialisation,
\[
 \Phi_2\bigl(D^b\Coh(K3)\bigr)
 \;=\; H_{\mathrm{Muk}},
 \qquad
 \HH^\bullet\bigl(D^b\Coh(K3)\bigr)
 \;=\; \bigoplus_{p+q=\bullet} H^p\bigl(K3, \Lambda^q T_{K3}\bigr),
\]
sends $D^b\Coh(K3)$ through its canonical $\PhiFA_2$ to an
$E_2$-hFA on K3, whose $\SpCh_{\Sigma_1^{\mathrm{Nakajima}}, C}$
specialisation on a Nakajima curve class lands in the Mukai
Heisenberg $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$; the K3 abelian Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$ is then natively $\Eone$-chiral with $24$
Heisenberg generators, Mukai-signature Serre relations, and
degree-$(24, 24)$ structure function
(Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-yangian}), and the orthogonal Yangian
$Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4, 20))$ remains conjectural as its
BKM-to-Yangian lift: the Mukai form is symmetric indefinite of
signature $(4, 20)$, symmetric on both even and odd parts, so the
structure-preserving Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(4, 20)$; Kac's
$\osp(4 \mid 20)$ is not the envelope because $\osp$ requires a
symplectic form on the odd part.
The CY$_3$
specialisation on the K3 fibre,
\[
 \SpCh_{K3, E}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(D^b\Coh(K3\times E))\bigr)
 \;\simeq\;
 U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{heis}_{\mathrm{Muk}})
 \otimes
 U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{BPS}}_{K3}),
\]
reads the same input category on its CY$_3$ enhancement and
produces the Borcherds--$\Delta_5$ chiral bialgebra
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5} =
\mathcal{D}_\hbar(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{Hall}}_\hbar(\mathrm{CoHA}_{K3\times E}))$,
attached to the Manin pair
$(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5},\, \mathfrak{n}_+^{\mathrm{imag}}
 \oplus \mathfrak{h}^{\mathrm{imag, rk\,23}})$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:g-delta5-is-sp-k3};
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}). The Koszul shift
on the K3 branch is $[2]$, not $[3]$; any $[3]$ in the
neighbourhood comes from auxiliary CY$_3$ spaces
($K3\times\C$, $K3\times\C^3$) at stage~1 rather than from the
input category. The K3 abelian Yangian and the K3 chiral
Hall--Drinfeld double are parallel specialisations of the
canonical $\PhiFA_2$- and $\PhiFA_3$-data, meeting on the Mukai
rank-$24$ datum; they are two faces of one CY-to-chiral picture,
not two names for one quantum group.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\En$}{E-n} hierarchy: native \texorpdfstring{$E_d$}{E-d} on \texorpdfstring{$X$}{X}, shadow \texorpdfstring{$E_{n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)}$}{E-n-native(d)} on \texorpdfstring{$C$}{C}}
\label{sec:e1-e2-hierarchy}

The CY dimension~$d$ fixes the operadic level of the
\emph{native} stage-$1$ object $\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$
at $E_d$: the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $1 - d$ on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ plus Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality on~$X$
makes $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ an $E_d$-hFA on the full CY$_d$ variety.
The operadic level of the \emph{shadow}
$\Phi_d(\cC) = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ on the
reference curve~$C$ is governed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}:
$n_{\mathrm{native}}(d) = \infty, 2, 1$ at $d = 1, 2, d \geq 3$
respectively.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0.3ex,itemsep=0.4ex]
 \item $d = 1$: stage-1 is $E_1$ on $X = E_\tau$; stage-2 yields
  an $E_\infty$-commutative shadow on~$C$. The degree-$0$
  bracket is Poisson, the Lie conformal algebra abelian, the
  factorisation envelope commutative; representation categories
  are symmetric monoidal. Example: $\Fuk(E_\tau)$ produces the
  Heisenberg VOA at level $\mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$.
 \item $d = 2$: stage-1 is $E_2$ on the CY$_2$ surface~$X$;
  stage-2 yields an $E_2$-braided shadow on~$C$. The degree-$(-1)$
  bracket is a Lie bracket (the $\lambda$-bracket); the
  $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing of $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ supplies $E_2$
  directly via Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos. Representation
  categories are braided monoidal: the natural habitat of quantum
  groups at this dimension.
 \item $d = 3$: stage-1 is $E_3$ on the CY$_3$ variety~$X$;
  stage-2 yields an $\Eone$-ordered shadow on~$C$ \emph{natively}.
  The degree-$(-2)$ bracket is a shifted Lie bracket; the CoHA
  multiplication is ordered (short exact sequences have a
  preferred direction), giving an associative but \emph{not}
  braided product. The braided $\Etwo$-datum is carried natively
  by $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ on pairs of $2$-disks in~$X$; on the shadow
  side it lives on the Drinfeld centre
  $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA_\cC))$, not on $\cA_\cC$ itself
  (Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre}).
 \item $d \geq 4$: stage-1 is $E_d$ on~$X$; stage-2 yields an
  $\Eone$-stabilised shadow. The $\pi_d(BU)$ obstruction
  governs shifted structure on the stage-1 side but the shadow
  level remains $\Eone$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}).
\end{itemize}

The passage $\Eone \to \Etwo$ on the shadow side is always
through the Drinfeld centre:
\[
 \cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))
 \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\Etwo}\!\bigl(\Zder(\cA)\bigr).
\]
By Dunn additivity, $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$, so an
$\Etwo$-algebra is an $\Eone$-algebra in $\Eone$-algebras. The
CY quantum groups of this volume are concrete $\Eone$-chiral
quantum groups (Volume~I) whose Drinfeld centres carry
$\Etwo$-braiding. Direct restriction $E_3 \to E_2$ of the
shadow on~$C$ gives only symmetric braiding
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \geq 3$); the Drinfeld-centre
mechanism is the sole source of non-trivial braiding reachable
from the chiral shadow alone.

The CY condition enters through Kontsevich's identification: a
$d$-dimensional CY structure on $\cC$ determines an
$\mathbb{S}^d$-framing of the Hochschild complex. For $d = 2$
(CY surfaces), this framing supplies $E_2$ at stage-1
unconditionally. For $d = 3$, the $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing
resolves the $E_3$-lifting obstruction at stage-1
(Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}); the shadow
chiral algebra is then $\Eone$, with $E_2$-braiding on
representation categories recovered through the Drinfeld centre.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\Eone$}{E1} stabilization theorem}
\label{subsec:e1-stabilization-intro}

For $d \geq 3$, the CY chiral output stabilizes at $\Eone$, not
$E_d$. The reason is the degree of the Gerstenhaber bracket: on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$, the bracket has degree $1 - d$, which for
$d \geq 3$ is $\leq -2$, too shifted to produce braiding.
Equivalently,
\[
 \pi_1\bigl(\Conf_2^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R^d)\bigr)
 \;=\; \pi_1(S^{d-1}) \;=\; 0
 \qquad (d \geq 3)\,.
\]
For toric CY$_d$ ($\C^d$ with $\GL(d)$-action), the
$\GL(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets vanish
identically (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}): the
classical Lie conformal algebra is abelian and all
noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.
For non-toric CY$_3$, the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket is
generically nonzero, but its shifted degree still prevents
braiding. The CoHA construction gives an ordered (associative)
product: the extension correspondence
$0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ has a preferred direction
(sub before quotient), which is $E_1$, not $E_2$. The braided
monoidal structure is constructed via the Drinfeld centre of
the $E_1$-monoidal representation category
(Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center},
Construction~\ref{constr:rmatrix-from-center}): the half-braiding
$\sigma_{V_u}(V_v)$ on evaluation modules is the quantum group
$R$-matrix.

The additional shifted structure beyond the base $\Eone$ level
is controlled by the framing obstruction $\pi_d(BU)$. Two
independent computations of this obstruction (the unitary path
via Bott periodicity for $BU$, period~$2$, and the
symplectic/orthogonal path via Bott periodicity for $\Sp$ and $O$,
period~$8$) produce the following landscape:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 $d$ & Native $\En$ & $\pi_d(BU)$
  & $\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d)$ & Shifted structure \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $E_\infty$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (commutative) \\
 $2$ & $E_2$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & braiding parameter ($c_1$) \\
 $3$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (topological obstruction $0$) \\
 $4$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted symplectic ($p_1$) \\
 $5$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$-shifted ($\Sp$ refinement) \\
 $6$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted ($c_3$) \\
 $7$ & $E_1$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (trivial, mirrors $d = 3$) \\
 $8$ & $E_1$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted (Bott-periodic) \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern repeats with period~$8$ by Bott periodicity of
$\Sp$ and $O$. The effective obstruction is trivial precisely
at $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$. All even $d \geq 2$ have
$\Z$-valued obstruction from $\pi_d(BU) = \Z$. For CY$_4$, the
obstruction is the first Pontryagin class
$p_1 \in \pi_4(BU) = \Z$: it provides a level (analogous to the
level of a Kac--Moody algebra) that governs the
$(d{-}2)$-shifted symplectic structure on the moduli of
objects (PTVV shift law: Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi
\cite{PTVV2013}). At $d = 3$, the
vanishing of this topological obstruction does not by itself
construct the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing required by
CY-A$_3$. For $K3 \times K3$ ($d = 4$), the $E_4$-framing of
$\Phi_4(\cC_{K3 \times K3})$ splits as the K\"unneth product
of two $E_2$-framings, one per $K3$ factor, compatible with
the Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos $\mathbb{S}^2$-action on each
factor's Hochschild homology; by Dunn additivity
$E_2 \otimes E_2 = E_4$, so the product carrier recovers $E_2$
per factor without invoking any (false) sphere identification
$\mathbb{S}^4 \cong \mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$.

(Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) is in
Chapter~\ref{ch:en-factorization}.

 \bigl(

\section{The analytic gap and the \v{C}ech resolution}
\label{sec:analytic-gap}

The native stage $\PhiFA_3$ on a CY$_3$ variety~$X$ requires
a chain-level contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault complex
of~$X$. For noncompact targets ($\C^3$, the conifold), the
$T^3$-equivariant structure provides the necessary data. For
compact CY$_3$, such as the quintic threefold
$X_5 \subset \bP^4$, the analytic construction of a
contracting homotopy on the Dolbeault resolution would
require solving a $\bar\partial$-equation with estimates: a
PDE problem foreign to the algebraic framework.

The algebraic \v{C}ech resolution closes this gap at the
perturbative level. The standard affine cover
$\{U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}\}_{i=0}^{4}$ of $\bP^4$ restricts to
the quintic, giving five affine open sets whose finite
intersections are all affine. Leray's theorem guarantees
acyclicity of the \v{C}ech complex, and the explicit
contracting homotopy
\[
 s^q\bigl(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}\bigr)
 \;:=\; f_{0\, i_0 \cdots i_q}
 \quad(\text{prepend the distinguished index }0)
\]
satisfies the strong deformation retract conditions
\[
 \delta s + s \delta \;=\; \Id - i \circ p,
 \qquad s^2 \;=\; 0,
 \qquad s \circ i \;=\; 0,
 \qquad p \circ s \;=\; 0.
\]
The SDR data $(i, p, s)$ interfaces directly with the
homotopy transfer theorem (HTT), transferring the
$\Ainf$-structure from \v{C}ech cochains
$\check{C}^\bullet(\cE nd(\cE))$ to its cohomology
$\Ext^\bullet(\cE, \cE)$. The transferred operations
$\mu_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ are given by sums over planar trees
whose internal edges are decorated by the homotopy~$s$; each
application of~$s$ introduces rational functions on the
affine intersections, and the resulting chiral operations
are well-defined as formal power series.

The scope is perturbative: the \v{C}ech homotopy produces an
algebraic $\Ainf$-structure on the Ext algebra, but convergence
of the resulting series to holomorphic functions (the
non-perturbative statement) requires separate input from the
analytic completion programme of Volume~I (MC5, the analytic
sewing section there). The perturbative statement suffices for
the formal CY-to-chiral correspondence and for all combinatorial
applications in this volume. The full construction, including
the proof that the SDR conditions hold and the scope
qualification, is Theorem~\ref{thm:cech-contracting-homotopy}
in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.

For the quintic $X_5 = \{x_0^5 + \cdots + x_4^5 = 0\} \subset
\bP^4$, the computation proceeds as follows. The Hodge
diamond gives
\[
 h^{1,1} \;=\; 1,
 \qquad h^{2,1} \;=\; 101,
 \qquad \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5) \;=\; 2(h^{1,1} - h^{2,1})
 \;=\; -200,
\]
so the MacMahon-normalized topological quantity is
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5)/2 = -100$. The \v{C}ech cover
$\{U_i\}_{i=0}^{4}$ has
$\binom{5}{2} = 10$ double intersections,
$\binom{5}{3} = 10$ triple intersections,
$\binom{5}{4} = 5$ quadruple intersections, and one quintuple
intersection
$U_{01234} = X_5 \cap \{x_i \neq 0 \text{ for all } i\}$. Each
$U_{i_0 \cdots i_q}$ is affine (intersection of an affine

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\Eone$}{E1}-chiral bialgebra and Hopf axioms}
\label{sec:e1-bialgebra-intro}

Which Hopf structure survives when the coproduct on an
$\Eone$-chiral algebra is deformed by a spectral parameter? The
$\Eone$-chiral quantum groups of Volume~I answer this abstractly,
as algebraic-geometric objects; here they acquire concrete
CY-geometric realisations, and the Hopf axioms specialise to the
deformed setting. The axioms, formalised in
\S\ref{sec:e1-chiral-bialgebras}, are recorded here for
reference; they constitute the algebraic framework into which
all CY quantum groups of this volume are placed.

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$\Eone$}{E1}-chiral Hopf algebra; preview]
\label{def:e1-chiral-bialgebra-intro}
\ClaimStatusDefinition\
An \emph{$\Eone$-chiral Hopf algebra} on a smooth curve~$C$
is a tuple
$(\cA, \mu, \Delta_z, \varepsilon, \eta, S)$ satisfying five
axioms:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)},topsep=0.3ex,
 itemsep=0.3ex]
\item \emph{$\Eone$-chiral algebra.}
 $(\cA, \mu, \varepsilon)$ is an augmented $\Eone$-chiral
 algebra on $C$: the product $\mu$ is the ordered OPE, and
 the operations live on configuration spaces
 $\Conf_k^{\mathrm{ord}}(C)$ of ordered points.
\item \emph{Spectral coproduct.} For each $z \in \Ran(C)$,
 $\Delta_z \colon \cA \to \cA \otimes_{\Eone, z} \cA$ is an
 $\Eone$-chiral coalgebra structure. At $z = 0$, the coproduct
 $\Delta_0$ coincides with the deconcatenation coproduct on
 $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$; the $z$-deformation encodes the
 spectral data.
\item \emph{Bialgebra compatibility.} The coproduct $\Delta_z$
 is a morphism of $\Eone$-chiral algebras: the diagram
 expressing ``$\Delta_z$ is an algebra map'' commutes, using
 the interchange law for the $\Eone$-monoidal structure.
\item \emph{Spectral coassociativity.} For $z, w \in \Ran(C)$
 in general position:
 \[
  (\Delta_w \otimes \id) \circ \Delta_{z+w}
  \;=\; (\id \otimes \Delta_z) \circ \Delta_w.
 \]
 At $z = w = 0$ this reduces to ordinary coassociativity.
\item \emph{Hopf axiom.} The antipode $S$ satisfies
 \[
  \mu_{\Eone} \circ (S \otimes \id) \circ \Delta_0
  \;=\; \eta \circ \varepsilon
  \;=\; \mu_{\Eone} \circ (\id \otimes S) \circ \Delta_0.
 \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$ preserves the
$R$-matrix; the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(\cA)$ of Volume~I
kills the Hopf structure via averaging
$\mathrm{av}(r(z)) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The
$\Eone$-chiral bialgebra (not the $E_\infty$-vertex bialgebra
of Li) is the correct Hopf framework: the coproduct $\Delta_z$
lives on the $\Eone$ (ordered) side, and the
$E_\infty$-averaging map destroys the Hopf data. The
associated $R$-matrix and Yang--Baxter equation are recovered
from the ordered Hopf data rather than postulated as separate
axioms. Two concrete instances are verified: the Heisenberg
algebra $H_k$ (where $\Delta_z$ is the constant coproduct and
$R(z) = k/z$) and $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at generic $\Psi$ (where
the universal Miura coefficient $(\Psi - 1)/\Psi$ governs the
leading cross-term at every spin $s \geq 2$). The full
development is in Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}.

\section{The ten research programmes}
\label{sec:ten-programmes}

The $K3 \times E$ prototype generates ten research programmes,
each with formal conjectures grounded in the $570{+}$ compute
engines. The formal development is in
Chapter~\ref{chap:toroidal-elliptic}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(Prog \Alph*)}]
\item \emph{Constructive CY gluing.}
 Reconstruct $D^b(K3 \times E)$ from quiver charts indexed by
 Bridgeland stability conditions. The bar functor commutes
 with homotopy colimits (Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-hocolim}),
 so the global chiral algebra is the hocolim of local CoHAs.
 The minimum chart number, the Brauer obstruction, and the
 extension to non-toric K3 surfaces are open.
\item \emph{$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ as universal moonshine
 multiplier.}
 The factor~$2$ in $Z_{K3} = 2\,\phi_{0,1}$ is
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3) = \chi(\cO_{K3})$. The BPS
 degeneracies factorise as
 $A_n = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} \cdot \dim(\rho_n)$, separating
 the homological part ($\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$, from the bar
 complex) from the group-theoretic part ($\rho_n$, from the
 sporadic symmetry). The programme: extend this factorisation
 to the Monster module, the Conway module, and all
 holomorphic VOAs.
\item \emph{Second-quantization bridge.}
 The ratio $5/3$ compares two different invariants on two
 different constructions: the single-copy Heisenberg branch sees
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}} = 3$, while the Borcherds
 lift sees $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$.
 For $K3 \times E$ at orbifold order $N = 1$, the apparent
 equality $2+3=5$ mixes the K3 fibre value with the
 Heisenberg branch and is not a structural decomposition of
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$. The formula
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} =
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}+\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fiber}})$
 fails already under the compact total-space convention and
 fails across the CHL family; the universal identity is
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$.
 At $N \geq 2$ no apparent equality remains
 (Remark~\ref{rem:bkm-decomposition-adversarial}). The
 Hilbert--Chow exceptional divisor accounts for
 $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^2) - \chi(\mathrm{Sym}^2) = 24 = \chi(K3)$,
 and the DMVV symmetric product formula implements the
 passage from single-copy bar complex to second-quantized
 partition function.
\item \emph{Schottky shadow programme.}
 At $g \leq 3$, the Torelli map $\cM_g \to \cA_g$ is dominant
 and the shadow obstruction tower sees nearly all of the
 moduli. At $g \geq 4$, the Schottky locus has positive
 codimension $\mathrm{codim} = (g-2)(g-3)/2$ and the shadow
 is imprisoned in tautological classes on
 $\overline{\cM}_g$. The programme: characterise the
 tautological projection of Siegel modular forms, and
 determine whether the shadow tower generates the image of
 $S_*(\Sp_{2g}(\Z)) \to R^*(\overline{\cM}_g)$. The first
 nontrivial case $g = 4$ (codimension~$1$) already constrains
 the genus-$4$ shadow coefficient.
\item \emph{Mock modularity and the bar complex.}
 The 1/4-BPS counting function is a mock modular form with
 shadow $24\,\eta^3$. The factor $24 = \chi(K3)$ is the
 surface announcing its existence. The programme: construct
 a half-integral-weight metaplectic bar complex whose shadow
 obstruction tower produces the mock modular completion.
\item \emph{Modular factorization envelope.}
 Construct the universal modular factorization envelope
 $U_X^{\mathrm{mod}}(L)$ for cyclically admissible Lie
 conformal algebras, carrying the full shadow obstruction
 tower. This would give the left adjoint of the modular
 primitive-current functor. The construction requires the
 modular operad structure on the collection of
 $\Ainf$-operations parametrised by $\overline{\cM}_{g,n}$;
 for CY input, the cyclic $\Ainf$-structure of~$\cC$ provides
 exactly the data needed for cyclical admissibility.
\item \emph{BKM algebras and scattering diagrams.}
 The Gross--Siebert scattering diagram consistency condition
 IS the $\Eone$ Maurer--Cartan equation
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:scattering-mc}). The programme: lift this
 identification to the full motivic Hall algebra level
 (where naive BCH is insufficient).
\item \emph{Descent theorem.}
 \v{C}ech descent for $\Eone$-algebras over the Bridgeland
 stability manifold: the global $\Eone$-chiral algebra is
 the homotopy colimit of local CoHAs indexed by stability
 chambers, with transition maps given by MC gauge equivalence
 across walls. Proved for toric CY$_3$
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:toric-chart-gluing}); open for compact
 CY$_3$ beyond the chart-atlas regime, where the number of
 charts may be infinite and the hocolim requires a
 convergence argument.
\item \emph{Higher-dimensional CY shadows.}
 The $\En$ stabilization theorem
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) gives a
 Bott-periodic 8-fold pattern: the effective obstruction
 $\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \in \pi_d(BU)$ is trivial at
 $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$ and $\Z$-valued at all even~$d$.
 For CY$_4$: the $p_1$ Pontryagin class controls the shifted
 symplectic structure via the obstruction
 $\pi_4(BU) = \Z$. For $K3 \times K3$: the $E_4$-framing of
 $\Phi_4(\cC_{K3 \times K3})$ splits as the K\"unneth product
 of two $E_2$-framings, one per $K3$ factor, compatible with
 the Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos $\mathbb{S}^2$-action on each
 factor's Hochschild homology; by Dunn additivity
 $E_2 \otimes E_2 = E_4$, recovering $E_2$ per factor without
 any (false) sphere identification
 $\mathbb{S}^4 \cong \mathbb{S}^2 \times \mathbb{S}^2$.
 On the hyperk\"ahler locus the functor $\Phi_4$ admits an
 HK-restricted evaluation:
 \[
  \Phi_4^{\mathrm{HK}}\colon
   \CY_4^{\mathrm{HK}}\text{-}\Cat
   \longrightarrow
   \En[1]\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(\cM_4),
 \]
 defined on smooth proper hyperk\"ahler 4-folds
 ($K3\times K3$, $\mathrm{Hilb}^2(K3)$, $K_3^{[2]}$), with
 conjectural pairing formula
 \[
  \langle\Phi_4^{\mathrm{HK}}(K3_1\times K3_2),
   \Phi_4^{\mathrm{HK}}(K3_1\times K3_2)\rangle
  \;=\;
  4\,\mathrm{Vol}(K3_1)\,\mathrm{Vol}(K3_2)\,(2\pi i)^4,
 \]
 where the factor $4 = 2 \cdot 2$ is $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3_1)\cdot
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3_2)$, the volumes are K\"ahler volumes on
 each factor, and $(2\pi i)^4$ is the CY-$4$ normalisation of the
 Mukai volume form. Three independent bridges support the formula:
 Donaldson--Thomas counts on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(K3\times K3)$ (DT-$4$
 virtual cycles of Borisov--Joyce / Oh--Thomas), Nekrasov's $8$d
 partition function on $\C^4$ / the $\Omega$-deformation (Nekrasov's
 eight-dimensional instanton counting at self-dual
 $\Omega$-background), and the Dijkgraaf--Moore--Verlinde--Verlinde
 symmetric-product formula for Hilbert schemes of surfaces extended
 to $K3\times K3$ (DMVV for $\mathrm{Hilb}^n$). The HK-restricted
 conjecture is strictly weaker than a general CY-$4$ $\Phi_4$: the
 non-HK CY-$4$ strata (sextic fourfold, quintic$\times E$, compact
 non-HK CY-$4$) are outside scope.
\item \emph{Convergence vs divergence.}
 The shadow partition function converges with radius $2\pi$
 (Bernoulli decay: $|F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}| \sim (2\pi)^{-2g}/g$);

\section{The landscape of examples}
\label{sec:landscape-examples}

The two-stage composite $\Phi_d$ operates on any CY category
with cyclic $\Ainf$-structure: $\PhiFA_d$ assembles the
native $E_d$-hFA on the CY$_d$ variety, and
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$ reads the chiral shadow on the
reference curve. Four families of CY categories provide the
standard landscape; each family contributes distinct
structural features to the theory.

\emph{Fukaya categories} (Chapter~\ref{ch:fukaya}). The
symplectic input. For an elliptic curve $E_\tau$, the Fukaya
category $\Fuk(E_\tau)$ is CY of dimension~$1$; $\PhiFA_1$
on $E_\tau$ together with $\SpCh_{\mathrm{pt}, E_\tau}$
produces the Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_k$ at level
$k = \mathrm{vol}(E_\tau)$, an $E_\infty$-chiral algebra
(commutative vertex algebra). For a K3 surface~$S$, $\Fuk(S)$
is CY of dimension~$2$; $\PhiFA_2$ followed by
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}$ for a curve class $\Sigma_1 \subset S$
produces an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra with
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_S) = 2$. For compact CY
threefolds, the native $\PhiFA_3$ is available via
Theorem~CY-A$_3$ and the chiral shadow by $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2,
C}$; the open-string sector ($\Fuk(X)$ with Lagrangian
boundary conditions) connects to the Volume~II Swiss-cheese
structure. Wrapped Fukaya categories
$\mathcal{W}(X)$ of Liouville manifolds provide the
non-compact analogue: for cotangent bundles $T^*M$, Abouzaid's
equivalence
$\mathcal{W}(T^*M) \simeq \mathrm{Mod}(C_*(\Omega M))$
reduces the CY-to-chiral correspondence to the based loop space.

\emph{Derived categories of coherent sheaves.} The algebraic
input. For a smooth projective CY manifold $X$, the bounded
derived category $D^b(\Coh(X))$ is CY of dimension~$d$ with
the CY trace induced by Serre duality. Homological mirror
symmetry identifies $\Fuk(X) \simeq D^b(\Coh(X^\vee))$; under
$\Phi$, this becomes an equivalence
$\cA_{\Fuk(X)} \simeq \cA_{D^b(\Coh(X^\vee))}$ of quantum
chiral algebras, providing a consistency check between the
symplectic and algebraic sides. When $D^b(\Coh(X))$ admits a
full exceptional collection, the CY-to-chiral correspondence reduces
to a quiver-with-potential computation: the critical CoHA of
the quiver (Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha}). Bridgeland stability
conditions parametrize the space of t-structures on
$D^b(\Coh(X))$; wall-crossing in the stability manifold
corresponds to mutations of the bar complex, and the global
chiral algebra is assembled as a homotopy colimit over

 5 = \chi(\mathcal O_{K3}) + \sum_{24\,I_1\text{ fibres}}\mathrm{Res}
 = 2 + 3,
\]
identifying $\chi(\mathcal O_{K3}) = 2$ as the smooth-bulk contribution
and $3$ as the residual sum over the 24 Kodaira fibres. The
class-$\mathcal S$ $A_1$ Schur index on $\Sigma_{0,24}$ reads the
Chacaltana--Distler pants decomposition
$c_{4d}(A_1, \Sigma_{0,24}) = (5n - 13)/6|_{n = 24} = 107/6$,
$c_{2d} = -214$, with integer $q$-coefficients $1, 72, 2678, \ldots$
\cite{ChacaltanaDistler2010, BeemPeelaersRastelli2014}. Both reduce,
at the decoupled self-dual-radius $T^2$ point, to the same $\Delta_5$
numerator datum. The five-frame closure cross-verifies the two
computations; the three remaining frames (heterotic decoupled,
F-theory, Type~I) await explicit computational instantiation.
\end{remark}
\section{Guide for the reader}
\label{sec:guide}

The volume has seven parts, organised by the two-stage
factorisation. Part~\ref{part:cy-categories} fixes CY input
categories; Part~\ref{part:bridge} constructs
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$ and its stage-$1$
$\infty$-categorical closure; Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} records
the $\En$-hierarchy on native and shadow sides;
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} evaluates at the K3 data at $d = 2$ and
the K3-fibre specialisation at $d = 3$; Part~\ref{part:examples}
surveys the wider CY landscape of stage-$2$ specialisations;
Part~\ref{part:connections} reads the seven faces of
$r_{\mathrm{CY}}$ through the three-tier stratification of
\S\ref{sec:r-cy-three-tier}; Part~\ref{part:frontier} collects the
unresolved extensions. The dependencies are:
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{I (CY input)}
\xrightarrow{\;\PhiFA_d\;}
\text{II (stage-1)}
\xrightarrow{\;\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\;}
\text{III (stage-2 $\En$-shadow)} \\[0.5em]
\text{III}
\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{IV (K3 Yangian $d = 2$; $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ at $d = 3$)}\\
\text{V (CY landscape: toric, quintic, conifold, $\bP^2$)}
\end{cases}
\longrightarrow
\text{VI ($r_{\mathrm{CY}}$ faces)}
\longrightarrow
\text{VII (frontiers)}
\end{array}
\]
Parts~\ref{part:k3-yangian} and~\ref{part:examples} are
independent of each other: either may be read first.
Part~\ref{part:connections} draws on both.
Part~\ref{part:frontier} is largely self-contained but
references all prior parts. Three reading paths follow,
keyed to the reader's entry point into the two-stage
factorisation.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~1: The theorem path}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:bridge};
approximately 8 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
This is the shortest path to the main theorems.
Part~\ref{part:cy-categories} establishes the categorical
foundations: CY categories (Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories}),
cyclic $\Ainf$-structures (Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}), and
the Hochschild calculus (Chapter~\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}).
Part~\ref{part:bridge} constructs the two-stage composite
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$ and proves
Theorems~CY-A$_2$ and CY-A$_3$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}), recounts the
$[m_3, B^{(2)}]$ obstruction at the $\PhiFA_3$ stage and its
resolution (Chapter~\ref{ch:m3-b2-obstruction}), and develops
the holomorphic Chern--Simons route as an independent
construction (Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-chiral-algebras}).
Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} includes the proof of
Theorem~CY-B$_3$ (the $\Etwo$-chiral Koszul dual identified
via the Verdier spectral functor).

A reader who wants only the construction of
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$ and its
proof can stop after Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}; the
remaining chapters of Part~\ref{part:bridge} provide context
and alternative routes. Prerequisites: familiarity with dg
categories and basic homotopical algebra at the level
of~\cite{LurieHA}; the bar--cobar formalism of Volume~I is
used freely but the essential definitions are recalled in
Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~2: The K3 path}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}, \ref{part:bridge}, and
\ref{part:k3-yangian}; approximately 15 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
This path follows Path~1 through the functor construction,
then jumps directly to Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} for the
central worked example. The K3 surface is the simplest
compact CY manifold on which all structures are explicit: the
Mukai lattice $\Lambda^{4,20}$ provides the root datum, and
$\Phi_2 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}\circ\PhiFA_2$ sends
$D^b\Coh(K3)$ to the lattice Heisenberg VOA $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$
on the reference curve; the K3 double current algebra
$\frakg_{K3}$ quantizes to the abelian Yangian
$Y(\frakg_{K3})$ with $24$ generators and degree-$(24, 24)$
structure function.

For the threefold $K3 \times E$, six independent constructions
(BKM, lattice VOA, Kummer, CY-to-chiral, sigma model, BLLPR)
witness the single canonical $\PhiFA_3(D^b\Coh(K3\times E))$:
each is the $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C}$-shadow for a distinct choice
of real surface $\Sigma_2 \subset K3\times E$, and the
resulting chiral shadows share the Igusa cusp form~$\Phi_{10}$
as denominator identity
(Remark~\ref{rem:multiple-e1-specialisations}). The mock modular
structure of the K3 elliptic genus is proved at $d = 2$
(shadow $24\,\eta^3$, Zwegers completion, Borcherds lift), and
the Stokes--wall-crossing identification connects Borel
singularities to walls of marginal stability. At
root-of-unity level $N = 3$, the K3 quantum group produces
the first non-abelian MTC from 6d geometry ($3200$ modules,
Ising fusion). Readers from vertex algebras, BPS state
counting, or Borcherds--Kac--Moody algebras will find this
path self-contained once the two-stage composite
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$ is in hand.
Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} may be consulted as needed for
the $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axioms
(Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}) and the Drinfeld centre
construction (Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center}), but the K3
material does not depend on the full operadic development.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Path~3: The landscape path (full programme)}
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:frontier};
all 25 chapters).

\smallskip

\noindent
The seven parts are written to be read in sequence.
Part~\ref{part:cy-categories} lays the categorical and
homological foundations: CY categories, cyclic
$\Ainf$-structures, and the Hochschild calculus that extracts
the Lie conformal algebra~$\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ from the
categorical data. Part~\ref{part:bridge} constructs and proves
the two-stage CY-to-chiral composite
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\circ\PhiFA_d$ at all~$d$,
including the $\infty$-categorical resolution of the
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction and the holomorphic
Chern--Simons route. Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} develops
the $\En$-hierarchy: $\Eone$-chiral bialgebras with their
Hopf axioms, $\Etwo$-chiral algebras with braided monoidal
representation categories, the $\Eone$-stabilization theorem,
classical quantum group theory (Drinfeld--Jimbo, RTT,
Kazhdan--Lusztig), and the Drinfeld centre passage from
$\Eone$ to $\Etwo$.

Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} works out the K3 Yangian as the
principal example: derived categories, the K3 chiral algebra,
the abelian Yangian, the BKM superalgebra, quantum toroidal
algebras, and the full $K3 \times E$ CY$_3$ programme.
Part~\ref{part:examples} surveys the broader CY landscape:
toric CY$_3$ CoHAs, matrix factorizations, Fukaya categories,
quantum group representations, and the modular trace.
Part~\ref{part:connections} develops the seven faces of
$r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$: the modular Koszul bridge
($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi^{\CY}$ at $d = 2$), the
bar--cobar bridge to Volume~I (shadow--Feynman dictionary,
$\Ainf$-coproduct tower), the CY holographic datum, the
Stokes--wall-crossing identification, and the BCOV--shadow

frontiers: the geometric Langlands programme, the nonabelian

(exact rational~$T$ matrix), the chiral volume conjecture,
the chiral Khovanov complex, and the Conway--Leech--Niemeier
rigidity connection. This path is recommended for readers
who intend to work with the full CY-to-chiral-to-modular
pipeline across all three volumes.

\section{Conventions and notation}
\label{sec:conventions-intro}

This volume inherits the conventions of Volumes~I and~II; the
points where the CY setting introduces additional notation or
where conventions differ across the literature are recorded
here. A comprehensive conventions appendix is
Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}.

\emph{Grading.} All grading is cohomological: differentials
have degree $+1$. The bar construction uses desuspension
$s^{-1}$, with $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$ (matching Volume~I; see
signs\_and\_shifts.tex therein). The CY dimension~$d$ enters
as a cohomological shift: the CY trace
$\Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$ is a map of
degree~$-d$.

\emph{$E_n$ notation.} $\En$ denotes the little $n$-disks
operad (Boardman--Vogt); $E_\infty$ denotes the commutative
operad. Dunn additivity: $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$. An
$E_2$-chiral algebra (Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra})
factorizes along two holomorphic directions; its
representation category is braided monoidal
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}). $\FM_k(\C)$ denotes the
Fulton--MacPherson compactification of $\Conf_k(\C)$; this is
a blowup along diagonals, not the complement of the diagonal.

\emph{Divided powers and the $\lambda$-bracket.} The
translation between OPE modes $a_{(n)} b$ and the
$\lambda$-bracket uses the divided-power convention
$\lambda^{(n)} = \lambda^n / n!$, so
$\{a_\lambda b\} = \sum_n \lambda^{(n)} a_{(n)} b$. The
$\lambda$-bracket coefficient at order~$n$ is
$a_{(n)} b / n!$, not $a_{(n)} b$ (Volume~I). This convention
is consistent with Volumes~I and~II; when consulting
references that use the Borel transform
$B(K)(\lambda) = \sum \lambda^{(n)} c_n$, the $1/n!$ is
already absorbed into $\lambda^{(n)}$.

\emph{CY categories.} $\CY_d$-$\Cat$ denotes the
$\infty$-category of smooth $d$-dimensional CY categories.
Tensor products of dg categories are derived unless stated
otherwise. ``Equivalence'' means quasi-equivalence of dg
categories (equivalence of associated $\infty$-categories).
The Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg isomorphism
$\HH_\bullet(D^b(\Coh(X))) \simeq H^*(X, \Omega^\bullet_X)$
is used freely for smooth projective varieties.

\emph{The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum.} A single CY manifold
admits multiple modular characteristics, each measuring a
different face of the geometry. This volume uses subscripted
$\kappa_\bullet$ throughout: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ (the
chiral algebra modular characteristic of Volume~I, defined as
the degree-$2$ projection of $\Theta_\cA$),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ (the holomorphic Euler characteristic
$\chi(\cO_X)$, a categorical invariant),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ (the half-weight of the Borcherds
automorphic form, an automorphic invariant), and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ (the lattice rank of the fiber,
relevant for fibered CY manifolds). Every modular-characteristic
symbol in this volume carries one of these explicit subscripts; each
subscript names a specific invariant and the subscripts do not
interchange.

\emph{Operadic terminology.} Following Volume~I, the word
``degree'' is used throughout for: bar complex grading,
operadic input count, tree vertex valence, Stasheff identity
level, Swiss-cheese mixed sector parameters, and all
index-counting contexts. The word ``arity'' does not appear.

\emph{Bar complex conventions.} The bar complex is
$B(\cA) = T^c(s^{-1} \bar{\cA})$ with
$\bar{\cA} = \ker(\varepsilon)$ the augmentation ideal. The
tensor coalgebra $T^c$ carries the deconcatenation coproduct.
The ordered bar $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA)$ is the primitive
object; the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(\cA)$ is the
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant quotient. The desuspension satisfies
$|s^{-1} v| = |v| - 1$.

\emph{Cross-volume references.} Results from Volume~I are
cited as ``Vol~I, Theorem~X'' or by their Volume~I label
(e.g., thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic). Results from Volume~II are
cited analogously. When a formula appears in multiple
volumes, the conventions may differ: Volume~I uses OPE modes,
Volume~II uses $\lambda$-brackets with divided powers, and
this volume uses both depending on context. Every cross-volume
formula citation in this work has been independently verified
against the source.

\subsection*{The K3 chiral bialgebra: a six-lens roadmap}

The K3 chiral bialgebra $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$, attached to the CY threefold
$K3 \times E$ through the Gritsenko--Nikulin Igusa cusp form $\Delta_5$ of weight~$5$
on $\mathrm{O}^+(3,2)$, is the crowning example of the $d=3$ programme. Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian}
reaches it in two chapters.
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-yangian} presents the integrable self-mirror facet: the
abelian Yangian $Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$ with $24$ Heisenberg generators, Mukai-signature
Serre relations, and degree-$(24,24)$ structure function, together with the
Maulik--Okounkov evaluation $R$-matrix on the Fock space
$\bigoplus_n K_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(K3\times E))$ at ADE/Kummer charts of K3 moduli.
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic} presents the
generalised-Borcherds facet: the K3 chiral Hall--Drinfeld double
$\mathcal{D}_\hbar(\mathcal{Y}^{\mathrm{Hall}}_\hbar(\mathrm{CoHA}_{K3\times E}))$,
hosted on a bi-based Ran-space datum $(\mathrm{Ran}(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}),\,
\overline{\mathcal{A}_2})$ with averaging morphism via Kodaira-$j$, Kuga--Satake and
K3 Torelli. The $4d$ $\mathcal{N}=2$ parent is the class-$\mathcal{S}$ $A_1$ theory on
the $24$-punctured sphere $\Sigma_{0,24}$. The Igusa form $\Delta_5$ is
the $1$-loop-forced output of paramodular anomaly cancellation in a
twisted 11D-SUGRA parent on $K3\times T^2$ with $24$ M5-branes wrapping
the $I_1$ Kodaira fibres. The gauge-equivalence class of
$\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ as a super-quasi-Hopf algebra is a $1$-dimensional
Lie-bialgebra cohomology
$H^2(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})^{\mathbb{Z}/2,\, K(1)}
\cong \mathbb{C}\cdot\Delta_5$, so the Igusa form is the classification
invariant. Chacaltana--Distler pants decomposition of
$\Sigma_{0,24}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:k3-chacaltana-distler-24}) yields
\[
 c_{4d} = \frac{2n_v + n_h}{12} = \frac{107}{6},
 \qquad
 a_{4d} = \frac{5n_v + n_h}{24} = \frac{403}{24},
 \qquad
 \mathrm{rank}_{\mathrm{Coul}} = 21,
 \qquad
 c_{2d} = -12\,c_{4d} = -214,
\]
with $(n_v, n_h) = (63, 88)$ and flavour
$\widehat{\mathfrak{su}}(2)^{\otimes 24}_{k_{2d} = -2}$. The
$\mathrm{SU}(2)$ $N_f = 4$ cross-check gives
$(c_{4d}, c_{2d}) = (7/6, -14)$ at $n = 4$, excluding the naive
$(12(g-1) + 7n)/6$ formula
(Remark~\ref{rem:k3-classS-numerical-caveat}).
The Schur sector reaches the K3 elliptic genus only through
\[
 \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Schur}}
 \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{av}_{M_{24}}\ }
 \phi_{0,1}
 \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{Borch}\ }
 \Delta_5.
\]

These two chapters do not construct neighbouring objects, and they do
not exhibit six normal forms of a single two-stage CY-to-chiral output.
They expose six comparison lenses around the fixed $K3\times E$
Borcherds shadow. The Drinfeld lens sees the
Hall--Drinfeld double; the Frenkel--Kac/Borcherds lens sees the
imaginary-root vertex closure; the Siegel/paramodular lens sees the
twisted genus-$2$ associator; the bi-based factorization lens sees the
averaging map from $\mathrm{Ran}(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24})$ to
$\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$ via Kodaira-$j$, Kuga--Satake, and Torelli;
the Costello lens sees $\Delta_5$ as the $1$-loop output of the twisted
parent; the class-$\mathcal{S}$/AGT lens sees the same object as the
chiral algebra attached to $\mathcal{T}[A_1,\Sigma_{0,24}]$. The six
lenses are six coordinate systems on the $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ comparison
locus
\cite{Drinfeld1985,Drinfeld1988,GritsenkoNikulin1995,GritsenkoNikulin1998,Gritsenko1999,Borcherds1992,Borcherds1998,Miki07}.
They meet again on the order-$8$ identity
\[
 K
 \;=\;
 2c_+(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3))
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{ord}\bigl(
   \mathrm{monodromy}\,\mathcal{L}^{\Delta_5}|_{H_1}
 \bigr)
 \;=\;
 \ell,
 \qquad
 \hbar^2=-1/8,
\]
with $\ell=8$ the Lusztig specialisation. Drinfeld, Bruinier,
Beilinson, and Witten are reading the same $\mathbb{Z}/8$-class
through different charts.

Seven structural refinements pin down the K3 chiral bialgebra: a classification
invariant (Igusa form), an explicit presentation (24-Miki with
$\widetilde{M}_{24}$-umbral cocycle), a hosted bi-based factorization datum, a CY-$2$
Koszul shift $[2]$, a $4d$ class-$\mathcal{S}$ parent, a $1$-loop-forced origin of
$\Delta_5$, and the abelian-at-Lie / non-abelian-at-vertex discipline. The reader who
wants the integrable Yangian facet should read Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-yangian}; the
reader who wants the BKM / Siegel-modular-form facet should read
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}; the reader who wants the full
crown should read them as a pair, meeting on the rank-$24$ Mukai data.
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic} is the crown chapter of
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian}; it opens into Part~\ref{part:examples},
where the K3 datum is tested against the wider CY landscape, then into
Part~\ref{part:connections}, where the six lenses reappear as faces of
$r_{\mathrm{CY}}$, and finally into Part~\ref{part:frontier}, where
the remaining unconstructed extensions are isolated.
