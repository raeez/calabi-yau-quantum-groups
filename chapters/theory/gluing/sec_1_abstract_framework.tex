\section{The gluing problem in categorical generality}
\label{sec:gluing:abstract}
\label{ch:gluing-part-i}
\subsection{The abstract set-up}
\label{sec:glue-abstract-setup}

Fix a Calabi--Yau variety $X$ of complex dimension $d$, smooth or
with at worst Gorenstein canonical singularities, equipped with a
nowhere-vanishing holomorphic volume form $\Omega_X \in
H^{d,0}(X)$. The cohomological Hall algebra $\CoHA(X)$ of
Kontsevich--Soibelman arXiv:1006.2706 is an associative graded
algebra built from vanishing-cycle cohomology of moduli of objects
in a Calabi--Yau category attached to $X$. The construction is
\emph{local-to-global by design}: critical cohomology lives on a
stack glued from local charts, and the Hall convolution is defined
in terms of local extension correspondences.

The local input is a quiver with potential $(Q_\alpha,W_\alpha)$, or
more generally a finite cyclic critical model, presenting the local
Calabi--Yau $A_\infty$ algebra on a chart $U_\alpha\subset X$.
Derksen--Weyman--Zelevinsky mutation and Keller--Yang kernels compare
some such presentations on overlaps; they are not the global gluing
mechanism. The global hCS--Hall comparison is a finite
Dolbeault--Weiss--Ran problem: one must place hCS observables and
oriented Rees critical Hall complexes on the same finite Ran nerve,
solve the convolution Maurer--Cartan equation there, and only then ask
for completion and vanishing-cycle realisation.

\begin{definition}[Critical CoHA on a smooth chart, after Kontsevich--Soibelman 2011]
\label{def:critical-coha-local}
Let $Y$ be a smooth quasi-projective variety with an action of a
reductive group $G$ and let $f: Y \to \bC$ be a $G$-invariant
regular function. The \emph{critical cohomological Hall algebra}
of $(Y, f, G)$ is the graded vector space
\[
 \sH(Y, f, G) \;=\; H^{*}_{G}\!\bigl(Y,\, \varphi_f\bigr)
\]
where $\varphi_f$ is the sheaf of vanishing cycles of $f$; the
convolution product, when $(Y, f, G)$ is equipped with a choice of
extension correspondence, is
$m(\alpha \otimes \beta) = q_* p^*(\alpha \boxtimes \beta)$ along
\[
 Y' \times Y''
 \;\xleftarrow{p}\;
 Y'_{\subset}
 \;\xrightarrow{q}\;
 Y
\]
twisted by the Joyce--Song sign
(Kontsevich--Soibelman arXiv:1006.2706, Definition~2; Davison
arXiv:1311.7172, Theorem~A for regularity of $\varphi_f$ under the
equivariant structure; Davison--Meinhardt arXiv:1601.02479,
Theorem~A for associativity and the dimensional-reduction
isomorphism).

The \emph{quiver-with-potential form} is the specialisation
$(Y, f, G) = (\Rep(Q, \mathbf d), W_{\mathbf d}, G_{\mathbf d})$
with $G_{\mathbf d} = \prod_i GL(d_i)$, $W_{\mathbf d}$ the trace
of the potential, and the extension correspondence given by
short-exact-sequence stacks $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ in
$\Rep(Q)$. When this specialisation is available, $\sH(Q, W) =
\bigoplus_{\mathbf d} \sH(\Rep(Q, \mathbf d), W_{\mathbf d}, G_{\mathbf d})$
is $\bN^{Q^0}$-graded.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[When a chart admits quiver presentation]
\label{prop:chart-quiver-presentation}
Let $U \subset X$ be an affine open in a Calabi--Yau threefold.
The critical CoHA $\sH(U) = \sH(Y_U, f_U, G_U)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:critical-coha-local} admits a
quiver-with-potential presentation, i.e.\ an isomorphism $\sH(U)
\cong \sH(Q, W)$ for some $(Q, W)$, under either of the following
\emph{global} hypotheses on $U$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(VdB)] $U$ admits a \emph{Van den Bergh tilting bundle}: a
 coherent sheaf $T$ on $U$ with $\mathrm{Ext}^{>0}(T, T) = 0$,
 $\mathrm{End}(T)$ Noetherian, and the functor
 $R\Hom(T, -): \Db(\Coh U) \to \Db(\mathrm{End}(T)\text{-mod})$ an
 equivalence (Van den Bergh arXiv:math/0211064, Theorem~A). Then
 $(Q, W)$ is the Ginzburg quiver of $\mathrm{End}(T)$, and
 $U = \Spec \Jac(Q, W)$ (Ginzburg arXiv:math/0612139,
 Theorem~3.6.4).
\item[(DM)] $U$ is toric without compact divisors, and the
 Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao presentation of
 Davison--Meinhardt arXiv:1601.02479 Theorem~A applies. Then
 $(Q, W)$ is the Jacobi quiver of the toric fan.
\end{enumerate}
Neither hypothesis is automatic: (VdB) fails on compact
Calabi--Yau threefolds (a tilting bundle would force $H^0(U, \sO)$
to be Noetherian, incompatible with the Čech assembly of a
projective CY\textsubscript{3}); (DM) fails whenever $U$ carries
a compact 4-cycle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both implications (VdB) $\Rightarrow$ Jacobi and (DM) $\Rightarrow$
toric Jacobi are by direct quotation of the cited theorems; the
negative statements are obstructions: any tilting bundle is
concentrated in degree 0, and toric compact-divisor support forces
the fan to have a compact cone, which then supports a non-zero
higher-direct-image obstructing tilting.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Chart cover hypothesis, named explicitly]
\label{rem:scope-critical-coha-local}
The rest of the chapter fixes an open cover $\mathfrak{U} =
\{U_\alpha\}$ of $X$ with each $U_\alpha$ satisfying either (VdB)
or (DM). Such a cover exists when $X$ is either toric (DM, fine
enough fan refinement) or admits a local-to-global Van den Bergh
resolution on affine charts (Buchweitz--Hille arXiv:1404.3007 for
the flop-local case; the compact CY\textsubscript{3} case requires a
separate construction).
The existence hypothesis is the chart-level input to the
gluing; it does not imply a global tilting bundle on $X$, which
would contradict compactness.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Finite DWR/Ran critical diagrams}
\label{sec:sheaf-of-cohas}

Vanishing cycles are local on the critical target, while Hall
convolution is local on extension correspondences. The descent site
therefore has two directions. The Dolbeault--Weiss direction records
holomorphic polydiscs and compact-support extension; the Ran direction
records finite disjoint configurations and the factorisation product.
For a cover $\mathfrak U=\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$, write
$\mathsf N_{\mathsf{DW\!R}}(\mathfrak U)$ for the simplicial category
whose $p$-simplices are finite families
\[
 \sigma=(S,\{P_s\Subset U_{i_{s,0}}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_{s,p}}\}_{s\in S})
\]
of pairwise disjoint holomorphic polydiscs. Face maps forget one
\v{C}ech index, degeneracies repeat one, refinements shrink the
polydiscs, and Ran multiplication is disjoint union. A DWR-good cover
is one for which finite intersections are refined by the same
polydisc basis, compact-support extension is continuous, and Weiss
descent is computed by the homotopy colimit over refinements.

\begin{definition}[Finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram]
\label{def:simplicial-sheaf-cohas}
Fix finite bounds
\[
 N\quad\text{(holomorphic jets)},\qquad
 r\quad\text{(renormalisation order)},\qquad
 L\quad\text{(charge/HN bound)},\qquad
 m\quad\text{(Ran arity)}.
\]
A finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram on $(X,\mathfrak U)$ consists,
for every
\[
 \sigma\in
 \mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L}),
\]
of the following data, where
$\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})$ denotes a
chosen finite full sub-nerve in the stated arity and charge bounds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A finite hCS observable complex
 $\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L}$ with bar coalgebra
 $B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L}$.
\item A finite cyclic critical model $(V_\sigma,W_\sigma,\omega_\sigma)$
 over $\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p)$ when $\sigma$ is a $p$-simplex.
\item A face-compatible cyclic contraction
 \[
 \left(
 H_\sigma
 \underset{p_\sigma}{\stackrel{i_\sigma}{\rightleftarrows}}
 \Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L}
 \otimes\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p),
 \ h_\sigma
 \right)
 \]
 with
 \[
 d_{\mathrm{tot}}h_\sigma+h_\sigma d_{\mathrm{tot}}
 =1-i_\sigma p_\sigma,\qquad
 \partial_j^*(i_\sigma,p_\sigma,h_\sigma)=
 (i_{\partial_j\sigma},p_{\partial_j\sigma},h_{\partial_j\sigma}).
 \]
\item The oriented Rees critical Hall complex
 \[
 \Hall_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or}}(\Delta^p)
 =
 \left(
 C^\bullet\!\left(
 \mathfrak g_\sigma,
 \Gamma(V_\sigma,\wedge^\bullet T_{V_\sigma})[[\hbar]]
 \otimes\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p)
 \right)^{G_\sigma},
 d_{\CE}+\iota_{d_VW_\sigma}
 +\hbar\Delta_{\omega_\sigma}+d_{\Delta^p}
 \right)
 \otimes o_\sigma[s_\sigma](t_\sigma),
 \]
 with orientation line, cohomological shift, and Tate twist compatible
 with every face and with disjoint union.
\end{enumerate}
The diagram is multiplicative when
$W_{\sigma_1\sqcup\sigma_2}=W_{\sigma_1}\boxplus W_{\sigma_2}$ and the
Rees twisted de Rham model sends external tensor product to the
Thom--Sebastiani Hall product.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[DWZ mutation and DWR/Ran descent]
\label{rem:strictness-fails}
Derksen--Weyman--Zelevinsky mutation and Keller--Yang kernels compare
quiver-with-potential presentations on individual Hall charts. They do
not by themselves supply a compact hCS--Hall comparison, a completed
Hall object, or a monoidal vanishing-cycle realisation. The DWR/Ran
diagram records the finite descent problem on which such chart maps
must live.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Finite hCS--Rees-Hall convolution dg Lie algebra]
\label{def:finite-hcs-rees-hall-convolution-lie-algebra-abstract}
For a finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram put
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)
 :=
 \operatorname*{Tot}_{\sigma\in
 \mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})}
 \Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}
 \left(
 B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L},
 \Hall_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or}}
 \right).
\]
The differential is
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}
 =
 d_{\Hall}\circ(-)
 -(-1)^{|-|}(-)\circ d_{B\Obs}
 +\delta_{\Cech/\Ran},
\]
and the bracket is the convolution bracket induced by the bar
coproduct on $B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q}$ and the Rees Hall product.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Maurer--Cartan equation for finite gluing]
\label{lem:finite-gluing-mc}
A degree-zero element
\[
 \Theta\in
 \mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)^0
\]
is a multiplicative natural transformation from finite hCS bar
observables to oriented Rees Hall complexes on
$\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})$ if and only if
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}\Theta+\frac12[\Theta,\Theta]=0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two internal parts of $D_{\mathrm{tot}}$ say exactly that the
simplex component $\Theta_\sigma$ is a chain map. The
$\Cech/\Ran$ part says that the restrictions to all faces of $\sigma$
agree with the maps attached to those faces, after the refinement maps
and compact-support extensions are inserted. The convolution bracket
measures the difference between first multiplying on the hCS bar side
and then applying $\Theta$, and first applying $\Theta$ and then using
Hall convolution. The displayed equation is therefore precisely the
chain, face, and product compatibility of a natural transformation on
the finite DWR/Ran nerve.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite simplex totalisation]
\label{prop:descent-reduction}
Let a finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram be given. Suppose each
$p$-simplex $\sigma$ carries a relative degree-zero map
\[
 \theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}\colon
 B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L}
 \longrightarrow
 \Hall_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or}}(\Delta^p)
\]
whose relative convolution curvature vanishes over
$\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p)$ and whose restrictions to faces are the
maps for the face simplices. Define
\[
 \Theta_\sigma^{(p)}
 :=
 \int_{\Delta^p}\theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}
 \in
 \Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}^{-p}
 \left(
 B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L},
 \Hall_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or}}
 \right)
\]
and
\[
 \Theta^{N,r,L,m}_{\hCS\to\Hall}
 :=
 \sum_{0\le p\le m}
 \sum_{\sigma\in
 \mathsf N^{\Ran}_{p,\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})}
 \Theta_\sigma^{(p)}.
\]
Then
\[
 \Theta^{N,r,L,m}_{\hCS\to\Hall}
 \in
 \MC\!\left(
 \mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)
 \right).
\]
If the vertex maps are quasi-isomorphisms and the contractions of
Definition~\ref{def:simplicial-sheaf-cohas} are face-compatible
quasi-isomorphism contractions, the induced map on finite DWR/Ran
totalisations is a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each simplex, the relative curvature identity is
\[
 (d_{\Hall}+d_{\Delta^p})\theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}
 -\theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}d_{B\Obs}
 +\frac12[\theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}},
 \theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}]_{\mathrm{conv}}=0.
\]
Integrating over $\Delta^p$ turns the $d_{\Delta^p}$ term into the
alternating sum of boundary restrictions:
\[
 \int_{\Delta^p}d_{\Delta^p}\eta
 =
 \sum_{j=0}^p(-1)^j
 \int_{\Delta^{p-1}}\partial_j^*\eta
\]
with the standard cochain sign convention. Face compatibility
identifies these boundary terms with $\delta_{\Cech/\Ran}$. The
remaining terms are exactly the two internal differentials and the
bar--Hall convolution bracket. Summing over the finite set of
simplices gives the Maurer--Cartan equation in
$\mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)$.

The nerve is finite in the bounds $(N,r,L,m)$ and the diagram consists
of complexes. Objectwise quasi-isomorphisms therefore induce a
quasi-isomorphism on the finite total complex. The face-compatible
contractions identify every higher-simplex map with the corresponding
refined vertex quasi-isomorphism, so the totalised comparison is a
quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite gluing category}
\label{sec:glue-2cat}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Rees gluing has no hidden higher cells]
\label{prop:glcoha-truncation-forced}
For fixed $(N,r,L,m)$ and a fixed DWR-good cover, the finite Rees
gluing datum is completely encoded by the convolution dg Lie algebra
$\mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)$ and its
Maurer--Cartan equation. No additional simplex of dimension
$>m$ appears in the construction, and no completed inverse-limit datum
is part of the finite object.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All simplices are objects of the chosen finite full sub-nerve
$\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})$.
The total differential contains the alternating face operator for every
face of every simplex in this finite category. Multiplication is
recorded by the convolution bracket. Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-gluing-mc}
therefore converts every chain, face, and product condition into one
Maurer--Cartan equation. A simplex outside the arity bound, a higher
charge, a higher jet, or a higher renormalisation term belongs to a
different member of the inverse system, not to this finite
totalisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite first, limits second]
\label{rem:truncation-property-not-choice}
The finite DWR/Ran object is an exact chain-level construction. Passing
from it to an all-jet, all-charge, all-arity compact comparison is an
inverse-limit problem; it is not a formal consequence of having one
finite Maurer--Cartan element.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The finite gluing category $\GlCoHA^{N,r,L,m}(X;\mathfrak U)$]
\label{def:glue-2cat}
The category $\GlCoHA^{N,r,L,m}(X;\mathfrak U)$ has:
\begin{itemize}
\item objects: finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagrams on
 $(X,\mathfrak U)$ together with a Maurer--Cartan element of
 $\mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)$;
\item morphisms: gauge equivalences of Maurer--Cartan elements induced
 by degree-zero elements of the positive DWR/Ran filtration and
 compatible with the orientation, shift, Tate, and
 Thom--Sebastiani transports;
\item products: disjoint-union Ran products, transported through the
 Hall Thom--Sebastiani isomorphism.
\end{itemize}
The unbounded symbol $\GlCoHA(X)$ denotes the further object obtained
only after a compatible inverse system in $(N,r,L,m)$ and a monoidal
vanishing-cycle realisation have been supplied.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finite Rees comparison and the two further arrows]
\label{thm:global-coha-limit}
Let $X$ be a smooth Calabi--Yau threefold with a DWR-good cover
$\mathfrak U$. A multiplicative finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram
with face-compatible contractions determines the finite oriented Rees
comparison
\[
 \Theta_{\hCS\to\Hall}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or};N,r,L,m}\colon
 \Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(-)_{\le L}
 \longrightarrow
 \Hall_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or},\le N,\le L}(-)
\]
on $\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})$. The
comparison is a quasi-isomorphism on finite DWR/Ran descent when the
vertex maps are quasi-isomorphisms.

The completed Rees comparison requires compatible finite
Maurer--Cartan elements under
\[
 N+1\to N,\qquad r+1\to r,\qquad
 \Gamma_{\le L+1}\to\Gamma_{\le L},\qquad
 \mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m+1}\to\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m},
\]
together with the Mittag--Leffler condition killing the corresponding
$\varprojlim^1$ term. The critical-CoHA comparison further requires a
monoidal vanishing-cycle realisation
\[
 \mathsf R_{\mathrm{vc}}\colon
 \Hall_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or},\wedge}(-)
 \longrightarrow
 \CoHA_{\mathrm{crit}}^{\mathrm{or},\wedge}(-)
\]
preserving Thom--Sebastiani products, compact-support pushforward, lci
pullback, equivariance, orientation lines, shifts, Tate twists, and the
chosen completions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The finite comparison is Proposition~\ref{prop:descent-reduction}
applied to the relative maps produced by cyclic homological
perturbation and the Fourier--BV Rees identification on every simplex.
Thom--Sebastiani multiplicativity gives the product compatibility, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-gluing-mc} packages the chain, face, and product
conditions as the Maurer--Cartan equation.

For the completed Rees comparison, the finite Maurer--Cartan elements
must form an inverse-system element in the completed convolution dg Lie
algebras. The obstruction to commuting cohomology with this inverse
limit is the usual derived-limit term; the Mittag--Leffler hypothesis
removes it, so the limiting element again satisfies the
Maurer--Cartan equation. The final displayed functor is a separate
monoidal realisation from Rees critical complexes to vanishing-cycle
Borel--Moore complexes. Its preservation properties are exactly the
ones needed for the image of the completed Rees comparison to remain a
degree-zero Hall product morphism with the same orientation and grading
normalisations.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the comparison can fail]
\label{rem:finite-comparison-failure}
For a compact Calabi--Yau threefold outside the known finite
DWR/Ran constructions, the missing datum is not a formal
$(2,1)$-categorical filler. It is a finite family of relative
chart maps and face-compatible homotopies solving the convolution
Maurer--Cartan equation, followed by inverse-limit compatibility and
monoidal vanishing-cycle realisation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the categorical viewpoint]
\label{rem:cat-viewpoint-coha}
The finite Rees comparison is a chain-level statement on explicit
complexes. The $(\infty,1)$-categorical CoHA of the
Kontsevich--Soibelman stack of objects in $\Perf(X)$ is a parallel
categorical construction with its own descent and representability
inputs. The finite DWR/Ran calculation is the surface on which the
hCS--Hall map is presently constructed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The four-stratum taxonomy}
\label{sec:four-stratum-taxonomy}

The finite Maurer--Cartan equation names the algebraic form of
gluing; geometry supplies the cochains entering that equation. The
known geometric sources of face-compatible homotopies sit in four
places.

\paragraph{The four strata from the automorphism tower.} Fix
$(X,\Omega_X)$. The available symmetry data lie in the tower
\[
 \mathrm{Aut}^{0}(X,\Omega_X)
 \;\lhd\;
 \mathrm{Aut}(X,\Omega_X)
 \;\lhd\;
 \mathrm{Aut}^{\widetilde{\phantom{X}}}(X,\Omega_X)
 \;\lhd\;
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{arith}}\bigl(\mathrm{Period}(X)\bigr).
\]
The rows are: continuous torus symmetry; a discrete automorphism of
$X$ itself; a finite symmetry visible only on a cover; and an
arithmetic symmetry of the period domain.

\emph{(i) Toric.} $\mathrm{Aut}^0(X,\Omega_X)$ contains a
volume-preserving torus $T^d=(\bC^\times)^d$. Examples are $\bC^3$,
the resolved conifold, local $\bP^2$, banana threefolds, and SPP.

\emph{(ii) Reduced torus times automorphism.}
$\mathrm{Aut}^0(X,\Omega_X)$ contains the $\Omega_X$-scaling
$\bC^\times$, and $\pi_0\mathrm{Aut}(X,\Omega_X)$ is non-trivial.
Examples are K3, $K3\times E$, abelian surfaces, and elliptic
fibrations with residual automorphism.

\emph{(iii) Orbifold inertia.} $X=[Y/G]$ with
$G\subset SU(d)$ finite acting on a smooth Calabi--Yau $Y$. The
inertia stack
\[
 I(X)=\coprod_{[g]\in G/\mathrm{conj}}Y^g/C_G(g)
\]
records the action after quotienting. Examples are
$[\bC^3/\bZ_n]$, $[\bC^3/G]$ with $G\subset SU(3)$ finite, and K3
with Mathieu-type twining data.

\emph{(iv) Lattice-polarised period.} The symmetry acts on the period
domain $\mathcal D_L$ attached to a polarisation lattice
$L\subset H^2(X,\bZ)$. The acting group is an arithmetic subgroup
$\Gamma\subset O(L\otimes\bR)$. Examples are K3 Humbert divisors,
$K3\times E$ via Gritsenko $\Delta_5$, and the unnormalised Igusa
square $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2$ in the stable $K3^{[n]}$
tower.

\begin{proposition}[The four strata exhaust the tower]
\label{prop:four-strata-exhaust}
A geometric source for DWR/Ran face homotopies acts in exactly one of
the four ways above: on the identity component of
$\mathrm{Aut}(X,\Omega_X)$, on the discrete components acting on $X$,
on a finite cover of $X$, or on the period domain. No fifth source is
obtained from automorphisms of $(X,\Omega_X)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A source acting on local charts acts either on $X$, on a cover
$Y\to X$, or on the period datum of $H^d(X,\bC)$. The first case splits
by connectedness: the identity component is stratum (i), while the
discrete component acting on $X$ is stratum (ii). The second case is
stratum (iii). In the third case, Deligne's monodromy theorem for
regular singular connections and Dolgachev's lattice-polarised K3
period map place integral period symmetries in an arithmetic subgroup,
which is stratum (iv). A symmetry outside these three loci acts on none
of the data entering a DWR/Ran simplex: not on its charts, not on a
cover, and not on the period class used to transport the coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Four-stratum sources for finite gluing]
\label{thm:four-stratum-structural}
Let a finite DWR/Ran Rees critical diagram on $(X,\mathfrak U)$ be
fixed. Every geometric cochain used to construct the relative maps
$\theta_\sigma^{\mathrm{rel}}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:descent-reduction} is supported in the strata
(i)--(iv). In the four cases its natural coefficient target is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\textup{stratum} & \textup{coefficient target}\\ \hline
\textup{(i)} &
H^1(X,\underline{\Pic^{T^d}})\\
\textup{(ii)} &
H^1(X/\Aut_s(X),\underline{\Aut_s(X)})\\
\textup{(iii)} &
H^1(I(X/G),\underline G)\\
\textup{(iv)} &
H^1(\mathcal D_L/\Gamma_{\mathrm{arith}},
\underline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{arith}}})
\end{array}
\]
For $X$ carrying several strata, these coefficient classes enter the
same convolution dg Lie algebra
$\mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)$; finite gluing is the
single Maurer--Cartan equation, not the independent vanishing of the
rows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In stratum (i), toric transition functions act on the quiver charts
and their determinant lines; Davison--Meinhardt dimensional reduction
and the torus-equivariant RSYZ construction place the resulting
cochain in the torus-equivariant Picard sheaf. In stratum (ii), a
residual volume-preserving automorphism transports chart
presentations and orientation lines, giving an
$\Aut_s(X)$-valued cochain on the quotient site. In stratum (iii), BKR
McKay equivalence transports the critical charts through the inertia
stack, hence gives the $G$-valued inertia cochain. In stratum (iv),
Borcherds and Gritsenko--Nikulin automorphic products transport the
period-domain coefficients through the arithmetic group.

These cochains do not define four separate Hall algebras. They modify
the face maps, orientation transports, and Thom--Sebastiani homotopies
in one finite DWR/Ran diagram. Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-gluing-mc} then
identifies compatibility of all rows with the single equation
$D_{\mathrm{tot}}\Theta+\frac12[\Theta,\Theta]=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Case-wise sources and uniform coefficient stacks]
\label{rem:bii-biv-scope}
For stratum (ii), K3 with Nikulin involution, abelian surfaces, and
local K3-fibrations supply concrete automorphism cochains. For stratum
(iv), each Borcherds lift in the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery catalogue supplies
its own arithmetic cochain. A uniform stack of all residual
$\Aut_s(X)$-cochains, or all Borcherds-lift cochains, requires a
separate stack presentation of the classifying sheaves.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Strata are sources, not the comparison]
\label{rem:part-a-scope}
A stratum supplies coefficients for face homotopies. It does not
produce the finite hCS--Hall map without the relative simplex maps,
the Maurer--Cartan correction, the vertex quasi-isomorphism datum, and
the orientation, grading, Thom--Sebastiani, and factorisation
transports. For generic compact Calabi--Yau threefolds such as the
quintic or Schoen bicubic, the absence of a toric, residual
automorphism, orbifold, or arithmetic source leaves precisely the
finite DWR/Ran primitive data of
Proposition~\ref{thm:global-coha-limit} to be constructed directly.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Overlap coherence inside the convolution complex]
\label{prop:overlap-coherence}
Let $S\subset\{(\text{i}),(\text{ii}),(\text{iii}),(\text{iv})\}$ be
the set of strata whose coefficient cochains are used on $X$. The
simultaneous gluing datum is a single element
\[
 \Theta_S\in
 \mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)^0
 \simeq
 \operatorname*{Tot}_{\sigma}
 \Hom_{\mathrm{cont}}
 \left(B\Obs_{\hCS}^{q,\le r,\le N}(\sigma)_{\le L},
 \Hall_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{Rees},\mathrm{or}}\right).
\]
For $|S|\ge2$, pairwise overlap compatibility is the vanishing of the
mixed part of
$D_{\mathrm{tot}}\Theta_S+\frac12[\Theta_S,\Theta_S]$ on two-stratum
faces. For $|S|\ge3$, triple compatibility is the corresponding
three-stratum component. Higher compatibility is already included
because the finite DWR/Ran totalisation ranges over all simplices of
arity $\le m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Theta_S=\sum_{s\in S}\Theta_s+\Theta_{\mathrm{mix}}$, where
the first summands are the pieces obtained from the individual
coefficient sources and $\Theta_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is supported on
overlaps of strata. The internal differential checks that each piece is
a chain map on its own coefficient system. The
$\delta_{\Cech/\Ran}$ term is exactly the alternating restriction to
faces where two or more coefficient systems meet. The convolution
bracket records incompatibility of the corresponding products and
Thom--Sebastiani transports. Thus every pairwise, triple, and higher
overlap condition is one component of the Maurer--Cartan curvature.
Vanishing of the curvature is precisely the claimed coherence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Strata overlap; the taxonomy is a power set]
\label{rem:strata-not-partition}
The four strata are not a partition of the Calabi--Yau
threefold landscape. A single $X$ can carry several coefficient
sources. Local $\bP^2$ occupies $\{(\text i),(\text{iii})\}$; K3
occupies $\{(\text{ii}),(\text{iii}),(\text{iv})\}$; and
$K3\times E$ occupies the same triple cell, with a latent toric source
on its local curve-stalk charts. The taxonomy is the subset lattice of
the four sources.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The 16 cells of the four-source lattice]
\label{rem:fifteen-cells}
The subset lattice
$\mathcal P(\{(\text{i}),(\text{ii}),(\text{iii}),(\text{iv})\})$ has
$16$ cells. The empty cell means that none of the four geometric
sources supplies face homotopies; the finite hCS--Hall comparison may
still be produced by a direct primitive construction, but not by this
taxonomy. A non-empty cell records the available coefficient sources,
not the completed critical CoHA. Completion and vanishing-cycle
realisation remain the two further arrows of
Proposition~\ref{thm:global-coha-limit}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Abstract framework in one paragraph}
\label{sec:gluing:abstract:summary}

The finite gluing object is the total complex over the bounded
DWR/Ran nerve
$\mathsf N^{\Ran}_{\le m}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\le L})$. A comparison
map is a degree-zero element of the convolution dg Lie algebra
$\mathfrak M_{\hCS,\Hall}^{N,r,L,m}(\mathfrak U)$ satisfying
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}\Theta+\frac12[\Theta,\Theta]=0.
\]
Face-compatible cyclic contractions over $\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p)$
produce the simplex maps; Stokes' formula turns their boundary terms
into the \v{C}ech/Ran differential. The four strata supply known
geometric coefficient sources for those face homotopies. They do not
replace the Maurer--Cartan solution, the inverse-limit completion, or
the monoidal vanishing-cycle realisation.

\S\ref{sec:gluing:toric-cech} realises the finite framework on the
toric source, beginning with the two-chart conifold.
