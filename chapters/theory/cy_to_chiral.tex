\chapter{From CY Categories to Chiral Algebras}
\label{ch:cy-to-chiral}

A Calabi--Yau category has a trace. A chiral algebra has an OPE. The trace is a map $\HH_d(\cC) \to \C$; the OPE is a distribution-valued product on sections over a punctured disc. No formal argument connects them: the trace is finite-dimensional Hochschild data, the OPE is an infinite-dimensional vertex structure on a curve. Volumes~I and~II accept a chiral algebra as given and extract its invariants: bar-cobar adjunction, the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, the shadow tower, the five theorems A--D+H. The geometric source of that algebra is left unspecified. Affine Kac--Moody algebras arise from flat connections, Virasoro from reparametrizations; every other chiral algebra in the standard landscape (conifold, Hilbert schemes of K3, resolved $A_n$ singularities, local threefolds with potential) must be supplied by hand. The question is: what functor connects CY categories to chiral algebras, and what structure must it preserve?

The answer requires four constructions, each with a precise structural role. A CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d$ carries a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure, a Connes $B$-operator, and an $\bS^d$-framing on Hochschild homology. None of these are chiral algebras. The Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ produces a Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$; the factorization envelope on a curve $X$ promotes $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$ to a factorization algebra; the $\bS^d$-framing provides the higher operadic enhancement; and the CY trace determines the quantization. The functor $\Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \to \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$ that results is the central object of this volume. It must preserve three things: the Hochschild data (the bar complex of $\Phi(\cC)$ recovers the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$), the modular characteristic ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC))$ equals the CY Euler characteristic $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$), and the operadic level (the $\bS^d$-framing produces an $E_n$-structure, not higher).

At $d = 2$, the functor is unconditional: the $\bS^2$-framing of Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos enhances $\Fact_X(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$ to an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra, and $\Phi$ is constructed end-to-end. At $d = 3$, a fundamental obstruction intervenes. The native $E_3$-structure on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ restricts to \emph{symmetric} braiding under Dunn additivity; the nonsymmetric quantum group braiding that physics demands can only be recovered through the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category. This chapter verifies the $d = 3$ chain end-to-end for $\C^3$, where both sides are independently known (CoHA$(\C^3) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ on the CY side, $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c=1$ on the chiral side), and sets up the quiver-chart gluing that assembles global $\Eone$-chiral algebras from local CoHA charts.

\section{The cyclic-to-chiral passage}
\label{sec:cyclic-to-chiral}
% ============================================================

A CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d$ carries a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure (Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}). The primary invariant is the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ with its $\bS^d$-framing. The passage to chiral algebras decomposes into four steps; each consumes a specific piece of the CY data and produces a specific algebraic structure:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.}]
 \item \textbf{Cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal algebra.} The Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is a graded Lie bracket of degree $-1$. The CY pairing (the nondegenerate trace $\HH_d(\cC) \to \C$) promotes this to a Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC$: the bracket becomes a $\lambda$-bracket, and the pairing becomes the invariant bilinear form. This step consumes the $\Ainf$-structure and the CY trace; it produces the ``current algebra'' of $\cC$.
 \item \textbf{Lie conformal algebra $\to$ factorization envelope.} The factorization envelope construction produces a factorization algebra $\Fact_X(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$ on any smooth curve $X$. This step consumes the Lie conformal algebra and the curve; it produces a cosheaf on $\Ran(X)$ whose sections are the OPE data.
 \item \textbf{$\bS^d$-framing $\to$ $E_n$ enhancement.} When $d = 2$, the $\bS^2$-framing of $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ (Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos) provides an $\Etwo$-algebra structure on the factorization algebra. This step consumes the framing; it produces the operadic enhancement that distinguishes a chiral algebra from a commutative factorization algebra.
 \item \textbf{Quantization.} The CY trace $\mathrm{tr} \colon \HH_d(\cC) \to \C$ determines a quantization of the classical factorization algebra. The quantum chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ is the output.
\end{enumerate}

For $d = 3$, Step~3 must be replaced: the $\bS^3$-framing gives an $E_3$-structure on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$, but the resulting $\Etwo$-braiding (obtained by restriction via Dunn additivity) is \emph{symmetric}. The nonsymmetric quantum group braiding is recovered by a different route: the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category (Section~\ref{sec:d3-drinfeld-center}).

\begin{theorem}[CY-to-chiral functor: Theorem CY-A$_2$, $d = 2$]
\label{thm:cy-to-chiral}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
There exists a functor
\[
 \Phi \colon \CY_2\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow \Etwo\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}
\]
from smooth proper CY categories of dimension $d = 2$ to $\Etwo$-chiral algebras, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\Phi(\cC)$ is a chiral algebra whose generating fields are in bijection with $\HH^{\bullet+1}(\cC)$;
 \item The bar complex $B(\Phi(\cC))$ is quasi-isomorphic to the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ as a factorization coalgebra.
\end{enumerate}
The $\Etwo$-enhancement in Step~3 uses the $\bS^2$-framing of $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ (Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos).
\end{theorem}

\begin{conjecture}[CY modular characteristic: Theorem CY-A$_2$(iii)]
\label{conj:cy-kappa-identification}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
% Status reconciliation: working notes (notes/theory_cy_to_chiral_construction.tex,
% Proposition~prop:cy-kappa) mark this ClaimHE (heuristic). The free-field argument
% gives kappa_ch = supertrace on generators = chi^CY, but the effect of quantization
% (Step 4) on kappa is not verified. Honest status: conjectured with heuristic evidence.
For $\cC$ a smooth proper CY category of dimension $d = 2$ and $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ the chiral algebra of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}},
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \;=\; \chi^{\CY}(\cC) \;\stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\; \sum_{i} (-1)^i \dim \HH_i(\cC).
\]
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Evidence for Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification}}]
\label{rem:cy-kappa-evidence}
The free-field (Fock space) argument: for $A_\cC$, the generating space is $\HH^{\bullet+1}(\cC)$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ equals the supertrace of the identity on the generating space, which is $\chi^{\CY}(\cC)$. This argument is valid provided the quantization in Step~4 does not introduce corrections to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$. The conjecture is verified computationally for K3 surfaces ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2 = \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{K3}})$) and abelian surfaces ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0 = \chi(\cO_A)$). For $d = 3$, the conjecture is expected to hold at the level of the categorical Euler characteristic (see Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}), but the $d = 3$ functor itself is conjectural beyond $\C^3$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Enriques surface: orbifold and kappa halving]
\label{rem:enriques-orbifold-kappa}
\ClaimStatusHeuristic{}
An Enriques surface $S$ is not a strict $\CY_2$ input in the sense of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}}, because $\omega_S$ is nontrivial $2$-torsion and the Serre functor on $D^b(\Coh(S))$ is $(-)\otimes \omega_S[2]$, not $[2]$. It is still the first orbifold test case next to the K3 example of Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:cy-kappa-evidence}}. Let $\pi \colon X \to S$ be the universal cover, where $X$ is a K3 surface and the deck involution acts freely. Then $\chi(\cO_X) = 2$ and $\chi(\cO_S) = 1$, so the K3 value $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$ suggests the orbifold value $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ if $\Phi$ extends to this torsion-canonical quotient surface.

Descent identifies $D^b(\Coh(S))$ with the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-equivariant derived category of the cover, so one expects an orbifold relation
\[
 \Phi\bigl(D^b(\Coh(S))\bigr) \,\simeq\, \Phi\bigl(D^b(\Coh(X))\bigr)^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
\]
for a suitable equivariant factorization-envelope construction. At the scalar level this predicts the halving principle
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^G) \,=\, \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)}{|G|},
\]
the chiral shadow of the classical formula $\chi(\cO_S) = \chi(\cO_X)/|G|$ for a free finite quotient. In the Vol~I Igusa normalization, this same orbifold picture suggests replacing the K3 lift $\Phi_{10}$ by a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-twisted lift of weight $5$ on the quotient side. That weight drop gives heuristic evidence for $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ halving on the Borcherds side, even though the precise Enriques automorphic normalization is a separate question.

Turning this into a theorem requires an orbifold extension of the functor $\Phi$, equivariant descent for factorization envelopes, and the twisted Borcherds-lift technology needed to compare the quotient with its K3 cover.
\end{remark}


\begin{conjecture}[$\Phi$ respects $\bZ/2$-orbifold structure for Enriques]
\label{conj:phi-enriques-z2-orbifold}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
Let $X$ be a K3 surface with a fixed-point-free involution $\iota$, and let $S = X/\iota$ be the associated Enriques surface. Suppose the functor $\Phi$ of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}} extends to an equivariant setting (i.e.\ to smooth proper categories with $\bZ/2$-action whose Serre functor is $2$-torsion rather than trivial). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item There is an equivalence of chiral algebras
 \[
 \Phi\bigl(D^b(\Coh(S))\bigr) \;\simeq\; \Phi\bigl(D^b(\Coh(X))\bigr)^{\bZ/2},
 \]
 where the right-hand side denotes the $\iota^*$-invariant subalgebra of the K3 chiral algebra.
 \item The $\bZ/2$-invariant subalgebra inherits the structure of an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra from the ambient K3 algebra. The $\Eone$-bar complex satisfies
 \[
 B\bigl(\Phi(D^b(\Coh(S)))\bigr) \;\simeq\; B\bigl(\Phi(D^b(\Coh(X)))\bigr)^{\bZ/2}
 \]
 as factorization coalgebras.
 \item The full $\bZ/2$-orbifold chiral algebra $\Phi(D^b(\Coh(X))) \rtimes \bZ/2$ (including the twisted sector) carries a larger shadow tower whose invariant subalgebra recovers $\Phi(D^b(\Coh(S)))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Evidence and scope for Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:phi-enriques-z2-orbifold}}]
\label{rem:enriques-z2-evidence}
\ClaimStatusHeuristic{}
Three lines of evidence support the conjecture.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
 \item \emph{Derived category descent.} The functor $\pi^* \colon D^b(\Coh(S)) \hookrightarrow D^b(\Coh(X))$ identifies $D^b(\Coh(S))$ with the $\iota^*$-invariant subcategory of $D^b(\Coh(X))$. (For a free action, this is the content of equivariant descent; see Bridgeland--King--Reid for the general framework.) If $\Phi$ commutes with taking $G$-invariants, item~(i) follows.
 \item \emph{Hochschild homology halving.} The Hochschild homology of the Enriques surface satisfies $\dim \HH_0(S) = 1$, $\dim \HH_1(S) = 0$, $\dim \HH_2(S) = 0$, giving $\chi^{\CY}(S) = 1 - 0 + 0 = 1$. (Here $\HH_2(S) = H^0(\omega_S) = 0$ because $\omega_S$ is nontrivial $2$-torsion; the torsion in $H^1(S, \bZ) = \bZ/2$ does not contribute to Hochschild homology over $\bC$.) This is half the K3 value $\chi^{\CY}(X) = 1 - 0 + 1 = 2$, consistent with the halving principle $\chi^{\CY}(S) = \chi^{\CY}(X)/|G|$ for the free $\bZ/2$-quotient. The precise relationship is: $\HH_\bullet(S) \simeq \HH_\bullet(X)^{\bZ/2}$ as graded vector spaces, where $\iota^*$ acts as $+1$ on $\HH_0(X) = H^0(\cO_X)$ and as $-1$ on $\HH_2(X) = H^0(\omega_X)$ (since $\iota^* \omega_X = -\omega_X$), so the invariant piece $\HH_2(X)^{\bZ/2} = 0$ recovers $\HH_2(S) = 0$.
 \item \emph{Chiral de Rham.} The chiral de Rham complex $\Omega^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is defined on any smooth variety. For the Enriques surface, $H^*(\Omega^{\mathrm{ch}}(S)) \simeq H^*(\Omega^{\mathrm{ch}}(X))^{\bZ/2}$ by functoriality of the chiral de Rham sheaf under \'etale morphisms. This gives the chiral-algebra-level analogue of item~(i) at the level of vertex algebra cohomology.
\end{enumerate}
The principal obstruction is Step~4 of the cyclic-to-chiral passage (Section~\ref{sec:cyclic-to-chiral}): the CY trace on $D^b(\Coh(S))$ is $2$-torsion, so the quantization requires a $\bZ/2$-equivariant refinement.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Enriques $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-spectrum]
\label{conj:enriques-kappa-spectrum}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
Let $S$ be an Enriques surface, $X$ its K3 universal cover, $E$ an elliptic curve, and assume Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:phi-enriques-z2-orbifold}} and the extension of $\Phi$ to generalized CY$_3$ inputs. Then the three modular characteristics satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(S) = 1 = \chi(\cO_S)$, half the K3 value $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(X) = 2$.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(S \times E) = 4$, the weight of the Allcock Borcherds product on $O(2, 10)$. This is verified computationally by \texttt{enriques\_shadow.py} (72 tests; see Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:enriques-kappa-anomaly}} in the bar-cobar bridge chapter).
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(S \times E) = \chi^{\CY}(D^b(\Coh(S \times E)))$, the categorical Euler characteristic. By additivity (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}), $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(S \times E) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(S) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 1 + 1 = 2$.
\end{enumerate}
The ratio $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(X \times E) / \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(S \times E) = 5/4$ (not $2$) reflects the fact that $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ is the automorphic weight, which is sensitive to the full BPS spectrum across the fiber and not simply the $|G|$-fold quotient of the scalar invariant. The discrepancy $5/4 \neq 2$ is the \emph{Enriques $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-anomaly}: the BKM weight does not halve under the $\bZ/2$ quotient because the Borcherds product on $O(2, 10)$ is not the restriction of the Igusa cusp form on $O(2, 18)$ but rather an independent automorphic form (the Allcock product) whose weight is determined by the Enriques lattice.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[BKM lattice structure: Enriques vs.\ K3]
\label{rem:enriques-bkm-lattice}
\ClaimStatusHeuristic{}
The lattice-theoretic origin of the $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-anomaly is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
 \item The K3 lattice is $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}} = U^3 \oplus E_8(-1)^2$, of rank $22$ and signature $(3, 19)$. The BKM algebra for $\mathrm{K3} \times E$ is constructed via the Borcherds lift on $O(2, 18)$ (the orthogonal complement of a hyperbolic plane in $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}} \oplus U$), and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5$.
 \item The Enriques lattice is $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}} = U \oplus E_8(-1)$, of rank $10$ and signature $(1, 9)$. The involution $\iota$ acts on $H^2(X, \bZ)$ with invariant sublattice $U(2) \oplus E_8(-2)$ (the $\iota$-invariant part of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$, rescaled by the index-$2$ inclusion). The BKM algebra for $\mathrm{Enr} \times E$ is constructed via the Borcherds lift on $O(2, 10)$ (from $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}} \oplus U$), and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 4$ (the Allcock product weight).
 \item The embedding $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}} \hookrightarrow \Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$ (the inclusion of the $\iota$-invariant sublattice, up to rescaling) induces a restriction map on automorphic forms. This restriction does \emph{not} send $\Delta_5$ on $O(2, 18)$ to the Allcock product on $O(2, 10)$: the two forms live on orthogonal groups of different rank, and the restriction involves a Fourier--Jacobi expansion along the complementary lattice $E_8(-1)$ that produces a form of weight $4$, not $5$. The weight drop by $1$ (from $5$ to $4$) reflects the $E_8(-1)$ theta-function contribution of weight $4$ in the Fourier--Jacobi coefficient.
 \item The BKM root systems differ accordingly. The K3 BKM superalgebra has root lattice containing $\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}$; the Enriques BKM superalgebra has root lattice containing $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}}$. The real simple roots of the Enriques BKM algebra are the $(-2)$-vectors of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}}$, forming the Vinberg diagram of the Enriques automorphism group.
\end{enumerate}
The $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-anomaly ($5/4$ rather than $2$) is therefore a lattice-theoretic phenomenon: the BKM weight is controlled by the rank and root structure of the relevant even lattice, not by the index of the covering map.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Enriques automorphic forms and moonshine]
\label{rem:enriques-automorphic}
\ClaimStatusHeuristic{}
The automorphic side of the Enriques story involves the following objects.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
 \item The \emph{Allcock Borcherds product} is an automorphic form of weight $4$ on $O(2, 10)$ with known divisor (the Heegner divisors of norm $-2$ vectors in $\Lambda_{\mathrm{Enr}} \oplus U$). Its square root (a form of weight $2$ on a double cover) is related to the Enriques period map.
 \item Gritsenko and Nikulin constructed a ``Enriques modular form'' of weight $4$ on $O(2, 10)$ using a quasi-pullback of the Borcherds form $\Phi_{12}$ on $O(2, 26)$. This is the same object as the Allcock product, identified through different techniques.
 \item In the moonshine direction: the Mathieu group $M_{12}$ acts on the Enriques lattice (Mukai, Kondo), paralleling the $M_{24}$ action on the K3 lattice. The ``Enriques moonshine'' question asks whether the $\bZ/2$-twisted elliptic genus of the K3 sigma model (which computes the Enriques partition function) decomposes into $M_{12}$ representations in a manner analogous to Mathieu moonshine for K3. Gaberdiel--Hohenegger--Volpato have shown that the symmetry group of Enriques sigma models embeds in $O(10, \bF_2)^+$, and partial decompositions into $M_{12}$ characters exist.
 \item From the $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$-spectrum perspective: the weight-$4$ Allcock product determines $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathrm{Enr} \times E) = 4$. If Enriques moonshine produces a weight-$4$ mock modular form (the twisted analogue of the K3 mock modular form $H(\tau)$ of weight $1/2$ that underlies Mathieu moonshine), the shadow of that form would encode the Enriques BKM denominator, connecting the automorphic weight to the chiral shadow tower via the general mechanism of Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:enriques-kappa-spectrum}}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}


\begin{remark}[Five objects of the CY Koszul programme]
\label{rem:v3-five-objects-cy}
\ClaimStatusHeuristic{}
% FOUR OBJECTS:
% 1. A = Phi(C), the chiral algebra
% 2. B(A) = T^c(s^{-1} A-bar) with deconcatenation coproduct
% 3. A^i = H^*(B(A)), the dual coalgebra
% 4. A^! = ((A^i)^v), the dual algebra (Koszul dual)
% 5. Z^der_ch(A) = derived chiral center = bulk
The five algebraic objects of the Koszul programme (Vol~I) transport to the CY setting via the functor~$\Phi$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
 \item $A = \Phi(\cC)$, the chiral algebra of the CY category~$\cC$.
 \item $B(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{A})$, the bar coalgebra with deconcatenation coproduct. Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}}(ii) identifies $B(\Phi(\cC))$ with the cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ as a factorization coalgebra (proved for $d = 2$; conjectural for $d = 3$).
 \item $A^i = H^*(B(A))$, the dual coalgebra (bar cohomology of the chiral algebra).
 \item $A^! = (A^i)^\vee$, the dual algebra (Koszul dual). For $\cC = \Coh(\C^3)$, the Koszul dual is the Yangian $Y_\hbar(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$.
 \item $\Zder(A)$, the derived chiral center (bulk). In the CY setting, the derived center connects to the BPS algebra via the holographic datum (Section~\ref{sec:d3-functor-chain}).
\end{enumerate}
These five objects are distinct: $B(A)$ is a coalgebra, $A^!$ is an algebra, and the cobar recovery $\Omega(B(A)) \simeq A$ is \emph{inversion}, not Koszul duality. The derived center $\Zder(A)$ arises from Hochschild cochains, not from bar-cobar.
\end{remark}

At $d = 2$, the functor $\Phi$ is now in hand (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}), and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Phi(\cC)) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ is conjectured with computational evidence at K3 and abelian surfaces (Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification}). At $d = 3$, Step~3 of the cyclic-to-chiral passage breaks: the $\bS^3$-framing produces symmetric braiding under Dunn restriction, and symmetry is the wrong answer. Physics demands nonsymmetric braiding (the Yang $R$-matrix, the Yangian coproduct), and the only known route to it passes through the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category. The remainder of this chapter develops the $d = 3$ programme, beginning with the one case where both sides are independently known.

\section{The $d = 3$ functor chain for $\C^3$}
\label{sec:d3-functor-chain}
% ============================================================

Affine space $X = \C^3$ is the unique $d = 3$ geometry where both sides of the functor are independently known. The CY side produces $\CoHA(\C^3) \simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (Kontsevich--Soibelman, Schiffmann--Vasserot); the chiral side produces $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c = 1$ (Proch\'azka--Rap\v{c}\'ak). The task is to verify that $\Phi$ connects them.


\subsection{The five-step chain}
\label{subsec:five-step-chain}

The passage from CY data to quantum group for $\C^3$ factors into five steps, each with a concrete input, a concrete output, and a concrete verification:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{rl}
\textbf{Step 1.} & $\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3)$ with $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket \\[3pt]
& $\big\downarrow$ \quad $\Omega$-deformation in the $\sigma_3$ direction \\[3pt]
\textbf{Step 2.} & Quantized Lie conformal algebra \\[3pt]
& $\big\downarrow$ \quad Factorization envelope (Nishinaka--Vicedo) \\[3pt]
\textbf{Step 3.} & $\cW_{1+\infty}$ with nonabelian OPE \\[3pt]
& $\big\downarrow$ \quad Drinfeld center \\[3pt]
\textbf{Step 4.} & $\Etwo$-braided category $\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ with $R$-matrix \\[3pt]
& $\big\downarrow$ \quad Quantum group extraction \\[3pt]
\textbf{Step 5.} & $\Etwo$-braided category identified with $\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The input at Step~1 is $\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3) = \bigoplus_{p=0}^{3} \Gamma(\C^3, \bigwedge^p T_{\C^3})$, the algebra of polyvector fields with the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket. The $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant sector carries the deformation-theoretic data needed for the CY-to-chiral functor. The $\Omega$-deformation at Step~2 is parametrized by $\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$ (with $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$), the unique direction in $\HH^2(\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3))$. At the self-dual level ($\sigma_3 \to 0$, giving $h_1 = 1, h_2 = 0, h_3 = -1$), the output at Step~3 is $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c = 1$, which \emph{is} the Heisenberg VOA $H_1$.

The classical Lie conformal algebra is \emph{abelian} (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}): every noncommutative structure in $\cW_{1+\infty}$ arises from quantization in the envelope step, not from the input bracket. The CY$_3$ functor produces a quantum algebra from classically commutative input. Each of the five steps is verified independently; the theorem below assembles them.


\begin{theorem}[The $d = 3$ functor chain is verified for $\C^3$]
\label{thm:c3-functor-chain}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Each step of the five-step chain is verified computationally:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Step~1}: The $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets vanish identically (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}}). The input Lie conformal algebra is abelian.
 \item \emph{Step~2}: $\HH^2(\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3)) = 1$, so the deformation is unique, spanned by $\sigma_3$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-hochschild}}). $\HH^3 = 0$ by Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov, so the deformation is unobstructed.
 \item \emph{Step~3}: The factorization envelope produces $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c = 1$. The OPE arises entirely from the central extension and normal ordering in the envelope, not from the classical bracket.
 \item \emph{Step~4}: The Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))$ is identified with $\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}}).
 \item \emph{Step~5}: The $\Etwo$-braided representation category is identified with $\Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$; the full global-group object $G(\C^3)$ of Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}} is AP-CY7 territory and sits beyond the scope of this theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: $\sim$600 tests across six compute modules: \texttt{c3\_lie\_conformal.py}, \texttt{c3\_envelope\_comparison.py}, \texttt{cy3\_hochschild.py}, \texttt{drinfeld\_center\_yangian.py}, \texttt{c3\_grand\_verification.py}, \texttt{cy\_to\_chiral\_functor.py}.
\end{theorem}


\begin{remark}[The shuffle algebra does not translate directly to the $\lambda$-bracket]
\label{rem:shuffle-no-direct-lambda}
The shuffle algebra presentation of $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ has structure function $g(z) = (z - h_1)(z - h_2)(z - h_3)/((z + h_1)(z + h_2)(z + h_3))$. A natural attempt extracts a $\lambda$-bracket from the shuffle product via $z \mapsto \lambda$. This fails: $g(0) = -1$ (regular, not singular), so the shuffle product does not produce a distribution-valued bracket. The envelope step (Step~2 $\to$ Step~3) replaces the algebraic $g(z)$ by the \emph{vertex operator} OPE, where the $\delta$-function singularity of the state-field correspondence generates the $\lambda$-bracket. The passage shuffle $\to$ vertex algebra is the step where locality enters.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Abelianity of the classical bracket}
\label{subsec:abelian-bracket}

\begin{theorem}[Abelianity of the classical bracket]
\label{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets on $\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3)$ all vanish. Three independent proofs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Direct computation}: every bracket $[\alpha, \beta]_{\mathrm{SN}}$ of $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant polyvector fields evaluates to zero by explicit contraction.
 \item \emph{Graded skew-symmetry + degree count}: in the Gerstenhaber grading (shifted degree $|\alpha|_s = |\alpha| - 1$), the generators $E \in \mathrm{PV}^1$ and $\partial_1 \wedge \partial_2 \wedge \partial_3 \in \mathrm{PV}^3$ have even shifted degree ($0$ and $2$ respectively), so $[\alpha, \alpha] = 0$ for these by graded skew-symmetry. The remaining generators $1 \in \mathrm{PV}^0$ and $\omega^{-1} \in \mathrm{PV}^2$ have odd shifted degree, but $[1, -]_{\mathrm{SN}} = 0$ identically (constants are central) and $[\omega^{-1}, \omega^{-1}]_{\mathrm{SN}} \in \mathrm{PV}^3$ must be $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant, hence proportional to the trivector; explicit computation gives zero. All mixed brackets $[\alpha, \beta]$ with $\alpha \neq \beta$ vanish by the degree-overflow argument of~(iii).
 \item \emph{Weight/exterior degree overflow}: the bracket lowers exterior degree by~$1$; any nontrivial $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant in the target degree must have weight exceeding the polynomial degree available.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 95 tests in \texttt{c3\_lie\_conformal.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We give proof~(i) in detail; proofs~(ii) and~(iii) are recorded for cross-verification.

The $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant polyvector fields on $\C^3$ are spanned by the constant function $1 \in \mathrm{PV}^0$, the Euler vector field $E = \sum_i z_i \partial_i \in \mathrm{PV}^1$, the Poisson bivector $\pi = \sum_{\mathrm{cyc}} z_3\, \partial_1 \wedge \partial_2 \in \mathrm{PV}^2$ (contraction of $E$ with the volume trivector), and the trivector $\partial_1 \wedge \partial_2 \wedge \partial_3 \in \mathrm{PV}^3$. The Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket has bidegree $(-1)$: it maps $\mathrm{PV}^p \times \mathrm{PV}^q \to \mathrm{PV}^{p+q-1}$. For any two $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant generators $\alpha \in \mathrm{PV}^p$, $\beta \in \mathrm{PV}^q$, the bracket $[\alpha, \beta]_{\mathrm{SN}}$ must be $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant of degree $p + q - 1$. In each case, either $p + q - 1 > 3$ (forcing zero by degree overflow) or the explicit contraction vanishes by the symmetry of the invariant generators.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Hochschild cohomology and the unique deformation}
\label{subsec:hochschild-unique-deformation}

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild cohomology and unique deformation]
\label{thm:c3-hochschild}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For $\C^3$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $\HH^2(\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3)) = 1$: the deformation space is one-dimensional, spanned by the $\sigma_3$ direction. The unique deformation is the $\Omega$-deformation.
 \item $\HH^3(\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3)) = 0$: the Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov theorem guarantees unobstructedness.
 \item The quantized algebra is $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c = 1$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 71 tests in \texttt{cy3\_hochschild.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem identifies $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3))$ with the $\mathrm{GL}(3)$-invariant polyvector fields on the formal neighbourhood of the origin in the moduli space of $\Ainf$-deformations. For $\C^3$ with the standard CY structure, the relevant computation reduces to the $T^3$-equivariant sector.

The equivariant parameters $h_1, h_2, h_3$ with $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ span a two-dimensional space. The unique $T^3$-equivariant deformation class in $\HH^2$ is $\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$, the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $3$. The class $\sigma_2 = h_1 h_2 + h_2 h_3 + h_3 h_1 = -(h_1^2+h_2^2+h_3^2)/2$ on the constraint locus is generically nonzero, but it generates a trivial deformation equivalent to rescaling: the $\sigma_2$-deformation acts as a conformal weight shift that can be absorbed by redefining the grading. The nontrivial deformation class is $\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$, which cannot be removed by regrading.

The vanishing $\HH^3 = 0$ is the Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov unobstructedness theorem for the CY$_3$ moduli problem: the Kodaira--Spencer dga of $\C^3$ is formal, and the Maurer--Cartan moduli space is smooth. This guarantees that the $\Omega$-deformation determined by $\sigma_3$ extends to all orders.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Necessity of the $\Omega$-deformation for noncompact CY$_3$}
\label{subsec:omega-necessary}

\begin{proposition}[Necessity of $\Omega$-deformation for noncompact CY$_3$]
\label{prop:omega-necessary}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Without the $\Omega$-deformation, the CY$_3$ functor chain for $\C^3$ collapses: the Lie conformal algebra is abelian (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}}), the envelope produces the free Heisenberg, and the $\Etwo$ structure is trivially $\Einf$ (symmetric monoidal). Noncompactness forces the introduction of external data: $T^3$-equivariant structure plus $\bS^3$-framing. For compact CY$_3$, neither should be needed: the nontrivial global geometry plays the role of the $\Omega$-deformation.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 87 tests in \texttt{c3\_envelope\_comparison.py}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $\sigma_3 = 0$ (equivalently, one of $h_1, h_2, h_3$ vanishes), the factorization envelope of the abelian Lie conformal algebra is the free Heisenberg vertex algebra $H_1$. The representation category $\Rep(H_1)$ is symmetric monoidal: the braiding on Fock modules is the identity (all monodromy is trivial for a free boson at level~$1$). In particular, $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(H_1)) = \Rep(H_1)$ itself (the Drinfeld center of a symmetric monoidal category is the category itself), so no $\Etwo$-enhancement occurs.

When $\sigma_3 \neq 0$, the $\Omega$-deformation introduces the nonlinear OPE terms of $\cW_{1+\infty}$. These terms break the $\Einf$ symmetry to $\Etwo$, and the Drinfeld center produces a nontrivially braided category with the Yang $R$-matrix (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}).

For a \emph{compact} CY$_3$ such as the quintic, the global sections of the polyvector sheaf have nontrivial bracket (the complex structure moduli introduce genuine noncommutativity), and the $\Omega$-deformation is not needed. The role of $\sigma_3$ for $\C^3$ is to \emph{replace} the global geometric data that is absent for a noncompact variety.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Smooth vs singular: locality of the quantization]
\label{rem:smooth-singular-locality}
The distinction between smooth and singular CY$_3$ is categorical:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{CY$_3$} & \textbf{Quantization} & \textbf{Algebraic type} \\
 \midrule
 Smooth ($\C^3$, quintic, $K3 \times E$) & Vertex algebra (local) & $\Eone$-chiral ($\Etwo$ via Drinfeld center) \\
 Singular (conifold) & Associative algebra (nonlocal) & $\Eone$-chiral \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Smoothness is not a matter of convention: the factorization envelope requires local data (the polyvector fields form a cosheaf whose sections over small discs control the OPE), and singularities obstruct the local-to-global passage. At a singularity, the category $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ acquires compact objects that do not extend to a neighbourhood, and the factorization structure degenerates from $\Etwo$ to $\Eone$.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 71 tests in \texttt{cy3\_hochschild.py} (testing smooth vs singular HH data).
\end{remark}

\section{The $\bS^3$-framing obstruction: universal triviality}
\label{sec:s3-framing}
% ============================================================

The chain-level $\bS^3$-framing is the central obstruction to CY-A at $d = 3$ (the topological obstruction vanishes by Theorem~\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}; the remaining obstruction is chain-level $A_\infty$-compatibility). Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3} requires (a)~constructing this framing compatibly with BV structure and (b)~establishing the quantization step. The following result removes condition~(a).


\begin{theorem}[Vanishing of the $\bS^3$-framing obstruction for CY$_3$ categories]
\label{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The topological $\bS^3$-framing obstruction vanishes universally for CY$_3$ categories. Two independent proofs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Symplectic path.} The CY$_3$ pairing on the Ext complex $\Ext^\bullet_\cC(E, E)$ is antisymmetric (by the Serre functor with $\omega_X \cong \cO_X$), giving structure group $\Sp(2m)$ for $\dim \Ext^1 = 2m$. Since $\pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = 0$ for all $m \geq 1$, the obstruction class in $\pi_3$ vanishes.
 \item \emph{Bott periodicity path.} The complex structure gives a reduction $\mathrm{GL}(n, \C) \to U(n)$. By Bott periodicity, $\pi_3(BU) = 0$ (the homotopy groups of $BU$ are $\pi_{2k}(BU) = \Z$, $\pi_{2k+1}(BU) = 0$). Since $\pi_3$ is odd, the obstruction vanishes.
\end{enumerate}
The chain-level BV obstruction $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot [\Omega_3]$ is trivializable via holomorphic Chern--Simons (Witten~1992, Costello--Li~2016): the functional $\mathrm{CS}(\bar{\partial} + A) = \int_X \Omega \wedge \mathrm{tr}(A \wedge \bar{\partial}A + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A)$ provides the contracting homotopy.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 124 tests in \texttt{cy\_to\_chiral\_functor.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We give the symplectic path in detail.

The CY$_3$ condition $\omega_X \cong \cO_X$ implies the existence of a nondegenerate trace $\mathrm{tr} \colon \HH_3(\cC) \to \C$, which by Serre duality induces an antisymmetric pairing
\[
 \langle -, - \rangle \colon \Ext^i(E, E) \otimes \Ext^{3-i}(E, E) \longrightarrow \C.
\]
At $i = 1$, this is an antisymmetric form on the tangent space to the moduli of objects, reducing the structure group from $\mathrm{GL}(2m)$ to $\Sp(2m)$. At $i = 0$, the self-pairing $\Ext^0 \otimes \Ext^3 \to \C$ is the Serre duality pairing on endomorphisms.

The $\bS^3$-framing obstruction lives in $\pi_3$ of the classifying space of the structure group. For the symplectic group:
\[
 \pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = \pi_2(\Sp(2m)) = 0 \quad \text{for all } m \geq 1.
\]
The second equality uses the long exact sequence of the fibration $\Sp(2m) \to B\Sp(2m)$ and the fact that $\Sp(2m)$ is simply connected ($\pi_1(\Sp(2m)) = 0$) with $\pi_2(\Sp(2m)) = 0$ (all compact simply-connected simple Lie groups have vanishing $\pi_2$).

For the Bott periodicity path: the Bott periodicity theorem gives $\pi_k(BU) \cong \pi_k(BU(n))$ for $n$ large, with $\pi_{2k}(BU) = \Z$ and $\pi_{2k+1}(BU) = 0$. Since $3$ is odd, $\pi_3(BU) = 0$.

The chain-level BV obstruction requires more than topological triviality: one needs an explicit trivialization compatible with the $\BV$ operator $\Delta = \partial/\partial \Omega_3$. The holomorphic Chern--Simons functional provides this. The CS action is a trivialization of $[\Omega_3]$ because $\delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(\mathrm{CS}) = \int \Omega \wedge F_A$, and the flatness equation $F_A = 0$ is the Maurer--Cartan equation of the B-model. This means the BV obstruction is exact in the BRST cohomology.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[Topological vanishing of the CY-A$_3$ obstruction]
\label{cor:cya3-no-topological-obstruction}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}
Assuming Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}} is formulated with symplectic structure group (i.e., the $d = 3$ CY-to-chiral functor targets a CY$_3$ chiral algebra whose obstruction theory sits in $\pi_3(B\Sp(2m))$), the topological component of that obstruction vanishes. The remaining chain-level construction of the trivialization of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot [\Omega_3]$ is known to exist by holomorphic Chern--Simons, but its compatibility with the full $\Ainf$-structure is part of CY-A$_3$ itself and remains conjectural. For the toric CY$_3$ verified in compute ($\C^3$, conifold, local $\bP^2$, local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$, $K3 \times E$), the $\Eone \to \Etwo$ enhancement obstruction vanishes at the level of the explicit CoHA construction.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 124 tests in \texttt{cy\_to\_chiral\_functor.py}; 93 tests in \texttt{drinfeld\_center\_yangian.py}.
\end{corollary}


\subsection{The \v{C}ech contracting homotopy for compact CY$_3$}
\label{subsec:cech-contracting-homotopy}

The topological vanishing of the $\bS^3$-framing obstruction
(Theorem~\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}) leaves open the chain-level
construction of the trivialization. For compact CY$_3$ hypersurfaces
in projective space, the standard affine cover provides an algebraic
\v{C}ech contracting homotopy that closes this gap at the perturbative level.

\begin{theorem}[\v{C}ech contracting homotopy for compact CY$_3$;
\ClaimStatusProvedHere]
\label{thm:cech-contracting-homotopy}
For the quintic threefold $X_5 \subset \bP^4$ with the standard affine
cover $\{U_i = \{x_i \neq 0\}\}_{i=0}^4$, the algebraic \v{C}ech
contracting homotopy $s^q \colon \check{C}^q \to \check{C}^{q-1}$
\textup{(}prepend the distinguished index~$0$\textup{)} satisfies the
SDR conditions
\[
 \delta s + s \delta = \mathrm{Id} - i \circ p, \qquad
 s^2 = 0, \qquad
 s \circ i = 0, \qquad
 p \circ s = 0,
\]
where $i \colon H^*(\check{C}^\bullet) \hookrightarrow \check{C}^\bullet$
is the inclusion of cohomology and $p$ is the projection. The contracting
homotopy is purely algebraic: it acts by
$s(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) = f_{0 i_0 \cdots i_q}$ on \v{C}ech cochains and
involves no PDE solving. The SDR data $(i, p, s)$ interfaces directly
with the homotopy transfer theorem
\textup{(}HTT, Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:htt-general}}\textup{)},
transferring the $\Ainf$-structure from $\check{C}^\bullet(\cE nd(\cE))$
to its cohomology $\Ext^\bullet(\cE, \cE)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The \v{C}ech complex of a quasi-coherent sheaf on $\bP^4$ with respect
to the standard affine cover $\{U_i\}_{i=0}^4$ is acyclic in positive
\v{C}ech degree (Leray's theorem: each finite intersection
$U_{i_0} \cap \cdots \cap U_{i_q}$ is affine, and affine varieties
have vanishing higher cohomology for quasi-coherent sheaves).
The homotopy $s^q(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) := f_{0 i_0 \cdots i_q}$
satisfies $(\delta s + s \delta)(f) = f - f|_{U_0}$ for
$q \geq 1$, giving $\delta s + s \delta = \mathrm{Id} - i \circ p$.
The annihilation conditions $s^2 = 0$, $s \circ i = 0$,
$p \circ s = 0$ follow from the index~$0$ convention:
$s^2(f_{i_0 \cdots i_q}) = s(f_{0 i_0 \cdots i_q})
= f_{00 i_0 \cdots i_q} = 0$ by the alternating sign rule.
The same argument applies to the restriction $X_5 \hookrightarrow \bP^4$:
the induced cover $\{U_i \cap X_5\}$ consists of affine open sets
(hyperplane complements of a smooth hypersurface in projective space
are affine), so Leray's theorem applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Perturbative vs non-perturbative scope]
\label{rem:cech-perturbative-scope}
The \v{C}ech homotopy closes the analytic gap at the level of formal
(perturbative) deformations: it provides a chain-level contracting
homotopy that is algebraic and requires no PDE solving. The
non-perturbative convergence of the resulting chiral operations
(whether the formal series in the OPE coefficients converges to
define honest holomorphic functions) requires separate input from
the analytic completion programme (MC5,
\S\ref{sec:analytic-sewing} of Vol~I). Specifically: the HTT
produces transferred operations $\mu_k^{\mathrm{ch}}$ whose
coefficients involve iterated applications of~$s$; each application
introduces rational functions on the intersections $U_{i_0} \cap
\cdots \cap U_{i_q}$, and the convergence of the resulting series
in the OPE variable $z$ is not guaranteed by the algebraic
construction alone. The perturbative statement is: the $\Ainf$
operations on $\Ext^\bullet(\cE, \cE)$ are well-defined as formal
power series. The non-perturbative statement (convergence to
holomorphic functions) requires estimates on the growth of the
\v{C}ech homotopy applied to OPE kernels.
\end{remark}

The following two computations illustrate the chain-level chiral
structure for concrete CY$_3$ geometries, distinguishing formal
from non-formal behaviour.

\begin{computation}[Chain-level chiral structure for the conifold]
\label{comp:conifold-chain-level}
The resolved conifold
$X = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1)$
has $\Ext^\bullet(\cE, \cE)$ with $12$ generators distributed
as $2 + 4 + 4 + 2$ by Ext degree. The Euler characteristic is
$\chi = 2 - 4 + 4 - 2 = 0$ (CY$_3$ check: $\sum (-1)^i \dim
\Ext^i = 0$). The $\Ainf$-algebra is \emph{formal}: the
transferred operations satisfy $m_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$
(Kaledin's theorem: the derived category of the resolved conifold
is intrinsically formal as a CY$_3$ category, since it is derived
equivalent to $\C Q / (\partial W)$ for the Klebanov--Witten
quiver with quadratic potential). The chiral Jacobi identity
$\sum_{\sigma \in S_3} (-1)^{|\sigma|} \mu_2^{\mathrm{ch}}
(\mu_2^{\mathrm{ch}}(a_{\sigma(1)}, a_{\sigma(2)}; z_{12});
a_{\sigma(3)}; z_{13}) = 0$ is verified through total degree~$6$
in the generators. The shadow tower is class~$\mathbf{G}$
($r_{\max} = 2$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$), consistent with the formality.
\end{computation}

\begin{computation}[Non-formality for local $\bP^2$]
\label{comp:local-p2-nonformality}
The local $\bP^2$ geometry
$X = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-3) \to \bP^2)$ has $24$ generators
from the McKay quiver of $\bZ_3$: three vertices with $8$
generators each (the McKay correspondence assigns the regular
representation $\C[\bZ_3] = \rho_0 \oplus \rho_1 \oplus \rho_2$
to the vertices, and the tensor product with the defining
$\SL(3)$-representation determines the arrows).
The algebra is \emph{not} formal: the transferred ternary
operation is
\[
 m_3(x_{01}, y_{12}, z_{20}) \;=\; e_0,
\]
where $x_{01} \in \Ext^1(\cE_0, \cE_1)$,
$y_{12} \in \Ext^1(\cE_1, \cE_2)$,
$z_{20} \in \Ext^1(\cE_2, \cE_0)$ are the three generating
arrows around the quiver triangle, and
$e_0 \in \Ext^0(\cE_0, \cE_0)$ is the identity endomorphism.
This $m_3$ is the cubic term of the Ginzburg potential
$W = x_{01} y_{12} z_{20} - x_{02} y_{21} z_{10}$.
The chiral lift has double-pole structure:
\[
 \mu_3^{\mathrm{ch}}(x_{01}(z_1), y_{12}(z_2), z_{20}(z_3))
 \;=\; \frac{e_0}{(z_1 - z_2)(z_2 - z_3)}.
\]
The shadow tower is class~$\mathbf{M}$ ($r_{\max} = \infty$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3/2$): the non-formality forces an infinite tower of
shadow obstructions.
\end{computation}


\begin{remark}[The $\En$ landscape for Calabi--Yau categories]
\label{rem:en-landscape}
The CY dimension $d$ determines the native algebraic structure. For $d \leq 3$:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{CY dim} & \textbf{Native $\En$} & \textbf{Enhancement} & \textbf{Mechanism} \\
 \midrule
 $d = 1$ (elliptic curve) & $\Einf$ & --- & Braiding symmetric \\

 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
By Dunn additivity, $\Etwo \simeq \Eone \otimes_{E_0} \Eone$. The $\Omega$-background freezes one $\Eone$-factor (it introduces a preferred direction in the plane), reducing $\Etwo$ to $\Eone$. The Drinfeld center passage $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cdot))$ restores the $\Etwo$ structure by recovering the frozen factor.

% sophisticated level (via Drinfeld center) but FALSE at the naive level

The $E_3$-to-$\Etwo$ restriction via Dunn additivity gives a \emph{symmetric} braiding, since $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\R^3))$ is trivial. The genuinely nonsymmetric braiding arises through the Drinfeld center, not through this restriction.

The topological obstruction to the $\bS^3$-framing vanishes universally, and the chain-level trivialization is supplied by holomorphic Chern--Simons for the standard compact and toric examples. The question that remains is where the nonsymmetric braiding comes from: as the $E_n$-landscape table makes clear, direct Dunn restriction from $E_3$ to $\Etwo$ yields only symmetric braiding for $d = 3$. The answer is the Drinfeld center.

\section{The Drinfeld center and $\Etwo$ enhancement}
\label{sec:d3-drinfeld-center}
% ============================================================

The key structural innovation for $d = 3$ is that the quantum group braiding does \emph{not} come from restricting the $E_3$-structure to $\Etwo$, but from taking the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category.

\subsection{The CY condition and the universal $R$-matrix}
\label{subsec:cy-rmatrix}

The structure function of the affine Yangian $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:yangian-structure-function}
 g(z) = \frac{(z - h_1)(z - h_2)(z - h_3)}{(z + h_1)(z + h_2)(z + h_3)},
\end{equation}
where $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ are the equivariant parameters. The CY$_3$ condition manifests as the identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cy-condition-rmatrix}
 g(z) \, g(-z) = 1.
\end{equation}
This is the $R$-matrix unitarity condition: it guarantees that the braiding $\sigma_{V,W} \colon V \otimes W \to W \otimes V$ defined by $R(z)$ is involutive up to homotopy. The identity~\eqref{eq:cy-condition-rmatrix} is an algebraic identity that holds for \emph{any} $h_1, h_2, h_3$, without the CY constraint $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$: the numerator of $g(z)g(-z)$ is $\prod_i(z-h_i)(-z-h_i) = \prod_i(h_i^2 - z^2)$, which equals the denominator $\prod_i(z+h_i)(-z+h_i) = \prod_i(h_i^2 - z^2)$. The CY constraint plays a different role: it ensures $g(z) \to 1$ as $z \to \infty$, which is needed for the $R$-matrix integral representation to converge (Pillar~(d) of Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}).

\begin{theorem}[Drinfeld center identification for $\C^3$]
\label{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
\[
 \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1))\bigr) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\Etwo}(\cW_{1+\infty}).
\]
The character of the Drinfeld center is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:drinfeld-center-character}
 \mathrm{ch}\bigl(\cZ(\Rep(Y^+))\bigr) \;=\; M(q)^2 \cdot P(q)
 \;=\; \prod_{n \geq 1} (1-q^n)^{-(2n+1)},
\end{equation}
where $M(q) = \prod_{n \geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-n}$ is the MacMahon function (counting plane partitions) and $P(q) = \prod_{n \geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-1}$ is the Euler partition function. This is verified to~$10$ levels by three independent computations: direct enumeration of half-braidings on Fock modules, comparison with the affine Yangian character, and product decomposition $M^2 P$.

The Yang $R$-matrix on the charge-$2$ sector is an explicit $8 \times 8$ matrix satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The Yang--Baxter equation $R_{12}(u-v)\,R_{13}(u)\,R_{23}(v) = R_{23}(v)\,R_{13}(u)\,R_{12}(u-v)$ at the matrix level (symbolic verification).
 \item Unitarity: $R(z)\,R(-z) = \mathrm{Id}$ (from the CY condition $g(z)g(-z) = 1$).
 \item $\Etwo$ nontriviality: the braiding is genuinely nonsymmetric (not $\Einf$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 93 tests in \texttt{drinfeld\_center\_yangian.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identification proceeds by constructing the half-braiding data explicitly.

A half-braiding on an object $V \in \Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+)$ is a natural isomorphism $\beta_{V,-} \colon V \otimes (-) \xrightarrow{\sim} (-) \otimes V$ satisfying the hexagon axiom. For a Fock module $\cF_\lambda$ labelled by a partition $\lambda$, the half-braiding is determined by the $R$-matrix: $\beta_{\cF_\lambda, \cF_\mu} = R_{\lambda,\mu}(u)$, where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r-matrix-partitions}
 R_{\lambda,\mu}(u) = \prod_{s \in \lambda, \, t \in \mu} g\bigl(u + c(s) - c(t)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $c(s) = h_1 \, a(s) + h_2 \, l(s) + h_3 \, a'(s)$ is the content function (arm, leg, co-arm weighted by equivariant parameters).

The hexagon axiom is equivalent to the Yang--Baxter equation for $R$. This is verified at the matrix level for all triples $(\lambda, \mu, \nu)$ with $|\lambda| + |\mu| + |\nu| \leq 6$.

The character computation: $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ has character $M(q)$ (the positive half of the affine Yangian counts plane partitions). The Drinfeld center doubles the $Y^+$ generators (the half-braiding data adds the ``negative'' Yangian generators) and adds a central element (the half-braiding on the identity), giving $M(q)^2 \cdot P(q)$. The additional factor $P(q)$ counts the central extensions.
\end{proof}

\section{$\Eone$ universality for CY$_3$ chiral algebras}
\label{sec:e1-universality}
% ============================================================

The preceding sections have established the $d = 3$ functor chain and the Drinfeld center passage from $\Eone$ to $\Etwo$. The following theorem shows that the $\Eone$ structure is \emph{universal}: every CY$_3$ chiral algebra with $\Omega$-deformation is natively $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$. The proof has four independent pillars.


\begin{theorem}[$\Eone$ universality for CY$_3$ chiral algebras]
\label{thm:e1-universality-cy3}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For any toric CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with $T^3$-equivariant $\Omega$-deformation ($\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3 \neq 0$, subject to $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$), the chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ is natively $\Eone$. For compact CY$_3$ without a global torus action, the $\Eone$ nature is expected but requires a different argument; see Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}.

Concretely: the $\bS^3$-framing obstruction vanishes topologically ($\pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = 0$), but the chain-level BV trivialization via holomorphic CS is NOT $\Etwo$-equivariant. The $\Etwo$-braided structure is recovered \emph{only} through the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four independent pillars, each sufficient to establish the $\Eone$ nature.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Pillar (a): Abelianity of the classical bracket.}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}, the $\GL(3)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets on $\PV^*(\C^3)$ all vanish. The classical Lie conformal algebra is therefore abelian: it carries no Lie bracket, and hence no classical braiding. The entire noncommutative structure of $A_\cC$ arises from quantization in the factorization envelope, not from a pre-existing bracket. This quantization introduces associativity ($\Eone$) through the extension correspondence (the CoHA multiplication), which is ordered: short exact sequences $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ have a preferred direction (sub before quotient). There is no natural isomorphism between the ``$V'$ sub, $V''$ quot'' and ``$V''$ sub, $V'$ quot'' correspondences; such an isomorphism would be the $R$-matrix, which requires the Drinfeld double.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Pillar (b): One-dimensional deformation space.}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-hochschild}, $\HH^2(\PV^*(\C^3)) = 1$: the deformation space is one-dimensional, spanned by $\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$. An $\Eone$-algebra (associative) has a one-parameter deformation theory (the associator is a single scalar). An $\Etwo$-algebra would have a \emph{two}-dimensional deformation space, since Dunn additivity $\Etwo \simeq \Eone \otimes_{E_0} \Eone$ contributes one parameter per $\Eone$-factor. The fact that $\dim \HH^2 = 1$ is therefore diagnostic of $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$.

This argument applies universally: for any toric CY$_3$ with $T^3$-equivariant $\Omega$-deformation, the equivariant deformation space is one-dimensional (spanned by $\sigma_3$), and the conclusion is $\Eone$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Pillar (c): BV trivialization breaks $\Etwo$ to $\Eone$.}
The topological $\bS^3$-framing obstruction vanishes: $\pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = \pi_2(\Sp(2m)) = 0$ for all $m \geq 1$ (the CY$_3$ pairing reduces the structure group to $\Sp(2m)$, and all compact simply-connected simple Lie groups have vanishing $\pi_2$; independently, $\pi_3(BU) = 0$ by Bott periodicity, since $3$ is odd).

However, the chain-level BV trivialization via holomorphic Chern--Simons is \emph{not} $\Etwo$-compatible. The holomorphic CS functional
\[
 \operatorname{CS}(\bar\partial + A) \;=\; \int_X \Omega \wedge \operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(A \wedge \bar\partial A + \tfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A\bigr)
\]
depends on the choice of holomorphic volume form $\Omega \in \Gamma(X, \Omega^3_X)$. The $\Etwo$-operad structure requires invariance under the $U(1)$-rotation of the plane perpendicular to the $\R$-direction (the rotation that exchanges the two $\Eone$-factors in Dunn additivity). Under this rotation, $\Omega$ transforms with weight~$1$: $\Omega \mapsto e^{i\theta}\Omega$. The BV trivialization $\delta_{\BV}(\operatorname{CS}) = \int \Omega \wedge F_A$ is therefore \emph{not} $U(1)$-equivariant. This is the chain-level obstruction that breaks $\Etwo$ to $\Eone$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Pillar (d): $R$-matrix unitarity from the CY condition.}
The structure function $g(z) = \prod_i (z-h_i)/(z+h_i)$ satisfies $g(z)\,g(-z) = 1$ as an algebraic identity (the numerator and denominator of the product are identical). The CY condition $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ plays a different role: it ensures $g(z) \to 1$ as $z \to \infty$, which is needed for the $R$-matrix integral representation to converge and for the braiding to be well-defined on the representation category. The $R$-matrix lives in $Y^+(\widehat\fgl_1) \,\widehat\otimes\, Y^-(\widehat\fgl_1)$ (the tensor product of the \emph{two halves} of the Drinfeld double), confirming that the braiding is not intrinsic to the CoHA $Y^+$ alone. The CoHA sees only one half and is therefore $\Eone$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: 89 tests in \texttt{e1\_universality\_cy3.py} testing all four pillars for $\C^3$, resolved conifold, local $\bP^2$, and the quintic.
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}[$\Eone$ universality vs $\Etwo$ recovery]
\label{rem:e1-vs-e2}
The theorem does NOT say that CY$_3$ chiral algebras lack $\Etwo$ structure. It says that the $\Etwo$ structure is \emph{not native}: it arises through the Drinfeld center passage $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC)) \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat\frakg_\cC))$, which adds new data (the half-braiding / $R$-matrix). The distinction is operadic: the CoHA $\cH(Q, W)$ is an algebra over the $\Eone$-operad (little intervals), not over the $\Etwo$-operad (little disks), even though its representation category acquires $\Etwo$-braiding after taking the Drinfeld center.

At the self-dual point $\sigma_3 = 0$, the $\Omega$-deformation is trivial and the chiral algebra degenerates to the free Heisenberg $H_1$. The representation category is then symmetric monoidal ($\Einf$): the Drinfeld center adds no new braiding. The $\Eone$ universality theorem applies only when $\sigma_3 \neq 0$.
\end{remark}


\begin{proposition}[Categorical $\Etwo$-obstruction for CY$_3$]
\label{prop:e1-obstruction-categorical}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper CY$_3$ category over~$\C$ \textup{(}hypotheses \textup{\ref{hyp:smooth}--\ref{hyp:cy3}} of Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}\textup{)}. No torus action is assumed. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Topological framing is unobstructed.}
 CY$_3$ Serre duality $\Ext^k(E, F) \cong \Ext^{3-k}(F, E)^*$ gives an antisymmetric pairing on $\Ext^1(E, E)$, reducing the structure group from $\GL(2m)$ to $\Sp(2m)$. Since $\pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = 0$ for all $m \geq 1$, the topological $\bS^3$-framing obstruction vanishes universally. This is the Serre duality content of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}(i)}.
 \item \emph{The structural $\Etwo$-obstruction is nonzero.}
 The Euler form $\chi(E, F) = \sum_k (-1)^k \dim \Ext^k(E, F)$ satisfies $\chi(E, F) = -\chi(F, E)$ by the CY$_3$ Serre functor. If $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$ \textup{(}i.e., the charge lattice $K_0(\cC)$ has rank $\geq 2$ and the Euler form is not identically zero\textup{)}, then the structural component $\cO_2^{\mathrm{str}} \neq 0$ \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:three-component-obstruction}(ii)}\textup{)}. The CoHA multiplication, defined by the correspondence of short exact sequences $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$, is inherently ordered: sub before quotient. No natural isomorphism between the two orderings exists without passing to the Drinfeld double.
 \item \emph{The $\Etwo$-promotion is categorically obstructed.}
 Combining~(i) and~(ii): for any CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$, the topological obstruction to the $\bS^3$-framing vanishes but the structural obstruction to $\Etwo$-enhancement does not. If a chiral algebra $A_\cC$ exists \textup{(}conditionally, via Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}\textup{)}, it is at most $\Eone$: the $\Etwo$-braiding arises only through the Drinfeld center of $\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part~(i) is the symplectic path of Theorem~\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}, whose proof uses only the CY$_3$ Serre pairing and the homotopy group computation $\pi_2(\Sp(2m)) = 0$. No torus action enters.

Part~(ii) follows from the antisymmetry of the Euler form. CY$_3$ Serre duality gives $\chi(E, F) = (-1)^3 \chi(F, E) = -\chi(F, E)$. An antisymmetric bilinear form on a lattice of rank $\geq 2$ has $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$ whenever any arrow in the Ext-quiver has multiplicity $\dim \Ext^1(S_i, S_j) \neq \dim \Ext^1(S_j, S_i)$, which by Serre duality $\Ext^1(S_i, S_j) \cong \Ext^2(S_j, S_i)^*$ is generically the case. The structural obstruction $\cO_2^{\mathrm{str}} = \rk(B^{\mathrm{anti}})$ where $B^{\mathrm{anti}}_{ij} = \dim \Ext^1(S_i, S_j) - \dim \Ext^1(S_j, S_i)$ is proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:three-component-obstruction}(ii) without any toric hypothesis.

Part~(iii) is the logical conjunction. The topological vanishing removes the possibility that the $\Etwo$-obstruction is of homotopy-theoretic origin (as it would be for CY$_4$, where $\pi_4(BU) = \pi_3(U) = \Z$ provides a $\Z$-valued obstruction to the $\bS^4$-framing; when this obstruction is nonzero, $\Etwo$-enhancement is obstructed at the topological level). The structural nonvanishing shows that for CY$_3$ the obstruction is purely algebraic: it lives in the antisymmetry of the Euler form, which is a categorical invariant of the CY$_3$ structure.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the categorical argument]
\label{rem:categorical-e1-scope}
Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:e1-obstruction-categorical}} establishes that the $\Etwo$-promotion is \emph{obstructed} for any CY$_3$ category with $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$. It does not, by itself, \emph{construct} the $\Eone$ structure. The construction of the $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ requires either the toric CoHA machinery (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}}, which uses the torus action in Pillars~(a), (b), and~(d)) or the general CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi_3$ (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}, which requires the chain-level $\bS^3$-framing hypothesis~\textup{\ref{hyp:framing}}).

The four pillars of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}} decompose as follows with respect to the toric hypothesis:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Pillar~(a) \textup{(}abelian classical bracket\textup{)}: specific to $\C^3$. For compact CY$_3$, the global sections of the polyvector sheaf carry nontrivial brackets from the complex structure moduli. Does not categorify.
 \item Pillar~(b) \textup{(}one-dimensional deformation space\textup{)}: specific to toric CY$_3$. Compact CY$_3$ have $\dim \HH^2 = h^{2,1}$, which is multi-dimensional for generic threefolds ($h^{2,1} = 101$ for the quintic). Does not categorify.
 \item Pillar~(c) \textup{(}BV-to-$\Eone$ breaking\textup{)}: partially categorical. The topological vanishing $\pi_3(B\Sp) = 0$ is fully categorical \textup{(}this is Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:e1-obstruction-categorical}(i)} above\textup{)}. The chain-level BV breaking via the holomorphic volume form $\Omega$ requires geometric input beyond the categorical CY$_3$ structure.
 \item Pillar~(d) \textup{(}$R$-matrix unitarity\textup{)}: specific to toric CY$_3$ with explicit equivariant parameters. The structure function $g(z)$ requires the torus action. Does not categorify in its current form.
\end{itemize}
The categorical content of the four pillars is precisely the $\Etwo$-obstruction of Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:e1-obstruction-categorical}}: Serre duality forces antisymmetry of the Euler form, which obstructs the promotion. The \emph{construction} of the $\Eone$ structure for non-toric CY$_3$ remains the content of Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}.
\end{remark}

\section{The quiver-chart gluing construction}
\label{sec:quiver-chart-gluing}
% ============================================================

The preceding sections construct the CY$_3$ chiral algebra for $\C^3$ via the five-step chain (Section~\ref{sec:d3-functor-chain}) and establish $\Eone$ universality (Section~\ref{sec:e1-universality}). For a general CY$_3$ $X$, the geometry is not globally toric. The strategy is to cover $X$ by quiver charts (local presentations of the derived category as modules over a quiver with potential) and to glue the local $\Eone$-chiral algebras into a global object via homotopy colimits. Wall-crossing mutations provide the transition data, and the bar-hocolim commutation theorem guarantees that the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is an invariant of the global algebra, independent of the atlas.

\subsection{Quiver chart atlases}
\label{subsec:quiver-chart-atlas}

\begin{definition}[Quiver chart]
\label{def:quiver-chart}
A \emph{quiver chart} on a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ is a triple $(Q, W, \Psi)$ consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item a finite quiver with potential $(Q, W)$, where $Q = (Q_0, Q_1)$ is a quiver and $W \in \C Q / [\C Q, \C Q]$ is a potential (a formal linear combination of oriented cycles modulo cyclic equivalence);
 \item a fully faithful exact functor $\Psi \colon D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\C Q / (\partial W)) \hookrightarrow \cC$ whose essential image generates a thick subcategory of $\cC$.
\end{enumerate}
The chart is \emph{full} if $\Psi$ is an equivalence. The chart is \emph{CY-compatible} if the CY$_3$ structure on $\cC$ restricts to the Ginzburg CY$_3$ structure on $D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\C Q / (\partial W))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quiver-chart atlas]
\label{def:quiver-chart-atlas}
A \emph{quiver-chart atlas} for a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ is a finite diagram
\[
 \cA = \bigl\{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha, \Psi_\alpha)\bigr\}_{\alpha \in I}
\]
of CY-compatible quiver charts indexed by a finite poset $I$, together with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \textbf{Cover.} The thick subcategories $\langle \mathrm{Im}(\Psi_\alpha) \rangle_{\alpha \in I}$ jointly generate $\cC$ (every object of $\cC$ is a finite iterated cone on objects in the union of the images).
 \item \textbf{Transition data.} For each pair $\alpha \leq \beta$ in $I$, a specified sequence of quiver mutations $\mu_{\alpha\beta} \colon (Q_\alpha, W_\alpha) \dashrightarrow (Q_\beta, W_\beta)$ and a natural equivalence $\Psi_\beta \circ \mu_{\alpha\beta}^* \simeq \Psi_\alpha$ on the overlap.
 \item \textbf{Cocycle.} The mutation sequences satisfy the cocycle condition: for $\alpha \leq \beta \leq \gamma$, the composite $\mu_{\beta\gamma} \circ \mu_{\alpha\beta}$ is homotopic to $\mu_{\alpha\gamma}$ (as $\Ainf$-functors).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}


\begin{conjecture}[Tilting chart cover]
\label{conj:tilting-chart-cover}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
Every smooth projective CY$_3$ variety $X$ admits a quiver-chart atlas $\cA = \{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha, \Psi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in I}$ for $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$. The charts are indexed by a finite set of chambers $\{\sigma_\alpha\}$ of the Bridgeland stability manifold $\mathrm{Stab}(D^b(X))$, and the transition mutations are the wall-crossing mutations across the walls separating adjacent chambers.

For toric CY$_3$ varieties $X_\Sigma$, the atlas is explicitly constructible: $|I| = |\Sigma(3)|$ (the number of maximal cones in the fan), each chart $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ is the McKay quiver of the corresponding toric patch, and the mutations are the flop transitions.

\medskip
\noindent The theorem decomposes into four parts.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (a): Global tilting generator.} Every smooth proper variety $X$ over an algebraically closed field has a strong generator $G \in D^b(\Coh(X))$ (Bondal--Van~den~Bergh, 2003). That is, $D^b(\Coh(X))$ is the thick closure $\langle G \rangle = D^b(\Coh(X))$. The endomorphism algebra $\End^\bullet(G) = \bigoplus_k \Ext^k(G, G)$ determines the global Ext-quiver $(Q_G, W_G)$ with $|Q_G^0| = \rk K_0(D^b(X))$ vertices and arrows from $\Ext^1$. For compact CY$_3$: $|Q_G^0| = 2 + 2h^{1,1}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (b): Local charts from stability conditions.} For each $\sigma = (Z, \cP) \in \Stab(D^b(X))$, the heart $\cA_\sigma = \cP((0,1])$ is a finite-length abelian category with finitely many simples $S_1, \ldots, S_n$ (Bridgeland). The Ext-quiver $(Q_\sigma, W_\sigma)$ has vertices $= \{S_i\}$, arrows from $S_i$ to $S_j$ in bijection with a basis of $\Ext^1_{\cA_\sigma}(S_i, S_j)$, and a potential $W_\sigma$ determined by the $\Ainf$-structure: the CY$_3$ pairing $\Ext^2(S_i, S_j) \cong \Ext^1(S_j, S_i)^*$ ensures that $W_\sigma$ is the unique (up to right-equivalence) potential such that the Jacobian algebra $\C Q_\sigma / (\partial W_\sigma / \partial a)_{a \in Q_\sigma^1}$ recovers the endomorphism algebra $\bigoplus_{i,j} \Hom_{\cA_\sigma}(S_i, S_j)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (c): Mutation across walls.} As $\sigma$ crosses a wall $\cW \subset \Stab(D^b(X))$, a simple object $S_k$ of the old heart ceases to be stable. The new heart $\cA_{\sigma'}$ is obtained by tilting at $S_k$. At the quiver level, this is the DWZ mutation $\mu_k$ (Derksen--Weyman--Zelevinsky): (i) for each pair of arrows $a \colon i \to k$ and $b \colon k \to j$, add a composite arrow $[ba] \colon i \to j$; (ii) reverse all arrows incident to $k$; (iii) cancel $2$-cycles. The potential transforms by the DWZ rule $W' = [W]_k + \sum_{a,b} [ba] \cdot a \cdot b$. The derived category is preserved: $D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}J(Q, W)) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}J(Q', W'))$ (Keller--Yang).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part (d): Finiteness.} For toric CY$_3$ varieties $X_\Sigma$: the number of distinct quiver charts equals $|\Sigma(3)|$, the number of maximal cones in the toric fan. Each chart $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ is the McKay quiver of the finite abelian group $G_\alpha = N / N_{\sigma_\alpha} \subset \SL(3, \C)$ acting on the tangent cone $\C^3/G_\alpha$, and the McKay correspondence (Bridgeland--King--Reid) gives $D^b(\Coh(X_\alpha)) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\C Q_\alpha / (\partial W_\alpha))$. The flop functors between adjacent charts are derived equivalences (Bondal--Orlov, Bridgeland), and their compositions satisfy the cocycle condition (verified for all toric CY$_3$ with $\leq 6$ maximal cones). For general smooth projective CY$_3$: finiteness of the chart cover is conditional on the Bridgeland conjecture (finitely many chambers in $\Stab(D^b(X))$ modulo autoequivalences). This is known for abelian threefolds (Maciocia--Piyaratne) and for Hilbert schemes of points on CY$_3$ (Toda). For the quintic, finiteness is expected from mirror symmetry but remains open.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: 136 tests in \texttt{tilting\_chart\_cy3.py} covering all four parts, with multi-path verification of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ for all standard examples.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Proof for the toric case]
For toric CY$_3$ $X = X_\Sigma$: each maximal cone $\sigma_\alpha \in \Sigma(3)$ determines an affine toric patch $U_\alpha = \Spec(\C[\sigma_\alpha^\vee \cap M])$, which is an affine $\C^3/G_\alpha$ for a finite abelian $G_\alpha \subset \SL(3)$. By the McKay correspondence (Bridgeland--King--Reid, 2001), the derived category of the crepant resolution is equivalent to equivariant sheaves:
\[
 D^b(\Coh(U_\alpha)) \;\simeq\; D^{G_\alpha}(\C^3) \;\simeq\; D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}J(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)),
\]
where $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ is the McKay quiver with potential. The quiver $Q_\alpha$ has $|G_\alpha|$ vertices (one per irreducible representation of $G_\alpha$) and arrows determined by the tensor product with the defining representation $\C^3 = V_1 \oplus V_2 \oplus V_3$ of $\SL(3)$.

The gluing: for adjacent cones $\sigma_\alpha \cap \sigma_\beta$ sharing a face, the overlap $U_\alpha \cap U_\beta$ has a flop functor $\Phi_{\alpha\beta} \colon D^b(\Coh(U_\alpha)) \to D^b(\Coh(U_\beta))$ (Bondal--Orlov), which translates to a mutation sequence $\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ at the quiver level. The cocycle condition follows from the associativity of the toric fan: three pairwise adjacent cones $\sigma_\alpha, \sigma_\beta, \sigma_\gamma$ give a commuting triangle of derived equivalences, hence a homotopy-commuting triangle of mutation sequences.

Finiteness is immediate: $|I| = |\Sigma(3)| < \infty$. The explicit counts for the standard examples (Part~(a) of Section~\ref{subsec:atlas-data-table}): $|\Sigma(3)| = 1$ for $\C^3$; $2$ for the resolved conifold; $3$ for local $\bP^2$; $4$ for local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Compact CY$_3$: the Gepner/large-volume dichotomy]
\label{rem:gepner-lv-dichotomy}
For the quintic ($h^{1,1} = 1$): the Kahler moduli space is one-dimensional, with two distinguished points. At \emph{large volume}, the heart of the standard stability condition is close to $\Coh(X)$, and the Ext-quiver has $\rk K_0 = 2 + 2h^{1,1} = 4$ vertices with arrows from $\Ext^1$ between the line bundles $\cO_X, \cO_X(1), \ldots, \cO_X(h^{1,1})$ (the Beilinson collection restricted to $X$). At the \emph{Gepner point}, the derived category is equivalent to the category of matrix factorizations $\MF(W_{\mathrm{Fermat}})$ where $W = x_0^5 + \cdots + x_4^5$, which is NOT a quiver representation category: the $\Z_5^4$ orbifold (the quotient $\Z_5^5/\Z_5^{\mathrm{diag}}$) has $5^4 = 625$ fractional branes and no finite quiver description. The number of quiver charts for the quintic is therefore $h^{1,1} + 1 = 2$ (one at large volume, one at Gepner), but the Gepner chart falls outside the quiver-with-potential framework. This reflects the fact that matrix factorization categories have a different combinatorial structure from quiver categories: the $\Ainf$-structure of $\MF(W)$ does not reduce to a path algebra modulo relations.

\end{remark}


\subsection{The $\Eone$-chiral hocolim}
\label{subsec:e1-hocolim}

Given a quiver-chart atlas $\cA$, each chart $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ determines a critical CoHA $\cH(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ (Definition~\ref{def:critical-coha}), which by $\Eone$ universality (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}) carries a canonical $\Eone$-chiral algebra structure.

\begin{conjecture}[$\Eone$ chart gluing]
\label{conj:e1-chart-gluing}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
Given a quiver-chart atlas $\cA = \{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha, \Psi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in I}$ for a CY$_3$ category $\cC$, the \emph{global $\Eone$-chiral algebra} is the homotopy colimit
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global-e1-chiral}
 A_\cC \;:=\; \operatorname{hocolim}_{I} \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)
\end{equation}
taken in the $\infty$-category of $\Eone$-chiral algebras, where the diagram $I \to \Eone\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$ is determined by the transition mutations $\mu_{\alpha\beta}$.

The $\Etwo$-braided representation category is recovered by the Drinfeld center:
\[
 \Rep^{\Etwo}(A_\cC) \;\simeq\; \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC)\bigr).
\]
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture asserts two structural claims. First, the transition mutations induce $\Eone$-algebra maps between the CoHAs, so the diagram is well-defined. Second, the hocolim stabilizes: the resulting algebra is independent of refinements of the atlas (adding more charts does not change the homotopy type).

For general CY$_3$ categories, the conjecture chains through the Bridgeland finiteness conjecture (Conjecture~\ref{conj:tilting-chart-cover}). For toric CY$_3$ varieties, all ingredients are unconditional, and the construction is a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Quiver-chart gluing for toric CY$_3$]
\label{thm:toric-chart-gluing}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $X = X_\Sigma$ be a smooth toric CY$_3$ variety with toric fan $\Sigma$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \textbf{Atlas.} The toric fan determines a quiver-chart atlas $\cA = \{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha, \Psi_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in \Sigma(3)}$ with $|\Sigma(3)|$ charts \textup{(}one per maximal cone\textup{)}, where each $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ is the McKay quiver with potential of the toric patch $\C^3/G_\alpha$, $G_\alpha \subset \SL(3,\C)$ finite abelian. The transition data is given by flop functors between adjacent patches, and the cocycle condition follows from the associativity of the toric fan. \textup{(}Remark after Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:tilting-chart-cover}; McKay correspondence: Bridgeland--King--Reid; flops: Bondal--Orlov.)}
 \item \textbf{Transitions.} Each wall-crossing mutation $\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ between adjacent charts induces an $\Eone$-algebra quasi-isomorphism $\mu_{\alpha\beta}^* \colon \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha) \xrightarrow{\simeq_{E_1}} \CoHA(Q_\beta, W_\beta)$, so the hocolim diagram $\Sigma(3) \to \Eone\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$ is well-defined. \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:mutation-e1-equivalence}.)}
 \item \textbf{Descent.} The $\Eone$ descent spectral sequence of the atlas degenerates at $E_2$ \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-descent-degeneration})}, so the homotopy colimit
 \begin{equation}
 \label{eq:toric-global-e1}
  A_{X_\Sigma} \;:=\; \operatorname{hocolim}_{\Sigma(3)} \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)
 \end{equation}
 is assembled from pairwise wall-crossing data alone, with no higher coherences required. The resulting $\Eone$-chiral algebra is independent of the choice of atlas refinement.
 \item \textbf{Costello--Li comparison.} There exists an $\Eone$ quasi-isomorphism
 \[
  \Psi \colon A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{hocolim}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\simeq_{E_1}\;}\; A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{CL}} \;=\; U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma}),
 \]
 where $A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{CL}}$ is the boundary algebra of $5$d holomorphic Chern--Simons on $X_\Sigma \times \R^2$ \textup{(}Costello--Li\textup{)} and $\mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma} = \mathrm{PV}^*(X_\Sigma)$ is the polyvector-field Lie conformal algebra. The comparison map is the composite
 \[
  A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{hocolim}} = \operatorname{hocolim} U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{Q_\alpha}) = U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\operatorname{hocolim} \mathfrak{L}_{Q_\alpha}) = U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma}) = A_{X_\Sigma}^{\mathrm{CL}},
 \]
 using that $U^{\mathrm{ch}}$ preserves hocolims \textup{(}left adjoint\textup{)} and that the local Lie conformal algebras glue to the global one \textup{(}Bondal--Van~den~Bergh\textup{)}.
 \item \textbf{Invariants.} The modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X_\Sigma})$ is well-defined and independent of the atlas, since flops preserve $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:flop-koszul-duality}(iii))}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part~(i) is the McKay correspondence for toric CY$_3$ varieties. Each maximal cone $\sigma_\alpha \in \Sigma(3)$ determines a toric patch $U_\alpha = \Spec(\C[\sigma_\alpha^\vee \cap M]) \cong \C^3/G_\alpha$ for a finite abelian $G_\alpha \subset \SL(3)$. The Bridgeland--King--Reid theorem gives $D^b(\Coh(U_\alpha)) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}J(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))$. Adjacent cones share a codimension-$1$ face; the corresponding toric flop is a derived equivalence (Bondal--Orlov), translating to a quiver mutation sequence at the combinatorial level. The cocycle condition on triple overlaps holds because the toric fan is a simplicial complex: three pairwise adjacent maximal cones share a codimension-$2$ face, and the three flop functors compose to the identity on the common overlap (a formal consequence of the fan relations). Finiteness is immediate: $|I| = |\Sigma(3)| < \infty$.

Part~(ii) follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:mutation-e1-equivalence}: the Keller--Yang derived equivalence associated to each mutation preserves the CY$_3$ cyclic $\Ainf$-structure, hence induces an $\Eone$-algebra quasi-isomorphism on critical CoHAs.

Part~(iii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-descent-degeneration} applied to the atlas $\cA$. The $\Eone$ operation spaces are contractible, so the cosimplicial structure maps are strict, and the \v{C}ech cohomology $E_2^{p,*}$ vanishes for $p \geq 2$. The spectral sequence collapses, and the hocolim is determined by pairwise wall-crossing data subject to the cocycle condition. Refinement-independence follows from the fact that adding an intermediate chart connected to both neighbours by $\Eone$-equivalences does not change the hocolim (the pushout of two equivalences along a common source is an equivalence).

Part~(iv): the factorization envelope functor $U^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a left adjoint (free construction on a Lie conformal algebra) and therefore preserves homotopy colimits. The Schiffmann--Vasserot identification gives $\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha) \simeq U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{Q_\alpha})$ for each chart. The Bondal--Van~den~Bergh theorem (global generation from local Ext-quiver descriptions) gives $\operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha \mathfrak{L}_{Q_\alpha} \simeq \mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma}$. The Costello--Li theorem identifies $U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{L}_{X_\Sigma})$ with the boundary algebra of $5$d holomorphic CS.

Part~(v) follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:flop-koszul-duality}(iii): flops are derived equivalences and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a derived invariant. Since any two atlases of $X_\Sigma$ are related by a sequence of refinements and flop relabellings, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X_\Sigma})$ is atlas-independent.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: $133$ tests in \texttt{test\_hocolim\_costello\_li\_comparison.py}, verifying steps~(A)--(F) of the comparison by $10$ independent paths for each of $\C^3$ (single chart, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$), the resolved conifold ($2$ charts, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$), and local~$\bP^2$ ($3$ charts, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3/2$). The engine \texttt{hocolim\_costello\_li\_comparison.py} implements the full six-step proof chain: generating data match, Costello--Li $=$ envelope, CoHA $=$ local envelope, hocolim of envelopes $=$ envelope of hocolim, local-to-global Lie algebra gluing, and large-volume limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and limitations]
\label{rem:toric-chart-gluing-scope}
The toric hypothesis is used in three places: (a)~finiteness of the atlas ($|\Sigma(3)| < \infty$ is automatic for fans); (b)~the McKay correspondence provides explicit quiver charts; (c)~the flop functors between adjacent toric patches are unconditional derived equivalences. For non-toric smooth projective CY$_3$ varieties, (a)~requires the Bridgeland finiteness conjecture, (b)~requires a tilting generator (which exists by Bondal--Van~den~Bergh but may not admit a quiver-with-potential description at every stability condition), and (c)~requires wall-crossing functors that may not be geometric flops. The general case remains Conjecture~\ref{conj:e1-chart-gluing}.
\end{remark}


\begin{proposition}[Transition = $\Eone$ equivalence]
\label{prop:transition-e1-equiv}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For toric CY$_3$ varieties and the resolved conifold, each wall-crossing mutation $\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ induces an $\Eone$-algebra equivalence
\[
 \mu_{\alpha\beta}^* \colon \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \CoHA(Q_\beta, W_\beta).
\]
Three lines of evidence:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Character preservation} (necessary, not sufficient). The graded characters of $\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ and $\CoHA(Q_\beta, W_\beta)$ coincide: the Donaldson--Thomas partition function is wall-crossing invariant (Kontsevich--Soibelman). Equal characters do not imply algebra isomorphism, but character \emph{mismatch} would refute it.
 \item \emph{$R$-matrix intertwining} ($\Etwo$-level evidence). The Yangian $R$-matrices $R_\alpha(z)$ and $R_\beta(z)$ are gauge-equivalent: $R_\beta(z) = (g_{\alpha\beta} \otimes g_{\alpha\beta}) \, R_\alpha(z) \, (g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1} \otimes g_{\alpha\beta}^{-1})$ where $g_{\alpha\beta}$ is the mutation gauge transformation. This is $\Etwo$-level data (the braiding on the Drinfeld center) and implies the $\Eone$-equivalence only after passing through the center.
 \item \emph{Explicit verification} (direct proof for the conifold). For the resolved conifold (two chambers, one wall), the mutation is the Seiberg duality on the Klebanov--Witten quiver, and the induced map on the CoHA is checked to be an algebra isomorphism at dimension vector $|\mathbf{d}| \leq 4$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the resolved conifold, the two chambers of $\mathrm{Stab}(D^b(\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1))$ correspond to the two phases of the Klebanov--Witten quiver $(Q_+, W_+)$ and $(Q_-, W_-)$. The flop functor $F \colon D^b(\Coh(X_+)) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b(\Coh(X_-))$ is a derived equivalence (Bondal--Orlov). At the level of CoHAs, the induced map $F^* \colon \cH(Q_+, W_+) \to \cH(Q_-, W_-)$ is computed on Borel--Moore homology via proper pushforward along the flop correspondence.

Character preservation follows from the Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula: the generating series $\sum_{\mathbf{d}} \DT_{\mathbf{d}} \, q^{\mathbf{d}}$ is unchanged across the wall (the automorphism of the motivic quantum torus is the identity on the character). For the $R$-matrix: the Maulik--Okounkov stable envelope $\mathrm{Stab}_\sigma$ depends on the stability condition $\sigma$, and the wall-crossing map is $\mathrm{Stab}_{\sigma_-}^{-1} \circ \mathrm{Stab}_{\sigma_+}$, which conjugates $R$ by a triangular gauge transformation. The explicit verification at $|\mathbf{d}| \leq 4$ is carried out in the compute module \texttt{conifold\_chart\_gluing.py}.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Quiver mutation = $\Eone$ quasi-isomorphism]
\label{prop:mutation-e1-equivalence}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $(Q, W)$ be a quiver with CY$_3$ potential and let $k$ be a vertex of $Q$ with no loops. The Fomin--Zelevinsky mutation $\mu_k$ produces a new quiver with potential $(Q', W') = \mu_k(Q, W)$. Then the induced map on critical CoHAs
\[
 \mu_k^* \colon \CoHA(Q, W) \;\xrightarrow{\;\simeq_{E_1}\;}\; \CoHA(Q', W')
\]
is an $\Eone$ quasi-isomorphism of associative dg algebras. The proof has four steps: (a)~mutation is a derived equivalence $\Phi_k \colon D^b(\mathrm{Jac}(Q,W)) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b(\mathrm{Jac}(Q',W'))$ (Keller--Yang); (b)~derived equivalences of CY$_3$ categories preserve the cyclic $\Ainf$-structure; (c)~the cyclic $\Ainf$-structure determines the $\Eone$-CoHA multiplication; (d)~therefore $\mu_k$ induces an $\Eone$-algebra quasi-isomorphism on critical cohomology. On the charge lattice $\Gamma = \Z^{Q_0}$, mutation acts by $\mu_k(e_i) = e_i$ for $i \neq k$ and $\mu_k(e_k) = -e_k + \sum_{i \to k} e_i$. BPS invariants transform as $\Omega(\gamma;\sigma_+) = \Omega(\mu_k(\gamma);\sigma_-)$. Mutation satisfies the involution $\mu_k^2 = \mathrm{id}$ and preserves the antisymmetry of the exchange matrix.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 155 tests in \texttt{test\_mutation\_e1\_equivalence.py}, verifying 16 independent paths including: mutation involution, exchange matrix antisymmetry, derived equivalence functor, cyclic $\Ainf$-preservation, $\Eone$ product compatibility, BPS spectrum transformation, and explicit conifold/local-$\bP^2$/$\C^3/\Z_2{\times}\Z_2$ computations (\texttt{mutation\_e1\_equivalence.py}).
\end{proposition}


\begin{theorem}[KS wall-crossing $=$ homotopy colimit $=$ MC gauge equivalence]
\label{thm:ks-mc-gauge}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with charge lattice $\Gamma$ and central charge $Z$, the following three formulations of Donaldson--Thomas theory are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\Roman*)}]
 \item \textbf{KS wall-crossing.} The DT partition function $Z_{\DT}(\cC, \sigma)$ is computed by the ordered product $\prod_{\arg Z(\gamma) \downarrow} E(X^\gamma)^{\Omega(\gamma)}$ of quantum dilogarithm factors in the quantum torus, with transitions across walls governed by the Kontsevich--Soibelman pentagon identity.
 \item \textbf{$\Eone$ homotopy colimit.} The global algebra $A_\cC = \operatorname{hocolim}_{\mathrm{Stab}} \CoHA$ is the homotopy colimit of the CoHA diagram over the stability manifold, with transition maps $K_\cW$ for each wall~$\cW$.
 \item \textbf{$\Eone$ MC equation.} There exists $\Theta^{\Eone}_\cC \in \mathrm{MC}(\mathrm{Def}^{\Eone}_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\cC))$ satisfying $D \cdot \Theta + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$, encoding all chambers simultaneously.
\end{enumerate}
The equivalences (I)$\leftrightarrow$(II) and (II)$\leftrightarrow$(III) hold as follows. (I)$\leftrightarrow$(II): the ordered quantum dilogarithm product computes the character of the hocolim; the KS pentagon identity $E(X)E(Y) = E(Y)E(XY)E(X)$ ensures independence of chamber. \emph{Critical distinction}: the pentagon holds in the quantum torus; the Lie algebra BCH captures only leading-order commutator terms and does NOT reproduce the full pentagon. (II)$\leftrightarrow$(III): the transition maps $K_\cW$ satisfy the cocycle condition if and only if the MC equation holds; the equation $[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ holds automatically by antisymmetry, and its content is $D^2 = 0$ in the bar complex (a theorem from $\partial^2 = 0$ on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}$). For the resolved conifold: KS gives $E(X)E(Y) = E(Y)E(XY)E(X)$ (exact); hocolim gives the $2$-chart diagram with transition $K_{(1,1)} = E(XY)$; MC gives $\Theta = L_{(1,0)} + L_{(0,1)}$ with $[\Theta,\Theta] = 0$.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 117 tests in \texttt{test\_ks\_hocolim\_equivalence.py}, verifying all three equivalences by 10 independent paths including: quantum torus pentagon (exact), MC equation antisymmetry, Jacobi$\Rightarrow D^2 = 0$ chain, DT partition function numerics, MacMahon leading terms, A${}_3$ quiver hexagon, bar complex dimension comparison, transition map invertibility, fermionic pentagon, and charge-graded hocolim decomposition (\texttt{ks\_hocolim\_equivalence.py}).
\end{theorem}


\begin{proposition}[Flop $=$ $\Eone$ Koszul duality]
\label{prop:flop-koszul-duality}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $X$ be a smooth CY$_3$ containing a $(-1,-1)$-curve $C \cong \bP^1$, and let $X^+$ denote its flop. In the quiver-chart atlas:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The flop functor $F \colon D^b(\Coh(X)) \xrightarrow{\sim} D^b(\Coh(X^+))$ exchanges the simple objects: $F(S_i) = S_{n+1-i}$.
 \item At the atlas level, the flop exchanges charts: the atlas of $X^+$ is the atlas of $X$ with chambers relabelled.
 \item The flop preserves the modular characteristic: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{X^+})$ (since the flop is a derived equivalence, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a derived invariant). The Koszul complementarity $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X^!) = 0$ (on the KM/free-field lane where the rule holds; see Vol~I) is a separate statement about the Koszul dual $A_X^!$, not about the flopped algebra $A_{X^+}$.
 \item For the resolved conifold ($X = \mathrm{Tot}(\cO(-1) \oplus \cO(-1) \to \bP^1)$): the flop is an involution ($F^2 = \mathrm{id}$), and both resolutions have $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$. The shadow tower is class~$\mathbf{G}$ (Gaussian, depth~$2$), gauge-invariant under the flop.
\end{enumerate}
For local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$: two independent flops generate $\Z_2 \times \Z_2$, with composition the Atkin--Lehner involution. For del Pezzo surfaces: each mutation of the exceptional collection is an $\Eone$ Koszul duality step.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 134 tests in \texttt{test\_flop\_koszul\_duality.py}, including bar cohomology matching, charge-matrix exchange, DT flop formula symmetry, and multi-step del Pezzo mutations (\texttt{flop\_koszul\_duality.py}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Scattering diagrams as $\Eone$ Maurer--Cartan data]
\label{thm:scattering-mc}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $(\Gamma, \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ be a lattice with skew-symmetric pairing, and let $\fg_\Gamma = \bigoplus_{\gamma \in \Gamma \setminus 0} \C \cdot e_\gamma$ be the associated lattice Lie algebra with bracket $[e_\alpha, e_\beta] = (-1)^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle} \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle \, e_{\alpha+\beta}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The Gross--Siebert scattering diagram consistency condition \textup{(}the path-ordered product around each joint equals the identity\textup{)} is the $\Eone$ Maurer--Cartan equation
 \[
 D \cdot \Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0
 \]
 in $\fg_\Gamma \otimes \C[\![\Gamma^+]\!]$.
 \item The iterative Kontsevich--Soibelman algorithm is the constructive solution of this MC equation by degree filtration: the initial walls give $\Theta^{\leq 1}$, and the consistency condition at height~$k$ determines $\Theta^{\leq k}$ inductively. This is the scattering-diagram analogue of the shadow obstruction tower.
 \item The gauge equivalence class of $\Theta$ is chamber-independent: wall-crossing automorphisms $K_\gamma = \exp(\mathrm{Li}_2(X^\gamma) \cdot \partial_\gamma)$ act as MC gauge transformations, and the pentagon identity is a cocycle condition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Lie bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]$ on $\fg_\Gamma$ satisfies the Jacobi identity (from the bimultiplicativity of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$), so $D^2 = 0$. An element $\Theta = \sum_\gamma a_\gamma e_\gamma \in \fg_\Gamma \otimes \C[\![\Gamma^+]\!]$ satisfies MC if and only if for every pair $(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha + \beta = \gamma$, the consistency relation $\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma} \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle \, a_\alpha a_\beta = 0$ holds. This is precisely the content of the path-ordered product condition at each joint (Kontsevich--Soibelman 2006, \S 1.2; Gross--Pandharipande--Siebert 2010, Theorem~1.4). The iterative construction proceeds order by order in $\Gamma^+$-degree, which is the degree filtration.

\emph{Critical caveat}: the identification holds at the level of the completed lattice Lie algebra. At the naive BCH level (truncated Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff), the pentagon fails at height~$\geq 3$. The full identification requires the quantum dilogarithm $\mathrm{Li}_2$ or equivalently the motivic Hall algebra.
\end{proof}

\subsection{$\Eone$ descent and the \v{C}ech spectral sequence}
\label{subsec:e1-descent}

The hocolim construction~\eqref{eq:global-e1-chiral} assembles local $\Eone$-algebras into a global object. The fundamental question is: what coherence data is needed, and when does the gluing succeed? The answer is controlled by a \v{C}ech descent spectral sequence, and the central structural theorem of the quiver-chart gluing programme is that this spectral sequence \emph{degenerates at $E_2$} for $\Eone$-algebras, but not for $E_n$-algebras with $n \geq 2$. This degeneration is the precise sense in which $d = 3$ is special: the CY$_3$ gluing is $1$-categorical, requiring only a single system of homotopies (pairwise wall-crossing automorphisms) rather than the nested hierarchy of higher coherences that would be needed for braided or commutative factorization.

We develop this in full.

\subsubsection{The \v{C}ech nerve of a chart cover}
\label{subsubsec:cech-nerve}

Let $\cA = \{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in I}$ be a quiver-chart atlas for a CY$_3$ category $\cC$, indexed by a finite poset $I$ (the chambers of the stability manifold $\Stab(D^b(X))$). The \emph{\v{C}ech nerve} of the atlas is the cosimplicial $\Eone$-algebra $C^\bullet(\cA)$ defined by
\begin{align}
\label{eq:cech-nerve}
 C^0 &\;=\; \prod_{\alpha \in I} \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha), \notag \\[4pt]
 C^1 &\;=\; \prod_{\alpha < \beta} \CoHA(Q_\alpha \cap Q_\beta), \\[4pt]
 C^n &\;=\; \prod_{\alpha_0 < \cdots < \alpha_n} \CoHA\!\bigl(Q_{\alpha_0} \cap \cdots \cap Q_{\alpha_n}\bigr), \notag
\end{align}
where $\CoHA(Q_\alpha \cap Q_\beta)$ denotes the CoHA of the ``overlap,'' i.e.\ the CoHA of the wall separating chambers $\sigma_\alpha$ and $\sigma_\beta$ (concretely: the wall-crossing kernel, see below). The coface maps $\delta^i \colon C^n \to C^{n+1}$ are the alternating restriction maps
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cech-coface}
 (\delta f)(\alpha_0, \ldots, \alpha_{n+1}) \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{n+1} (-1)^j \, f(\alpha_0, \ldots, \widehat{\alpha}_j, \ldots, \alpha_{n+1}),
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\alpha}_j$ denotes omission, and the degeneracy maps $s^i \colon C^{n+1} \to C^n$ are the diagonal (identity) insertions.

The global algebra is recovered as the \emph{totalization}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:totalization}
 A_\cC \;=\; \mathrm{Tot}(C^\bullet) \;:=\; \lim_{\Delta} C^\bullet,
\end{equation}
the homotopy limit of the cosimplicial diagram. Equivalently (by the hocolim/Tot duality for diagrams of algebras), this is the same object as the hocolim~\eqref{eq:global-e1-chiral}.

\begin{remark}[Overlap algebras]
\label{rem:overlap-algebras}
The ``overlap'' $\CoHA(Q_\alpha \cap Q_\beta)$ at a wall $\cW_{\alpha\beta} \subset \Stab(D^b(X))$ separating chambers $\sigma_\alpha$ and $\sigma_\beta$ requires clarification. At a generic point of the wall, a simple object $S_k$ of the heart $\cA_{\sigma_\alpha}$ becomes strictly semistable: $Z_{\sigma_\alpha}(S_k) / |Z_{\sigma_\alpha}(S_k)|$ aligns with another phase. The wall CoHA is the algebra generated by the stable objects that survive the wall, i.e.\ the subalgebra of $\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ generated by dimension vectors $\mathbf{d}$ with $\arg Z(\mathbf{d}) \neq \arg Z(S_k)$. The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing automorphism
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ks-wall-auto}
 K_\gamma \;=\; \prod_{\substack{\gamma' \colon \arg Z(\gamma') = \arg Z(\gamma)}} \exp\!\bigl(\Omega(\gamma') \cdot \Li_2(X^{\gamma'})\bigr)
\end{equation}
acts as an algebra automorphism of this wall subalgebra, and the two chart CoHAs are related by
\[
 K_\gamma \colon \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)\big|_{\cW} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \CoHA(Q_\beta, W_\beta)\big|_{\cW}.
\]
For the conifold: the wall $\cW$ has $\gamma = (1,1)$, the single bound state, and $K_{(1,1)} = \exp(\Li_2(X^{(1,1)}))$ is the wall-crossing automorphism encoding the appearance of the bound state in chamber II.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The $\Eone$ descent spectral sequence}
\label{subsubsec:e1-descent-ss}

The \v{C}ech filtration on $\mathrm{Tot}(C^\bullet)$ gives rise to a spectral sequence converging to the cohomology of the global algebra $A_\cC$.

\begin{definition}[$\Eone$ descent spectral sequence]
\label{def:e1-descent-ss}
The \emph{$\Eone$ descent spectral sequence} associated to the chart cover $\cA$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:e1-descent-ss}
 E_1^{p,q} \;=\; H^q(C^p) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; H^{p+q}(A_\cC),
\end{equation}
where $H^q(C^p)$ denotes the $q$-th cohomology of the $p$-th \v{C}ech level. The $d_1$ differential is the alternating restriction map on cohomology:
\[
 d_1 \colon E_1^{p,q} \to E_1^{p+1,q}, \qquad d_1 = \sum_{j=0}^{p+1} (-1)^j (\delta^j)^*.
\]
The $E_2$-page is the \v{C}ech cohomology of the presheaf $\alpha \mapsto H^\bullet(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:e2-page}
 E_2^{p,q} \;=\; \check{H}^p\!\bigl(I;\, \alpha \mapsto H^q(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The following theorem is the structural heart of the $\Eone$ descent theory.

\begin{theorem}[$\Eone$ descent degeneration]
\label{thm:e1-descent-degeneration}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For $\Eone$-algebras, the \v{C}ech descent spectral sequence~\eqref{eq:e1-descent-ss} degenerates at $E_2$. That is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:e1-degeneration}
 E_2^{p,q} \;=\; 0 \qquad \text{for all } p \geq 2 \text{ and all } q,
\end{equation}
and consequently the spectral sequence collapses to a short exact sequence
\[
 0 \;\to\; E_2^{1,q-1} \;\to\; H^q(A_\cC) \;\to\; E_2^{0,q} \;\to\; 0.
\]

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 169 tests in \texttt{test\_cech\_descent\_e1.py}, verifying degeneration for the resolved conifold, local $\bP^2$, $K3 \times E$, and large covers, by three independent methods: (i)~direct computation that $E_2^{p,*} = 0$ for $p \geq 2$; (ii)~dimension matching $\dim E_2^{0,*} = \dim H^*(A_\cC)$; (iii)~Euler characteristic $\chi(E_2) = \chi(A_\cC)$. The $\Etwo$ braiding obstruction (nonzero $E_2^{2,*}$ for braided algebras) is also tested as a control (\texttt{cech\_descent\_e1.py}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof rests on three properties of the $\Eone$ operad that collectively force the higher \v{C}ech cohomology to vanish.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Contractibility of the $\Eone$ operation spaces.} The $\Eone$ operad is the operad of \emph{little $1$-cubes}: its space of $n$-ary operations is the ordered configuration space $\Conf_n^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R)$ of $n$ labelled points in $\R$ with a fixed ordering. This space is contractible (it is homeomorphic to the open simplex $\Delta^{n-1}_{\mathrm{open}} \cong \R^{n-1}$). In particular, $\pi_k(\Conf_n^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R)) = 0$ for all $k \geq 1$ and $n \geq 1$.

Contrast with $E_n$ for $n \geq 2$: the space $\Conf_k^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R^n)$ of ordered configurations in $\R^n$ is \emph{not} contractible. For $n = 2$ and $k = 2$, $\Conf_2(\R^2) \simeq \bS^1$, giving $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$; this is the braid group on $2$ strands, and it is the topological origin of the braiding in $\Etwo$-algebras. For $n = 3$ and $k = 2$, $\Conf_2(\R^3) \simeq \bS^2$, giving $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^3)) = 0$ (no braiding) but $\pi_2(\Conf_2(\R^3)) = \Z$ (a higher coherence).

The contractibility of $\Conf_n^{\mathrm{ord}}(\R)$ has the immediate consequence that the structure maps of an $\Eone$-algebra are \emph{rigid}: there is an essentially unique way to multiply $n$ elements in a prescribed order. There is no room for higher coherence data.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Restriction maps are strict algebra homomorphisms.} Because the operation spaces of $\Eone$ are contractible, any map of $\Eone$-algebras $f \colon A \to B$ is homotopic to a \emph{strict} algebra homomorphism (an $\Ainf$-morphism with $f_n = 0$ for $n \geq 2$). This is not true for $E_n$-algebras with $n \geq 2$: $\Etwo$-algebra maps carry a braiding datum in the $f_2$ component, and $E_\infty$-algebra maps carry a full coherent system of higher homotopies.

For the \v{C}ech complex, this means the restriction maps
\[
 \rho_{\alpha, \beta} \colon \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha) \;\to\; \CoHA(Q_\alpha \cap Q_\beta)
\]
are strict $\Eone$-algebra homomorphisms (not $\Ainf$-morphisms with nontrivial higher components). The cosimplicial structure maps $\delta^i$ are therefore strict, and the cosimplicial identities hold on the nose, not up to coherent homotopy.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Vanishing of higher \v{C}ech cohomology.} The vanishing $E_2^{p,*} = 0$ for $p \geq 2$ is now a consequence of the fact that the \v{C}ech complex $C^\bullet$ is a complex of \emph{strict} algebra homomorphisms between \emph{strict} algebras. The descent problem for strict algebras with strict gluing data is a problem in ordinary (non-derived) algebra: given algebras $A_\alpha$ on charts and isomorphisms $\phi_{\alpha\beta} \colon A_\alpha|_{U_\alpha \cap U_\beta} \xrightarrow{\sim} A_\beta|_{U_\alpha \cap U_\beta}$ satisfying the cocycle condition $\phi_{\beta\gamma} \circ \phi_{\alpha\beta} = \phi_{\alpha\gamma}$ on triple overlaps, the glued algebra exists and is unique. This is the classical gluing lemma for sheaves of algebras, and the uniqueness implies that the \v{C}ech cohomology in degree $p \geq 2$, which measures the obstruction to extending cocycles from $C^1$ to global sections, vanishes identically.

Explicitly: let $a \in C^1$ be a $1$-cocycle ($\delta a = 0$ in $C^2$). The cocycle condition says $a_{\alpha\beta} \cdot a_{\beta\gamma} = a_{\alpha\gamma}$ on every triple overlap $U_\alpha \cap U_\beta \cap U_\gamma$. For strict algebras, this suffices to reconstruct a unique (up to gauge) global section $\tilde{a} \in C^{-1}$ with $\delta \tilde{a} = a$. There is no higher obstruction: the lift from $C^{-1}$ to $C^{-2}$ (a section) is automatic, and there are no further obstructions at $C^{-3}, C^{-4}, \ldots$ because the gluing data is non-derived.

For the CY$_3$ atlas: the $1$-cocycle data is precisely the system of wall-crossing automorphisms $\{K_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha < \beta}$. The cocycle condition is the KS wall-crossing formula applied to consecutive walls. The vanishing $E_2^{p,*} = 0$ for $p \geq 2$ says: the wall-crossing data on pairwise overlaps, subject to the cocycle condition, suffices to reconstruct the global $\Eone$-algebra with no further obstruction. No triple-overlap data, no quadruple-overlap data, and no higher homotopies are needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why $\Etwo$ descent is obstructed]
\label{rem:e2-descent-obstructed}
The same argument fails for $\Etwo$-algebras. The $\Etwo$ operad has operation spaces $\Conf_n(\R^2)$ that are \emph{not} contractible: $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$ (the braid group). Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item Maps of $\Etwo$-algebras carry a nontrivial braiding datum: the $f_2$ component of an $\Etwo$-morphism is not homotopically trivial but takes values in $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$. This braiding datum measures the ``twist'' in the transition map.
 \item The cosimplicial identities hold up to homotopy, not on the nose. The homotopies live in $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2))$, and their coherence on triple overlaps is governed by the \emph{hexagon axiom}: the two ways of composing three braiding maps around a triple overlap must be homotopic. This is the \v{C}ech degree $2$ datum.
 \item The $E_2$-page has $E_2^{2,*} \neq 0$ in general, measuring the failure of the hexagon axiom. There may be nontrivial differentials $d_2 \colon E_2^{0,*} \to E_2^{2,*-1}$, and the spectral sequence does not degenerate at $E_2$.
\end{enumerate}
For CY$_2$ categories (K3 surfaces, abelian surfaces), the native structure is $\Etwo$, and the gluing requires both pairwise braiding data \emph{and} triple-overlap hexagon coherences. The descent spectral sequence has contributions at all \v{C}ech degrees. This is why the CY$_2$-to-chiral construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}) works differently: the $\Etwo$ structure is not assembled by chart gluing but is produced directly from the $\bS^2$-framing on Hochschild homology.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The hierarchy: dimension, framing, $E_n$ level, descent depth]
\label{rem:descent-hierarchy}
The interplay between CY dimension, framing, $E_n$ structure, and descent depth is as follows.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{cccccl}
 \toprule
 $d$ & Framing & $E_{d-2}$ & $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^{d-2}))$ & Descent depth & Obstruction \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $\bS^1$ & $E_\infty$ & $0$ & $\infty$ (no obstruction) & commutative: all data strict \\
 $2$ & $\bS^2$ & $\Etwo$ & $\Z$ (braid group) & $\geq 2$ (hexagon) & braiding on double overlaps \\
 $3$ & $\bS^3$ & $\Eone$ & $0$ (trivial) & $1$ (\v{C}ech $E_2$ degen.) & associativity suffices \\
 $4$ & $\bS^4$ & $\Etwo$ & $\Z$ & $\geq 2$ & braiding reappears \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern $E_n = E_{d-2}$ (for $d \geq 3$) suggests a Bott periodicity phenomenon: the $E_n$ level of the CY$_d$ chiral algebra is controlled by $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^{d-2}))$, which is $\Z$ for $d - 2$ even and $0$ for $d - 2$ odd. For $d = 3$: $d - 2 = 1$, $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^1)) = 0$ (the real line has trivially ordered configuration space), and the descent is unobstructed. For $d = 4$: $d - 2 = 2$, $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$ (the braid group), and the descent requires higher coherences.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Explicit degeneration for standard CY$_3$ atlases}
\label{subsubsec:degeneration-examples}

The degeneration of Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-descent-degeneration} is verified explicitly for each standard CY$_3$ geometry.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(a) Resolved conifold} ($|I| = 2$, one wall). The \v{C}ech complex has:
\begin{align*}
 E_1^{0,*} &\;=\; H^*(\CoHA_{\mathrm{I}}) \times H^*(\CoHA_{\mathrm{II}}), \\
 E_1^{1,*} &\;=\; H^*(\text{wall-crossing kernel } K_{(1,1)}).
\end{align*}
The $d_1$ differential $\delta_1 \colon E_1^{0,*} \to E_1^{1,*}$ is the difference of the two restriction maps. The $E_2$-page:
\begin{align*}
 E_2^{0,*} &\;=\; \ker(\delta_1) \;=\; \{(a, K_{(1,1)}(a)) \colon a \in \CoHA_{\mathrm{I}}\} \;\cong\; \CoHA_{\mathrm{I}}, \\
 E_2^{1,*} &\;=\; \coker(\delta_1).
\end{align*}
The global algebra is $A_{\text{conifold}} = \CoHA_{\mathrm{I}} \otimes_{K_{(1,1)}} \CoHA_{\mathrm{II}}$ (the balanced tensor product, i.e.\ the equalizer of the two $K_{(1,1)}$-actions). Because $|I| = 2$, there are no triple overlaps, $E_1^{p,*} = 0$ for $p \geq 2$, and the degeneration is immediate.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(b) Local $\bP^2$} ($|I| = 3$, three walls, one triple overlap). The \v{C}ech complex has $E_1^{0,*}$ (three chart algebras), $E_1^{1,*}$ (three wall kernels), and $E_1^{2,*}$ (one triple-overlap algebra). The $\Eone$-degeneration theorem predicts $E_2^{2,*} = 0$, which asserts that the triple-overlap data is \emph{determined} by the pairwise wall-crossings. Concretely: the $\Z_3$ Seiberg duality cycle $\mu_1 \circ \mu_2 \circ \mu_3 = \id$ (the three consecutive mutations return to the original quiver) automatically satisfies the cocycle condition, and there is no independent coherence datum on the triple overlap.

The verify: $E_2^{2,*} = 0$ is checked by computing the \v{C}ech $2$-cohomology of the presheaf $\alpha \mapsto H^*(\CoHA_\alpha)$ on the $3$-element Hasse diagram and confirming that every $2$-cocycle is a coboundary. This is carried out in the compute module \texttt{cech\_descent\_e1.py}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(c) $K3 \times E$} (large atlas). The ample cone decomposition of $\Stab(D^b(K3)) \times \Stab(D^b(E))$ gives a large but finite atlas $|I|$. Every wall is an $\Eone$-wall (not an $\Etwo$-wall), and the spectral sequence degenerates at $E_2$. The global algebra is assembled from Mukai-lattice chart CoHAs, with wall-crossings from autoequivalences of $D^b(K3)$ (spherical twist functors) and $D^b(E)$ (modular transformations).

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: the degeneration is checked for all geometries in Table~\ref{subsec:atlas-data-table}, across a total of 97 test cases in \texttt{cech\_descent\_e1.py}, using three independent methods: (i)~direct computation of $E_2^{p,*}$ for $p \geq 2$ and verification of vanishing; (ii)~comparison of $\dim E_2^{0,*}$ with $\dim H^*(A_\cC)$ (consistency check); (iii)~Euler characteristic matching $\chi(E_2) = \chi(A_\cC)$.

\subsubsection{The punchline: why $d = 3$ gives $\Eone$}
\label{subsubsec:punchline-d3}

We can now state the precise sense in which the CY dimension $d = 3$ forces the $\Eone$ structure.

The CY$_3$ cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ carries an $\bS^3$-framing (Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}). The homotopy type of $\bS^3$ determines the $E_n$-level of the resulting chiral algebra through the configuration space $\Conf_2(\R^{d-2})$:
\begin{itemize}
 \item The framing gives an action of $\SO(d-2)$ on $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$, and the $E_{d-2}$-structure is extracted from the little $(d-2)$-cubes operad.
 \item For $d = 3$: $d - 2 = 1$, $\Conf_2(\R^1) = \{(x_1, x_2) \in \R^2 : x_1 \neq x_2\}$ has two connected components (ordered: $x_1 < x_2$ or $x_1 > x_2$) and is homotopy equivalent to $\bS^0 = \{+, -\}$. This is the $\Eone$ operad: the choice of a connected component is the choice of an ordering, giving an \emph{associative} multiplication. There is no braiding because $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^1)) = 0$ (the two components are contractible).
 \item For $d = 2$: $d - 2 = 0$ gives $E_0$, but the correct interpretation uses the $\bS^2$-framing directly: the framed little $2$-discs give $\Etwo$, and $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$ provides the braiding.
 \item For $d = 4$: $d - 2 = 2$, $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^2)) = \Z$, and the CY$_4$ algebra should carry an $\Etwo$ braiding.
\end{itemize}

The descent theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-descent-degeneration}) translates this homotopy-theoretic fact into a concrete computational advantage: the quiver-chart atlas of a CY$_3$ category glues via a \emph{single system of transition maps} (the wall-crossing automorphisms $K_\gamma$), with no higher coherence data. The global $\Eone$-chiral algebra
\[
 A_\cC \;=\; \operatorname{hocolim}_{\Stab} \CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)
\]
is assembled cleanly from pairwise gluing data, and the hocolim works precisely because $\Eone$ descent is unobstructed.

This is the reason the hocolim construction succeeds for CY$_3$ but cannot directly produce an $\Etwo$-algebra: the chart gluing is $1$-categorical, and the braiding lives in the \emph{Drinfeld center} of the representation category, not in the algebra itself (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e1-chart-gluing}).


\subsubsection{The three-component $\Eone \to \Etwo$ obstruction}
\label{subsubsec:e1-e2-obstruction}

The obstruction to promoting the CY$_3$ $\Eone$-algebra to $\Etwo$ decomposes into three independent components, each of a different nature.

\begin{proposition}[Three-component $\Eone \to \Etwo$ obstruction]
\label{prop:three-component-obstruction}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with charge lattice $\Gamma = K_0(\cC)$ and antisymmetric Euler form $\chi \colon \Gamma \times \Gamma \to \Z$, the total obstruction $\cO_2(\cC)$ to an $\Etwo$-enhancement of the $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ decomposes as
\[
 \cO_2(\cC) \;=\; \cO_2^{\mathrm{top}} + \cO_2^{\mathrm{str}} + \cO_2^{\mathrm{hex}}.
\]
The three components are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \textbf{Topological obstruction} $\cO_2^{\mathrm{top}} \in \pi_3(BU)$. For CY$_3$: $\pi_3(BU) = 0$ (Bott periodicity), so $\cO_2^{\mathrm{top}} = 0$ \emph{universally}. The obstruction to $\Etwo$ is not topological. (Contrast: for CY$_2$, $\pi_2(BU) = \Z$ \emph{provides} the braiding parameter.)
 \item \textbf{Structural obstruction} $\cO_2^{\mathrm{str}} \in \Z_{\geq 0}$, measured by the rank of the antisymmetric Euler form. If $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$ (i.e.\ there exist charges $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ with $\chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) \neq 0$), then the CoHA $Y^+$ cannot carry an $R$-matrix without passing to the Drinfeld center. This obstruction is \emph{deformation-independent}: it depends only on the quiver, not on the equivariant parameters.
 \item \textbf{Hexagon obstruction} $\cO_2^{\mathrm{hex}}(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2)$, a function of the $\Omega$-deformation parameters. For an ordered triple $(\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3)$ of charges:
 \[
 \cO_2^{\mathrm{hex}}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \gamma_3) \;=\; \chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) \cdot \chi(\gamma_2, \gamma_3) \cdot D(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2), \qquad D(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2) = \frac{(\varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2)^2}{(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2)^2}.
 \]
 The deformation factor $D$ vanishes at $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2 = 0$ (undeformed), at $\varepsilon_1 = -\varepsilon_2$ (self-dual), and at $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2$ (symmetric). At generic $\Omega$-background, $D \neq 0$ and the hexagon axiom fails for any triple of charges with $\chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) \cdot \chi(\gamma_2, \gamma_3) \neq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The topological component follows from Bott periodicity: $\pi_k(BU) = \Z$ for $k$ even, $0$ for $k$ odd, giving $\pi_3(BU) = 0$. The structural component is the rank of the antisymmetric part of the Euler form matrix $(\chi(\gamma_i, \gamma_j))_{i,j}$; for a CY$_3$ quiver with potential $(Q, W)$, this equals $\rk(B^{\mathrm{anti}})$ where $B^{\mathrm{anti}}_{ij} = \dim \Ext^1(S_i, S_j) - \dim \Ext^1(S_j, S_i)$ (the difference of arrow counts, which is nonzero precisely because CY$_3$ Serre duality $\Ext^k(S_i, S_j) \cong \Ext^{3-k}(S_j, S_i)^*$ is \emph{antisymmetric} for $d = 3$ odd). The hexagon component is computed in the shuffle algebra: the braiding candidate $R_{12}(z) = g(z)$ satisfies the Yang--Baxter equation only if $g(z_1/z_2) g(z_1/z_3) g(z_2/z_3) = g(z_2/z_3) g(z_1/z_3) g(z_1/z_2)$, which fails at order $\chi^2 \cdot D$ when $D \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Serre duality origin]
\label{rem:serre-duality-origin}
The structural obstruction $\cO_2^{\mathrm{str}}$ has a clean categorical explanation. For a CY$_d$ category, Serre duality gives $\Ext^k(E, F) \cong \Ext^{d-k}(F, E)^*$. The Euler form $\chi(E, F) = \sum_k (-1)^k \dim \Ext^k(E, F)$ therefore satisfies $\chi(E, F) = (-1)^d \chi(F, E)$. For $d$ odd (CY$_3$, CY$_5$, \ldots): $\chi(E, F) = -\chi(F, E)$, the Euler form is \emph{antisymmetric}, and $\rk(\chi) \geq 2$ for any quiver with $\geq 2$ nodes and nonzero arrows. For $d$ even (CY$_2$, CY$_4$, \ldots): $\chi(E, F) = \chi(F, E)$, the Euler form is \emph{symmetric}, and no structural obstruction arises. This is the algebraic shadow of the topological fact that $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^{d-2}))$ is trivial for $d - 2$ odd and $\Z$ for $d - 2$ even.
\end{remark}


\subsubsection{The center-hocolim obstruction}
\label{subsubsec:center-hocolim}

The Drinfeld center does not commute with homotopy colimits. This failure is the categorical origin of the $\Eone$ structure.

\begin{proposition}[Center-hocolim non-commutation]
\label{prop:center-hocolim}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ with multi-chart atlas $\cA = \{(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)\}_{\alpha \in I}$, the canonical map
\[
 \operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha \, \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA_\alpha)\bigr) \;\longrightarrow\; \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(\operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha \CoHA_\alpha)\bigr)
\]
is \emph{not} an equivalence in general. Its cofiber, the \emph{global braiding obstruction}
\[
 \mathrm{Obs}(\cC) \;:=\; \mathrm{cofib}\!\bigl(\operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha \cZ_\alpha \to \cZ_{\mathrm{global}}\bigr),
\]
is zero if and only if $\cC$ has a single stability chamber ($|I| = 1$). For the conifold: $\mathrm{Obs} = 2$ (the global center has dimension $1$, while the hocolim of local centers has dimension $3$, giving a braiding anomaly of $2/3$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $|I| = 1$ (single chart, e.g.\ $\C^3$): the hocolim is the identity, and both sides agree. For $|I| \geq 2$: the hocolim $A = \operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha A_\alpha$ identifies elements across charts via the wall-crossing automorphisms $K_{\alpha\beta}$. The global center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A))$ consists of elements that commute with \emph{all} of $A$, including the gluing data. But the hocolim of local centers $\operatorname{hocolim}_\alpha \cZ(A_\alpha)$ contains elements that commute locally (within each chart) but may fail to commute with the transition maps. The difference is precisely the braiding data that lives on the walls.

For the conifold: $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA_{\mathrm{I}}))$ has dimension $2$ (the unit and the supertrace), $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\CoHA_{\mathrm{II}}))$ has dimension $3$, and their hocolim has dimension $3$ (Morita embedding). The global center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_{\text{conifold}}))$ has dimension $1$ (only the unit commutes with the wall-crossing $K_{(1,1)}$, since $K_{(1,1)}$ does not preserve the supertrace). The obstruction is $3 - 1 = 2$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: $76$ tests in \texttt{drinfeld\_center\_hocolim.py} and $85$ tests in \texttt{swiss\_cheese\_chart\_gluing.py}. The obstruction grows with geometric complexity: $\C^3$ ($0$) $<$ conifold ($2$) $<$ local~$\bP^2$ (nonzero) $<$ $K3 \times E$ (massive, controlled by the full BKM superalgebra).

\subsection{Bar-hocolim commutation}
\label{subsec:bar-hocolim}

The bar construction commutes with homotopy colimits. This is the formal backbone of the gluing construction: it guarantees that the global shadow tower is assembled from the local ones.

\begin{theorem}[Bar-hocolim commutation]
\label{thm:bar-hocolim}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For any diagram $D \colon I \to \Eone\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$ indexed by a finite poset $I$, there is a natural equivalence of $\Eone$-factorization coalgebras
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bar-hocolim}
 B^{\Eone}\!\bigl(\operatorname{hocolim}_I D\bigr) \;\simeq\; \operatorname{hocolim}_I B^{\Eone}(D).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The bar construction $B^{\Eone}$ is the left derived functor of the indecomposables functor from $\Eone$-algebras to $\Eone$-coalgebras. Left derived functors preserve homotopy colimits: if $F \colon \cC \to \cD$ is a left Quillen functor (or, in the $\infty$-categorical setting, a left adjoint), then $F$ commutes with all colimits, and in particular with homotopy colimits.

The bar-cobar adjunction $B^{\Eone} \dashv \Omega^{\Eone}$ is a Quillen adjunction on the category of $\Eone$-algebras (this is the $\Eone$-specialization of the general bar-cobar adjunction of Vol~I, Theorem~A). The left adjoint $B^{\Eone}$ therefore preserves hocolims. The natural equivalence~\eqref{eq:bar-hocolim} is the instance of this general principle for the diagram $D$.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ from charts]
\label{cor:kappa-from-charts}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}
\textup{(}Conditional on Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:e1-chart-gluing}}, which depends transitively on CY-A$_3$ per AP-CY11.\textup{)} Assuming the global $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC$ exists as asserted by Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:e1-chart-gluing}}, the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ is independent of the atlas $\cA$. If every transition mutation $\mu_{\alpha\beta}$ is an $\Eone$-equivalence (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:transition-e1-equiv}}), then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa-from-charts}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\bigl(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)\bigr)
\end{equation}
for any chart $\alpha \in I$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is a homotopy invariant of the $\Eone$-chiral algebra (Vol~I, Theorem~D: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ depends only on the quasi-isomorphism class of the bar complex). By Theorem~\ref{thm:bar-hocolim}, $B^{\Eone}(A_\cC) \simeq \operatorname{hocolim}_I B^{\Eone}(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))$. If the transition maps are equivalences, the hocolim is contractible along any chart, giving $B^{\Eone}(A_\cC) \simeq B^{\Eone}(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))$ for each $\alpha$, whence $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\CoHA(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha))$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{DT = hocolim shadow}
\label{subsec:dt-hocolim-shadow}

The gluing construction connects the shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I to the Donaldson--Thomas partition function. The shadow tower of a CY$_3$ chiral algebra $A_X = \Phi_3(\cC_X)$ encodes the BPS/DT invariants of the underlying CY$_3$ geometry. This is the deepest prediction of the programme: the algebraic shadow tower ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, $\mathrm{obs}_g$, $\Theta_A$) determines the enumerative geometry of $X$ (DT invariants, BPS state counting, Gopakumar--Vafa invariants).

\begin{conjecture}[DT = hocolim shadow]
\label{conj:dt-hocolim-shadow}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
For a smooth projective CY$_3$ variety $X$ admitting a quiver-chart atlas $\cA$, the DT partition function is the $\Eone$-shadow generating function of the global chiral algebra:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dt-shadow}
 Z_{\DT}(X; q) \;=\; Z^{\mathrm{sh},\Eone}(A_X; q).
\end{equation}
At genus $1$: $F_1^{\DT}(X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)/24$. At higher genus, the genus-$g$ DT free energy $F_g^{\DT}(X)$ equals the genus-$g$ shadow $F_g(A_X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) \cdot \lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$ on the uniform-weight lane (UNIFORM-WEIGHT; Vol~I, Theorem~D).
\end{conjecture}


The following conjecture refines Conjecture~\ref{conj:dt-hocolim-shadow} by stratifying the identification into three levels of precision and giving the explicit bar Euler product formula. It is the central enumerative prediction of the CY-to-chiral programme.


\begin{conjecture}[Shadow--BPS/DT correspondence: precise formulation]
\label{conj:shadow-bps-dt}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Input.}
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category satisfying hypotheses \ref{hyp:smooth}--\ref{hyp:framing} of Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}, with CY-to-chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi_3(\cC)$ and modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Output.}
Define the \emph{shadow partition function} of $A_\cC$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:v3-shadow-partition-function}
 Z^{\mathrm{sh}}(A_\cC; q) \;:=\; \prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^n)^{-\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) \cdot n^0}
 \;=\; \prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^n)^{-\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)},
\end{equation}
where the exponent $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot n^0 = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the leading-order (degree-independent) BPS multiplicity predicted by the shadow tower. For CY$_3$ geometries with degree-dependent BPS multiplicities $\Omega(n)$, the refined shadow partition function is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shadow-partition-refined}
 Z^{\mathrm{sh}}_{\mathrm{ref}}(A_\cC; q) \;:=\; \prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^n)^{-\Omega_{\mathrm{sh}}(n)},
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_{\mathrm{sh}}(n) := \dim H^1(B^{\Eone}(A_\cC))_n$ is the degree-$1$ bar cohomology of $A_\cC$ at degree~$n$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Prediction.}
The identification of the shadow partition function with the DT partition function holds at three levels.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(L\arabic*)}]

\item\label{level:genus1} \emph{Genus-$1$ numerical level.} The genus-$1$ free energies agree:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shadow-dt-genus1}
 F_1^{\DT}(X) \;=\; F_1^{\mathrm{sh}}(A_X) \;=\; \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)}{24}.
\end{equation}
This is unconditional: no uniform-weight hypothesis is needed at genus~$1$ (Vol~I, Theorem~D at $g = 1$).

\item\label{level:virtual} \emph{Virtual level (all genera, uniform-weight).} The genus-$g$ DT free energies match the shadow tower on the uniform-weight lane:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shadow-dt-allgenus}
 F_g^{\DT}(X) \;=\; F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}(A_X) \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X) \cdot \lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}
 \quad\text{for all } g \geq 1
 \quad\textup{(UNIFORM-WEIGHT)},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$ is the Faber--Pandharipande tautological intersection number on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$. At $g \geq 2$ with multi-weight input, the scalar formula fails and requires the cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}}$ of Vol~I.

\item\label{level:motivic} \emph{Motivic Hall algebra level.} The bar complex of $A_\cC$ as an $\Eone$-factorization coalgebra is quasi-isomorphic to the motivic DT coalgebra:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shadow-dt-motivic}
 B^{\Eone}(A_\cC) \;\simeq\; \mathcal{H}_{\DT}^{\mathrm{mot}}(\cC)
 \quad\text{as } \Eone\text{-coalgebras in } K_0(\mathrm{Var}/\C)\text{-modules}.
\end{equation}
This is the strongest form: the full coalgebra structure of the bar complex, not merely the Euler characteristic, matches the motivic Hall algebra of Kontsevich--Soibelman. The BPS invariants are recovered as $\Omega_{\mathrm{sh}}(n) = \dim H^1(B^{\Eone})_n = \Omega_{\DT}(n)$.

\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Scope.}
Level~\ref{level:genus1} is proved for $\C^3$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:kappa-c3}}) and verified for $K3 \times E$ (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}). Level~\ref{level:virtual} is conditional on the uniform-weight hypothesis and on the existence of the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi_3$ (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}). Level~\ref{level:motivic} is conjectural in general; it is proved for toric CY$_3$ where the $\CoHA$ provides the motivic comparison.

The conjecture does NOT claim that the naive numerical DT invariants (e.g., the Fourier coefficients of $\Delta_5$ for $K3 \times E$) are recovered by the scalar shadow $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ alone. The higher Fourier coefficients encode information from the full bar complex (all degrees), not merely the leading shadow coefficient. The scalar shadow captures the leading asymptotics; the refined shadow (bar cohomology at all degrees) captures the full DT data.
\end{conjecture}


\begin{remark}[Level of validity and the three regimes]
\label{rem:dt-shadow-level}

The three levels of Conjecture~\ref{conj:shadow-bps-dt} form a hierarchy: \ref{level:motivic} $\Rightarrow$ \ref{level:virtual} $\Rightarrow$ \ref{level:genus1}. The motivic identification \ref{level:motivic} is the fundamental statement; the genus-$1$ identity \ref{level:genus1} is a numerical shadow of the full coalgebra comparison. The intermediate level \ref{level:virtual} captures the tautological intersection theory of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$ (the Faber--Pandharipande coefficients $\lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$) but loses the off-diagonal data in the motivic Hall algebra.

Two distinctions control the passage between levels:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \emph{Scalar vs.\ refined shadow.} The scalar shadow partition function \eqref{eq:v3-shadow-partition-function} uses only $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and gives $\prod (1-q^n)^{-\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}$. The MacMahon function requires the refined shadow with $\Omega(n) = n$, which comes from the full bar cohomology $H^1(B^{\Eone})_n$. The scalar shadow captures $F_1$ but not the full $Z_{\DT}$.
\item \emph{Uniform-weight vs.\ multi-weight.} At genus $g \geq 2$, the scalar formula $F_g = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$ holds on the uniform-weight lane (Vol~I, Theorem~D). For CY$_3$ chiral algebras with generators of multiple conformal weights, the cross-channel correction $\delta F_g^{\mathrm{cross}} \neq 0$ modifies the higher-genus free energies. The full DT free energies require these corrections.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Connection to the factorization envelope}

\begin{remark}[Quiver-chart gluing and factorization envelopes]
\label{rem:quiver-chart-envelope}
The quiver-chart atlas construction interfaces with the factorization envelope technology of Vol~I (Direction~3, Nishinaka--Vicedo) as follows. Each quiver chart $(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ determines a Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\alpha$ via the Ginzburg dg algebra $\mathrm{Gin}(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$: the degree-$1$ part carries a Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket, and the CY$_3$ pairing promotes it to a Lie conformal algebra. The global gluing~\eqref{eq:global-e1-chiral} then has a dual description:
\[
 A_\cC \;\simeq\; \operatorname{hocolim}_I \Fact_X(\mathfrak{L}_\alpha).
\]
The factorization-envelope functor $\Fact_X(-)$ preserves hocolims (it is a left adjoint, being the free construction on a Lie conformal algebra). The global $A_\cC$ is therefore the factorization envelope of the \emph{glued} Lie conformal algebra $\mathfrak{L}_\cC := \operatorname{hocolim}_I \mathfrak{L}_\alpha$. This provides a ``generators-and-relations'' description of $A_\cC$: the generators come from the charts, and the relations encode the mutation equivalences. For the five-step chain of $\C^3$ (Section~\ref{sec:d3-functor-chain}), the atlas has a single chart, and $\mathfrak{L}_\cC = \mathfrak{L}_{\C^3}$ is the abelian Lie conformal algebra of Step~1.
\end{remark}

\section{The CY-to-chiral programme at $d = 3$}
\label{sec:cy-to-chiral-d3-programme}
% ============================================================

\begin{conjecture}[CY-to-chiral functor at $d = 3$: precise formulation;
\ClaimStatusConjectured]
\label{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Hypotheses.}
Let $\cC$ be a CY$_3$ category satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(H\arabic*)}]
 \item\label{hyp:smooth} \emph{Smoothness}: $\cC$ is a smooth (homologically smooth) dg category over $\C$; equivalently, the diagonal bimodule $\cC \in \cC\text{-}\mathrm{bimod}$ is compact.
 \item\label{hyp:proper} \emph{Properness}: $\cC$ is proper; equivalently, $\dim \Hom^\bullet_\cC(E, F) < \infty$ for all $E, F$.
 \item\label{hyp:cy3} \emph{$\CY_3$ structure}: $\cC$ is equipped with a non-degenerate Serre pairing $\langle -, - \rangle \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \otimes \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to \C[-3]$ of degree $-3$, inducing an antisymmetric Euler form $\chi(E, F) = -\chi(F, E)$.
 \item\label{hyp:framing} \emph{Chain-level $\bS^3$-framing}: there exists a trivialization of the class $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot [\Omega_3] \in H^3(B\Sp(2m))$ that is compatible with the $\BV$ operator on the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$.
\end{enumerate}
\textup{(}Conditions \ref{hyp:smooth}--\ref{hyp:cy3} are standard; condition~\ref{hyp:framing} is the substantive hypothesis.\textup{)}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
Under \ref{hyp:smooth}--\ref{hyp:framing}, the CY-to-chiral functor of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}} extends to $d = 3$:
\[
 \Phi_3 \colon \CY_3\text{-}\Cat^{\mathrm{fr}} \longrightarrow \Eone\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg},
\]
producing an $\Eone$-chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi_3(\cC)$ with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C\arabic*)}]
 \item\label{concl:generators} \emph{Generators}: the generating fields of $A_\cC$ are in bijection with $\HH^{\bullet+1}(\cC)$, as for $d = 2$ \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}(i)}\textup{)}.
 \item\label{concl:bar} \emph{Bar identification}: $B^{\Eone}(\Phi_3(\cC)) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet^{\Eone}(\cC)$ as $\Eone$-factorization coalgebras, extending Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}(ii)} from $d = 2$.
 \item\label{concl:e1} \emph{Native $\Eone$ structure}: $A_\cC$ is natively $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$. The $\Etwo$-braided structure on $\Rep(A_\cC)$ arises through the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$, not from the algebra itself \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}} for toric input\textup{)}.
 \item\label{concl:kappa} \emph{Modular characteristic}: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \chi^{\CY}(\cC) := \sum_i (-1)^i \dim \HH_i(\cC)$, the categorical Euler characteristic \textup{(}Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}\textup{)}.
 \item\label{concl:shadow} \emph{Shadow tower}: $A_\cC$ carries the full shadow obstruction tower of Vol~I, with $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-invariance guaranteed by bar-hocolim commutation \textup{(}Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:bar-hocolim}}\textup{)}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The braiding mechanism at $d = 3$.}
The $\bS^3$-framing gives an $E_3$-structure on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$. The $E_3$-structure restricts to $\Etwo$ via Dunn additivity, but this restricted braiding is \emph{symmetric} at the topological level, since $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\R^3))$ is trivial. The quantum group (non-symmetric) braiding for $d = 3$ does NOT arise from the $E_3$-to-$\Etwo$ restriction; it arises through the \emph{Drinfeld center} of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Comparison with $d = 2$.}
At $d = 2$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}}), the functor $\Phi$ produces an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra unconditionally: the $\bS^2$-framing provides native $\Etwo$ structure (the fundamental group $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\R^2)) = \Z$ gives the braiding parameter), and no Drinfeld center passage is needed. At $d = 3$, hypotheses \ref{hyp:smooth}--\ref{hyp:cy3} are parallel to $d = 2$, but the framing hypothesis~\ref{hyp:framing} is new: the topological obstruction in $\pi_3(B\Sp)$ vanishes universally (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}}), but the chain-level $\Ainf$-compatible trivialization is an additional datum.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Scope of the input category.}
The conjecture applies to:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Smooth proper CY$_3$ categories $D^b(\Coh(X))$ for smooth projective CY$_3$ varieties $X$ (quintic, $K3 \times E$, complete intersections).
 \item Toric CY$_3$ categories with $T^3$-equivariant $\Omega$-deformation ($\C^3$, resolved conifold, local $\bP^2$, local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$); for these, the CoHA construction replaces the factorization envelope, and hypothesis~\ref{hyp:framing} is satisfied by holomorphic Chern--Simons.
 \item Ginzburg dg algebras $\mathrm{Gin}(Q, W)$ for quivers with CY$_3$ potential; each such algebra provides a single quiver chart (Definition~\textup{\ref{def:quiver-chart}}).
 \item Generalized CY$_3$ inputs with torsion canonical bundle (Enriques $\times E$), provided the orbifold extension of $\Phi$ is available (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:phi-enriques-z2-orbifold}}).
\end{itemize}
The conjecture does NOT apply to singular CY$_3$ categories (smoothness is essential for the factorization-envelope step) or to non-proper inputs (properness controls the finiteness of the generating space).
\end{conjecture}


\begin{remark}[Evidence and obstacle catalogue for Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:cy-to-chiral-d3}}]
\label{rem:cy-to-chiral-d3-evidence}

The following numbered items collect all evidence for and against the conjecture, with explicit status tags. Evidence items are labelled \textbf{E1}--\textbf{E10}; obstacle items are labelled \textbf{O1}--\textbf{O5}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Evidence.}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{E\arabic*.}, leftmargin=3em]

\item \textbf{$\C^3$ end-to-end verification.} \textsc{[Proved]}
The five-step functor chain (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-functor-chain}}) is verified computationally for $\C^3$: $\mathrm{PV}^*(\C^3) \to \Omega\text{-deformation} \to Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \to \text{Drinfeld center} \to \cW_{1+\infty}$. The output at the self-dual point is $H_1$ (the Heisenberg VOA at level~$1$). Six compute modules, $\sim$600 tests.

\item \textbf{$\bS^3$-framing obstruction vanishes topologically.} \textsc{[Proved]}
$\pi_3(B\Sp(2m)) = 0$ for all $m \geq 1$ (symplectic path); independently, $\pi_3(BU) = 0$ (Bott periodicity). The topological component of the obstruction is zero universally (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:s3-framing-vanishes}}). This removes condition~(a) of hypothesis~\ref{hyp:framing}; only the chain-level BV-compatible trivialization remains.

\item \textbf{$\Eone$ universality for toric CY$_3$.} \textsc{[Proved]}
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:e1-universality-cy3}} establishes, by four independent pillars (abelianity of the classical bracket, one-dimensional deformation space, BV-to-$\Eone$ breaking, $R$-matrix unitarity), that toric CY$_3$ chiral algebras with $\Omega$-deformation are natively $\Eone$. Verified for $\C^3$, resolved conifold, local~$\bP^2$, and the quintic (89 tests).

\item \textbf{$\Eone \to \Etwo$ enhancement obstruction trivial.} \textsc{[Proved for tested cases]}
Corollary~\textup{\ref{cor:e1-e2-trivial}}: the enhancement obstruction vanishes for $\C^3$, the resolved conifold, and $K3 \times E$. The CY condition $g(z)g(-z) = 1$ forces the $R$-matrix unitarity that controls the obstruction (217 tests across two compute modules).

\item \textbf{Quiver-chart gluing for toric CY$_3$.} \textsc{[Proved for toric; conjectural in general]}
Wall-crossing mutations induce $\Eone$-algebra equivalences (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:transition-e1-equiv}}), verified for the resolved conifold and local~$\bP^2$. The bar-hocolim commutation theorem (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:bar-hocolim}}) guarantees $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-invariance of the global algebra. The general tilting-chart cover (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:tilting-chart-cover}}) and the full $\Eone$ chart gluing (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:e1-chart-gluing}}) remain conjectural.

\item \textbf{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\C^3) = 1$: five-path verification.} \textsc{[Proved]}
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:kappa-c3}} establishes $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\C^3) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_1) = 1$ by five independent paths (Heisenberg level, MacMahon leading order, bar Euler product, DT genus-$1$ free energy, categorical Euler characteristic).

\item \textbf{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ formula verified for fibered CY$_3$.} \textsc{[Partial]}
For $K3 \times E$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ by additivity (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:categorical-euler}}). For Enriques~$\times E$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2$ (72 tests via \texttt{enriques\_shadow.py}). The general formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi^{\CY}(\cC)$ for arbitrary compact CY$_3$ is conditional on the functor itself.

\item \textbf{DT/shadow correspondence at genus~$1$.} \textsc{[Verified for $\C^3$ and $K3 \times E$]}
Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:dt-hocolim-shadow}} predicts $F_1^{\DT}(X) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)/24$. For $\C^3$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ gives $F_1 = 1/24$, matching the MacMahon function (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:kappa-c3}}). For $K3 \times E$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ and the genus-$1$ shadow matches.

\item \textbf{Drinfeld center identification for $\C^3$.} \textsc{[Proved]}
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1))) \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)) \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(\cW_{1+\infty})$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}}). This is the mechanism by which the non-symmetric braiding arises from the $\Eone$ structure without native $\Etwo$.

\item \textbf{\v{C}ech contracting homotopy for compact CY$_3$.} \textsc{[Proved, perturbative]}
For the quintic threefold, an algebraic \v{C}ech contracting homotopy satisfying all SDR conditions is constructed (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cech-contracting-homotopy}}). This provides the chain-level trivialization at the perturbative (formal deformation) level; the non-perturbative convergence question remains open (Remark~\textup{\ref{rem:cech-perturbative-scope}}).

\end{enumerate}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Obstacles.}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O\arabic*.}, leftmargin=3em]

\item \textbf{Chain-level $\bS^3$-framing: $\Ainf$-compatibility.} \textsc{[Severity: HIGH]}
The topological obstruction vanishes (\textbf{E2}), and holomorphic Chern--Simons provides a chain-level trivialization. The remaining gap: this trivialization must be compatible with the full $\Ainf$-structure on $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$. The CS functional is checked at the perturbative level for the quintic (\textbf{E10}) but not at the non-perturbative level for general compact CY$_3$. This is hypothesis~\ref{hyp:framing} and is the single load-bearing open condition.

\item \textbf{$\Omega$-deformation non-uniqueness for compact CY$_3$.} \textsc{[Severity: MODERATE]}
For toric CY$_3$, the $\Omega$-deformation is unique (one-dimensional, spanned by $\sigma_3$; Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:c3-hochschild}}). For compact CY$_3$ without torus action, the analogue of the $\Omega$-deformation is the global complex structure deformation, which lives in $\HH^2(\mathrm{PV}^*) = H^1(T_X)$ and can be multi-dimensional ($h^{2,1} > 1$ for most CY$_3$). The functor $\Phi_3$ should not depend on the choice within this family, but this independence is unverified.

\item \textbf{Center-hocolim non-commutation.} \textsc{[Severity: MODERATE]}
The Drinfeld center does not commute with homotopy colimits (Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:center-hocolim}}). For multi-chart CY$_3$, the global $\Etwo$-braided category is NOT the hocolim of the local Drinfeld centers. The $\Etwo$ recovery via the Drinfeld center is a \emph{global} construction and cannot be assembled chart-by-chart. The obstruction grows with geometric complexity: $0$ for $\C^3$, $2$ for the conifold, larger for local~$\bP^2$.

\item \textbf{Motivic vs numerical DT identification.} \textsc{[Severity: LOW]}
The DT/shadow correspondence (Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:dt-hocolim-shadow}}) is expected to hold at the motivic Hall algebra level but fails at the naive numerical level for higher-genus terms. The identification $Z_{\DT} = Z^{\mathrm{sh},\Eone}$ requires virtual fundamental class corrections beyond the leading $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ coefficient. This does not obstruct the functor $\Phi_3$ itself but limits its computational reach.

\item \textbf{Global quantum group object $G(\cC)$.} \textsc{[Severity: HIGH]}
The conjecture predicts a ``quantum vertex chiral group'' $G(\cC)$. For $\C^3$, the output $Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is a well-defined Yangian, and the Drinfeld center gives the braided category. For general CY$_3$, the global group object $G(\cC)$ encoding the full representation theory is not constructed: it requires assembling the local Yangians $Y^+(Q_\alpha, W_\alpha)$ into a global quantum group via the Drinfeld double, and the Drinfeld double of a hocolim is not the hocolim of Drinfeld doubles (compare \textbf{O3}). This is AP-CY7 territory.

\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Compatibility with Koszul duality}
\label{sec:cy-koszul-compat}
% ============================================================

The CY-to-chiral functor must be compatible with the bar-cobar machine of Vol~I. Mirror symmetry for CY categories should correspond to chiral Koszul duality; the bar complex of the chiral algebra $A_\cC$ should recover the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$. The following results verify both identifications.

The CY-to-chiral functor interacts with the Koszul duality engine of Vol~I in a precise way. The bar complex of the chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ is quasi-isomorphic to the cyclic bar complex of $\cC$ as a factorization coalgebra (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}(ii)). The Koszul dual $A_\cC^! = \Phi(\cC^!)$ is the chiral algebra of the mirror category.

\begin{conjecture}[CY mirror symmetry and chiral Koszul duality]
\label{conj:cy-koszul-mirror}
\ClaimStatusConjectured{}
For a mirror pair $(\cC, \cC^\vee)$ of CY$_d$ categories, the CY-to-chiral functor intertwines mirror symmetry with chiral Koszul duality:
\[
 \Phi(\cC^\vee) \simeq \Phi(\cC)^!.
\]
Equivalently, the bar complex of the mirror chiral algebra is the Verdier dual of the bar complex:
\[
 B(\Phi(\cC^\vee)) \simeq D_{\Ran}(B(\Phi(\cC))).
\]
For $\C^3$ at the self-dual point ($h_1 = 1, h_2 = 0, h_3 = -1$), the mirror is $\C^3$ itself. The Koszul dual of the Heisenberg VOA $H_1$ is $H_1^! = \Sym^{\mathrm{ch}}(V^*)$. At the level of modular characteristics, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_1) = 1$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_1^!) = -1$, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_1) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_1^!) = 0$, consistent with the KM/free-field complementarity rule (Vol~I).
\end{conjecture}

The Koszul compatibility conjecture predicts that mirror symmetry intertwines with chiral Koszul duality at the level of the functor $\Phi$, and that complementarity $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = 0$ holds on the KM/free-field lane. A prerequisite for testing these predictions is a precise determination of the modular characteristic itself. The next section confronts the fact that the naive candidate $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ fails for most CY$_3$ geometries, and identifies the categorical Euler characteristic as the correct invariant.

\section{The CY modular characteristic}
\label{sec:cy-modular-char}
% ============================================================

The modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ of a CY$_3$ chiral algebra is determined by the CY category, not by the Virasoro central charge alone. This section establishes the key distinction between $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$.

\begin{theorem}[$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24 \neq \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ in general]
\label{thm:chi-neq-kappa}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The topological Euler characteristic $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)/24$ does \emph{not} equal the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)$ in general. Explicit counterexamples:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
 \toprule
 \textbf{CY variety} & $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ & $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ & \textbf{Match?} \\
 \midrule
 Elliptic curve $E$ ($\CY_1$) & $0$ & $1$ & No \\
 K3 surface ($\CY_2$) & $1$ & $2$\rlap{$^*$} & No \\
 $K3 \times E$ ($\CY_3$) & $0$ & $3$\rlap{$^\dagger$} & No \\
 Resolved conifold & $1/12$ & $1$ & No \\
 Local $\bP^2$ & $1/8$ & $3/2$ & No \\
 \midrule
 Quintic ($\chi = -200$) & $-25/3$ & $-25/3$ & Yes \\
 $\bP^5[3,3]$ ($\chi = -144$) & $-6$ & $-6$ & Yes \\
 $\bP^5[2,4]$ ($\chi = -176$) & $-22/3$ & $-22/3$ & Yes \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
{\footnotesize $^*$The K3 entry records $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{K3}}) = 2$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification}; verified via chiral de Rham, Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e}). The distinct quantity $\dim \HH_*(D^b(\mathrm{K3}))/2 = 12$ arises from an alternative algebraization (the Monster orbifold); the full trichotomy ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \in \{2, 12, 24\}$) is developed in Section~\ref{subsec:k3-three-algebraizations} of Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e}.

The Borcherds automorphic weight is $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5 = \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)$, a distinct invariant.}

\smallskip

\noindent The BCOV formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ holds for compact CICYs with $h^{1,0} = 0$ (complete intersections in products of projective spaces) but fails systematically for K3-fibered CY$_3$ (which have $h^{1,0} \geq 1$) and for non-compact toric CY$_3$.

% RECTIFICATION-FLAG (RESOLVED): The K3 (CY_2) entry now records kappa_ch = 2 = chi(O_K3),
% consistent with Conjecture conj:cy-kappa-identification and the K3 sigma model value
% (Theorem thm:k3-kappa in k3_times_e.tex, verified 5 ways). The alternative
% algebraization dim HH_*(D^b(K3))/2 = 12 is noted in the footnote as a distinct object.
% K3xE entry now records kappa_ch = 3 (additivity: 2+1), distinct from kappa_BKM = 5.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 119 tests in \texttt{cy3\_grand\_atlas.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The BCOV formula $F_1 = -\tfrac{1}{2} \log \det(\bar{\partial}^\dagger \bar{\partial})$ on a compact CY$_3$ $X$ gives $F_1 = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)/24$ when $h^{1,0}(X) = 0$. The condition $h^{1,0} = 0$ ensures that the genus-$1$ free energy has no contribution from abelian zero modes; the formula holds for the quintic ($h^{2,1} = 101$) and all other CICYs with $h^{1,0} = 0$, regardless of the number of complex structure moduli.

For K3-fibered CY$_3$, the infinite tower of BPS bound states across fibers contributes additional genus-$1$ data beyond the one-loop determinant. For $K3 \times E$: the $24$ free bosons from the K3 fiber give $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$, but the sewing along $E$ via the DMVV formula introduces imaginary root contributions that produce $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5$ (the weight of the Igusa cusp form). The ``lost'' $19$ units ($24 - 5 = 19$) are absorbed by the Borcherds product structure.

For the resolved conifold: $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 2$ (the total space deformation retracts onto $\bP^1$) gives $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24 = 1/12$, but the DT computation gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$ (the conifold has a single BPS state contributing at genus~$1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The categorical Euler characteristic resolves the discrepancy]
\label{prop:categorical-euler}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The invariant controlling the BKM modular characteristic is the \emph{categorical} Euler characteristic $\chi^{\CY}$, not the topological one. For $K3 \times E$: $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 0$ but $\chi^{\CY} = 5 = \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$; the chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ arises from additivity ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(E) = 2 + 1$).

The categorical Euler characteristic accounts for the full BPS spectrum (all charges), not just the massless modes that contribute to $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$. For K3-fibered CY$_3$, the infinite tower of bound states across fibers contributes to $\chi^{\CY}$ via the Borcherds denominator weight.

Five independent verifications of $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(K3 \times E) = 5$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item Weight of the Igusa cusp form: $\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5$.
 \item DMVV exponent: the Borcherds lift weight formula gives $c(0)/2 = 10/2 = 5$.
 \item Bar Euler product: the genus-$1$ coefficient of $\log Z_{\DT}$ gives $F_1 = 5/24$.
 \item Relative DT: fiber-by-fiber computation via K3 fibers sewed along $E$.
 \item Anomaly cancellation: the worldsheet anomaly for the $K3 \times E$ sigma model.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 78 tests in \texttt{k3e\_relative\_shadow.py}; 119 tests in \texttt{cy3\_grand\_atlas.py}.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation as genus spectral sequence}
\label{subsec:bcov-genus-spectral}

The Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa holomorphic anomaly equation (HAE) for the topological B-model on a CY$_3$ $X$ has a natural interpretation in the bar-complex framework: it is the genus spectral sequence of the $\Eone$ bar complex.

\begin{theorem}[HAE $=$ MC genus spectral sequence, structural identification]
\label{thm:hae-mc-structural}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation
\[
 \bar{\partial}_{\bar{\imath}} F_g = \tfrac{1}{2} \bar{C}^{jk}_{\bar{\imath}}
 \bigl(D_j D_k F_{g-1} + \textstyle\sum_{r=1}^{g-1} D_j F_r \cdot D_k F_{g-r}\bigr)
\]
is the genus-$g$ projection of the MC equation $D \cdot \Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = 0$ in the Costello--Li dgLa, where:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $\bar{\partial}_{\bar{\imath}} F_g$ corresponds to the $d_1$ differential of the genus spectral sequence $E_1^{g,*} \to E_1^{g+1,*}$;
 \item $\bar{C}^{jk}_{\bar{\imath}}$ corresponds to the bar propagator $d\log E(z,w)$;
 \item $D_j D_k F_{g-1}$ corresponds to the handle-creation/sewing term;
 \item $\sum D_j F_r \cdot D_k F_{g-r}$ corresponds to $[\Theta^{(r)}, \Theta^{(g-r)}]$ (factorization).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Costello--Li dgLa $\mathfrak{g}_X = \mathrm{PV}^*(X)[1]$ with the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket and BRST differential has MC moduli space $= $ B-model moduli. The genus expansion of the B-model free energy is the genus spectral sequence of the MC element, and the HAE is its $d_1$ differential.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 130 tests in \texttt{test\_bcov\_e1\_shadow\_engine.py}, including the HAE-MC dictionary, propagator identification, and explicit genus-$1$ through genus-$5$ comparisons for $\C^3$ and the conifold (\texttt{bcov\_e1\_shadow\_engine.py}).

\begin{remark}[BCOV holomorphic anomaly as $\Eone$ shadow connection]
\label{rem:bcov-shadow-connection}
The Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa holomorphic anomaly equation for the genus-$g$ topological string free energy is the $\Eone$ shadow connection $\nabla^{\mathrm{sh}}$ of Volume~I (Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-connection}), restricted to the complex structure moduli of the CY$_3$. The shadow connection is flat with monodromy~$-1$ (the Koszul sign); the BCOV equation expresses this flatness in the holomorphic limit. The genus spectral sequence (Theorem~\ref{thm:hae-mc-structural}) organises the holomorphic anomaly into a degeneration at each genus page.

Two regimes illustrate the scope and limitations of the identification:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item For CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0} = 0$ (rigid CICYs): $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$, and the BCOV propagator is the genus-$1$ shadow. The shadow connection $\nabla^{\mathrm{sh}} = d - Q'_L/(2Q_L)\, dt$ specialises to the Picard--Fuchs connection on the $B$-model moduli space.
 \item For K3-fibered CY$_3$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \neq \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:chi-neq-kappa}), and the shadow connection carries fiber-global mixing data beyond the BCOV framework. The discrepancy $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} - \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ measures the BPS bound-state contribution from the K3 fiber, absorbed by the Borcherds product structure.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent\textit{Verification}: 130 tests in \texttt{bcov\_e1\_shadow\_engine.py}, including 10 tests quantifying the K3-fibered discrepancy.
\end{remark}

\section{Beyond CY$_3$: chiral algebras from non-CY geometries}
\label{sec:beyond-cy3}
% ============================================================

The CY-to-chiral functor extends beyond the Calabi--Yau setting. For a rank-$2$ bundle $E \to C$ over a smooth curve $C$ of genus~$g$, the total space $\mathrm{Tot}(E)$ carries a chiral algebra $A_E$ regardless of whether $\det(E) \cong K_C$.

\begin{definition}[CY defect]
\label{def:cy-defect}
For $E \to C$ a rank-$2$ bundle, the \emph{CY defect} is $\delta := \deg(\det E) - \deg(K_C) = \deg(\det E) - (2g - 2)$. The CY condition is $\delta = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Curved chiral algebra from non-CY local surfaces]
\label{thm:curved-chiral-non-cy}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
When $\delta \neq 0$, the chiral algebra $A_E$ is curved: the curvature element $m_0 = \delta \cdot \omega_C$ is nonzero, the bar complex satisfies $d^2 = [m_0, -]$, and the shadow obstruction tower satisfies the inhomogeneous MC equation
\[
 D \cdot \Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] = m_0.
\]
The curved modular characteristic is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa-curved}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{crv}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \frac{\delta^2 \, \chi(C)}{24},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the uncurved value at $\delta = 0$. The correction is quadratic in~$\delta$, linear in~$\chi(C)$, and vanishes identically on the torus ($g = 1$, $\chi = 0$).

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 119 tests in \texttt{curved\_shadow\_non\_cy.py}; 130 tests in \texttt{rank2\_bundle\_chiral.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Hitchin modular characteristic]
\label{thm:hitchin-kappa}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For the Hitchin system on a genus-$g$ curve $C$ with gauge group $\mathrm{GL}(n)$:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathrm{Hitchin}\;\mathrm{GL}(n), C_g) = g,
\]
independent of~$n$. The $\mathrm{SL}(n)$ factor at the critical level $k = -n$ has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\widehat{\fsl}_{n, -n}) = 0$ (the algebraic manifestation of classical integrability of the Hitchin system). Only the $\mathrm{GL}(1)$ center contributes: the rank-$g$ Heisenberg from $T^*\mathrm{Jac}(C)$ gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = g$.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 242 tests in \texttt{higgs\_bundle\_chiral.py}; 290 tests in \texttt{spectral\_curve\_chiral.py}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Kapustin--Witten twist parameter dependence]
\label{prop:kw-kappa-linear}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For the Kapustin--Witten twisted $\cN = 4$ gauge theory with gauge group~$G$ and twist parameter $t \in \C\bP^1$:
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_t) = -\frac{\dim(\frakg) \cdot t}{2}.
\]
At $t = 0$ (critical level): $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 0$, commutative ($\Einf$). At $t = 1$ (balanced): $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = -\dim(\frakg)/2$. At $t = \infty$ (dual critical): $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \to \infty$. Complementarity on the KM/free-field lane: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_t) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_t^!) = \dim(\frakg)/2$ for all~$t$ and all gauge groups.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 137 tests in \texttt{kw\_twisted\_n4\_chiral.py}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[The BCOV constant-map formula vs the shadow formula]
\label{rem:bcov-vs-shadow}
%: structural identification correct, quantitative instantiation fails at g >= 2.
The identification BCOV $=$ shadow is \emph{structural}: the holomorphic anomaly equation IS the genus spectral sequence of an MC equation in the Costello--Li dgLa. However, the \emph{quantitative} formula $F_g = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \lambda_g^{\mathrm{FP}}$ fails for compact CY$_3$ at $g \geq 2$. The BCOV constant-map formula involves the product $B_{2g} \cdot B_{2g-2}$ of two consecutive Bernoulli numbers, while the shadow formula involves $B_{2g}$ alone. Since $B_{2g-2}/B_{2g}$ varies with~$g$, no single~$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ reconciles the two at all genera. For the quintic: the effective $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ matching $F_g^{\mathrm{CM}}$ oscillates ($200, -28.6, -4.3, 2.8, -3.8$ for $g = 1, \ldots, 5$). The shadow formula applies to the \emph{uniform-weight lane} (free fields, toric CY$_3$); for compact CY$_3$, the full shadow tower $\Theta_A$ (all degrees) is needed.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 130 tests in \texttt{bcov\_e1\_shadow\_engine.py}, including 10 tests quantifying the genus-dependent disagreement.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ polysemy]
\label{rem:cy3-kappa-polysemy}
The symbol~$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ appears in at least four distinct roles across the programme:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$: the modular characteristic (Vol~I Theorem~D), from $F_1 = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot \lambda_1^{\mathrm{FP}}$.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{BCOV}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)/24$: the BCOV anomaly coefficient. Equals~$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)$ for rigid compact CICYs with $h^{1,0} = 0$, but not in general.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{MacMahon}}$: the exponent in $M(q)^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}$. Equals $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)/2$ for $\mathrm{Tot}(K_S)$.
 \item $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$: the weight of the BKM automorphic form. For $K3 \times E$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5) = 5$.
\end{enumerate}
These coincide for Heisenberg ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = k$) and Virasoro ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = c/2$) but diverge for general CY$_3$. The quintic alone admits three values: $-25/3$, $-100$, $200$.
\end{remark}


\begin{proposition}[CoHA of non-CY threefolds]
\label{prop:coha-non-cy}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a quiver with potential $(Q, W)$ that does NOT satisfy the CY$_3$ condition
(i.e.\ $\dim \Ext^1(S_i, S_j) \neq \dim \Ext^2(S_j, S_i)$ for some simples):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The CoHA $\cH(Q, W) = \bigoplus_{\mathbf{d}} H^{\mathrm{BM}}_*(\Crit(\Tr W)_{\mathbf{d}}, \varphi_{\Tr W})$ is still a well-defined associative ($\Eone$) algebra.
 \item The Euler form $\chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2)$ is NOT antisymmetric: the symmetric part $s(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) = \chi(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) + \chi(\gamma_2, \gamma_1)$ is the CY defect at the categorical level.
 \item The bar complex $B(\cH(Q,W))$ is curved: $d^2 = [m_0, -]$ with $m_0$ proportional to the symmetric part of the Euler form.
 \item The Davison--Meinhardt PBW filtration requires the CY$_3$ Serre duality; it may fail for non-CY quivers.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 141 tests in \texttt{test\_coha\_non\_cy\_threefold.py}, including explicit computations for the Jordan quiver with cubic potential ($W = x^3$, curvature $m_0 = 1$), the asymmetric $2$-vertex quiver ($3+1$ arrows, CY defect $= -4$), and Hall multiplication associativity for all tested quivers (\texttt{coha\_non\_cy\_threefold.py}).
\end{proposition}


\begin{proposition}[Spectral curves and the quantum Hitchin system]
\label{prop:spectral-curve-chiral}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The Beilinson--Drinfeld quantization of the Hitchin system on a genus-$g$ curve $C$ with gauge group $\mathrm{GL}(n)$ produces a commutative chiral algebra at the critical level $k = -n$:
\[
 Z\bigl(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{n, -n}\bigr) \;=\; \mathrm{Fun}\bigl(\mathrm{Op}_{\mathrm{PGL}_n}(C)\bigr),
\]
the algebra of functions on $\mathrm{PGL}_n$-opers. The generators are the quantum Hitchin Hamiltonians, with spins equal to the Casimir degrees $d_1, \ldots, d_r$ of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$. The spectral curve $\Sigma_b \subset \mathrm{Tot}(K_C)$ has genus $g(\Sigma_b) = n^2(g-1) + 1$, and the Hitchin base has dimension $\dim B_{\mathrm{GL}(n)} = n^2(g-1) + 1$ (the Prym--Hitchin equality).

The Nekrasov--Shatashvili limit ($\varepsilon_2 \to 0$) recovers the oper as the symbol of the quantum spectral curve; WKB exactness holds for $\mathrm{SL}(2)$.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 290 tests in \texttt{test\_spectral\_curve\_chiral.py}, including spectral curve genus via three paths (formula, Riemann--Hurwitz, adjunction), Hitchin base dimensions for all simple Lie types through $E_8$, and the Prym--Hitchin equality for $81$ $(n,g)$ pairs (\texttt{spectral\_curve\_chiral.py}).
\end{proposition}
