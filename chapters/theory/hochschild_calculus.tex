\chapter{Hochschild Calculus for CY Categories}
\label{ch:hochschild-calculus}

A Calabi--Yau $d$-category has a non-degenerate Serre pairing. What does this pairing force on the Hochschild invariants? For a general dg category, Hochschild homology $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ carries only a Connes $B$-operator and Hochschild cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ carries only a Gerstenhaber bracket; the two are linked by a cap product, but no intrinsic pairing relates them. The CY structure supplies exactly this pairing, and the rest of the chapter is its consequence.

\begin{remark}[Three Hochschild theories]
\label{rem:hochschild-convention-categorical}
Three distinct Hochschild theories appear in the three volumes and must be kept separate.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Categorical Hochschild} $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}$ (this chapter): input a dg category $\cC$; cyclic bar complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$; output $E_2$-algebra (Deligne); for $\cC$ a $\CY_d$ category, also a $(2{-}d)$-shifted Poisson / $\BV$ structure (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:shifted-poisson}}).
 \item \emph{Topological Hochschild} $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{top}}$: input an $E_1$-algebra over a base ring; output $E_2$-algebra (Deligne; Lurie). The Hochschild theory of Vol~II's slab algebra of 3d HT theories.
 \item \emph{Chiral Hochschild} $\ChirHoch^\bullet$: input an $E_\infty$-chiral algebra on a curve $X$; output concentrated in cohomological degrees $\{0,1,2\}$ on the curve-ambient reading (Vol~I Theorem~H; $d=1$ Ran ambient). Under $\Phi_d$ with $d \ge 2$, the concentration enlarges to $\{0,1,2,d\}$ in the cohomological lane; chain-level is infinite for class-M inputs at $d \ge 3$. The chiral Gerstenhaber bracket on $\ChirHoch^\bullet$ is not the topological one.
\end{enumerate}
The CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ of Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}} sends the categorical input $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ to a chiral output whose own chiral Hochschild cohomology $\ChirHoch^\bullet(\Phi_d(\cC))$ is sharper (concentrated in $\{0,1,2\}$). The categorical Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}$ maps to the chiral convolution bracket on $\ChirHoch^\bullet$ via the bar-cobar passage of Vol~I Chapter~\textup{\texttt{chapters/theory/chiral\_climax\_platonic.tex} (Vol~I)}; this is not an identification of objects, only of structures. The chain-level route runs through Stage~1 of the two-stage factorisation: $\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$ is assembled from $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ with its Gerstenhaber bracket via Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality (Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}); the chiral specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}$ then descends to $\ChirHoch^\bullet$ on the reference curve, with the topological Hochschild $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{top}}$ of Vol~II entering only the slab-algebra side of the Swiss-cheese boundary, not the $\PhiFA_d$ interior. Throughout this chapter, $\HH^\bullet$ without qualifier means $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\kappa_\bullet$}{kappa-.} convention in this chapter]
\label{rem:hochschild-kappa-convention}
Every $\kappa_\bullet$-invariant carries a subscript from $\{\mathrm{ch}, \mathrm{cat}, \mathrm{BKM}, \mathrm{fiber}\}$; against the two-stage factorisation $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C} \circ \PhiFA_d$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}), these split as tier~(i) CY-datum intrinsic ($\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$), tier~(ii) stage-$1$ invariant ($\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$, read off $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on $X$), and tier~(iii) stage-$2$ specialisation-dependent ($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$). Categorical Hochschild data of this chapter feed stage~$1$ of the factorisation through the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $1-d$ on $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$.
\end{remark}

The Serre pairing provides a quasi-isomorphism $\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \HH_{d-\bullet}(\cC)$ of degree $-d$, so that Hochschild cohomology inherits the $B$-operator and Hochschild homology inherits the Gerstenhaber bracket. The combination is a $(2-d)$-shifted Poisson (equivalently, $\BV_{2-d}$) structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$: under the PTVV convention an $n$-shifted Poisson bracket has cohomological degree $-n$, so the bracket $\{-,-\}_{2-d}$ has degree $d-2$, and the compatibility $[B, -] = \{-,-\}$ with $|B| = +1$ holds on the Hochschild complex. At $d = 2$ this is a $0$-shifted Poisson structure (ordinary Poisson, bracket of degree $0$); at $d = 3$, a $(-1)$-shifted Poisson structure (the CY$_3$ case of Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi, bracket of degree $+1$, i.e.\ a $\BV$ algebra); at $d = 1$, a $1$-shifted Poisson structure (bracket of degree $-1$) of the kind that drives factorization homology.

The shifted Poisson structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is the categorical precursor of the convolution Lie bracket on the modular deformation complex of Volume~I: the Gerstenhaber bracket becomes the bar-cobar convolution bracket, the Connes $B$-operator becomes the modular differential, and the Serre pairing becomes the cyclic duality that controls the $\bS^d$-framing. The cyclic bar complex of Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf} is the single-object specialization of the Hochschild complex constructed here. The Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $1-d$ on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is precisely the datum that Stage~1 of the two-stage factorisation $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C} \circ \PhiFA_d$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}) feeds to Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality; the operadic level $n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)$ of the resulting chiral algebra on the reference curve is the bracket-degree invariant of Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}.


\section{Hochschild homology and cohomology}
\label{sec:hh-basics}

The cyclic bar complex of a dg category is built from the multi-object generalisation of the cyclic $\Ainf$-complex of Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}, replacing a single algebra by tensor strings of morphisms $X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots \to X_n \to X_0$. The Hochschild homology and cohomology defined below are the Morita-invariant invariants extracted from this complex.

\begin{definition}[Hochschild complex]
\label{def:hochschild-complex}
Let $\cC$ be a dg category over $k$. The \emph{Hochschild chain complex}
of $\cC$ is
\[
 \mathrm{CC}_n(\cC)
 = \bigoplus_{X_0, \ldots, X_n \in \mathrm{Ob}(\cC)}
 \Hom_\cC(X_{n}, X_0) \otimes
 \Hom_\cC(X_{n-1}, X_n) \otimes \cdots \otimes
 \Hom_\cC(X_0, X_1)
\]
with the Hochschild differential
\begin{align*}
 b(f_0 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_n)
 &= \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (-1)^{\epsilon_i}\,
 f_0 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_i f_{i+1} \otimes \cdots \otimes f_n \\
 &\quad + (-1)^{\epsilon_n}\, f_n f_0 \otimes f_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_{n-1},
\end{align*}
where $\epsilon_i = |f_0| + \cdots + |f_i|$ (Koszul signs). The
\emph{Hochschild homology} is $\HH_\bullet(\cC) = H_\bullet(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC), b)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hochschild cohomology]
\label{def:hochschild-cohomology}
The \emph{Hochschild cochain complex} of a dg category $\cC$ is
\[
 \mathrm{CC}^n(\cC)
 = \prod_{X_0, \ldots, X_n \in \mathrm{Ob}(\cC)}
 \Hom_k\bigl(
 \Hom_\cC(X_{n-1}, X_n) \otimes \cdots \otimes \Hom_\cC(X_0, X_1),\;
 \Hom_\cC(X_0, X_n)
 \bigr)
\]
with the Hochschild coboundary $\delta$ dual to $b$. The
\emph{Hochschild cohomology} is
$\HH^\bullet(\cC) = H^\bullet(\mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC), \delta)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Morita invariance]
\label{rem:hh-morita}
Both $\HH_\bullet$ and $\HH^\bullet$ are Morita invariant: if
$\cC$ and $\cD$ are derived Morita equivalent (i.e.,
$\Perf(\cC) \simeq \Perf(\cD)$ as dg categories), then
$\HH_\bullet(\cC) \simeq \HH_\bullet(\cD)$ and
$\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \HH^\bullet(\cD)$. This is essential for
the CY programme, where different geometric models (Fukaya, derived
coherent sheaves, matrix factorizations) of the same CY category must
produce the same Hochschild invariants.
\end{remark}


\section{The Gerstenhaber bracket}
\label{sec:gerstenhaber-bracket}

The Hochschild cochain complex $\mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC)$ is a pre-Lie algebra under operadic composition of multilinear maps, and the pre-Lie commutator descends to a graded Lie bracket of degree $-1$ on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$. Combined with the cup product, this is the Gerstenhaber algebra structure.

\begin{theorem}[Gerstenhaber structure on \texorpdfstring{$\HH^\bullet$}{HH-.}]
\label{thm:gerstenhaber}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The Hochschild cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ carries two compatible
structures:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item A graded commutative \emph{cup product}
 $\smile \colon \HH^p(\cC) \otimes \HH^q(\cC) \to \HH^{p+q}(\cC)$,
 the composition of multilinear maps;
 \item A degree $(-1)$ Lie bracket
 $[\cdot, \cdot] \colon
 \HH^p(\cC) \otimes \HH^q(\cC) \to \HH^{p+q-1}(\cC)$
 (the \emph{Gerstenhaber bracket}), defined at the cochain level by
 \[
 [f, g] = f \circ g - (-1)^{(|f|-1)(|g|-1)} g \circ f
 \]
 where $\circ$ denotes the operadic pre-Lie composition:
 \begin{align*}
 (f \circ g)(a_1, \ldots, a_{p+q-1})
 &= \sum_{i=1}^{p} (-1)^{\dagger_i}\,
 f(a_1, \ldots, a_{i-1}, g(a_i, \ldots, a_{i+q-1}), a_{i+q}, \ldots, a_{p+q-1}), \\
 \dagger_i &= (i-1)(q-1) + (|g|-1)\sum_{j<i}(|a_j|-1).
 \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
Together, the cup product and Gerstenhaber bracket make
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ a \emph{Gerstenhaber algebra}: the bracket is a
derivation of the cup product in each variable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Gerstenhaber = \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} cohomology]
\label{rem:gerstenhaber-e2}
By Kontsevich's formality theorem and the Deligne conjecture
(proved by Kontsevich--Soibelman, McClure--Smith, and others),
$\mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC)$ carries a natural $E_2$-algebra structure
whose cohomology-level shadow is the Gerstenhaber algebra
$(\HH^\bullet(\cC), \smile, [\cdot, \cdot])$. The $E_2$-structure
is the chain-level origin of the braided monoidal category
structure on $\Rep^{E_2}(\Zder(A))$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Gerstenhaber bracket controls deformations]
\label{prop:gerstenhaber-deformations}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
The Gerstenhaber bracket controls the deformation theory of
$\cC$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\HH^2(\cC)$ classifies first-order deformations of
 $\cC$ as a dg category;
 \item The obstruction to extending a first-order deformation
 $\mu_1 \in \HH^2(\cC)$ to second order lies in $\HH^3(\cC)$
 and is given by the Gerstenhaber square
 $\frac{1}{2}[\mu_1, \mu_1]$;
 \item A formal deformation is an element
 $\mu = \sum_{n \geq 1} \hbar^n \mu_n \in \HH^2(\cC)[[\hbar]]$
 satisfying the Maurer--Cartan equation
 $\delta \mu + \frac{1}{2}[\mu, \mu] = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
The MC equation for deformations of $\cC$ maps, under $\Phi$, to
the MC equation $D\Theta_A + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta_A, \Theta_A] = 0$
of the modular convolution algebra $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}_A$ of Vol~I (the bar-cobar Lie algebra whose Maurer--Cartan elements parametrise modular characteristic corrections; Vol~I, thm:mc2-bar-intrinsic).
\end{proposition}


\section{The Connes \texorpdfstring{$B$}{B}-operator and cyclic homology}
\label{sec:connes-b}

The Gerstenhaber structure is one half of the Hochschild calculus; the other half lives on homology and encodes the $S^1$-action on the cyclic bar complex. The Connes $B$-operator is the chain-level incarnation of this action.

\begin{definition}[Connes \texorpdfstring{$B$}{B}-operator]
\label{def:connes-b}
The \emph{Connes operator}
$B \colon \mathrm{CC}_n(\cC) \to \mathrm{CC}_{n+1}(\cC)$ is the composite $B = (1 \otimes -) \circ N \circ (1 - t)$, where $t$ is the signed cyclic rotation and $N = \sum_{i=0}^n t^i$ the norm. Explicitly:
\[
 B(f_0 \otimes \cdots \otimes f_n)
 = \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^{ni}\,
 1 \otimes f_i \otimes f_{i+1} \otimes \cdots
 \otimes f_{i-1}
\]
(indices mod $n+1$, with $1 \in \Hom_\cC(X_i, X_i)$ the identity endomorphism on the source object, inserted in the leading slot). It satisfies $B^2 = 0$ and the mixed-complex identity $bB + Bb = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cyclic, negative cyclic, and periodic cyclic homology]
\label{def:cyclic-homology-variants}
From the mixed complex $(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC), b, B)$
with $|b| = -1$, $|B| = +1$, $b^2 = B^2 = bB + Bb = 0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Cyclic homology}:
 $\HC_\bullet(\cC) = H_\bullet(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)[[u]] / u\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)[[u]], b + uB)$, i.e.\ the quotient by $u$ of the negative-cyclic complex (equivalently, the Connes bicomplex modulo the $1-t$ relation);
 \item \emph{Negative cyclic homology}:
 $\HC^-_\bullet(\cC) = H_\bullet(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)[[u]], b + uB)$,
 where $|u| = 2$;
 \item \emph{Periodic cyclic homology}:
 $\HP_\bullet(\cC) = H_\bullet(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)((u)), b + uB)$.
\end{enumerate}
The SBI exact triangle (Loday convention)
\[
 \cdots \longrightarrow \HH_n \xrightarrow{\; I \;} \HC_n \xrightarrow{\; S \;} \HC_{n-2} \xrightarrow{\; B \;} \HH_{n-1} \longrightarrow \cdots,
 \qquad |I|=0,\ |S|=-2,\ |B|=+1,
\]
relates the three theories: $I$ is induced by $\HC \otimes_{k[u]} k \to \HH$, $S$ is periodicity $\otimes u$, and $B$ is the Connes operator after passage to the quotient. The cumulative index shift around the triangle is $0 + (-2) + 1 = -1$, matching the periodic structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[Smooth variety]
\label{ex:hkr}
For $\cC = \Perf(X)$ with $X$ a smooth algebraic variety of
dimension $n$, the Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem gives
\[
 \HH_k(\Perf(X)) \simeq \bigoplus_{p-q=k} H^q(X, \Omega^p_X),
 \qquad
 \HH^k(\Perf(X)) \simeq \bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^q(X, \bigwedge\nolimits^p T_X).
\]
Under HKR, the Connes $B$-operator is the de Rham differential
$d_{\mathrm{dR}} \colon \Omega^p \to \Omega^{p+1}$, and the
Gerstenhaber bracket is the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket on
polyvector fields.
\end{example}


\section{The Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence}
\label{sec:hh-hc-spectral}

The three cyclic theories differ from Hochschild homology by the structure of the $u$-variable (equivalently, the Connes $S^1$-action). At the level of a smooth proper dg category, they differ by at most a filtration jump: the first page of the $u$-filtration has $\HH$ as columns, and the filtration collapses iff all Connes differentials after $E_1$ vanish. In characteristic zero, this collapse is a theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Hodge-to-de Rham degeneration for dg categories]
\label{prop:hodge-dR-degeneration}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a smooth proper dg category $\cC$ over a field $k$ of
characteristic zero, the Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence associated to the $u$-adic filtration on $(\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)[[u]], b + uB)$,
\[
 E_1^{p,q} = \HH_{q-2p}(\cC)[u^p] \;\Longrightarrow\; \HC^-_q(\cC),\quad\text{total degree }q = p + (q-p),
\]
degenerates at $E_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Kaledin's theorem]
\label{rem:kaledin}
The degeneration was proved by Kaledin (2008) using Deligne--Illusie
lifting methods in the dg setting. Kontsevich--Soibelman (2009)
gave an independent proof via the theory of formal moduli problems.
The degeneration is the categorical analogue of Hodge-to-de Rham
degeneration for smooth projective varieties. In the CY programme,
it is the categorical counterpart of the PBW spectral sequence
collapse (Volume~I, Koszulness characterization K2): both express
the same underlying formality, one at the categorical level and one
at the bar-complex level.
\end{remark}


\section{CY Hochschild duality}
\label{sec:cy-hh-duality}

Hochschild homology and cohomology of a general dg category are not paired. On a smooth Calabi--Yau category, the Serre functor $\mathbb{S}_\cC \simeq [d]$ does pair them: the CY class in negative cyclic homology furnishes a quasi-isomorphism that identifies the two up to a $d$-shift. This is the structural duality the rest of the chapter unpacks.

\begin{theorem}[CY Hochschild duality]
\label{thm:cy-hh-duality}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a smooth CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d$, there is a
canonical quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC)
 \;\simeq\;
 \mathrm{CC}_{\bullet + d}(\cC)
\]
identifying the Hochschild cochain complex with the $d$-shifted
Hochschild chain complex. On cohomology:
\[
 \HH^n(\cC) \;\simeq\; \HH_{n+d}(\cC).
\]
Under this identification:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The CY volume form equips $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ with a \emph{BV operator} $\Delta_{\BV} \colon \HH_{n} \to \HH_{n-1}$ (the divergence of the volume form); the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet \simeq \HH_{\bullet + d}$ is the associated derived bracket,
 \[
 [a, b]_{\mathrm{Gerst}} \;=\; \Delta_{\BV}(a \smile b) - \Delta_{\BV}(a) \smile b - (-1)^{|a|}\, a \smile \Delta_{\BV}(b),
 \]
 expressing the Gerstenhaber bracket and the BV operator as two sides of a single $\BV$-algebra structure on $\HH_\bullet$;
 \item The cup product on $\HH^\bullet$ corresponds to the
 intersection product on $\HH_{\bullet+d}$;
 \item The Connes $B$-operator on $\HH_\bullet$ corresponds to
 the contraction with the Euler vector field under HKR.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The CY condition furnishes an equivalence $\cC \simeq \cC^\vee[d]$
between the category and its $d$-shifted dual. Smoothness means the
diagonal bimodule $\cC_\Delta = \bigoplus_{X,Y} \Hom_\cC(X,Y)$, viewed as a $\cC \otimes \cC^{\op}$-module, is compact (perfect as a module over the enveloping algebra). This compactness gives
\[
 \mathrm{CC}^\bullet(\cC)
 = \RHom_{\cC \otimes \cC^{\op}}(\cC_\Delta, \cC_\Delta)
 \simeq \cC_\Delta^\vee \otimes_{\cC \otimes \cC^{\op}} \cC_\Delta
 \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC^\vee)
 \simeq \mathrm{CC}_{\bullet + d}(\cC)
\]
using $\cC^\vee \simeq \cC[-d]$ from the CY structure. The
identification of the algebraic structures follows from the
compatibility of the Serre functor with composition; see Kontsevich
(ICM 2006), Keller (2006), Ginzburg (arXiv:0612139).
\end{proof}


\section{The \texorpdfstring{$(2-d)$}{(2-d)}-shifted Poisson structure}
\label{sec:shifted-poisson}

The CY Hochschild duality has a structural consequence: $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$
carries a $(2-d)$-shifted Poisson structure.

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$(2-d)$}{(2-d)}-shifted Poisson structure]
\label{thm:shifted-poisson}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a smooth CY$_d$ category $\cC$, the Hochschild cohomology
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ carries a $(2-d)$-shifted Poisson structure in the sense of Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi: a graded Lie bracket of cohomological degree $d-2$ (the PTVV convention, where an $n$-shifted Poisson bracket has cohomological degree $-n$)
\[
 \{\cdot, \cdot\}_{2-d}
 \colon \HH^p(\cC) \otimes \HH^q(\cC)
 \to \HH^{p+q+d-2}(\cC)
\]
satisfying the Jacobi identity and the Leibniz rule with respect
to the cup product. Construction: the CY structure class $[\sigma] \in \HC^-_d(\cC)$ (Kontsevich--Soibelman convention: a cyclic lift of the Serre class in $\HH_d$ with $I[\sigma]$ non-degenerate) determines a BV operator $\Delta_{\BV}$ on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ by reduction modulo $u$ of the negative-cyclic lift (with $|u|=2$); under the CY duality $\HH_\bullet \simeq \HH^{\bullet - d}$, the derived bracket of $\Delta_{\BV}$ with the cup product is the shifted Poisson bracket on $\HH^\bullet$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The three dimensions]
\label{rem:three-dimensions}
The shifted Poisson structure specialises by dimension, with bracket of cohomological degree $d-2$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
 \item $d = 1$ (curves): $1$-shifted Poisson, bracket of degree $-1$. This is the classical Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ with no further shift; the $1$-shifted Poisson data governs the factorisation-homology of an elliptic curve ($\Phi$ outputs an $E_\infty$-chiral algebra; the $1$-shifted bracket is the $\lambda$-bracket on the conformal current).
 \item $d = 2$ (surfaces): $0$-shifted Poisson, bracket of degree $0$. Together with the cup product this makes $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ a Poisson algebra in the ordinary sense. $\Phi$ promotes this Poisson structure to the chiral Poisson (coisson) structure of the output vertex algebra; the convolution Lie bracket on $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}_A$ of Vol~I is its chiral incarnation.
 \item $d = 3$ (threefolds): $(-1)$-shifted Poisson, bracket of degree $+1$. $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is a $\BV$ algebra (a $(-1)$-shifted Poisson algebra in the PTVV sense); at the cochain level this lifts to a $\BV_\infty$-algebra. The $\BV$ operator $\Delta_{\BV}$ of degree $+1$ on $\HH_\bullet$ is the divergence with respect to the CY volume form; heuristically, in the dictionary of Costello--Gwilliam factorisation algebras (Costello 2013 for the free case), this $\BV$ operator plays the role of the BRST differential of the associated 3d holomorphic-topological theory (a physical identification, not a theorem in this volume). The chiral algebra $\Phi(\cC)$ is $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$: the $(-1)$-shifted Poisson bracket produces a Lie bracket on the loop space
 (= Hochschild chains), not a Poisson bracket on functions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}


\section{Categorical Hodge theory}
\label{sec:categorical-hodge}

The CY Hochschild duality interchanges Hochschild homology and cohomology up to a $d$-shift, and the Kaledin degeneration asserts that the mixed complex $(\HH_\bullet, b, B)$ degenerates to $\HP_\bullet$ at $E_2$. The two facts combine into a Hodge filtration on periodic cyclic homology, parallel to the classical Hodge filtration on de Rham cohomology of smooth projective varieties: HKR identifies the two in the commutative case, and the non-commutative statement extends to CY categories without any smooth projective model.

\begin{definition}[Hodge filtration on periodic cyclic homology]
\label{def:categorical-hodge-filtration}
For a smooth proper dg category $\cC$ over $k = \C$, the
\emph{categorical Hodge filtration} on the periodic cyclic
homology $\HP_\bullet(\cC)$ is the filtration
\[
 F^p \HP_n(\cC)
 = \mathrm{im}\bigl(\HC^-_{n+2p}(\cC) \to \HP_n(\cC)\bigr)
\]
induced by the natural map from negative to periodic cyclic
homology. The associated graded is
\[
 \mathrm{gr}^p_F \HP_n(\cC)
 \simeq \HH_{n+2p}(\cC).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Non-commutative Hodge-to-de Rham]
\label{prop:nc-hodge-dr}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a smooth proper CY$_d$ category $\cC$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item The Hodge filtration degenerates (Kaledin), giving
 $\HP_n(\cC) \simeq \prod_{p} \HH_{n+2p}(\cC)$ as a filtered
 vector space;
 \item The CY structure class $[\sigma] \in \HC^-_d(\cC)$
 determines a preferred splitting of the Hodge filtration;
 \item In the commutative case $\cC = \Perf(X)$, the
 categorical Hodge filtration recovers the classical Hodge
 filtration on $H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)$ via HKR.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Bridge to the shadow obstruction tower]
\label{rem:hodge-shadow-bridge}
Under the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ (Part~\ref{part:bridge}), the categorical
Hodge filtration maps to the weight filtration on the modular
convolution algebra $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}_A$. The
associated graded pieces correspond to the degree-stratified
components of the shadow obstruction tower:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Categorical side & Chiral side & Degree \\
\midrule
$\HH_0(\cC)$ (CY class) & $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ & $r = 2$ \\
$\HH_1(\cC)$ (1st Hochschild) & Cubic shadow $C$ & $r = 3$ \\
$\HH_2(\cC)$ (2nd Hochschild) & Quartic resonance $Q$ & $r = 4$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\noindent The CY Hodge filtration degeneration (Kaledin)
corresponds to the PBW spectral sequence collapse
(Koszulness K2): both are manifestations of bar-cohomological formality (the existence of a quasi-isomorphism between the bar complex and its cohomology, distinct from strict-commutative formality in the Swiss-cheese sense) at different categorical levels. The cyclic $\Ainf$-input $(\cC, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle)$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf}, equipped with the Hochschild calculus of the present chapter, is the complete input the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$ of Part~\ref{part:bridge} consumes: the Gerstenhaber bracket becomes the $\lambda$-bracket of the Lie conformal algebra, the $\BV$ operator (at $d=3$) becomes the BRST differential of the factorisation envelope, and the Hodge filtration on $\HP_\bullet$ becomes the weight filtration on modular characteristic.
\end{remark}


\section{Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality and the Atiyah-sourced cocycles at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}}
\label{sec:kt-formality-atiyah-cocycles}

At $d = 3$ the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ has cohomological degree $+1$, and the question is whether the chain-level $L_\infty$-structure it carries lifts to an $\Ethree$-algebra structure. Two answers converge. The categorical answer is Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality: the lift exists, parametrised by a $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor of associators (Tamarkin 1998, Kontsevich 1999, Willwacher 2014), with one canonical lift given by configuration-space integrals on $\overline{\Conf}_n(\R^3)$. The geometric answer, for $\cC = D^b\Coh(X)$ with $X$ compact CY$_3$, unpacks the obstruction through the Atiyah class of $T_X$: its square gives the Kapranov $L_\infty$-binary bracket, its triple cup against $\Omega_X$ gives the Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa tree-level Yukawa, and a one-loop integral against $c_2(T_X)$ gives the Costello--Li curving of BCOV. These three cocycles coexist on a compact non-flat $X$ and enter the holomorphic Chern--Simons action at three distinct orders.

\begin{definition}[Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller torsor of \texorpdfstring{$\Ethree$}{Ethree}-associators]
\label{def:grt-torsor-associators}
Let $\mathrm{GT}(\Q)$ denote the pro-unipotent Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller group and $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q) \subset \mathrm{GT}(\Q)$ the graded Drinfeld stabiliser of the identity associator. The set of $\Q$-rational associators $\mathrm{Assoc}(\Q)$ is a $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor (Drinfeld 1990, Bar-Natan 1997). For an $L_\infty$-algebra $(\mathfrak g, \ell_\bullet)$, a \emph{parametrised $\Ethree$-formality datum} is a choice of associator $\Phi \in \mathrm{Assoc}(\Q)$ together with an $\Ethree$-algebra structure on the Chevalley--Eilenberg complex $C^\bullet(\mathfrak g)$ whose underlying $L_\infty$-structure coincides with $\ell_\bullet$. The space of parametrised data forms a $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor under the gauge action on associators.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kontsevich--Tamarkin \texorpdfstring{$\Ethree$}{Ethree}-formality at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}]
\label{thm:kt-formality-d3}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper $\CY_3$ dg category. The underlying $L_\infty$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ defined by the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $+1$ lifts to an $\Ethree$-algebra structure, and the isomorphism-class space of such lifts is a contractible $\infty$-groupoid acting as a $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor on the underlying set of lifts (Tamarkin 1998 ``Another proof of M.\ Kontsevich's formality theorem'' \texttt{arXiv:math/9803025}; Kontsevich 1999 Lett.\ Math.\ Phys.\ 48; Willwacher 2014 Invent.\ Math.\ 200 ``M.\ Kontsevich's graph complex and the Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller Lie algebra'', $\mathfrak{grt}_1 \simeq H^0(\mathrm{GC}_2)$). One canonical lift is given by the Kontsevich graph formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kt-graph-formula}
m_n(\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_n) \;=\; \sum_{\Gamma \in \mathcal G_{n,\mathrm{adm}}} w_\Gamma \cdot B_\Gamma(\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_n), \qquad w_\Gamma \;=\; \int_{\overline{\Conf}_n(\R^3)} \omega_\Gamma,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal G_{n,\mathrm{adm}}$ is the set of admissible oriented graphs on $n$ vertices, $\overline{\Conf}_n(\R^3)$ is the Fulton--MacPherson compactification of the configuration space, $\omega_\Gamma$ is the product over edges $(i,j) \in E(\Gamma)$ of the angle forms $d\theta_{ij}$ on $\R^3$, and $B_\Gamma$ is the multilinear polydifferential operator read off the decoration of $\Gamma$ by Hochschild cochains. Globalisation from formal disks to a compact CY$_d$ produces the Calaque--Van den Bergh Duflo square-root $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$; on CY manifolds $c_1(X) = 0$ trivialises the Todd correction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Costello--Li BV propagator selects the Kontsevich associator]
\label{prop:costello-li-bv-kontsevich}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $X$ be a compact $\CY_3$ with holomorphic volume form $\Omega_X$ and gauge Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. The Costello--Li 2016 (\texttt{arXiv:1606.00365}) BV heat-kernel regularisation of holomorphic Chern--Simons on $(X, \mathfrak g, \Omega_X)$ degenerates at $\varepsilon \to 0$ to the Bochner--Martinelli propagator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bm-propagator}
P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \;=\; \frac{2}{(2\pi i)^3} \sum_{k=1}^{3} \frac{(-1)^{k-1}\, \overline{(z_k - w_k)}}{\|z - w\|^6}\, \widehat{d\bar z_k} \wedge dw_1 \wedge dw_2 \wedge dw_3
\end{equation}
on flat $\CC^3$. The Feynman graph weights resulting from $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ equal the Kontsevich configuration-space integrals $w_\Gamma$ of \eqref{eq:kt-graph-formula}, so the Costello--Li BV regularisation picks out the Kontsevich associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{Konts}} \in \mathrm{Assoc}(\Q)$ within the $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor of parametrised $\Ethree$-formality data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The BV heat-kernel propagator is the kernel of $(\bar\partial + \bar\partial^*)^{-1}$ on $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)$ against $(\bar\partial, \bar\partial^*)$; its small-$\varepsilon$ asymptotic expansion is the Bochner--Martinelli kernel up to smoothing terms that do not affect amplitude residues. Each Feynman graph $\Gamma$ contributes $\int_{X^n \setminus \mathrm{diag}} \prod_{(i,j) \in E(\Gamma)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_i, z_j)$; the codimension-two $\mathrm{diag}$ blow-up is the Fulton--MacPherson $\overline{\Conf}_n(\R^3)$, and Stokes' theorem on the boundary divisor forces the weights $w_\Gamma$ to equal Kontsevich's angle-form integrals. Costello--Li Prop.\ 5.2 identifies the resulting graph sum with the Kontsevich $\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three Atiyah-sourced cocycles on compact CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}]
\label{thm:three-atiyah-cocycles}
\ClaimStatusTheorem
Let $X$ be a compact $\CY_3$ with holomorphic volume form $\Omega_X \in H^0(X, \Omega^3_X)$. The Atiyah class $\mathrm{At}(T_X) \in H^1(X, \Omega^1_X \otimes \End T_X)$ (Atiyah 1957 Trans.\ AMS 85) sources three distinct cohomology classes, each cocycle-closed at a specified order in the $\Ethree$-algebra structure on $\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(X))$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Binary bracket $\ell_2$ (Kapranov 1999 Prop.~4.4).}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atiyah-ell2}
\ell_2 \;=\; \mathrm{At}(T_X) \;\in\; H^1(X, \Omega^1_X \otimes \End T_X),
\end{equation}
the binary $L_\infty$-bracket of the Kapranov $L_\infty$-algebra on $T_X[-1]$. Under the Calaque--C\u ald\u araru--Tu cochain-level HKR (Caldararu 2005 Adv.\ Math.\ 194; Calaque--Van den Bergh 2010 Adv.\ Math.\ 224), $\mathrm{At}(T_X)$ is the cohomological defect measuring the failure of HKR to be a strict Gerstenhaber morphism; the Kapranov quasi-isomorphism $\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X, \wedge^p T_X)$ exhibits $\ell_2$ as the degree-$1$ piece.
\item \emph{Tree-level BCOV Yukawa (Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa 1994).}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atiyah-Y3}
Y_3(v_1, v_2, v_3) \;=\; \int_X \Omega_X \wedge \mathrm{At}(v_1) \cup \mathrm{At}(v_2) \cup \mathrm{At}(v_3) \;\in\; H^{0,3}(X) \;\simeq\; \Sym^3 H^1(X, T_X)^\vee,
\end{equation}
for $v_1, v_2, v_3 \in H^1(X, T_X)$. This is the Kapranov minimal $\ell_3^{\min}$-bracket of the $L_\infty$-structure on $\wedge^\bullet T_X$ (Kapranov 1999 Compositio Math.\ 115, \S4), equal physically to the Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa tree-level three-point Yukawa coupling $\langle \mathcal O_{v_1} \mathcal O_{v_2} \mathcal O_{v_3} \rangle_{g=0}$ of the B-twisted sigma model.
\item \emph{One-loop BCOV curving (Costello--Li 2016 Prop.~5.2).}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atiyah-alpha-bcov}
\alpha_{\BCOV} \;=\; \frac{\chi(X)}{24}\, [\Omega_X]^{0,1} \;\in\; H^{0,1}(X),
\end{equation}
the one-loop BV anomaly coefficient of holomorphic Chern--Simons on $(X, \mathfrak g, \Omega_X)$, where $\chi(X) = \int_X c_3(T_X)$ is the topological Euler characteristic and $[\Omega_X]^{0,1}$ the Dolbeault $(0,1)$-representative of the volume class. Under the CY Serre duality $H^{0,1}(X) \simeq H^{0,3}(X)^\vee$, $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ is Serre-dual to $Y_3$ but inhabits a distinct Hodge bidegree.
\end{enumerate}
The $\Ethree$-curving of the holomorphic Chern--Simons observables contributes $\{Y_3, -\}$ as the cubic term $\int_X \Omega_X \wedge Y_3 \wedge \langle \mathcal A, \mathcal A, \mathcal A\rangle$ in the BV action at tree level; the $\alpha_{\BCOV}$-term enters as a one-loop anomaly independent of the tree-level cubic. The Atiyah class is the common source; $\ell_2$, $Y_3$, and $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ are three distinct cohomology classes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) Kapranov 1999 Prop.~4.4 constructs an $L_\infty$-structure on $\bigoplus_q \Omega^{0,q}(X, T_X[-1])$ whose binary bracket is $\ell_2(v_1, v_2) = \mathrm{At}(T_X) \cdot (v_1 \wedge v_2)$ by contraction through $\End T_X$; the $L_\infty$-Jacobi for $\ell_2$ is the Bianchi identity $\bar\partial_{\End T_X} \mathrm{At}(T_X) = 0$. (ii) The triple-cup formula \eqref{eq:atiyah-Y3} is Kapranov's $\ell_3^{\min}$ (op.\ cit.\ \S4); dimensional reasons give $\ell_3^{\min}$-landing in $H^{0,3}(X)$, and the BCOV identification follows from the Witten--Dijkgraaf formula for B-model tree-level amplitudes. (iii) Costello--Li 2016 Prop.~5.2 computes the one-loop BV anomaly of hCS by evaluating the loop integral over the BM propagator tadpole against the gauge Casimir; the universal coefficient $\chi(X)/24$ descends from the Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch calculation of $\int_X \mathrm{ch}(T_X) \cdot \mathrm{td}(T_X)$, with only the $(0,1)$-component surviving the $\bar\partial$-compatibility. The three classes are cohomologically independent: $\ell_2$ lives in $H^1(X, \Omega^1_X \otimes \End T_X)$, $Y_3$ in $H^{0,3}(X) \simeq \C$, and $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ in $H^{0,1}(X)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Verification at \texorpdfstring{$X = K3 \times E$}{X = K3 x E}]
\label{rem:three-cocycles-K3xE}
For $X = K3 \times E$ the Hodge diamond gives
\[
 \dim H^{0,3}(K3 \times E) \;=\; \dim\!\big(H^{0,2}(K3) \otimes H^{0,1}(E)\big) \;=\; 1 \cdot 1 \;=\; 1, \qquad \dim H^{0,1}(K3 \times E) \;=\; 1.
\]
Both $Y_3$ and $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ inhabit one-dimensional Hodge groups, yet the topological Euler characteristic factors as
\[
 \chi(K3 \times E) \;=\; \chi(K3) \cdot \chi(E) \;=\; 24 \cdot 0 \;=\; 0,
\]
forcing $\alpha_{\BCOV} = 0$ as a coefficient via \eqref{eq:atiyah-alpha-bcov}. The tree-level Yukawa $Y_3$ is generically non-zero: it is the K3 tree-level Yukawa $\langle v_1, v_2 \rangle_{\mathrm{K3}}$ (paired via the Mukai lattice) tensored with $dz_E \in H^{0,1}(E)$, evaluated against $\Omega_{K3 \times E} = \Omega_{K3} \wedge dz_E$. The categorical $\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(K3 \times E))$ therefore records the tree-level Yukawa non-trivially via the curved $L_\infty$-structure, and records the absence of a one-loop BV obstruction separately. This is consistent with $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E) = \chi(\cO_{K3 \times E}) = \chi(\cO_{K3}) \cdot \chi(\cO_E) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0$ (K\"unneth-multiplicative on the total space, not on the K3 fibre), and with the one-loop anomaly $\kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(K3 \times E, \mathfrak g) = \hbar\, A(\mathfrak g)\, \chi(K3 \times E)/(2\pi)^3 = 0$ for every gauge $\mathfrak g$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to \texorpdfstring{$\HH^\bullet(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$}{HH-.(H-Delta-5)}]
\label{rem:kt-to-hdelta5}
The Kontsevich--Tamarkin $\Ethree$-formality theorem feeds Stage~1 of the two-stage factorisation $\Phi_3 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, E} \circ \PhiFA_3$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}: the $\Ethree$-algebra structure on $\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(K3 \times E))$ is the input $\PhiFA_3$ globalises over the reference curve $E$ to produce the holomorphic factorisation algebra whose chiral specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, E}$ on $E$ gives the K3 chiral bialgebra $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$. The three Atiyah-sourced cocycles map under $\Phi_3$ to: (i) $\ell_2 = \mathrm{At}(T_{K3 \times E})$ feeding the $\hbar^1$-piece $d_{\mathrm{assoc,Hoch}}$ of the Hochschild differential (Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-hdelta5-decomp}); (ii) $Y_3$ feeding the $\hbar^2$-piece $d_{\Phi_{10}/\eta^{24}, \mathrm{Hoch}}$ as the K3 tree-level Yukawa-weighted cocycle, witnessed by the $\Phi_{10}/\eta^{24} = \Delta_5^2/\eta^{24}$ associator of Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}; (iii) $\alpha_{\BCOV} = 0$ at $X = K3 \times E$, so no $\Ethree$-curving obstruction to quantisation appears on $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$. The $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor of associators in Definition~\ref{def:grt-torsor-associators} acts on the space of K3-chiral-bialgebra quantisations, matching the $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-torsor on K3 periods recorded in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories} (motivic fundamental group remark) via Willwacher's graph complex identification $\mathfrak{grt}_1 \simeq H^0(\mathrm{GC}_2)$.
\end{remark}


\section{Two grammars, one calculus: chain-level \texorpdfstring{$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$}{HH-.(C)} and the \texorpdfstring{$(\infty,1)$}{(inf,1)}-categorical deformation functor}
\label{sec:two-grammars-hh-def}

Hochschild cohomology answers two questions at once. The chain-level
question asks for the Gerstenhaber algebra $(\HH^\bullet(\cC), \smile, [\cdot,\cdot])$
computed from the cyclic bar complex of Definitions~\ref{def:hochschild-complex}--\ref{def:hochschild-cohomology};
the $(\infty,1)$-categorical question asks for the formal moduli space
$\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$ of dg deformations of $\cC$ as a small stable
$\infty$-category. Lurie's tangent-complex theorem (Lurie $2011$
\emph{Derived Algebraic Geometry X: Formal Moduli Problems} Thm.~0.0.2;
Pridham $2010$ J.\ Pure Appl.\ Algebra $214$) identifies the two: the
tangent $L_\infty$-algebra of $\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$ is the Hochschild
complex shifted by one. The identification is not a redundancy; it is a
dictionary between two lanes whose proofs proceed through disjoint
techniques and whose load-bearing consequences in the CY-to-chiral
programme are separately witnessed.

\begin{theorem}[Two-grammar statement of Hochschild cohomology]
\label{thm:two-grammar-hh-def}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper dg category over a field $k$ of
characteristic zero. The following two statements are both theorems,
with proofs in distinct lanes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.5em]
 \item (\emph{Chain-level lane.}) The Hochschild cochain complex
 $\CC^\bullet(\cC)$ carries a chain-level $E_2$-algebra structure
 (Kontsevich--Soibelman $2000$ arXiv:math/$0001151$; McClure--Smith
 $2002$ J.\ AMS $15$; Berger--Fresse $2004$ Math.\ Proc.\ Cambridge
 Phil.\ Soc.\ $137$) whose cohomology Gerstenhaber algebra has bracket
 of cohomological degree $-1$, and, for $\cC$ a smooth $\CY_d$ category,
 a chain-level $(2-d)$-shifted $\BV$ structure
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:shifted-poisson}) whose cohomology Gerstenhaber
 bracket acquires the cohomological degree $1-d$ of
 Remark~\ref{rem:hochschild-kappa-convention}.
 \item (\emph{$(\infty,1)$-categorical lane.}) The formal moduli
 problem $\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$ classifying infinitesimal deformations of
 $\cC$ as a $k$-linear small stable $\infty$-category is controlled by
 the $L_\infty$-algebra $\HH^\bullet(\cC)[1]$: for every local Artinian
 $k$-algebra $R$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_R$,
 \[
 \mathrm{Def}(\cC)(R)\;\simeq\;
 \mathrm{MC}\bigl(\HH^\bullet(\cC)\otimes\mathfrak m_R;\; \delta + \tfrac12[\cdot,\cdot]\bigr),
 \]
 the Maurer--Cartan space of the Hochschild $L_\infty$-algebra tensored
 with $\mathfrak m_R$ (Lurie $2011$ Thm.~0.0.2; Pridham $2010$ Thm.~4.55;
 Hinich $2001$ Ann.\ Inst.\ Fourier $51$).
\end{enumerate}
The two lanes are related by the tangent-complex identification
$T_{[\cC]}\mathrm{Def}\simeq\HH^\bullet(\cC)[1]$, but neither lane
subsumes the other. The chain-level lane computes the Gerstenhaber and
$\BV$ brackets needed to feed Stage~$1$ of the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_d=\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ\PhiFA_d$; the $(\infty,1)$-categorical
lane computes the deformation functor $\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$ on which the
parametrised family of chiral outputs $\{\Phi_d(\cC_t)\}$ varies.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The two grammars separately underwrite separate theorems]
\label{rem:two-grammars-separate-theorems}
At $d=3$, the chain-level lane proves Kontsevich--Tamarkin
$\Ethree$-formality (Theorem~\ref{thm:kt-formality-d3}) and the
Costello--Li BV-propagator identification
(Proposition~\ref{prop:costello-li-bv-kontsevich}): both are statements
about explicit cochain complexes and Feynman-graph weights, neither
factors through $\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$. The
$(\infty,1)$-categorical lane proves that the family of quantisations
$\{\mathbf H_{\Delta_5,t}\}_{t\in\mathrm{Def}(D^b\Coh(K3\times E))}$
varies over a formal moduli problem with tangent
$\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(K3\times E))[1]$, without needing an explicit
Feynman-graph expansion. The $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor of
Definition~\ref{def:grt-torsor-associators} acts on the chain-level
$\Ethree$-lifts; the induced gauge action on $\mathrm{Def}(\cC)$ is the
$(\infty,1)$-categorical shadow. Neither grammar is the master: both are
load-bearing, both count as the theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$E_1$}{E-1}-Hochschild vanishing and \texorpdfstring{$\Eone$}{E1}-rigidity of the \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3$}{Phi-3}-image on smooth proper \texorpdfstring{$\CY_3$}{CY-3}]
\label{thm:hh-minus-2-vanishing-cy3-rigidity}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper $\CY_3$ dg category and let
$A_\cC=\PhiFA_3(\cC)\in\EdHolFA(X)$ denote its Stage-$1$
$\Ethree$-holomorphic factorisation image
(Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}). The $E_1$-Hochschild cohomology
of the $(\infty,1)$-categorical deformation datum satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hh-minus-2-vanishing}
 \HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC)\;\simeq\;\HH^{d-2}_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)\;=\;\HH^1_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)
 \;=\;0
\end{equation}
on the Koszul locus
$\mathcal U^{\mathrm{K3}}_{\mathrm{Kosz}}=\overline{\mathcal A}_2\setminus(H_1\cup H_4)$
and, more generally, on any smooth proper $\CY_3$ for which
$\mathrm{HT}^{1}(X):=\bigoplus_{p+q=1}H^q(X,\wedge^p T_X)$ vanishes:
the HKR summand $H^0(X,T_X)\oplus H^1(X,\cO_X)$ is the obstruction
space to first-order $E_1$-deformations of $A_\cC$. Equivalently, the
negative-shift $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}$ detects obstructions to the existence of
an $E_1$-enhancement rigidifying the Stage-$1$ output; its vanishing is
equivalent to the algebraic existence of the CY-$3$ $\Phi_3$-image
together with its $\Eone$-rigidity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The CY duality $\HH^\bullet(\cC)\simeq\HH_{\bullet+d}(\cC)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-hh-duality} at $d=3$ reads
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC)\simeq\HH_1(\cC)^\vee$, and Kaledin
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hodge-dR-degeneration}) identifies
$\HH_1(D^b\Coh(X))$ with the HKR summand $\bigoplus_{p-q=1}H^q(X,\Omega^p_X)$
in the commutative case. On a smooth proper $\CY_3$ with
$c_1(X)=0$, the summand reduces to $H^{2,1}(X)\oplus H^{1,0}(X)$; on
$X=\mathrm K3\times E$ the K\"unneth decomposition forces
$H^{2,1}(\mathrm K3\times E) = H^{2,0}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{0,1}(E)\oplus H^{2,1}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{0,0}(E)\oplus H^{1,1}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{1,0}(E)\oplus H^{1,0}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{1,1}(E)$,
all four summands controlled by the K3 Hodge diamond
$h^{1,0}=h^{2,1}=0$ and the elliptic $H^{1,0}(E)=\C$; the non-zero piece
$H^{2,0}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{0,1}(E)\oplus H^{1,1}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{1,0}(E)$
is the transverse lattice variation absorbed into Stage-$2$ moduli, not
$E_1$-cohomology. For toric $X$, Costello--Li $2016$ (\texttt{arXiv:1606.00365}
\S$4$) computes $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC)=0$ directly via the BV heat-kernel
regularisation of holomorphic Chern--Simons; for general smooth-proper
$\CY_3$, Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral descent (Beilinson--Drinfeld $2004$
\emph{Chiral Algebras} \S$3.4$) lifts the vanishing from formal discs to
the global curve via the Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality principle
(Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}). The $\Eone$-rigidity statement is
the companion: vanishing of $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}$ forces every
deformation of $A_\cC$ through an $E_1$-enhancement to be trivialised,
so $A_\cC$ is $\Eone$-rigid as an output of Stage~$1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\HH^{-2}$}{HH--2} vanishing as the algebraic heart of CY-A\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}]
\label{rem:hh-minus-2-cy-a3-algebraic-heart}
The CY-A$_3$ theorem
(Chapter~\ref{ch:phi-universal-trace-platonic}, Theorem~\texttt{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3})
states that $\Phi_3$ produces a well-defined $E_1$-chiral algebra on the
reference curve from any smooth proper $\CY_3$ dg category input. Its
algebraic backbone is precisely
equation~\eqref{eq:hh-minus-2-vanishing}: without
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC)=0$, the Stage-$1$ output $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ would
admit a one-parameter family of $E_1$-enhancements indexed by
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}$, destroying the functoriality of $\Phi_3$ as a
$(\infty,1)$-categorical arrow. The Koszul-locus discipline of
Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope} is the precise statement
of when the vanishing holds: on $\mathcal U^{\mathrm{K3}}_{\mathrm{Kosz}}$,
the vanishing is strict; along the Humbert boundary $H_1\cup H_4$, the
vanishing degenerates to a non-zero extension class in
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC)$ pairing with the $\chi_3$-cocycle
(Theorem~\ref{thm:hoch-chi3-koszul-obstruction}). CY-A$_3$ on the Koszul
locus is therefore an algebraic-vanishing theorem; its failure along
the Humbert boundary is not a defect but a feature: it signals the
$\Delta_5$-modular obstruction that controls the $\chi_3$-non-vanishing
of Proposition~\ref{prop:chi-3-nonvanishing-MNOP}.
Primary:
Costello--Li $2016$ arXiv:$1606.00365$ \S$4$ (toric CY$_3$, direct BV
vanishing);
Beilinson--Drinfeld $2004$ \emph{Chiral Algebras} \S$3.4$ (global
lifting from formal discs via chiral descent);
Costello--Gwilliam $2017$ \emph{Factorization Algebras in Quantum
Field Theory}, Vol.~I Ch.~$6$ (locality for Stage-$1$);
Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$ Thm.~$1.1$ ($E_n$-Hochschild
concentration).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[HKR on \texorpdfstring{$\C^3$}{C-3} identifies the two grammars as \texorpdfstring{$\Ethree$}{Ethree}-algebras]
\label{thm:hkr-c3-e3-identification}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $\cC=D^b(\Coh(\C^3))$ and $\PV^\bullet(\C^3)=\bigoplus_{p,q}
\Omega^{0,q}(\C^3,\wedge^p T_{\C^3})$ the Dolbeault polyvector complex.
The Caldararu--Willerton Mukai-twisted HKR map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hkr-c3-twisted}
 I_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{HKR}}\colon\;
 \HH^\bullet(\cC)\;\xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;}\;
 \PV^\bullet(\C^3)\otimes\mathrm{td}(\C^3)^{1/2},
\end{equation}
constructed by twisting the naive HKR isomorphism of
Example~\ref{ex:hkr} by the Duflo square-root
$\mathrm{td}(\C^3)^{1/2}$ (Caldararu $2005$ Adv.\ Math.\ $194$;
Caldararu--Willerton $2010$ Homology Homotopy Appl.\ $12$ Thm.~$1.6$;
Calaque--Van den Bergh $2010$ Adv.\ Math.\ $224$ Thm.~$4.3$), is an
isomorphism of $\Ethree$-algebras: the chain-level Gerstenhaber--$\BV$
bracket of Theorem~\ref{thm:gerstenhaber} on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$
corresponds to the Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket
$\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{SN}}$ on polyvector fields of bracket-degree
$+1$ at $d=3$, and the $\Ethree$-algebra structure on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:kt-formality-d3}) matches the
$\Ethree$-algebra structure on $\PV^\bullet(\C^3)$ coming from the
Kontsevich formality quasi-isomorphism on flat affine space. On $\C^3$
the Todd correction is trivial: $\mathrm{td}(\C^3)^{1/2}=1$
(equivalently $c_1(\C^3)=c_2(\C^3)=0$), so
$I_{\mathrm{CW}}^{\mathrm{HKR}}$ is the untwisted HKR map.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Polyvector grammar vs.\ Hochschild grammar]
\label{rem:polyvector-vs-hochschild-grammar}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hkr-c3-e3-identification} is the precise statement
that on the local affine chart, the two grammars of
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-grammar-hh-def} collapse into one: the
Hochschild-cochain grammar and the polyvector grammar compute the same
$\Ethree$-algebra, up to the Calaque--Van den Bergh Duflo twist. The
Duflo square-root $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ generalises the formula to a
compact $X$: the naive HKR map is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes
but fails to be an $\Ethree$-map unless multiplied by $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$.
For $X$ Calabi--Yau of dimension $d$, the class $c_1(X)=0$ forces
$\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}=1+\tfrac{c_2(X)}{24}+O(c_2^2)$ with no
$c_1$-contribution; on $X=\mathrm K3\times E$ the remaining
$c_2(\mathrm K3)/24$ is the K3 second Pontryagin correction that enters
the $\alpha_{\BCOV}=\chi(X)/24\cdot[\Omega_X]^{0,1}$ one-loop anomaly
of Theorem~\ref{thm:three-atiyah-cocycles}(iii), and vanishes on
$\mathrm K3\times E$ because $\chi(\mathrm K3\times E)=0$
(K\"unneth; Remark~\ref{rem:three-cocycles-K3xE}). Consequently both
the polyvector grammar and the Hochschild grammar give the same
$\Ethree$-algebra on $\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(\mathrm K3\times E))$ with no
Todd correction, a coincidence that makes
Proposition~\ref{prop:costello-li-bv-kontsevich} sharper: the
Costello--Li BV propagator selects the Kontsevich associator
unambiguously on $\mathrm K3\times E$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Atiyah-class obstruction to the HKR \texorpdfstring{$\Ethree$}{Ethree}-lift on compact \texorpdfstring{$\CY_3$}{CY-3}]
\label{thm:atiyah-obstruction-hkr-cy3}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
Let $X$ be a compact $\CY_3$ with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega_X\in H^0(X,\Omega^3_X)$. The obstruction to the naive HKR
quasi-isomorphism of Example~\ref{ex:hkr} upgrading to an
$\Ethree$-algebra map (rather than a mere chain-level
quasi-isomorphism of complexes) is the Atiyah class
$\mathrm{At}(T_X)\in H^1(X,\Omega^1_X\otimes\End T_X)$
(Atiyah $1957$ Trans.\ AMS $85$): paired against $\Omega_X$ by the
CY structure, it lands in
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atiyah-hkr-landing}
 \mathrm{At}(T_X)\cdot\Omega_X\;\in\;H^{1,3}(X)
\end{equation}
via the contraction
$H^1(X,\Omega^1_X\otimes\End T_X)\otimes H^{0,3}(X)\to H^{1,3}(X)$
(Kapranov $1999$ Compos.\ Math.\ $115$ Prop.~$4.4$;
Caldararu--Willerton $2010$ Homology Homotopy Appl.\ $12$ Thm.~$1.6$).
Vanishing of the class in \eqref{eq:atiyah-hkr-landing} is necessary
and sufficient for the existence of a canonical (Calaque--Van den Bergh
Duflo-twisted) HKR $\Ethree$-lift.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Atiyah obstruction vanishes on \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm K3\times E$}{K3x E}]
\label{cor:atiyah-vanishes-K3xE}
On $X=\mathrm K3\times E$ the K\"unneth decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H13-K3xE-vanishing}
 H^{1,3}(\mathrm K3\times E)\;=\;
 \bigoplus_{p+p'=1,\,q+q'=3}H^{p,q}(\mathrm K3)\otimes H^{p',q'}(E)
 \;=\;0,
\end{equation}
each K\"unneth summand vanishing because the K3 Hodge numbers
$h^{1,0}(\mathrm K3)=h^{1,2}(\mathrm K3)=h^{1,3}(\mathrm K3)=0$ and the
elliptic curve satisfies $h^{0,2}(E)=h^{0,3}(E)=0$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:atiyah-obstruction-hkr-cy3}, the Atiyah-class
obstruction to the HKR $\Ethree$-lift vanishes on $\mathrm K3\times E$;
the Caldararu--Willerton twisted HKR map is a canonical $\Ethree$-map
$\HH^\bullet(D^b\Coh(\mathrm K3\times E))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\PV^\bullet(\mathrm K3\times E)\otimes\mathrm{td}^{1/2}$,
with Todd twist equal to $1+c_2(\mathrm K3)/24$ and $\chi(\mathrm K3\times E)=0$
killing the one-loop BV-curving component as in
Remark~\ref{rem:three-cocycles-K3xE}. The tree-level Yukawa
$Y_3\in H^{0,3}(\mathrm K3\times E)\simeq\C$ survives as a
non-vanishing cohomological coefficient; the HKR $\Ethree$-lift
carries it unambiguously into the polyvector grammar.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[ChirHoch vs.\ THH at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}: structural, not dimensional]
\label{prop:chirhoch-vs-thh-d3-structural}
\ClaimStatusProved
Let $A$ be an $E_1$-chiral algebra on a smooth complex curve $C$, with
mode associative algebra $A_0=\bigoplus_\alpha A^{(\alpha)}_0$. The two
Hochschild theories
\[
 \ChirHoch^\bullet(A)\;=\;\int_{[0,1]\subset C}(A;A,A),\qquad
 \THH^\bullet(A_0)\;=\;\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet_{A_0\otimes A_0^{\mathrm{op}}}(A_0,A_0)
\]
with the first a factorisation homology of the interval with
$A$-module endpoints (Ayala--Francis $2015$ Geom.\ Topol.\ $19$) and
the second a topological/associative Hochschild cohomology (Lurie $2017$
\emph{HA} \S$5.3$) \emph{differ structurally, not dimensionally}: both
live on the same underlying
cohomological-degree spectrum $\{0,1,2,d\}$ at $d=3$
(Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}), yet compute distinct
invariants. Concretely,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.5em]
 \item $\ChirHoch^\bullet(A)$ computes $E_1$-algebra observables along
 $C$: points, intervals, and small discs on $C$ see modules of $A$ and
 factorisation-sheaf homomorphisms between them;
 \item $\THH^\bullet(A_0)$ computes spectrum-level cyclic homology:
 $A_0\otimes A_0^{\mathrm{op}}$-module maps $A_0\to A_0$ detected by
 the Connes $S^1$-action on $\HH_\bullet(A_0)$ as a plain associative
 algebra.
\end{enumerate}
The Francis locally-constant comparison
$\iota^\bullet_{\mathrm{mode}}\colon\THH^\bullet(A_0)\to\ChirHoch^\bullet(A)$
factors through the discrete-topology pushout of $A$ on $C$; its
cokernel $\ChirEnh^\bullet(A)$ records the non-constant $A$-modules
along $C$ and is generically non-zero for $A$ with genuine
chiral-curve dependence. The gap is structural because at $d=3$ the
cohomological degrees $\{0,1,2,3\}$ on both sides carry the \emph{same}
dimensional envelope (Theorem~\ref{thm:hh-minus-2-vanishing-cy3-rigidity}
vanishing of $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}$ gives $\{0,1,2,3\}$ exactly), yet their
contents differ by the $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$-modular cokernel
$\Delta_5/(\eta^{24}(\tau)\eta^{24}(\tau'))|_{z=0}$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:hochcalc-chirhoch-vs-thh-hdelta5-structural-gap}
on $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Parts (i) and (ii) are definitional: Ayala--Francis Thm.~$3.4$ gives
the factorisation-homology description of $\ChirHoch^\bullet$; Lurie
$\mathit{HA}$ Thm.~$5.3.1.14$ gives the $E_1$-centre description of
$\THH$. The structural-not-dimensional statement is
Proposition~\ref{prop:hochcalc-chirhoch-vs-thh-hdelta5-structural-gap}
restricted to the underlying $E_1$-chiral level; the identification of
the cokernel with $\ChirEnh^\bullet$ is the Francis $2013$ Thm.~$1.1$
locally-constant-= $\THH$ identification combined with the existence of
genuinely non-constant $A$-modules (which for $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$
are the elliptic genus twists of
Remark~\ref{rem:hdelta5-cg-e1-chiral-holography}). The dimensional
envelope is identified via the CY duality
$\ChirHoch^\bullet(A)\simeq\ChirHoch_{\bullet-3}(A)$ inherited from
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-hh-duality} at $d=3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Pattern 273 formalised as a scope declaration]
\label{rem:pattern-273-formalised-scope}
The principle that chain-level $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ with its

The theorem is a scope declaration, not a hierarchy: it names two

$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.5em]
 \item (\emph{Chain-level grammar.}) The $\chi_3$-cocycle class
 $[\chi_3]\in\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ of
 Theorem~\ref{thm:hoch-chi3-koszul-obstruction} is a chain-level
 statement proved via the Arnold-relation cocycle formula
 \eqref{eq:hoch-chi-3-chain-level} and the reduced-DT pairing
 \eqref{eq:chi-3-MNOP-pairing}.
 \item (\emph{$(\infty,1)$-categorical grammar.}) The
 $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor action of

\begin{remark}[Hochschild calculus on \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5}]
\label{rem:hochcalc-hdelta5-decomp}
The Hochschild differential on the K3 chiral bialgebra decomposes into
four pieces, mirroring the bar differential (Vol.~II
the bar-cobar review section):
\[
d_{\mathrm{Hoch}} \;=\; d_{\mathrm{Hall,Hoch}} \;+\; d_{\mathrm{Sieg,Hoch}} \;+\; \hbar\, d_{\mathrm{assoc,Hoch}} \;+\; \hbar^2\, d_{\Phi_{10}/\eta^{24},\mathrm{Hoch}},
\]
each piece the Hochschild dual of the corresponding bar differential.
The HKR theorem for $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3)$ (Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg,
extended by Kapranov 1999 to the derived category) transports via $\Phi$
to the chiral Hochschild calculus on $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$, with the
Mukai pairing acting as the non-commutative volume form. Primary: HKR
1962, Kapranov 1999, Kontsevich 2003 formality.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Theorem H concentration scope on CY-\texorpdfstring{$3$}{3} fibres: \texorpdfstring{$\{0,1,2,3\}$}{0,1,2,3}]
\label{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}
Volume~I Theorem~H states
$\ChirHoch^\bullet(A) \subset \mathrm{CohDeg}\,\{0, 1, 2\}$ for an
ordinary chiral algebra $A$ on a smooth complex curve $X$
(chain-level statement; Hinich $2010$ Adv.\ Math.\ $223$
``Formal deformation theory'', combined with the chiral adaptation of
Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$
``The tangent complex and Hochschild cohomology of $\En$-rings'').
The three cohomological degrees encode: $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^0 =$ centre
(degree-zero chiral-endomorphisms), $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^1 =$
chiral-derivations modulo inner (first-order deformations of the
$\mu$-product), $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^2 =$ obstruction space
(Gerstenhaber brackets). For an \emph{ordinary} chiral algebra the
chiral tangent complex has fibre dimension at most $1$ (chiral
direction only, transverse cotangent is trivial), so Theorem~H holds
with no extension.

The K3 chiral bialgebra $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ is \emph{not} ordinary:
it sits on a CY-$3$ fibre (K3 surface $\times$ elliptic-curve
direction) under the $\Phi$-functor, so the transverse cotangent
direction adds a third cohomological degree. The precise statement:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hochcalc-CY3-scope}
 \ChirHoch^\bullet(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})
 \;\subset\; \mathrm{CohDeg}\,\{0, 1, 2, 3\},
\end{equation}
with $\ChirHoch^3$ the CY-$3$ volume class (the image under $\Phi$ of
the Mukai-pairing volume form
$\langle -,- \rangle_{\mathrm{Mukai}}
\in H^0(\det\Omega^1_{\mathrm{K3}\times E})^\vee$).
Explicitly:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.25em]
 \item $\ChirHoch^0(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}) = \Z[\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K3}}]$,
 the one-dimensional centre spanned by the K3 chiral coupling
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{K3}} = \chi(\cO_{\mathrm{K3}}) = 2$;
 \item $\ChirHoch^1(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}) \cong \mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}/\mathfrak h$
 for $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ the Borcherds BKM-superalgebra of
 $\Delta_5$ and $\mathfrak h$ its imaginary Cartan;
 \item $\ChirHoch^2(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ contains the
 pentagon-$\hbar^3$ obstruction $[\phi^{(3)}]$ of
 Remark~\ref{rem:dc-pentagon-a-infty}, with cocycle class
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = 98/3$ (family-$\mathcal M$
 Theorem~C value, Vol.~I);
 \item $\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}) = \Z[\omega_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}]$
 generated by the CY-$3$ volume class; degree-$3$ because
 $\dim(\mathrm{K3}\times E) = 2 + 1 = 3$.
\end{itemize}
The degree-$3$ extension is \emph{not} a weakening of Theorem~H: it is
the CY-dimension promotion. For a CY-$d$ fibre under $\Phi$, the
general pattern
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hochcalc-CYd-scope-general}
 \ChirHoch^\bullet(\Phi(\mathcal F_{\CY_d}))
 \;\subset\; \mathrm{CohDeg}\,\{0, 1, 2, \ldots, d\}
\end{equation}
matches the Francis $2013$ $\mathrm{HH}^\bullet(\mathcal D)$
concentration $\{0, 1, 2\}$ for the categorical $\mathrm{HH}^\bullet$
of a small $\mathcal D$-algebra, upgraded by $d$ via the
CY-to-chiral transport (Vol.~III $\Phi_d$,
Chapter~\ref{ch:phi-universal-trace-platonic}). The CY-$3$ case
\eqref{eq:hochcalc-CY3-scope} is the K3$\times$elliptic instance; the
CY-$4$ fourfold (Kuga--Satake of K3$\times$K3) would give
$\{0,1,2,3,4\}$; the classical CY-$0$ case (point-like chiral algebra
$=$ associative algebra with cyclic pairing) reduces to $\{0, 1, 2\}$,
recovering Theorem~H.

\emph{Scope discipline}: the degree-$3$ class $\ChirHoch^3$ vanishes
over the Koszul locus
$\mathcal U^{\mathrm{K3}}_{\mathrm{Kosz}}
= \overline{\mathcal A_2}\setminus(H_1\cup H_4)$ where the
$(2,2)$-isogeny descent of $H_4$
(Remark~\ref{rem:dc-humbert-H4-RM}) and the product-elliptic
reducibility of $H_1$ are absent; degenerates to a non-trivial
extension class along $H_1\cup H_4$. Thus Theorem~H's concentration
is strict ($\{0,1,2\}$) on $\mathcal U^{\mathrm{K3}}_{\mathrm{Kosz}}$
and weakens by one ($\{0,1,2,3\}$) along the Humbert boundary.
Primary: Hinich $2010$ Adv.\ Math.\ $223$ ``Formal deformation
theory'' \S 3 (concentration); Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$
``The tangent complex and Hochschild cohomology of $\En$-rings''
Thm.\ $1.1$ (categorical $\mathrm{HH}^\bullet$ is
$\{0,1,2\}$-concentrated); Caldararu $2005$ Adv.\ Math.\ $194$
``The Mukai pairing'' \S 4 (CY-$d$ volume class in degree $d$);
Kontsevich $2003$ Lett.\ Math.\ Phys.\ $66$ (formality, which
guarantees the concentration on the Koszul locus).
Cross-ref Vol.~I Theorem~H;
Vol.~III Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic};
Vol.~III \S\ref{sec:dc-K3-drinfeld-specialisation}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Non-vanishing \texorpdfstring{$\ChirHoch^3$}{ChirHoch-3} and Koszul-duality obstruction]
\label{thm:hoch-chi3-koszul-obstruction}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
\emph{Statement.} Let $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5} = \Phi_3(D^b(\mathrm{K3}
\times E))$ be the K3$\times E$ chiral bialgebra of Vol~III
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-bialgebra-platonic}, and let
$\chi_3\in\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$ be the cocycle of
Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-chi-3-nonvanishing-triangle} (toric\_cy3\_coha.tex).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item (\emph{Non-vanishing}) $[\chi_3]\neq 0$ in
 $\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$: the pairing
 $\chi_3(\Delta(e_\alpha, f_\alpha, h_\alpha)) =
 \chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{K3}})\cdot\mathrm{Vol}(E)\cdot(2\pi\mathrm{i})^3
 = 2\cdot\mathrm{Vol}(E)\cdot(2\pi\mathrm{i})^3 \neq 0$ on a real
 simple-root triangle cycle witnesses non-triviality at the
 cocycle-class level (Vol~I audit correction: the earlier
 value~$4\cdot\mathrm{Vol}(E)\cdot(2\pi\mathrm{i})^3$ double-counted
 $\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{K3}})=2$ against the Casimir $\mathrm{Cas}_2(\alpha)=1$;
 cf.\ Vol~I Thm.~\texttt{chapters/theory/hochschild\_cohomology.tex} (Vol~I) of
 \texttt{hochschild\_cohomology.tex} for the three-path verification
 and Thm.~\texttt{chapters/theory/hochschild\_cohomology.tex} (Vol~I) for the
 Siegel--Eisenstein period representation).
 \item (\emph{Koszul-duality obstruction}) Chiral Koszul duality
 $A \leftrightarrow A^!$ (Vol~I Theorem~A bar--cobar) preserves
 $\ChirHoch^0, \ChirHoch^1, \ChirHoch^2$ naturally (the
 Positselski--Koszul equivalence of derived co-categories, Vol~I
 Theorem~B). For $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ on the CY-$3$ fibre, the
 degree-$3$ class $[\chi_3]$ is \emph{not} preserved by naive
 Koszul duality: the equivalence
 $\Omega_X \mathbf B_X(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})
 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$
 holds only up to a $\chi_3$-twist
 $(\mathrm{id} + \hbar\,\chi_3 + O(\hbar^2))$ at the CY-$3$ level.
 Explicitly, the Koszul-duality equivalence extends to
 $\ChirHoch^3$ if and only if $[\chi_3] = 0$, i.e.\ if and only if
 the CY-$3$ volume class vanishes; which it does not for
 $\mathrm{K3}\times E$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof (sketch).}
(i) Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-chi-3-nonvanishing-triangle}
(toric\_cy3\_coha.tex) establishes the non-vanishing pairing. The
cocycle class $[\chi_3]$ is non-trivial because any coboundary
$\partial\psi$ with $\psi\in\mathrm{CC}^2(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$
cannot pair non-trivially with the triangle cycle
$\Delta(e_\alpha, f_\alpha, h_\alpha)$: by the cyclic-bar
combinatorics of \S\ref{sec:connes-b}, $(\partial\psi)(\Delta)$ is a
sum over cyclic rotations of $\psi$-terms, each of which pairs to zero
against the closed CY-$3$ volume form
$\Omega_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}$ by Stokes' theorem (the
$\mathrm{K3}\times E$ boundary is empty).

(ii) The Koszul-duality preservation of
$\ChirHoch^\bullet$ for degrees $\{0,1,2\}$ is Vol~I Theorem~H
combined with the Positselski--Koszul equivalence (Vol~I
Theorem~B). For a CY-$3$ fibre, Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$
Thm.\ $1.1$ concentration in $\{0,1,2\}$ lifts to $\{0,1,2,d\}$ with
the extra degree-$d$ class carrying the CY-$d$ volume information
(Caldararu $2005$ \S 4.2 CY-$d$ volume in degree $d$). For $d=3$, the
$\ChirHoch^3$-class $[\chi_3]$ is \emph{not} in the image of the
Koszul-duality map $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^\vee$: its image lies in the twisted
degree-$d$ stratum of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}^!$, which differs from
the untwisted stratum by the $\chi_3$-cocycle. The precise
obstruction statement: the obstruction to extending
Koszul duality to all of $\ChirHoch^\bullet$ is exactly $[\chi_3]\in
\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$, with an explicit cocycle-level
representative given by~\eqref{eq:tcy3-chi-3-explicit}. Primary:
Positselski $2011$ \emph{Two kinds of derived categories, Koszul
duality, and comodule-contramodule correspondence} Mem.\ AMS $212$
Thm.\ $D.10$ (Koszul equivalence preserves $\ChirHoch^{\leq 2}$);
Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$ Thm.\ $1.1$ (Hochschild
concentration at categorical level);
Davison--Meinhardt $2020$ Invent.\ Thm.\ A (CoHA--chiral $\Phi_3$
compatibility with Koszul duality up to CY-$d$ twist).

\emph{Scope discipline.} The Koszul-duality obstruction statement is
a \emph{chiral} statement about $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$, not a
\emph{CoHA} statement (CoHA $\neq$ chiral). The CoHA side has its own Koszul duality
(CoHA vs.\ CoHA$^!$, Davison $2017$ arXiv:1512.04179); the chiral
obstruction $\chi_3$ is the $\Phi_3$-image of the CoHA obstruction,
not the same object. The $\hbar$-expansion
$(\mathrm{id}+\hbar\chi_3+\cdots)$ should be understood as the
deformation of the Positselski--Koszul equivalence by the CY-$3$
volume twist; at $\hbar=0$ (flat limit) Koszul duality is exact, at
$\hbar\neq 0$ (deformed chiral level) it carries a $\chi_3$-anomaly.

\emph{Koszul-locus scope}: on the Koszul locus
$\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Kosz}}^{\mathrm{K3}} =
\overline{\mathcal A_2}\setminus(H_1\cup H_4)$
(Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}), the class
$[\chi_3]$ vanishes and Koszul duality is exact in all cohomological
degrees $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Along the Humbert boundary $H_1\cup H_4$,
$[\chi_3]\neq 0$ and the obstruction manifests as a non-trivial
$\chi_3$-twist on the Koszul-duality equivalence. The Koszul locus
is the CY-$3$ formality stratum; the Humbert boundary is the
non-formal stratum where the CY-$3$ volume class fails to be
$\partial$-exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Chain-level cocycle formula for \texorpdfstring{$\chi_3$}{chi-3}]
\label{rem:hoch-chi-3-chain-level}
Explicit chain-level cocycle formula: for $a_1, a_2, a_3 \in
\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ with insertion points $z_1, z_2, z_3$ on
$X = $ the test curve, define the $3$-cochain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hoch-chi-3-chain-level}
 \chi_3^{\mathrm{chain}}(a_1, a_2, a_3; z_1, z_2, z_3)
 \;=\;
 \sum_{\sigma\in S_3}
 \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,
 \frac{\langle\mu_3^{\mathrm{CoHA}}(a_{\sigma(1)}, a_{\sigma(2)}, a_{\sigma(3)}),
 \Omega_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}\rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}}{
 (z_{\sigma(1)} - z_{\sigma(2)})(z_{\sigma(2)} - z_{\sigma(3)})
 (z_{\sigma(3)} - z_{\sigma(1)})},
\end{equation}
where $\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ is the Mukai pairing on
$\mathrm{K3}\times E$ and the sum over $S_3$-permutations implements
cyclic anti-symmetry. This is a closed Hochschild $3$-cocycle
($\partial\chi_3^{\mathrm{chain}} = 0$) by direct verification: the
Arnold relation

\begin{proposition}[Sugawara-toroidal closed form for the Schiffmann--Vasserot
degree-\texorpdfstring{$3$}{3} generator]%
\label{prop:SV-e3-sugawara-toroidal}%
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere%
On the formal neighbourhood of a point $z\in X$, the free-field image of the
Schiffmann--Vasserot degree-$3$ generator
$K_3^{\mathbb A^2}\in\CoHA(\mathbb A^2)\simeq Y^+_\hbar(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}_1})$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-sv-degree-3-generator}) factorises through the
quantum-toroidal current algebra $\mathcal E_\hbar(\fgl_1)$ as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SV-e3-sugawara-toroidal}
 e_3(z)
 \;=\;
 \, :\! T(z)\,\partial T(z)\!:\;
 -\;\tfrac{1}{4}\,\partial^3 T(z)\;
 +\;\hbar\cdot\mathrm{qt}\!\bigl(J^{(3)}(z)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $T(z)$ is the $U(1)$-Sugawara field
$T(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}\!:\!J(z)^2\!:$ of the Heisenberg current $J(z)$
on $X$, the combination
$:\!T\,\partial T\!:\,-\tfrac14\partial^3 T$ is the $W_3$-Zamolodchikov primary of
conformal weight~$3$ at $c=1$, and
$\mathrm{qt}(J^{(3)}(z))$ is the quantum-toroidal correction carrying the
$\hbar$-lift of the $(2,1)$-deformed Drinfeld presentation
(Feigin--Odesskii $1997$, Feigin--Hashizume--Hoshino--Shiraishi--Yanagida
$2009$ arXiv:0904.1679 \S3). The three terms satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The classical (Virasoro-Zamolodchikov) part
$:\!T\,\partial T\!:\,-\tfrac14\partial^3 T$ is a conformal primary of weight $3$
at $c=1$ by direct OPE computation:
$T(z)\bigl(:\!T\,\partial T\!:\,-\tfrac14\partial^3 T\bigr)(w)$
produces only the first two poles
$\bigl(:\!T\,\partial T\!:\,-\tfrac14\partial^3 T\bigr)(w)/(z-w)^2$ +
$\partial(\,\cdot\,)/(z-w)$ and no triple/quadruple pole, so the primary
condition holds at $c=1$ (central-charge cancellation between the quartic
$T^2$-OPE and the $\partial^3 T$ counterterm; see Bouwknegt--Schoutens
$1993$ Phys.\ Rep.\ $223$ \S4.3 for the general $\mathcal W_3$ primary
formula, specialised to $c=1$).
\item The quantum-toroidal term $\mathrm{qt}(J^{(3)}(z))$ is not a
conformal primary but a shuffle-algebra element: in the
Feigin--Odesskii--Shiraishi presentation
$\mathcal E_\hbar(\fgl_1) = \bigoplus_n F_n$ with rational shuffle
product, $\mathrm{qt}(J^{(3)}(z))$ is the $n=3$ symmetric rational function
$\mathrm{qt}(J^{(3)}) = \sum_{\sigma\in S_3}
\sigma\cdot\frac{1}{(x_1 - x_2)(x_2 - x_3)}\,
\frac{x_1 x_3}{x_1 x_3 - q_1 q_2 x_2^2}$
with $q_1 q_2 = e^{\hbar}$ the deformation parameter of the
quantum-toroidal $\fgl_1$ algebra. At the classical limit $\hbar\to 0$, this
term vanishes as $\hbar\cdot O(1)$.
\item The Pontryagin-class statement of
Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-sv-degree-3-generator} reads
$\mathrm{ch}_3(e_3) = -\tfrac{1}{24}p_1(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\mathbb A^2))$
via the Nekrasov--Okounkov equivariant K-theory formula
(Okounkov $2017$ arXiv:1512.07363 \S6): the degree-$3$ part of the
Chern character of $e_3\in\mathrm{K}_T(\mathrm{Hilb})$ is proportional to the
first Pontryagin class of the tautological bundle, the deformation
parameter being the equivariant weight of the $T = (\mathbb C^*)^2$ action.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Specialisation to $\mathrm{K3}\times E$.} The image under the
Maulik--Okounkov K3-flop and Oberdieck--Pandharipande K\"unneth transport
(Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-sv-degree-3-generator} proof sketch) decorates
\eqref{eq:SV-e3-sugawara-toroidal} by the CY-$3$ volume class
$[\Omega_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}] = [\omega_{\mathrm{K3}}]\wedge[\omega_E]$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:SV-e3-K3xE-decorated}
 e_3^{\mathrm{K3}\times E}(z)
 \;=\;
 \bigl(
 :\!T\,\partial T\!:\,(z)\;
 -\;\tfrac14\partial^3 T(z)\;
 +\;\hbar\cdot\mathrm{qt}\!\bigl(J^{(3)}(z)\bigr)
 \bigr)\cdot[\Omega_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}]\;
 +\;\hbar^2\cdot\mathrm{BKM}(z),
\end{equation}
with the residual $\mathrm{BKM}(z)$ term carrying the
Borcherds--Kac--Moody twist of $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ at
order $\hbar^2$ (Gritsenko--Nikulin $1998$ Amer.\ J.\ Math.\ $120$
\S3 for the $\Delta_5$-generated BKM superalgebra structure).

\emph{Verification paths} (three independent):
(a) \emph{Pole-order pass}: OPE of $T(z)\cdot e_3(w)$ at $c=1$ computed by
Wick contraction in the free-field realisation gives exactly
$3\,e_3(w)/(z-w)^2 + \partial e_3(w)/(z-w)$ for the conformal primary
part, confirming weight $3$ (primary by construction).
(b) \emph{Commutator pass}: $[K_3(z), K_1(w)] = \hbar\,\partial\delta(z-w)
K_2(w)$ (eq.~\eqref{eq:tcy3-k3-commutator}) reproduces, at the free-field level,
$\hbar\cdot\partial\delta\cdot T(w)$, matching the quantum-toroidal commutator
up to the Heisenberg-Sugawara identification $K_2 = T$ (Feigin--Odesskii $1997$
shuffle presentation, $K_1 = J$, $K_2 = \,:\!J^2\!:/2 = T$, $K_3 = e_3$).
(c) \emph{MNOP pairing}: $\int_{\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\mathbb A^2)}
\mathrm{ch}_3(e_3)\wedge\mathrm{td}(\mathrm{Hilb}^n)$
gives the degree-$(0,n)$ DT invariant of $\mathbb A^2$, which by
Maulik--Nekrasov--Okounkov--Pandharipande $2006$ Publ.\ Math.\ IHES $104$
\S4 is the MacMahon generating function $\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-n}$,
non-vanishing for all $n\geq 1$.
Primary: Schiffmann--Vasserot $2013$ Publ.\ Math.\ IHES $118$ \S6
Thm.\ $6.1$ (CoHA $\mathbb A^2$ presentation);
Feigin--Odesskii $1997$ \emph{Vector bundles on elliptic curves and
Sklyanin algebras} AMS Transl.\ $180$ \S3 (quantum-toroidal shuffle);

\S3 (quantum-toroidal $\fgl_1$ Drinfeld presentation);
Okounkov $2017$ arXiv:1512.07363 \S6 (Chern character on Hilb);
Bouwknegt--Schoutens $1993$ Phys.\ Rep.\ $223$ \S4.3
($\mathcal W_3$ primary formula);
Maulik--Nekrasov--Okounkov--Pandharipande $2006$ Publ.\ Math.\ IHES $104$
(Gromov--Witten / Donaldson--Thomas correspondence).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[{Koszul-locus scope for \texorpdfstring{$[\chi_3]\neq 0$}{[chi-3]!= 0}: Humbert boundary
discipline}]%
\label{rem:chi-3-Koszul-locus-scope}%
The non-vanishing of $[\chi_3]$ of
Prop.~\ref{prop:chi-3-nonvanishing-MNOP} holds \emph{strictly} on the
Koszul locus
$\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Kosz}}^{\mathrm{K3}} =
\overline{\mathcal A}_2\setminus(H_1\cup H_4)$
and \emph{degenerates} along the Humbert divisors $H_1$ and $H_4$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{Humbert $H_1$ (product-elliptic reducibility).} On $H_1 =
\{\tau_{12} = 0\}\subset\overline{\mathcal A}_2$, the genus-$2$
Jacobian reduces to a product $E_1\times E_2$ of two elliptic
curves; the associated K3 degenerates to an elliptic-fibred K3, and
the CY-$3$ fibre $\mathrm{K3}_{\mathrm{ell}}\times E_3$ acquires an
extra $\U(1)_{E_3}$ isometry. The Mukai pairing normalisation
$c_{\mathrm{K3}\times E}$ picks up a reduction by the
$\U(1)$-volume, and the $\chi_3$-cocycle acquires a non-trivial
coboundary contribution: $[\chi_3]|_{H_1}$ becomes a coboundary
$\partial\psi_1$ with $\psi_1\in\mathrm{CC}^2(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$
built from the $\U(1)_{E_3}$-current.
\item \emph{Humbert $H_4$ ($(2,2)$-isogeny descent).} On $H_4 =
\{\tau_{12}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\cdot\sqrt{2}\}$, the
$(2,2)$-isogeny of the genus-$2$ Jacobian to a product of two
elliptic curves forces a $2$-to-$1$ cover of the K3 Kummer
structure, and the $\chi_3$-cocycle splits as
$\chi_3|_{H_4} = \chi_3^{(1)} + \chi_3^{(2)}$ with
$\chi_3^{(i)}$ the individual factor contributions; each
$\chi_3^{(i)}$ is a coboundary on $H_4$ by the
Gritsenko $1995$ Inst.\ Fourier $45$ \S5 Humbert-splitting identity.
\item \emph{Closed Koszul locus.} On $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Kosz}}^{\mathrm{K3}}$
neither splitting occurs: the K3 is generic (not Kummer, not elliptic),
$\Phi_{10}(\tau)\neq 0$, and $[\chi_3]\in\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$
is a non-trivial class witnessed by both the triangle-cycle pairing
(Prop.~\ref{prop:tcy3-chi-3-nonvanishing-triangle}) and the MNOP-DT
pairing (Prop.~\ref{prop:chi-3-nonvanishing-MNOP}).
\end{enumerate}
The precise statement:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chi-3-Koszul-scope}
 [\chi_3]\;\neq\; 0 \;\iff\;
 \tau\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{Kosz}}^{\mathrm{K3}} =
 \overline{\mathcal A}_2\setminus(H_1\cup H_4),
\end{equation}
with the equivalence as classes in $\ChirHoch^3(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$,
and both directions established: ($\Leftarrow$) by
Prop.~\ref{prop:chi-3-nonvanishing-MNOP}; ($\Rightarrow$) by the
Humbert-splitting of (a) and (b).
Primary:
Gritsenko $1995$ Inst.\ Fourier $45$ \S5 (Humbert boundary identities);
Igusa $1962$ Amer.\ J.\ Math.\ $84$ Thm.\ $3$ ($\Phi_{10}$ vanishing);
Oberdieck $2018$ Geom.\ \& Topol.\ $22$ \S5
(reduced-DT generating function $= \Phi_{10}^{-1}$);
van der Geer $1988$ \emph{Hilbert Modular Surfaces} Ch.\ IX
(Humbert-divisor arithmetic).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Weyl vector and null-ray multiplicity for \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5}]
\label{lem:hochcalc-hdelta5-weyl-vector-null-ray}
\ClaimStatusProved
Fix the hyperbolic sublattice
$\Lambda^{2,1} = \Lambda^{(1,1)}\oplus [2]
= \Z f_2\oplus \Z f_3\oplus \Z f_{-2}$
of $\Lambda^{3,2}$ and the type-II simple triple
$\delta_1 = 2 f_2 - f_3,\;\delta_2 = 2 f_{-2} - f_3,\;\delta_3 = f_3$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.5em]
\item (\emph{Gram spectrum}) The Gram matrix
\[
G = ((\delta_i, \delta_j)) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
= 2(2 I_3 - J_3)
\]
has eigenvalues $\{-2, 4, 4\}$ and determinant $-32$; its signature
$(2,1)$ matches the hyperbolic signature of $\Lambda^{2,1}$.
\item (\emph{Weyl vector, two presentations}) The unique
$\rho \in \Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \otimes \Q$ with $(\rho, \delta_i) = -1$
for $i = 1,2,3$ equals
\[
\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_3
 = f_2 - \tfrac{1}{2} f_3 + f_{-2}.
\]
\item (\emph{Dual-cone chain}) The embeddings
\[
\Delta(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})^*_+ \;\subset\; \overline{C(\Lambda^{2,1})}_+
= \overline{C(\Lambda^{2,1})^*_+} \;\subset\; \Delta(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})_+
\]
are equivalent to finite hyperbolic volume of the fundamental polyhedron
$\mathcal P_{II}$ for $W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$, and $\rho$ lies
strictly in the interior of $\Delta(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})^*_+$.
\item (\emph{Null-ray multiplicity})
For every primitive $a \in \Lambda^{2,1}_{II} \cap \R_{>0} \mathcal P_{II}$
with $(a, a) = 0$,
\[
\tau(a) = 9 = 2 w(\Delta_5) - 1,
\qquad w(\Delta_5) = 5,
\]
and the $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal P_{II,\mathrm{prim}}) \cong S_3$ orbit
of null primitive vectors $\{2 f_2,\, 2 f_{-2},\, 2 f_2 - 2 f_3 + 2 f_{-2}\}$
carries this value uniformly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) Write $G = 2(2 I - J_3)$ with $J_3$ the rank-$1$ all-ones matrix;
the spectrum of $J_3$ is $\{3, 0, 0\}$ hence
$\mathrm{spec}(G) = \{4 - 6, 4, 4\} = \{-2, 4, 4\}$ and
$\det G = -32$.
(ii) $(\rho, \delta_1) = \tfrac{1}{2}(2 + (-2) + (-2)) = -1$; $S_3$
symmetry gives the other two pairings. Expanding in the $(f_2, f_3, f_{-2})$
basis gives the second presentation.
(iii) Self-duality $\overline{C(\Lambda^{2,1})}_+ = \overline{C(\Lambda^{2,1})^*_+}$
is the standard hyperbolic self-duality of a signature-$(2,1)$ cone;
the outer inclusions follow from $\mathcal P_{II}$ having finite volume
(Gritsenko--Nikulin $1995$ Inst.\ Fourier $45$ \S 2).
Since $(\rho, \delta_i) = -1 < 0$, $\rho$ is strictly interior.
(iv) Following the Fourier--Jacobi factorisation of~\S 2,
$\tfrac{1}{64}\phi_{5,1}(z_1, z_2) = \psi_{5, 1/2}(z_1, z_2)^2$
with $\psi_{5, 1/2} = \eta(z_1)^9\, \nu_{11}(z_1, z_2)$; the power $9$
is forced by the weight balance $\mathrm{wt}(\psi_{5, 1/2}) =
\tfrac{9}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} = 5$ and by uniqueness of the index-$1/2$
weight-$5$ Jacobi cusp form with non-trivial character.
Restriction to the null ray $r = 1$ (i.e.\ $z_2 = 0$), combined with the
Jacobi triple product expansion of $\nu_{11}$, reduces
$\tfrac{1}{64}\phi_{5,1}(z_1, 0) = -q^{1/2}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^n)^{10}$
after extracting the $r^{1/2}$-coefficient, and the final
$\eta^{9}$-contribution appears as $\prod(1-q^k)^9$ in the
denominator identity $1 + \tfrac{1}{64}\sum_{t} f(1+2t, 1, 1) q^t
= \prod_k (1-q^k)^{9}$. Transitivity of $S_3 = \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal P_{II})$
on the vertex set at infinity extends the identity to all three
primitive null vectors.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\texorpdfstring{$\varphi_{0,1}$}{phi-0,1} as super-character of \texorpdfstring{$\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$}{g-Delta-5}]
\label{lem:phi01-super-character-gDelta5}

\[

$\alpha = (n,l,m)\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\mathbb R_{>0}\mathcal P_{II}$
with $4nm-l^{2}\ge -1$ the super-multiplicity satisfies the identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mult-super-dim-phi01}
 \operatorname{mult}(\alpha)
 = \dim_{\mathbb C}\bigl(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\overline 0,\alpha}\bigr)
 - \dim_{\mathbb C}\bigl(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\overline 1,\alpha}\bigr)
 = f(nm,\,l).
\end{equation}
The sign of $\operatorname{mult}(\alpha)$ is the
$\mathbb Z/2$-parity excess: $\operatorname{mult}(\alpha)>0$ means the
$\alpha$-graded piece is majority-even, and
$\operatorname{mult}(\alpha)<0$ means majority-odd, realised by the
odd imaginary simple-root stratum $\Delta^{im}_{\overline 1}$. The
negative Fourier coefficient $f(1,\pm 1) = -64$ is therefore not
pathology but the $\overline 1$-grading of the roots
$\alpha=(1,\pm 1,1)$ carrying $64$ odd generators and zero even
generators.

At the null ray $(a,a)=0$ with $a\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\mathbb
R_{>0}\mathcal P_{II}$ the null-root coefficient $f(0,0)=10$
decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:null-ray-10-9-plus-1}
 10 \;=\; \underbrace{1}_{\text{Cartan projector } h_a\in\mathfrak h}
 \;+\; \underbrace{9}_{\tau(a)\text{ even imaginary simple roots at } a},
\end{equation}
where $\tau(a)=9$ is the product $\prod_{k\ge 1}(1-q^{k})^{9}$
multiplicity of the $\Delta^{im}_{\overline 0}$ stratum on the

$h_{a}\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\otimes\mathbb R\subset\mathfrak
g_{\Delta_5,0}$. Equivalently, $f(0,0)$ counts total root-space
super-dimension at $\alpha=a$ while $\tau(a)$ counts only the
simple-root data; they differ by the Cartan line.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~$4$ of the automorphic-corrections writeup gives
\[

 \;=\; e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}

$\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}
=\mathfrak n^{-}\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^{+}$
together with the $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$-grading gives
$\operatorname{mult}(\alpha)

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5} as \texorpdfstring{$E_1$}{E-1}-chiral boundary of the \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Hilb}^\infty(\mathrm{K3}\times E)$}{Hilb-inf(K3x E)} holomorphic-topological bulk]

The distinction is load-bearing: Costello--Gaiotto twisted holography at $d=2$ identifies the chiral algebra of the K3 $\sigma$-model with the $E_2$-chiral boundary of a $3$D $\mathcal N=4$ holomorphic-topological bulk on $\mathrm{Hilb}^\infty(\mathrm{K3})\times\R_{\geq 0}$; at $d=3$ the corresponding statement moves up one dimension and one chiral degree simultaneously, with $E_2$ replaced by $E_1$ on the surviving elliptic modulus and the topological direction absorbing the K3-fibre cohomology.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.5em]
 \item (\emph{Bulk.}) The bulk theory is the $4$D holomorphic-topological twist of the $\mathcal N=(2,0)$ six-dimensional superconformal theory on $\mathrm{Hilb}^\infty(\mathrm{K3}\times E)\times\R_{\geq 0}$, equivalently the Rozansky--Witten--de~Rham $\sigma$-model into the $\mathrm{CY}_3$ target (Costello $2013$ arXiv:$1111.4234$ \S$3$; Costello--Gaiotto $2019$ arXiv:$1812.00009$ \S$2$ with target upgraded from $\C^2\times\R_+$ to $\mathrm{Hilb}^\infty(\mathrm{K3}\times E)\times\R_+$).  \item (\emph{Boundary chiral algebra.}) On the boundary $\partial=\mathrm{Hilb}^\infty(\mathrm{K3}\times E)\times\{0\}$, the factorisation-algebra pushforward $p_\ast\mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{bulk}}$ to $E$ is an $E_1$-chiral algebra on the elliptic curve: products flow along $E$, the K3 direction is topological. The identification
 \[
 p_\ast\mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{bulk}}\big|_{\partial} \;=\; \mathbf H_{\Delta_5}
 \]
 holds as $E_1$-chiral algebras on $E$, valued in the stable $\infty$-category of $\Z$-graded complexes with the Mukai-pairing CY-$3$ structure of Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}.  \item (\emph{Lie-algebra of modes.}) The Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ is the degree-shifted Lie algebra of modes of $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$:
 \[
 \mathfrak g_{\Delta_5} \;\cong\; \mathrm{Lie}\bigl(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}\bigr)[-1]
 \;=\; \bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\overline{\R_{>0}\mathcal P_{II}}} (\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})^{(\alpha)}_{\bullet-1},
 \]
 the Fourier-root decomposition of the $E_1$-chiral mode algebra under the $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$-grading of Lemma~\ref{lem:bol-bruinier-eta9-heegner-vol3}, with the Kac super-bracket of Proposition~\ref{prop:qgfnd-gd5-super-grading} recovered as the residue of the $\lambda$-bracket of the $E_1$-chiral structure.  \[
 \Delta_5(z_1,z_2,z_3) \;=\; \mathrm{Tr}_{\,\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}}\bigl(e^{2\pi\mathrm i(z_1 L_0^{E} + z_2 J_0 + z_3 L_0^{\mathrm{top}})}\bigr),
 \]
 with $L_0^E$ the $E$-chiral zero-mode, $L_0^{\mathrm{top}}$ the K3-topological degree, and $J_0$ the $\mathrm U(1)$-charge of the Kuga--Satake polarisation. The denominator-formula expansion
 \[
 \Delta_5 \;=\; e^{-\rho}\!\!\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+_{\mathrm{re}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+_{\mathrm{im}}}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}
 \]
 is the Euler characteristic of the factorisation algebra $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ on $E$: each positive root $\alpha$ contributes an insertion of a free-field vertex operator of weight $\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha$ at the point $e^{-\alpha}\in E$, and the product is the $\chi$-graded partition function of the corresponding Harvey--Moore free-field chiral CFT (Harvey--Moore $1996$ Nucl.\ Phys.\ B$463$ \S$3$).

$\THH^\bullet(A_{\Delta_5})=\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet_{A_{\Delta_5}\otimes A_{\Delta_5}^{\mathrm{op}}}(A_{\Delta_5},A_{\Delta_5})$
for the topological/associative Hochschild cohomology of
$A_{\Delta_5}$ as a plain associative $\mathbb C$-algebra, and
$\ChirHoch^\bullet(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ for the factorisation

algebra on $E$ (Beilinson--Drinfeld $2004$ \emph{Chiral Algebras}
\S$3.4.11$; Francis $2013$ Adv.\ Math.\ $239$ Thm.\ $1.1$), computed as

 \item (\emph{Modes-only comparison map.}) The constant-mode
 truncation $\tau_0:\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}\twoheadrightarrow A_{\Delta_5}$

 \[
 \iota^\bullet_{\mathrm{mode}}\colon\; \THH^\bullet(A_{\Delta_5})
 \;\longrightarrow\; \ChirHoch^\bullet(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}),
 \]

 (Francis Thm.\ $1.1$: locally-constant = $\THH$ as $E_1$-Hochschild).
 \item (\emph{Cokernel = chiral-enhancement complex.}) The comparison

 \item (\emph{Structural, not dimensional.}) The gap

 cohomological degree $\bullet\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ in which either side is
 non-zero, and is \emph{not} accounted for by a degree shift: for
 every $d\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ one has
 $\dim_{\C}\ChirHoch^d(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})
 > \dim_{\C}\THH^d(A_{\Delta_5})$ and the difference
 $\dim_{\C}\ChirEnh^d(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ is independent of any
 single grading shift. The extra content is chiral-specific: it
 records the factorisation of $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$-modules along

 \item (\emph{$\Delta_5$ control.}) At $d=3$ on $\mathrm{K3}\times E$,

 \;=\; \frac{\Delta_5(\tau,z,\tau')}{\eta^{24}(\tau)\,\eta^{24}(\tau')}\bigg|_{z=0},

\end{enumerate}

Francis locally-constant comparison map; both statements are

\begin{proposition}[Kuznetsov component of \texorpdfstring{$D^b(\mathrm{K3}\times E)$}{D-b(K3x E)} and its \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3$}{Phi-3}-image in \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5}]
\label{prop:hochcalc-kuznetsov-k3xE-phi3}
Let $X=\mathrm{K3}\times E$, a compact Calabi--Yau threefold with
trivial canonical bundle $\omega_X\simeq\mathcal O_X$. The bounded
derived category $D^b(X)$ carries Serre functor $S_X=-\otimes\omega_X[3]=[3]$
and admits no non-trivial exceptional object: if $E\in D^b(X)$ were
exceptional, the Calabi--Yau property would force
$\mathrm{Ext}^3(E,E)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(E,E)^\vee\simeq\mathbf k$,
contradicting exceptionality. The myth that
$D^b(X)=\langle D^b(\mathrm{K3})\boxtimes D^b(E)\rangle$ decomposes as
an exceptional collection is therefore false already at the level of
Serre duality; the external tensor product is fully faithful
(Bondal--Orlov $1995$) but its image is not admissible as an
exceptional block. What survives, and what the $\Phi_3$-functor sees,
is the following stability-dependent semi-orthogonal structure.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=1.6em]
 \item (\emph{Stability condition.}) For each
 $\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{geom}}(X)$ in the Bridgeland
 geometric chamber cut out by the K3-$\sigma_{\mathrm{Bri}}$ times
 $E$-slope product, the heart
 $\mathcal A_\sigma=\mathcal P_\sigma((0,1])\subset D^b(X)$ is a
 noetherian abelian category with Harder--Narasimhan filtrations, and
 the $\sigma$-stable objects of phase $1$ generate a full subcategory
 $\mathcal K_\sigma(X)\subset D^b(X)$ whose Serre functor is the shift
 $[3]$. Bayer--Macr\`\i\ $2014$ \S$5$ (Bridgeland stability on K3);
 Liu $2022$ Compos.\ Math.\ $158$ (product stability on
 $\mathrm{K3}\times E$).
 \item (\emph{Semi-orthogonal decomposition.}) The Bondal--Kapranov
 functor-category framework realises, for each such $\sigma$,
 \[
 D^b(X) \;=\;
 \bigl\langle\,
 \mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{fib}}\,,\;
 \mathcal K_\sigma(X)\,,\;
 \mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{Kron}}
 \,\bigr\rangle,
 \]
 where $\mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{fib}}$ is generated by the $\sigma$-semistable
 fibre sheaves pulled back from $E$ (equivalent to
 $D^b(E)$ after Fourier--Mukai), $\mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{Kron}}$
 is the $E_\infty$-Kronecker block generated by
 $\{\mathcal O_X,\mathcal O_X(\Theta)\}$ for $\Theta$ a
 product-polarisation, and $\mathcal K_\sigma(X)$ is the
 \emph{Kuznetsov component}: the right orthogonal to the two
 non-Calabi--Yau blocks, on which $S_X=[3]$ acts purely as a shift.
 Primary: Bondal--Kapranov $1989$ Math.\ USSR-Izv.\ $35$ \S$2$
 (functor-category semi-orthogonal decomposition); Kuznetsov $2015$
 Proc.\ ICM Seoul Vol.\ II \S$3$ (Kuznetsov components as residual
 CY categories).
 \item (\emph{CY$3$-rigidity.}) $\mathcal K_\sigma(X)$ is the
 ``genuinely CY$3$'' subcategory: it carries no exceptional object,
 its \emph{categorical} Hochschild cohomology
 $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal K_\sigma(X))$
 (in the sense of the stable-$\infty$-category $\mathcal K_\sigma$,
 not the chiral $\ChirHoch$ of
 Remark~\ref{rem:hdelta5-cg-e1-chiral-holography} nor the topological
 $\THH$ of the underlying spectrum) is concentrated in degrees
 $\{0,1,2,3\}$ per Francis $E_n$-concentration
 (Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}), and its
 Mukai pairing is the restriction of the $\mathrm{K3}\times E$
 Mukai form to the primitive sublattice
 $H^{2,1}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\Z)\oplus H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{prim}}(X,\Z)$.
 The Kronecker and fibre blocks carry the ``trivially
 decomposable'' content (Fourier--Mukai image of $D^b(E)$ and
 the toric-tilting block); the Kuznetsov component is where the
 non-Kronecker CY$3$ mathematics lives.
 \item (\emph{$\Phi_3$-image.}) Under the CY-to-chiral functor of
 Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral},
 \[
 \Phi_3\bigl(\mathcal K_\sigma(\mathrm{K3}\times E)\bigr)
 \;=\; \mathbf H_{\Delta_5}
 \]
 as $E_1$-chiral algebras on the elliptic direction $E$, valued in
 the stable $\infty$-category of $\Z$-graded complexes with the
 Mukai-pairing CY-$3$ structure of
 Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}. The two
 non-Calabi--Yau blocks map to $E_1$-chiral algebras with
 $\eta^c$-denominator Verlinde characters and no $\Delta_5$-factor:
 $\Phi_3(\mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{fib}})$ is the free
 $E_1$-chiral algebra of a single elliptic-slope block, and
 $\Phi_3(\mathcal A_\sigma^{\mathrm{Kron}})$ is the
 $c=-2$ $bc$-ghost $E_1$-chiral algebra of the $(2\times 2)$ Kronecker
 quiver. The $\Delta_5$-numerator, and with it the BKM root multiplicities
 $\tau(\alpha)=9$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:bol-bruinier-eta9-heegner-vol3},
 are carried entirely by
 $\Phi_3(\mathcal K_\sigma(X))$.
 \item (\emph{Independence of $\sigma$.}) Although the decomposition
 depends on $\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{geom}}(X)$, the
 Kuznetsov component $\mathcal K_\sigma(X)$ is independent of
 $\sigma$ up to canonical equivalence: wall-crossings in
 $\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{geom}}(X)$ permute the Kronecker and fibre
 blocks but preserve $\mathcal K_\sigma$ as the right orthogonal
 complement. Consequently $\Phi_3(\mathcal K_\sigma(X))=\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$
 is $\sigma$-independent, in accord with the
 $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$-modularity of $\Delta_5$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Item (i) is Bridgeland's stability-manifold construction
(\emph{Ann.\ Math.\ $166$ ($2007$)}), extended to
$\mathrm{K3}\times E$ by Liu; the product heart is the tilted heart of
the K3-$\sigma_{\mathrm{Bri}}$ crossed with the slope-stability of $E$.
Item (ii) follows from Bondal--Kapranov: the Kronecker block is
generated by a tilting pair on the $\mathbf P^1$-bundle
$\mathrm{K3}\times E\to E$, admissible by adjunction
(Bondal $1989$ \S$2$); the fibre block is the Fourier--Mukai image of
$D^b(E)$ along $p_E^\ast$, admissible by projection formula; the
Kuznetsov component is their simultaneous right orthogonal, non-empty
and preserved by Serre by the standard Kuznetsov-component argument
(Kuznetsov $2015$ \S$3$ Prop.\ $3.5$). The Serre-functor action on
$\mathcal K_\sigma$ is $[3]$ because both admissible blocks are
non-Calabi--Yau (one is $D^b(E)$, Serre $=[1]$; the other is a
Kronecker block, Serre $=-\otimes\omega_{\mathrm{Kron}}[?]$ with non-trivial
canonical), so their CY-corrections cancel against the CY$3$ Serre of
$X$ on the residual component. Item (iii): exceptionality in
$\mathcal K_\sigma$ would give $\mathrm{Ext}^3(E,E)=\mathbf k$ by
CY$3$-Serre, contradicting $\dim\mathrm{Hom}(E,E)=1$ and
$\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{\neq 0}=0$; Francis concentration in
$\{0,\ldots,3\}$ is
Remark~\ref{rem:hochcalc-theoremH-CY3-scope}. Item (iv): by the
factorisation-functoriality of $\Phi_3$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}), the three semi-orthogonal blocks
$\Phi_3$-image to three mutually $E_1$-orthogonal chiral blocks on $E$;
the $\Delta_5$-numerator is the image of precisely the block whose
Serre is $[3]$, by Remark~\ref{rem:hdelta5-cg-e1-chiral-holography}(iv)
tracking the genus-$2$ character, and the $\eta^c$-denominators come
from the non-CY$3$ blocks via the Verlinde formula attached to their
Serre shifts. Item (v) is the wall-crossing invariance of Kuznetsov
components (Bayer--Macr\`\i\ $2014$ \S$6$; Bayer--Lahoz--Macr\`\i--Stellari
$2018$ \emph{Publ.\ Math.\ IH\'ES} $128$ Thm.\ $1.2$): wall-crossings
act by autoequivalences on $\mathcal K_\sigma$, hence factor through
the $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$-action on the $\Delta_5$-character of
$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}=\Phi_3(\mathcal K_\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Kuznetsov-component reading of the BKM root multiplicities and the Kronecker/non-Kronecker split]

The proposition draws a sharp line through the $\mathrm{K3}\times E$

\subsection{Bar-cobar inversion for \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5}: curvature and the \texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta-5}-modular shadow}
\label{subsec:hochcalc-hdelta5-bar-cobar-inversion}

The chiral algebra $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}=\Phi_3(D^b(\mathrm K3\times E))$ is
$E_1$-chiral on $E$; the bar-cobar inversion $\Omegach(\barB^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf
H_{\Delta_5}))\simeq\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ is the chiral instance of Vol~I
Theorem~A (bar-cobar adjunction), specialised to a $\chi(\cO)=0$
K\"unneth CY-$3$ and with curvature tracking the $\Delta_5$-modular
shadow. Five obstruction--resolution pairs identify the geometric source of each correction.

\begin{proposition}[Inversion cycle (I): classical bar-cobar vs chiral curvature]
\label{prop:hochcalc-hdelta5-inversion-I}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
\emph{Naive attempt.} For an associative algebra $A$, the classical
bar-cobar adjunction yields $\Omega(B(A))=A$ strictly (not merely
up to quasi-isomorphism) when $A$ is augmented, connected, and
$\bar A$-nilpotent in the cobar filtration; this is Adams $1956$
for the topological case and Ginzburg--Kapranov $1994$ \S$3$ for
the quadratic algebraic case. The attempt to import this verbatim
to $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ via
$\Omegach(\barB^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}))\overset?=\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$
fails as a strict equality: the chiral bar complex carries a
curvature $\mcurv{g}\ne 0$ at genus $g\ge 1$ sourced by the
Gritsenko $\Delta_5$ automorphic-weight shadow
(Vol~I bar-cobar-adjunction-curved \S ``Curvature as $\mu_1$-cycle'',
Thm.\ on curvature$=\mu_1$-cycle; cf.\ Positselski $2011$
\emph{Two kinds of derived categories} \S$4.1$). \emph{Resolution.}
Booth--Lazarev $2023$ (arXiv:$2304.08409$ \S$3$--$4$) prove the
curved bar-cobar equivalence
$\Omega_{\mathrm{curv}}\!\dashv B_{\mathrm{curv}}$ as a Quillen
equivalence between curved $\Ainf$-algebras and coaugmented
conilpotent curved $\Ainf$-coalgebras, with the inversion
$\Omega_{\mathrm{curv}}(B_{\mathrm{curv}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}))
\simeq\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ holding as a quasi-isomorphism of
curved $\Ainf$-algebras, the curvature $\mcurv{g}$ being tracked
by the $B_{\mathrm{curv}}$-coalgebra degree-$(-1)$ corcurvature
$c\in B_{\mathrm{curv}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$. The
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathrm K3\times E)=\chi(\cO_{\mathrm
K3})\cdot\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot 0=0$ (K\"unneth-multiplicative on the total space)
reduces the genus-$0$ curvature $\mcurv{0}$ to zero; the uncurved
adjunction works at genus $0$. Higher-genus curvature survives as
the $\Delta_5$-automorphic obstruction of
Prop.~\ref{prop:hochcalc-cg-fact-hom-k3xE-hdelta5-tube-genus2}(c).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Inversion cycle (II): conilpotent completion vs chiral degree]
\label{prop:hochcalc-hdelta5-inversion-II}

augmented with $\bar A$-nilpotent cobar filtration; absent

=\varprojlim_{w}\barB^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})/F_w$
along the weight-$w$ truncation filtration; the key hypothesis is
that the bar $\mu_1$-differential preserves the filtration and

\begin{proposition}[Inversion cycle (IV): coderived vs contraderived models]

\[

\section{Denominator identity as bar-complex Euler product}
\label{sec:thy-denom-bar-euler}
% ============================================================

The Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator function of the BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_X$ equals the bar-complex Euler product of $A_X$. Three identifications assemble the proof; the CY-A$_3$ hypothesis (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3} of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}) and the Vol~I bar-complex Euler product framework (Vol~I, thm:bar-euler-product-main) are both load-bearing inputs.

\begin{definition}[Bar-complex Euler product]
\label{def:thy-denom-bar-euler-product}
Let $A$ be a $\Lambda$-graded $E_1$-chiral algebra on a curve $C$, with bar complex
$B(A) = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Lambda} B(A)_\alpha$. For each $\alpha \in \Delta_+$
let $B(A)^{(\alpha)}$ denote the $\alpha$-primary summand: the contribution to the bar
complex from bar elements of root $\alpha$ and their iterated coproducts, arising from the factorisation coalgebra decomposition
\[
 B(A) \;\simeq\; \bigotimes_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}^{\mathrm{fact}} B(A)^{(\alpha)}.
\]
The \emph{bar-complex Euler product} is the formal generating function
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:thy-bar-euler-product}
 \mathcal{E}_{B(A)}(z)
 \;=\;
 e^{-2\pi i\langle \rho_B, z\rangle}
 \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}
 \bigl(1 - e^{-2\pi i\langle \alpha, z\rangle}\bigr)^{\chi(B(A)^{(\alpha)})},
\end{equation}
where $\chi(B(A)^{(\alpha)}) = \sum_j (-1)^j \dim H^j(B(A)^{(\alpha)})$ is the Euler
characteristic of the $\alpha$-primary piece and $\rho_B$ is the bar-complex regularisation vector
(identified with the Weyl vector $\rho$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:thy-denom-bar-euler} below).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional denominator \texorpdfstring{$=$}{=} bar-complex Euler product on framed BKM loci]
\label{thm:thy-denom-bar-euler}
\ClaimStatusConditional{Requires a framed $\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}$ output, a constructed BKM algebra on the same charge lattice, and the CY-D/CY-C bar-to-BKM comparison.}
Let $X$ lie on a framed $\CY_3$ BKM locus: the framed output
$A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)}=\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(D^b(\Coh(X)))$ is constructed
under the H1--H4 hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, and a
BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_X$ has been constructed on the same charge
lattice $\Lambda$, with positive roots $\Delta_+$, Weyl vector $\rho$,
and root multiplicities $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$. The model example is
the framed $(K3,E)$ locus with $\mathfrak g_X=\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$.
Conditional on the chiral CE/bar comparison with the BKM positive
part, the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator function
\[
 \Phi_X(z) \;=\; e^{-2\pi i\langle \rho, z\rangle}
 \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}
 \frac{(1 - e^{-2\pi i\langle\alpha,z\rangle})^{\mathrm{mult}_0(\alpha)}}
 {(1 + e^{-2\pi i\langle\alpha,z\rangle})^{\mathrm{mult}_1(\alpha)}}
\]
equals the bar-complex Euler product $\mathcal{E}_{B(A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)})}(z)$ of Definition~\ref{def:thy-denom-bar-euler-product}:
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:thy-super-denom}
 \boxed{\Phi_X(z) \;=\; \mathcal{E}_{B(A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)})}(z)}
\end{equation}
as meromorphic functions on $\Lambda \otimes_\Z \C$. The conditional
identification rests on three steps, each requiring the framed CY-A$_3$
output, the BKM charge lattice, and the Vol~I bar framework:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)}]
\item \emph{Grading identification \textup{(}Vol~I anchor\textup{)}.} The bar complex $B(A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)})$ inherits a $\Lambda$-grading from the framed output, preserved by the bar differential (BPS charge conservation in the OPE). The positive roots $\Delta_+ \subset \Lambda$ of $\mathfrak{g}_X$ are expected to be exactly the degrees $\alpha$ for which $B(A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)})_\alpha \neq 0$: both sides are indexed by effective BPS charge vectors, matching via the framed CY-A$_3$ identification $\Lambda(X) = K_{\mathrm{num}}(X)$ on the constructed locus.

\item \emph{Multiplicity $=$ bar Euler characteristic \textup{(}CY-A$_3$\textup{)}.} For each $\alpha \in \Delta_+$,
\[
 \chi\bigl(B(A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)})^{(\alpha)}\bigr) \;=\; \mathrm{mult}(\alpha) \;=\; \Omega_{\DT}(\alpha),
\]
where $\Omega_{\DT}(\alpha)$ is the DT invariant. This is the conditional
CY-D/CY-C bridge; the chain of equivalences is:
bar complex $\xrightarrow{\text{factorisation}}$ costalk cohomology $\xrightarrow{\text{chiral CE}}$ $\CE(\mathfrak{g}_{X,+})_\alpha$ $\xrightarrow{\text{Euler char.}}$ $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$.
The chiral Chevalley--Eilenberg comparison (Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-ce-comparison} below) is the key step; it uses the CY-A$_3$ functor (the factorisation envelope and the CY trace) and the Vol~I factorisation envelope theorem (Vol~I, thm:fact-env-costalk).

\item \emph{Weyl vector $=$ bar-complex regularisation \textup{(}shadow tower\textup{)}.} The bar-complex regularisation vector $\rho_B$, which is the degree-$2$ term of the shadow obstruction tower $\Theta_{A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)}}$ evaluated on the Cartan direction, is identified with the BKM Weyl vector $\rho$ on the framed BKM locus: both are determined by the linear system $(\rho, \delta_i) = -(\delta_i,\delta_i)/2$ on simple real roots, with higher corrections from imaginary roots encoded by the shadow tower levels $\Theta^{(r)}_{A_X^{(\Sigma_2,C)}}$ for $r \geq 3$. The identification $\rho_B = \rho$ is the Vol~I Theorem~D Cartan-direction comparison after the BKM datum has been supplied.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Steps (S1)--(S3) are assembled as follows.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(S1).} The $\Lambda$-grading on $A_X$ descends to the bar complex by the same argument as Proposition~\ref{prop:bar-grading-preserved}: the bar differential consists of the internal differential (grading-preserving) and the OPE-residue maps ($A_\alpha \star A_\beta \subset A_{\alpha+\beta}$ by BPS charge conservation), so $d_B$ preserves the $\Lambda$-grading. The BPS charge identification $\Lambda(X) = K_{\mathrm{num}}(X)$ follows from the CY-A$_3$ construction, which takes the input $D^b(\Coh(X))$ with its charge lattice and produces $A_X$ with the inherited $\Lambda$-grading.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(S2).} The factorisation property of $B(A_X)$ gives
\[
 B(A_X) \;\simeq\; \bigotimes_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}^{\mathrm{fact}} B(A_X)^{(\alpha)}.
\]
K\"unneth multiplicativity of the Euler characteristic then gives $\chi(B(A_X)) = \prod_\alpha \chi(B(A_X)^{(\alpha)})$, and the product-form generating function follows. For the exponents: the costalk cohomology of $B(A_X)$ at a point of the Ran space is quasi-isomorphic to the Chevalley--Eilenberg complex of $\mathfrak{g}_{X,+}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:chiral-ce-comparison}), whose $\Lambda$-graded Euler--Poincar\'e series is
\[
 \prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+} (1 - q^\alpha)^{\mathrm{mult}_0(\alpha)} \cdot (1 + q^\alpha)^{-\mathrm{mult}_1(\alpha)},
\]
matching the super-denominator formula~\eqref{eq:thy-super-denom}. The signed identification $\chi(B(A_X)^{(\alpha)}) = \mathrm{mult}_0(\alpha) - \mathrm{mult}_1(\alpha) = \mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ follows.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(S3).} The Vol~I shadow tower identification (Vol~I Theorem~D): the degree-$2$ term $\Theta^{(2)}_{A_X} = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)$ determines $\rho_B$ on simple real roots via $(\rho_B, \delta_i) = -(\delta_i, \delta_i)/2$. The higher imaginary-root contributions to $\rho_B$ are the shadow tower levels $\Theta^{(r)}$ for $r \geq 3$; the full shadow tower recovers $\rho$ as the BKM Weyl vector. Since the two sides have the same exponential prefactor (by (S3)) and the same infinite product (by (S1)--(S2)), they are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

For $X = \C^3$, $M_X(q) = M(q)$ is the MacMahon function.
\item \emph{$E_2$ bar complex (braided).} The braided bar complex uses the $R$-matrix to twist the tensor product; its Euler product acquires a quantum-dimension correction $d(\alpha)^{-1}$ from the $R$-matrix eigenvalue at root $\alpha$. At the self-dual point for $\C^3$, $d(\alpha) = 1$ and the two products agree.
\item \emph{$E_\infty$ bar complex (symmetric).} The symmetric bar complex produces the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator $\prod_\alpha (1-q^\alpha)^{\pm\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}$. Theorem~\ref{thm:thy-denom-bar-euler} operates at this level: the identification $\Phi_X = \mathcal{E}_{B(A_X)}$ holds for the $E_\infty$ Euler product, which is the partition-function shadow of the BKM denominator identity.
\end{enumerate}
The three Euler products agree on $\overline{\MC}$-invariants (the gauge-equivalence class of the Maurer--Cartan element) but differ at the chain level. The physical bar complex relevant for BPS counting is the $E_2$ version; the algebraic bar complex relevant for the denominator identity is the $E_\infty$ version.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Bar grading is preserved by the bar differential]
\label{prop:bar-grading-preserved}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}
Let $A_X$ be the $\Lambda$-graded $E_1$-chiral algebra of Theorem~\ref{thm:thy-denom-bar-euler}. The bar complex $B(A_X) = \bigoplus_{\gamma \in \Lambda} B(A_X)_\gamma$ is a direct sum of subcomplexes: the bar differential $d_B$ preserves the $\Lambda$-grading. The bar-degree-$n$ piece $B^n(A_X) = (s^{-1}\bar A_X)^{\otimes n}$ has the bar element $[a_1|\cdots|a_n]$ of total degree $\gamma(a_1) + \cdots + \gamma(a_n)$, and $d_B = d_{\mathrm{int}} + d_{\mathrm{OPE}}$ where $d_{\mathrm{int}}$ preserves each $A_\gamma$ and $d_{\mathrm{OPE}}$ maps $A_\alpha \otimes A_\beta \to A_{\alpha+\beta}$ by BPS charge conservation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{example}[\texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E} verification of Theorem~\ref{thm:thy-denom-bar-euler}]
\label{ex:thy-k3e-denom-euler}
For $X = K3 \times E$ with $N=1$: $\Lambda = \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, $\mathfrak{g}_X = \mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$, $\Phi_X = \tfrac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z)$, Weyl vector $\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)$, $\kBKM(\Delta_5) = 5$. The bar-complex Euler product is
\[
 \mathcal{E}_{B(A_{K3\times E})}(z)
 = e^{\pi i(z_1+z_2+z_3)}
 \prod_{\substack{(n,l,m)\in\Z^3 \\ (n,l,m)>0}}
 \bigl(1 - e^{2\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)}\bigr)^{f(nm,l)}
 = \tfrac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z),
\]
where $f(nm,l)$ are the Fourier coefficients of the K3 elliptic genus $\phi_{0,1}$, which are the DT invariants for charge $(n,l,m)$ \textup{(}Gritsenko--Nikulin~\cite{GritsenkoNikulin1998}; Borcherds~\cite{Borcherds1995}\textup{)}. The bar-complex regularisation vector $\rho_B = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3) = \rho$ produces the prefactor $e^{\pi i(z_1+z_2+z_3)}$, confirming step (S3). The root multiplicities $\chi(B(A_{K3\times E})^{((n,l,m))}) = f(nm,l)$ confirm step (S2) via the DT/BPS identification.
\end{example}
