\chapter{Hochschild Calculus for CY Categories}
\label{ch:hochschild-calculus}

The Hochschild complex $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$ of a CY category $\cC$ is the categorical analogue of the bar complex $B(A)$ of a chiral algebra: both are chain complexes built from iterated compositions, with the CY trace playing the role of the modular characteristic $\kappa$.  This chapter develops the Hochschild calculus in the CY setting, culminating in the CY Hochschild duality that identifies cohomology with shifted homology.  The bridge to chiral algebras is the content of Part~II: the functor $\Phi$ converts Hochschild data into bar-complex data, and the CY trace becomes $\kappa(\Phi(\cC))$.

\section{Hochschild homology and cohomology}
\label{sec:hh-basics}

This section reviews the Hochschild homology $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ and cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ of a dg category $\cC$, with emphasis on the structures relevant to the CY programme: the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet$ (which becomes the convolution Lie bracket of Volume~I), the Connes $B$-operator on $\HH_\bullet$ (which becomes the cyclic structure), and the HKR decomposition when $\cC = \Perf(X)$ for a smooth variety $X$.

\section{The Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence}
\label{sec:hh-hc-spectral}

The Hochschild-to-cyclic spectral sequence $E_1^{p,q} = \HH_{q-p}(\cC) \Rightarrow \HC_{q-p}(\cC)$ degenerates at $E_2$ for smooth proper CY categories (by the Hodge-to-de Rham degeneration theorem of Kaledin).  This degeneration is the categorical analogue of the PBW spectral sequence collapse (Volume~I, Koszulness characterisation K2): it converts the cyclic homology into a direct sum of Hochschild homology groups, just as Koszulness converts the bar cohomology into a direct sum of PBW-graded pieces.

\section{CY Hochschild duality}
\label{sec:cy-hh-duality}

\begin{theorem}[CY Hochschild duality]
\label{thm:cy-hh-duality}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere
For a smooth CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d$, there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \HH_{\bullet + d}(\cC)
\]
identifying Hochschild cohomology with (shifted) Hochschild homology.  Under this isomorphism, the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet$ corresponds to the BV operator (divergence operator) on $\HH_\bullet$; the Connes $B$-operator on $\HH_\bullet$ corresponds to the de~Rham differential under the HKR decomposition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The isomorphism $\HH^\bullet \simeq \HH_{\bullet+d}$ (``CY duality'' or ``Poincar\'{e} duality for Hochschild (co)homology'') follows from smoothness (the diagonal bimodule is compact) combined with the CY condition ($\cC \simeq \cC^\vee[d]$).  See Kontsevich (2006 ICM), Keller (2006), Ginzburg (2006 arXiv:0612139).
\end{remark}

\section{Categorical Hodge theory}
\label{sec:categorical-hodge}

Categorical Hodge theory (Kaledin, Kontsevich--Soibelman, Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev) provides the non-commutative analogue of the Hodge decomposition.  For a smooth proper dg category $\cC$, the periodic cyclic homology $\HP_\bullet(\cC)$ carries a filtration whose associated graded recovers $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$.  In the CY setting, the CY structure class $[\sigma] \in \HC^-_d(\cC)$ determines a preferred splitting.  The target: the categorical Hodge filtration on $\HP_\bullet(\cC)$ should correspond, under $\Phi$, to the shadow obstruction tower filtration on the modular convolution algebra $\mathfrak{g}^{\mathrm{mod}}_A$ (Volume~I).
