\chapter{Cyclic \texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-Structures}
\label{ch:cyclic-ainf}

A Calabi--Yau category enters the CY-to-chiral construction through a
cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of dimension $d$. The correspondence
$\{\Phi_d\}$, the chiral modular characteristic
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, and the algebraisation-dependent entries of
the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum all read this input. The cyclic dimension
$d$ is the CY dimension in the CY-A construction.

\begin{remark}[Three-axis scope of this chapter]
\label{rem:cyclic-ainf-three-axis-scope}
The cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of dimension $d$, the cyclic invariance
identity, the negative-cyclic CY class $[\sigma] \in \HC^-_d(\cC)$,
the Connes $B$-operator, the cyclic bar complex
$\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$, the $\mathbb S^d$-framing of Hochschild
homology, the Costello operadic description, and the Mukai pairing on
$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ all live at \emph{level $0$} of the universal
arrow. The chart datum is empty: the cyclic $\Ainf$-structure is
intrinsic to the input $\cC$ and is identical across the four
equivariance strata. The ambient is chain-level cyclic $\Ainf$
(equivalently, $(\infty,1)$-categorical $\CY_d$ in the Kontsevich--Soibelman
sense); both lanes are load-bearing. Promotion to level $\geq 1$
occurs via the bridge to $\PhiFA_d$ in
\S\ref{sec:bridge-to-functor} and is the subject of
Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cyclic input for \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_d$}{Phi-d}]
\label{rem:cyclic-ainf-cross-deps}
The cyclic $\Ainf$-input fixed here is the data acted on by the
correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}$ of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:phi-platonic}}
(Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}}), characterised by the four
universal properties \textup{(U1)}--\textup{(U4)}. The dimension $d$ of
the cyclic structure is the integer entering the dimension
stratification of $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ values in
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}}
(Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:cy-d-kappa-stratification}}); for
K3-fibered CY$_3$s, the Borcherds weight
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ of
Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:bkm-weight-universal}} supplies the
second algebraisation invariant. The cyclic bar complex of
Section~\textup{\ref{sec:cyclic-structures}} is the chain-level
carrier of the universal Maurer--Cartan element; the per-family Koszul
conductor $K = -c_{\mathrm{ghost}}(\BRST)$ provides an independent
cross-volume check on the additive constants.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\kappa_\bullet$}{kappa-.} invariants]
\label{rem:cyclic-ainf-kappa-convention}
The spectrum uses four subscripted invariants
$\{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\}$; the whole spectrum
is denoted $\kappa_\bullet$. Against the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C} \circ \PhiFA_d$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}), the four entries split
across three tiers: tier~(i) CY-datum intrinsic
($\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$, Hodge-diamond input); tier~(ii) stage-$1$
invariant ($\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$, read off the canonical
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on $X$ before specialisation); tier~(iii)
stage-$2$ specialisation-dependent
($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$, attached to the output
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ on the reference curve). Only
the tier~(iii) pair is visible to the cyclic $\Ainf$-input through
$\Phi_d$ via its dependence on the specialisation datum
$(\Sigma_{d-1},C)$; the tier-(i)/(ii) pair is read off directly from
the Hodge structure and the Mukai lattice.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-algebras}
\label{sec:ainf-algebras}

The derived category of a CY manifold is not a strict associative algebra: the composition of $\Ext$-classes is associative only up to homotopy, and the homotopy itself is associative only up to a higher homotopy. Stasheff's $\Ainf$-formalism records the entire tower of homotopies as primary data $\mu_n$ rather than secondary corrections.

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-algebra]
\label{def:ainf-algebra}
An \emph{$\Ainf$-algebra} over a field $k$ is a $\Z$-graded $k$-vector space $A$ equipped with operations
\[
 \mu_n \colon A^{\otimes n} \longrightarrow A, \qquad \deg(\mu_n) = 2 - n, \quad n \geq 1,
\]
satisfying, for every $n \geq 1$, the $\Ainf$-relation
\[
 \sum_{\substack{p+q = n+1 \\ 1 \leq i \leq p}} (-1)^{\dagger(i,p,q)}\,
 \mu_p\bigl(a_1, \ldots, a_{i-1},\, \mu_q(a_i, \ldots, a_{i+q-1}),\, a_{i+q}, \ldots, a_n\bigr) = 0,
\]
where $\dagger(i,p,q) = (i-1)(q-1) + q \sum_{j=1}^{i-1}|a_j|$ follows the Koszul sign rule (see Appendix~\ref{app:conventions}).
\end{definition}

Specializing to small $n$ yields the familiar relations:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $n = 1$: $\mu_1^2 = 0$, so $(A, \mu_1)$ is a cochain complex.
 \item $n = 2$: $\mu_1(\mu_2(a,b)) = \mu_2(\mu_1 a, b) + (-1)^{|a|} \mu_2(a, \mu_1 b)$, so $\mu_1$ is a derivation of $\mu_2$.
 \item $n = 3$: $\mu_2$ is associative up to the explicit homotopy $\mu_3$, giving the Stasheff associahedron relation.
\end{itemize}
An $\Ainf$-algebra with $\mu_n = 0$ for $n \geq 3$ is a differential graded associative algebra. The $\mu_n$ for $n \geq 3$ record the higher homotopies that obstruct strict associativity after homological projection (Kadeishvili's theorem: any dg algebra transfers to a minimal $\Ainf$-algebra on its cohomology).

The $\Ainf$-formalism originates with Stasheff's associahedra~\cite{Stasheff1963}. Its modern use, as the natural habitat for derived categories, mirror symmetry, and deformation quantization, is due to Kontsevich and Soibelman~\cite{KontsevichSoibelman2009} and Keller~\cite{Keller2001Ainfty}. For a CY category $\cC$, the derived category $D^b(\cC)$ lifts canonically to an $\Ainf$-enhancement; this enhancement is unique up to quasi-isomorphism (Lunts--Orlov, Canonaco--Stellari). The cochain-level (non-minimal) model is the input used by the CY-to-chiral correspondence: cyclicity is chain-level data and is not recoverable from the minimal model on cohomology alone.

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-category vs \texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-algebra]
\label{rem:ainf-cat-vs-algebra}
An $\Ainf$-category is the many-object generalization: objects $\mathrm{Ob}(\cC)$ and Hom-complexes $\Hom_\cC(X,Y)$ with composition maps $\mu_n \colon \Hom(X_0,X_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes \Hom(X_{n-1},X_n) \to \Hom(X_0,X_n)[2-n]$. An $\Ainf$-algebra is the one-object case. The category version is verbatim by tagging arguments with source and target objects.
\end{remark}

\section{Cyclic structures and CY dimension}
\label{sec:cyclic-structures}

A strictly associative algebra equipped with an invariant non-degenerate pairing is a Frobenius algebra; the pairing satisfies $\langle ab, c \rangle = \langle a, bc \rangle$. Once the multiplication is replaced by a tower $\{\mu_n\}$, a single cyclic identity for $\mu_2$ is no longer sufficient: each higher product must individually respect the pairing, and the resulting tower of identities is what carries the $\bS^d$-framing on Hochschild homology.

\begin{definition}[Cyclic \texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-algebra]
\label{def:cyclic-ainf-d}
A \emph{cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of dimension $d$} is a pair $\bigl((A, \{\mu_n\}_{n \geq 1}),\, \langle -, - \rangle\bigr)$, where $(A, \{\mu_n\})$ is an $\Ainf$-algebra and
\[
 \langle -, - \rangle \colon A \otimes A \longrightarrow k[-d]
\]
is a non-degenerate graded symmetric pairing of degree $-d$ (equivalently, $\langle a, b \rangle$ is nonzero only when $|a| + |b| = d$), satisfying the \emph{cyclic invariance} identity
\[
 \langle \mu_n(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n), a_{n+1} \rangle
 \;=\; (-1)^{\epsilon_n}\,
 \langle a_1, \mu_n(a_2, \ldots, a_{n+1}) \rangle
 \qquad \text{for all } n \geq 1,
\]
with sign $\epsilon_n = n + |a_1|(|a_2| + \cdots + |a_{n+1}|)$ determined by the Koszul rule.
\end{definition}

The integer $d$ is the \emph{CY dimension} of the cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra. In the language of Costello~\cite{Costello2005TCFT,Costello2007Ainfty}, a cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of dimension $d$ is precisely an algebra over the operad of framed chains on the moduli of disks with marked points, with a $d$-shifted symplectic structure. This operadic viewpoint is what produces, after homotopy transfer, the $\bS^d$-framing of Hochschild homology used in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}.

\begin{proposition}[Cyclic invariance on the Hochschild complex]
\label{prop:cyclic-invariance-hochschild}
Let $(A, \langle -, - \rangle)$ be a cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of dimension $d$. The pairing induces a non-degenerate pairing on the Hochschild complex
\[
 \langle -, - \rangle_{\mathrm{HH}} \colon \HH_\bullet(A) \otimes \HH_{d-\bullet}(A) \longrightarrow k
\]
implementing Poincar\'e duality of dimension $d$. Equivalently, the cyclic structure lifts Hochschild homology to negative cyclic homology with a class in $\HC^-_d(A)$ that is closed under the Connes differential.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Costello~\cite{Costello2007Ainfty} Thm.~A; Kontsevich--Soibelman~\cite{KontsevichSoibelman2009} Sec.~10. The distinction is essential: the CY trace lives in $\HC^-_d$, not in $\HH_d \to k$, because the $S^1$-invariance of the cyclic pairing is needed to produce the $\bS^d$-framing.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Geometric origin of \texorpdfstring{$d$}{d}]
\label{rem:d-geometric}
For $X$ a smooth projective CY $n$-fold, the derived category $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ carries a cyclic $\Ainf$-enhancement of dimension $d = n$. The pairing is the Serre-duality pairing
\[
 \langle -, - \rangle_{\mathrm{Serre}} \colon
 \Ext^i(\cF, \cG) \otimes \Ext^{n-i}(\cG, \cF) \longrightarrow \Ext^n(\cF, \cF) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{tr}} H^n(X, \omega_X) \cong k,
\]
using the trivialization $\omega_X \cong \cO_X$ that defines the CY structure. $d$ is the complex dimension, not the real dimension $2n$. The Serre functor $\mathbb{S} = (-) \otimes \omega_X[n]$ becomes $[n]$-shift under the CY trivialization, which is what produces the $d$-shifted pairing.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cyclic bar complex]
\label{rem:cyclic-bar-complex}
The cyclic pairing enters the bar complex $B(A) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar A)$ through the cyclic quotient $\mathrm{CC}_\bullet(A) = B(A)/(1 - t)$ where $t$ is the signed cyclic rotation. The factor $s^{-1}$ desuspends: $|s^{-1}v| = |v| - 1$. The augmentation ideal $\bar A = \ker(\varepsilon)$ is used rather than $A$ itself. The cyclic bar complex is the primary invariant of $(A, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle)$ and is what Part~\ref{part:bridge} promotes to a factorization coalgebra on curves.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cyclic symmetry at \texorpdfstring{$n = 2$}{n = 2}]
\label{rem:cyclic-n2}
Specializing the cyclic invariance identity to $n = 2$ gives
\[
 \langle \mu_2(a_1, a_2), a_3 \rangle \;=\; (-1)^{\epsilon_2}\, \langle a_1, \mu_2(a_2, a_3) \rangle,
\]
which for a strictly associative cyclic algebra ($\mu_n = 0$ for $n \geq 3$) reduces to the standard invariance condition for a Frobenius algebra of degree $d$. The higher-$n$ cases are the $\Ainf$-correction: $\mu_3, \mu_4, \ldots$ must individually respect the pairing. In the $n = 3$ case, the identity
\[
 \langle \mu_3(a_1, a_2, a_3), a_4 \rangle \;=\; (-1)^{\epsilon_3}\, \langle a_1, \mu_3(a_2, a_3, a_4) \rangle
\]
is the first genuinely homotopical constraint and is what fails in a naive restriction of the Fukaya category to a sub-Lagrangian: cyclic invariance distinguishes the true CY input from merely $\Ainf$-enhanceable ones.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Cyclic invariance controls adjacent contractions only]
\label{rem:cyclic-adjacent-only}
Cyclic invariance of $\mu_n$ as stated in
Definition~\ref{def:cyclic-ainf-d} is a pointwise identity on the
$(n+1)$-tuple of factors entering the pairing
$\langle \mu_n(a_1, \ldots, a_n), a_{n+1} \rangle$. It permutes
factors cyclically \emph{within} the $\mu_n$-block together with the
pairing partner, but does not move factors \emph{across} the boundary
of the $\mu_n$-application. Consequently, when the operator
$B^{(2)}$ of the Connes hierarchy contracts one factor inside the
$\mu_n$-block with one factor outside, the resulting ``non-adjacent''
contraction is unreachable by cyclic invariance alone. This is the
mechanism by which the strict chain-level commutator $[m_k, B^{(2)}]$
is generically nonzero for non-formal cyclic $\Ainf$-algebras of CY
dimension~$3$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:chain-nonvanishing-generic} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:m3-b2-obstruction}). The cancellation belongs to
Costello's corrected TCFT carrier $B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{TCFT}}$ after the
moduli-chain correction and comparison datum have been supplied; it is
not an identity for the raw termwise operator $B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{term}}$.
In the explicit two-letter witness,
\[
 [m_3,B^{(2)}_{\mathrm{term}}][a|a|a|a|b]
 \;=\; 2\alpha\,[b] \;\neq\;0 .
\]
The cyclic identity of Definition~\ref{def:cyclic-ainf-d} is therefore
the adjacent-contraction datum of the chain-level $CY_3$ obstruction,
not the full cross-arity cancellation.
\end{remark}

\section{Examples}
\label{sec:cyclic-ainf-examples}

The CY dimensions $d \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ exhibit distinct numerical
invariants visible to the correspondence $\Phi_d$.

\begin{example}[\texorpdfstring{$d = 1$}{d = 1}: \texorpdfstring{$\mathfrak{sl}_2$}{sl-2} as cyclic \texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf}-algebra]
\label{ex:cy1-sl2}
Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with non-degenerate invariant form $\langle -, - \rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}$. The Chevalley--Eilenberg cochain complex $C^\bullet(\mathfrak{g})$ is a dg commutative algebra of dimension $\dim \mathfrak{g}$; applying Koszul duality twice produces a cyclic $\Ainf$-algebra of CY dimension $1$ whose underlying vector space is $\mathfrak{g}[1]$ with pairing $\langle -, - \rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}$ (shifted to degree $-1$). For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, fix the basis $\{e[1], f[1], h[1]\}$ of $\mathfrak{sl}_2[1]$ in degree $-1$. The binary operation $\mu_2$ is the desuspended Lie bracket:
\[
 \mu_2(e[1], f[1]) = h[1], \qquad \mu_2(h[1], e[1]) = 2\, e[1], \qquad \mu_2(h[1], f[1]) = -2\, f[1],
\]
with the other values determined by graded antisymmetry. The cyclic pairing has nonzero values
\[
 \langle e[1], f[1] \rangle = 1, \qquad \langle h[1], h[1] \rangle = 2,
\]
which is the trace form $\mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{def}}(XY)$ on the defining representation, shifted to degree $-1$, producing a CY structure of dimension $d = 1$. (The Killing form $B(h,h) = 8$ differs by a factor of $4 = 2 h^\vee$.) The $\Ainf$-relations reduce to the Jacobi identity, and all $\mu_n$ for $n \geq 3$ vanish. Under $\Phi_1$ this produces the affine Kac--Moody algebra $\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_2$ at the critical level: the simplest nontrivial entry of the CY landscape.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[\texorpdfstring{$d = 1$}{d = 1} from an elliptic curve]
\label{ex:cy1-elliptic}
Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\C$. The derived category $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(E))$ has a minimal cyclic $\Ainf$-enhancement of dimension $d = 1$, computed explicitly by Polishchuk~\cite{Polishchuk2011}. The generators are $\cO_E$ and a single degree-$1$ class in $\mathrm{Ext}^1(\cO_E, \cO_E) = H^1(E, \cO_E) \cong \C$. The Serre pairing $\mathrm{Ext}^1(\cO_E, \cO_E) \otimes \Hom(\cO_E, \cO_E) \to \C$ realizes the $d = 1$ cyclic structure. For higher-degree line bundles, Polishchuk's theta-function computation gives explicit formulas for $\mu_n$, $n \geq 3$, in terms of modular forms on $\Gamma_1(N)$. This example sits at the boundary between the classical $\bS^1$-framing and modular-form cyclic operations.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[\texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2} from K3]
\label{ex:cy2-k3}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective K3 surface. The derived category $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))$ is a cyclic $\Ainf$-category of dimension $d = 2$ (Caldararu~\cite{Caldararu2005}). The Hochschild cohomology is
\[
 \HH^\bullet(D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X))) \;\cong\; H^\bullet(X, \wedge^\bullet T_X) \;\cong\; H^\bullet(X, \C),
\]
using the CY trivialization $\wedge^2 T_X \cong \cO_X$. The Kontsevich HKR decomposition gives $\HH^0 \simeq k$, $\HH^1 \simeq 0$, $\HH^2 \simeq k^{22}$, $\HH^3 \simeq 0$, $\HH^4 \simeq k$ (Example~\ref{ex:hh-k3} of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories}), so
\[
 \dim \HH^\bullet(K3) = 1 + 0 + 22 + 0 + 1 = 24,
\]
recovering the Euler characteristic $\chi(X) = 24$ familiar from lattice K3 geometry. The Mukai row-grading $\HH_{p,q}$ with $p = q$ collapses the same total to $(1+1) + 20 + (1+1) = 2 + 20 + 2 = 24$. The holomorphic Euler characteristic, which is the relevant invariant for the CY kappa-spectrum, is
\[
 \chi(\cO_X) \;=\; 2.
\]
This is the compact K3 value $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathrm{K3}) = 2$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[\texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3} from the quintic]
\label{ex:cy3-quintic}
Let $Q \subset \P^4$ be a smooth quintic threefold. $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(Q))$ is a cyclic $\Ainf$-category of dimension $d = 3$. By homological mirror symmetry (Kontsevich's conjecture, established for the quintic at genus $0$ by Sheridan~\cite{Sheridan2015}), there is an $\Ainf$-equivalence
\[
 D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(Q)) \;\simeq\; D^\pi \mathrm{Fuk}(Q^\vee),
\]
where $Q^\vee$ is the mirror quintic and $\mathrm{Fuk}$ is the Fukaya category (wrapped, compact, depending on geometric input). Both sides are cyclic $\Ainf$ of dimension $d = 3$, and HMS is an equivalence of cyclic $\Ainf$-categories: the Serre pairing on the B-side matches the Poincar\'e pairing on Lagrangian intersections on the A-side. The Hochschild cohomology
\[
 \HH^\bullet(Q) \cong H^\bullet(Q, \wedge^\bullet T_Q)
\]
gives, using the CY$_3$ isomorphism $\wedge^r T_Q \simeq \Omega^{3-r}_Q$,
\[
 \HH^2(Q) \simeq H^1(Q, T_Q) \cong k^{101}
 \quad \text{(Kodaira--Spencer, $q+r = 2$ with $q=1,r=1$)},
\]
\[
 \HH^3(Q) \simeq H^0(\wedge^3 T_Q) \oplus H^1(\wedge^2 T_Q) \oplus H^2(T_Q) \oplus H^3(\cO_Q)
 \cong k^4
\]
(the summands identify with $H^0(\cO_Q), H^1(\Omega^1_Q), H^2(\Omega^2_Q), H^3(\cO_Q)$, of dimensions $1,1,1,1$, including the Yukawa-coupling generator $H^0(\wedge^3 T_Q) \cong H^0(\cO_Q)$). The holomorphic Euler characteristic is $\chi(\cO_Q) = 0$ by Serre duality on a CY$_3$, so the naive $\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ vanishes; the nontrivial chiral data enters through the Kodaira--Spencer summand of $\HH^2$, not through the top pairing. The notation $A_Q^{(\Sigma_2,C)} = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(D^b(\Coh(Q)))$ denotes the framed object-level output of Theorem~CY-A$_3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}) when H1--H4 and the admissible specialisation datum are fixed.
\end{example}

\section{The bridge to the CY-to-chiral functor}
\label{sec:bridge-to-functor}

The cyclic $\Ainf$-structure is the complete input to the CY-to-chiral correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}_{d \geq 1}$. The $d$-th signature is
\[
 \Phi_d \colon \mathrm{CY}_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow \mathrm{E}_{n(d)}\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg},
 \qquad (\cC, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle) \longmapsto A_\cC,
\]
where the operadic level $n(d) = n_{\mathrm{native}}(d) \in \{\infty, 2, 1, 1\}$ for $d \in \{1, 2, 3, \geq 4\}$ is dictated by property~\textup{(U3)} of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:phi-platonic}} (Gerstenhaber-bracket degree $1 - d$; Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}). Each constructed $\Phi_d$ factors as a two-stage construction $\Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)} = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C} \circ \PhiFA_d$: Stage~1 produces the native $E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the CY$_d$ variety, pinned by the cyclic pairing via Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality; Stage~2 applies the chiral specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}$ along a $(d-1)$-cycle and reference curve (Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}). At $d \geq 3$ the native output is $\Eone$ on the constructed specialisation locus; the braided $\Etwo$-structure is recovered on the Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$ via half-braidings (Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre}). The centre is the right adjoint to the forgetful functor $\mathrm{BrMon} \to \mathrm{Mon}$, a categorified commutant construction, not a categorified averaging map (averaging projects $\Eone$ to $E_\infty$ and kills the Hopf structure). The data of $(\cC, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle)$ factors through $\Phi$ by the four-arrow passage in Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}: \emph{categorical} Hochschild cohomology with Gerstenhaber bracket (distinct from the chiral Hochschild theory of the output and the topological Hochschild theory of slab $E_1$-algebras, see Remark~\ref{rem:cyclic-ainf-three-hochschild}), Lie conformal algebra via the cyclic pairing, factorisation envelope, and at $d=3$ the H4 $\bS^3$-framing datum needed for the framed object-level assignment. The cyclic pairing $\langle-,-\rangle$ becomes the invariant form on $A_\cC$; the trace on the chiral algebra is sourced from this pairing through the factorisation envelope.

\begin{remark}[Three Hochschild theories enter here separately]
\label{rem:cyclic-ainf-three-hochschild}
The first arrow of $\Phi$ forms the \emph{categorical} Hochschild
cohomology $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ of the cyclic
$\Ainf$-input (Chapter~\textup{\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}}). The
output~$A_\cC$ has the \emph{chiral} Hochschild complex
$C^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC,A_\cC)$. When this chart carries a
family datum $H_H(A_\cC;S_{A_\cC})$, Volume~I Theorem~H gives
\[
 \ChirHoch^n(A_\cC)\cong H^n(K_{A_\cC,S_{A_\cC}}),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Supp}\ChirHoch^\bullet(A_\cC)
 \subseteq S_{A_\cC}.
\]
Topological Hochschild theory $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{top}}$ for slab
$E_1$-algebras of spectra supplies the third functor. Thus the three
ambient categories are cyclic $\Ainf$-categories, chiral algebras on
a curve, and $E_1$-algebras of spectra; their constructions are
categorical derived endomorphisms, Ran-space chiral endomorphisms, and
$\THH$ of an $E_1$-ring. A passage between two of them is a specified
comparison map such as $\chi_{\Phi_d}$ or the mode-to-chiral map.
Accordingly, an unsubscripted Hochschild invariant of~$\cC$ denotes
$\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Cyclic \texorpdfstring{$\Ainf$}{A-inf} input determines \texorpdfstring{$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$}{kappa-cat} at \texorpdfstring{$d = 2$}{d = 2}, \texorpdfstring{$h^{1,0} = 0$}{h-1,0 = 0}]
\label{thm:cyclic-ainf-kappa-cat-d2}
\textsl{Scope:} level $0 \leftrightarrow$ level $2$ comparison
(tier-(i) input vs.\ Stage-$2$ output); chart $(\Sigma_1, C)$ on a
$\CY_2$ surface; ambient chain-level cyclic $\Ainf$.

Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper cyclic $\Ainf$-category of dimension $d = 2$ such that $\HH_{-1}(\cC) = 0$ (equivalently, in the geometric case $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$, the hypothesis $h^{1,0}(X) = 0$), and let $A_\cC = \Phi_2(\cC)$ be its image under the $d = 2$ CY-to-chiral correspondence (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral}). Then the categorical kappa (level $0$, tier-(i) intrinsic; Definition~\ref{def:cy-cat-hodge-supertrace}) is the Hodge supertrace
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC) \;=\; \chi^{\mathrm{CY}}(\cC) \;=\; \chi(\cO_X) \;=\; \sum_{q=0}^{d} (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X),
\]
and this equals the chiral modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC)$ (level $2$, Stage-$2$ output) of the image chiral algebra (Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-kappa-d2}; the Serre duality $\mathbb{S}_\cC \simeq [2]$ kills the one-loop correction from quantisation Arrow~4). The level-$0$ to level-$2$ promotion is the named comparison arrow $\Phi_2 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C} \circ \PhiFA_2$ at $h^{1,0} = 0$. For $\cC = D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(\mathrm{K3}))$, both invariants evaluate to
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathrm{K3}) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(\Phi_2(D^b(\Coh(K3)))) = 2$.
At $d \geq 3$ the identification $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}} = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$
holds only on the dimension-stratified replacement formula
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:cy-kappa-identification}); the structural reason
is recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:cy-d-d2-hypothesis} and
Remark~\ref{rem:cy-cat-d3}.

\section{Kapranov's \texorpdfstring{$L_\infty$}{L-inf}-structure on \texorpdfstring{$T_X[-1]$}{T-X[-1]} and the three Atiyah cocycles at \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3}}
\label{sec:cycainf-kapranov-linf}

A cyclic $\Ainf$-structure of dimension $d = 3$ on a geometric input
$\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$ with $X$ a compact Calabi--Yau threefold carries
more refined data than the shifted Serre-pairing alone: the tangent
complex $T_X[-1]$ is itself an $L_\infty$-algebra built from the
Atiyah class $\At(T_X) \in H^1(X, \Om^1_X \otimes \End T_X)$, and this
$L_\infty$-structure is the chain-level input that Stage~1 of the
two-stage factorisation $\Phi_3 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C} \circ \PhiFA_3$
reads from $\cC$. Three cohomology classes extracted from the Kapranov
$L_\infty$-structure enter $\PhiFA_3$ in distinct roles; they live in
Hodge-disjoint bidegrees, they are not coupled by Hodge-Serre duality,
and they are distinct. What follows is the chain-level
identification and the dictionary to the cyclic $\Ainf$-input of
Definition~\ref{def:cyclic-ainf-d}.

\subsection{The Atiyah class as chain-level datum}
\label{subsec:cycainf-atiyah-chain-level}

\begin{definition}[Dolbeault Atiyah representative]
\label{def:cycainf-atiyah-dolbeault}
For $X$ a complex manifold and $E \to X$ a holomorphic vector bundle,
choose any smooth $(1,0)$-connection $\nabla^{(1,0)}$ on $E$. The
Dolbeault representative of the Atiyah class is
\[
 \At(E) \;=\; [\bar\partial, \nabla^{(1,0)}] \;\in\; A^{0,1}(X,
 \Om^1_X \otimes \End E),
\]
whose $\bar\partial$-cohomology class in $H^1(X, \Om^1_X \otimes \End E)$
is independent of the choice of $\nabla^{(1,0)}$. On a K\"ahler manifold
with the Chern connection of the metric, the representative is
$\At(T_X) = R^i_{j k \bar \ell}\, dz^k \otimes d\bar z^\ell \otimes dz^j
\otimes \partial_i$, with $R^i_{j k \bar \ell}$ the Chern curvature.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kapranov's $L_\infty$-structure on $T_X{[-1]}$]
\label{thm:cycainf-kapranov-linf}
Let $X$ be a complex manifold. The Dolbeault complex
\[
 \mathfrak g_X \;:=\; A^{0,\bullet}(X, T_X[-1])
\]
carries a canonical $L_\infty$-structure $\{\ell_n\}_{n \geq 1}$, natural
in $X$, with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ell_1 = \bar\partial$ is the Dolbeault differential.
\item $\ell_2(v, w) = \At(T_X) \cdot (v \wedge w)$, antisymmetrised, is
the binary bracket, cohomologically equal to $\At(T_X)$ contracted
through the $\End T_X$-slot.
\item For $n \geq 3$, $\ell_n$ is an iterated-Atiyah-class operation:
\[
 \ell_n(v_1, \ldots, v_n) \;=\; \sum_{T}\, w_T \cdot
 \mathrm{Iterate}_T(\At(T_X))(v_1, \ldots, v_n),
\]
where $T$ runs over binary rooted trees with $n$ leaves, $w_T \in \Q$
are weights uniquely determined by the Maurer--Cartan recursion, and
$\mathrm{Iterate}_T$ is the iterated composition of $\At(T_X)$ along
$T$.
\item The $L_\infty$-Jacobi relation at arity $n$ follows from the
Bianchi identity $\bar\partial \At(T_X) = 0$ for $n = 3$ and from the
holomorphic Todd identity for $n \geq 4$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Kapranov, \emph{Rozansky--Witten invariants via Atiyah classes},
Compositio Math.~115 (1999) 71--113, Theorem~2.6 and Proposition~4.4;
Yu, \emph{A formula for the Atiyah class of a complex of vector
bundles}, Adv.\ Math.~271 (2015) 303--360 gives explicit covariant-
derivative formulas for the higher $\ell_n$. The Maurer--Cartan
recursion form is the statement that, starting from $\ell_2 = \At(T_X)$,
the brackets $\ell_n$ for $n \geq 3$ are determined (up to
$L_\infty$-homotopy) by the requirement that the $L_\infty$-Jacobi
relations hold order-by-order; the obstructions vanish at arity $3$ by
the Bianchi identity and at arity $n \geq 4$ by the holomorphic Todd
identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Minimal model and $\ell_3^{\min}$]
\label{rem:cycainf-ell3-min}
Taking $\bar\partial$-cohomology of $\mathfrak g_X$ replaces
$A^{0, \bullet}$ with $\bigoplus_q H^q(X, T_X)[-1-q]$. The Kadeishvili
transfer produces a minimal $L_\infty$-algebra on $H^\bullet(X, T_X[-1])$
whose brackets $\ell_n^{\min}$ are read off from the chain-level
$\ell_n$. For three inputs $v_1, v_2, v_3 \in H^1(X, T_X)$, the
triple-cup formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cycainf-ell3-min-triple-cup}
 \ell_3^{\min}(v_1, v_2, v_3) \;=\; \bigl[\At(v_1) \cup \At(v_2)
 \cup \At(v_3)\bigr]_{\bar\partial\text{-class}}
 \in H^3(X, T_X \otimes \Om^3_X \otimes \wedge^3 T_X^\vee),
\end{equation}
records the Kapranov ternary bracket at cohomology, where
$\At(v_i) \in A^{0,1}(X, \Om^1_X)$ is the contraction of $\At(T_X)$ with
$v_i$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three Hodge slots on a compact \texorpdfstring{$\CY_3$}{CY3}}
\label{subsec:cycainf-three-hodge-slots}

\begin{definition}[Three Atiyah cocycles]
\label{def:cycainf-three-cocycles}
For $X$ a compact Calabi--Yau threefold with holomorphic volume form
$\Om_X \in H^0(X, \Om^3_X)$, set
\begin{align*}
 \ell_2 &\;:=\; \At(T_X) \;\in\; H^1(X, \Om^1_X \otimes \End T_X),\\
 Y_3(v_1, v_2, v_3) &\;:=\; \int_X \Om_X \wedge \At(v_1) \cup \At(v_2)
 \cup \At(v_3) \;\in\; H^{0,3}(X) \;\simeq\; \Sym^3 H^1(X, T_X)^\vee,\\
 \alpha_{\BCOV} &\;:=\; \frac{\chi(X)}{24} \cdot \gamma_X
 \;\in\; H^{0,1}(X),
\end{align*}
where $\chi(X) = \int_X c_3(T_X)$ is the topological Euler
characteristic, and $\gamma_X \in H^{0,1}(X)$ is the Costello--Li
normalised dilaton class (the Dolbeault $(0,1)$-representative of the
BV anomaly density on the $\hCS$ BV complex; Costello--Li 2016
Proposition~5.2). When $h^{0,1}(X) = 0$, the group $H^{0,1}(X)$ vanishes
and $\alpha_{\BCOV} = 0$ identically; when $\chi(X) = 0$ the scalar
coefficient vanishes.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Hodge-disjointness of the three cocycles]
\label{prop:cycainf-hodge-disjoint}
The three classes $\ell_2$, $Y_3$, $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} inhabit pairwise distinct
Hodge components with pairwise distinct target bundles:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
Cocycle & Hodge bidegree & Target bundle\\ \hline
$\ell_2$ & $(1,1)$ & $\End T_X$\\
$Y_3$ & $(0,3)$ & $\cO_X$ (after $\Om_X$-trace)\\
$\alpha_{\BCOV}$ & $(0,1)$ & $\cO_X$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No two of these groups share a natural pairing via the Hodge decomposition;
in particular $H^{0,3}(X)$ pairs via Serre duality with $H^{3,0}(X)$
(not with $H^{0,1}(X)$), and $H^{0,1}(X)$ pairs with $H^{3,2}(X)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct inspection of the bidegrees and target bundles. The Serre-duality
statement is the standard identification $H^{p,q}(X) \simeq
H^{3-p, 3-q}(X)^\vee$ for a compact $\CY_3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What ``Serre-dual'' means in the BV pairing of $Y_3$ and $\alpha_{\BCOV}$]
\label{rem:cycainf-y3-alphabcov-bv-dual}
The Hodge-theoretic reading of Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-hodge-disjoint}
is that $Y_3$ and $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ are \emph{not} Hodge-Serre-dual.
The Costello--Gwilliam BV bracket on the $\hCS$ BV observables pairs
$Y_3$ (the tree-level cubic coupling) with $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ (the one-loop
anomaly) through the BV Poisson structure $\{-,-\}_{BV}$; this is a
quantum pairing at the level of the BV measure, not a duality of raw
Hodge classes. The statement ``$\alpha_{\BCOV}$ is Serre-dual to $Y_3$''
records the BV-bracket pairing, not a Hodge pairing.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[$Y_3$ as the $\Om_X$-trace of Kapranov's $\ell_3^{\min}$]
\label{prop:cycainf-y3-equals-omega-trace-ell3-min}
For $X$ a compact $\CY_3$, the symmetric cubic form $Y_3: \Sym^3 H^1(X,
T_X) \to \CC$ of Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} equals
the $\Om_X$-trace of Kapranov's $\ell_3^{\min}$:
\[
 Y_3 \;=\; \Om_X^* \, \ell_3^{\min}
 \quad \text{in } \Sym^3 H^1(X, T_X)^\vee,
\]
where $\Om_X^*$ denotes the pairing against $\Om_X$ via the
$\CY_3$ trivialisation $\wedge^3 T_X \simeq \cO_X$. Equivalently, the
Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa tree-level three-point Yukawa
coupling of the B-twisted sigma model on $X$ is the Kapranov minimal
ternary bracket, read through the CY trivialisation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Kapranov's $\ell_3^{\min}$ takes three inputs $v_i \in H^1(X, T_X)$ and
produces a class in $H^3(X, T_X \otimes \Om^3_X \otimes \wedge^3 T_X^\vee)$
via the triple-cup formula~\eqref{eq:cycainf-ell3-min-triple-cup}. The
$\CY_3$ trivialisation $\Om_X: \wedge^3 T_X \simeq \cO_X$ identifies
\[
 T_X \otimes \Om^3_X \otimes \wedge^3 T_X^\vee \;\simeq\;
 T_X \otimes \Om^3_X \otimes \Om^3_X \;\simeq\; T_X \otimes \Om^3_X
 \otimes \Om^3_X.
\]
Further trivialising $\Om^3_X \simeq \cO_X$ by contraction with $\Om_X$
on one factor and pairing with the volume integration on the other,
the output lives in $H^3(X, \cO_X) = H^{0,3}(X)$. The resulting triple
cup is exactly the Bershadsky--Cecotti--Ooguri--Vafa formula for the
tree-level Yukawa; compare Kapranov 1999 Proposition~4.4 and BCOV 1994
eq.~(2.4).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scope of $\alpha_{\BCOV}$]
\label{prop:cycainf-alphabcov-scope}
Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau threefold. The one-loop BCOV curving
$\alpha_{\BCOV}$ of Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} is
controlled by two independent vanishing conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $h^{0,1}(X) = 0$ (equivalently, $b_1(X) = 0$ on a strict
simply-connected $\CY_3$), then $H^{0,1}(X) = 0$, so $\alpha_{\BCOV}
= 0$ regardless of $\chi(X)$. This covers the quintic $Q \subset \P^4$,
every smooth CICY$_3$ with $h^{1,0} = 0$, and every non-fibered simply-
connected $\CY_3$.
\item If $\chi(X) = \int_X c_3(T_X) = 0$, then $\alpha_{\BCOV} = 0$
regardless of $h^{0,1}(X)$. This covers $X = K3 \times E$ (where
$\chi = \chi(K3) \chi(E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$), the abelian threefold
$A_3$, and any $\CY_3$ admitting a free $S^1$-action.
\item For $K3 \times E$ specifically, $\alpha_{\BCOV}(K3 \times E) = 0$
by~(2), even though $h^{0,1}(K3 \times E) = 1$ so the target group
$H^{0,1}(K3 \times E) \neq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) is immediate: if $H^{0,1}(X) = 0$, every class in that group is
zero. (2) follows from the definition: $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ is proportional
to $\chi(X)$, and zero coefficient gives the zero class. (3) specialises
(2) to $K3 \times E$ with the K\"unneth decomposition $\chi(K3 \times
E) = \chi(K3) \cdot \chi(E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Chain-level recursion: \texorpdfstring{$\ell_n$}{ell-n} from Maurer--Cartan}
\label{subsec:cycainf-ell-n-mc-recursion}

\begin{proposition}[Dolbeault--Christoffel form of $\ell_3$]
\label{prop:cycainf-ell3-christoffel}
In holomorphic coordinates $(z^a)$ on a coordinate patch $U \subset X$
with Chern connection $\nabla^{(1,0)} \partial_j = \Gamma^i_{j a}
\, dz^a \, \partial_i$ and Chern curvature $R^i_{j k \bar \ell} =
-\bar\partial_{\bar \ell} \Gamma^i_{j k}$, the Kapranov ternary bracket is
\[
 \ell_3(\partial_i, \partial_j, \partial_k)
 \;=\;
 \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\sigma \in \Sigma_3} (-1)^\sigma\,
 R^m_{\sigma(i) \sigma(j) \bar n}\,
 g^{n \bar k}\, \partial_m,
\]
modulo the $\bar\partial$-cohomology class of
Remark~\ref{rem:cycainf-ell3-min}. The $L_\infty$-Jacobi relation at
arity $3$
\[
 \bar\partial \ell_3 + \ell_2(\ell_2 \otimes \id) + \ell_2(\id
 \otimes \ell_2) \circ (1 + \sigma + \sigma^2) \;=\; 0
\]
is the Bianchi identity $\bar\partial R^i_{j k \bar \ell} = 0$ combined
with the Kapranov weight $w_T = 1$ at the single binary tree with three
leaves.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation from the Dolbeault resolution
$0 \to A^{0,0}(T_X)[-1] \xrightarrow{\bar\partial} A^{0,1}(T_X)[-1]
\xrightarrow{\bar\partial} \cdots \xrightarrow{\bar\partial} A^{0,3}(T_X)[-1]
\to 0$ with leading term $\ell_2 = R^i_{j k \bar \ell}$. The
$L_\infty$-Jacobi at arity $3$ is a cochain-level identity whose
cohomology class is the Bianchi identity; Kapranov 1999 \S 2.6 shows
the chain-level realisation is forced up to $L_\infty$-homotopy.
Yu 2015 \S 3 gives the analogous Christoffel formula for the higher
$\ell_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Recursive solution for $\ell_n$, $n \geq 4$]
\label{prop:cycainf-ell-n-recursion}
The Kapranov $L_\infty$-structure $\{\ell_n\}_{n \geq 2}$ on $\mathfrak
g_X$ is uniquely determined (up to $L_\infty$-homotopy) by the recursive
solution of the Maurer--Cartan equation
\[
 \bar\partial \Theta + \frac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] +
 \frac{1}{3!}[\Theta, \Theta, \Theta] + \cdots = 0
\]
with leading term $\ell_2 = \At(T_X)$. At each arity $n \geq 3$, the
cohomology obstruction lies in a determined subspace of $\HH^n(X)$
and vanishes by successive use of the Bianchi identity (arity $3$) and
the holomorphic Todd identity (arity $n \geq 4$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Kapranov 1999 \S 2.6 proves the recursion closes; Yu 2015 Theorem~1.1
gives the closed-form covariant-derivative expression for the weights
$w_T \in \Q$ of the iterated Atiyah tensor.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Verification of the three cocycles at the landscape archetypes}
\label{subsec:cycainf-three-cocycles-archetypes}

\begin{example}[Quintic threefold $Q \subset \P^4$]
\label{ex:cycainf-quintic-three-cocycles}
$\dim H^1(Q, T_Q) = 101$. The Atiyah class $\At(T_Q) \in H^1(Q, \Om^1_Q
\otimes \End T_Q)$ is nonzero (computed directly from the Fermat
Christoffel symbols at a generic complex structure). The tree-level
Yukawa cubic form is
\[
 Y_3^Q(H, H, H) \;=\; 5 + \sum_{n \geq 1} N_n^Q \, q^n \;=\;
 5 + 2875\, q + 4876875\, q^2 + \cdots,
\]
with $5 = \int_Q H^3$ the classical intersection and $N_n^Q$ the
Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--Parkes 1991 rational Gromov--Witten
invariants. The BCOV curving $\alpha_{\BCOV}(Q)$ sits in $H^{0,1}(Q) = 0$
(since $h^{0,1}(Q) = 0$ by Lefschetz on a smooth complete
intersection), so $\alpha_{\BCOV}(Q) = 0$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-alphabcov-scope}(1). The
Euler-characteristic coefficient
$\chi(Q)/24 = -200/24 = -25/3$ does not promote to a class in
$H^{0,1}(Q) = 0$; it instead appears as the coefficient of the
next-to-leading genus-one free energy $F_1^{\BCOV}(Q)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[$K3 \times E$]
\label{ex:cycainf-k3e-three-cocycles}
$\dim H^1(K3 \times E, T_{K3 \times E}) = \dim H^1(K3, T_{K3}) +
\dim H^1(E, T_E) = 20 + 1 = 21$, by K\"unneth. The Atiyah class
$\At(T_{K3 \times E})$ splits via the K\"unneth decomposition into
$\At(T_{K3}) \boxplus \At(T_E)$; the elliptic factor's Atiyah class
is zero (flat tangent bundle on $E$), so $\At(T_{K3 \times E}) =
\At(T_{K3}) \boxtimes \id_{T_E}$. The tree-level Yukawa evaluates as
\[
 Y_3^{K3 \times E}(v_{K3} \otimes v_E, w_{K3} \otimes w_E,
 u_{K3} \otimes u_E) \;=\; \bigl\langle v_{K3}, w_{K3}
 \bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}} \cdot \int_E u_E,
\]
through the K\"unneth product of the K3 Mukai pairing and the elliptic
integration. This is generically nonzero. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-alphabcov-scope}(3),
$\alpha_{\BCOV}(K3 \times E) = 0$, because $\chi(K3 \times E) =
24 \cdot 0 = 0$ trivialises the scalar coefficient even though
$H^{0,1}(K3 \times E) = \C$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Resolved conifold]
\label{ex:cycainf-conifold-three-cocycles}
On the non-compact toric resolved conifold $\mathcal O(-1) \oplus
\mathcal O(-1) \to \P^1$, the Atiyah class and higher Kapranov brackets
are computed with compact support. The tree-level Yukawa contracts via
the Schwinger--Dyson triple cup to
\[
 Y_3^{\mathrm{cf}} \;=\; \mathrm{Li}_3(q) \;=\; \sum_{n \geq 1}
 \frac{q^n}{n^3},
\]
where $q$ is the K\"ahler parameter; each genus-zero Gopakumar--Vafa
invariant on the conifold equals $1$. The one-loop BCOV coefficient
vanishes on the compact-support reduced class; the effective
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{conifold}) = 1$ by direct McKay
(cf.\ Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-d-kappa-stratification},
Theorem~\ref{thm:kappa-stratification-by-d}, conifold row).
\end{example}

\subsection{Feed into Stage~1 \texorpdfstring{$\PhiFA_3$}{PhiFA3} of the two-stage factorisation}
\label{subsec:cycainf-stage-1-phifa3-feed}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional three-cocycle chain-level witness for Stage~1 $\PhiFA_3$]
\label{thm:cycainf-stage-1-three-cocycle-witness}
Fix the Stage-$1$ $E_3$ formality point and the Costello--Li
holomorphic witness. This theorem identifies the
chain-level inputs read by any constructed $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$; it does not
assert morphism-level functoriality.
Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau threefold and $\cC = D^b(\Coh(X))$
the associated cyclic $\Ainf$-category of dimension $d = 3$ (with
Serre pairing of degree $-3$). Stage~1 of the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_3 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C} \circ \PhiFA_3$ takes as input the
curved $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathfrak g_X = A^{0,\bullet}(X, T_X[-1])$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-kapranov-linf}, with the three
Atiyah cocycles of Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles}
feeding three distinct pieces of the $E_3$-holomorphic factorisation
algebra $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ell_2 = \At(T_X)$ contributes the $\hbar^1$ Hochschild
differential piece $d_{\mathrm{assoc, Hoch}}$ via the Calaque--
C\u ald\u araru--Tu twisted HKR; this is the obstruction to HKR being
a strict Gerstenhaber morphism.
\item $Y_3 = \Om_X^* \ell_3^{\min}$ contributes the $\hbar^2$
Hochschild differential piece as the cubic classical correction to the
$\hCS$ action: $\int_X \Om_X \wedge Y_3 \wedge \langle \cA, \cA, \cA
\rangle$.
\item $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ contributes the $\hbar^1$-loop BV anomaly term
$\int_X \Om_X \wedge \alpha_{\BCOV} \wedge \langle \cA, \cA \rangle$;
this is zero at $X = K3 \times E$ and at every simply-connected
$\CY_3$.
\end{enumerate}
When the Stage-$1$ formality and holomorphic witnesses have been fixed,
the chain-level data $(\ell_2, Y_3,\alpha_{\BCOV})$ determine the
corresponding perturbative components of the constructed object
$\PhiFA_3(\cC)_F$. Varying the formality point changes the construction
by the usual $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor; no canonical equality between
different choices is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Costello--Li 2016 identify the $\hCS$ BV action with the classical
functional of $\mathfrak g_X$; the quadratic, cubic, and one-loop
pieces of the BV action correspond to the three Kapranov brackets
$\ell_2, \ell_3^{\min}, \ell_1^{\text{loop}}$ respectively. The
Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality theorem for $E_3$-algebras on $\HH^\bullet
(\cC)$ identifies the chain-level $L_\infty$-structure with the
$E_3$-algebra on the Hochschild cochain complex, modulo
$\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$. The specialisation to the Kontsevich associator
selects the BM propagator of
Proposition~\textup{\ref{prop:cy3-c3-bridge-core}}. The three
Atiyah cocycles appear in three different pieces of the
resulting factorisation algebra: the binary classical bracket, the
cubic classical coupling, and the one-loop BV anomaly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three perturbative orders, not one]
\label{rem:cycainf-three-orders}
Three distinct ``orders'' organise the Kapranov--$\hCS$ correspondence
and are distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Atiyah-tower arity}: $\ell_2, \ell_3^{\min}, \ell_4^{\min},
\ldots$, indexing the Kapranov $L_\infty$-brackets by the arity of
input.
\item \emph{Classical action order}: $\ell_2$ enters the quadratic-plus-
cubic $\hCS$ action, $Y_3$ enters as the cubic classical correction
(non-flat tangent bundle), and one-loop BV corrections $\alpha_{\BCOV}$
enter at the first quantum loop.
\item \emph{Hochschild-$\hbar$ order}: $\ell_2$ contributes at order
$\hbar^1$, $Y_3$ at order $\hbar^2$, $\alpha_{\BCOV}$ at $\hbar^1$-loop,
in the BV-quantised Hochschild differential.
\end{enumerate}
The three orderings are distinct; a cocycle appearing at order $k$ in
one ordering may appear at order $k' \neq k$ in another. Statements
about the three cocycles must name which ordering is in use.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The cyclic $\Ainf$-input absorbs the three cocycles through $\Phi_3$]
\label{rem:cycainf-absorbs-three-cocycles}
The cyclic $\Ainf$-structure $(\cC, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:cyclic-ainf-d} at $d = 3$ is the universal
chain-level input to $\Phi_3 = \SpCh_{\Sigma_2, C} \circ \PhiFA_3$.
Stage~1 $\PhiFA_3$ reads from $\cC$ the Kapranov $L_\infty$-structure
on $T_X[-1]$ through the HKR identification of
$\HH^\bullet(\cC) \simeq \bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X, \wedge^p T_X)$; the
three Atiyah cocycles of
Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} enter as the three
perturbative ingredients of Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-stage-1-three-cocycle-witness}.
The cyclic pairing $\langle-,-\rangle$ of degree $-3$ couples to
$\Om_X$ through the CY trivialisation: $\Om_X: \wedge^3 T_X
\simeq \cO_X$ identifies the minimal Kapranov ternary bracket $\ell_3^{\min}$
with the tree-level Yukawa $Y_3$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-y3-equals-omega-trace-ell3-min}.
The cyclic-$\Ainf$-input is therefore the
entire chain-level package $(\cC, \mu_n, \langle-,-\rangle, \{\ell_n\})$
whose Stage~1 output records the three Atiyah cocycles.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Finite cyclic potential on a DWR/Ran simplex]
\label{prop:cycainf-finite-cyclic-potential-rees}
Fix bounds $(N,r,L,m)$ for holomorphic jets, renormalisation weight,
charge, and Ran arity. Let
$\sigma \in \mathsf N_{\leq m}^{\mathrm{Ran}}(\mathfrak U;\Gamma_{\leq L})$
be a $p$-simplex of a DWR-good cover, and suppose that the finite hCS
quotient $C_\sigma$ admits a $G_\sigma$-equivariant face-compatible
cyclic contraction
\[
 \left(
 H_\sigma
 \underset{p_\sigma}{\stackrel{i_\sigma}{\rightleftarrows}}
 C_\sigma\otimes\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p),
 \ h_\sigma
 \right)
\]
with finite-dimensional $H_\sigma$, non-degenerate cyclic pairing
$\langle-,-\rangle_\sigma$, and transferred cyclic operations
$\nu_{n,\sigma}$ for $1\leq n\leq m$. Write
$\mathfrak g_\sigma=\mathrm{Lie}(G_\sigma)$, and let
$\mathrm{vol}_\sigma$ be the algebraic volume form fixed by the finite
BV polarisation. Put
$V_\sigma=H_\sigma[1]$. The shifted pairing gives a constant
symplectic form $\varpi_\sigma$ on $V_\sigma$, and the cyclic operations
define
\[
 W_{\sigma,n+1}(sx_0,\ldots,sx_n)
 =
 \frac{1}{n+1}(-1)^{\diamond}
 \langle x_0,\nu_{n,\sigma}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\rangle_\sigma,
 \qquad
 1\leq n\leq m,
\]
where $\diamond$ is the suspension Koszul sign. Then
\[
 W_\sigma:=\sum_{1\leq n\leq m}W_{\sigma,n+1}
 \in
 \mathrm{Cyc}^{\leq m}(V_\sigma^\vee)\otimes
 \Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p),
 \qquad
 \mathrm{Cyc}^{\leq m}(V_\sigma^\vee)
 :=
 \prod_{2\leq q\leq m+1}
 \bigl((V_\sigma^\vee)^{\otimes q}\bigr)_{C_q},
\]
is a finite cyclic potential. It satisfies the finite master equation
\[
 d_{\Delta^p}W_\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\{W_\sigma,W_\sigma\}_{\varpi_\sigma}=0
\]
in the quotient by words exceeding the bounds $(N,r,L,m)$. Consequently
\[
 \left(
 C^\bullet\!\left(
 \mathfrak g_\sigma,
 \Gamma(V_\sigma,\wedge^\bullet T_{V_\sigma})[[\hbar]]
 \otimes\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p)
 \right),
 d_{\CE}+\iota_{d_VW_\sigma}
 +\hbar\Delta_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}+d_{\Delta^p}
 \right)
\]
is a finite Rees critical complex. Under the Fourier--BV transform
$\xi\mapsto(-1)^{|\xi|(|\xi|-1)/2}\iota_\xi\mathrm{vol}_\sigma$, it is
identified with the twisted Rees de Rham complex
\[
 \left(
 \Omega^\bullet(V_\sigma)[[\hbar]]\otimes\Omega^\bullet(\Delta^p),
 \hbar d_V+d_VW_\sigma\wedge+d_{\Delta^p}
 \right).
\]

Let $L_\sigma$ be the cyclic $L_\infty$ algebra encoded by
$W_\sigma$, and let
$A_\sigma=\operatorname{Env}^{\mathrm{fact}}(L_\sigma)$. For every
costalk $x$ on the reference Ran curve there is a filtered
quasi-isomorphism
\[
 B(A_\sigma)_x\simeq \CE(L_{\sigma,x})
\]
compatible with the finite Rees filtration and with restriction to
faces of $\Delta^p$. If $\sigma=\sigma_1\sqcup\sigma_2$ is a disjoint
Ran union and the contractions are block-sum contractions, then
\[
 H_\sigma=H_{\sigma_1}\oplus H_{\sigma_2},\qquad
 W_\sigma=W_{\sigma_1}\boxplus W_{\sigma_2},
\]
and the Rees complexes satisfy the strict Thom--Sebastiani identity
\[
 \mathrm{DR}_{W_{\sigma_1},\hbar}\widehat{\otimes}
 \mathrm{DR}_{W_{\sigma_2},\hbar}
 \cong
 \mathrm{DR}_{W_{\sigma_1}\boxplus W_{\sigma_2},\hbar}.
\]
Here $\mathrm{DR}_{W,\hbar}$ denotes the displayed twisted Rees de Rham
complex.
The bar--CE comparison is monoidal for the same disjoint union.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Cyclic homological perturbation transfers the hCS differential and
brackets along $(i_\sigma,p_\sigma,h_\sigma)$. The tree formula for the
transferred operations is finite in the quotient $(N,r,L,m)$, because
only words of cyclic length at most $m+1$, retained charge, retained
jet order, and retained renormalisation weight survive. The side
conditions on the contraction make the transferred pairing cyclic, so
the multilinear forms $W_{\sigma,n+1}$ are $C_{n+1}$-coinvariants.

On the completed tensor coalgebra $T^c(V_\sigma)$ the transferred
operations are a coderivation $b_\sigma$. The Stasheff identities are
$b_\sigma^2=0$. A non-degenerate cyclic pairing identifies cyclic
coderivations of $T^c(V_\sigma)$ with Hamiltonian vector fields for the
non-commutative symplectic bracket
$\{-,-\}_{\varpi_\sigma}$ on cyclic words. Under this identification
$b_\sigma=X_{W_\sigma}$, hence
\[
 0=b_\sigma^2=\frac{1}{2}[X_{W_\sigma},X_{W_\sigma}]
 =X_{\frac{1}{2}\{W_\sigma,W_\sigma\}_{\varpi_\sigma}}.
\]
The relative simplex term adds $d_{\Delta^p}W_\sigma$; face
compatibility of the contraction is exactly the statement that the
relative Hamiltonian satisfies
$d_{\Delta^p}W_\sigma+\frac12\{W_\sigma,W_\sigma\}_{\varpi_\sigma}=0$
in the finite quotient.

The Fourier--BV identity is the coordinate calculation
\[
 \mathfrak F_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}(\iota_{d_VW_\sigma}\xi)
 =
 d_VW_\sigma\wedge\mathfrak F_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}(\xi),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak F_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}(\Delta_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}\xi)
 =
 d_V\mathfrak F_{\mathrm{vol}_\sigma}(\xi),
\]
with $d_{\CE}$ and $d_{\Delta^p}$ commuting with the transform. Thus
the polyvector Rees differential is carried to
$\hbar d_V+d_VW_\sigma\wedge+d_{\Delta^p}$.

The factorisation envelope of $L_\sigma$ has costalk bar coalgebra
whose differential is the sum of the internal differential and the
deconcatenation terms dual to the brackets of $L_{\sigma,x}$. This is
the Chevalley--Eilenberg differential of $L_{\sigma,x}$, giving
$B(A_\sigma)_x\simeq\CE(L_{\sigma,x})$. The finite Rees functor is
exact on the retained finite filtration, so the quasi-isomorphism
survives the bounds.

For disjoint Ran configurations the block-sum contraction has no mixed
trees. The transferred operations split, the symplectic form is the
direct sum, and the Hamiltonian is the external sum
$W_{\sigma_1}\boxplus W_{\sigma_2}$. The de Rham differential and
wedge by $dW$ split on the product, giving the displayed
Thom--Sebastiani identity. Since the bar coproduct and the
Chevalley--Eilenberg coproduct are both defined by the same
deconcatenation on disjoint factors, the bar--CE map is monoidal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{All-loops Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality on \texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E} and the precise two-loop obstruction on the quintic}
\label{subsec:cycainf-all-loops-k3e-quintic}

Stage~1 $\PhiFA_3$ on a compact Calabi--Yau threefold is not obtained
from the rational Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality theorem by transport
alone. The local $E_3$ formality point lives over $\R^3$; the compact
holomorphic theory adds Costello--Li heat-kernel regularisation,
DWR descent, and loop-order anomaly classes. Costello--Li gives the
one-loop obstruction class. Beyond that order the truthful compact
statement is a primitive criterion: each renormalised BV obstruction
cocycle must be killed by an explicit counterterm/nullhomotopy
compatible with descent and factorisation. The leading symbols of
these obstruction cocycles are built from the iterated Atiyah tower.

\begin{definition}[Compact iterated-Atiyah nullhomotopy hypothesis \texorpdfstring{$\widetilde{\mathrm H}_*$}{H-tilde-star}]
\label{def:cycainf-h-tilde-star}
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler Calabi--Yau threefold. For each rooted
binary tree $T$ of arity $n \geq 2$, let $\ell_T^X : \mathrm{Sym}^n
H^1(X, T_X) \to H^{0,1}(X, T_X^{\otimes n})$ denote the iterated-Atiyah
contraction map of Kapranov's $L_\infty$-structure
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-kapranov-linf}), realised by the composition
of $\At(T_X)$ along the edges of $T$ followed by $\bar\partial$-cohomology.
Write $\mathrm{Im}(T)_X := \mathrm{Im}(\ell_T^X) \subseteq H^{0,1}(X,
T_X^{\otimes n})$. The hypothesis
\[
 \widetilde{\mathrm H}_*(X)
 \;:\;
 \text{for every $T$, the projected obstruction has a primitive}
\]
means the following chain-level datum: after projection from the
iterated-Atiyah leading symbol to the completed renormalised BV
deformation complex, a cochain $h_T$ is chosen with
$d_{\BV} h_T=o_T$. Vanishing of the Dolbeault cohomology image
$\mathrm{Im}(T)_X$ is a sufficient special case; it is not the definition
of the compact witness, because a non-zero leading tensor may still be
absorbed by a local BV counterterm in the larger deformation complex.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Tensor-rank stratification of the \texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l}-loop anomaly]
\label{thm:cycainf-ell-loop-stratification}
Fix the Costello--Gwilliam renormalised graph expansion at loop order
$\ell$ and the compact heat-kernel regularisation. The theorem
identifies the obstruction target; it does not assert vanishing.
Let $X$ be a compact K\"ahler Calabi--Yau threefold and $\mathfrak g$
a reductive Lie algebra with non-degenerate invariant pairing. The
$\ell$-loop wheel-graph anomaly of the holomorphic Chern--Simons BV
action on $(X, \mathfrak g, \Om_X)$ decomposes as
\[
 \alpha^{(\ell)}(X, \mathfrak g)
 \;=\;
 \sum_{W_\ell} \frac{a_{W_\ell}(\mathfrak g)}{\nu(W_\ell)}
 \cdot \left(\int_X \mathcal P_{W_\ell}(c(T_X))\right)
 \cdot [\Om_X]^{0,1}_{W_\ell},
\]
where $W_\ell$ runs over connected $\ell$-loop cubic graphs,
$a_{W_\ell}(\mathfrak g) \in \Q$ is a Lie-algebraic weight,
$\nu(W_\ell) \in \N$ the symmetry factor, $\mathcal P_{W_\ell}(c(T_X))$
a Chern-class polynomial of total degree $6$ in $c_1, c_2, c_3$ with
$c_1 = 0$ by CY, and
\[
 [\Om_X]^{0,1}_{W_\ell} \;\in\; H^{0,1}\bigl(X, T_X^{\otimes k(W_\ell)}
 \otimes \mathrm{Sym}^{j(W_\ell)} T_X^\vee\bigr),
 \qquad k(W_\ell) + j(W_\ell) \;\leq\; 2\ell - 2.
\]
At $\ell = 1$ the unique graph is the circle and the stratification
reproduces $\alpha_{\BCOV} = (\chi(X)/24) [\Om_X]^{0,1}$ in
$H^{0,1}(X, \cO_X)$; at $\ell \geq 2$ the stratification activates
the $k + j \geq 1$ components in $H^{0,1}(X, T_X^{\otimes k} \otimes
\mathrm{Sym}^j T_X^\vee)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph sum over connected $\ell$-loop cubic graphs is the
Costello--Gwilliam Feynman expansion of the BV action at loop
order $\ell$. Costello--Gwilliam 2017 Volume~2 Chapter~10 shows
the integrand on the Fulton--MacPherson compactification
$\overline{F}(|V(W_\ell)|, X)^{\mathrm{FM}}$ extends smoothly to
the boundary divisor, and evaluation on the deepest diagonal reduces
to a Chern-class polynomial via Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch on the
normal bundle to the diagonal. The $(k, j)$-tensor rank at the vertex
where the anomaly $[\Om_X]^{0,1}_{W_\ell}$ is computed is
read off the combinatorics of the graph: each uncontracted $T_X$-leg
contributes to $k$, each uncontracted $T_X^\vee$-leg to $j$, and
Kontsevich's graph-degree formula gives $k + j \leq 2\ell - 2$.
The one-loop specialisation recovers Costello--Li 2016 Proposition~5.2.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compact higher-loop primitive criterion]
\label{prop:cycainf-compact-higher-loop-primitive-criterion}
This is a formal obstruction criterion. The compact primitives remain
additional analytic data.
Fix a compact K\"ahler Calabi--Yau threefold $X$, a metric Lie algebra
$\mathfrak g$, a Stage-$1$ formality point $F$, and a Costello--Li
regularisation scheme. Let
\[
 o_\ell(X,\mathfrak g,F)
 \in
 Z^1\!\left(\mathfrak g_{\BV}^{(\ell)}(X,\mathfrak g,F)\right),
 \qquad \ell\geq 1,
\]
be the renormalised $\ell$-loop obstruction cocycle in the completed
local BV deformation complex controlling the quantum master equation,
descent, and factorisation products. A compact Stage-$1$
$\PhiFA_3(D^b(\Coh(X)))_F$ exists through loop order $L$ if and only if
for every $1\leq \ell\leq L$ there are compatible primitives
\[
 h_\ell\in \mathfrak g_{\BV}^{(\ell)}(X,\mathfrak g,F)^0,
 \qquad
 d_{\BV}h_\ell=o_\ell,
\]
whose restrictions agree on the DWR \v{C}ech nerve and whose
renormalisation-group transports preserve the factorisation product.
At $\ell=1$, Costello--Li identifies $o_1$ with the BCOV curving
$\alpha_{\BCOV}$. For $\ell\geq2$, the associated graded of $o_\ell$
lies in the tensor-rank receptacle of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-ell-loop-stratification}; killing its
iterated-Atiyah leading symbol is necessary unless the remaining graph
terms cancel it inside the same completed BV complex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Costello--Gwilliam construction is an induction on loop order in a
complete filtered dg Lie algebra of local functionals. At order
$\ell$, all lower counterterms have already been chosen, and the
failure of the quantum master equation, descent, or factorisation
compatibility is a closed degree-$1$ element $o_\ell$. Adding an
$\ell$-loop counterterm $h_\ell$ changes the obstruction by
$d_{\BV}h_\ell$. Thus the order-$\ell$ step closes exactly when the
class $[o_\ell]$ vanishes and a primitive has been chosen. The DWR and
RG compatibility requirements state that the chosen primitives define a
single factorisation algebra rather than unrelated chartwise repairs.
The one-loop identification is Costello--Li's anomaly calculation; the
higher-loop leading-symbol statement is precisely the tensor-rank
stratification above.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional all-loops Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality on $K3 \times E$]
\label{thm:cycainf-all-loops-k3e}
Assume a compact chain-level proof that the higher iterated-Atiyah
images vanish or are null-homotopic in
renormalised BV cohomology. The K\"unneth and Euler-characteristic
computations below prove the one-loop cancellation and identify the
possible higher-loop targets; they do not prove all-loop KT formality by
themselves.
Let $X = K3 \times E$ be the compact K\"ahler Calabi--Yau threefold
formed as the product of a smooth projective K3 surface with an
elliptic curve, carrying the volume form $\Om_X = \Om_{K3} \wedge
\Om_E$. If the compact higher-loop hypothesis
$\widetilde{\mathrm H}_*(K3 \times E)$ is supplied by an explicit
primitive for every projected iterated-Atiyah obstruction cocycle of
arity $\geq 2$ in the completed renormalised BV complex, then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item For every reductive Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with non-degenerate
invariant pairing, the Costello--Li heat-kernel BV regularisation of
the holomorphic Chern--Simons action on $(K3 \times E, \mathfrak g,
\Om_{K3 \times E})$ solves the quantum master equation at every order
in $\hbar$.
\item The Stage~1 factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_3(D^b(\Coh(K3 \times
E)))$ is rigorously defined at all loops as an object of
$\mathcal E_3^{\mathrm{hol}}\text{-}\mathrm{FA}(K3 \times E)$ with the
chosen formality point and chosen compact primitives. Varying the
formality point changes the object by the usual $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$
torsor action.
\item The Kontsevich--Tamarkin $E_3$-formality zig-zag extends from
$\R^3$ to $K3 \times E$ at every loop order, producing an all-orders
$E_3$-hFA on $K3 \times E$ whose cohomology is the Gerstenhaber
operad of shift $-2$ acting on $\HH^\bullet(D^b(\Coh(K3 \times E)))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The tangent bundle splits by K\"unneth as $T_{K3 \times E} =
\pi_1^* T_{K3} \oplus \pi_2^* T_E$; triviality of $T_E$ forces
$\At(T_{K3 \times E}) = \pi_1^* \At(T_{K3})$. This proves that all
higher iterated-Atiyah targets are pulled back from the K3 factor and
that the elliptic factor contributes no new Atiyah class. It does not
prove their vanishing: the K3 contribution has
$\chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$, so the clean cancellation available from the
elliptic Euler characteristic at one loop does not automatically
extend to every higher graph.

The one-loop anomaly vanishes because
$\chi(K3 \times E) = \chi(K3)\chi(E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$, hence
Costello--Li's one-loop class
$(\chi(X)/24)[\Om_X]^{0,1}$ is zero. For $\ell \geq 2$ the
stratification theorem places the possible obstructions in the completed
BV complex with associated graded controlled by the iterated-Atiyah
images of Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-h-tilde-star}. If the stated
compact nullhomotopies are supplied, Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-compact-higher-loop-primitive-criterion}
applies at every loop order, so the Costello--Gwilliam induction
constructs the all-orders solution of the quantum master equation.
Claims~(1)--(3) are then formal consequences of the supplied primitives,
the selected $E_3$ formality point, and the holomorphic twist.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$\hbar$}{hbar}-exactness of \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$}{H-Delta-5} under compact nullhomotopies]
\label{cor:cycainf-h-delta5-exact}
Assume the compact all-loop $K3 \times E$ null-homotopy hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-all-loops-k3e} and the finite Hall--Borcherds
source-recognition gates. The Stage~2 specialisation
\[
A_{K3,E}:=\SpCh_{\Sigma_2, E}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(D^b(\Coh(K3\times E)))\bigr)
\]
is an $\hbar$-exact candidate over the completed Rees parameter; after
compact Hall promotion, PBW/no-extra-relations, parity, completion, and
Heegner characteristic comparison it is identified with the recognised
K3 chiral bialgebra $\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}$ on the reference elliptic
curve $E$. Its five
$\kappa_\bullet$-construction values $(\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Hodge}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}) = (0, 0, 3, 5, 24)$ are $\hbar$-exact values,
computed from the Hodge, Mukai, and Borcherds data rather than from the
choice of compact primitive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-all-loops-k3e}(2) and the
factorisation-homology construction of Stage~2 (Costello--Gwilliam
2017 Volume~2 Section~11) to the candidate $A_{K3,E}$. The
Hall--Borcherds gates identify this candidate with the compact source;
without them one has only the Stage-$2$ target. The four numerical
invariants are fixed by their independent derivations:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}$ by
$\chi(\cO_{K3\times E})$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}$ by the
Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ by the
Borcherds weight $c_1(0)/2$, and $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ by the Mukai
lattice rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Correct target for the two-loop Kontsevich--Tamarkin obstruction on the quintic]
\label{thm:cycainf-quintic-two-loop-obstruction}
The two-loop obstruction problem is reduced to an explicit BV graph
projection in $H^1(Q,T_Q)$ and a non-absorbability calculation. The
former coefficient $(5/24)[\omega_Q]$ is not asserted: it used the
invalid threefold integral $\int_Q c_2(T_Q)^2$ and placed the class in
the wrong Hodge target.
Let $Q \subset \P^4$ be a smooth quintic Calabi--Yau threefold, and
let $\mathfrak g$ be a simply-laced reductive Lie algebra with
symmetric cubic Casimir $d^{abc}_{\mathfrak g}$. The two-loop
wheel-graph anomaly of the holomorphic Chern--Simons BV action on
$(Q, \mathfrak g, \Om_Q)$, if non-zero, has target
\[
 \alpha^{(2)}(Q, \mathfrak g)
 \;\in\;
 H^1(Q,T_Q) \otimes \mathrm{Sym}^3 \mathfrak g^\vee \otimes
 \mathrm{Sym}^3 \mathfrak g^\vee,
\]
and, by contraction with $\Om_Q$, $H^1(Q,T_Q) \cong H^{2,1}(Q)$ has
dimension $101$. It is not a class in the K\"ahler line
$H^{1,1}(Q)$. The valid ambient Chern data are
\[
 c(T_Q)=\frac{(1+H)^5}{1+5H}
 = 1 + 10H^2 - 40H^3,\qquad
 \int_Q H^3=5,
\]
so
\[
 c_2(T_Q)=10H^2,\qquad
 \int_Q c_2(T_Q)\wedge H=50,\qquad
 \int_Q c_3(T_Q)=-200.
\]
The expression $\int_Q c_2(T_Q)^2$ has cohomological degree $8$ and
is not defined on the threefold. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item For $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, the cubic symmetric tensor
vanishes in the standard normalisation, so the displayed cubic-weight
component of the two-loop anomaly vanishes.
\item For $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak{sl}_N$ with $N \geq 3$, the
cubic weight need not vanish, but proved non-vanishing of
$\alpha^{(2)}(Q,\mathfrak g)$ still requires the explicit spectacles
graph projection to a non-zero class in $H^1(Q,T_Q)$ and a proof that
no local BV counter-term kills that class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two-loop spectacles graph $W_{\mathrm{Spec}_2}$ contributes
the dominant term at $\ell = 2$. Its Lie-algebraic weight is
$a_{W_{\mathrm{Spec}_2}}(\mathfrak g) = d^{abc}_{\mathfrak g}
d^{abc}_{\mathfrak g}$ (Costello 2011 \emph{Renormalization and
Effective Field Theory} AMS Lecture Series 57 Theorem~5.6.1). The
geometric target is fixed by Hodge theory. Since $Q$ is a Calabi--Yau
threefold, contraction with $\Om_Q$ gives
$T_Q \simeq \Omega_Q^2$, hence
$H^1(Q,T_Q) \simeq H^1(Q,\Omega_Q^2)=H^{2,1}(Q)$; for the quintic this
space has dimension $101$. The K\"ahler class lies instead in
$H^{1,1}(Q)$, so it cannot be the target of this Kodaira--Spencer
component.

The Chern calculation is the standard adjunction calculation:
$c(T_Q)=(1+H)^5/(1+5H)$ in the quotient ring of $Q$, whence
$c_2(T_Q)=10H^2$ and $c_3(T_Q)=-40H^3$. Because $\dim_{\C}Q=3$,
only degree-$6$ classes integrate. Thus $\int_Q c_2(T_Q)\wedge H=50$
and $\int_Q c_3(T_Q)=-200$ are meaningful, while
$\int_Q c_2(T_Q)^2$ is degree $8$ and meaningless on $Q$. The
previous scalar coefficient therefore cannot support a theorem.

The remaining obstruction problem is explicit and finite: compute the
renormalised two-loop graph projection to $H^1(Q,T_Q)$ under the
chosen BV normalisation, then compare it with the image of local
counter-terms in the same cohomology group. Until those two maps are
written down, the quintic two-loop statement is a conditional
obstruction statement, not a proved non-vanishing theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[All-loops dichotomy of the compact $\CY_3$ landscape]
\label{rem:cycainf-all-loops-dichotomy}
The compact K\"ahler Calabi--Yau threefolds are separated by the
actual higher-loop BV obstruction classes, not by the ill-defined
threefold number $\int_X c_2(T_X)^2$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item $K3 \times E$, the $6$-torus $T^6$, abelian-product
threefolds $A_2 \times E$, and their Fourier--Mukai partners and
crepant birational equivalents have vanishing one-loop Euler anomaly
when $\chi(X)=0$. All-loop KT formality additionally requires the
compact higher-loop null-homotopies named in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-all-loops-k3e}.
\item The quintic $Q$, the bicubic, and every complete-intersection
Calabi--Yau threefold with non-trivial $H^1(T_X)$ has a possible
Kodaira--Spencer higher-loop target. For the quintic, the valid
ambient numbers are $\int_Q c_2(T_Q)\wedge H=50$ and
$\int_Q c_3(T_Q)=-200$; they signal non-trivial geometry but do not
replace the required two-loop BV graph projection.
\end{enumerate}
Thus Class~(A) is the one-loop-cancelled compact locus whose all-loop
formality requires higher-loop nullhomotopies; Class~(B) is the locus
where the two-loop projection is expected to produce a genuine
Kodaira--Spencer obstruction. A Green--Schwarz-type cancellation or
other extra field content would be additional structure, not a
consequence of the cyclic $\Ainf$ data alone.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Three lenses on all-loops formality]
\label{rem:cycainf-three-lenses}
The all-loops KT formality problem on a compact K\"ahler $\CY_3$ $X$
has three parallel formulations. Establishing equivalence between
them is part of the compact chain-level witness:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item (Analytic/BV) The Costello--Li heat-kernel BV action of
$\hCS$ on $(X, \mathfrak g, \Om_X)$ solves the QME at all orders
in $\hbar$.
\item (Algebro-geometric) The Kapranov iterated-Atiyah obstruction
cocycles admit primitives in the target Dolbeault/BV deformation
complex. Raw brackets such as the Yukawa cubic need not vanish.
\item (Derived-categorical) The Hochschild higher-operation obstruction
classes become exact in the cyclic/negative-cyclic deformation complex.
The underlying Massey products may still be non-zero as chain-level
operations.
\end{enumerate}
The three formulations are cohomological, algebraic, and analytic
interfaces to the same chain-level problem. Equivalence between them is
not automatic; it is precisely the compact witness demanded by
Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-compact-higher-loop-primitive-criterion}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Fourier--Mukai naturality of the Kapranov structure]
\label{rem:cycainf-fm-naturality}
For $X \dashrightarrow X'$ a crepant birational equivalence of
$\CY_3$s (e.g.\ a flop) with Fourier--Mukai kernel $\mathcal K \in
D^b(\Coh(X \times X'))$ inducing an equivalence $\Phi_{\mathcal K}:
D^b(\Coh(X)) \simeq D^b(\Coh(X'))$, the two Kapranov $L_\infty$-
structures are intertwined:
\[
 \Phi_{\mathcal K} \circ \ell_n^{X} \;=\; \ell_n^{X'} \circ
 \Phi_{\mathcal K}^{\otimes n}
\]
for every $n \geq 2$. In particular, the tree-level Yukawa $Y_3$ is
preserved under flops (Li--Ruan 2001; Li 2013), and Stage~1
$\PhiFA_3$ is flop-invariant. Bridgeland 2002 Invent.\ Math.\ 147 gives
the flop equivalence; Maulik--Oblomkov 2014 gives DT flop-invariance.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Kapranov $L_\infty$-structure and the HKR-extended formality]
\label{rem:cycainf-kapranov-hkr}
The Kapranov $L_\infty$-structure on $T_X[-1]$ identifies with the
Hochschild $L_\infty$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)[-1]$ through an
$L_\infty$-quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \mathrm{HKR}_\infty : A^{0, \bullet}(X, T_X[-1]) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X, \wedge^p T_X)[-1-q] \;=\;
 \HH^\bullet(\cC)[-1],
\]
natural up to a $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-torsor choice. The standard choice
selects Kontsevich's original formality map (Kontsevich 2003 Lett.\
Math.\ Phys.\ 66). Under this identification, the three Atiyah
cocycles of Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} correspond to
three Hochschild-cohomology classes:
$\ell_2 \leftrightarrow \At(T_X) \in \HH^2(\cC)$, $Y_3 \leftrightarrow
[\Om_X \wedge Y_3] \in \HH^3(\cC)^{\mathrm{cyc}}$, and $\alpha_{\BCOV}
\leftrightarrow [\alpha_{\BCOV}] \in \HH^1(\cC)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Lattice pairing: Hodge-disjoint vs BV-dual]
\label{rem:cycainf-hodge-vs-bv}
The three Atiyah cocycles $(\ell_2, Y_3, \alpha_{\BCOV})$ of
Definition~\ref{def:cycainf-three-cocycles} satisfy Hodge-disjointness
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cycainf-hodge-disjoint}) and BV-duality
(Remark~\ref{rem:cycainf-y3-alphabcov-bv-dual}). These are different
statements: Hodge-disjointness is a cohomological independence of the
target groups, while BV-duality is a Poisson pairing at the level of
the $\hCS$ BV observables. Neither implies the other; both are
load-bearing in the chain-level Stage~1 $\PhiFA_3$ construction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cycainf-stage-1-three-cocycle-witness}.
\end{remark}
