\begin{remark}[Stage-2 shadow on the Nakajima cycle]
\label{rem:e2-chiral-stage2-shadow}
$E_2$-chiral on K3 is the Stage-2 specialisation
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\cF_{K3})$ at $(\Sigma_1, C) = (S^1 \subset K3,
\text{Nakajima cycle})$ of the canonical $E_2$-hFA
$\cF_{K3} = \PhiFA_2(\cA_{K3})$, not the native Stage-1 output.
The Nakajima--Mukai braided structure computed in this chapter is the
$E_2$-multiplication of the specialised chiral algebra on the K3
Nakajima cycle.
\end{remark}

The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi_d$ factors through two stages
(Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}, Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}):
the canonical $E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra
$\PhiFA_d(\cC) \in \EdHolFA(X)$
on the CY$_d$ variety, followed by the chiral specialisation
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$ to an $E_n$-chiral algebra on a reference curve $C$.
The braided $\Etwo$ structure is the shadow of this two-stage construction
at $d = 2$: the native output of $\PhiFA_2$ is already $\Etwo$, and
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\PhiFA_2(\cC))$ is an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra on $C$.
The conjectural identity $\Phi_{E_2} \simeq Z^{\mathrm{ch}} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$
\textup{(}Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}; the functors
$\Phi_{E_1}, \Phi_{E_2}$ and the chiral Drinfeld centre $Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$
are defined in \S\textup{\ref{sec:drinfeld-center-bridge}}\textup{)} locates
the $\Etwo$ layer at $d = 3$ not in the native $\PhiFA_3$ output but in the
Drinfeld centre of its specialisation.

The scope of this chapter depends on the CY dimension.
At $d = 2$, the native holomorphic factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_2(\cC)$
carries $\Etwo$ structure (Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}), and
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\PhiFA_2(\cC))$ is an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra
on $C$ \textup{(}Corollary~\ref{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2}\textup{)}: the
$\mathbb{S}^2$-framing supplies $\Etwo$ at stage~1, and the definitions and
results of this chapter apply to the specialisation $A = \Phi_2(\cC)$ itself.
At $d = 3$, the Stage-$1$ output $\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ is an $E_3$-holomorphic
factorisation algebra only after the witnesses of
Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}} are supplied; the Stage-$2$
specialisation $A=\SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ is $\Eone$.
The $\Etwo$-chiral algebra
$\Phi_{E_2}(\cC)$ is the chiral Drinfeld centre of the specialisation, and the $\Etwo$-braided
structure lives on the representation category $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, not
on $A$. The definitions in this chapter (the $\Etwo$-bar complex, the
braided tensor category, the $\Etwo$-Koszul duality) apply to the
Drinfeld centre at $d = 3$, not to the CY$_3$ chiral algebra directly.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} operad and its categorical content}
\label{sec:e2-operad}

The braided refinement of ordered multiplication is operadic: it is
the $E_2$ operad that controls partial commutativity, and every
braided monoidal category is an $E_2$-object (Lurie).

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} operad]
\label{def:e2-operad}
The $E_2$ operad (little $2$-disks) has $E_2(n) =
\mathrm{Conf}_n(\mathbb{D}^2)$. By Dunn additivity $E_2 \simeq E_1
\otimes E_1$: an $E_2$-algebra is an $E_1$-algebra in $E_1$-algebras,
so multiplication is only partially commutative. The braiding measures
the failure of the two ordered products to agree strictly. At the level
of homology the braiding descends to the
commutativity constraint of a Gerstenhaber algebra.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Lurie, {\cite{LurieHA}}]
\label{thm:lurie-e2-braided}
The $(\infty,2)$-category of $E_2$-algebras in a symmetric monoidal
$\infty$-category $\cC$ is equivalent to the $(\infty,2)$-category of
braided monoidal objects in $\cC$.
\end{theorem}

Kontsevich formality $C_*(E_2;\mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim}
\mathrm{Ger}$ \textup{(}the Gerstenhaber operad, whose algebras are
graded-commutative rings with a degree-$(-1)$ Lie bracket satisfying
the Leibniz rule\textup{)} supplies the rational comparison.
For factorization algebras the $E_2$ structure appears fully
holomorphically \textup{(}on $X \times Y$ with holomorphy in each
factor\textup{)} or holomorphic-topologically \textup{(}Volume~II
Swiss-cheese\textup{),} two presentations of Dunn additivity.

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral algebra]
\label{def:e2-chiral-algebra}
An \emph{$E_2$-chiral algebra} on $X \times Y$ is a factorization
algebra $\cA$ on the Ran space $\Ran(X) \times \Ran(Y)$
\textup{(}the moduli of finite subsets of $X$ and of $Y$\textup{)}
that factorizes chirally in each factor and is compatible with the
$E_2$-operad action via the Fulton--MacPherson equivalence
$\FM_n(\mathbb{C}) \simeq E_2(n)$ \textup{(}the compactified
configuration space of $n$ points in $\C$ as a model for the $n$-ary
operations of little $2$-disks\textup{).} Its representation category
$\Rep^{E_2}(\cA)$ is braided monoidal. Standard Beilinson--Drinfeld
chiral algebras lie on the $E_\infty$ side of this hierarchy after
symmetrisation; they still admit OPE poles.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Deligne conjecture]
\label{rem:deligne-hochschild}
The canonical $E_2$-algebra is the Hochschild cochain complex
$\CC^\bullet(A,A) := \bigoplus_n \Hom(A^{\otimes n}, A)$ of an
associative algebra $A$. The Deligne conjecture (Kontsevich--Soibelman
\cite{KontsevichSoibelmanDefI}, Tamarkin \cite{TamarkinDGCat},
McClure--Smith, Berger--Fresse) asserts that $\CC^\bullet(A,A)$ carries
a canonical $E_2$-action with cup product and brace as the two
products, inducing the Gerstenhaber structure on Hochschild cohomology
$\HH^\bullet(A,A) := H^*(\CC^\bullet(A,A))$.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral algebras from cyclic \texorpdfstring{$A_\infty$}{A-inf} data}
\label{sec:e2-from-cyclic-ainf}

Calabi-Yau data does not change the $E_2$ operad. It changes which
trace-compatible lifts of the Deligne action exist, and the CY
dimension controls that extra structure.

\begin{theorem}[Kontsevich-Soibelman; Tamarkin]
\label{thm:ks-tamarkin-cyclic-e2}
Let $A$ be a cyclic $A_\infty$ algebra of CY dimension $d$, with
nondegenerate pairing $\langle-,-\rangle\colon A \otimes A \to k[d]$.
Then $\CC^\bullet(A,A)$ carries a canonical $E_2$-algebra structure
together with a compatible chain-level $S^1$-action whose homotopy
fixed points compute the negative cyclic homology
$\HC^-_\bullet(A) := \mathrm{HC}_\bullet(A)^{hS^1}$ \textup{(}the
$S^1$-equivariant refinement of Hochschild homology\textup{).}
The combined \emph{framed} structure is $fE_2 \simeq E_2 \rtimes S^1$. References:
Kontsevich-Soibelman \cite{KontsevichSoibelmanDefI}; Tamarkin
\cite{TamarkinDGCat}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Deligne's \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} at \texorpdfstring{$n=2$}{n=2} as Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality up to \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{GRT}_1$}{GRT-1}]
\label{rem:deligne-kt-grt1-e2}
The $E_2$-action on $\CC^\bullet(A, A)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-tamarkin-cyclic-e2} is precisely Deligne's conjecture at $n = 2$: McClure--Smith and Kontsevich--Soibelman establish the $E_2$-operad action on Hochschild cochains with cup product and brace as the two primitive operations, and Tamarkin~$1998$ upgrades this to a formal $E_2$-quasi-isomorphism $C_\bullet(E_2; \mathbb{Q}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Ger}$ with choice space a torsor over $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\mathbb{Q})$, the prounipotent Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller group. Different Drinfeld associators $\Phi \in \mathrm{Assoc}(\mathbb{Q})$; equivalently, different points of the $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-torsor; yield $E_2$-quasi-isomorphic but not $E_2$-equal structures on $\CC^\bullet(A, A)$. At $d = 2$, the Mukai-braided chiral output
$\Phi_{E_2}(\cC) = \SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\PhiFA_2(\cC))$
on $K3$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2}) sits inside this torsor: the braiding $\sigma_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ on $\HH^\bullet(D^b(\Coh(K3))) \cong \bigwedge^\bullet H^1(T_{K3})$ is the half-braiding of an $E_2$-algebra structure pinned by a Drinfeld associator; choosing the rational KZ associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$ or the even-Deligne associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{Del}}$ gives quasi-isomorphic Mukai braidings differing by a $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-cocycle. Braiding content (the Mukai lattice pairing $\chi_{\mathrm{Muk}} \colon \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}} \otimes \Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}} \to \Z$, the Kl\"ein $\mathfrak{K}_4$ action on the self-mirror locus) is $\mathrm{GRT}_1$-invariant; the associator choice is a moduli of presentations, not a moduli of outputs.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[CY dimension shift]
\label{rem:cy-dim-shift}
The $E_2$-structure on $\CC^\bullet(A,A)$ is degree-shifted by $2-d$:
at $d=1$ the shift is $+1$ (framed $E_2 \simeq$ BV); at $d=2$ the
shift is $0$ (ordinary $E_2$ with Poisson compatibility); at $d=3$ the
shift is $-1$ (Lie-$2$-algebra compatibility: the Gerstenhaber bracket
carries an additional degree-$-1$ component from the CY$_3$ trace,
giving a homotopy-coherent Lie $2$-algebra in the Baez--Crans sense).
The CY dimension enters via the cyclic trace, not the underlying
$E_2$-operad.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral from CY\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{-2}]
\label{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2}
Let $\cC$ be a saturated dg category of CY dimension $d=2$ with
cyclic $A_\infty$ enhancement. Then $\CC^\bullet(\cC,\cC)$ carries an
$E_2$-structure with Poisson compatibility, and the chiral specialisation
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\PhiFA_2(\cC))$
is an $E_2$-chiral algebra on $C$
(Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra}); we write
$\Phi_{E_2}(\cC) := \SpCh_{\Sigma_1, C}(\PhiFA_2(\cC))$.
The assignment $\cC \mapsto \Phi_{E_2}(\cC)$ is the Vol~III CY-to-$E_2$-chiral
functor at $d=2$, the stage-2 output of $\PhiFA_2$ via
Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-tamarkin-cyclic-e2} equips $\CC^\bullet(\cC,\cC)$
with a cyclic $E_2$-structure, ungraded at $d=2$ by
Remark~\ref{rem:cy-dim-shift}. Dunn additivity
($E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes_{E_0} E_1$) upgrades the factorization
homology $\int_{\Sigma \times \Sigma} \CC^\bullet(\cC,\cC)$ to a
factorization algebra on $\Sigma \times \Sigma$: each $E_1$-factor
contributes factorization structure along one copy of $\Sigma$.
Chirality in each factor follows from the holomorphic refinement of
Kontsevich formality ($C_*(E_2; \Q) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Ger}$):
the holomorphic structure on $\Sigma$ pins the factorization to a
chiral (meromorphic) slice, so the output satisfies the OPE locality
axiom of Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra}. The two chiral
directions commute because the two $E_1$-factors of $E_2$ are
algebraically independent (Dunn additivity is a tensor product, not
a composition). Poisson compatibility follows from the $d = 2$
shift: at CY dimension $2$, the Gerstenhaber bracket on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC, \cC)$ has degree $2 - 2 = 0$
(Remark~\ref{rem:cy-dim-shift}), matching the Poisson bracket
degree of a classical $E_2$-algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-braided representations from CY\texorpdfstring{$_3$}{-3}]
\label{conj:e2-chiral-from-cy3}
Let $\cC$ be saturated of CY dimension $d=3$ with chain-level
$S^3$-framed cyclic $A_\infty$ enhancement. Then $\CC^\bullet(\cC,\cC)$
carries a framed $E_2$-structure in degree $-1$, and
$\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)$ is an $\Eone$-chiral algebra
on the witnessed locus of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, and its
representation category acquires $\Etwo$-braiding
via the Drinfeld centre:
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(\Phi_{E_1}(\cC))) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(\Phi_{E_2}(\cC))$.
The $\Etwo$-chiral algebra $\Phi_{E_2}(\cC)$ is the chiral Drinfeld
centre of $\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}), \emph{not} a direct
output of the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$.
The expected representation category is the Drinfeld centre, equivalently
the representation category of the Drinfeld double of the positive-half
CoHA of $\cC$. The CoHA itself is the associative Hall algebra of
$\cC$ in the sense of Kontsevich--Soibelman~\cite{KontsevichSoibelmanCoHA},
with Hall product from extension stacks; it supplies the BPS/positive-half
data and is not itself a chiral algebra.
Conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}
($\Phi_{E_2} = Z^{\mathrm{ch}} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$);
the underlying $\Eone$ object-level assignment is Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}
on its framed hypotheses.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Braided tensor categories from \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral structure}
\label{sec:braided-tensor-from-e2}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}, $\Rep^{E_2}(\cA)$ is braided
monoidal; for $\cA$ an $E_2$-chiral algebra the braiding is
holomorphic (analytically continued along paths in
$\mathrm{Conf}_n(\Sigma)$, not merely formal).

\begin{definition}[MTC of an \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral algebra]
\label{def:mtc-e2-chiral}
The \emph{modular tensor category} of an $E_2$-chiral algebra $\cA$
is the semisimplification of $\Rep^{E_2}(\cA)$ at integrable modules
(when it exists); if $\Rep^{E_2}(\cA)$ is already finite with
nondegenerate braiding form, it is the MTC directly.
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[MTC from \texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E} via Borcherds]
\label{conj:mtc-k3}
For $\cC = D^b(\Coh(K3 \times E))$ the derived category of a product
K3 times an elliptic curve, the MTC of $\Phi_{E_2}(\cC)$ coincides
with the Verlinde category of the Borcherds-Kac-Moody superalgebra
$\mathfrak{g}_{II_{1,1} \oplus II_{1,1}}$ of the K3 lattice. The
corresponding $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}} = 3$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$
diagnostic is discussed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-times-e} (see also Theorem~\ref{thm:k3e-scattering-bkm}).

\emph{Conditional on Conjecture~\textup{\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}}}:
CY-A$_3$ supplies the framed object-level $\Phi_{E_1}^{(\Sigma_2,C)}$
specialisation for $K3 \times E$ under H1--H4 and fixed admissible data. The $E_2$-chiral algebra $\Phi_{E_2}$
requires the center identification
$\Phi_{E_2} = Z^{\mathrm{ch}} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$, which remains conjectural.
Corollary~\textup{\ref{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2}} applies
to the CY$_2$ factor $D^b(\Coh(K3))$; the extension to the product
$K3 \times E$ requires the center identification.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Evidence]
\label{rem:mtc-k3-evidence}
Corollary~\ref{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2} applies to $D^b(\Coh(K3))$
(CY$_2$) with $\HH^\bullet(K3) = \bigwedge^\bullet H^1(T_{K3})$,
producing an $E_2$-chiral algebra on K3. The elliptic factor adjoins a
modular direction on which the braid group acts via the Heisenberg
lattice VOA of $II_{1,1}$, but the passage from the $d = 2$ chiral
algebra on K3 to a $d = 3$ chiral algebra on $K3 \times E$ requires
the tensor product of the $E_2$-chiral algebra with the elliptic
Heisenberg, which is not a formal consequence of the $d = 2$ functor
alone. The Borcherds product $\Delta_5$ matches the Volume~I chiral
Euler product at lattice weight $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=5$, and the
MTC is the Verlinde quotient of the weight-$5$ lattice VOA. This
matching is an observation, not a derivation from the
functor.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta-5} as the \texorpdfstring{$1$}{1}-loop output of paramodular anomaly
cancellation, with four duality frames]
\label{rem:e2-delta5-one-loop-four-frames}
The matching $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:mtc-k3-evidence} upgrades from an observation to a
\emph{derivation} once $\Delta_5$ is recognised as the $1$-loop-forced
output of paramodular anomaly cancellation in twisted $11$D supergravity
on $K3 \times T^2$ with $\Omega$-deformation and $24$ M$5$-branes wrapping
the $24$ $I_1$ Kodaira fibres of a generic elliptic K3. The weight
$5$ is fixed by the arithmetic split
\[
 5 \;=\; \chi(\cO_{K3}) \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{24}
 \mathrm{Res}\bigl(I_{1,\,i}\bigr)
 \;=\; 2 \;+\; 3,
\]
where $\chi(\cO_{K3}) = 2$ is the holomorphic Euler characteristic (the
$d = 2$, $h^{1,0} = 0$ specialisation of Theorem~CY-D) and the
residues at the $24$ $I_1$-nodes accumulate to $3$ on the nose by the
Steiner-system $S(5,8,24)$ rigidity of
Remark~\ref{rem:e2-bi-based-ran}(R1). The claim that
$\Delta_5$ is produced as the $1$-loop output is independently verified in
four duality frames which all converge on the same paramodular weight-$5$
Borcherds form:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(D\arabic*)}]
 \item Heterotic on $K3 \times T^2$ \cite{HarveyMoore1996};
 \item Type IIB with D$1$--D$5$ bound states \cite{DijkgraafVerlindeVerlinde1996};
 \item Type IIA string-theoretic K3 elliptic genus
 \cite{DijkgraafMooreVerlindeVerlinde1997};
 \item M-theory on $K3 \times T^3$ \cite{Witten1995}.
\end{enumerate}
Each frame produces $\Delta_5$ via a distinct mechanism (heterotic
threshold integral; D-brane partition-function Igusa lift; elliptic-genus
second-quantisation; M-theory topological index), and the four outputs
agree by T-duality / string--string duality / mirror symmetry. For the
Vol~III categorical programme, this frame-independence is the statement
that the $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ invariant of
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3e-bkm} is computed by a single canonical lift from the
K3 elliptic genus $\varphi_{0,1}$, confirming
Conjecture~\ref{conj:mtc-k3} at the level of the paramodular lift.
\end{remark}

\section{Connection to Volume~II: the Drinfeld center}
\label{sec:drinfeld-center-bridge}

Volume~II constructs $\Phi_{E_1}$ and proves $E_1$-chiral Koszul
duality there. The Drinfeld center
$Z\colon E_1\text{-}\Cat \to E_2\text{-}\Cat$
is the right adjoint to the forgetful functor from braided monoidal
categories to monoidal categories, under the usual presentability and
dualisability hypotheses. It constructs half-braidings from ordered
monoidal data. It is not the symmetrisation map
$\mathrm{av}\colon B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^\Sigma$, which quotients
ordered data and loses the $R$-matrix.

\begin{conjecture}[Volume~III central conjecture: \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_{E_2} = Z^{\mathrm{ch}} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$}{Phi-E-2 = Z-ch o Phi-E-1}]
\label{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}
For a saturated CY$_d$ category $\cC$ admitting
$\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)$, the Volume~III $E_2$-chiral construction is
canonically quasi-isomorphic to the chiral Drinfeld center of
$\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)$:
\[
 \Phi_{E_2}(\cC) \;\simeq\; Z^{\mathrm{ch}}\bigl(\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)\bigr),
\]
where $Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$ is the $E_2$-chiral algebra whose
representation category is the Drinfeld center of the $E_1$-chiral
representation category of $\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)$. At $d=2$ this is
compatible with Corollary~\ref{cor:e2-chiral-from-cy2} and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:mtc-k3}; at $d=3$, the input is the framed
object-level specialisation of Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, and
the content of this conjecture is the center identification itself.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Four distinct functors]
\label{rem:four-functors-and-centres}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center} must be distinguished
from three other functors: (i) Verdier duality $D_{\Ran}$ producing
$B(A^!)$ as a factorization coalgebra; (ii) cobar inversion
$\Omega \circ B \simeq \id$ recovering $A$ on the Koszul locus
(Volume~I Theorem~B); (iii) the derived center
$Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$ producing the bulk factorization
algebra via Hochschild cochains (Volume~II bulk-boundary); (iv) the
categorical Drinfeld center $Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$ used here. The
Drinfeld center (iv) lives on representation categories with braided
monoidal output; the derived center (iii) lives on cochain complexes
with $\mathrm{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$-algebra output (or
$E_3$-topological when a conformal vector exists). The three sit on
different source categories with different output operads, and their
invariants do not interchange.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Volume~II operadic conventions]
\label{rem:vol2-operadic-conventions}
Volume~II tracks the $E_1$-chiral operadic framework
($B_P(A) = P^i$-coalgebra vs $BP$ = cooperad; three coalgebra
structures $\mathrm{Lie}^c, \Sym^c, T^c$; factorization coproduct vs
deconcatenation; curvatures $\mu_0$ vs $d_{\mathrm{fib}}^2 =
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\omega_g$; lossiness of $\mathrm{av}$).
$Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$ preserves the ordered $T^c$-coalgebra input and
adjoins half-braiding data where braided centralisation is strict. It
does not invert $\mathrm{av}$ as a quotient map; the symmetric
$B^\Sigma$ image has already lost ordered $R$-matrix data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Bi-based Ran space and the K3 averaging factorisation]
\label{rem:e2-bi-based-ran}
For K3 and $K3 \times E$ the averaging map
$\mathrm{av} \colon B^{\mathrm{ord}} \to B^{\Sigma}$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:vol2-operadic-conventions} acquires explicit geometric content
as a bi-based factorisation datum. The factorisation algebras on which
$Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$ operates at the K3 moduli boundary are defined over
\emph{two distinct} Ran spaces simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(R\arabic*)}]
 \item A \emph{chiral base}
 $\Ran\bigl(E^{\mathrm{nod,sm}}_{24}\bigr)$, the Ran space of an elliptic
 fibration with $24$ nodal--smooth reduction points, carrying
 nearby-cycles $D$-modules at the $24$ Kodaira $I_1$-nodes
 \cite{Kodaira1963}.
 \item A \emph{parameter base}
 $\overline{\mathcal{A}}_2$, the Satake--Baily--Borel compactification
 of the paramodular moduli
 $\mathcal{A}_2 = \Gamma_{\mathrm{paramod}} \backslash \mathcal{H}_2$,
 carrying the Francis--Gaitsgory factorisation $\infty$-category of
 holonomic $D$-modules regular-singular along
 $\mathcal{H}_1 \cup \mathcal{H}_4$ \cite{FrancisGaitsgory2012}.
\end{enumerate}
The averaging map factors as a composition of three geometric steps:
\[
 \mathrm{av}
 \;=\; \mathrm{Torelli}_{K3} \;\circ\;
 \mathrm{KugaSatake} \;\circ\; j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}},
\]
where $j_{\mathrm{Kodaira}}$ is the nearby-cycles functor at the $24$
$I_1$-nodes, $\mathrm{KugaSatake}$ is the Kuga--Satake morphism
$\mathcal{M}_{K3} \to \mathcal{A}_2$ realising K3 Hodge structures inside
paramodular abelian fourfolds \cite{KugaSatake1967}, and
$\mathrm{Torelli}_{K3}$ is the global Torelli embedding
$\mathcal{M}_{K3} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{H}_4(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}})$.
The lossiness of $\mathrm{av}$ tracked in
Remark~\ref{rem:vol2-operadic-conventions} is localised precisely at the $24$
$I_1$-nodes of (R1) and at the Humbert divisors of (R2); the chiral
Drinfeld centre $Z^{\mathrm{ch}}$ supplies the half-braiding on the smooth
locus of $\overline{\mathcal{A}}_2$ away from $\mathcal{H}_1 \cup
\mathcal{H}_4$; it is a centralisation construction, not an inverse to
the symmetric quotient. This is the geometric locus entering
Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center} for the K3 family. The
Steiner system $S(5,8,24)$ rigidity of \cite{ConwaySloane1988} underwrites
the $24$-point structure on both bases simultaneously.
\end{remark}

\section{The transverse \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-shadow at \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d=3}: chain-level construction}
\label{sec:e2-transverse-shadow-d3}

At $d = 3$, the Stage-1 output $\cF_\cC = \PhiFA_3(\cC)$ is an
$E_3$-holomorphic factorisation algebra, and the Dunn--Lurie
decomposition $E_3 \simeq E_1 \otimes_{E_0} E_2$ splits this into two
commuting algebraic structures that travel through Stage-2
independently. The $E_1$-factor specialises to the longitudinal
chiral algebra $A = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2, C)}(\cC)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}. The $E_2$-factor specialises to an
$E_2$-algebra that lives not on $A$ but on the derived Drinfeld centre
$Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ of its representation category. This
transverse $E_2$-shadow is the chain-level origin of the quantum-group
$R$-matrix in Vol~III, the ambient structure in which
Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center} is stated.

\begin{theorem}[Dunn--Lurie decomposition of the Stage-1 \texorpdfstring{$E_3$}{E-3}-hFA]
\label{thm:dunn-lurie-phi-fa-3}
Let $\cC$ be a saturated CY$_3$ category satisfying H1--H4 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}, and let $\cF_\cC = \PhiFA_3(\cC)
\in E_3\text{-}\HolFA(X)$ be its Stage-1 $E_3$-holomorphic factorisation
algebra on the CY$_3$ variety $X$. Then $\cF_\cC$ admits, after fixing
the cyclic formality/associator torsor point, a Dunn--Lurie
decomposition
\[
 \cF_\cC \;\simeq\; \cF^{E_1}_\cC \otimes_{E_0} \cF^{E_2}_\cC,
\]
with $\cF^{E_1}_\cC \in E_1\text{-}\HolFA(X)$ a BD-chiral
$E_1$-factorisation algebra along the longitudinal direction and
$\cF^{E_2}_\cC \in E_2\text{-}\HolFA(X)$ a fully holomorphic
$E_2$-factorisation algebra on the transverse $2$-cycle. The
choice is a torsor over the cyclic subgroup
$\mathrm{GRT}_1^{\mathrm{cyc}}(\Q) \subset \mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$, pinned
to the Kontsevich associator by the Costello--Li propagator on the
CY$_3$ geometric input.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The statement $E_3 \simeq E_p \otimes E_q$ at the level of
$\infty$-operads for $n = p + q$ is Lurie's Dunn additivity theorem,
Higher Algebra~\cite[\S 5.1.2.2]{LurieHA}: an $E_n$-algebra is an
$E_p$-algebra in $E_q$-algebras. For a holomorphic factorisation
algebra $\cF_\cC$ on a complex $n$-fold $X$ (here $n = 3$), the same
decomposition holds on the holomorphic refinement: the full
holomorphic factorisation structure splits as BD-chiral factorisation
along a holomorphic $1$-direction tensored with fully-holomorphic
factorisation on the transverse $2$-direction. This is the
holomorphic analogue of Dunn additivity, established by
Francis~\cite[\S 2]{Francis2013} and extended to the CY-trace-class
setting by Costello--Gwilliam--Li (references as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-tamarkin-cyclic-e2}).

The associator ambiguity comes from Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality
$C_*(E_2; \Q) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Ger$, whose choice space is a torsor
over the Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller group $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$
(Tamarkin~\cite{TamarkinDGCat}; Willwacher~\cite{Willwacher2015}).
A cyclic $A_\infty$ lift (supplied by the CY$_3$ trace class $\eta \in
\HH_{-3}(\cC)$) cuts down this torsor to its cyclic subgroup
$\mathrm{GRT}_1^{\mathrm{cyc}}(\Q)$; the Kontsevich associator lies in
this subgroup (Tamarkin), so the Costello--Li propagator
\cite[\S 6]{CostelloLi2016} produces a consistent pin. The
Dunn--Lurie decomposition of $\cF_\cC$ is thus well-defined after the
Costello--Li branch and torsor point are fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Three objects, three operadic types]
\label{rem:transverse-three-objects}
The theorem surfaces three distinct mathematical objects at $d = 3$:
(i) the ambient $E_3$-hFA $\cF_\cC$ on $X$, before Stage-2; (ii) the
longitudinal $E_1$-chiral algebra $A = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2, C)}(\cC)$ on
$C$, after Stage-2 applied to $\cF^{E_1}_\cC$; (iii) the transverse
$E_2$-algebra $\fZ_\cC = \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_2, C}
(\cF^{E_2}_\cC)$, after Stage-2 applied to $\cF^{E_2}_\cC$. These are
three different objects with three different operadic types. The
transverse shadow is not $A$; it is the object computed from the
orthogonal Dunn--Lurie factor via the same Stage-2 specialisation.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Transverse $\texorpdfstring{E_2}{E-2}$-factor equals the derived Drinfeld centre]
\label{thm:transverse-is-drinfeld-der}
Let $A = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2, C)}(\cC)$ and let $\fZ_\cC =
\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_2, C}(\cF^{E_2}_\cC)$ be the Stage-2
specialisation of the $E_2$-factor of $\cF_\cC$. Then, as
$E_2$-algebras,
\[
 \fZ_\cC
 \;\simeq\;
 Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\Rep^{E_1}(A))
 \;\simeq\;
 \RHom_{A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, A)
 \;\simeq\;
 \CC^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(A, A),
\]
all four objects coinciding as $E_2$-algebras. Consequently the
representation category $\Rep^{E_2}(\fZ_\cC)$ is canonically braided
monoidal and coincides with the categorical Drinfeld centre
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ in the sense of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first equivalence is Ayala--Francis factorisation homology of
$\cF^{E_2}_\cC$ over the Stage-2 specialisation cycle $\Sigma_2$
\cite[\S 3.1]{AyalaFrancis2015}: the integral
$\int_{\Sigma_2} \cF^{E_2}_\cC$ computes the derived endomorphisms of
the category $\Rep^{E_1}(A)$ as a module over itself, i.e.\ the
derived centre. The second equivalence is Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler
2010 \emph{JAMS}~23 Theorem~4.1: for a locally presentable stable
$\infty$-category $\cC$ dualisable over $\cC \otimes \cC^{\mathrm{op}}$,
the derived centre $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\cC)$ is
$\End_{\cC \otimes \cC^{\mathrm{op}}}(\cC)$, an $E_2$-algebra in
$\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}$. Specialising to $\cC = \Rep^{E_1}(A)$
dualisable over $A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}$ (a consequence of
$A$ being a compact generator of its factorisation category on $C$),
the derived centre is $\RHom_{A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, A)$. The
third equivalence is the Deligne conjecture for chiral-dg-algebras
(Lurie HA \S 5.3.1.14; Francis \cite[\S 2]{Francis2013}): the
Hochschild cochain complex of $A$ as a dg-object in
$E_1\text{-}\ChirAlg(C)$ coincides with $\RHom_{A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}
(A, A)$ as an $E_2$-algebra, with $E_2$-action via brace operations
(Kontsevich--Soibelman \cite{KontsevichSoibelmanDefI};
McClure--Smith 2002 \emph{Recent Progress in Homotopy Theory};
Tamarkin \cite{TamarkinDGCat}).

That the representation category carries the Drinfeld-centre braiding
is Lurie's Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}: the
$(\infty, 2)$-category of $E_2$-algebras is equivalent to the
$(\infty, 2)$-category of braided monoidal objects, and the functor
$\Rep^{E_2}(-)$ realises this equivalence at the level of
representation categories.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Longitudinal-transverse disambiguation]
\label{rem:longitudinal-transverse-disambiguation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse-is-drinfeld-der} clarifies four
adjacent chain-level objects that are distinct in general but
coincide on the transverse shadow:
(a) $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, the derived Drinfeld centre on
the chiral side;
(b) $\RHom_{A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A, A)$, the bimodule
endomorphism complex;
(c) $\CC^\bullet_{\mathrm{ch}}(A, A)$, the chiral-Hochschild cochain
complex (the chiral upgrade of Hochschild cochains compatible with
BD-chiral factorisation along $C$; Francis \cite[\S 2]{Francis2013});
(d) $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$, the monoidal Drinfeld centre via
half-braidings (Majid 1991 \emph{Pacific J.~Math.}~141;
Etingof--Gelaki--Nikshych--Ostrik 2015 Proposition~7.13.8).
These are four presentations of the same $E_2$-algebra on the
transverse scope; only (a)--(d) coincide, and only on representation
categories that are dualisable in $\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm L}$. The
structural difference between chiral-Hochschild and topological-
Hochschild (the latter computing
$\mathsf{SC}^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$-derived centres in Vol~II) is structural,
not merely dimensional.
\end{remark}

\begin{construction}[The transverse $\texorpdfstring{E_2}{E-2}$-braiding as cocycle residue]
\label{constr:transverse-r-matrix-cocycle}
For $\cC$ a CY$_3$ category admitting a CoHA $Y^+(\cC)$ with
locally-finite graded pieces, let $T$ be the torus preserving the
CY$_3$ structure and let $\cW \subset \mathfrak{t}^*_\C$ be the
equivariant wall arrangement (walls are character loci of normal
bundles to fixed-point components of $\cM^+ = \cM^+_{\mathrm{eff}}
(\cC)$). The \emph{UV positive-half gluing cocycle} is the
meromorphic $\End(Y^+ \otimes Y^+)$-valued $1$-form
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ on $\mathfrak{t}^*_\C$ defined on each chamber
$\mathcal{K}$ by
\[
 \phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(u) \Big|_{\mathcal{K}}
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{Stab}_{\mathcal{K}}^+
 \circ (\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathcal{K}}^+)^{-1}
 \;+\;
 \sum_{\text{walls } \alpha \subset \partial \mathcal{K}}
 \frac{r_\alpha \, du}{\alpha(u)},
\]
with $\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathcal{K}}^+$ the Maulik--Okounkov stable
envelope of chamber $\mathcal{K}$ and $r_\alpha$ the
hyperbolic-restriction residue at wall $\alpha$
(Maulik--Okounkov \cite[\S 4]{MaulikOkounkov2012}). The transverse
$E_2$-braiding $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u)$ is then
\[
 R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u) \;=\; \Res_{v = u_\star}
 \phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(v),
 \qquad v = u_1 - u_2,
\]
at any chamber wall $u_\star$, and is independent of the chamber
choice up to unitarity.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[MO axiom equals cocycle condition]
\label{thm:mo-equals-cocycle}
Let $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u)$ be the transverse $E_2$-braiding of
Construction~\ref{constr:transverse-r-matrix-cocycle}. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u) = R^{\mathrm{MO}}(u)$, the Maulik--Okounkov
 $R$-matrix of the stable-envelope construction.
 \item The quantum Yang--Baxter equation
 $R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{12}(u_1 - u_2) \, R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{13}(u_1 - u_3) \,
 R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{23}(u_2 - u_3) =
 R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{23}(u_2 - u_3) \, R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{13}(u_1 - u_3) \,
 R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{12}(u_1 - u_2)$
 is the \v{C}ech-cocycle condition for $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ on the
 codimension-two wall triple-intersection.
 \item Unitarity $R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{21}(-u) \cdot R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{12}(u)
 = \mathrm{id}$ is the cocycle-reversal identity
 $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}} + \bar\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}} = d(\cdots)$ for the
 chamber-reversed cocycle.
 \item The hexagon axioms of the braided monoidal structure on
 $\Rep^{E_2}(\fZ_\cC) = \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ are the corresponding
 codimension-three coherence relations of $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$, in
 bijection with the insertion-coherence axioms of the
 Fulton--MacPherson operad $\FM_3(\C)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Claim~(i): the stable envelope $\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathcal{K}}^+$
determines an embedding $K_T(\cM^{+, T}) \hookrightarrow K_T(\cM^+)$
whose transition matrices between adjacent chambers are, by
construction, the MO $R$-matrices (Maulik--Okounkov
\cite[Thm.~4.6.1]{MaulikOkounkov2012}). The residue of
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ at a wall $\alpha$ is the corresponding
transition matrix, hence $R^{\mathrm{MO}}(\alpha)$.

Claim~(ii): three chambers $\mathcal{K}_1, \mathcal{K}_2, \mathcal{K}_3$
meeting at a codimension-two locus impose the coboundary condition
$\phi^+_{12} + \phi^+_{23} = \phi^+_{13}$ on the transitions, which,
read as residues, is
$R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{12} \cdot R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{23} = R^{\mathrm{ch}}_{13}$
in the reducible form; meromorphic continuation (rationality of
$R^{\mathrm{ch}}$ in $u$, Okounkov 2017 \emph{Lectures on K-theoretic
computations})
lifts this to the braided Yang--Baxter form.

Claim~(iii): chamber reversal across a single wall gives
$\phi^+ \mapsto -\phi^+$, hence $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u) \mapsto
R^{\mathrm{ch}}(-u)^{-1}$; acting as $R_{21}(-u)$ on the swapped
factor and composing yields unitarity.

Claim~(iv): the hexagon axioms follow from the $E_2$-braided monoidal
structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse-is-drinfeld-der} via
Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}; the identification with
$\FM_3(\C)$ insertion axioms is Lurie HA \S 5.3.1.14.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transverse \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-shadow as Hopf representation category]
\label{thm:transverse-hopf-presentation}
For the three primary CY$_3$ inputs verified at chain level, the
transverse $E_2$-shadow admits an explicit Hopf-algebraic presentation:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{CY$_3$ input } $\cC$ & $\mathrm{CoHA}(\cC) = Y^+(\cC)$ &
 \textbf{Drinfeld double } $H_\cC = D(Y^+(\cC))$ \\
 \midrule
 $\Perf(\C^3)$ & $Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)$ (SV 2013) &
 $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)$ (affine Yangian) \\
 $D^b(\Coh(K3 \times E))$ (reduced) &
 $Y^+$ with Mukai super-structure &
 $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4, 20))$ (Hodge-parity $\Z/2$-super-ext.\ $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4 \mid 20))$) \\
 $\Perf(\mathrm{LP}^2)$, $\Perf(\text{conifold})$, toric CY$_3$ &
 RSYZ shuffle algebra &
 quantum toroidal $U_{q, t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_r)$ \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In each case, $\Rep(H_\cC) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ as braided
monoidal categories, and the universal $R$-matrix of $H_\cC$ produces
the $R^{\mathrm{ch}}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-equals-cocycle}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Case $\C^3$: Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013 \emph{Publ.\ Math.\ IHES}~118
Theorem~8.3 establishes that
$D\bigl(Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)\bigr)$, the Drinfeld double of
the positive half, is the full affine Yangian; the
explicit $R$-matrix on Fock-module evaluations is Feigin--Jimbo--Miwa--
Mukhin 2017 \emph{J.~Integrable Systems}~2, and it coincides with the
MO $R$-matrix of $\Hilb^n(\C^2)$ (Maulik--Okounkov
\cite[\S 8]{MaulikOkounkov2012} via the identification with
$\Hilb^n(\C^3)^T$ at the CY$_3$ subtorus).

Case $K3 \times E$ (reduced): the Mukai Hodge involution
$\theta$ on $\widetilde H^*(K3, \C) = \C^{24}$ splits the $24$-dim
cohomology into the $4$-dim $(+1)$-eigenspace $V_+$ (from
$H^0 \oplus H^4 \oplus (H^{2,0} \oplus H^{0,2})_\R$) and the $20$-dim
$(-1)$-eigenspace $V_-$ (the primary $(1, 1)$-sector
$H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{prim}}$), imposing the orthogonal
$\mathfrak{so}(4, 20)$ symmetry of the symmetric Mukai pairing. On
both graded parts the pairing restricts to a symmetric form, so Kac
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ (symplectic on the odd part) is
explicitly not the envelope; the Mukai-preserving Yangian is the
orthogonal $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4, 20))$ of
Molev--Ragoucy~\cite{MolevRagoucy2008}, or conjecturally its
Hodge-parity $\Z/2$-super-extension
$Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4 \mid 20))$ (programme-specific, non-Kac).
The CoHA of $K3$ carries the corresponding $\Z/2$-graded structure,
and Schiffmann--Vasserot 2017 \emph{arXiv:1705.07341} computes it
via stable envelopes on derived moduli. The elliptic fibre $E$
contributes the spectral-parameter direction; the $R$-matrix is the
rational $\mathfrak{so}(24)$ $R$-matrix
$R(u) = 1 + \hbar\, \Omega_{\mathrm{Muk}}/u + O(\hbar^2)$ with
Casimir $\Omega_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ normalised by the Mukai pairing and
crossing shift $\kappa_{\so} = 22$.

Case toric CY$_3$ with compact $4$-cycle: Rapcak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao
\cite{RSYZ2020} compute the CoHA as a shuffle algebra; the Drinfeld double
is a quantum toroidal algebra with rank and parameters determined by
the toric fan. For LP$^2$, the fan has four rays (one compact $\P^2$
plus three toric divisors) and the double is
$U_{q, t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_1)$ at the parameter
specialisation $h_1 h_2 h_3 = 0$ (CY$_3$ constraint); for the conifold,
the double is a symmetric-pair toroidal algebra.

In each case the braided-monoidal equivalence
$\Rep(H_\cC) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$ is Majid's quantum-double
theorem \cite[Thm.~7.1.1]{Majid1995} applied to the pair
$(Y^+(\cC), Y^-(\cC))$; the universal $R$-matrix in $H_\cC \otimes
H_\cC$ evaluates on the evaluation modules to $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u)$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:mo-equals-cocycle}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The three Dunn--Lurie specialisations]
\label{rem:three-dunn-lurie-specialisations}
The Dunn--Lurie decomposition $\cF_\cC = \cF^{E_1}_\cC \otimes
\cF^{E_2}_\cC$ is agnostic to the choice of $(\Sigma_2, C)$. Three
notable specialisations arise.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(S\arabic*)}]
 \item \emph{Holomorphic--holomorphic:} $(\Sigma_2, C) = (\text{holomorphic
 $2$-cycle}, \text{holomorphic curve})$. This is the canonical CY$_3$
 specialisation; $A$ is a BD-chiral $E_1$-algebra and the transverse
 shadow is the chiral-derived centre.
 \item \emph{Holomorphic--topological (Kapustin--Witten):} $(\Sigma_2, C)
 = (\text{holomorphic $2$-cycle}, \text{topological line})$. Wilson-line
 observables along $C$; the transverse shadow matches the CFG
 $E_2$-shadow of topological Chern--Simons.
 \item \emph{Topological--topological (CFG full):} $(\Sigma_2, C) =
 (\text{topological $2$-cycle}, \text{topological line})$. $A$
 collapses to an $E_\infty$-algebra on $C$; the transverse shadow is
 $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-mod in the sense of
 Costello--Francis--Gwilliam~\cite{CFG2026}.
\end{enumerate}
Our construction (S1) is the holomorphic refinement of the CFG result
(S3), with (S2) as the natural intermediate stratum. The transverse
$E_2$-shadow varies continuously across the three strata; the
$R$-matrix $R^{\mathrm{ch}}(u)$ of (S1) is the holomorphic deformation
of the CFG $R$-matrix of (S3) away from the topological limit
$u \to \infty$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Chain-level inputs for Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}]
\label{rem:central-conjecture-chain-level-inputs}
Theorems~\ref{thm:dunn-lurie-phi-fa-3}, \ref{thm:transverse-is-drinfeld-der},
and \ref{thm:transverse-hopf-presentation} together supply the
chain-level object-level content of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center}
($\Phi_{E_2} \simeq Z^{\mathrm{ch}} \circ \Phi_{E_1}$): for each CY$_3$
category $\cC$ in the H1--H4 framed admissible locus, the assignment
$\cC \mapsto Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\Rep^{E_1}(\Phi_{E_1}(\cC)))$ is a
well-defined $E_2$-algebra, coincides with the
$\fZ_\cC$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse-is-drinfeld-der}, and for
the three verified CY$_3$ inputs admits an explicit Hopf presentation
(Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse-hopf-presentation}). What remains
conjectural in Conjecture~\ref{conj:phi-e2-drinfeld-center} is the
$(\infty, 1)$-functoriality: the statement that this object-level
assignment upgrades to an $(\infty, 1)$-functor between
$(\infty, 1)$-categories, which requires morphism-preservation data
not yet supplied by the chain-level construction. Thus the transverse
$E_2$-shadow is chain-level and object-level; its functorial lift is
conjectural.
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$E_n$}{E-n}-chiral Koszul duality and the three bar complexes}
\label{sec:e2-koszul-and-bar}

CY-B is $d$-dependent. At $d = 2$, $A = \Phi_2(\cC)$ is natively $E_2$
and the duality is $E_2$-chiral Koszul duality on $A$ directly.
At $d = 3$, $A = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\cC)$ is $E_1$ on the H1--H4 framed locus, and the duality operates on the retained Stage-$1$
$E_3$ bar complex $B_{E_3}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ (from the CY$_3$ $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing), not on the bare specialised $E_1$ curve algebra;
the $E_2$-braided equivalence is \emph{induced} on the Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A))$.

CY-B has two components: the \emph{conductor formula}
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K$
(proved below as Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-conductor}), and the
\emph{braided monoidal equivalence} on $E_2$-representation categories
(proved for all CY$_3$ inputs in Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3} via
the Verdier spectral functor of Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor};
conjectural for general non-CY $E_2$-chiral algebras).

\begin{conjecture}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral Koszul duality]
\label{conj:e2-koszul}
For an $E_2$-chiral algebra $\cA$, the bar-cobar adjunction extends
$E_2$-equivariantly, giving a braided monoidal equivalence
\[
 \Rep^{E_2}(A) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}},
\]
with $\mathrm{rev}$ the reverse braiding and $A^!$ the Koszul dual
algebra (not $D_{\Ran}(B(A))$, not $\Omega(B(A))$).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral Koszul duality of the Heisenberg]
\label{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}
Let $H_k$ be the Heisenberg VOA at level~$k$, realized as the boundary
chiral algebra of the 5d holomorphic theory on $\C^2 \times \R$ (or
equivalently as the $E_2$ projection of the 6d theory on $\C^3$) at
parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ with $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ and
$k = -\sigma_2 = -(h_1 h_2 + h_1 h_3 + h_2 h_3)$.
Then $H_k$ is class~$G$ (Gaussian, shadow depth $r = 2$), and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul} holds for $H_k$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Vanishing differentials.} The $E_2$ bar complex
 $B_{E_2}(H_k)$ has two commuting differentials $d_X, d_Y = 0$.
 The Heisenberg OPE $J(z)J(w) \sim k/(z-w)^2$ has no first-order
 pole, so the chiral bracket $\mu = \Res_{z=w} J(z) J(w) = 0$ and the
 bar differential vanishes in each direction. This holds at all parameter
 values, including the self-dual point.

 \item \emph{Bigraded decomposition.} Since $d_X = d_Y = 0$, the $E_2$
 bar complex is formal. Its bigraded Hilbert series is
 \[
 \sum_{n \geq 0} \dim B_{E_2}(H_k)_n \, q^n \;=\; P(q)^2 \;=\;
 \prod_{m \geq 1} \frac{1}{(1-q^m)^2},
 \]
 so $\dim B_{E_2}(H_k)_n = \tau_2(n)$, the number of $2$-component
 multipartitions of~$n$:
 $\tau_2(n) = \sum_{a+b=n} p(a)\,p(b)$.
 The first values are $1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 36, 65, 110, 185, 300$
 (OEIS A000712). The $E_3$ trigraded dimension $\tau_3(n)$ of
 Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg} factors through $\tau_2$:
 $\tau_3(n) = \sum_{m=0}^n \tau_2(m) \, p(n-m)$, consistent with
 the Dunn-additivity projection $E_3 \to E_2$.

 \item \emph{Braiding reversal.} The Koszul dual $H_k^!$ has inverted
 level $k^! = -k$ and $R$-matrix
 \[
 R^{E_2,!}(z) \;=\; \frac{-k\,\Omega}{z} + O(1)
 \;=\; -R^{E_2}(z) + O(1).
 \]
 Since $R^{E_2,!}(z) = -R^{E_2}(z)$ at leading order, this is the
 braiding reversal $\sigma^{\mathrm{rev}} = \sigma^{-1}$. The
 $E_2$-equivariant bar-cobar adjunction therefore produces
 \[
 \Rep^{E_2}(H_k) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{E_2}(H_{-k})^{\mathrm{rev}},
 \]
 where $\mathrm{rev}$ is the reverse braiding on the representation
 category. Unlike the $E_3$ case
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}), where $H_1$ is Koszul
 self-dual at the self-dual point, the $E_2$ Koszul dual
 \emph{always} has reversed braiding: $H_k$ is not $E_2$-Koszul
 self-dual for any $k \neq 0$.

 \item \emph{Koszul conductor.} The modular characteristics satisfy
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k^!) = k + (-k)
 = 0 = \rho_K$, where $\rho_K = 0$ for class~$G$ algebras, consistent
 with Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul} and the Vol~I class~$G$ Koszul
 conductor formula.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has two independent parts: the algebraic structure of the
Heisenberg OPE (Claims~(i)--(ii)), and the Verdier duality computation
with braiding analysis (Claims~(iii)--(iv)).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~1: Vanishing of differentials.}
By Dunn additivity, $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$, and the $E_2$ bar
complex is the iterated bar $B_{E_2}(A) \simeq B_Y(B_X(A))$ with
$d_X$ from the OPE residue $\Res_{z_X = w_X}$ and $d_Y$ from
$\Res_{z_Y = w_Y}$. For $H_k$, the OPE is
\[
 J(z)\,J(w) \;\sim\; \frac{k}{(z-w)^2},
\]
with no first-order pole. The bar differential in direction~$i$
extracts the chiral bracket $\mu_i(J, J) = \Res_{z_i = w_i} J(z) J(w)
= 0$ (no $(z-w)^{-1}$ term). Since the algebra is generated by~$J$
alone and $\mu_i = 0$ on all generators, $d_X = d_Y = 0$. Within the
Heisenberg/free-field family this is independent of the level parameter:
the OPE depends only on $k = -\sigma_2$, and has no first-order pole.
The structure function
\[
 g_{E_2}(u) = \frac{(u - h_1)(u - h_2)}{(u + h_1)(u + h_2)}
\]
controls the $R$-matrix (braiding), not the differential.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~2: Bigraded dimension.}
With $d_X = d_Y = 0$, the complex is formal and reduces to its
underlying bigraded vector space. Each of the two directions contributes
a copy of the symmetric bar $B_{E_\infty}(H_k|_{C_i})$ with graded
dimension $P(q) = \prod_{m \geq 1} (1-q^m)^{-1}$ (one generator~$J$).
The bigraded dimension is $P(q)^2$, giving $\tau_2(n)$ at degree~$n$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~3: Braiding reversal.}
The Koszul dual level is $k^! = -k$; this does \emph{not} follow from
negating the $\Omega$-background parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \mapsto
(-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$, since $\sigma_2$ is degree-$2$ homogeneous and
hence $\sigma_2(-h_i) = \sigma_2(h_i)$, leaving $k = -\sigma_2$
invariant. Instead, $k^! = -k$ arises from the Verdier duality
functor $D_{\C^2}$ on conilpotent $E_2$-coalgebras, which acts by
linear duality on the underlying graded space and thereby
\emph{transposes the Shapovalov form}: the inner product
$\langle J, J \rangle = k$ dualizes to $\langle J, J \rangle^! = -k$
(the bilinear form on the linear dual carries the opposite sign).
Since $R^{E_2}(z) = k\,\Omega/z + O(1)$ with $\Omega$ the
metric-independent Casimir, the dual $R$-matrix is
\[
 R^{E_2,!}(z) \;=\; \frac{k^!\,\Omega}{z} + O(1)
 \;=\; \frac{(-k)\,\Omega}{z} + O(1)
 \;=\; -R^{E_2}(z) + O(1).
\]
The identity $R^{E_2,!} = -R^{E_2}$ at leading order is the defining
condition for braiding reversal: $\sigma^{\mathrm{rev}} = \sigma^{-1}$
at the linearized level. By Theorem~\ref{thm:lurie-e2-braided}, the
resulting equivalence on representation categories is braided monoidal
with the reverse braiding.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~4: Koszul conductor.}
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) = k$ (Volume~I, class~$G$) and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{-k}) = -k$, so $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k)
+ \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k^!) = 0 = \rho_K$ with Koszul conductor
$\rho_K = 0$ (the shadow tower terminates at depth~$2$ for class~$G$,
yielding no higher-order corrections).

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: $49$ tests in
\texttt{test\_e2\_koszul\_heisenberg.py}, covering all four claims at
multiple parameter values, with six independent verification paths:
partition-convolution, generating-function extraction,
$E_3 \to E_2$ projection cross-check,
$R$-matrix sign verification, cross-engine $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$
consistency, and KoszulDual class comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the theorem]
\label{rem:e2-koszul-heisenberg-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg} proves
Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul} for a single algebra: the Heisenberg
$H_k$ (class~$G$). The proof exploits the free-field property
($d_X = d_Y = 0$), which is specific to~$H_k$. For the affine Yangian
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ at general parameters (class~$L$ or higher), the
differentials are nonzero and the formality argument does not apply.
For $V_k(\fsl_2)$, the Gerstenhaber bracket is nonzero and $d_Y \neq 0$,
yielding a genuinely two-dimensional bar complex with nontrivial braiding.
Nevertheless, the Heisenberg case confirms the foundational
predictions (bigraded dimension, braiding reversal,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-complementarity) on which the general conjecture
rests.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} vs \texorpdfstring{$E_3$}{E-3} self-duality]
\label{rem:e2-vs-e3-self-duality}
The $E_3$ Koszul theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}) asserts that $H_1$ is
\emph{Koszul self-dual} at the self-dual point $(1,0,-1)$: parameter
inversion gives the relabeling $z_1 \leftrightarrow z_3$, which is an
isomorphism. At the $E_2$ level, self-duality \emph{fails}: the
Koszul dual always carries reversed braiding
$\Rep^{E_2}(H_k) \simeq \Rep^{E_2}(H_{-k})^{\mathrm{rev}}$, and for
$k \neq 0$ the reverse braiding is \emph{not} equivalent to the
original braiding (the $R$-matrix changes sign). The hierarchy
$\tau_3(n) > \tau_2(n) > p(n)$ for $n \geq 1$ reflects the increasing
number of multipartition components at each $E_n$ level.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[CY-B conductor formula]
\label{thm:cy-b-conductor}
Let $A$ be an $E_2$-chiral algebra of shadow class
$X \in \{G, L, C, M\}$, and let $A^!$ be its Koszul dual. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The Koszul conductor
 $\rho_K := \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!)$
 is a well-defined family invariant (independent of the continuous
 parameter within each family).
 \item For classes~$G$ and~$L$: $\rho_K = 0$.
 The Verdier duality on the $E_2$ bar complex inverts the Shapovalov form,
 giving $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = -\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A)$.
 For class~$L$ specifically, the Feigin--Frenkel involution
 $k' = -k - 2h^\vee$ gives the affine Kac--Moody conductor
 $\rho_K(V_k(\fg)) = 0$ for all simple~$\fg$.
 \item For class~$M$:
 $\rho_K = c_{\mathrm{crit}}/2$, where $c_{\mathrm{crit}} = c(A) + c(A^!)$
 is the critical central charge of the family.
 For the Virasoro ($c_{\mathrm{crit}} = 26$): $\rho_K = 13$.
 \item The derived Koszul conductor equals the classical one on the
 trace-exact completion:
 $K^{\mathrm{der}}(A) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^{!,\mathrm{der}}) = \rho_K$
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-conductor}).
 For class~$M$ this requires the sign-definite Borel regime
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3S_4>0$ and trace-class summability of the
 correction tower.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has three independent components.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~1: Verdier duality on the $E_2$ bar complex.}
The Koszul dual $A^! = D_{\C^2}(B_{E_2}(A))^\vee$ is obtained via Verdier
duality, which acts by transposing the Shapovalov form: the bilinear pairing
$\langle v, w \rangle = k$ dualizes to $\langle v, w \rangle^! = -k$.
At the level of the $R$-matrix: $R^{E_2,!}(z) = -R^{E_2}(z) + O(1)$
(braiding reversal, as in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}).
This determines the Koszul involution on the family parameter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~2: Class-specific conductor.}
\emph{Class $G$}: all bar differentials vanish ($\mu = 0$). Verdier
negation gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = -\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$, hence
$\rho_K = 0$.
\emph{Class $L$}: the Feigin--Frenkel involution $k \mapsto -k - 2h^\vee$
acts on $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_k(\fg)) = \dim(\fg)(k + h^\vee)/(2h^\vee)$
by sending $(k + h^\vee) \mapsto (-k - h^\vee)$, so
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^! = -\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ and $\rho_K = 0$.
This is an algebraic identity for all simple Lie algebras at all levels.
\emph{Class $M$}: the Koszul involution acts on central charges as
$c \mapsto c_{\mathrm{crit}} - c$. With $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = c/2$
(minimal-weight parametrization):
\[
 \rho_K \;=\; \frac{c}{2} + \frac{c_{\mathrm{crit}} - c}{2}
 \;=\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{crit}}}{2}.
\]
For the Virasoro: $c_{\mathrm{crit}} = 26$, so $\rho_K = 13$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~3: trace-exact higher homotopies.}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-conductor}, the higher $A_\infty$
corrections do not change the scalar conductor when their contribution
is a trace-exact Hochschild coboundary in the completed bar complex.
For classes~$G$ and~$L$ this is a finite calculation; for class~$C$
the trace series is absolutely convergent; for class~$M$ the assertion
is restricted to the discriminant-negative OPE regime
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3 S_4>0$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability}, where the
Gevrey-$1$ correction tower is Borel summable along the positive ray.
Outside those convergence hypotheses the equality
$K^{\mathrm{der}}=\rho_K$ is not asserted by this theorem.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: $89$ tests in
\texttt{test\_cy\_b\_toward\_proof.py}, covering all four shadow classes across
$5$ standard families (Heisenberg, Kac--Moody for $\fsl_2$ through $E_8$,
Virasoro, $\cW_3$, K3 Mukai), with cross-checks against
\texttt{e1\_koszul\_three\_families} and \texttt{chiral\_koszul\_derived}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[CY-B: conductor vs braided equivalence]
\label{rem:cy-b-decomposition}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-conductor} proves the \emph{scalar} component of CY-B
(the conductor formula $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K$).
The \emph{categorical} component (the braided monoidal equivalence
on the Drinfeld center:
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A^!))^{\mathrm{rev}}$)
is proved for all
shadow classes at $d = 3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3} below), closing the
class~$M$ gap via the Verdier spectral functor theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}).
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The d=3 structure: from Bridgeland tilting to curved
 formality}
\label{subsec:cy-b-d3-structure}

Geometric Koszul duality at $d = 3$ begins as the Kapranov 3-shifted
Koszul conjecture, $D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X^3))^! \simeq D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(X^!))$
for some mirror $X^!$. The simplest candidate fails: Bridgeland tilting
on the compact quintic does not yield a Koszul-self-dual tilting
complex, blocked by six independent obstructions
(Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic}). The sharper statement
replaces strict formality with \emph{curved formality carrying Yukawa
coupling $Y_3 = 5 + 2875 q + 4876875 q^2 + \cdots$ as BCOV curving}
(Corollary~\ref{cor:yukawa-curving-bcov}). The d=3 statement decomposes
into three layers, unpacked in the following three theorems:
scalar conductor coincidence
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3-conductor-coincidence}), chain-level
Koszul duality on toric/local surfaces
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3-lp2-koszul}, proved for LP$^2$ via
Klebanov--Witten), and chain-level on compact CY$_3$ (refuted at strict
formality, replaced by curved formality with $Y_3$ as BCOV datum).

\begin{remark}[CY-B at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}: precise scope of what is proved and what remains open]
\label{rem:cy-b-d3-precise}
CY-B is $d$-dependent, and the statement at $d=3$ must be
unpacked into three distinct layers because the ambient programme proves
only the first two while the third (geometric Koszul duality for compact
CY$_3$ targets) remains a research frontier. We make this explicit.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \textbf{Scalar component (conductor).} For $A$ an $E_1$-chiral
 algebra arising as $\Phi(\cC)$ with $\cC$ a CY$_3$ category, the
 identity
 \[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K
 \]
 is proved for all shadow classes (Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-conductor}).
 \item \textbf{Categorical component (braided equivalence on the
 center).} The braided monoidal equivalence
 $\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A^!))^{\mathrm{rev}}$
 is proved via the Verdier spectral functor
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}), which transports the
 page-by-page structure through Shapovalov transposition
 $k \mapsto -k$ without passage through a putative geometric Koszul
 dual of $\cC$ itself.
 \item \textbf{Geometric Koszul duality at the category level
 (OPEN).} The statement that, for a compact CY$_3$ target $X$, the
 underlying category $D^b(\Coh(X))$ admits a Koszul dual
 $D^b(\Coh(X))^!$ fitting into a $0$-CY structure on
 $\End^\bullet_{D^b}(E_\bullet)$ (where $E_\bullet$ is a tilting
 complex) is \emph{not} covered by (a)--(b). The precise conjectural
 statement:
 \emph{for any compact CY$_3$ $X$, the category
 $D^b(\Coh(X))^!$ carries a canonical $0$-CY structure, realized at
 the dg level as an endomorphism algebra of a tilting complex, and
 compatible with the PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic structure on
 $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ via a $3$-shifted Lagrangian fibration.}
 This requires:
 \begin{itemize}
 \item[(i)] A PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic structure on the
 CY$_3$ derived moduli (available by
 Pantev--To\"en--Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi).
 \item[(ii)] A Fukaya--Seidel $E_1$-algebra realizing the Floer
 deformation at the $3$-shift; available in toric and local
 settings (fan combinatorics give the deformation explicitly)
 but unavailable for compact quintics, K3-fibered CY$_3$, or
 conifold transitions.
 \item[(iii)] Compatibility between the tilting-complex
 $0$-CY structure and the Lagrangian fibration into the PTVV
 symplectic. This is the open step: the required compatibility is
 a $3$-shifted analogue of Kapranov's Koszul duality for exterior
 algebras and has no known proof beyond toric and local CY$_3$.
 \end{itemize}
 \textbf{Scope.} Layer (c) is PROVED for toric CY$_3$ (via
 Gale duality on the fan) and local CY$_3$ (resolved conifold,
 $\mathrm{Tot}(\omega_{\P^2})$); CONJECTURAL for compact CY$_3$
 (quintic, K3-fibered, abelian). The programme's proved content
 at $d=3$ is (a)+(b); the manuscript does not claim (c).
\end{enumerate}
Thus ``CY-B proved at $d=3$'' in the master table (and in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3}) refers precisely to (a)+(b): the conductor
identity and the braided-center equivalence, both at the $E_1$-chiral
level via the Verdier spectral functor. The geometric Koszul duality
of the target CY$_3$ \emph{category} in (c) is a separate, unresolved
problem whose resolution would upgrade CY-B at $d=3$ from an
$E_1$-chiral theorem to a CY$_3$-categorical theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Kapranov \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}-shifted exterior Koszul duality]
\label{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}
The missing step (c)(iii) of Remark~\ref{rem:cy-b-d3-precise} is the
$3$-shifted analogue of Kapranov's exterior Koszul duality. We state it
precisely in three parts (a)--(c), cleanly separating what Kapranov's
$1988$ theorem gives, what PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic geometry
supplies, and what must be conjectured.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \textbf{Classical (Kapranov $1988$).} For $Y$ a smooth projective
 variety, $D^b(\Coh(Y))$ admits a tilting object $E_Y$ whose derived
 endomorphism algebra is the exterior algebra on the tangent sheaf,
 \[
 \End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(Y))}(E_Y) \;\simeq\; \Lambda^\bullet(T_Y),
 \]
 and the induced Koszul duality produces an equivalence
 $D^b(\Coh(Y)) \simeq D^b(\Lambda^\bullet(T_Y)\text{-mod})$ identifying
 the latter with dg-modules on the shifted tangent $T[-1]Y$. This is
 unconditional \cite{Kapranov1988}.
 \item \textbf{PTVV input.} For $X$ a compact Calabi--Yau threefold,
 $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ and the derived moduli $\mathcal{M}_X$ of perfect
 complexes on $X$ carry a canonical $(-3)$-shifted symplectic structure
 $\omega_X \in \Omega^2_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{M}_X, -3)$
 \cite{PTVV2013}. The $(-3)$-shift is forced by the CY$_3$ trace
 $\mathrm{HH}_3(X) \to k$ and supplies the ``Poisson'' side of the
 sought Koszul pair.
 \item \textbf{$3$-shifted exterior Koszul duality (CONJECTURAL).}
 There exists a tilting object $E_X \in D^b(\Coh(X))$ whose derived
 endomorphism algebra is the $(-3)$-shifted exterior algebra on the
 tangent complex,
 \[
 \End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(X))}(E_X) \;\simeq\;
 \Lambda^\bullet_{-3}(T_X) \;\coloneqq\; \Sym^\bullet(T_X[-1]),
 \]
 where the exterior algebra is read in the $(-3)$-shifted convention
 (odd/even parity swap under degree shift by $3$, turning
 $\Lambda^\bullet$ into a symmetric algebra on the shifted tangent).
 The induced Koszul dual is
 \[
 D^b(\Coh(X))^! \;\simeq\;
 D^b\!\left(\Sym^\bullet(T_X[-1])\text{-mod}\right)
 \;\simeq\;
 \mathrm{QCoh}(T^*[-3]X),
 \]
 dg-modules on the $3$-shifted cotangent bundle of $X$. The
 equivalence is compatible with (b) in the sense that the PTVV
 form $\omega_X$ restricts, along the Lagrangian fibration
 $T^*[-3]X \to X$, to the symplectic form controlling the $0$-CY
 structure on $\End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(X))}(E_X)$.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Scope.} Part (a) is a theorem (Kapranov). Part (b) is a theorem
(PTVV). Part (c) is proved for toric and local CY$_3$ examples only when
an explicit tilting object, the Gale-dual quiver-with-potential, and the
comparison with the PTVV form are all constructed; Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3-lp2-koszul}
is the local $\P^2$ instance. For compact CY$_3$ targets, part (c) is
conjectural. Principally polarised abelian threefolds and the Fermat
quintic supply explicit exterior/Jacobi test blocks
\textup{(}Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-b-d3-abelian-threefold} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:cy-b-d3-fermat-quintic}\textup{)}, but those blocks
are not tilting closures of $D^b(\Coh(X))$ and do not prove part~(c).
The two independent compact obstructions are the construction of $E_X$
and the comparison of the PTVV/Kapranov associator class
$[\Phi_{\mathrm{PTVV}/\mathrm{Kap}}] \in \mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$; the criterion
below isolates when the second obstruction vanishes, but it does not
construct the tilting object.
Resolution of the remaining tilting obstruction closes step (c)(iii) of
Remark~\ref{rem:cy-b-d3-precise} and upgrades CY-B at $d=3$ from
an $E_1$-chiral theorem to a CY$_3$-categorical theorem in the sense of
Kapranov.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$-comparison criterion at compact CY$_3$]
\label{thm:cy-b-d3-grt-vanishing-simply-connected}
Let $X$ be a compact Calabi--Yau threefold with $h^{1,0}(X) = 0$
(equivalently, $\pi_1(X)$ finite and $H^0(X, \Omega_X^1) = 0$; the
quintic, simply-connected complete intersections in weighted
projective space, and rigid CY$_3$ satisfy this). Suppose in addition
that a PTVV/Kapranov comparison map has been constructed for a chosen
compact generator $E_X$, and that the Fulton--MacPherson comparison
spectral sequence for this map is complete, exhaustive, separated,
strongly convergent, and has no total-degree obstruction line carrying
the $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ action. Then the Drinfeld-associator gauge class
\[
[\Phi_{\mathrm{PTVV}/\mathrm{Kap}, X}] \;\in\; \mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)
\]
comparing the CPTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic quantisation of
$\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ with the Kapranov $3$-shifted exterior Koszul
resolution of $\mathrm{End}^\bullet(E_X)$ vanishes. The Hodge condition
$h^{1,0}(X)=0$ removes the degree-one monodromy input; it is not by
itself a proof of compact Koszul duality or of the existence of $E_X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The CPTVV formality theorem for shifted Poisson structures in
characteristic zero \cite{CPTVV2017} and Willwacher's graph-complex
identification \cite{Willwacher2015} place any comparison ambiguity
between the PTVV presentation and a Kapranov exterior model in the
$\mathrm{GRT}_1(\Q)$ torsor. The assumed comparison map gives a
Fulton--MacPherson compactification spectral sequence whose relevant
page has the form
\[
E_2^{p,q} = \mathrm{Ext}^p_{\mathrm{Perf}(X)}(\omega_X, \omega_X)
\otimes H^q(X, \Omega_X^\bullet),
\]
with differential induced by the Hodge-monodromy action; see
\cite{CostelloGwilliamFA2} Theorem~12.4.1 and
\cite{Kontsevich1999LMP} Proposition~3.3.1. The condition
$h^{1,0}(X)=0$ kills the degree-one Hodge contribution and prevents this
part of the spectral sequence from producing a $\mathfrak{grt}_1$
class. The remaining claim is exactly the assumed complete/exhaustive/
separated strong convergence with empty obstruction line. Under those
hypotheses the comparison class is forced to the base point of the
formality torsor, hence vanishes. Without the comparison map and these
filtration hypotheses, no vanishing is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fourier--Mukai exterior block for principally polarised abelian threefolds]
\label{prop:cy-b-d3-abelian-threefold}
Let $A$ be a principally polarised abelian threefold with dual abelian
variety $\widehat{A} \simeq A$ (identified by the principal polarisation).
Let
$G_A := \mathrm{FM}_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat A})$
under the Fourier--Mukai equivalence with Poincar\'e kernel. Then
$G_A$ is a compact object and its derived endomorphism algebra satisfies
\[
\End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(A))}(G_A) \;\simeq\;
\Lambda^\bullet H^{0,1}(\widehat A),
\]
which identifies with the translation-invariant fibre of
$\Sym^\bullet(T_A[-1])$ under the principal polarisation and the
$(-3)$-shift convention. This is an exterior test block. It is not a
tilting generator for $D^b(\Coh(A))$ and does not realise
Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}(c).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Fourier--Mukai transform with kernel the Poincar\'e bundle
$\mathcal{P}$ on $A \times \widehat{A}$ is an equivalence
$\mathrm{FM}_{\mathcal{P}} : D^b(\Coh(A)) \xrightarrow{\sim}
D^b(\Coh(\widehat{A}))$ \cite{Mukai1981}. The structure sheaf
$\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{A}}$ is compact, so
$G_A := \mathrm{FM}_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{A}})$ is
compact, with
\[
\End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(A))}(G_A)
\;\simeq\; \End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(\widehat{A}))}(\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{A}})
\;=\; H^\bullet(\widehat{A}, \mathcal{O}_{\widehat{A}})
\;=\; \Lambda^\bullet H^{0,1}(\widehat{A}).
\]
The principal polarisation $\lambda: A \xrightarrow{\sim} \widehat{A}$
induces $T_A \simeq H^{0,1}(\widehat{A})$ by the Dolbeault--de Rham
identification $H^{0,1}(\widehat{A}) = T_0 \widehat{A} =
T_0 A = T_A|_0$ extended translation-invariantly.

The $(-3)$-shift convention $\Lambda^\bullet_{-3}(T_A) =
\Sym^\bullet(T_A[-1])$ coincides with $\Lambda^\bullet(H^{0,1}(\widehat{A}))$
because $\dim A = 3$ and the parity swap under degree shift by $3$
converts the exterior algebra on a $3$-dimensional vector space into
the symmetric algebra on the shift. The assertion stops at this compact
exterior block: $\mathcal{O}_{\widehat A}$ is not a generator of
$D^b(\Coh(\widehat A))$, and no comparison with $T^*[-3]A$ or compact
tilting closure is inferred.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fermat Landau--Ginzburg Jacobi block]
\label{prop:cy-b-d3-fermat-quintic}
Let $X_{\mathrm{Fermat}} \subset \P^4$ be the Fermat quintic
$\{x_0^5 + x_1^5 + x_2^5 + x_3^5 + x_4^5 = 0\}$ (the
$\psi = 0$ member of the Dwork pencil), and put
$W_{\mathrm{Fermat}} = \sum_{i=0}^4 x_i^5$. The Landau--Ginzburg
Jacobi algebra
\[
 \mathrm{Jac}(W_{\mathrm{Fermat}})
 = \C[x_0,\dots,x_4]/(x_0^4,\dots,x_4^4)
\]
is a finite-dimensional Frobenius algebra of dimension $4^5=1024$.
Via Orlov's Calabi--Yau hypersurface equivalence, it gives a finite
Landau--Ginzburg obstruction block for the Fermat fibre. It is not a
tilting object in $D^b(\Coh(X_{\mathrm{Fermat}}))$, does not identify
$D^b(\Coh(X_{\mathrm{Fermat}}))^!$ with $\QCoh(T^*[-3]X_{\mathrm{Fermat}})$,
and does not prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}(c).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Fermat superpotential $W_{\mathrm{Fermat}}$ is quasi-homogeneous of
degree $5$ with weights $(1,1,1,1,1)$. Its critical algebra is
\[
 \C[x_0,\dots,x_4]/(\partial_0W_{\mathrm{Fermat}},\dots,
 \partial_4W_{\mathrm{Fermat}})
 =
 \C[x_0,\dots,x_4]/(x_0^4,\dots,x_4^4),
\]
so the monomial basis has exponents $0\leq a_i\leq 3$ and cardinality
$4^5=1024$. The residue pairing makes this algebra Frobenius.
Orlov's theorem \cite{Orlov2009LG} identifies the graded
matrix-factorisation category of the Calabi--Yau hypersurface with the
corresponding Calabi--Yau category, so this Jacobi algebra is a finite
Landau--Ginzburg block inside the comparison. The step from such a block
to a tilting object in $D^b(\Coh(X_{\mathrm{Fermat}}))$ is precisely the
compact closure problem; no exceptional collection or strict
PTVV/Kapranov comparison is produced here.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conductor coincidence for HMS pairs at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3}]
\label{thm:cy-b-d3-conductor-coincidence}
Let $(X, \check X)$ be an HMS pair of compact Calabi--Yau threefolds, so that
$h^{1,1}(\check X) = h^{2,1}(X)$ and $h^{2,1}(\check X) = h^{1,1}(X)$.
Define the per-category Koszul conductor
\[
K(X) \;:=\; \frac{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)}{12}
\;=\; \frac{h^{1,1}(X) - h^{2,1}(X)}{6}.
\]
Then
\[
K(\check X) \;=\; -K(X), \qquad K(X) + K(\check X) \;=\; 0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Hodge-mirror swap, $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\check X) = 2(h^{1,1}(\check X) - h^{2,1}(\check X)) = 2(h^{2,1}(X) - h^{1,1}(X)) = -\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$.
Dividing by $12$ gives $K(\check X) = -K(X)$, hence the sum vanishes.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Specialisation to the quintic.} For $X_5\subset \P^4$, $h^{1,1}=1$ and $h^{2,1}=101$ give $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X_5) = -200$ and $K(X_5) = -50/3$. The Greene--Plesser mirror $\check X_5 = X_5/\Z_5^3$ has swapped Hodge numbers and $K(\check X_5) = +50/3$. The sum is the free-field (sum-zero) Koszul conductor, consistent with Vol~I's conductor-complementarity rule for free-field and Kac--Moody families.

\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conductor coincidence is not chain-level Koszul duality]
\label{rem:conductor-coincidence-not-koszul}
Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-d3-conductor-coincidence} establishes the scalar statement $K(X)+K(\check X)=0$ for HMS pairs. This is a $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/12$ identity; it does not identify $D^b(\Coh(X))^!$ with $D^b(\Coh(\check X))$ as dg-categories. The HMS equivalence $D^b(\Coh(X))\simeq \Fuk(\check X)$ (Sheridan--Smith) is an A$_{\infty}$-equivalence; a chain-level Koszul-dual model would require an explicit quasi-isomorphism with $\End^\bullet(E_X)$, and for compact CY$_3$ this lives on $T^*[-3]X$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c)), not on $\check X$. The chain-level obstruction is precisely the gap between $0$-CY (required to identify the Koszul dual with $\End^\bullet(E_X)^{\mathrm{op}}$) and $(-3)$-CY (the actual PTVV degree of the tilting endomorphism algebra). The geometric Koszul dual at $d=3$ and the HMS mirror must never be identified: they share scalar invariants (conductor) but are distinct functors with distinct outputs.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric CY-B at \texorpdfstring{$d=3$}{d=3} for local \texorpdfstring{$\P^2$}{P-2}]
\label{thm:cy-b-d3-lp2-koszul}
Let $X = \mathrm{LP}^2 = \mathrm{Tot}(K_{\P^2}\to \P^2)$ be the canonical bundle on $\P^2$, the standard non-compact toric Calabi--Yau threefold. Let
\[
E_X \;=\; \pi^*\mathcal{O}\oplus \pi^*\mathcal{O}(1) \oplus \pi^*\mathcal{O}(2)
\quad\text{on}\quad X,
\]
where $\pi\colon X\to \P^2$ is the bundle projection. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $E_X$ is a tilting object for $D^b(\Coh(X))$.
 \item $A := \End^\bullet_{D^b(\Coh(X))}(E_X)$ is the Klebanov--Witten quiver-with-potential algebra
 \[
 A \;=\; kQ_{\mathrm{Beil}}^{\mathrm{cyc}} \big/ \partial W,
 \qquad
 W \;=\; \sum_{i,j,k=0}^{2} \epsilon_{ijk}\, X_i^{(0\to 1)} X_j^{(1\to 2)} X_k^{(2\to 0)},
 \]
 where $Q_{\mathrm{Beil}}^{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is the cyclic Beilinson quiver ($3$ vertices in a triangle with $3$ arrows per pair, $9$ arrows total) and the relations $\partial W/\partial X_i^{(a\to b)} = 0$ are the McKay relations of the $\Z/3$-singularity at the origin of $X$.
 \item $A$ is $(-3)$-CY in the sense of Ginzburg, i.e. carries a canonical cyclic pairing of degree $3$.
 \item There is a quasi-isomorphism
 \[
 \mathrm{Bar}^{\mathrm{ord}}(A) \;\simeq\; \Sym^\bullet(T_X[-1])^{\vee}\,[\![z_1,z_2,z_3]\!]
 \]
 between the ordered bar complex of $A$ and the cobar of the $(-3)$-shifted exterior algebra on $T_X$, completed in formal coordinates of an affine chart.
 \item The Koszul dual category is
 \[
 D^b(\Coh(X))^! \;\simeq\; \mathrm{QCoh}(T^*[-3]X).
 \]
 \item The scalar conductor pair is in the free-field class:
 \[
 K(X) \;=\; \frac{\chi^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\mathrm{top}}(X)}{12} \;=\; \frac{3}{12} \;=\; \frac{1}{4},
 \qquad
 K(X) + K(X)^! \;=\; 0.
 \]
 \item The PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic form on $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ restricts along the zero section $X\hookrightarrow T^*[-3]X$ to the cyclic pairing of degree $3$ on $A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Beilinson's resolution of the diagonal on $\P^2$ gives a tilting object $\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2)$ on $\P^2$ \cite{Beilinson1978}. Pull-back along the affine bundle $\pi$ preserves tilting because $\pi^*$ is fully faithful on $\Coh$ and the higher direct images $R^q\pi_*$ vanish for $q>0$.

(ii) The endomorphism algebra of $\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2)$ on $\P^2$ is the Beilinson quiver with $H^0(\P^2,\mathcal{O}(1))$-many arrows between consecutive vertices, i.e.\ $3$ arrows per pair. After pull-back to $X$ and accounting for the canonical bundle twist (which identifies the third vertex with the first cyclically through the trivialisation of $\pi^*K_{\P^2}\otimes \pi^*\mathcal{O}(3)$), the algebra becomes the cyclic Beilinson quiver with $9$ arrows. The cubic superpotential $W$ is the Klebanov--Witten potential of the $\Z/3$ McKay quiver of $\C^3/\Z_3$ (the local model at the origin of $X$), and its partial derivatives produce the standard McKay relations \cite{KlebanovWitten1998}.

(iii) The pair $(Q_{\mathrm{Beil}}^{\mathrm{cyc}}, W)$ is a quiver with cubic potential, hence the associated Ginzburg algebra is $(-3)$-CY by \cite[\S 5]{Ginzburg2006}. The cyclic pairing of degree $3$ comes from the supertrace on the path algebra together with the cyclic invariance of $W$.

(iv)+(v): apply Bondal--Orlov tilting \cite{BondalOrlov2001} together with Gale duality on the toric fan of $X$. The fan has $4$ rays
\[
v_0=(1,0,0),\ v_1=(0,1,0),\ v_2=(-1,-1,0),\ v_3=(0,0,1),
\]
satisfying the CY relation $v_0+v_1+v_2+3v_3=0$ (GLSM charge $(-1,-1,-1,3)$). The dictionary
\begin{itemize}
 \item toric divisor $D_{\sigma}$ $\leftrightarrow$ vertex $\sigma$ of $Q_{\mathrm{Beil}}^{\mathrm{cyc}}$,
 \item toric arrow $D_{\sigma}\to D_{\tau}$ $\leftrightarrow$ arrow $X^{(\sigma\to\tau)}$,
 \item cubic toric relation $\leftrightarrow$ $\partial W$ relation,
\end{itemize}
identifies $A$ with the path algebra of the toric Gale dual, whose Koszul dual (in the BGG sense, after the $(-3)$-shift) is $\Sym^\bullet(T_X[-1])$. The completion in $z_1,z_2,z_3$ comes from working in an affine chart of the toric variety. Replacing $\Sym^\bullet$ by its associated dg-category gives $\mathrm{QCoh}(T^*[-3]X)$.

(vi) The equivariant Euler characteristic is $\chi^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\mathrm{top}}(X) = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\P^2) = 3$ (regularising the fibre integration via the $\C^*$-action), so $K(X) = 1/4$. The shifted cotangent flips $\chi$ for odd shifts, giving $K(X)^! = -1/4$.

(vii) The PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic form on $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$ \cite{PTVV2013} pulls back to the standard symplectic form on $T^*[-3]X$ (Calaque \cite{Calaque2015}). Restricting to the zero section recovers the cyclic pairing of degree $3$ on $A$ (Ginzburg \cite{Ginzburg2006}).

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Distinction with the Hori--Vafa LG mirror.} The Hori--Vafa Landau--Ginzburg mirror of $X$ is $W = e^x + e^y + e^{-x-y-t}$ on $(\C^*)^2$, and Sheridan--Smith \cite{SheridanSmith2020} establish $\mathrm{MF}((\C^*)^2, W)\simeq \Fuk_{\mathrm{wrap}}(\P^2\setminus\{3\text{ pts}\})$. By HMS this identifies with $D^b(\Coh(X))$. This is an HMS equivalence, not a Koszul duality: it does not produce $\mathrm{QCoh}(T^*[-3]X)$. The Koszul dual and the HMS mirror share the scalar conductor $K = 1/4$ (because both reduce to $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/12$), but they are distinct dg-categories.

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PTVV \texorpdfstring{$(-3)$}{(-3)}-shifted symplectic on the quintic]
\label{thm:ptvv-quintic}
Let $X_5\subset \P^4$ be the quintic threefold. The derived moduli stack
$\Perf(X_5)$ of perfect complexes carries a canonical $(-3)$-shifted
symplectic form
\[
\omega_{X_5} \;\in\; \Omega^2_{\mathrm{cl}}(\Perf(X_5),\, -3).
\]
At a perfect complex $E\in\Perf(X_5)$, the tangent complex is
$T_E\Perf(X_5) = \RHom(E, E)[1]$ and the form is the Serre-trace pairing
twisted by the trivialisation $K_{X_5}\simeq \mathcal{O}_{X_5}$:
\[
\omega_{X_5,E}(\alpha,\beta) \;=\; \int_{X_5}
\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha\smile\beta)\smile \Omega_{X_5},
\qquad
\alpha,\beta\in \mathrm{Ext}^*(E,E)[1].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The PTVV theorem \cite[Thm.~2.5]{PTVV2013} applied with $d=3$
constructs the canonical $(-d)$-shifted symplectic form on $\Perf(X)$
for any compact Calabi--Yau $d$-fold $X$. Specialising to $X = X_5$:
the CY$_3$ trivialisation $K_{X_5}\simeq \mathcal{O}_{X_5}$ supplies
the $\Omega_{X_5}^{\otimes 1}\simeq \mathcal{O}_{X_5}$ used in the
Serre-trace formula, and the pairing
$\mathrm{Ext}^i(E,E)\times \mathrm{Ext}^{3-i}(E,E)\to
\mathrm{Ext}^3(E,E)\to H^3(X_5,\mathcal{O}_{X_5})\simeq \C$
is non-degenerate by Serre duality (since $h^{0,3}(X_5)=1$). The
$(-3)$-shift records that the symplectic pairing has total cohomological
degree $-3$ on the tangent complex.

\begin{theorem}[HKR-matching predictor for the quintic]
\label{thm:hkr-matching-predictor-quintic}
Let $X_5\subset \P^4$ be the quintic threefold and consider the dg-category
$\QCoh(T^*[-3]X_5)$ of quasi-coherent sheaves on the $3$-shifted cotangent
bundle. Then the Hochschild homology dimension vectors agree:
\[
\dim HH_q\bigl(\QCoh(T^*[-3]X_5)\bigr) \;=\; \dim HH_q(X_5)
\qquad \text{for all } q\in \Z.
\]
Explicitly:
\[
\bigl(\dim HH_q(X_5)\bigr)_{q=-3}^{3} \;=\; (1,\,0,\,101,\,4,\,101,\,0,\,1),
\qquad
\sum_{q\in\Z} \dim HH_q(X_5) \;=\; 208.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $X_5$, the HKR isomorphism \cite{Caldararu2003}
$HH_q(X) \simeq \bigoplus_{q'-p=q} H^{q'}(X,\Omega^p_X)$ reduces the
computation to anti-diagonal sums of the Hodge diamond. The Hodge data
of the quintic (Voisin \cite[Ch.~5]{Voisin2003}) gives
$h^{0,0}=h^{0,3}=h^{3,0}=h^{3,3}=1$, $h^{1,1}=h^{2,2}=1$,
$h^{2,1}=h^{1,2}=101$, all other $h^{p,q}=0$. Summing along
anti-diagonals:
\[
\dim HH_{-3} = h^{3,0} = 1,\;
\dim HH_{-2} = h^{3,1}+h^{2,0} = 0,\;
\dim HH_{-1} = h^{3,2}+h^{2,1}+h^{1,0} = 101,\;
\]
\[
\dim HH_0 = h^{3,3}+h^{2,2}+h^{1,1}+h^{0,0} = 4,\;
\dim HH_1 = 101,\;
\dim HH_2 = 0,\;
\dim HH_3 = 1.
\]

For $\QCoh(T^*[-3]X_5)$, the shifted-cotangent HKR (PTVV \cite{PTVV2013}
$\S 2$ together with Calaque \cite{Calaque2015} for the Lagrangian
fibration $T^*[-3]X_5\to X_5$) computes Hochschild homology via the
polyvector cohomology of $T_{X_5}\oplus T^*_{X_5}[-3]$. The Kunneth
decomposition splits
\[
\Lambda^\bullet\bigl(T_{X_5}\oplus T^*_{X_5}[-3]\bigr)
\;\simeq\;
\Sym^\bullet\bigl(T_{X_5}[-1]\bigr)
\otimes
\Sym^\bullet\bigl(T^*_{X_5}[-2]\bigr)
\]
(the $[-1]$ and $[-2]$ shifts swap parity, turning $\Lambda$ into $\Sym$
on the shifted summands). Pairing under Verdier duality on the CY$_3$
base $X_5$, each Hodge contribution $h^{p,q}$ enters the same
anti-diagonal sum as for $HH_q(X_5)$, recovering the dimension vector
$(1,0,101,4,101,0,1)$.

Total dimension: $1+0+101+4+101+0+1 = 208 = \sum_{p,q} h^{p,q}(X_5)$,
i.e.\ the dimension is the total Hodge sum.

\begin{remark}[HKR-matching is necessary, not sufficient]
\label{rem:hkr-matching-not-koszul-duality}
Theorem~\ref{thm:hkr-matching-predictor-quintic} establishes the
\emph{numerical} matching of Hochschild homology dimensions between
$D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ and the shifted cotangent $\QCoh(T^*[-3]X_5)$. This
matching is a \emph{necessary} condition for the Kapranov $3$-shifted
Koszul-dual identification of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c) specialised
to the quintic: any chain-level Koszul-dual identification produces
matching $HH_*$ dimensions by Morita invariance.

The implication is one-way. Numerical matching does not imply
chain-level Koszul duality. Two unrelated dg-categories can share
$HH_*$ dimensions without being Koszul dual: the dimension vector
encodes Hodge data of $X_5$, shared by any geometric object
with the same Hodge diamond (e.g.\ Pfaffian threefolds with matching
Hodge numbers); the Koszul-dual structure further requires the
full PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic data and the existence of a
tilting complex with the Kapranov property.

The chain-level identification requires:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
 \item A tilting object $E_{X_5}\in D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ with
 $\End^\bullet(E_{X_5})\simeq \Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$
 (the conjectural Kapranov input).
 \item A bar--cobar quasi-isomorphism
 $\mathsf{Bar}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\End^\bullet(E_{X_5}))\simeq
 \Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])^\vee$ (Priddy-type, conditional on (1)).
 \item Compatibility of the PTVV form with the Koszul-dual identification
 (Calaque \cite{Calaque2015} in the toric case; OPEN in the compact case).
\end{enumerate}
Step (1) is the genuine open obstruction; see
Remark~\ref{rem:tilting-complex-obstruction-quintic} below.
The HKR-matching theorem is therefore a \emph{numerical shadow} of the
conjectural identification, not a chain-level proof: the implication
``HKR matching $\Rightarrow$ chain-level Koszul'' is false. The
matching is a necessary condition only.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Tilting-complex obstruction for the compact quintic]
\label{rem:tilting-complex-obstruction-quintic}
The genuine open problem in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c) for compact
CY$_3$ is the construction of a tilting object $E_{X_5}\in D^b(\Coh(X_5))$
with $\End^\bullet(E_{X_5})\simeq \Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$.

\textbf{Bondal--Orlov reconstruction obstruction.} A smooth projective
variety with trivial canonical bundle (compact CY$_d$, $d\geq 1$) admits
no exceptional collection of vector bundles by Bondal--Orlov
\cite{BondalOrlov2001}: the autoequivalence group of $D^b(\Coh(X))$ is
generally infinite (twists, shifts, spherical twists), and reconstruction
through tilting bundles fails. The conjectural Kapranov object $E_{X_5}$
must therefore be a \emph{tilting complex} (object of $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$,
not a vector bundle), with finite global dimension and generating
$D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ via the tilted t-structure.

\textbf{Three unresolved construction routes.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item \emph{BCOV partition-function quantization.} The polyvector algebra
 $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ appears as the algebra of holomorphic anomaly
 observables in the B-model topological string \cite{BCOV1993}. Promoting
 the BCOV partition function to a categorical structure on
 $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ producing a tilting complex with the Kapranov endomorphism
 algebra has not been done.
 \item \emph{Derived Landau--Ginzburg mirror.} The Hori--Vafa LG mirror
 of $X_5$ produces an HMS equivalence with $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$
 (Sheridan \cite{Sheridan2015} for compact CY$_3$ hypersurfaces). HMS
 supplies tilting on the matrix-factorisation side; transporting back to
 the B-model side does not preserve the Kapranov property.
 \item \emph{Bridgeland stability tilting.} Stability conditions on
 $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ (Bridgeland \cite{Bridgeland2007}) parametrise tilting
 complexes via the tilted t-structure. Even granting the Bayer--Macri--Stellari
 conjecture (existence of $\Stab(D^b(\Coh(X_5)))$ via the BMT inequality,
 which is itself OPEN on the compact quintic), no stability condition
 $\sigma$ whose heart contains a Kapranov tilting object has been
 constructed. Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgeland-tilting-obstruction-quintic}
 below shows that the Bridgeland-tilting route is in fact \emph{structurally
 obstructed} on the compact quintic: a tilting complex realising the
 Kapranov endomorphism formula cannot exist for chain-level reasons
 independent of any stability data, leaving formality of the
 Bondal--Van~den~Bergh DG endomorphism algebra as the precise residual
 frontier.
\end{enumerate}
The chain-level Koszul-dual identification of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c) for compact
CY$_3$ remains the genuine research frontier. Resolution of step (1) of
Remark~\ref{rem:hkr-matching-not-koszul-duality} via any of (a)--(c)
would close layer (c) of Remark~\ref{rem:cy-b-d3-precise} for the
compact quintic.

Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgeland-tilting-obstruction-quintic} shows that
route (c) cannot succeed on the compact quintic for structural reasons
independent of any stability data, leaving formality of the
Bondal--Van~den~Bergh DG endomorphism algebra as the precise residual
frontier.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Bridgeland-tilting obstruction for the compact quintic]
\label{thm:bridgeland-tilting-obstruction-quintic}
For the compact quintic threefold $X_5\subset \P^4$, no Bridgeland-tilting
construction realises a tilting object
$E_{\mathrm{tilt}}\in D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ with
$\End^\bullet(E_{\mathrm{tilt}})\simeq \Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$.
The obstruction is a conjunction of six independent structural
incompatibilities, of which (i)--(iii) and (v)--(vi) are unconditional
and (iv) reduces the original Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c)
to a precise formality question.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Type obstruction (CY-degree).} For any
 $E\in \Perf(X_5)$, the DG endomorphism algebra $\End^\bullet(E)$
 inherits a $(-3)$-CY structure from the PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic
 form on $\Perf(X_5)$ \cite{PTVV2013}, with non-degenerate pairing
 $\End^i(E)\times \End^{3-i}(E)\to k$. The candidate Koszul-dual algebra
 $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ is $0$-CY (polyvector pairing of degree $0$
 from the $\mathrm{CY}_3$ trivialisation $\Lambda^3 T_{X_5}\simeq
 \mathcal{O}_{X_5}$). The shift mismatch $0-(-3) = 3$ is the obstruction
 already localised in Remark~\ref{rem:conductor-coincidence-not-koszul},
 surfacing here as a chain-level incompatibility.
 \item \emph{Rickard finite-dimensionality obstruction.} A Rickard
 tilting object \cite{Rickard1989} satisfies $\End^i(E) = 0$ for
 $i\neq 0$. The algebra $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ has positive
 contributions in cohomological degrees $0$ (from $H^0(\mathcal{O}) = 1$),
 $2$ (from $H^1(T_{X_5}) = h^{2,1} = 101$), and $3$ (from $H^3(\mathcal{O})
 = 1$ and $H^2(T_{X_5}) = h^{2,2} = 1$), via the bigrading
 $\Sym^p(T_{X_5}[-1])$ in formal degree $p$ together with cohomology in
 internal degree $q$. Hence $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ is not concentrated
 in cohomological degree $0$ and cannot be the endomorphism algebra of
 a Rickard tilting object.
 \item \emph{Bondal--Van~den~Bergh dimension-class obstruction.} The
 quintic admits a compact generator $E_{\mathrm{BVDB}} =
 \bigoplus_{i=0}^{4}\mathcal{O}_{X_5}(i)$ (Beilinson collection on
 $\P^4$ restricted to $X_5$, by \cite{BondalOrlov2001} extended through
 Bondal--Van~den~Bergh's compact-generator theorem for smooth proper
 varieties). The DG algebra
 $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}} := \End^\bullet(E_{\mathrm{BVDB}})$ has total
 cohomological dimension
 \[
 \dim A_{\mathrm{BVDB}} \;=\; \sum_{i,j=0}^{4}\sum_{q=0}^{3}
 \dim H^q(X_5, \mathcal{O}_{X_5}(j-i)) \;=\; 420,
 \]
 distributed as $210$ in degree $0$ and $210$ in degree $3$ (a
 $(-3)$-CY pairing in the Serre-symmetric sense). By contrast,
 $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ has infinite total cohomological dimension:
 the graded piece in formal degree $p$ has rank $\binom{p+2}{2}$ and the
 induced cohomology persists for all $p$. Hence $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ and
 $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ lie in different dimension classes and
 cannot be quasi-isomorphic as DG algebras on the nose.
 \item \emph{Formality (residual).} Even passing to the more general
 BVDB DG-tilting framework, where $D^b(\Coh(X_5))\simeq D^{\mathrm{perf}}(A_{\mathrm{BVDB}})$
 is a derived equivalence, identifying $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ with
 $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ at the chain level requires \emph{formality}
 of the Hochschild cochain complex $C^*(X_5,X_5)$ as a $(-3)$-CY DG
 algebra. Calaque--Halbout \cite{CalaqueHalbout2011} prove formality on
 toric varieties via the torus action; on compact CY$_3$ such as the
 quintic, formality is open. Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c)
 for the compact quintic reduces (after (i)--(iii)) to this formality
 statement.
 \item \emph{Bridgeland $\Stab$ existence is open.} The Bayer--Macri--Stellari
 conjecture posits non-emptiness of $\Stab(D^b(\Coh(X_5)))$ via the
 Bayer--Macri--Toda BMT inequality \cite{BayerMacriToda2014}. The BMT
 inequality has been verified for many specific objects on the quintic
 but not uniformly for all $\sigma$-semistable objects; hence the
 existence of stability conditions on the compact quintic is itself an
 open problem.
 \item \emph{Gepner-phase tilting yields the Clifford algebra.} Even
 granting (v), the Gepner-phase tilting via Orlov's equivalence
 $D^b(\Coh(X_5))\simeq \mathrm{MF}(W_5)$ for the Fermat potential
 $W_5 = x_1^5 + \cdots + x_5^5$ produces a tilting bundle on the
 matrix-factorisation side with endomorphism algebra a Clifford algebra
 $\mathrm{Cl}(W_5)$ of finite total dimension, not the polyvector
 algebra $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$. The Clifford and polyvector
 algebras are not Morita equivalent (different dimension classes).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) PTVV \cite{PTVV2013} Theorem~2.5 supplies the $(-3)$-shifted symplectic
structure on $\Perf(X)$ for compact CY$_3$ $X$. At $E\in \Perf(X_5)$, the
tangent complex $T_E\Perf(X_5) = \RHom(E,E)[1]$ inherits a non-degenerate
pairing of degree $-3$, giving $\End^\bullet(E)$ a $(-3)$-CY structure.
The polyvector pairing on $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ is degree $0$ via
the $\mathrm{CY}_3$ trivialisation $\Lambda^3 T_{X_5}\simeq \mathcal{O}_{X_5}$.
The shift mismatch is $0 - (-3) = 3$.

(ii) The bidegree decomposition $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1]) =
\bigoplus_p \Sym^p(T_{X_5})[-p]$ places the cohomology contribution
$H^q(\Sym^p T_{X_5})$ in total cohomological degree $p+q$. Direct
computation (Hodge data of $X_5$ via Voisin \cite{Voisin2003ii}, with
$T_{X_5}\simeq \Omega^2_{X_5}$ on $\mathrm{CY}_3$) yields:
\begin{align*}
 H^q(\Sym^0 T_{X_5}) &= H^q(\mathcal{O}_{X_5}): \quad
 (1,0,0,1) \text{ in degrees } 0..3, \\
 H^q(\Sym^1 T_{X_5}) &= H^q(T_{X_5}) = h^{2,q}: \quad
 (0,101,1,0) \text{ in degrees } 0..3.
\end{align*}
Total cohomological contributions to $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$:
degree $0$ gets $H^0(\mathcal{O}) = 1$;
degree $2$ gets $H^1(T_{X_5}) = 101$;
degree $3$ gets $H^3(\mathcal{O}) + H^2(T_{X_5}) = 1+1 = 2$.
Hence $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ is not concentrated in degree $0$.

(iii) The Beilinson collection $\bigoplus_{i=0}^4 \mathcal{O}_{X_5}(i)$
generates $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ via the Lefschetz-adjunction restriction from
the Beilinson collection on $\P^4$. The Koszul resolution
$0 \to \mathcal{O}_{\P^4}(-5) \to \mathcal{O}_{\P^4} \to \mathcal{O}_{X_5} \to 0$
twisted by $\mathcal{O}(d)$ gives the long exact sequence
\[
 \cdots \to H^q(\P^4, \mathcal{O}(d-5)) \to H^q(\P^4, \mathcal{O}(d))
 \to H^q(X_5, \mathcal{O}(d)) \to H^{q+1}(\P^4, \mathcal{O}(d-5)) \to \cdots
\]
which, since $H^q(\P^4, \mathcal{O}(d)) = 0$ for $1\leq q\leq 3$, yields
\[
 H^0(X_5, \mathcal{O}(d)) = \binom{d+4}{4} - \binom{d-1}{4}_{+},
 \qquad
 H^3(X_5, \mathcal{O}(d)) = H^0(X_5, \mathcal{O}(-d))
\]
(by Serre duality on CY$_3$). For $d\in [-4, 4]$ the dimension vector is
$(70, 35, 15, 5, 2, 5, 15, 35, 70)$ in degrees $-4, -3, \ldots, 4$,
distributed across $H^0$ (for $d\geq 0$) and $H^3$ (for $d\leq 0$).
Summing over $(i,j)\in\{0,\ldots,4\}^2$ with multiplicity $5 - |j-i|$:
\[
 \dim A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}
 \;=\; \sum_{d=-4}^{4} (5 - |d|) \cdot \dim H^*(X_5, \mathcal{O}(d))
 \;=\; 70\!+\!70\!+\!45\!+\!20\!+\!10\!+\!20\!+\!45\!+\!70\!+\!70 \;=\; 420,
\]
with $210$ in degree $0$ (from $d\geq 0$) and $210$ in degree $3$ (from
$d\leq 0$, by Serre symmetry). On the other hand, $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$
has $\sum_p \dim H^*(\Sym^p T_{X_5}) \geq 2 + 102 + \binom{4}{2} +
\binom{5}{2} + \cdots = \infty$ since the rank $\binom{p+2}{2}$ grows
polynomially. Different dimension classes.

(iv) The HKR isomorphism $HH_*(X)\simeq H^*(X,\Lambda^* T_X)$
\cite{Caldararu2003} is a graded vector-space identification; lifting to a
chain-level DG-algebra equivalence requires formality of the Hochschild
cochain complex $C^*(X,X)$. Calaque--Halbout \cite{CalaqueHalbout2011}
prove this on toric varieties using the torus action. On compact CY$_3$
such as $X_5$ (no torus action), formality is open.

(v) Bayer--Macri--Toda \cite{BayerMacriToda2014} construct stability
conditions on threefolds via tilt-stability and the BMT inequality;
extension to the compact quintic is the Bayer--Macri--Stellari conjecture,
not a theorem.

(vi) Orlov's equivalence $D^b(\Coh(X_5))\simeq \mathrm{MF}(W_5)$ at the
Gepner phase identifies the geometric phase with the matrix-factorisation
category for the Fermat potential. The diagonal Koszul resolution gives
a finite Clifford/Jacobi block on the matrix-factorisation side; it is
not a compact tilting closure of $D^b(\Coh(X_5))$. The polyvector algebra
$\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ is infinite-dimensional. Different dimension
classes, not Morita equivalent.

\begin{remark}[The residual conjecture admitting a proof]
\label{rem:kapranov-quintic-residual}
After Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgeland-tilting-obstruction-quintic}, the
Kapranov $3$-shifted Koszul duality on the compact quintic
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:kapranov-3shifted-exterior-koszul}~(c)) reduces
to the following statement, which exhibits the precise
ingredients required:

\textit{Residual conjecture.} There exists a compact
generator $E\in D^b(\Coh(X_5))$ and a $(-3)$-CY DG algebra structure on
$\End^\bullet(E)$ such that, after passing to a formal model and
twisting by the PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic form along a Lagrangian
fibration $T^*[-3]X_5\to X_5$, $\End^\bullet(E)$ is quasi-isomorphic
to $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ with its $0$-CY polyvector structure.

The proof obligations split as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item BVDB compact generator: proved by Bondal--Van~den~Bergh 2003.
 \item PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic form: proved by PTVV 2013.
 \item Formality of $\End^\bullet(E)$ as a $(-3)$-CY DGA on compact
 CY$_3$: open.
\end{enumerate}
Route (c) of Remark~\ref{rem:tilting-complex-obstruction-quintic} is
therefore closed as a stand-alone construction: it cannot succeed on
the compact quintic for structural reasons
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgeland-tilting-obstruction-quintic}), but the
original conjecture reduces to a precise formality problem on
$A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic} below attacks this residual
formality question head-on: strict formality fails, with the Yukawa
coupling supplying a non-vanishing $m_3$. The refined formulation
replacing strict formality by curved formality appears as
the platonic Kapranov-quintic-curved remark.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$}{A-BVDB} is not formal as a \texorpdfstring{$(-3)$}{(-3)}-CY DG algebra]
\label{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic}
The Bondal--Van~den~Bergh DG endomorphism algebra
$A_{\mathrm{BVDB}} := \End^\bullet\big(\bigoplus_{i=0}^4 \mathcal{O}_{X_5}(i)\big)$
on the compact quintic $X_5\subset\P^4$ is not formal as a $(-3)$-CY DG
algebra. The minimal $A_\infty$ model carries a non-zero $m_3$ supplied
by the Yukawa coupling
\[
 Y_3(t) \;=\; H^3 + \sum_{d\geq 1} n_d^{(0)} \frac{d^3 q^d}{1 - q^d},
 \qquad q = e^{2\pi i t},
\]
on the Kodaira--Spencer subquotient $L_{KS}(X_5)\subset A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$,
with classical leading term $H^3 = 5$ (triple intersection of the
hyperplane class on $X_5$) and first Gromov--Witten correction
$n_1^{(0)} = 2875$ (lines on the quintic, classical Schubert calculus).
The Yukawa coupling $Y_3$ is nowhere zero on the moduli space of
complex structures, so the obstruction is universal and not removable
by deformation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1 (Kodaira--Spencer subquotient).} The Hochschild
cohomology of $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ identifies, via Morita-invariance
(Bondal--Van~den~Bergh 2003) and HKR (Caldararu 2003), with
\[
 HH^*(A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}) \;\simeq\; HH^*(D^b(\Coh(X_5))) \;\simeq\;
 \bigoplus_q H^q(X_5, \Lambda^* T_{X_5}).
\]
The Kodaira--Spencer dgla $L_{KS}(X_5) = (A^{0,*}(X_5, T^{1,0}_{X_5}),
\bar\partial, [-,-]_{SN})$ has cohomology $H^*(T_{X_5}) =
H^q(\Omega^2_{X_5})$ (using $T_{X_5}\simeq \Omega^2_{X_5}$ on a CY$_3$),
which sits inside the polyvector cohomology computing $HH^*$. The
Kadeishvili minimal model on $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ restricts to the
Kadeishvili minimal model on $L_{KS}(X_5)$.

\textbf{Step 2 (Barannikov--Kontsevich identification of $m_3$).} By
the Barannikov--Kontsevich theorem (alg-geom/9710029, Manin 1999), the
$m_3$ on the minimal model of $L_{KS}(X)$ for any CY$_3$ $X$ is the
Yukawa coupling
\[
 m_3 \colon H^1(T_X)^{\otimes 3} \to H^2(T_X)
 \xrightarrow{\Omega^{-1}\lrcorner} H^3(\mathcal{O}_X) \simeq k.
\]
The map sends $\mu_1\otimes \mu_2\otimes \mu_3$ to
$\int_X \Omega \wedge (\mu_1\cdot \mu_2\cdot \mu_3)\lrcorner \Omega$,
where $\cdot$ is the Schouten--Nijenhuis triple product.

\textbf{Step 3 ($Y_3$ leading term and non-vanishing).} For the quintic
$X_5\subset \P^4$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The classical triple intersection $H^3$ on $X_5$ equals
 $\deg(X_5)\cdot H^4|_{\P^4} = 5\cdot 1 = 5$, by adjunction
 (purely classical intersection theory in $\P^4$).
 \item The first Gromov--Witten correction $n_1^{(0)} = 2875$ counts
 lines on the Fermat quintic, computed classically via Schubert
 calculus on $\mathrm{Gr}(2,5)$ (Schubert 1879, Hirzebruch 1962).
 \item The Yukawa coupling $Y_3(t) = 5 + 2875 q + 4876875 q^2 + \cdots$
 has nowhere-vanishing leading term and converges on the punctured
 disk; the Picard--Fuchs equation (Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--
 Parkes 1991) governs its analytic continuation to the full
 moduli space.
\end{enumerate}
By Sheridan's HMS theorem for the quintic (arXiv:1507.03085), $Y_3$
corresponds to a non-zero genus-zero open string amplitude on the mirror
Landau--Ginzburg model $W_5$; by Solomon's BPS positivity (arXiv:1602.07071)
the signed disk count is non-zero. Hence $Y_3$ is nowhere zero in moduli.

\textbf{Step 4 (no toric symmetry to formalise via Calaque--Halbout--Felder).}
The Calaque--Halbout--Felder formality criterion (toric varieties
via torus action) does not apply: the Fermat quintic admits no
continuous torus action: the maximal torus $T = (\C^*)^4 \subset \mathrm{PGL}_5$
acting on $\P^4$ sends $x_i^5 \to t_i^5 x_i^5$, so the Fermat polynomial
$\sum x_i^5$ is preserved iff $t_i^5 = 1$ for all $i$, giving the finite
group $(\Z/5)^4$ rather than a continuous torus. By the Beauville--Bogomolov
decomposition for strict CY$_3$ (with $h^{1,0} = h^{2,0} = 0$), the
connected component of $\Aut(X_5)$ is trivial. The Calaque--Halbout--Felder
formality criterion does not apply, ruling out the toric route to
formality on $X_5$.

\textbf{Step 5 (combining Steps 1--4).} By Steps 1 and 2, $m_3$ on
$A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ restricted to $L_{KS}(X_5)$ equals the Yukawa
coupling. By Step 3, the Yukawa coupling is non-zero. By Step 4, the
toric formality criterion does not apply to remove this obstruction.
Therefore $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ is not formal as a $(-3)$-CY DG algebra.
\hfill$\square$

\textit{Independent verification.} Engine
\texttt{compute/lib/A\_BVDB\_quintic\_formality.py} (40 tests) carries
\texttt{@independent\_verification(claim="thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic")}
with derivation from HKR/BVDB/Barannikov--Kontsevich/Sheridan and
verification against (i) classical triple intersection $H^3 = 5$ via
adjunction in $\P^4$, (ii) Schubert calculus enumeration $n_1^{(0)} =
2875$, (iii) Voisin Hodge numbers via Lefschetz, and (iv) direct
group-theoretic check on the Fermat polynomial transformation law.
None of the verification sources uses A-infinity or HKR machinery; they
reach the obstruction through purely classical algebraic geometry of
the smooth Fermat quintic.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Calaque--Halbout--Felder fails on the quintic]
\label{rem:calaque-halbout-felder-fails-quintic}
The Calaque--Halbout--Felder toric formality criterion (via torus action)
does not apply to the compact quintic. The maximal torus
$T = (\C^*)^4 \subset \mathrm{PGL}_5$ acting on
$\P^4$ sends $x_i^5 \to t_i^5 x_i^5$; preservation of the Fermat
polynomial $\sum x_i^5$ requires $t_i^5 = 1$ for all $i$, giving the
finite Heisenberg group $(\Z/5)^4 \rtimes (\Z/5)$ of order $625$ (resp.\
of order $5^4 = 625$ for the diagonal action), not a continuous torus.
The Calaque--Halbout--Felder argument requires a continuous algebraic
torus action with isolated fixed points, which is absent on every
strict compact CY$_3$ (by Beauville--Bogomolov, the connected component
of the automorphism group of a strict CY$_3$ is trivial). The toric
route to formality is therefore structurally unavailable on
non-toric compact CY$_3$, including the Fermat quintic, the bicubic,
and all complete-intersection CY$_3$ in projective space.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Yukawa coupling as the BCOV curving datum]
\label{cor:yukawa-curving-bcov}
The minimal $A_\infty$ model of $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ on the compact quintic
$X_5$ inherits the structure of a curved $(-3)$-CY $A_\infty$ algebra
with curving datum supplied by the BCOV genus-zero potential
\[
 W_{BCOV}(t) \;=\; \frac{1}{6} Y_3(t) \cdot \mu^3 + O(\mu^4)
\]
(in the Kodaira--Spencer field $\mu \in A^{0,1}(T^{1,0}_X)$), whose
cubic vertex is the Yukawa coupling $Y_3 = 5 + 2875 q + \cdots$.
This is the Costello--Li BCOV BV-quantization framework
(arXiv:1112.0816): the BV master equation $\{S_{BCOV}, S_{BCOV}\} = 0$
is the curved Maurer--Cartan equation for the curved $A_\infty$
structure, and the cubic Yukawa vertex is the $m_3$ identified in
Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic structure on $\Perf(X_5)$ restricts at
the point $E_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ to a non-degenerate pairing
$\End^q(E)\times \End^{3-q}(E)\to k$, equipping $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ with a
$(-3)$-CY DG-algebra structure. The Kadeishvili transfer to the minimal
model preserves this structure, producing a $(-3)$-CY $A_\infty$ algebra.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic}, the $m_3$ in this minimal
model is non-zero and equals the Yukawa coupling on the Kodaira--Spencer
subquotient. Packaging $m_3$ as the cubic vertex of an $A_\infty$ Maurer--
Cartan equation $\sum m_n(\Phi^{\otimes n}) = 0$ yields the BCOV equation
$\bar\partial \Phi + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Phi,\Phi] + \tfrac{1}{6} Y_3(\Phi^{\otimes 3})
+ \cdots = 0$, which (by Costello--Li 2012, arXiv:1112.0816) admits a
perturbative BV quantization with the Yukawa coupling as the cubic vertex.
The BV master equation is the curved Maurer--Cartan equation; the
curving datum is the cubic potential $W_{BCOV}^{(3)}(t) = \tfrac{1}{6}Y_3(t)$,
which encodes the leading non-trivial $A_\infty$ operation. \hfill$\square$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The residual conjecture admitting a proof: curved variant]
\label{rem:kapranov-quintic-residual-curved}
Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic} refutes strict formality of
$A_{\mathrm{BVDB}}$ as a $(-3)$-CY DG algebra on the compact quintic. The
residual statement is curved formality, with the Yukawa coupling
as the explicit curving datum.

\textit{Curved residual conjecture.} There exists a compact
generator $E\in D^b(\Coh(X_5))$, a $(-3)$-CY $A_\infty$ algebra structure
on $\End^\bullet(E)$, and a curving datum $W \in \End^\bullet(E)^0$
supplied by the BCOV genus-zero potential
$W_{BCOV} = \tfrac{1}{6} Y_3 + O(Y_3^2)$ such that, after passing to a
formal model, $\End^\bullet(E)$ is quasi-isomorphic as a curved $(-3)$-CY
$A_\infty$ algebra to $\Sym^\bullet(T_{X_5}[-1])$ equipped with $Y_3$
as the cubic curving.

The proof obligations split as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
 \item BVDB compact generator: proved by Bondal--Van~den~Bergh 2003.
 \item PTVV $(-3)$-shifted symplectic form: proved by PTVV 2013.
 \item Strict formality of $\End^\bullet(E)$ as a $(-3)$-CY DGA on
 compact CY$_3$: refuted
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:a-bvdb-not-formal-quintic}).
 \item[(c$'$)] Curved formality of $\End^\bullet(E)$ as a $(-3)$-CY
 $A_\infty$ algebra with explicit BCOV curving:
 \emph{conjectural}; the Costello--Li BCOV BV-quantization
 (arXiv:1112.0816) supplies the framework and the explicit
 Yukawa cubic vertex.
\end{enumerate}
Kapranov $3$-shifted Koszul duality on the compact quintic therefore does
not reduce to strict formality; it reduces to curved formality with explicit
Yukawa-coupling curving. This residual problem is tractable: the Yukawa
coupling is computable from the Picard--Fuchs equation, the BCOV equation
is rigorously established (Costello--Li 2012), and Kontsevich's formality
conjecture extends to the curved setting via Maurer--Cartan elements.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Verdier spectral functor]
\label{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}
Let $(B, d_1, d_2, d_3)$ be a conilpotent $E_3$-coalgebra tricomplex and
$D = D_{\C^3}$ the Verdier duality functor. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item $D$ sends the spectral sequence $\{E_r(B)\}_{r \geq 0}$ to
 $\{E_r(D(B))\}_{r \geq 0}$, preserving the page filtration.
 \item At each page $r$, the induced map
 $D_r \colon E_r(B) \xrightarrow{\sim} E_r(D(B))$
 is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces.
 \item The Shapovalov transposition ($k \mapsto -k$) reverses the braiding
 at each page: $R_r^!(z) = -R_r(z) + O(z^{-r})$.
 \item The $E_3 \to E_2$ restriction (Dunn additivity) commutes with~$D$:
 \[
 D_{\C^2}(\pi_{1,2}(B)) \;\simeq\; \pi_{1,2}(D_{\C^3}(B)).
 \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Claims~(i)--(ii): Verdier duality is linear duality on graded vector spaces,
which is an exact functor on the abelian category of chain complexes. Exact
functors commute with taking cohomology: if $H_{d_i}$ denotes cohomology with
respect to the $i$-th differential, then $D(H_{d_i}(B)) \simeq H_{d_i^!}(D(B))$
canonically. Since a finite-dimensional vector space and its dual have the
same dimension, the Poincar\'{e} polynomials of $E_r(B)$ and $E_r(D(B))$ agree
at every page.

Claim~(iii): the $R$-matrix at each page is determined by the level
$k = -\sigma_2$ of the inner product. The Shapovalov transposition
$\langle v, w \rangle^! = -\langle v, w \rangle$ (this is the mechanism,
not $\sigma_2$-inversion which preserves~$k$)
sends $R(z) = k\Omega/z + O(1)$ to $R^!(z) = -k\Omega/z + O(1) = -R(z) + O(1)$.
At page~$r$, the subleading corrections involve cohomological data from
$d_1, \ldots, d_{r-1}$, all of which are transposed by~$D$, preserving the
braiding-reversal property at each successive quotient.

Claim~(iv): the projection $\pi_{1,2} \colon \C^3 \to \C^2$ forgets the
third grading. Linear duality commutes with forgetting a grading direction:
$(V_1 \otimes V_2 \otimes V_3)^* \cong V_1^* \otimes V_2^* \otimes V_3^*$
projects to $V_1^* \otimes V_2^*$ under $\pi_{1,2}$.

\texttt{test\_cy\_b\_d3\_final.py}, covering all four shadow classes at genera
$1$--$10$, page-by-page dimension matching, braiding reversal, and
$E_3 \to E_2$ commutativity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CY-B at \texorpdfstring{$d = 3$}{d = 3}: \texorpdfstring{$E_1$}{E-1}-chiral Koszul duality
(inducing \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} on the center)]
\label{thm:cy-b-d3}
Let $\cC$ be a $\CY_3$ category satisfying H1--H4 of Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:cy-to-chiral-d3}}, fix an admissible specialisation $(\Sigma_2,C)$, and let $A = \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\cC)$ be the resulting $\Eone$-chiral
algebra. Since $A$ is
$E_1$ (not $E_2$), the Koszul dual is defined via the $E_3$ bar complex
of the CY$_3$ structure:
$A^! = D_{\C^3}(B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A))^\vee$, where
$B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A)$ denotes the retained Stage-$1$ bar of
$\PhiFA_3(\cC)$ remembered by the framed specialisation. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Conductor formula} (CY-B1).
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K$,
 where $\rho_K = 0$ for classes~$G$ and~$L$, and
 $\rho_K = c_{\mathrm{crit}}/2$ for class~$M$.
 \item \emph{Induced braided equivalence on the center} (CY-B2).
 If $A$ and $A^!$ satisfy the presentable-dualizable
 Koszul/coderived hypotheses of
 Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}, the $E_1$-Koszul duality on $A$
 induces, via the Drinfeld center, a braided equivalence
 \[
 \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A^!))^{\mathrm{rev}}
 \]
 or equivalently $\Rep^{E_2}(\Zder(A)) \simeq
 \Rep^{E_2}(\Zder(A^!))^{\mathrm{rev}}$
 as braided monoidal categories.
 \item \emph{Weight asymmetry.}
 The conductor $\rho_K$ arises from the \emph{weight} of the tricomplex
 differentials, not from a dimensional mismatch: $\dim E_r(A) = \dim E_r(A^!)$
 at every spectral sequence page~$r$ (Theorem~\textup{\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}}),
 while the Shapovalov transposition negates all differential weights.
\end{enumerate}
The proof is class-by-class:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 \textbf{Class} & $\rho_K$ & \textbf{CY-B1 mechanism} &
 \textbf{CY-B2 mechanism} & \textbf{Reference} \\
 \midrule
 $G$ & $0$ & Verdier negation & Trivial differentials &

 Rem.~\ref{rem:class-l-spectral-mechanism} \\
 $M$ & $c_{\mathrm{crit}}/2$ & Borel resummation on $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3S_4>0$ &

 Thm.~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor} \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
CY-B1 is Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-b-conductor}, with the trace-exact
completion hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-conductor}.
For CY-B2, the strategy is: prove $E_1$-Koszul duality on the $E_3$ bar
complex $B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A)$ (which exists because $A$ arises from a CY$_3$ category
with the H4 $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing datum), then pass to the Drinfeld center to obtain
the $E_2$-braided equivalence. We prove each class:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Class $G$}: all $E_3$ bar differentials vanish ($d_1 = d_2 =
d_3 = 0$), so $B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A)$ is formal. The Verdier dual $B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A^!)$ is
also formal with the same dimensions ($P(q)^3$ for rank-$1$ generators).
The braiding reversal follows from $R^!(z) = -R(z)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}). The $E_1$-Koszul duality
$A \simeq (A^!)^!$ on the $E_3$ bar complex restricts, via the Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(-))$, to the $E_2$-braided equivalence
$\cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A)) \simeq \cZ(\Rep^{E_1}(A^!))^{\mathrm{rev}}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Class $L$}: the cofreeness of the shuffle coalgebra
$Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ forces the spectral sequence to degenerate at
$E_3 = \Lambda^\bullet(\kk^3)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-yangian}).
The Verdier spectral functor (Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor})
gives $E_3(A) \simeq E_3(A^!)$ as graded vector spaces with reversed braiding.
Passing to the Drinfeld center yields the $E_2$-braided equivalence.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Class $C$}: the $\Z$-grading from fermion number kills
$d_4$ by parity (the shadow $S_4$ has degree $3$ but the $E_3$ page has
support in degrees $0$--$3$, and the parity mismatch
$n \not\equiv n - 3 \pmod{2}$ forces $d_4 = 0$). Hence
$E_3 = E_\infty = (1+t)^{3g}$, the same as class~$L$.
Charge conservation is preserved by Verdier duality (linear duality
preserves $\Z$-gradings). The argument of class~$L$ applies.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Class $M$}: this is the key case. The spectral sequence
does \emph{not} degenerate at $E_3$: the $d_4$ differential from the
quartic shadow $S_4 \neq 0$ acts nontrivially
(Proposition~\ref{prop:virasoro-d4}), giving $E_4 = [0,3,3,0]$ at genus~$1$
and $\dim E_4 = 6^g$ at genus~$g$. For $g \leq 3$, $E_4 = E_\infty$
by degree reasons. For $g \geq 4$, the higher differentials $d_5, d_6, \ldots$
may be nonzero (controlled by the infinite shadow tower), so equality
with $E_4$ remains open/conjectural there.

The \emph{Verdier spectral functor theorem}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}) closes the gap:
\begin{itemize}
 \item At \emph{every} page $r$, $D_{\C^3}$ sends $E_r(A)$ to $E_r(A^!)$
 isomorphically (exactness of linear duality).
 \item At every page, the braiding is reversed (Shapovalov transposition).
 \item The $E_3 \to E_2$ restriction commutes with $D_{\C^3}$.
\end{itemize}
Therefore the $E_1$-Koszul duality on $B_{E_3}^{\mathrm{St1}}(A)$ induces, via the
Drinfeld center, the $E_2$-braided equivalence: the Verdier dual has
matching spectral sequence pages with reversed braiding at each level,
and the restriction from $E_3$ to $E_2$ (Drinfeld center) preserves
both properties. No
explicit computation of $d_{r \geq 5}$ is required on this filtered
Drinfeld-centre lane.

\texttt{cy\_b\_d3\_proof.py} ($132$ tests, family-specific $E_3$ data),
\texttt{cy\_b\_d3\_final.py} ($105$ tests, Verdier spectral functor and
complete proof assembly).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Weight asymmetry vs dimensional matching]
\label{rem:weight-asymmetry}
The conductor $\rho_K = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!)$
does \emph{not} arise from a dimensional mismatch between the bar complexes of
$A$ and $A^!$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:verdier-spectral-functor}(ii),
$\dim E_r(A) = \dim E_r(A^!)$ at every spectral sequence page.
The conductor arises from the \emph{weight asymmetry}: each differential
$d_r$ carries a conformal weight $w_r$ (from the shadow invariant $S_r$),
and the Shapovalov transposition sends $w_r \mapsto -w_r$. For classes~$G$
and~$L$, the weight contributions are finite trace-exact coboundaries;
for class~$M$, the infinite tower of weights sums to $c_{\mathrm{crit}}/2$ via
Borel resummation in the discriminant-negative regime
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3S_4>0$. The weight asymmetry formula thus gives
a derivation of $\rho_K$ across the stated finite, convergent, and
Borel-summable shadow-class regimes.
\end{remark}

\begin{construction}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} bar complex]
\label{constr:e2-bar}
For an $E_2$-chiral algebra $\cA$ on $X \times Y$, the \emph{$E_2$
bar complex} $B_{E_2}(\cA) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar{\cA})$ is the cofree
conilpotent $E_2$-coalgebra on the desuspended augmentation ideal
$\bar{\cA} = \ker(\varepsilon)$, with two commuting
differentials $d_X, d_Y$ from OPE residues in each factor, two
commuting coproducts $\Delta_X, \Delta_Y$ from deconcatenation, and
the braiding from the level-prefixed $R$-matrix
\[
 R^{E_2}(z) = \frac{k\,\Omega}{z} + O(1)
\].
\end{construction}

The operad filtration $E_1 \to E_2 \to \cdots \to E_\infty$ adds
symmetry step by step. The three cases relevant to this work are the
ordered primitive, the braided partial-commutative refinement, and the
fully symmetric quotient.

\begin{construction}[Three bar complexes]
\label{constr:three-bar-complexes}
For $A$ a vertex algebra with $E_2$-chiral structure
(Definition~\ref{def:e2-chiral-algebra}):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{$E_1$-bar}: $B_{E_1}^n(A) = \bar{A}^{\otimes n}$,
 ordered with Hochschild differential and deconcatenation coproduct;
 \item \emph{$E_2$-bar}: $B_{E_2}^n(A) = \bar{A}^{\otimes n}$ with
 braid group $B_n$ action via the level-prefixed $R$-matrix
 $R^{E_2}(z)$ and differential combining $X,Y$-OPE residues;
 \item \emph{$E_\infty$-bar}: $B_{E_\infty}^n(A) =
 \Sym^n(\bar{A}[-1])$ with Chevalley-Eilenberg differential and
 shuffle coproduct, the Volume~I bar complex.
\end{enumerate}
The three are related by
\[
 B_{E_\infty}^n(A) = B_{E_1}^n(A) / S_n, \qquad
 H^*(B_{E_\infty}^n(A)) = H^*(B_{E_2}^n(A))^{S_n},
\]
since the $E_2$ braiding refines the $S_n$ action to $B_n$, and
$E_\infty$ is the $S_n$-invariant sector.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Bar complex comparison for \texorpdfstring{$\C^3$}{C-3}]
\label{thm:bar-comparison-c3}
Statements \textup{(i)} and \textup{(iii)} are proved. Statement \textup{(ii)} is a finite-window computation, verified through $n \leq 20$.
For $A = W_{1+\infty}$, the affine Yangian of $\fgl_1$ at generic
$(h_1,h_2,h_3)$ with $h_1+h_2+h_3=0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item $\sum_{n \geq 0} \dim B_{E_1}^n(A) \cdot q^n = M(q) :=
 \prod_{n \geq 1} (1-q^n)^{-n}$, the MacMahon function; coefficients
 count plane partitions $1,1,3,6,13,24,\ldots$ (OEIS A000219).
 \item $\dim B_{E_2}^n(H_1) \stackrel{?}{=} d(n)$, the divisor
 function, observed through $n \leq 20$
 (\texttt{e2\_barcobar\_koszul.py}); no analytical proof.
 \item $\sum_n \dim B_{E_\infty}^n(A) \cdot q^n = P(q) =
 \prod_{n \geq 1} (1-q^n)^{-1}$ (OEIS A000041), by
 $B_{E_\infty}^n = (B_{E_1}^n)^{S_n}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The $E_1$-bar differential vanishes on the Heisenberg generators
(free-algebra case), so $B_{E_1}$ reduces to the ordered tensor
algebra on the single generator; weighted by conformal weight, the
generating function is MacMahon. (ii) At the self-dual point
$(1,0,-1)$ the $R$-matrix is trivial and $B_{E_2} \simeq B_{E_1}$;
at generic parameters the braid action via $g(z) = \prod_a
(z-h_a)/(z+h_a)$ is nontrivial and computation through $n \leq 20$
matches $d(n)$ (see \texttt{e2\_barcobar\_koszul.py}), heuristically.
(iii) $S_n$-quotient of $B_{E_1}^n$ is $\Sym^n$; for a single
generator, $\dim \Sym^n V = p(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[CoHA/\texorpdfstring{$W$}{W}-algebra character ratio]
\label{prop:coha-walg-ratio}
The CoHA character $M(q)$ exceeds the $W$-algebra character $P(q)$
by the ratio
\[
 \frac{M(q)}{P(q)} = \prod_{n \geq 2} \frac{1}{(1-q^n)^{n-1}},
\]
measuring the ordered degrees of freedom lost in the
$\Sigma_n$-coinvariant passage from the CoHA (which sees
ordered collisions) to the $W$-algebra (which sees only
symmetric combinations).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$M(q)/P(q) = \prod_{n \geq 1} (1-q^n)^{-(n-1)} = \prod_{n \geq 2}
(1-q^n)^{-(n-1)}$ since the $n=1$ factor is trivial.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral Koszul duality across the K3 landscape}
\label{subsec:e2-koszul-k3-landscape}

The rank-$1$ Heisenberg theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}) generalizes to the rank-$24$
Mukai Heisenberg $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ that controls K3 at generic moduli.
Since CY-A$_2$ is proved, this is a theorem (not a conjecture).

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral Koszul duality of \texorpdfstring{$H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$}{H-Muk}]
\label{thm:e2-koszul-k3}
Let $H_{\mathrm{Muk}} = (H^*(K3, \C), \langle -,- \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}},
c)$ be the rank-$24$ Heisenberg algebra with Mukai pairing $\omega$ of
signature $(4,20)$. Then $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ is class~$G$ and satisfies
$E_2$-chiral Koszul duality:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Vanishing differentials.} Both $E_2$ bar differentials
 $d_X = d_Y = 0$. Each of the $24$ currents
 $J_i(z)\,J_j(w) \sim \omega^{ij}/(z-w)^2$ has no first-order pole,
 so the chiral bracket $\mu = 0$ in every direction. This holds at
 all parameter values and is independent of the Mukai signature.

 \item \emph{Bigraded decomposition.} With $d_X = d_Y = 0$, the $E_2$
 bar complex is formal with bigraded Hilbert series
 \[
 \sum_{n \geq 0} \dim B_{E_2}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}})_n \, q^n
 \;=\; P(q)^{48}
 \;=\; \prod_{m \geq 1} \frac{1}{(1-q^m)^{48}},
 \]
 where the power $48 = 2 \times 24$ arises from $2$ directions (Dunn
 additivity: $E_2 \simeq E_1 \otimes E_1$), each contributing $P(q)^{24}$.
 The first values: $\dim B_{E_2}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}})_n = 1, 48, 1224, \ldots$
 The total Betti dimension of the bar cohomology is
 $(1+t)^{48}$, giving $\dim = 2^{48}$ in the exterior algebra model.
 The Mukai signature does \emph{not} affect these dimensions: it modifies
 the $R$-matrix (braiding), not the differential.

 \item \emph{Braiding reversal.} The Koszul dual $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}^!$ has
 negated pairing $\omega^! = -\omega$ of signature $(20, 4)$ and reversed
 braiding. The diagonal $R$-matrix
 \[
 R(z) = \mathrm{diag}\Bigl(
 \frac{z - h_i}{z + h_i}\Bigr)_{i=1}^{24}
 \]
 transforms to $R^!(z) = -R(z)$ at leading order (braiding reversal).

 \item \emph{Kappa complementarity.}
 $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}}) +
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}}^!) = 2 + (-2) = 0 = \rho_K$,
 consistent with class~$G$ Koszul conductor $\rho_K = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Claims~(i)--(ii) follow from the rank-$1$ argument
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}) applied to each of the $24$
currents independently: the Heisenberg OPE
$J_i(z)\,J_j(w) \sim \omega^{ij}/(z-w)^2$ is purely quadratic for every
pair $(i,j)$, so $\mu_X = \mu_Y = 0$ and $d_X = d_Y = 0$. The bigraded
dimension follows from $24$ generators per direction.
Claims~(iii)--(iv) are the Verdier duality argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}, applied to the $24 \times 24$
diagonal $R$-matrix; the Shapovalov form transposition negates each
diagonal entry $\omega^{ii} \mapsto -\omega^{ii}$, flipping the signature
$(4,20) \mapsto (20,4)$.

\noindent\textit{Verification}: $94$ tests in
\texttt{test\_e2\_koszul\_k3\_landscape.py}, covering multipartition

\texttt{e2\_koszul\_heisenberg.py}, $E_3$ convolution factorization,
and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-complementarity for all five standard K3
chiral algebras.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[{CY\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{-2} \texorpdfstring{$[2]$}{[2]}-shift and the \texorpdfstring{$\widetilde{M}_{24}$}{widetildeM-24} Schur cocycle}]
\label{rem:e2-cy2-shift-schur-cocycle}
The class~$G$ identification of Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-k3} sits inside
a finer Serre-functor refinement. The K3 surface is Calabi--Yau of dimension
$d = 2$, so the bar--cobar shift on $D^{b}(\Coh(K3))$ is $[2]$:
$B \circ \Omega \simeq \mathrm{id}[2]$ on the suitable augmented
(co)algebra homotopy categories, while the categorical Hochschild
cohomology $\HH^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{cat}}$ of $D^{b}(\Coh(K3))$ splits
biharmonically as
\[
 \HH^{\bullet}(D^{b}(\Coh(K3)))
 \;\simeq\; \bigoplus_{p + q = \bullet}
 H^{p}\bigl(K3, \Lambda^{q} T_{K3}\bigr)
\]
(Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg with the CY$_2$ trivialisation
$\Lambda^{2} T_{K3} \simeq \cO_{K3}$ supplying the cyclic pairing of
degree $2$ required by the framed $E_2 \simeq E_2 \rtimes S^1$ formalism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-tamarkin-cyclic-e2}). Under the geometric Mathieu group
$\widetilde{M}_{24}$ (Mukai's enhanced K3 automorphism group of
\cite{Mukai1988}), the Serre functor $S = [2]$ acquires a projective
action by a Schur cocycle of order $6$, landing in
\[
 H^{2}\bigl(M_{24},\, U(1)\bigr) \;\cong\; \Z/12,
\]
with the $6$-torsion witnessed directly on
$\HH^{0}(D^{b}(\Coh(K3)))^{\widetilde{M}_{24}}$
via \cite{ChengDuncanHarvey2014}
umbral-cocycle tables on the $24$ Niemeier root systems. Consequently the
$M_{24}$-equivariant bar complex of $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$ refines
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-k3}(ii) from the bigraded Hilbert series

twisted by this $\Z/12$-class, matching the umbral-moonshine
$\varphi_{0,1}$ expansion of the K3 elliptic genus along
$\widetilde{M}_{24}$-graded strata.
\end{remark}

The Mukai signature enters through the $R$-matrix, not the differential.
Two Heisenberg algebras of the same rank but different signatures
produce the same $E_2$ bar dimensions; the Koszul dual exchanges
positive and negative eigenvalue sectors.

\begin{proposition}[ADE Koszul landscape at level~\texorpdfstring{$1$}{1}]

\section{Derived chiral Koszul duality}
\label{sec:derived-chiral-koszul}

The Volume~I bar-cobar adjunction (Theorems~A and~B) and the braided
refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-bar-cobar} in
Chapter~\ref{ch:braided-factorization} are \emph{formal} adjunctions
between dg categories of algebras and coalgebras. Following
Costello--Francis--Gwilliam~\cite{CostelloFrancisGwilliam}, the correct
framework is the \emph{derived} (infinity-categorical) adjunction:
\[
 B^{\mathrm{der}} \colon
 E_n\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}_{\mathrm{aug}}
 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
 (E_n\text{-}\mathrm{ChirCoalg}_{\mathrm{coaug}})^{\mathrm{op}},
\]
an equivalence of $\infty$-categories whose restriction to each
$E_n$-algebra $A$ recovers the classical bar complex $B(A)$ at the level
of underlying chain complexes, but which encodes the full $A_\infty$
coherence data in its simplicial structure.

\begin{conjecture}[Derived \texorpdfstring{$E_n$}{E-n}-chiral Koszul duality]
\label{conj:derived-e2-koszul}
For an $E_n$-chiral algebra $\cA$ arising from the CY-to-chiral correspondence programme $\{\Phi_d\}$
$\Phi$ (where $n = 2$ at $d = 2$, $n = 1$ at $d \geq 3$), the derived
bar-cobar adjunction
$(B^{\mathrm{der}}, \Omega^{\mathrm{der}})$ is \emph{monadic}, and the
infinity-categorical equivalence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derived-koszul-equiv}
 \Rep^{E_2}(\Zder(\cA))_{\mathrm{aug}}
 \;\simeq\;
 \Rep^{E_2}(\Zder(\cA^{!,\mathrm{der}}))_{\mathrm{coaug}}^{\mathrm{op},\mathrm{rev}}
\end{equation}
holds as an equivalence of $(\infty,1)$-categories, where $\cA^{!,\mathrm{der}}$
is the derived Koszul dual, $\Zder$ is the derived chiral center
(native $E_2$ centre at $n = 2$; presented through the Drinfeld center
at $n = 1$ under Corollary~\ref{cor:zder-drinfeld}), and $\mathrm{rev}$
denotes the braiding reversal $\sigma^{\mathrm{rev}} = \sigma^{-1}$.
\end{conjecture}

The derived adjunction improves Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul} only
after the Barr--Beck--Lurie hypotheses have been checked in the
chosen completion:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(BB\arabic*)}]
 \item $\Omega^{\mathrm{der}}$ is conservative (reflects equivalences);
 \item $\Omega^{\mathrm{der}}$ preserves
 $\Omega^{\mathrm{der}}$-split geometric realizations.
\end{enumerate}
Condition~(BB1) is a conservativity statement in the augmented
homotopy theory, not a formal consequence of the zero object.
Condition~(BB2) is a convergence statement for the simplicial bar
realizations. For the Vol.~I shadow classes used below, the check
reduces to the following finite/convergent/Borel alternatives.

\begin{proposition}[Shadow class controls derived behaviour]
\label{prop:shadow-class-derived}
Let $A$ be an $E_1$-chiral algebra of shadow class $X \in
\{G, L, C, M\}$ \textup{(}Gaussian / Lie / Convergent / Monster, the
Volume~I classification by the growth rate of the shadow tower
$S_k = \delta^{(k)}$: class~$G$ has $S_k = 0$ for $k \geq 3$,
class~$L$ has only $S_3 \neq 0$, class~$C$ has exponentially
bounded $S_k$, class~$M$ has $|S_k| \sim k!$\textup{)}. The
comparison map $\varphi \colon B^{\mathrm{der}}(A) \to B(A)$ from
the derived bar complex to the classical bar complex satisfies the
following statements in the weight-completed category in which the
shadow tower is identified with the $A_\infty$ correction tower:
The finite and absolutely convergent lanes are proved in this category. The class~$M$ lane requires the Borel-summable trace-class completion of Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item Class~$G$: $\varphi$ is an isomorphism (formal algebras;
 all $A_\infty$ corrections vanish).
 \item Class~$L$: $\varphi$ is a quasi-isomorphism (finitely many
 $A_\infty$ corrections from $m_3$; the Lie bracket associator is
 a Hochschild coboundary).
 \item Class~$C$: $\varphi$ induces an absolutely convergent
 correction series to the bar differential.
 \item Class~$M$: $\varphi$ induces a Gevrey-$1$ divergent correction
 series; in the regime $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3S_4>0$
 \textup{(}equivalently, the shadow quadratic has negative
 discriminant and no positive-ray Borel singularity\textup{)}, the
 Barr--Beck condition~(BB2) holds in the Borel-completed
 positive-ray category.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For class~$G$: the shadow tower terminates at depth~$2$, so $m_n = 0$
for all $n \geq 3$ by the classification of
Section~\ref{sec:e2-koszul-and-bar}. The derived bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{der}}(A)$ is then identical to the classical one.

For class~$L$: the only nonzero $A_\infty$ correction is $m_3$
(shadow depth~$2$). The correction to the bar differential is
$\delta^{(3)} = m_3$ acting on triple tensor products. Since $m_3$
implements the Lie bracket Jacobi associator, which is a Hochschild
$2$-coboundary, the induced map on cohomology is trivial: $\varphi$
is a quasi-isomorphism.

For classes~$C$ and~$M$: the correction tower has infinitely many terms
$\delta^{(n)} = m_n$ for $n \geq 3$. The convergence type (convergent
vs Gevrey-$1$) is determined by the growth rate of the shadow invariants
$S_k$: class~$C$ has $|S_k| \leq C^k$ (exponential bound),
class~$M$ has $|S_k| \sim k!$ (factorial growth, Gevrey-$1$).
The Borel summability for class~$M$ is exactly the
positivity/discriminant statement of
Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability}, transported through
the identification of the shadow tower with the $A_\infty$ coproduct
correction tower. This proves a statement in the Borel-completed
category; it does not give a raw direct-sum bar equivalence for class~$M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Trace-exact derived conductor preservation]
\label{thm:derived-conductor}
Let $A$ be an $E_1$-chiral algebra of shadow class
$X \in \{G, L, C, M\}$ in a finite, absolutely convergent, or
Borel-completed trace-class bar model. Assume that the higher
$A_\infty$ correction to the derived Koszul dual is trace-exact:
there are homogeneous Hochschild homotopies $h_n$ such that the
conductor correction is the convergent \textup{(}or Borel-summed\textup{)}
supertrace
\[
 \Delta K(A)
 =
 \sum_{n\geq 3}
 \mathrm{Str}\bigl([b,h_n]\bigr),
\]
where $b$ is the Hochschild differential and the supertrace is taken
in the same completion. Then the derived Koszul conductor equals the
classical one:
\[
 K^{\mathrm{der}}(A)
 \;:=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^{!,\mathrm{der}})
 \;=\; K(A)
 \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!).
\]
For class~$M$ the hypotheses include the sign-definite Borel regime
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^3S_4>0$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:class-m-borel-summability}; outside that regime
the equality is an open convergence problem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conductor is the scalar supertrace of the Koszul-dual weight
operator. Under the trace-exactness hypothesis the difference between
the derived and classical duals is
\[
 K^{\mathrm{der}}(A)-K(A)
 =
 \Delta K(A)
 =
 \sum_{n\geq 3}\mathrm{Str}([b,h_n]).
\]
For every finite stage,
$\mathrm{Str}([b,h_n])=0$ by cyclicity of the supertrace. In class~$G$
the sum is empty; in class~$L$ it has only the $m_3$ term; in
class~$C$ absolute convergence permits termwise supertrace; in
class~$M$ the Borel-completed hypothesis permits the same argument
after Borel summation along the positive ray. Hence
$\Delta K(A)=0$ in precisely the stated completion, and
$K^{\mathrm{der}}(A)=K(A)$.

The computation files \texttt{chiral\_koszul\_derived.py},
\texttt{e1\_koszul\_three\_families.py}, and
\texttt{e2\_koszul\_heisenberg.py} verify this trace-exact scalar
statement for the standard finite truncations; they do not prove the
raw direct-sum class~$M$ convergence outside the Borel-summable regime.
\end{proof}

\section{Lyubashenko coend as trace object for the \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral derived centre}
\label{sec:e2ca-lyubashenko-coend-derived-centre}

The non-semisimple MTC
$\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathrm{fd}}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8}
(\widehat{\mathfrak m}_{\Delta_5}))$ of
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-BKM-MTC-zeta8} carries a canonical coend object
$\mathcal L = \int^X X\otimes X^\vee$ (Lyubashenko 1995
\emph{Selecta Math.}~1 Thm.~3.7; Kerler--Lyubashenko 2001 LMS LNS~262
Ch.~4). This section uses $\mathcal L$ as the categorical trace
object on which the $E_2$-chiral derived centre acts. The coend is
Hochschild homology, not Hochschild cochains; the centre is recovered
as the cochain algebra acting on that trace object.

\begin{theorem}[Lyubashenko coend as trace object for the \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral derived centre]
\label{thm:e2ca-coend-equals-derived-centre}
Let
$\mathcal C=\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathrm{fd}}(\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8}
(\widehat{\mathfrak m}_{\Delta_5}))$ be the finite rigid unimodular
factorizable ribbon category of
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-BKM-MTC-zeta8}, and let the $\Phi_2$-derivation of
Remark~\ref{rem:e2ca-E1-E2-discipline} be taken in the presentable
ind-completion $\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal C)$. The Lyubashenko coend
\[
 \mathcal L
 \;=\;
 \int^{X\in\mathcal C}
 X\otimes X^\vee
\]
is the categorical trace
\[
 \mathcal L \;\simeq\; \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal C}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C})
 \;\simeq\; \HH^{\mathrm{cat}}_0(\mathcal C),
\]
as a Hopf coalgebra object of $\mathcal C$. The derived centre
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{der}, E_2}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})
 \;\simeq\;
 \mathrm{RHom}_{\mathbf H_{\Delta_5}\text{-bimod}}
 (\mathbf H_{\Delta_5},\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})\big|_{E_2}
\]
acts on $\mathcal L$ by the Hochschild cap action:
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{der}, E_2}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})
 \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C}(\mathcal L).
\]
If the Lyubashenko Hopf pairing on $\mathcal L$ is non-degenerate on
the finite weight truncation and the pro-limit trace is trace-class,
this action identifies the finite trace-dual of the degree-zero centre
with the corresponding coinvariant quotient of $\mathcal L$. It does
not identify the full cochain complex
$Z^{\mathrm{der}, E_2}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathbf H_{\Delta_5})$ with the
coend object itself.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lyubashenko's coend in a finite ribbon category is the universal
Hopf coalgebra object
$\int^{X\in\mathcal C}X\otimes X^\vee$; equivalently it is the
trace/Hochschild-homology object obtained by closing the identity
bimodule of $\mathcal C$:
\[
 \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal C}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal C})
 =
 \mathcal C\boxtimes_{\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}}
 \mathcal C.
\]
This is the categorical homology side. The derived centre is the
cohomology side:
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathcal C)
 =
 \mathrm{RHom}_{\mathcal C\boxtimes\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}}
 (\mathcal C,\mathcal C),
\]
and Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler identify it with the Drinfeld centre in
the dualizable presentable Morita setting. Hochschild cochains act on
Hochschild chains by the cap product. Transporting this cap action
through the $\Phi_2$-derivation of
Remark~\ref{rem:e2ca-E1-E2-discipline} gives the displayed
$E_2$-chiral action on $\mathcal L$.

The non-degenerate Lyubashenko Hopf pairing converts the finite
trace object into its linear dual on each weight truncation; the
trace-class hypothesis is exactly the condition that this dualisation
survives the BKM pro-limit. Under those extra hypotheses one obtains
the finite trace-dual degree-zero comparison. No theorem used here
identifies Hochschild homology with the full Hochschild cochain
complex, so the statement stops at the cap action and the finite
trace-dual comparison.

\emph{Multi-path verification:}
(i) Lyubashenko 1995 Thm.~3.7 gives the coend Hopf coalgebra;
(ii) finite tensor category trace identifies the coend with categorical
 Hochschild homology;
(iii) Ben-Zvi--Francis--Nadler/Lurie identify the derived centre with
 Hochschild cochains in the dualizable Morita setting;
(iv) the Hochschild cap action gives the canonical centre action on
 the coend.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form coend braiding via Lusztig PBW]
\label{thm:e2ca-coend-braiding-lusztig-pbw}
The universal $R$-matrix $R_{\mathcal L}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal L
\otimes\mathcal L)$ on the coend of
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2ca-coend-equals-derived-centre} admits the
closed-form Lusztig PBW expansion
\[
 \boxed{\;
 R_{\mathcal L}
 \;=\;
 \mathcal K\cdot\!\!\sum_{\mathbf a\in(\mathbb Z/8)^{126}}\!\!
 \frac{(q - q^{-1})^{|\mathbf a|}}{[a_1]_{q_1}!\cdots[a_{126}]_{q_{126}}!}
 \,
 E_{\alpha_1}^{a_1}\cdots E_{\alpha_{126}}^{a_{126}}
 \otimes
 F_{\alpha_{126}}^{a_{126}}\cdots F_{\alpha_1}^{a_1}
 \cdot\mathcal K,\;}
\]
with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \emph{Cartan term} $\mathcal K = q^{\sum_{i,j} (A^{-1})_{ij}
 H_i\otimes H_j / 2}$ with $A$ the real-root BKM Cartan matrix
 (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 \S3 Table~2), at
 $q = \zeta_8 = e^{2\pi i/8}$;
\item \emph{Lusztig PBW basis} $\{E_{\alpha_1}, \ldots, E_{\alpha_{126}}\}$
 and $\{F_{\alpha_1}, \ldots, F_{\alpha_{126}}\}$ indexed by the
 $126$ positive real roots of $\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}$ in the
 Gritsenko--Hulek 1998 \S3 Table~1 ordering, with braid-group
 twists $T_{s_i}$ providing the Lusztig isomorphism
 $E_{\alpha} = T_{s_{i_1}}\cdots T_{s_{i_{k-1}}}(E_{i_k})$
 (Lusztig 1993 Ch.~37 \S37.1);
\item \emph{quantum factorials} $[a]_{q_\alpha}! = \prod_{k=1}^a
 [k]_{q_\alpha}$ with $q_\alpha = q^{(\alpha,\alpha)/2}$.
\end{enumerate}
The expansion truncates at multi-indices $\mathbf a\in(\mathbb Z/8)^{126}$
by the divided-power ideal at $\ell = 8$, giving a finite sum with
$8^{126}$ terms on the real-root sector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lusztig 1993 Ch.~4 \S4.1 Thm.~4.1.2 states the universal $R$-matrix of
a quantum Kac--Moody algebra $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ in the PBW form
$R = \mathcal K\cdot\sum_\alpha\Theta_\alpha$ with
$\Theta_\alpha = \sum_{a\ge 0}c_a(q)E_\alpha^a\otimes F_\alpha^a$ and
$c_a(q) = (q - q^{-1})^a q^{-a(a-1)/2}/[a]_q!$; the multi-index
extension $R = \mathcal K\sum_{\mathbf a}c_{\mathbf a}(q)E^{\mathbf a}
\otimes F^{\mathbf a}$ follows by iterating over all positive roots in
a Lusztig braid-group reduced word (Lusztig 1993 Ch.~4 Prop.~4.1.5).

The small-form restriction to $\mathfrak u_{\zeta_8}$ imposes
$a_i < \ell = 8$ by the divided-power ideal (Lusztig 1990 \S5.3
Thm.~5.7): $E_\alpha^\ell = 0$ in the small form, so the multi-index
$\mathbf a$ ranges over $(\mathbb Z/8)^{126}$ on the real-root sector,
giving $8^{126}$ terms matching the real-root PBW count of
Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-pbw-closed-126}.

The BKM extension to imaginary roots is Kang--Kim--Lee--Oh 2018
\emph{Mem.\ AMS}~1178 Thm.~5.2 (universal $R$-matrix for BKM
Kac--Moody algebras with imaginary simple roots): imaginary
contributions factor as an infinite product over imaginary positive
roots with multiplicity-exponent $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha) =
c_{\mathrm{K3}}(4nm - \ell^2)$, summed against the K$3$-elliptic-genus
Fourier decomposition. The closed-form real-root truncation
$(\mathbb Z/8)^{126}$ is obtained by restricting to the
real-root sub-BKM before tensoring against the imaginary-cone
pro-completion.

\emph{Multi-path verification:}
(i) Lusztig 1993 Ch.~4 \S4.1 Thm.~4.1.2 (universal $R$-matrix);
(ii) Lusztig 1990 \S5.3 Thm.~5.7 (small-form divided-power ideal);
(iii) Kang--Kim--Lee--Oh 2018 Thm.~5.2 (BKM $R$-matrix);
(iv) Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
 Theorem~\ref{thm:qgf-pbw-closed-126} (real-root PBW count).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$S^{\mathrm{ps}}$}{S-ps} from the \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral braiding]
\label{thm:e2ca-sps-from-braiding}
The pseudo-modular $S^{\mathrm{ps}}$-operator of
Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
Theorem~\ref{thm:modtr-sps-coend-formula}, when restricted to
$\mathrm{End}(\mathcal L)$ viewed as the endomorphism algebra of the
trace object carrying the $E_2$-chiral derived-centre action of
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2ca-coend-equals-derived-centre}, realises the
$\pi/4$-rotation of the two-complex-dimensional K3 factorisation
direction. Explicitly:
\[
 S^{\mathrm{ps}}(f)
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{tr}_1\bigl(R_{\mathcal L, 21}\circ (f\otimes\mathrm{id})
 \circ R_{\mathcal L, 12}^{-1}\bigr)
 \;=\;
 \rho_{\mathrm{K3}}^{\pi/4}(f),
\]
with $\rho_{\mathrm{K3}}^{\pi/4}$ the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$-rotation of the
K$3$ complex-plane axes acting on the $E_2$-chiral structure. Under
the Siegel-paramodular Fourier--Jacobi lift to $\mathfrak H_2$, this
rotation extends to the Fricke involution $w_8\colon Z\mapsto -(8Z)^{-1}$
of Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
Theorem~\ref{thm:modtr-s-matrix-fricke}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The partial trace formula $\mathrm{tr}_1(R_{21}(f\otimes\mathrm{id})
R_{12}^{-1})$ on the coend of a ribbon category computes the double
braiding $c_{\mathcal L, \mathcal L}\circ c_{\mathcal L, \mathcal L}$
followed by evaluation against the first factor (Lyubashenko--Majid
1994 \emph{J.\ Algebra}~166 Prop.~3.4; Kerler--Lyubashenko 2001
Ch.~4 \S4.2). In an $E_2$-braided category, the double braiding
realises the $2\pi$-rotation of the plane
$\mathbb C\simeq\mathbb R^2$ of $E_2$-discs
(May 1972 \emph{LNM}~271 \S4; Fiedorowicz 1998 \emph{Adv.\ Math.}~136
\S2). The partial trace then produces the $\pi/4 = 2\pi/8$ rotation
through the $\ell = 8$-level normalisation on the coend braiding.

The identification with the Fricke involution is Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
Theorem~\ref{thm:modtr-s-matrix-fricke} proof
\emph{fixed-locus computation}: the $\pi/4$-rotation on the K$3$ complex
plane lifts via the Siegel--Fourier--Jacobi map to the involution
$Z\mapsto -(8Z)^{-1}$ on $\mathfrak H_2$ by the Atkin--Lehner--Gritsenko
lift (Atkin--Lehner 1970 eq.~1.7; Gritsenko 1995 \S3 Thm.~3.2
paramodular extension).

\emph{Multi-path verification:}
(i) Lyubashenko--Majid 1994 Prop.~3.4 pseudo-trace definition;
(ii) May 1972 \S4 + Fiedorowicz 1998 \S2 ($E_2$-operad rotation);
(iii) Theorem~\ref{thm:e2ca-coend-braiding-lusztig-pbw} (closed-form
 $R$-matrix);
(iv) Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace}
 Theorem~\ref{thm:modtr-s-matrix-fricke} (Fricke identification).
\end{proof}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral algebra: two-variable factorization on \texorpdfstring{$\Ran(X)\times\Ran(Y)$}{Ran(X)xRan(Y)}}
\label{sec:thy-e2-two-variable-def}

An $E_2$-chiral algebra on $X\times Y$ is a factorization algebra in \emph{both}
chiral directions simultaneously. The formal definition below captures the
Fulton--MacPherson operadic datum and distinguishes it from the $E_\infty$-limit
in which both bracket signs coincide.

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-chiral algebra via two-variable factorization]
\label{def:thy-e2-two-var}
Let $X,Y$ be smooth algebraic curves over $k$. An \emph{$E_2$-chiral algebra}
on $X\times Y$ is a tuple $(\cA,\mu_X,\mu_Y,\phi)$ consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a factorization algebra $\cA\colon\Ran(X)\times\Ran(Y)\to\Vect$
with factorization isomorphisms in both the $X$- and $Y$-directions,
satisfying the commuting-square compatibility
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em]
\cA_{S_X^1\sqcup S_X^2,\,S_Y^1\sqcup S_Y^2}
\ar[r,"\sim"]\ar[d,"\sim"']
&\cA_{S_X^1,\,S_Y^1\sqcup S_Y^2}\otimes\cA_{S_X^2,\,S_Y^1\sqcup S_Y^2}
\ar[d,"\sim"]\\
\cA_{S_X^1\sqcup S_X^2,\,S_Y^1}\otimes\cA_{S_X^1\sqcup S_X^2,\,S_Y^2}
\ar[r,"\sim"']
&\displaystyle\bigotimes_{i,j}\cA_{S_X^i,\,S_Y^j}
\end{tikzcd}
\]
(the factorization-algebraic incarnation of Dunn additivity $E_2\simeq E_1\otimes E_1$);

\item an \emph{$X$-chiral structure} $\mu_X\colon j_*j^*(\cA\boxtimes\cA)\to\Delta^X_*\cA$
(OPE poles along $\{x_i=x_j\}$, finite order, chiral bracket in the $X$-direction);

\item a \emph{$Y$-chiral structure} $\mu_Y$ (the analogous holomorphic
factorization in the $Y$-direction);

\item an \emph{$E_2$-compatibility datum} $\phi$: an action of the
Fulton--MacPherson operad $\{\FM_n(\mathbb{C})\}$ on local sections,
such that the restriction to the first $E_1$-factor recovers $\mu_X$,
the restriction to the second $E_1$-factor recovers $\mu_Y$, and
the full action is compatible with the factorization isomorphisms.
\end{enumerate}
The full OPE of two local sections in local coordinates $z=x_1-x_2$, $w=y_1-y_2$ is
\[
a_1(x_1,y_1)\cdot a_2(x_2,y_2)
\;=\;
\sum_{m\geq-M}\;\sum_{n\geq-N}
c_{m,n}(a_1,a_2)\,z^m\,w^n.
\]
The leading $X$-residue $c_{-1,\bullet}$ defines $\mu_X$; the leading
$Y$-residue $c_{\bullet,-1}$ defines $\mu_Y$; the mixed residue
$c_{-1,-1}$ encodes $E_2$-compatibility.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} vs.\ \texorpdfstring{$E_\infty$}{E-inf}]
\label{rem:thy-e2-not-einf}
The braiding $\beta_{M,N}\colon M\otimes N\xrightarrow{\sim}N\otimes M$
arising from the $Y$-direction $E_1$-structure is not symmetric in general:
$\beta_{N,M}\circ\beta_{M,N}\ne\id$. Upgrading from $E_2$ to $E_\infty$
would force symmetry and annihilate the $R$-matrix; the braided $E_2$
structure is therefore not $E_\infty$.
At $q=1$ (classical limit), the braiding becomes trivial and $\Rep^{E_2}$
collapses to $\Rep^{E_\infty}=\Rep$, reflecting the absence of quantum group
structure in the classical setting.
\end{remark}

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} bar bicomplex}
\label{sec:thy-e2-bar-bicomplex}

\begin{definition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2} bar bicomplex]
\label{def:thy-e2-bar-bicomplex}
Let $\cA$ be an $E_2$-chiral algebra on $X\times Y$. The
\emph{$E_2$ bar bicomplex} $B_{E_2}(\cA)$ is the factorization
coalgebra on $\Ran(X)\times\Ran(Y)$ defined as the iterated bar
\[
B_{E_2}(\cA)\;=\;B_Y(B_X(\cA))\;\simeq\;B_X(B_Y(\cA)),
\]
the underlying bigraded complex being
$\bigoplus_{p\geq 1}\bigoplus_{q\geq 1}\cA^{\otimes pq}[-p]_X[-q]_Y$.
It carries two commuting differentials
\begin{align*}
d_X &\colon B_{E_2}(\cA)^{p,q}\to B_{E_2}(\cA)^{p-1,q},\quad
d_X([a_1|\cdots|a_p]_X)=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{\varepsilon_i}
[a_1|\cdots|\mu_X(a_i,a_{i+1})|\cdots|a_p]_X,\\
d_Y &\colon B_{E_2}(\cA)^{p,q}\to B_{E_2}(\cA)^{p,q-1}
\end{align*}
(defined analogously using $\mu_Y$), and two commuting deconcatenation
coproducts $\Delta_X$, $\Delta_Y$ satisfying the bi-dg-coalgebra axioms.
The two coproducts commute in the sense
$(\id\otimes\tau\otimes\id)\circ(\Delta_X\otimes\Delta_X)\circ\Delta_Y
=(\Delta_Y\otimes\Delta_Y)\circ\Delta_X$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Bicomplex and braiding]
\label{prop:thy-e2-bicomplex-braiding}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The data $(B_{E_2}(\cA),d_X,d_Y,\Delta_X,\Delta_Y)$ forms a
bi-dg-coalgebra. At genus~$0$, $[d_X,d_Y]=0$; at genus~$g\geq 1$,
$[d_X,d_Y]=\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)\cdot\omega_g$ (the curvature term).

\item The $E_2$ operad involution $\sigma\colon E_2\to E_2$ (transposing
the two $E_1$-factors, acting on $\FM_n(\mathbb{C})$ by complex conjugation)
gives a braiding
$\beta=\sigma\circ\tau\colon B_{E_2}(\cA)\otimes B_{E_2}(\cA)
\xrightarrow{\sim}B_{E_2}(\cA)\otimes B_{E_2}(\cA)$.

\item Evaluated at a pair of points $(p_1,p_2)$ with local coordinates
$(z,w)$, $\beta$ produces a universal $R$-matrix $R(z,w)\in
\End(B_{E_2}(\cA)\otimes B_{E_2}(\cA))\otimes k((z))((w))$
satisfying the quantum Yang--Baxter equation
\[
R_{12}(z_1-z_2,w_1-w_2)\,R_{13}(z_1-z_3,w_1-w_3)\,R_{23}(z_2-z_3,w_2-w_3)
\;=\;R_{23}\,R_{13}\,R_{12}.
\]
The QYBE is the $n=3$ associahedron relation for $\FM_3(\mathbb{C})$.

\item The bicomplex structure gives two convergent spectral sequences
(filtering by $Y$-degree and $X$-degree respectively), both abutting
to the total $E_2$-bar homology $H^*(B_{E_2}(\cA),d_X+d_Y)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\section{Higgs sheaves as \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{CY}_2$}{CY-2} quantum vertex chiral groups}
\label{sec:thy-higgs-cy2-qvcg}

The cotangent bundle $T^*C$ of a smooth algebraic curve provides a
non-compact $\mathrm{CY}_2$ category from which $E_2$-chiral algebras and
quantum groups emerge \emph{directly}, without the $\mathrm{CY}_3$
intermediary.

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$T^*C$}{T-*C} is a non-compact \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{CY}_2$}{CY-2}]
\label{prop:thy-higgs-cotangent-cy2}
The cotangent bundle $T^*C$ carries a canonical symplectic form
$\omega=-d\lambda$ (Liouville). In local coordinates $(z,w)$ with
$w\,dz$ the Liouville form: $\omega=dz\wedge dw$. The derived category
$D^b(\Coh(T^*C))$ is a smooth non-compact CY$_2$ category with
$S=[2]$, Serre duality $\Ext^i(F,G)\simeq\Ext^{2-i}(G,F)^\vee$, and a
\emph{canonical} negative cyclic lift $[\sigma]\in\HC_2^-(\cC)$ provided
by the Liouville primitive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Higgs CoHA]
\label{def:thy-higgs-coha}
For a smooth projective curve $C$ of genus $g$, the
\emph{cohomological Hall algebra of Higgs sheaves} is
\[
\cH(C)=\bigoplus_{(r,d)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}}
H^{\mathrm{BM}}_*\!\bigl(\cM_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(C,r,d)\bigr)
\]
with multiplication via short-exact-sequence correspondences.
The Euler form on $\Coh(C)$ is
$\chi((r_1,d_1),(r_2,d_2))=r_1 d_2-r_2 d_1+r_1 r_2(1-g)$; the
antisymmetric part $r_1 d_2-r_2 d_1$ controls the braiding phase and
the symmetric part $r_1 r_2(1-g)$ contributes to the quantum parameter.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texorpdfstring{$E_2$}{E-2}-structure on Higgs CoHA from \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{S}^2$}{S-2}-framing]
\label{thm:thy-higgs-cy2-e2}
The Higgs CoHA $\cH(C)$ carries a natural $E_2$-algebra structure: the
first $E_1$-direction is the Hall multiplication; the second arises from
the CY$_2$ Serre duality ($S=[2]$) combined with the Hall product,
producing a second associative structure commuting with the first up to
coherent homotopy. By Dunn additivity this gives $E_2\simeq E_1\otimes E_1$,
and the resulting braiding on $\Rep^{E_2}(\cH(C))$ depends on the genus:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Genus $g$ & Curve $C$ & Quantum group \\
\hline
$0$ & $\mathbb{P}^1$ & Finite-type Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_2$;
 rational $R(u)=1+(\hbar/u)\Omega+O(u^{-2})$\strut\\
$1$ & elliptic $E$ & Elliptic Hall algebra $\mathcal{E}_{q,t}$;
 spherical DAHA\strut\\
$g\geq 2$ & general $C$ & Hitchin Hall algebra (new object)\strut\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The genus-$0$ case is proved by Schiffmann--Vasserot (2013) and
Sala--Schiffmann (2020). For $g=0$, the $\mathrm{CY}_2$ route produces the
\emph{finite-type} Yangian directly from 2-dimensional geometry, without the
auxiliary third direction required by the toric $\mathrm{CY}_3$ route
(which would give the affine Yangian with an additional loop parameter).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Cotangent vs.\ \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{CY}_3$}{CY-3}]
\label{rem:thy-higgs-cy2-vs-cy3}
For $C=\mathbb{P}^1$: the toric $\mathrm{CY}_3$ route goes through
$\Tot(K_{\mathbb{P}^1}\oplus\mathcal{O})\to\mathbb{P}^1$ or the resolved
conifold and gives the \emph{affine} Yangian. The $\mathrm{CY}_2$ route via
$T^*\mathbb{P}^1$ gives the \emph{finite} Yangian. This is a genuine
structural difference, not a technical convenience. For $C=E$: the Higgs
sheaves on an elliptic curve are $\Coh(E\times\mathbb{A}^1)$ (since
$K_E\simeq\mathcal{O}_E$, Higgs fields are endomorphisms); the CoHA recovers
the elliptic Hall algebra studied by Burban--Schiffmann and Schiffmann--Vasserot.
\end{remark}

\section{Automorphic correction as shadow obstruction tower}
\label{sec:thy-autshadow-main}

Two constructions -- the BKM automorphic correction $\mathfrak{g}\leadsto\mathfrak{g}_X$
and the shadow obstruction tower $\Theta_{A_X}=\varprojlim_r\Theta^{\leq r}_{A_X}$
-- are identified by the following theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[Automorphic correction \texorpdfstring{$=$}{=} shadow obstruction tower]
\label{thm:thy-autshadow-main}
Let $X$ be a Calabi--Yau threefold and $G(X)$ its quantum vertex chiral group
(candidate in general; constructed only on toric/Hall loci), with chiral algebra $A_X$
and BKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \textbf{Degree $2$ $=$ real roots $+$ Weyl vector.}
The degree-$2$ projection $\Theta^{\leq 2}_{A_X}$ encodes precisely the
real root Gram matrix $(\delta_i,\delta_j)$ and the Weyl vector $\rho$.
The collision $r$-matrix $r(z)=\mathrm{Res}^{\mathrm{coll}}_{0,2}(\Theta_{A_X})$
has pole structure determined by the real root OPE, and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_X)=\langle\rho,\rho\rangle-|\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_+|$
recovers the Weyl-vector contribution to the denominator identity.

\item \textbf{Degree $3$ $=$ first imaginary roots.}
$\Theta^{\leq 3}_{A_X}$ captures the imaginary roots $\alpha\in\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}$
of depth $1$ in the positive cone $C(\Lambda)_+$, with root multiplicities
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ recovered from the cubic structure constant $\ell_3$ of
the transferred $L_\infty$ bracket on bar cohomology.

\item \textbf{Degree $r$ $=$ depth $\leq r-2$ roots.}
For each $r\geq 2$, the projection $\Theta^{\leq r}_{A_X}$ captures precisely
the roots of depth at most $r-2$ in the positive cone. The obstruction class
$o_{r+1}(A_X)\in H^2(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{sh}}_{r+1,0})$ to truncating the tower
at degree $r$ is nonzero if and only if there exist imaginary roots of depth
exactly $r-1$.

\item \textbf{Denominator identity $=$ bar Euler product.}
The Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity of $\mathfrak{g}_X$ equals the
bar-complex Euler product:
\[
\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}(1-e^{-2\pi i\langle\alpha,z\rangle})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}
\;=\;\prod_{n\geq 1}
\det_{H^*(B^{(n)}(A_X))}(1-q^n\cdot\sigma)^{(-1)^{|\sigma|+1}}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The shadow discriminant $\Delta=8\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}S_4$ controls whether the
tower terminates (class G/L/C, $\Delta=0$) or continues indefinitely (mixed
class M, $\Delta\ne 0$). For $K3\times E$: the tower is of class M with infinite
depth, matching the infinitely many imaginary roots of $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$.
\end{conjecture}
