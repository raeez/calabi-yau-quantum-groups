\chapter{$E_n$-Factorization and Higher Chiral Structure}
\label{ch:en-factorization}

Factorization algebras on a complex curve carry an $\Etwo$ structure; factorization algebras on a point carry an $\Eone$ structure. Where is the transition? The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral} gives a definite answer for low dimensions: $E_\infty$ at $d = 1$, $\Etwo$ at $d = 2$, $\Eone$ at $d = 3$. The pattern suggests the formula $E_{4-d}$, but this formula breaks at $d = 4$, because $E_0$ does not exist as a separate operad (it is the category of pointed objects). Something else must happen.

What happens is stabilization. The $\bS^d$-framing on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ provides an $E_d$-algebra structure, but for $d \geq 3$ the restriction to the chiral level via Dunn additivity produces only an $\Eone$-algebra. The higher $E_d$-structure does not disappear: it survives as \emph{additional shifted symplectic and shifted Poisson data} beyond the base $\Eone$ level, classified by the framing obstruction. The question then becomes: for which CY dimensions $d$ is this shifted data nontrivial, and what invariant detects it?

The answer is Bott periodicity. The framing obstruction lives in $\pi_d(BU)$ or $\pi_d(BO)$ or $\pi_d(B\Sp)$ depending on the parity and reduction of the structure group of the CY pairing. For the unitary path, $\pi_d(BU) = \Z$ when $d$ is even and vanishes when $d$ is odd. For the symplectic/orthogonal path, the 8-fold periodicity of the classical groups produces a richer pattern, with refinements at $d \equiv 5 \pmod 8$. The main result of this chapter (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) assembles these obstruction computations into a single statement: the framing obstruction is trivial precisely when $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$, and the CY chiral algebra is $\Eone$-stabilized with additional shifted structure controlled by $\pi_d(BU)$ elsewhere.


\section{Dunn additivity and the $E_n$ hierarchy}
\label{sec:dunn-additivity}

Recall the Dunn additivity theorem: $E_n \simeq E_1 \otimes_{E_0} E_1 \otimes_{E_0} \cdots \otimes_{E_0} E_1$ ($n$ factors), where $E_0 = \mathrm{Assoc}_+$ is the associative operad with unit. In particular, $\Etwo \simeq \Eone \otimes_{E_0} \Eone$: an $\Etwo$-algebra is an $\Eone$-algebra in $\Eone$-algebras. An $\Einf$-algebra is commutative.

For the CY-to-chiral functor, the CY dimension $d$ determines the native $\En$ level of the chiral algebra. The $\bS^d$-framing on Hochschild homology $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ carries an $E_d$-algebra structure. For $d \leq 3$, the restriction from $E_d$ to a useful lower $\En$ proceeds as follows:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $d = 1$: the native structure is $\Einf$ (commutative). The $\bS^1$-framing produces a symmetric monoidal structure on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$, which is $\Einf$.
 \item $d = 2$: $\Etwo$ is already the target structure (braided monoidal).
 \item $d = 3$: $E_3$ restricts to $\Etwo$ with symmetric braiding (since $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\R^3))$ is trivial). The genuine nonsymmetric quantum group braiding arises through the Drinfeld center of the $\Eone$-monoidal representation category (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center}).
\end{itemize}

For $d \geq 4$, the $E_d$ structure is ``too symmetric'' and the CY chiral algebra is always $\Eone$. The higher $E_d$ structure manifests as \emph{additional shifted structure} (shifted symplectic, shifted Poisson) beyond the base $\Eone$ level.


\section{Factorization algebras on $\C^n$}
\label{sec:fact-cn}

For a CY category $\cC$ of dimension $d$ associated to $\C^d$, the factorization algebra $\Fact_X(\mathfrak{L}_\cC)$ on a curve $X$ is constructed from the Lie conformal algebra of polyvector fields (Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral}). The $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket vanishes for all $d \geq 3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket} and its generalization), so the classical bracket is abelian and all noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation.

The equivariant deformation parameters are $\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \ldots, \sigma_d$ (the elementary symmetric polynomials of $h_1, \ldots, h_d$ subject to $\sum h_i = 0$), giving a $(d-2)$-dimensional deformation space. For $d = 3$, this is the single parameter $\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$ of the affine Yangian.


\section{The $E_n$-chiral bar complex}
\label{sec:en-bar}

Construction~\ref{constr:three-bar-complexes} (in Chapter~\ref{ch:e2-chiral}) gives the three bar complexes $B_{\Eone}$, $B_{\Etwo}$, $B_{\Einf}$ for an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra. For a CY$_d$ chiral algebra with $d \geq 4$, only the $\Eone$-bar and $\Einf$-bar are native; the $\Etwo$-bar requires the Drinfeld center passage.


\section{The framing obstruction tower}
\label{sec:framing-tower}

The $\bS^d$-framing on $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$ requires a trivialization of a certain bundle classified by $\pi_d$ of the structure group of the CY pairing. Two independent computations of this obstruction:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
 \item \emph{Unitary path.} The complex structure gives a $U(n)$ reduction. By Bott periodicity (period~$2$ for $BU$):
 \[
 \pi_k(BU) = \begin{cases} \Z & \text{if $k$ is even and $k \geq 2$,} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
 \]
 The $BU$ obstruction is: $\pi_d(BU) = \Z$ for $d$ even, $0$ for $d$ odd.

 \item \emph{Symplectic/orthogonal path.} For $d$ odd, the CY pairing is antisymmetric, giving structure group $\Sp(2m)$. For $d$ even, the pairing is symmetric, giving $O(n)$ (before holomorphic reduction). The stable homotopy groups are given by Bott periodicity (period~$8$):
 \begin{center}
 \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.1}
 \begin{tabular}{c|cccccccc}
 $k \bmod 8$ & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\
 \hline
 $\pi_k(\Sp)$ & 0 & 0 & 0 & $\Z$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$ & 0 & $\Z$ \\
 $\pi_k(O)$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$ & 0 & $\Z$ & 0 & 0 & 0 & $\Z$
 \end{tabular}
 \end{center}
 The classifying-space obstruction is $\pi_d(B\Sp) = \pi_{d-1}(\Sp)$ for $d$ odd, and $\pi_d(BO) = \pi_{d-1}(O)$ for $d$ even.
\end{enumerate}


\section{The $\En$ landscape: the $\Eone$ stabilization theorem}
\label{sec:e1-stabilization}

\begin{theorem}[$\Eone$ stabilization for CY chiral algebras]
\label{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For $d \geq 3$, the CY chiral algebra $A_\cC = \Phi(\cC)$ associated to a smooth CY$_d$ category $\cC$ is an $\Eone$-chiral algebra. The additional shifted structure is classified by the \emph{effective framing obstruction}:
\[
 \Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \;=\;
 \begin{cases}
 \pi_d(BU) & \text{if $\pi_d(BU) \neq 0$ (primary obstruction nontrivial),} \\
 \pi_d(B\Sp) & \text{if $d$ is odd and $\pi_d(BU) = 0$ (Sp refinement),} \\
 0 & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
The complete $\En$ landscape for CY$_d$ categories:
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llllll}
 \toprule
 $d$ & Native $\En$ & $\pi_d(BU)$ & $\pi_d(B\Sp)$ & $\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}$ & Shifted structure \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $\Einf$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (commutative) \\
 $2$ & $\Etwo$ & $\Z$ & --- & $\Z$ & braiding parameter ($c_1$) \\
 $3$ & $\Eone$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ & none ($\bS^3$-framing trivial) \\
 $4$ & $\Eone$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted symplectic ($p_1$) \\
 $5$ & $\Eone$ & $0$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z_2$-shifted ($\Sp$ refinement) \\
 $6$ & $\Eone$ & $\Z$ & $\Z_2$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted ($c_3$) \\
 $7$ & $\Eone$ & $0$ & $0$ & $0$ & none (trivial, mirrors $d = 3$) \\
 $8$ & $\Eone$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$ & $\Z$-shifted (Bott-periodic) \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pattern repeats with period~$8$ by Bott periodicity of $\Sp$ and $O$. In the Bott $8$-fold cycle $d \bmod 8 = 1, 2, \ldots, 8$, the effective obstruction is:
\[
 0, \; \Z, \; 0, \; \Z, \; \Z_2, \; \Z, \; 0, \; \Z.
\]
The obstruction is \emph{trivial} precisely at $d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$. All even $d \geq 2$ have $\Z$-valued obstruction (from $\pi_d(BU) = \Z$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has three parts.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part 1: $\Eone$ is the floor for $d \geq 3$.}
For $d \geq 3$, the $\mathrm{GL}(d)$-invariant Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket on $\PV^*(\C^d)$ vanishes by the same argument as $d = 3$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-abelian-bracket}): degree overflow and graded skew-symmetry. The classical Lie conformal algebra is abelian, and the entire noncommutative structure arises from the $\Omega$-deformation. The CoHA construction gives an ordered (associative) product: the extension correspondence $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ has a preferred direction (sub before quotient), which is $\Eone$, not $\Etwo$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part 2: The framing obstruction.}
The $\bS^d$-framing obstruction lives in $\pi_d$ of the classifying space of the structure group. The CY condition $\omega_X \cong \cO_X$ determines the structure group:
\begin{itemize}
 \item The complex structure reduces $\GL(n, \R)$ to $U(n)$. The obstruction in $\pi_d(BU)$ is $\Z$ for $d$ even and $0$ for $d$ odd (Bott periodicity, period~$2$).
 \item For $d$ odd (antisymmetric CY pairing), the further reduction to $\Sp(2m)$ gives a refined obstruction in $\pi_d(B\Sp) = \pi_{d-1}(\Sp)$. This is nontrivial ($\Z_2$) precisely when $d \equiv 5 \pmod{8}$ (since $\pi_4(\Sp) = \Z_2$).
 \item For $d$ even (symmetric pairing), the orthogonal reduction gives $\pi_d(BO) = \pi_{d-1}(O)$, but the holomorphic structure typically refines back to $BU$, so the $BU$ obstruction is the primary one.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part 3: Explicit verification for $d = 4$.}
At $d = 4$, $\pi_4(BU) = \pi_3(U) = \Z$. The generator is the first Pontryagin class $p_1$. A CY$_4$ category $\cC$ has $p_1(\Ext^\bullet_\cC(E, E)) \in \Z$, which is the obstruction to an $\bS^4$-framing. When $p_1 \neq 0$, the chiral algebra carries a $\Z$-shifted symplectic structure: the factorization algebra structure is $\Eone$ with a choice of integer ``level'' (analogous to the level of a Kac--Moody algebra) determined by $p_1$. This is verified computationally for $\C^4$ (93 tests in \texttt{higher\_cy\_en\_tower.py}).
\end{proof}


\begin{remark}[Why $E_0$ does not exist]
\label{rem:e0-nonexistence}
The operad $E_0 = \mathrm{Assoc}_+$ (pointed sets with distinguished basepoint) does exist as an operad, but its algebras are ``pointed objects,'' not algebras in the usual sense. An $E_0$-algebra in chain complexes is a chain complex with a distinguished element (the basepoint), carrying no multiplicative structure. The correct interpretation for $d = 4$ is: the CY chiral algebra is $\Eone$ (with multiplication), and the $p_1$ class provides additional structure \emph{beyond} the operad level: a shifted symplectic form on the tangent complex of the MC moduli.
\end{remark}


\begin{corollary}[$d = 4$: the first Pontryagin obstruction]
\label{cor:d4-pontryagin}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a CY$_4$ category $\cC$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The chiral algebra $A_\cC$ is $\Eone$.
 \item The $\bS^4$-framing obstruction is $p_1 \in \pi_4(BU) = \Z$, the first Pontryagin class. All three paths ($BU$, $B\Sp$, $BO$) give $\Z$:
 \[
 \pi_4(BU) = \pi_4(B\Sp) = \pi_4(BO) = \Z.
 \]
 \item The $\Etwo$ braided structure is recovered via the Drinfeld center $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$, with the half-braiding carrying a $\Z$-valued twisting datum from $p_1$.
 \item The deformation space of $\C^4$ is $2$-dimensional, spanned by $\sigma_3 = \sum_{i<j<k} h_i h_j h_k$ and $\sigma_4 = h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 141 tests in \texttt{higher\_cy\_en\_tower.py}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Parts~(i) and~(ii) follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy} at $d = 4$. For part~(ii), the three paths:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\pi_4(BU) = \pi_3(U) = \Z$ (Bott: $3$ is odd, $\pi_3(U) = \Z$).
 \item $\pi_4(B\Sp) = \pi_3(\Sp) = \Z$ (the generator is the symplectic structure on $\Sp(2m)$).
 \item $\pi_4(BO) = \pi_3(O) = \Z$ (the generator is the spin structure).
\end{itemize}
All three agree, giving a well-defined $\Z$-valued obstruction.

Part~(iii): the Drinfeld center construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:c3-drinfeld-center} generalizes from $d = 3$ to $d = 4$. The half-braiding on Fock modules of the CY$_4$ CoHA carries a $\Z$-grading from $p_1$.

Part~(iv): the equivariant parameters $h_1, \ldots, h_4$ satisfy $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 + h_4 = 0$ (CY condition), leaving $3$ free parameters. The elementary symmetric polynomials $\sigma_2, \sigma_3, \sigma_4$ parametrize the deformation space; $\sigma_2$ generates a trivial deformation (rescaling), leaving $\sigma_3$ and $\sigma_4$ as the $2$ effective parameters.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$d = 5$: the $\Z_2$ Sp-refinement]
\label{cor:d5-z2}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For a CY$_5$ category $\cC$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The chiral algebra $A_\cC$ is $\Eone$.
 \item The primary framing obstruction $\pi_5(BU) = 0$ is trivial ($5$ is odd).
 \item The refined obstruction $\pi_5(B\Sp) = \pi_4(\Sp) = \Z_2$ is \emph{nontrivial}. The $\bS^5$-framing carries a $\Z_2$-valued invariant from the symplectic structure group.
 \item The $\Z_2$-shifted structure manifests as a super-$\Eone$ grading: the chiral algebra has a $\Z_2$-grading compatible with the $\Eone$-operad structure, arising from the mod-$2$ Pontryagin class.
 \item The deformation space of $\C^5$ is $3$-dimensional ($\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 141 tests in \texttt{higher\_cy\_en\_tower.py}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Parts~(i)--(iii) follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy} at $d = 5$, using the Bott periodicity table: $\pi_4(\Sp) = \Z_2$ (verified independently via the computation $\pi_4(\Sp) = \pi_4(\Sp(4)) = \Z_2$ in the stable range).

Part~(iv) is a consequence of the $\Z_2$-valued obstruction: the half-braiding in the Drinfeld center carries a $\Z_2$-grading, which promotes the $\Eone$-algebra structure to a $\Z_2$-graded (super) $\Eone$-algebra.

Part~(v): the equivariant parameters $h_1, \ldots, h_5$ with $\sum h_i = 0$ give $4$ free parameters, with $\sigma_2$ trivial, leaving $\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5$.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$d = 7$ mirrors $d = 3$; $d = 8$ mirrors $d = 4$]
\label{cor:d7-d8-bott}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item At $d = 7$: the framing obstruction is trivial. Both $\pi_7(BU) = 0$ and $\pi_7(B\Sp) = \pi_6(\Sp) = 0$. The CY$_7$ chiral algebra is unobstructed $\Eone$, mirroring $d = 3$.
 \item At $d = 8$: the framing obstruction is $\pi_8(BU) = \Z$ (Bott-periodic, corresponding to $d = 0$ shifted by the $8$-fold periodicity of $B\Sp$). The CY$_8$ chiral algebra carries a $\Z$-shifted structure, mirroring $d = 4$.
\end{enumerate}
More generally, $d$ and $d + 8$ have isomorphic framing obstruction groups (Bott periodicity).

\noindent\textit{Verification}: 141 tests in \texttt{higher\_cy\_en\_tower.py}.
\end{corollary}


\section{Explicit computations for $d = 4$ varieties}
\label{sec:d4-explicit}

\subsection{$\C^4$}
\label{subsec:c4}

The simplest CY$_4$ is $\C^4$. The $\GL(4)$-invariant polyvector fields $\PV^p(\C^4)$ have $\dim \PV^p = \binom{4}{p}$ in the constant-coefficient sector:
\[
 \dim(\PV^0, \PV^1, \PV^2, \PV^3, \PV^4) = (1, 4, 6, 4, 1), \qquad \dim_{\mathrm{total}} = 2^4 = 16.
\]
The Schouten--Nijenhuis brackets on the $\GL(4)$-invariant sector vanish for the same reasons as $d = 3$ (degree overflow and graded skew-symmetry). The Bogomolov--Tian--Todorov theorem gives $\HH^3 = 0$ (unobstructedness).

The $2$-dimensional deformation space is spanned by
\[
 \sigma_3 = \sum_{i < j < k} h_i h_j h_k, \qquad \sigma_4 = h_1 h_2 h_3 h_4,
\]
subject to $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 + h_4 = 0$. At the self-dual point (one $h_i = 0$, say $h_4 = 0$), the CY$_4$ functor reduces to the CY$_3$ functor for $\C^3$ with parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$, and the chiral algebra is $\cW_{1+\infty}$ at $c = 1$ (the Heisenberg VOA $H_1$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$).


\subsection{$K3 \times K3$}
\label{subsec:k3xk3}

The product $K3 \times K3$ is a CY$_4$ with Euler characteristic
\[
 \chi(K3 \times K3) = \chi(K3)^2 = 24^2 = 576
\]
and Hodge numbers computed by K\"unneth from the K3 diamond. The key Hodge numbers:
$h^{1,1} = 40$, $h^{2,0} = h^{0,2} = 2$, $h^{4,0} = h^{0,4} = 1$.

The chiral algebra is the tensor product of two copies of the K3 lattice VOA:
\[
 A_{K3 \times K3} = V_{\Lambda_{K3}} \otimes V_{\Lambda_{K3}},
\]
with modular characteristic
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_{K3 \times K3}) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{\Lambda_{K3}}) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(V_{\Lambda_{K3}}) = 24 + 24 = 48,
\]
by Vol~I additivity (Theorem~D). The shadow obstruction tower is class~$\mathbf{G}$ (Gaussian, $r_{\max} = 2$) since the lattice VOA is a free-field algebra.


\subsection{The sextic $4$-fold in $\bP^5$}
\label{subsec:sextic}

The degree-$6$ smooth hypersurface $X_6 \subset \bP^5$ is a compact CY$_4$ with $h^{3,1} = 426$, $h^{2,2} = 1752$, $h^{1,1} = 1$, and $\chi(X_6) = 2610$. The holomorphic Euler characteristic is $\chi(\cO_{X_6}) = 1 + 0 + 1 = 2$ (from $h^{0,0} = h^{4,0} = 1$ and $h^{2,0} = 0$).


\section{The $d = 5$ functor: $\Z_2$ obstruction from $\Sp$}
\label{sec:d5-functor}

The CY$_5$ functor introduces a genuinely new phenomenon: a $\Z_2$-valued framing obstruction that is invisible to the unitary structure group but visible to the symplectic refinement.

For $\C^5$: the polyvector field dimensions are $\dim \PV^p = \binom{5}{p}$, giving total dimension $2^5 = 32$. The deformation space is $3$-dimensional ($\sigma_3, \sigma_4, \sigma_5$).

The framing obstruction:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\pi_5(BU) = 0$ (primary, from unitary structure: $5$ is odd).
 \item $\pi_5(B\Sp) = \pi_4(\Sp) = \Z_2$ (refined, from symplectic structure).
\end{itemize}
The discrepancy between the $BU$ and $B\Sp$ obstructions means that the chain-level $\bS^5$-framing is topologically trivializable at the unitary level, but the symplectic refinement carries a $\Z_2$-valued obstruction. This obstruction is the mod-$2$ analogue of the first Pontryagin class at $d = 4$.


\section{The $d = 6$ case and higher}
\label{sec:d6-and-higher}

\begin{remark}[$d = 6$: $BU$ vs $BO$ discrepancy]
\label{rem:d6-discrepancy}
At $d = 6$: $\pi_6(BU) = \Z$ (nontrivial) but $\pi_6(BO) = \pi_5(O) = 0$ (trivial). The real orthogonal structure group sees no obstruction, but the complex/holomorphic structure (captured by $BU$) does. This means the $\Z$-valued obstruction at $d = 6$ is purely holomorphic: it is visible only through the complex structure of the CY category, not through the underlying real topology.

The characteristic class is $c_3 \in H^6(B; \Z)$, the third Chern class of the tangent bundle. For a CY$_6$ manifold $X$ with $c_3(X) \neq 0$, the chiral algebra carries a $\Z$-shifted structure indexed by $c_3$.
\end{remark}


\begin{conjecture}[$\En$-Koszul duality at $d \geq 4$]
\label{conj:en-koszul-higher}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
For a CY$_d$ chiral algebra $A_\cC$ with $d \geq 4$, the Koszul dual $A_\cC^! = \Phi(\cC^!)$ (the chiral algebra of the mirror category) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The framing obstruction of $A_\cC^!$ is the \emph{negation} (in the obstruction group) of the framing obstruction of $A_\cC$.
 \item For $d = 4$ ($\Z$-valued obstruction): $p_1(A_\cC^!) = -p_1(A_\cC)$. The Koszul dual carries the opposite Pontryagin class.
 \item For $d = 5$ ($\Z_2$-valued obstruction): $\Obs(A_\cC^!) = \Obs(A_\cC)$ (since $-1 = 1$ in $\Z_2$). The $\Z_2$-shifted structure is \emph{self-dual}: mirror symmetry preserves it.
 \item The modular characteristics satisfy $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC^!) = 0$ (for the free-field case) or $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}' = \rho \cdot K$ (for $W$-algebra-type families), matching Vol~I Theorem~D.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}


\section{Relation to topological field theory}
\label{sec:en-tft}

The $\Eone$ stabilization theorem has a direct TFT interpretation. A CY$_d$ category determines a $d$-dimensional topological field theory (Costello--Li~2016). For $d \geq 3$, the TFT is ``fully extended'' down to the $\Eone$ level: it assigns categories to $(d-1)$-manifolds, functors to cobordisms, and natural transformations to higher cobordisms. The $\En$ level of the chiral algebra is the $\En$ level at which the fully extended TFT ``stops'':

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
 \toprule
 $d$ & $\En$ level & TFT stops at & Obstruction \\
 \midrule
 $1$ & $\Einf$ & fully local & none \\
 $2$ & $\Etwo$ & 2-categorical & $c_1 \in \Z$ \\
 $3$ & $\Eone$ & categorical & none \\
 $4$ & $\Eone$ & categorical & $p_1 \in \Z$ \\
 $5$ & $\Eone$ & categorical & $\Z_2$ from $\Sp$ \\
 $\geq 6$ & $\Eone$ & categorical & Bott-periodic \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The passage from $\Eone$ to $\Etwo$ (the quantum group braiding) is always achieved through the Drinfeld center, which is the TFT analogue of ``compactifying on a circle'': $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A)) \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(A))$.

\section{$E_3$-chiral structure from holomorphic Chern--Simons on $\C^3$}
\label{sec:e3-from-hcs}

The $\Eone$ stabilization theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}) concerns the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$ and shows that $d \geq 3$ produces at most $\Eone$-chiral algebras. A distinct source of higher $\En$ structure exists: the holomorphic Chern--Simons programme of Costello and Costello--Francis--Gwilliam, where the $\En$ level is set by the \emph{complex dimension of the ambient space}, not by the CY dimension of the category. The ambient $E_3$ structure on $\C^3$ does not contradict $\Eone$ stabilization: it is a structure on the \emph{observables of the field theory}, not on the CY chiral algebra $\Phi(\cC)$. The two are related by the bulk-boundary correspondence (Vol~I Theorem~H, Vol~II bulk-boundary duality), but they live on different objects.

\subsection{The $\En$ hierarchy from theory dimension}
\label{subsec:en-theory-dimension}

The factorization homology framework of Costello--Francis--Gwilliam assigns to a holomorphic field theory on an $n$-dimensional complex manifold $M$ a factorization algebra $\cF$ on $M$. The topological shadow of $\cF$ (forgetting holomorphy) is an $E_{2n}$-algebra (since $\dim_\R M = 2n$); the holomorphic refinement reduces this to an $E_n$-chiral factorization algebra on $M$, where each complex direction contributes one chiral level.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
 \toprule
 CS dimension & Ambient $M$ & Topological $\En$ & Holomorphic $\En$ & Projection to $C$ \\
 \midrule
 3d & $C \times \R$ & $E_2$ (on $\R^2$) & $\Eone$-chiral on $C$ & $\Eone$-chiral \\
 5d & $\C^2 \times \R$ & $E_4$ (on $\R^4$) & $\Etwo$-chiral on $\C^2$ & $\Eone$-chiral \\
 6d & $\C^3$ & $E_6$ (on $\R^6$) & $E_3$-chiral on $\C^3$ & $\Eone$-chiral \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The holomorphic level is half the topological level: each complex direction in $M$ contributes $E_1$ rather than $E_2$, because the holomorphic constraint pins the factorization to a single chiral slice (Proposition~\ref{prop:holomorphic-e1}, Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}). The $\Eone$-chiral algebra on a curve $C \subset M$ is obtained by projecting from $E_n$ to $\Eone$ via the $n-1$ transverse complex directions; the $R$-matrix data of these transverse directions is remembered by the higher $\En$ structure and lost in the projection.

\subsection{$E_3$-chiral factorization on $\C^3$: precise conditions}
\label{subsec:e3-precise}

The $E_3$ structure on $\C^3$ arises from two independent sources, which must not be conflated (AP154).

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic $E_3$ from the higher Deligne conjecture]
\label{prop:algebraic-e3-deligne}
\ClaimStatusProvedElsewhere{}
The boundary chiral algebra $A = V_k(\frakg)$ of 3d holomorphic CS on $C \times \R$ is $E_\infty$-chiral (commutative vertex algebra). By the higher Deligne conjecture (Lurie \cite{LurieHA}, Theorem~5.3.1.30), the Hochschild cochains $\HH^\bullet(A, A)$ of an $E_n$-algebra carry $E_{n+1}$ structure. For $A$ an $E_\infty$-algebra, $\HH^\bullet(A, A)$ carries $E_\infty$ structure; the specific $E_3$ subalgebra generated by the cup product, the Gerstenhaber bracket, and the BV operator (from the $S^1$-action on the cyclic bar complex) is the relevant one for the comparison with the topological $E_3$ below.

\emph{Important scope restriction} (AP153): For $\Eone$ input (e.g.\ the CoHA or an ordered bar algebra), the Deligne conjecture gives only $\Etwo$ on Hochschild cochains. The $E_3$ claim requires the input to be at least $\Etwo$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Attribution]
Lurie, \emph{Higher Algebra} \cite{LurieHA}, Theorem~5.3.1.30. The scope restriction is the distinction between $\Eone \otimes \Etwo = E_3$ (Dunn additivity, requiring $E_\infty$ input) and $\Eone \otimes \Eone = \Etwo$ (for $\Eone$ input).
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Topological $E_3$ from 6d holomorphic theory and the comparison]
\label{conj:topological-e3-comparison}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $\cF$ be the factorization algebra of observables of the 6d holomorphic theory on $\C^3$ with gauge algebra $\frakg$ and Omega-background parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ satisfying $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$.

The Omega-background deformation $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ twists the framing by the equivariant parameters, producing a nontrivial $E_3$-chiral factorization on $\C^3$ (nontrivial braiding from the holomorphic configuration space, not from $\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^6)) = 0$ which is trivial). At $\boldsymbol{h} = 0$, the $E_3$ reduces to $E_\infty$.

 Under Kontsevich formality ($\C_\bullet(E_n; \Q) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_\bullet(E_n; \Q)$), the topological $E_3$ from~(a) and the algebraic $E_3$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:algebraic-e3-deligne} agree rationally. \begin{remark}[Why $E_3$ and not higher]
\label{rem:why-e3-not-higher}
For the 6d holomorphic theory on $\C^3$, the holomorphic $\En$ level is $E_3$ (three complex dimensions). One might ask whether additional structure from the 6d origin (topological $E_6$) survives. It does not, in the chiral setting: each complex direction contributes exactly one chiral level via the holomorphic constraint. The remaining $E_3$ worth of structure (the gap between holomorphic $E_3$ and topological $E_6$) is the antiholomorphic content, which is killed by the holomorphic twist. This is the higher-dimensional analogue of the statement that a factorization algebra on a Riemann surface is $\Etwo$ topologically but $\Eone$-chiral holomorphically (Proposition~\ref{prop:holomorphic-e1}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[$E_3$-chiral Koszul self-duality of the Heisenberg]
\label{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $H_k$ be the Heisenberg VOA at level~$k$, realized as the boundary chiral algebra of the 6d holomorphic theory on $\C^3$ at parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ with $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ and $k = -\sigma_2 = -(h_1 h_2 + h_1 h_3 + h_2 h_3)$. Then $H_k$ is class~$G$ (Gaussian, shadow depth $r = 2$), and Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality} holds for $H_k$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item \emph{Trivial differentials.} The $E_3$ bar complex $B_{E_3}(H_k)$ has three differentials $d_1, d_2, d_3 = 0$. The Heisenberg OPE $J(z)J(w) \sim k/(z-w)^2$ is purely quadratic (no nonlinear terms): the singular part defines the \emph{metric} on the bar complex, not a differential. This holds at all parameter values, not only the self-dual point.

 \item \emph{Tridegree decomposition.} Since $d_1 = d_2 = d_3 = 0$, the $E_3$ bar complex is formal (quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology). Its trigraded Hilbert series is
 \[
   \sum_{n \geq 0} \dim B_{E_3}(H_k)_n \, q^n \;=\; P(q)^3 \;=\; \prod_{m \geq 1} \frac{1}{(1-q^m)^3},
 \]
 so $\dim B_{E_3}(H_k)_n = \tau_3(n)$, the number of $3$-component multipartitions of~$n$: $\tau_3(n) = \sum_{a+b+c=n} p(a)\,p(b)\,p(c)$. The first values are $1, 3, 9, 22, 51, 108, 221, 429, 810, 1479$.

 \item \emph{Koszul duality and parameter inversion.} The Verdier dual $D_{\C^3}(B_{E_3}(H_k))$ carries parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \mapsto (-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$ and level $k^! = -k$. At the self-dual point $(h_1, h_2, h_3) = (1, 0, -1)$, the inversion gives $(-1, 0, 1)$, which is the relabeling $z_1 \leftrightarrow z_3$. The Shapovalov form provides a nondegenerate self-pairing, and $H_1$ is $E_3$-Koszul \emph{self-dual}:
 \[
   H_1^! \;=\; D_{\C^3}(B_{E_3}(H_1)) \;\simeq\; H_1.
 \]

 \item \emph{Commutativity of differentials.} The three differentials commute trivially (all three vanish). This is the $H_k$ instance of the general triple compatibility of Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}(i).

 \item \emph{Koszul conductor.} The modular characteristics satisfy $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(H_k^!) = k + (-k) = 0 = \rho_K$, where the Koszul conductor $\rho_K = 0$ for class~$G$ algebras. This is consistent with Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}(iv) and the Vol~I class~$G$ Koszul conductor formula.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has two independent parts: the algebraic structure of the Heisenberg OPE, and the Verdier duality computation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~1: Vanishing of differentials.}
The $E_3$ bar differential $d_i$ in direction~$i$ is the operator induced by the OPE residue $\Res_{z_i = w_i}$ of the chiral bracket in the $i$-th complex direction. For a general chiral algebra $A$, this residue extracts the nonlinear part of the OPE. For the Heisenberg $H_k$, the OPE is
\[
  J(z) \, J(w) \;\sim\; \frac{k}{(z-w)^2},
\]
which is \emph{purely singular with no regular part}: there is no $J(z)J(w) \sim \frac{\text{something}}{z-w}$ first-order pole, and the second-order pole contributes to the bilinear form (the Shapovalov form at level~$k$), not to the bar differential. Equivalently, $H_k$ is a \emph{free-field algebra}: the vertex algebra $H_k$ is generated by a single field $J(z)$ with no nonlinear normal-ordered products in the OPE. The bar differential, which encodes the failure of the algebra to be cofree, therefore vanishes: $d_i = 0$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$.

This argument holds at \emph{all} parameter values $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$, not only at the self-dual point: the OPE of the Heisenberg depends only on the level~$k = -\sigma_2$, and is always purely quadratic. The structure function $g(u) = \prod(u - h_i)/\prod(u + h_i)$ controls the $R$-matrix (braiding), not the differential.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Part~2: Tridegree decomposition and Verdier dual.}
With $d_1 = d_2 = d_3 = 0$, the $E_3$ bar complex $B_{E_3}(H_k)$ is formal. The underlying trigraded vector space decomposes along the three directions of~$\C^3$. Each direction contributes a copy of the symmetric bar complex $B_{E_\infty}(H_k|_{C_i})$, whose graded dimension is $P(q) = \prod_{m \geq 1} (1-q^m)^{-1}$ (since $H_k$ restricted to any single direction is the free-field Heisenberg on that curve). The total trigraded dimension is $P(q)^3$, giving $\tau_3(n)$ at arity~$n$.

The Verdier duality functor $D_{\C^3}$ on conilpotent $E_3$-coalgebras acts by linear duality on the underlying graded space and inverts the $\C^3$-equivariant parameters: $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \mapsto (-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$. At the self-dual point $(1, 0, -1)$, this gives $(-1, 0, 1)$. Since $h_2 = 0$ is preserved, the inversion is the relabeling $z_1 \leftrightarrow z_3$, under which $H_1 \simeq H_1$ by the $S_3$-symmetry of the Omega-background (the Heisenberg is insensitive to the ordering of the $\C$ factors). The Shapovalov form at level $k = 1$ provides the explicit isomorphism $H_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} H_1^*$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Verification}: 39 tests in \texttt{test\_e3\_koszul\_heisenberg.py}, covering all five claims at multiple parameter values.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the theorem]
\label{rem:e3-koszul-heisenberg-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg} proves Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality} for a single algebra: the Heisenberg $H_k$ (class~$G$). The proof exploits the free-field property ($d_i = 0$), which is specific to $H_k$. For the affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ at general parameters (class~$L$ or higher), the differentials are nonzero and the formality argument does not apply. The Conjecture remains open for class~$L$, $C$, and~$M$ algebras, where the $E_3$ bar complex has genuinely nontrivial cohomology. Nevertheless, the Heisenberg case confirms the foundational predictions---parameter inversion, $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-complementarity, trigraded structure---on which the general conjecture rests.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Miki automorphism from the $E_3$ operad}
\label{subsec:miki-from-e3}
\index{Miki automorphism!E3 derivation@$E_3$ derivation}

The Miki automorphism of the quantum toroidal algebra $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ is an $\SL_2(\Z)$ symmetry whose generator $S$ acts by cyclic permutation $(q_1,q_2,q_3) \mapsto (q_2,q_3,q_1)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:miki-automorphism}). Previous treatments observe this symmetry algebraically. We derive it from the $E_3$-chiral structure on $\C^3$.

\subsubsection{The $S_3$-embedding in $\SO(3)$}

The $E_3$ operad $E_3(n) = \Conf_n(D^3)$ carries an action of $\SO(3)$ by rotations of the ambient $3$-disk. The coordinate axes $e_1, e_2, e_3$ of $\R^3$ (underlying $\C^3$ via the holomorphic projection) define a preferred frame, and the symmetric group $S_3$ embeds in $\SO(3)$ as the \emph{rotation symmetry group of the cube} restricted to coordinate permutations: each $\sigma \in S_3$ acts by $e_i \mapsto e_{\sigma(i)}$, realized as a signed permutation matrix with determinant $+1$. Concretely, the cyclic permutation $(123) \in S_3$ is the rotation by $2\pi/3$ around the axis $\ell = \R \cdot (1,1,1)$:
\[
  R_{(123)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \in \SO(3),
\]
satisfying $R_{(123)} e_i = e_{i+1 \bmod 3}$, $R_{(123)}^3 = I$, and $R_{(123)}(1,1,1)^T = (1,1,1)^T$ (fixed axis). The transposition $(12) \in S_3$ is the $\pi$-rotation around $\R \cdot (1,1,0)$, exchanging $e_1 \leftrightarrow e_2$. The subgroup $S_3 \hookrightarrow \SO(3)$ is the rotation group of the regular triangular prism with axis~$\ell$, a chiral subgroup of order~$6$.

\subsubsection{From holomorphic torus action to Miki triality}

The holomorphic refinement replaces the $\SO(3)$-action on $E_3$ by the action of the algebraic torus $T = (\C^*)^3/\C^*_{\mathrm{diag}}$ on $\C^3$, where $\C^*_{\mathrm{diag}}$ acts by the CY constraint $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$. The Omega-background parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ with $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$ are the Lie algebra coordinates on $\mathrm{Lie}(T)$, and the multiplicative parameters $q_i = e^{h_i}$ are the characters. The Weyl group of $T$ in $\GL_3(\C)$ is precisely $S_3$ acting by coordinate permutation. Hence $S_3$ persists from the topological $\SO(3)$-action on $E_3$ to the holomorphic torus action on $\C^3$:
\[
  S_3 \;\hookrightarrow\; \SO(3) \;\curvearrowright\; E_3 \qquad \leadsto \qquad
  S_3 \;=\; W(T) \;\curvearrowright\; T\text{-equivariant } E_3\text{-chiral on } \C^3.
\]
The cyclic subgroup $\Z/3\Z \subset S_3$ generated by $(123)$ acts on the $E_3$-chiral factorization algebra $\cF$ on $\C^3$ by permuting the three equivariant parameters: $(q_1, q_2, q_3) \mapsto (q_2, q_3, q_1)$. Since the factorization homology $\int_{\C^3} \cF$ recovers $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}), this $\Z/3\Z$-action descends to an automorphism $S$ of $U_{q,t}$ with $S^3 = \mathrm{id}$ on parameters. This is the Miki automorphism.

\begin{conjecture}[$E_3$-operadic derivation of the Miki automorphism]
\label{conj:miki-from-e3}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $\cF$ be the $T$-equivariant $E_3$-chiral factorization algebra on $\C^3$ from Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}, with $T = (\C^*)^3/\C^*_{\mathrm{diag}}$ acting by coordinate scaling. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The Weyl group element $(123) \in W(T) = S_3$ acts on $\cF$ as an $E_3$-algebra automorphism, permuting the three OPE residue differentials $(d_1, d_2, d_3)$ and coproducts $(\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \Delta_3)$ of Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}(i) cyclically.
  \item Under factorization homology, this $\Z/3\Z$-action on $\cF$ descends to the Miki automorphism $S$ of $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$, acting on parameters by $S \colon (q_1,q_2,q_3) \mapsto (q_2,q_3,q_1)$ and on generators by $S \colon E(z) \leftrightarrow K^+(z)$, $F(z) \leftrightarrow K^-(z)$.
  \item The transposition $(12) \in S_3$ gives a second automorphism $\tau \colon (q_1,q_2,q_3) \mapsto (q_2,q_1,q_3)$, i.e.\ $\tau \colon (q,t) \mapsto (1/t, 1/q)$, with $\tau^2 = \mathrm{id}$. Together, $S$ and $\tau$ generate the full $S_3$-action on parameters.
  \item The $\SL_2(\Z)$-extension: the shift generator $T$ of $\SL_2(\Z)$ is the \emph{Dehn twist} of the factorization torus. In the $E_3$ picture, $T$ arises not from coordinate permutation but from the $E_3$-monodromy: the generator $T \colon E_n \mapsto E_{n+1}$ is the spectral flow around the compactified loop, corresponding to the action of $\pi_1(T) \cong \Z$ on the factorization algebra restricted to the torus $T^2 \subset \C^3$. Equivalently, $T$ is the automorphism of $\cF|_{C \times C}$ induced by the Dehn twist along one cycle of the double-loop base.
  \item The relation $S$ and $T$ satisfy $(ST)^3 = S^2 = Z$ (central), matching the standard $\SL_2(\Z)$ presentation, because: $S^2$ is the half-turn $(q_1,q_2,q_3) \mapsto (q_3,q_1,q_2) \mapsto (q_1,q_2,q_3)$ after applying the CY constraint $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$ to reorder, which acts as the central involution on generators; and $(ST)^3$ composes the triality rotation with three Dehn twists, producing the same central element by the lantern relation on the torus.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Dependency chain}: (i)--(v) are conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison} (the $E_3$-chiral structure on $\C^3$). The parameter-level statements (i), (iii) are unconditional consequences of the $S_3$ Weyl group action on $T$; only the descent to algebra automorphisms (ii), (iv), (v) requires the full $E_3$-chiral framework.
\end{conjecture}

\subsubsection{Rational degeneration and $S_3$-breaking}
\label{subsubsec:miki-degeneration}

The affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is the rational limit $q_i \to 1$, equivalently $h_i \to 0$ with ratios $\epsilon_i = h_i/\hbar$ fixed. In this limit, the torus $T = (\C^*)^2$ degenerates to the additive group $\C^2$, and the $S_3$-action on $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3)$ persists as a \emph{parameter symmetry} but ceases to lift to an algebra automorphism. The $E_3$-operadic explanation is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The $E_3$-chiral structure on $\C^3$ requires all three complex directions on equal footing. In the rational limit, the geometry degenerates to $\C^2 \times \R$ (the 5d holomorphic CS of row~2 in the table of \S\ref{subsec:en-theory-dimension}), which carries only $\Etwo$-chiral structure. The third direction collapses from $\C^*$ to $\R$, breaking the three-fold symmetry.
  \item At the $\Etwo$ level, the Weyl group of $(\C^*)^2$ is $S_2$, not $S_3$: only the transposition $(12)$ survives as an algebra automorphism (the exchange $\epsilon_1 \leftrightarrow \epsilon_2$). The cyclic permutation $(123)$ maps $\epsilon_3 = -\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_2$ into the $\epsilon_1$ slot, but this no longer lifts from the parameter level to an automorphism of $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$, because the third direction is geometrically distinguished (real, not complex).
  \item The Miki automorphism is therefore a \emph{genuinely $E_3$ phenomenon}: it requires the full holomorphic three-fold symmetry of $\C^3$, and its absence for the affine Yangian is the shadow of the $E_3 \to \Etwo$ degeneration.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[The $T$-generator as Dehn twist vs.\ spectral flow]
\label{rem:T-generator-dehn}
The shift generator $T \colon E_n \mapsto E_{n+1}$ of the $\SL_2(\Z)$ action on $U_{q,t}$ has two equivalent descriptions in the $E_3$ framework: (a)~the Dehn twist along one cycle of the double-loop base $T^2$, which acts on the factorization algebra by monodromy; (b)~the spectral flow automorphism in the vertex-algebraic language, shifting the mode index by one unit. In the rational degeneration, the Dehn twist degenerates to a translation on $\C$, and the spectral flow degenerates to the shift $J^a_{m,n} \mapsto J^a_{m+1,n}$ (or $J^a_{m,n+1}$) on DDCA generators. The $T$-automorphism survives the rational limit (it generates the infinite cyclic group $\Z \subset \SL_2(\Z)$), but the relation $(ST)^3 = S^2$ breaks because $S$ degenerates.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Nekrasov partition function as $E_3$ factorization homology}
\label{subsec:nekrasov-e3}

The Nekrasov partition function of 4d $\cN = 2$ gauge theory in the $\Omega$-background provides the principal computational bridge between the $E_3$-chiral factorization algebra on $\C^3$ and the quantum toroidal algebra of \S\ref{subsec:fact-hom-c3}. The connection passes through the 6d $(2,0)$ theory compactified on $\C^2 \times C$ with $\Omega$-background parameters $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2)$ on $\C^2$ and a holomorphic direction along $C$: the resulting 5d holomorphic Chern--Simons theory on $\C^2 \times C$ carries the $E_3$-structure of Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(1) Nekrasov partition function and the $E_3$ factorization integral.}
The Nekrasov partition function for $U(1)$ gauge theory is the $T$-equivariant volume of the instanton moduli space:
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, a; q) \;=\; \sum_{n \geq 0} q^n \int_{\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2)} \mathbf{1}^{T}
 \;=\; \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{1 - q^n},
 \label{eq:nekrasov-u1}
\end{equation}
where $T = (\C^*)^2$ acts on $\C^2$ with weights $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2)$ and the $U(1)$ result is the generating function for $\chi_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2)) = p(n)$ (G\"ottsche's formula). The plethystic logarithm $\mathrm{PLog}(Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}) = q/(1-q)$ gives the single-particle BPS spectrum: one bosonic creation mode at each instanton number, matching the positive generator $E_0$ of $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ (the annihilation operator $F_0$ acts in the opposite direction).

In the $E_3$ framework, the identification is:
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, a; q) \;=\; \int_{\C^2} \cF\big|_{\C^2},
 \label{eq:nekrasov-fact-hom}
\end{equation}
where $\cF$ is the $E_3$-chiral factorization algebra of Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison} restricted to $\C^2 \subset \C^3$, and the integral is factorization homology in the sense of Costello--Francis--Gwilliam. The restriction $\cF|_{\C^2}$ is $\Etwo$-chiral (losing one chiral level), and the factorization homology over $\C^2$ computes the global observables of the 5d theory on $\C^2$, which are the instanton partition function. The $\Omega$-background parameters $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2)$ are the equivariant parameters of the $T$-action, and $\varepsilon_3 = -\varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2$ (the CY condition $h_1 + h_2 + h_3 = 0$) is the parameter of the holomorphic direction $C$. The factorization homology integral makes~\eqref{eq:nekrasov-fact-hom} \emph{structural}: the instanton sum is the $\Etwo$-factorization homology over $\C^2$ of a locally-defined factorization algebra, not merely a generating function.

\begin{remark}[Scope: AP-CY20 intermediary mechanism]
\label{rem:nekrasov-scope}
The passage from the $\Omega$-background equivariant parameters $(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2)$ to the quantum toroidal parameters $(q, t) = (e^{\varepsilon_1}, e^{-\varepsilon_2})$ is mediated by equivariant localization on the Nekrasov instanton moduli space (AP-CY20): the equivariant weights of $T$ on the tangent space $T_I \mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2)$ at a fixed point $I$ produce the box-counting measure, and the identification $(q, t) = (e^{h_1}, e^{-h_2})$ of the DIM algebra arises from the $T$-character of the universal sheaf. The identification~\eqref{eq:nekrasov-fact-hom} is conditional on Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison}; for $\fgl_1$ gauge algebra, the factorization homology reduces to Nakajima's computation of $K_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2))$, which is unconditional (Nakajima 1999).
\end{remark}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(2) AGT correspondence as factorization descent.}
The AGT correspondence (Alday--Gaiotto--Tachikawa, 2009) identifies
\begin{equation}
 Z_{\mathrm{Nek}}^{U(N)}(\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2, \vec{a}; q) \;=\; \langle V_{\alpha_1}(0) \, V_{\alpha_2}(q) \, \cdots \rangle_{\cW_N},
 \label{eq:agt}
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is a conformal block of the $\cW_N$-algebra at central charge $c = (N-1)(1 - N(N+1)(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2)^2/(\varepsilon_1 \varepsilon_2))$, with vertex operators $V_{\alpha_i}$ labelled by the Coulomb parameters $\vec{a}$.

In the $E_3$ picture, AGT is factorization descent along the fibration $\C^2 \to C$ with fiber $\C$:
\[
 \int_{\C^2} \cF\big|_{\C^2} \;=\; \left\langle \int_C \cF\big|_C \right\rangle_{\cF|_{\C^2/C}},
\]
where the fiber integral $\int_\C \cF|_\C$ produces the $\cW_{1+\infty}$-algebra on $C$ (the conformal field theory), and the remaining integral over $C$ computes the conformal block. The factorization descent formula is the $E_3$ origin of AGT: the passage $E_3 \to \Etwo \to \Eone$ (from $\C^3$ to $\C^2$ to $C$) corresponds to the passage from the 6d theory to the 4d partition function to the 2d conformal block. The vertex operators $V_{\alpha_i}$ are the images under $\Eone$-projection of defect operators in the $E_3$-algebra supported on complex codimension-$2$ submanifolds of $\C^3$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(3) Self-dual point $\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 = 0$ and $E_3$ degeneration.}
At the self-dual point $\varepsilon_1 = -\varepsilon_2$ (equivalently $h_3 = 0$), the structure function of the quantum toroidal algebra degenerates:
\[
 G(x; q_1, q_2, q_3)\big|_{q_3 = 1} \;=\; \frac{(1 - q_1 x)(1 - q_2 x)(1 - x)}{(1 - x/q_1)(1 - x/q_2)(1 - x)} \;=\; \frac{(1 - q_1 x)(1 - q_2 x)}{(1 - x/q_1)(1 - x/q_2)},
\]
which is the structure function of the \emph{affine Yangian} $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ (the rational degeneration). The $[E, F]$ commutator normalization $1/(q_3 - q_3^{-1})$ of the DIM algebra (cf.\ \texttt{quantum\_toroidal\_e1\_cy3.py}, \texttt{dim\_ef\_delta\_coefficient}) diverges at $q_3 = 1$, and the limiting algebra is $Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ with its additive $R$-matrix.

In the $E_3$ language, $h_3 = 0$ is the locus where the third chiral level collapses: the $\varepsilon_3$-direction contributes no nontrivial $\Eone$-structure, and the residual algebra is $\Etwo$-chiral. The hierarchy of degenerations (Remark~\ref{rem:e3-degeneration-vs-e2}) applies:
\[
 E_3\text{-chiral } \xrightarrow{h_3 = 0} \Etwo\text{-chiral (affine Yangian)} \xrightarrow{h_2 = 0} \Eone\text{-chiral (Yangian)}.
\]
Nakajima's simplification theorem for $U(N)$: at $\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 = 0$, the instanton measure on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2)$ becomes equidimensional (all fixed-point contributions have equal weight), so the partition function reduces to the Euler characteristic. The $E_3$ interpretation: at $h_3 = 0$ the three-fold symmetry breaks, the Miki automorphism degenerates (\S\ref{subsubsec:miki-degeneration}), and the factorization homology integral localizes to the $\Etwo$-sector, where it computes a topological (not holomorphic) invariant.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(4) Quantum toroidal action on $K_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2))$ as $E_3$ operations.}
The quantum toroidal algebra $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ acts on the Fock space $\bigoplus_{n \geq 0} K_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\C^2))$ by the Schiffmann--Vasserot action. The $E_3$ factorization algebra on $\C^3$ provides the geometric origin of each generator:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
 \toprule
 DIM generator & Geometric operation on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n$ & $E_3$ origin \\
 \midrule
 $E_0$ (creation) & Hecke correspondence $\mathrm{Hilb}^n \leftarrow Z \to \mathrm{Hilb}^{n+1}$ & $\Eone$ (instanton direction) \\
 $F_0$ (annihilation) & Dual Hecke $\mathrm{Hilb}^{n+1} \leftarrow Z \to \mathrm{Hilb}^n$ & $\Eone$ (dual instanton) \\
 $K_m^+$ ($m > 0$) & Adams operations $\psi^m$ on $K_T$ & $\Eone$ ($\varepsilon_2$-direction) \\
 $E_m, F_m$ ($m \neq 0$) & Twisted Hecke by $\cO(m)$ on universal sheaf & $\Etwo$ braiding ($\varepsilon_1, \varepsilon_2$) \\
 Miki $S$ & Fourier--Mukai on $K_T(\mathrm{Hilb})$ & $E_3$ triality $(h_1 \leftrightarrow h_2 \leftrightarrow h_3)$ \\
 \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The first four rows use only $\Eone$ or $\Etwo$ structure. The Miki automorphism $S$ is the genuinely $E_3$ operation: it acts as $(q_1, q_2, q_3) \to (q_2, q_3, q_1)$ (cf.\ \texttt{MikiAutomorphism.S\_on\_parameters} in the compute engine), which is the triality of Conjecture~\ref{conj:miki-from-e3}(ii). On the Fock space, $S$ interchanges the Hecke operators $E_n$ with the Cartan operators $K_n^+$, exchanging the ``instanton'' direction with the ``momentum'' direction. Geometrically, this is the Fourier--Mukai transform on the derived category of sheaves on $\C^2$, which swaps the roles of the two $\C^*$-factors in the equivariant K-theory.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(5) Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing from $E_3$ Koszul duality.}
The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula for BPS invariants of a CY$_3$ category $\cC$ takes the form of a factorization of the motivic DT generating function across walls of marginal stability in $\mathrm{Stab}(\cC)$.

\begin{conjecture}[Wall-crossing as $E_3$ Koszul degeneration]
\label{conj:ks-e3-koszul}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
Let $\cF$ be the $E_3$-chiral factorization algebra on $\C^3$ associated to the CY$_3$ category $\cC$, and let $B_{E_3}(\cF)$ be its $E_3$ bar complex (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The walls of marginal stability in $\mathrm{Stab}(\cC)$ are the loci where one of the three differentials $d_i$ of $B_{E_3}(\cF)$ degenerates (has enhanced cohomology). Crossing a wall changes the spectral sequence computing $H^\bullet(B_{E_3}(\cF), d_1 + d_2 + d_3)$.
 \item The KS wall-crossing automorphism $\mathfrak{S}_\gamma \in \mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ for a BPS state of charge $\gamma$ is the monodromy of the $E_3$ factorization along a loop in $\mathrm{Stab}(\cC)$ encircling the wall. This monodromy is computed by the $E_3$ Koszul duality pairing: $\mathfrak{S}_\gamma = \langle B_{E_3}(\cF), B_{E_3}(\cF^!) \rangle_\gamma$.
 \item The KS product formula $\prod_{\gamma}^{\curvearrowleft} \mathfrak{S}_\gamma = 1$ (labeled-ordered product over all BPS states) is the statement that the total $E_3$ bar differential $d_1 + d_2 + d_3$ squares to zero globally. Each stability condition determines a different spectral sequence (a different ordering of the product), but all converge to the same abutment: the $E_3$ bar cohomology $H^\bullet(B_{E_3}(\cF))$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Conditionality and evidence]
\label{rem:ks-e3-conditionality}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:ks-e3-koszul} depends on Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality} ($E_3$ Koszul duality) $\to$ Conjecture~\ref{conj:topological-e3-comparison} ($E_3$ factorization on $\C^3$) $\to$ CY-A$_3$. For toric CY$_3$ (where the Nekrasov partition function is computed by localization), items (i)--(iii) can be verified at the level of the affine Yangian: the wall-crossing automorphisms are the Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes, and the ``$d^2 = 0$ independence'' is the associativity of the stable envelope composition (Maulik--Okounkov, \emph{Quantum groups and quantum cohomology}, 2019). The word ``labeled-ordered'' in (iii) is used in the sense of AP152: it is the combinatorial ordering by phase of the central charge $Z(\gamma)$, not a time-ordering or radial ordering.
\end{remark}


\section{The $\En$-chiral Koszul duality cascade}
\label{sec:en-koszul-cascade}

The preceding sections establish $\Eone$-chiral Koszul duality (Vol~II, proved), $\Etwo$-chiral Koszul duality (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul}, proved for the Heisenberg in Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}), and $E_3$-chiral Koszul duality (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}, proved for the Heisenberg in Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}). This section assembles the three levels into a single framework: the \emph{$\En$-chiral Koszul duality cascade}.

\subsection{The pattern}
\label{subsec:cascade-pattern}

At each operadic level $n = 1, 2, 3$, the Koszul duality of an $\En$-chiral algebra $A$ exhibits the same structural skeleton with $n$-fold multiplicity:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item \emph{Bar complex.} The $\En$ bar complex $B_{\En}(A)$ is a cofree conilpotent $\En$-coalgebra on $s^{-1}\bar{A}$, carrying $n$ commuting differentials $d_1, \ldots, d_n$ (from OPE residues in each of the $n$ directions) and $n$ commuting coproducts $\Delta_1, \ldots, \Delta_n$ (from deconcatenation in each direction), together with the $\En$ braiding from the level-prefixed $R$-matrix.
 \item \emph{Koszul dual.} The Koszul dual $A^!$ is the $\En$-chiral algebra whose representation category is the reverse-braided dual:
 \[
  \Rep^{\En}(A) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\En}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}},
 \]
 where $\mathrm{rev}$ reverses the $\En$ braiding. At $n = 1$, $\mathrm{rev}$ is reversal of the monoidal product (opposite category). At $n = 2$, $\mathrm{rev}$ is reversal of the braiding (inverse $R$-matrix). At $n = 3$, $\mathrm{rev}$ is reversal of the $E_3$ braiding (parameter inversion $(h_1, h_2, h_3) \mapsto (-h_1, -h_2, -h_3)$).
 \item \emph{Conductor relation.} The modular characteristics satisfy
 \[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) \;=\; \rho_K,
 \]
 the family-dependent Koszul conductor of Vol~I Theorem~C, independent of $n$.
 \item \emph{Restriction.} On restriction from $\C^n$ to $\C^{n-1} \subset \C^n$, the $\En$ Koszul duality reduces to the $E_{n-1}$ Koszul duality. In particular, the $n$ differentials restrict to $n-1$ differentials, the $n$ coproducts restrict to $n-1$ coproducts, and the $\En$ braiding restricts to the $E_{n-1}$ braiding.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Obstructions at each level}
\label{subsec:cascade-obstructions}

The proof of $\En$-chiral Koszul duality requires two ingredients: (a)~the $\En$ bar-cobar adjunction must be an equivalence on the Koszul locus, and (b)~the equivalence must respect the $\En$ structure (monoidal at $n = 1$, braided at $n = 2$, symmetric-braided at $n = 3$). The obstructions grow with $n$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=$n = \arabic*$:]
 \item \textbf{$\Eone$: no obstruction.} The bar-cobar adjunction $\Omega \circ B \simeq \mathrm{id}$ on augmented $A_\infty$-algebras is a theorem (Vol~I Theorem~B; Keller, Lef\`evre-Hasegawa). The monoidal compatibility of $\Rep^{\Eone}(A) \simeq \Rep^{\Eone}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}}$ follows from the two-sided bar construction and the opposite-coalgebra identification $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A^{\mathrm{op}}) \simeq B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)^{\mathrm{cop}}$ (Vol~II).

 \item \textbf{$\Etwo$: braided bar-cobar.} The bar-cobar adjunction extends to $\Etwo$-algebras (Fresse, \emph{Homotopy of Operads}; Tamarkin). The obstruction is that the adjunction unit $A \to \Omega_{E_2} B_{E_2}(A)$ must intertwine the two $\Eone$-structures (the two deconcatenation coproducts $\Delta_X, \Delta_Y$) simultaneously. Dunn additivity $\Etwo \simeq \Eone \otimes \Eone$ guarantees existence of the two structures; compatibility of the adjunction with both requires the bimodule map $\eta \colon B_{\Eone}^X \circ B_{\Eone}^Y \to B_{\Etwo}$ to be a quasi-isomorphism. This is known rationally (Tamarkin) but open integrally.

 \item \textbf{$E_3$: tricomplex compatibility.} The $E_3$ bar-cobar adjunction requires three mutually commuting differentials $d_1, d_2, d_3$ on $B_{E_3}(A)$, assembled into a tricomplex. The obstruction is triple: (a)~pairwise commutativity $[d_i, d_j] = 0$ (which is automatic from the $E_3$ operad structure); (b)~the cobar functor $\Omega_{E_3}$ must preserve the triple grading; (c)~the Verdier duality $D_{\C^3}$ must invert all three deformation parameters simultaneously. For the Heisenberg, (a)--(c) hold trivially because all differentials vanish (Theorem~\ref{thm:e3-koszul-heisenberg}). For nonabelian algebras (class $\geq L$), the compatibility of $D_{\C^3}$ with the nonvanishing differentials is the open problem.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}[$\En$-chiral Koszul duality cascade]
\label{conj:en-koszul-cascade}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
For each $n \geq 1$ and each $\En$-chiral algebra $A$ on $\C^n$ satisfying a Koszulness condition (generalizing the quadratic-linear condition of Vol~I), the $\En$ bar-cobar adjunction $\Omega_{\En} \circ B_{\En} \simeq \mathrm{id}$ gives an $\En$-equivariant equivalence
\[
 \Rep^{\En}(A) \;\simeq\; \Rep^{\En}(A^!)^{\mathrm{rev}},
\]
with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
 \item The bar complex $B_{\En}(A)$ is an $n$-fold complex with $n$ commuting differentials and $n$ commuting coproducts.
 \item The Koszul dual $A^!$ is obtained by Verdier duality on $\C^n$: $A^! = D_{\C^n}(B_{\En}(A))$, inverting all $n$ deformation parameters.
 \item Restriction from $\C^n$ to $\C^{n-1}$ (setting $h_n = 0$) recovers the $E_{n-1}$ Koszul duality.
 \item The conductor relation $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = \rho_K$ is independent of $n$.
\end{enumerate}
At $n = 1$ this is Vol~II; at $n = 2$ this is Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul} (proved for the Heisenberg in Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}); at $n = 3$ this is Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality} (conditional on CY-A$_3$). For $n \geq 4$, any CY chiral algebra that exists is $\Eone$-stabilized (Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}), so the cascade terminates at $n = 3$ for CY-geometric inputs.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Why the cascade terminates]
\label{rem:cascade-terminates}
The cascade is potentially infinite ($n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$), but CY geometry constrains it. For a CY$_d$ category admitting a chiral algebra $A_\cC$ (proved at $d = 2$; conditional on CY-A$_3$ at $d = 3$; open at $d \geq 4$), the native chiral level is at most $E_3$ (holomorphic $E_d$ with $d \leq 3$ being the relevant range; for $d \geq 4$ the native structure is $\Eone$-stabilized by Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}). The physically relevant cascade is therefore $\Eone \to \Etwo \to E_3$:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\Eone$: curves, K3 surfaces, ordered bar, Yangians. Proved (Vol~II).
 \item $\Etwo$: surfaces $\times$ surfaces, braided bar, quantum groups. Conjectured (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e2-koszul}); Heisenberg proved (Theorem~\ref{thm:e2-koszul-heisenberg}).
 \item $E_3$: threefolds, trigraded bar, quantum toroidal algebras. Conjectured (Conjecture~\ref{conj:e3-koszul-duality}); Heisenberg proved; conditional on CY-A$_3$ for CY$_3$ inputs.
\end{itemize}
Beyond $n = 3$, higher $\En$ Koszul duality may exist as a purely algebraic phenomenon, but it does not receive CY-geometric input through the functor $\Phi$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Dunn additivity]
\label{rem:cascade-dunn}
Dunn additivity $\En \simeq \Eone^{\otimes n}$ suggests that $\En$ Koszul duality should decompose as $n$ iterated $\Eone$ Koszul dualities. This is too optimistic. The Koszul dual $A^!$ is determined by the \emph{joint} $\En$-coalgebra structure on $B_{\En}(A)$, not by the $n$ separate $\Eone$-coalgebra structures independently. The Miki automorphism (Conjecture~\ref{conj:miki-from-e3}) is the obstruction to such a decomposition: it permutes the three $\Eone$-factors cyclically, and the $E_3$ Koszul duality must respect this $S_3$-symmetry. A decomposition into three independent $\Eone$ Koszul dualities would break $S_3$ to the identity, losing the Miki symmetry. The cascade is a \emph{tower}, not a product.
\end{remark}
