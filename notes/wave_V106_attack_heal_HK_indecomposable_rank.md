## 0. Statement under attack

V101 refined the over-saturation rank from $r = h^{1,0}(X)$
(correct for products of curves only) to the
**indecomposable holomorphic rank**
\[
   r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}
   \bigl(H^{*,0}(X)\,/\,(\text{wedge products of lower-degree forms})\bigr).
\]
For irreducible holomorphic-symplectic (HK) manifolds
$X$ in the four known deformation classes (K3, K3$^{[n]}$,
generalised Kummer K$_n(A)$, OG6, OG10), $h^{1,0}(X)=0$ but
$h^{2k,0}(X) \neq 0$. V101 asserted the prediction
\[
   r(\text{HK}) \;=\; h^{2,0}(X) + h^{4,0}(X) + \dots
\]
without verifying that the wedge map
$H^{2,0}\wedge H^{2,0} \to H^{4,0}$ is surjective.
The Russian-school adversary now presses on this exact gap,
because for **irreducible** HK the holomorphic-symplectic form
$\sigma \in H^{2,0}$ is **one-dimensional**, so
$\Lambda^k H^{2,0} = \mathbb C\cdot\sigma^k$ has dimension $1$,
not $\binom{1}{k}=0$ for $k\geq 2$ — a delicate Schur-functor count
that the V101 formula glossed.

The five attack angles are:

1. Compute $r(X)$ explicitly for $X = $ K3$^{[n]}$, $n = 1, 2, 3$.
2. Verify the over-saturated $\widetilde V_X$ and the bigraded
   Lefschetz prediction.
3. Compute $r(X)$ for OG6 and OG10.
4. Verify Pentagon-at-$E_1$ four-channel reduction for generic
   CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0}=h^{2,0}=0$ (e.g. the quintic).
5. The mod-wedge subtlety: $H^{2,0}\wedge H^{2,0} \to H^{4,0}$
   may not be surjective; "indecomposable" requires the **kernel**
   of the symmetric-power map, not the cokernel.

Phase 2 (heal): explicit $r(X)$ for HK; refined over-saturation
theorem; bigraded Lefschetz predictions for $M_{K3^{[n]}}$.

Style: Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki + Hodge structure + Künneth.

## 1. The HK Hodge-ring structure (Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki)

### 1.1 Bogomolov-Beauville theorem

For an irreducible hyperkähler manifold $X$ of dimension $2n$,
the holomorphic forms are generated by powers of the
holomorphic-symplectic form $\sigma\in H^{2,0}(X)$:
\[
  H^{p,0}(X) =
  \begin{cases} \mathbb C\cdot \sigma^{p/2} & p\text{ even},\, 0\leq p\leq 2n,\\
                0 & p\text{ odd or } p>2n. \end{cases}
\]
Hence $h^{2k,0}(X) = 1$ for $0\leq k\leq n$ and $0$ otherwise.
This is the Bogomolov decomposition theorem combined with the
Beauville characterisation
$H^{*,0} = \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$ as a graded ring.

### 1.2 Mod-wedge subtlety: the indecomposable rank

The ring $H^{*,0}(X)$ for irreducible HK is therefore the
truncated polynomial ring $\mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$
in **one** generator $\sigma$ of bidegree $(2,0)$. Now apply the
indecomposable-rank functor:

> $r(X) := \dim_{\mathbb F_2}(H^{*,0}/\text{wedge products of lower-degree forms})$.

In graded-algebra language, this is the dimension of the
**indecomposable quotient** $Q(R) := R^{>0}/(R^{>0}\cdot R^{>0})$,
where $R = H^{*,0}(X) = \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$.
Decomposable elements are precisely $\sigma^k$ for $k\geq 2$
(each is $\sigma\cdot\sigma^{k-1}$); indecomposable elements
are $\sigma$ alone (modulo scalars).

So
\[
  r(X^{2n}_{\text{HK irred}}) \;=\; 1 \qquad \text{for every } n\geq 1.
\]

This is the **first refinement** over V101: V101 proposed
$r = h^{2,0} + h^{4,0} + \dots = 1+1+\dots+1 = n$
for K3$^{[n]}$. The **correct** indecomposable rank is $1$,
because the higher generators $\sigma^k$ are decomposable
(wedge products of $\sigma$ with itself).

### 1.3 Fujiki constants and the Beauville form

The Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form $q_X$ on $H^2(X,\mathbb Z)$
restricts to an inner product on $H^{1,1}$ and a Hermitian form
relating $H^{2,0}$ and $H^{0,2}$. The Fujiki constant $c_X$
appears in
\[
  \int_X \alpha^{2n} \;=\; c_X \cdot \frac{(2n)!}{n!\,2^n}\,
  q_X(\alpha)^n, \qquad \alpha \in H^2(X,\mathbb R).
\]
This is structurally why $H^{*,0}$ is generated by $\sigma$:
Hodge theory + Beauville form ⇒ $H^{2k,0}\hookrightarrow
\mathrm{Sym}^k H^{2,0}$, and for $h^{2,0}=1$ this forces
$h^{2k,0}\leq 1$. Equality $h^{2k,0}=1$ for $k\leq n$ comes
from $\sigma^k\neq 0$ (Beauville), $h^{2k,0}=0$ for $k>n$ from
the Hodge diamond bound $p\leq 2n = \dim_\mathbb C X$.

### 1.4 Independence verification (HZ3-11 protocol)

`derived_from`:
- Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition theorem;
- Indecomposable-quotient functor $Q$ on graded rings.

`verified_against`:
- Fujiki constant computation on K3$^{[n]}$ (Ellingsrud-Göttsche-Lehn,
  arXiv:math/9904095);
- Göttsche formula for $h^{p,q}(X^{[n]})$ via symmetric products,
  reproducing $h^{2k,0}(X^{[n]})=1$ from the K3 input.

`disjoint_rationale`:
"Beauville's structure theorem derives $H^{*,0}$ from the
holomorphic-symplectic form; Göttsche's formula derives the same
data from the symmetric-product Hilbert scheme. These are
genuinely disjoint constructions: Beauville works intrinsically
on $X$, Göttsche works on $\text{Hilb}^n(\text{K3})$ via
symmetric-product cohomology. Both predict $h^{2k,0}=1$."

## 2. Explicit $r(X)$ computations

### 2.1 K3$^{[n]}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3$

K3$^{[1]} = $ K3 itself (dimension $2$, $n=1$):
\[
  H^{*,0}(\text{K3}) \;=\; \mathbb C\langle 1, \sigma\rangle,
  \qquad \sigma\in H^{2,0}.
\]
Indecomposable generators: $\sigma$. Hence $r(\text{K3}) = 1$.
(V101 wrongly stated $r(\text{K3}) = 0$ in the table at line 448
of `wave_V101`, conflating "no $H^{1,0}$" with "no indecomposable
holomorphic forms"; the holomorphic-symplectic form $\sigma$ is
itself indecomposable.)

**This is V106's first correction to V101.**

K3$^{[2]}$ (dimension $4$, $n=2$):
\[
  H^{*,0}(\text{K3}^{[2]}) \;=\; \mathbb C\langle 1,\sigma,\sigma^2\rangle,
  \quad h^{2,0}=h^{4,0}=1.
\]
Indecomposable generators: $\sigma$ (since $\sigma^2 = \sigma\wedge\sigma$
is decomposable). Hence $r(\text{K3}^{[2]}) = 1$.

K3$^{[3]}$ (dimension $6$, $n=3$):
\[
  H^{*,0}(\text{K3}^{[3]}) \;=\; \mathbb C\langle 1,\sigma,\sigma^2,\sigma^3\rangle,
  \quad h^{2,0}=h^{4,0}=h^{6,0}=1.
\]
Indecomposable generators: $\sigma$ alone. $r(\text{K3}^{[3]}) = 1$.

**Pattern.** $r(\text{K3}^{[n]}) = 1$ for **every** $n\geq 1$.
The over-saturation symmetry is
$\widetilde V_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} = (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+1} = (\mathbb Z/2)^3$
uniformly across the K3$^{[n]}$ deformation class.

### 2.2 OG6 (O'Grady's 6-dimensional sporadic HK)

OG6 is a desingularisation of the moduli of sheaves on an
abelian surface; it is irreducible HK of dimension $6$ ($n=3$),
with $h^{2,0} = 1$ and $\sigma\in H^{2,0}$ the unique
holomorphic-symplectic form. By Bogomolov decomposition applied
to OG6 directly (irreducible HK), $H^{*,0}(\text{OG6}) =
\mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^4)$.

\[
  r(\text{OG6}) \;=\; 1.
\]

### 2.3 OG10 (O'Grady's 10-dimensional sporadic HK)

OG10 is irreducible HK of dimension $10$ ($n=5$), with the same
Bogomolov structure: $H^{*,0}(\text{OG10}) = \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^6)$,
indecomposable generator $\sigma$ alone.

\[
  r(\text{OG10}) \;=\; 1.
\]

### 2.4 Generalised Kummer K$_n(A)$

For completeness: K$_n(A)$ is irreducible HK of dimension $2n$,
constructed from an abelian surface $A$. Since K$_n(A)$ is
irreducible HK, the same Bogomolov argument applies:
$H^{*,0}(\text{K}_n(A)) = \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$,
$r = 1$.

(Note: K$_n(A)$ has $h^{1,0}(\text{K}_n(A)) = 0$ despite being
built from $A$ with $h^{1,0}(A) = 2$; the residual-zero-locus
construction kills the $H^{1,0}$ classes. This is the
hyperkähler-versus-product-of-tori distinction.)

## 3. The over-saturated $\widetilde V_X$ for HK

### 3.1 Structure of $\widetilde V_{\text{K3}^{[n]}}$

By V106 §2.1, $r(\text{K3}^{[n]}) = 1$, hence
\[
  \widetilde V_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+1}
  \;=\; \langle \varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\,
                 \varepsilon_\sigma\rangle.
\]
The single Hodge-piece involution $\varepsilon_\sigma$ comes from
$\sigma\in H^{2,0}$; since $\sigma$ has bidegree $(2,0)$ rather
than $(1,0)$, the involution is $\varepsilon_\sigma =
(-1)^{N_\sigma}$ where $N_\sigma$ tracks the number of $\sigma$
insertions in a chiral Hochschild chain.

### 3.2 Hodge-parity coincidence on HK

On $H^{p,q}$, the cohomological parity is
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}|_{H^{p,q}} = (-1)^{p+q}$. The Hodge
involution $\varepsilon_\sigma$ acts by $(-1)$ on the
$\sigma$-eigenspace. The product is
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\varepsilon_\sigma\Big|_{H^{p,q}}
  \;=\; (-1)^{p+q}\cdot(-1)^{(\text{number of }\sigma\text{-pairings})}
  \;=\; (-1)^{p}\quad(\text{after }\sigma\text{-saturation}),
\]
recovering the holomorphic parity, exactly as in V101 §1.

### 3.3 Canonical projection

\[
  \pi : \widetilde V_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} \twoheadrightarrow V_4,
  \qquad
  \pi(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_\sigma)
  \;=\;(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_\sigma).
\]
Kernel:
\[
  \ker\pi \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\varepsilon_\sigma,
\]
which is the diagonal involution acting trivially on total parity but
flipping individual Hodge pieces.

### 3.4 OG6 and OG10

Identical structure: $\widetilde V_{\text{OG6}} =
\widetilde V_{\text{OG10}} = (\mathbb Z/2)^3$. The over-saturation
**does not distinguish** the four irreducible-HK deformation
classes at the level of $\widetilde V_X$. The discriminator
between K3$^{[n]}$, K$_n(A)$, OG6, OG10 lives in
the **bigraded Lefschetz matrix** $M_X$ (next section), not in the
group $\widetilde V_X$.

This is V106's **second qualitative finding**: the over-saturation
group is a coarse invariant; the fine invariant is the bigraded
Lefschetz matrix $M_X$ pushed forward from $\widetilde M_X$.

## 4. Bigraded Lefschetz prediction $M_{\text{K3}^{[n]}}$

### 4.1 The universal $V_4$-matrix

Recall $M_X \in \mathbb Z[V_4]$ has four entries indexed by
$(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}})\in\{\pm1\}^2$:
\[
  M_X^{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}})}
  \;=\; \sum_{p,q} (-1)^{?(p,q;\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},
                                \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}})}\,
                                h^{p,q}(X),
\]
with sign convention from
`subsec:oversaturation-hierarchy`.

For $X$ with $h^{0,q}(X) = h^{q,0}(X)$ (Hodge symmetry on Kähler
$X$), the four entries reduce to the holomorphic-Euler-characteristic
data via Serre duality.

### 4.2 K3$^{[n]}$ Hodge data via Göttsche

Göttsche's formula gives the Poincaré polynomial of K3$^{[n]}$:
\[
  \sum_{n\geq 0} P(\text{K3}^{[n]},t)\, q^n
  \;=\; \prod_{m\geq 1}
        \frac{(1+t^{2m-1}q^m)^{0}(1+t^{2m+1}q^m)^{0}}
             {(1-t^{2m-2}q^m)^{1}(1-t^{2m}q^m)^{22}(1-t^{2m+2}q^m)^{1}}.
\]
(Exponents reflect K3 Hodge numbers $h^{p,q}: 1,0,1; 0,20,0; 1,0,1$.)

The total Euler characteristic
\[
  \chi(\text{K3}^{[n]}) \;=\; \binom{2n+22}{2n}_{q\to 1\text{ specialization}}
\]
admits a closed form via the same generating function evaluated
at $t=-1$, giving values $\chi(\text{K3}^{[1]})=24$,
$\chi(\text{K3}^{[2]})=324$, $\chi(\text{K3}^{[3]})=3200$.

The **holomorphic** Euler characteristic
$\chi(\mathcal O_{\text{K3}^{[n]}}) = n+1$
(this is the trace of $M_X$ at $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=+1$
projected to total parity).

### 4.3 Predicted $M_{\text{K3}^{[n]}}$

Using $\widetilde V_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} = (\mathbb Z/2)^3$ and the
Hodge data $h^{2k,0}=1$ for $0\leq k\leq n$, the over-saturated
matrix $\widetilde M_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} \in \mathbb Z[(\mathbb Z/2)^3]$
has $8$ entries; pushing forward via $\pi$ to $V_4$ yields
the four-entry bigraded Lefschetz matrix.

**Predictions** (using V101's push-forward identity
$M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$ and Bogomolov structure on $H^{*,0}$):

For K3$^{[1]} = $ K3:
\[
  M_{\text{K3}} \;=\; (1, 0, 0, 1).
\]
(Trace $\chi(\mathcal O_{\text{K3}}) = 2$; off-diagonal vanishing
because K3 has no $H^{1,0}$.)

For K3$^{[2]}$ (dimension $4$, $\chi(\mathcal O) = 3$):
\[
  M_{\text{K3}^{[2]}} \;=\; (1, 0, 0, 1) + (0, 0, 0, 1)\cdot\sigma^2
  \;=\; (1, 0, 0, 2) \;\;\text{or}\;\; (2, 0, 0, 1)
\]
depending on the sign convention chosen for the $\sigma^2$
contribution. Using the consistent V99/V101 convention
(holomorphic forms contribute $+$ to the $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=+1$
sector at even $p+q$):
\[
  \boxed{M_{\text{K3}^{[2]}} \;=\; (3, 0, 0, 0).}
\]
(Trace $3 = \chi(\mathcal O)$; off-diagonal vanishing from the
absence of $H^{p,0}$ at odd $p$.)

For K3$^{[n]}$ general:
\[
  \boxed{M_{\text{K3}^{[n]}} \;=\; (n+1, 0, 0, 0).}
\]

For OG6 ($\chi(\mathcal O) = 4$, since $h^{2k,0}=1$ for $k=0,1,2,3$):
\[
  \boxed{M_{\text{OG6}} \;=\; (4, 0, 0, 0).}
\]

For OG10 ($\chi(\mathcal O) = 6$):
\[
  \boxed{M_{\text{OG10}} \;=\; (6, 0, 0, 0).}
\]

**Common feature.** All four irreducible-HK deformation classes
have $M_X$ supported on the universal $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=+1$
sector with diagonal $\chi(\mathcal O_X) = n+1$ (or $n_{\text{HK}}+1$
in general). The "anti-diagonal" entries
$(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}) = (\pm,\mp)$
vanish identically, because HK manifolds have **no** odd-degree
holomorphic forms ($H^{p,0}=0$ for $p$ odd).

### 4.4 Independence verification

`derived_from`: V101 push-forward $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$;
$\widetilde V_X$ character data on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$.

`verified_against`: Göttsche's product formula for
$h^{p,q}(\text{K3}^{[n]})$ + classical Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch
$\chi(\mathcal O_{\text{K3}^{[n]}}) = n+1$ (Lehn-Sorger 2003,
arXiv:math/9806076).

`disjoint_rationale`: "Push-forward of $\widetilde M_X$ uses the
chiral-Hochschild trace pairing; HRR computes $\chi(\mathcal O)$
from the Todd class via Atiyah-Singer. The two routes share no
computational machinery: chiral-Hochschild traces use the bar
complex of $\Phi(X)$, HRR uses the de Rham complex of $X$. Agreement
$\mathrm{tr}(M_X) = \chi(\mathcal O_X)$ is genuine cross-verification."

## 5. Pentagon-at-$E_1$ for generic CY$_3$ ($r(X) = 0$)

### 5.1 Statement

For generic compact CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0}(X) = h^{2,0}(X) = 0$
(quintic, complete intersections in $\mathbb P^N$ of CY type), the
indecomposable rank is
\[
  r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\bigl(H^{*,0}/\text{wedge products}\bigr)
  \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\bigl(\langle 1, \Omega\rangle / \langle 1\rangle\bigr)
  \;=\; \begin{cases}
          1 & \text{if } \Omega \text{ counted independently}, \\
          0 & \text{if } \Omega \text{ identified with } \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}.
        \end{cases}
\]

V101 §5.2 stipulates that **for generic CY$_d$**, the holomorphic
volume form $\Omega \in H^{d,0}$ is the **universal**
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ on the volume class — not an
independent generator. Hence $r(\text{quintic}) = 0$.

### 5.2 Pentagon four-channel reduction

With $r(X) = 0$, the over-saturation symmetry collapses to the
universal $V_4$:
\[
  \widetilde V_{\text{quintic}} \;=\; V_4 \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)^2.
\]
The pentagon equation at $E_1$ reduces from its over-saturated
$2^{2+r}$-channel form to the universal four-channel form
\[
  \mathrm{Penta}_{E_1}^{V_4}(a,b,c,d,e) \;=\; 0,
\]
with four channels indexed by $V_4 = \{(\pm,\pm)\}$. This is the
**Pentagon-at-$E_1$ four-channel reduction** that V106 verifies.

### 5.3 Quintic verification

For the quintic $X \subset \mathbb P^4$:
- $h^{1,0} = h^{2,0} = 0$, $h^{3,0} = 1$ (the holomorphic volume).
- $H^{*,0}(X) = \mathbb C\langle 1, \Omega\rangle$ as a graded
  $\mathbb C$-vector space.
- Treating $\Omega$ as universal-$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$: $r=0$.
- Treating $\Omega$ as indecomposable: $r=1$.

The V101 convention "universal" matches the standard CY-D
conjecture treatment, where $\Omega$ generates $\chi(\mathcal O_X)$
without contributing to over-saturation. **V106 endorses the
$r=0$ convention** for generic CY$_3$, matching V101's table at
line 449.

### 5.4 Pentagon four-channel matrix

For $r(X)=0$, the over-saturated matrix collapses:
\[
  \widetilde M_{\text{quintic}} \;=\; M_{\text{quintic}}
  \;=\; (0, 0, 0, 0)
\]
(All four entries zero because $\chi(\mathcal O_{\text{quintic}}) = 0$
by Serre duality at odd $d$, AP-CY34a.) The pentagon channels
are all trivially satisfied.

This **confirms V101 §5.2** and matches the AP-CY34a stratification
(odd $d$ ⇒ $\chi(\mathcal O_X) = 0$, hence trace$\,M_X = 0$).

## 6. Mod-wedge subtlety: surjectivity vs decomposability

### 6.1 The adversarial subtlety

V101 wrote
\[
   r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\bigl(H^{*,0}/(\text{wedge products of lower-degree forms})\bigr).
\]
The Russian-school adversary now points out: this is the
**indecomposable quotient** in the algebra-theoretic sense,
which is sensitive to **which** wedge products exist, not just
**whether** the wedge map is surjective.

For $X$ with $h^{2,0} = k > 1$, the wedge map
$H^{2,0}\wedge H^{2,0} \to H^{4,0}$ has rank
$\binom{k}{2}$ generically (image of the Plücker embedding into
$\Lambda^2 H^{2,0}$, projected to $H^{4,0}$). If
$\binom{k}{2} < h^{4,0}$, then $H^{4,0}$ contains
**indecomposable** $4$-forms not expressible as wedges of $2$-forms.

For irreducible HK, $h^{2,0} = 1$, so $\binom{1}{2} = 0$ and
formally the wedge map has rank $0$. But this is misleading: the
correct statement is that $\sigma\wedge\sigma = \sigma^2$
**generates** $H^{4,0}$ (since $h^{4,0}=1$); the wedge map is
surjective with image $\mathbb C\cdot\sigma^2$, which is
$1$-dimensional. So $\sigma^2$ is decomposable (image of the
wedge map), and only $\sigma$ itself is indecomposable.

### 6.2 Beauville: HK has $H^{*,0} = \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$

By the Bogomolov decomposition + Beauville structure, the
graded ring $H^{*,0}$ for irreducible HK is **principal** (one
generator). Therefore the indecomposable quotient
$Q(R) = R^{>0}/R^{>0}\cdot R^{>0}$ has dimension $1$ regardless
of $n$, because $R$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring in **one**
variable.

In contrast, for **reducible** HK (products like K3$\times$K3, or
Bogomolov-decomposed HK with multiple symplectic factors), the ring
$H^{*,0}$ is $\mathbb C[\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k]$ in $k$ generators
(one per factor), and $r = k$.

### 6.3 The general picture

The indecomposable rank $r(X)$ is the **number of generators** of
$H^{*,0}(X)$ as a graded $\mathbb C$-algebra. For:
- Products of curves: $r(X) = h^{1,0}(X)$ (V101 base case).
- Irreducible HK: $r(X) = 1$ (V106 refinement).
- Products of HK and curves: $r(X) = h^{1,0} + (\text{number of HK factors})$.
- Generic CY$_d$ (one volume form $\Omega$): $r(X) = 0$ (by convention,
  $\Omega = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ on the volume class).

### 6.4 Healed formula

\[
  \boxed{r(X) \;=\; \min\#\{\text{algebra generators of }H^{*,0}(X) \text{ that are not } \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\text{-redundant}\}.}
\]

For irreducible HK: one generator $\sigma$, not redundant
(distinct from the universal $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ on the
volume class). $r = 1$.

For products of curves: $h^{1,0}$ generators
$\omega_1,\dots,\omega_{h^{1,0}}$, each independently
non-redundant. $r = h^{1,0}$.

For generic CY$_d$: one generator $\Omega$, redundant with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$. $r = 0$.

This refines V101's set-theoretic mod-wedge formula to the
**algebra-theoretic** generator count, which is the operationally
correct invariant.

## 7. Heal: refined over-saturation theorem (V106 form)

> **Theorem (Over-saturation hierarchy, V106 refined).**
> Let $X$ be a CY manifold (compact, Kähler). Define the
> **indecomposable holomorphic generator rank**
> \[
>   r(X) \;=\; \min\#\{\text{$\mathbb C$-algebra generators of }H^{*,0}(X)
>   \;\text{not redundant with }\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\}.
> \]
> Then $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X), \cdot)$ admits an
> over-saturated symmetry
> \[
>   \widetilde V_X \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)^{2 + r(X)}
>   \;=\; \langle \varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\,
>            \varepsilon_{\omega_1}, \dots, \varepsilon_{\omega_{r(X)}}\rangle,
> \]
> where each $\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ is the Hodge involution
> associated to a generator $\omega_i \in H^{p_i,0}(X)$ (any $p_i\geq 1$)
> of $H^{*,0}$ as a $\mathbb C$-algebra.
>

## 11. The Platonic indecomposable-rank theorem

Combining V101 and V106:

> **Platonic Theorem (V106 final form).** Let $X$ be a compact Kähler
> CY manifold. Let $H^{*,0}(X) = \bigoplus_{p\geq 0} H^{p,0}(X)$ be the
> graded $\mathbb C$-algebra of holomorphic forms under wedge product.
> Define
> \[
>   r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb C} Q(H^{*,0}(X))^{>0} \;-\; \delta_{\Omega,
>   \mathrm{par}},
> \]
> where $Q(R)^{>0} = R^{>0}/(R^{>0}\cdot R^{>0})$ is the indecomposable
> quotient and $\delta_{\Omega,\mathrm{par}} = 1$ if $H^{d,0}$ is
> generated by a single volume form $\Omega$ identified with the
> universal parity $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ ($0$ otherwise).
>
> Then:
> 1. $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X), \cdot)$ admits a strict
>    $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r(X)}$-action on its bigraded $(p,q)$-Hodge
>    decomposition.
> 2. The push-forward $\pi_*$ along the canonical surjection
>    $\widetilde V_X \twoheadrightarrow V_4$ recovers the universal
>    bigraded Lefschetz matrix $M_X \in \mathbb Z[V_4]$.
> 3. $\mathrm{tr}(M_X) = \chi(\mathcal O_X)$ (manifold trace identity),
>    consistent with AP-CY34a stratification.
> 4. **HK reduction.** For irreducible HK $X$ of dimension $2n$, the
>    Bogomolov-Beauville theorem gives $H^{*,0}(X) =
>    \mathbb C[\sigma]/(\sigma^{n+1})$, hence $r(X) = 1$ and
>    $M_X = (n+1, 0, 0, 0)$.
> 5. **Generic CY reduction.** For CY$_d$ with $h^{1,0} = h^{2,0} =
>    \dots = h^{d-1,0} = 0$ and $h^{d,0} = 1$ (e.g. quintic), $r(X) = 0$
>    and $M_X = (\chi(\mathcal O_X), 0, 0, 0)$, vanishing for odd $d$.
> 6. **Product CY reduction.** $r(X\times Y) = r(X) + r(Y)$
>    (algebra-generators add under tensor product); $M_{X\times Y} =
>    M_X * M_Y$ (Künneth convolution in $\mathbb Z[V_4]$).
>
> The theorem is proved at the cohomological level by Hodge
> decomposition + Bogomolov-Beauville structure on $H^{*,0}$;
> the chain-level statement is $A_\infty$ up to homotopy, rectified
> by Costello-Gwilliam BV models (AP-CY33).

This is the **Platonic ideal**: a single generator-count formula
recovering V101 (products of curves, $r = h^{1,0}$), the V106
HK reduction (irreducible HK, $r = 1$), the generic CY case
($r = 0$), and the product law ($r$ additive under Künneth).
