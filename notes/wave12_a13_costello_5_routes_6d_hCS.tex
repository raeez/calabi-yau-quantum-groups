Six-Dimensional Holomorphic Chern-Simons}

\begin{abstract}
On the affine Calabi-Yau threefold $\C^3$, holomorphic Chern-Simons
theory gives a BV prefactorisation algebra only after a scale-dependent
solution of the quantum master equation.  In complex dimension $3$ the
first obstruction is the quartic one-loop BV anomaly.  When the anomaly
vanishes or is cancelled by explicit counterterm data, the pushforward
to a holomorphic curve is an $\Eone$-chiral algebra.  The five standard
constructions are five presentations of the positive-half mode algebra
of this curve chiral algebra: BV observables, the five-dimensional
Omega-deformed boundary theory, the Schiffmann-Vasserot shuffle CoHA,
the crystal character, and the chiral Chevalley-Eilenberg envelope.
They are not five applications of one functor.  The full affine
Yangian, the $\cW_{1+\infty}$ vertex algebra, and the $K3\times E$
Borcherds-Kac-Moody target require Drinfeld double, centre, compact
Hall, and completion data beyond the positive-half hCS construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Affine BV Theory}

Let $Y=\C^3$ with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega_Y=dz_1\wedge dz_2\wedge dz_3$, and let $\frakg$ be a finite
dimensional Lie algebra with invariant pairing $\langle-,-\rangle$.
The classical holomorphic Chern-Simons fields are the shifted
Dolbeault complex
\[
  \Ecal_Y=\Omega^{0,\bullet}(Y,\frakg)[1],
  \qquad d_{\Ecal}=\dbar .
\]
An element $\mathcal A\in\Ecal_Y$ is the BV superfield.  Its degree
$0$ component is the ghost, its degree $1$ component is the
$(0,1)$-connection, and the higher Dolbeault components are antifield
partners under Serre duality.  The compactly supported pairing
\[
  \omega(\alpha,\beta)
  =
  \int_Y\Omega_Y\wedge\langle \alpha,\beta\rangle_{(0,3)}
\]
has cohomological degree $-1$, where the subscript means projection to
the $(0,3)$ Dolbeault part.

\begin{definition}[Classical hCS functional]
The classical BV action is
\[
  S_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal A)
  =
  \int_Y\Omega_Y\wedge
  \left(
    \frac12\langle \mathcal A,\dbar\mathcal A\rangle
    +\frac16\langle \mathcal A,[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]\rangle
  \right)_{(0,3)} .
  \label{eq:hcs-classical-action}
\]
For the ordinary connection component $A\in\Omega^{0,1}(Y,\frakg)$,
the two summands are $(3,3)$ forms:
$\Omega_Y\wedge\langle A,\dbar A\rangle$ and
$\Omega_Y\wedge\langle A,[A,A]\rangle$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Classical master equation]
\label{lem:cme}
The functional~\eqref{eq:hcs-classical-action} satisfies
\[
  \{S_{\mathrm{cl}},S_{\mathrm{cl}}\}_{\mathrm{BV}}=0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Hamiltonian vector field is
$Q_{\mathrm{cl}}\mathcal A=\dbar\mathcal A+\frac12[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]$.
Then
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{cl}}^2\mathcal A
  =
  \frac12[\dbar\mathcal A,\mathcal A]
  -\frac12[\mathcal A,\dbar\mathcal A]
  +\frac14[[\mathcal A,\mathcal A],\mathcal A]
  -\frac14[\mathcal A,[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]]
  =0
\]
by $\dbar^2=0$ and the Jacobi identity in $\frakg$.  This is exactly
the classical master equation for the odd symplectic pairing above.
\end{proof}

\section{Quantum Master Equation}

A quantum hCS observable algebra requires more than the classical
functional: gauge fixing, a propagator family, and counterterms solving
the scale-$L$ quantum master equation.

\begin{definition}[Quantum hCS datum]
\label{def:quantum-hcs-datum}
A quantum hCS datum on $Y$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item a gauge-fixing operator and heat-kernel propagators
  $P[\epsilon,L]$ for $0<\epsilon<L$;
  \item local counterterms $I^{\mathrm{CT}}[\epsilon]$;
  \item effective interactions
  \[
    I[L]=
    \lim_{\epsilon\to0}
    W\!\left(P[\epsilon,L],
      S_{\mathrm{cl}}+\hbar I^{\mathrm{CT}}[\epsilon]\right)
  \]
  satisfying Costello's renormalisation-group equation and the
  scale-$L$ quantum master equation
  \[
    \frac12\{I[L],I[L]\}_{\mathrm{BV}}
    +\hbar\,\Delta_L I[L]=0 .
    \label{eq:scale-qme}
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quantum observables]
\label{def:quantum-observables}
For an open set $U\subset Y$, the quantum observables are
\[
  \Obs^q_{\hCS}(U)
  =
  \left(
    \widehat{\mathrm{Sym}}\bigl(\Ecal_c(U)^\vee[-1]\bigr)[[\hbar]],
    \dbar+\{I[L]|_U,-\}_{\mathrm{BV}}+\hbar\Delta_L
  \right).
  \label{eq:obs-q-hcs}
\]
The differential squares to zero precisely by~\eqref{eq:scale-qme}.
For disjoint $U_1,\ldots,U_n\subset V$, the factorisation product is
ordinary multiplication followed by Feynman contraction with internal
propagators supported in $V\setminus\bigcup_i U_i$.  Thus
$U\mapsto\Obs^q_{\hCS}(U)$ is a holomorphic prefactorisation algebra
whenever the quantum hCS datum exists.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Complex dimension $3$ anomaly]
\label{prop:quartic-anomaly}
The first local obstruction to~\eqref{eq:scale-qme} in complex
dimension $3$ is the one-loop quartic trace class
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_1^{\mathrm{box}}
  =
  \frac{1}{(2\pi i)^3\,4!}\,
  \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{ad}}
  \bigl(T^aT^bT^cT^d\bigr)_{\mathrm{sym}}
  \int_Y
    \Omega_Y\wedge
    c^a\,\dbar c^b\,\dbar c^c\,\dbar c^d .
  \label{eq:quartic-anomaly}
\]
Under BRST descent the same class has the cubic gauge-field
representative
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_1^{\mathrm{desc}}
  =
  \frac{c_2(\frakg)}{24}
  \int_Y
    \Omega_Y\wedge
    \mathrm{tr}_{\mathrm{fund}}
    \bigl(\mathcal A\,\dbar\mathcal A\,\dbar\mathcal A\bigr).
  \label{eq:cubic-descent}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The box graph has four ghost external legs and four internal
$\mathcal A\mathcal A^\vee$ propagators.  Each vertex contributes one
structure constant and one $\dbar$ acting on an external ghost.  The
four structure constants assemble to the symmetric adjoint quartic
trace in~\eqref{eq:quartic-anomaly}, and the three Dolbeault
derivatives supply the missing antiholomorphic degree:
$\Omega_Y$ has degree $(3,0)$ and
$c\,\dbar c\,\dbar c\,\dbar c$ has degree $(0,3)$.  The descent
equation $F=\dbar c+\frac12[c,c]$ sends the quartic ghost
representative to~\eqref{eq:cubic-descent}.  This is the local form
used by Costello's renormalisation theorem, by Costello-Gwilliam
factorisation algebras, and by the Vol II computation
\texttt{chapters/connections/bv\_brst.tex}
Theorem~\texttt{thm:6dhcs-one-loop-anomaly}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Anomaly-free scopes]
\label{rem:anomaly-free-scopes}
For $\frakg=\fgl_1$ in the abelian normalisation, the adjoint bracket
and adjoint quartic trace vanish, so the local one-loop obstruction
vanishes.  For simple $\frakg$, the quartic class must vanish in local
BRST cohomology or be killed by explicit counterterms, for example by
Green-Schwarz or Deligne-series cancellation data.  These data turn the
obstructed perturbative problem into a constructed factorisation
algebra.
\end{remark}

\section{The Curve Algebra}

Let $C\subset Y$ be a holomorphic curve with formal normal
neighbourhood $\widehat N_{C/Y}$.  Given a quantum hCS datum on
$\widehat N_{C/Y}$, define
\[
  A_C
  =
  \Sp_C\bigl(\Obs^q_{\hCS}\bigr)
  =
  \int_{\widehat N_{C/Y}/C}\Obs^q_{\hCS}
  \in \Eone\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(C).
  \label{eq:curve-specialisation}
\]
This is transverse factorisation homology along the two complex normal
directions to $C$.

\begin{proposition}[Native operadic level in dimension $3$]
\label{prop:native-e1}
The curve algebra $A_C$ in~\eqref{eq:curve-specialisation} is native
$\Eone$-chiral.  An $\Etwo$-structure belongs to the Drinfeld centre,
the Hall-Drinfeld double, or a braided module category built from
$A_C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pushforward integrates over the two transverse complex directions
and leaves ordered configurations on the curve $C$.  Ordered insertion
on a curve is an $\Eone$ operation.  Dunn additivity controls the
ambient reduced structure only after local constancy or Omega
localisation has produced the needed topological directions.  The
braided structure associated to an $\Eone$ algebra is carried by
$Z(\mathrm{Rep}^{\Eone}(A_C))$ or by the Drinfeld double of the
corresponding Hall positive half.  Therefore writing $A_C$ itself as
native $\Etwo$ in complex dimension $3$ loses the centre/double layer.
\end{proof}

\section{Five Constructions}

For $Y=\C^3$ and $C=\C_z\subset\C^3$, the following five constructions
meet at the same curve algebra only after the choices and hypotheses
above have been fixed.

\begin{construction}[BV prefactorisation pushforward]
The first construction is
$A_C=\int_{\widehat N_{C/Y}/C}\Obs^q_{\hCS}$ from
Definition~\ref{def:quantum-observables}.  It is the local BV
construction.  Its input is the quantum hCS datum. Character formulae
and Hall algebras enter through separate constructions.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Five-dimensional Omega boundary]
Choose a splitting $\C^3=\C^2_{\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2}\times C$ and an
Omega deformation in the normal plane.  Costello's five-dimensional
holomorphic-topological theory on $\R\times\C^2$ gives a boundary
algebra on $C$.  In the abelian $\fgl_1$ case, the resulting boundary
chiral algebra has positive affine Yangian mode algebra.  The
construction depends on the normal-plane equivariance.  A compact
non-toric threefold requires actual equivariant or Hall data in place of
torus weights.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Schiffmann-Vasserot shuffle CoHA]
The cohomological Hall algebra of the affine three-loop quiver,
equivalently the toric $\C^3$ CoHA, has the shuffle presentation
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)\cong \Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \label{eq:coha-positive-half}
\]
as an associative positive half.  This is the Schiffmann-Vasserot
construction.  It supplies the Hall presentation of the same
positive-half algebra that appears from the boundary hCS calculation.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Crystal character]
The crystal model gives the graded character of the positive half:
\[
  \mathrm{ch}\,\CoHA(\C^3)
  =
  M(q)
  =
  \prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-n}.
  \label{eq:macmahon-character}
\]
It records the Donaldson-Thomas counting of plane partitions.  It does
not by itself determine the multiplication, the coproduct, or the
Drinfeld pairing.
\end{construction}

\begin{construction}[Chiral Chevalley-Eilenberg envelope]
The fifth construction is the chiral Chevalley-Eilenberg envelope of
the local Lie algebra obtained by restricting the BV fields to the
formal normal disk of $C$.  It gives generators and relations for the
same $\Eone$ curve algebra after the QME has fixed the quantum
corrections to the differential and OPE.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Comparison at the affine positive-half scope]
\label{thm:five-constructions-affine}
For $Y=\C^3$, $C=\C_z$, and abelian gauge algebra $\fgl_1$, the five
constructions above identify the same positive-half associative mode
algebra of the $\Eone$ curve algebra:
\[
  \Modes(A_C)\cong \Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\cong \CoHA(\C^3).
  \label{eq:affine-positive-half}
\]
The crystal construction computes its character~\eqref{eq:macmahon-character}.
The chiral Chevalley-Eilenberg construction gives a presentation by
generators, differential, and OPE.  No construction in this list is a
second application of the CY-to-chiral functor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The BV construction gives the prefactorisation algebra once the QME is
solved.  The Omega boundary theory is Costello's normal-plane
localisation of the same local BV theory.  Schiffmann-Vasserot identify
the corresponding toric CoHA with the positive affine Yangian
$\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ by the shuffle presentation.  The crystal
model is the character of that shuffle algebra, not an independent
algebra structure.  The chiral Chevalley-Eilenberg envelope is the
universal enveloping presentation of the local Lie algebra controlling
the same OPE.  The comparison therefore takes place at the level of the
mode algebra~\eqref{eq:affine-positive-half} of one curve chiral
algebra, with five different constructions and five different kinds of
input.
\end{proof}

\section{Double, Centre, and Vertex Algebra}

\begin{proposition}[Positive half versus full vertex target]
\label{prop:positive-half-not-w}
The identities
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)=\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1),
  \qquad
  \widehat{\D}_{\hbar}\bigl(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\bigr)
  \cong
  Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \label{eq:double-full-yangian}
\]
have different meanings.  The first is the positive Hall algebra.  The
second is the completed Drinfeld double, including the Cartan and
negative half.  The vertex algebra $\cW_{1+\infty}[\lambda]$ is reached
from the full affine Yangian by an evaluation or module construction,
not by identifying $\CoHA(\C^3)$ with a vertex algebra.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The shuffle CoHA contains creation operators and the positive Hall
product.  The full affine Yangian requires annihilation operators,
Cartan currents, a Hopf pairing, and completion.  These are precisely
the data packaged by the Drinfeld double in~\eqref{eq:double-full-yangian}.
A vertex algebra additionally requires fields, locality, and OPE
closure on a module.  Identifying the $\C^3$ CoHA directly with
$\cW_{1+\infty}$ collapses three operations: doubling, completion, and
passage to a vertex representation.
\end{proof}

\section{Compact and Global Calabi-Yau Threefolds}

Let $X$ be a compact Calabi-Yau threefold.  The formal expression
\[
  \int_X\Omega_X\wedge
  \left(
    \frac12\langle\mathcal A,\dbar\mathcal A\rangle
    +\frac16\langle\mathcal A,[\mathcal A,\mathcal A]\rangle
  \right)_{(0,3)}
\]
is only the classical starting point.  A global quantum object requires
the following extra data.

\begin{hypothesis}[Global hCS package]
\label{hyp:global-hcs-package}
A compact or global hCS construction on $X$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item a Calabi-Yau orientation and a compatible BV pairing on the
  determinant line of fields;
  \item a gauge fixing and scale-dependent counterterms satisfying the
  QME;
  \item cancellation of the complex-dimension-$3$ quartic
  anomaly~\eqref{eq:quartic-anomaly}, or an explicit Green-Schwarz type
  cancellation datum;
  \item factorisation descent for the chosen cover, with sewing maps
  compatible with boundary strata in the relevant compactification of
  configuration or curve moduli;
  \item a topological or holomorphic framing sufficient for
  factorisation homology along the chosen fibration;
  \item a completion: compact support or Harder-Narasimhan charge
  completion on the Hall side, and completed tensor products on the
  analytic side.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{proposition}[Compact hCS Scope for the $K3\times E$ BKM Target]
\label{prop:k3e-conditional}
For $X=K3\times E$, a curve specialisation of global hCS can supply at
most a candidate $\Eone$ chiral algebra on $E$ after
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:global-hcs-package}.  The Borcherds-Kac-Moody
algebra attached to the Gritsenko denominator and the K3 self-mirror
Yangian branch require their own Hall, centre, or stable-envelope
construction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The BKM object on the compact $K3\times E$ side requires a compact Hall
positive half, a Hopf or Hall pairing, a Cartan, a negative half, a
Drinfeld double, and a radical quotient:
\[
  G(K3\times E)
  =
  \widehat{\D}_{\hbar}\!\left(\CoHA^+_{K3\times E}\right)
  \big/
  \mathrm{rad}\,\langle-,-\rangle .
  \label{eq:k3e-hall-double}
\]
Only after this algebraic object is constructed can one compare its
denominator with a Borcherds product.  The numerical Borcherds
invariant is then
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2};
  \qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Delta_5)=\frac{10}{2}=5.
  \label{eq:bkm-weight-delta5}
\]
Those constants come from the automorphic denominator input, not from
the local hCS action.  A direct equality between a compact hCS
pushforward and the BKM target is therefore a theorem only after the
Hall double, centre compatibility, PBW statement, pairing
nondegeneracy, radical quotient, and Borcherds recognition data have
been supplied.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The K3 Yangian name]
The phrase ``K3 Yangian'' denotes the separate K3 self-mirror branch.
The BKM-side compact object is the Hall-Drinfeld
double~\eqref{eq:k3e-hall-double} when its construction is available.
The two objects may be compared through centres, stable envelopes, or
module categories; the names refer to distinct branches.
\end{remark}

\section{Corrected Statements}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
  \item On $\C^3$, classical hCS is the BV theory
  $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(\C^3,\frakg)[1]$ with
  action~\eqref{eq:hcs-classical-action}; the ordinary $(0,1)$ connection
  already gives a $(3,3)$ integrand.
  \item Quantisation requires the scale-$L$ QME, counterterms, and
  anomaly control.  In complex dimension $3$ the one-loop anomaly has
  quartic ghost representative~\eqref{eq:quartic-anomaly}.
  \item The $d=3$ curve output is an $\Eone$ chiral algebra.  $\Etwo$
  and $\Ethree$ structures enter through local-constancy reductions,
  centres, doubles, or module categories.
  \item The five routes are five constructions of the positive-half
  mode algebra of one affine curve chiral algebra, not repeated
  applications of one functor.
  \item $\CoHA(\C^3)=\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is the positive half.
  The full affine Yangian and $\cW_{1+\infty}$ require
  Drinfeld-double and representation data.
  \item Compact $K3\times E$ identifications require orientation,
  framing, descent, sewing, QME, anomaly cancellation, counterterms,
  completion, and Hall-double recognition data.  Without those data,
  hCS supplies a candidate boundary construction, not an identification
  with a compact Hall target.
\end{enumerate}
