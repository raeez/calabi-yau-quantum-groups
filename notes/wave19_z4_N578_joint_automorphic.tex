\section*{Non-CHL Nikulin orders and diagonal-divisor automorphic rows}
\label{sec:n578-non-chl-nikulin-automorphic}

The primitive compact \(K3\times E\) CHL denominator ladder is the
five-order list
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},\qquad
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The restriction comes from the elliptic factor: an elliptic curve has
origin-fixing automorphisms only of orders \(1,2,3,4,6\).  The
symplectic K3 orders \(5,7,8\) therefore lie outside this compact CHL
quotient construction.  Their order-indexed Nikulin-Gritsenko
Borcherds products have
\[
  N\in\{5,7,8\},\qquad
  c_N(0)=(4,2,2),\qquad
  \wt(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2=(2,1,1).
\]
These three numbers are automorphic weights.  A compact, stacky, or
hyperkahler host is additional geometry and must carry its own
orientation, chamber, partition function, and Hall-Borcherds comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Borcherds weights on the CHL and non-CHL Nikulin rows]
\label{thm:n578-nikulin-order-borcherds-weights}
Let \(g_N\) be a symplectic K3 automorphism of order
\[
   N\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\},
\]
and let \(\phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)}\) be the corresponding twined weak Jacobi
input in the Gritsenko-Nikulin/Gritsenko normalisation.  If
\(\Phi_N=\operatorname{Borch}(\phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)})\), then Borcherds'
singular-theta weight theorem gives
\[
  \wt(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2},
\]
where \(c_N(0)\) is the \((q^0\zeta^0)\)-coefficient of the input.
For the compact CHL denominator rows,
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)&5&4&3&2&1 .
\end{array}
\]
For the non-CHL Nikulin rows,
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
N&5&7&8\\ \hline
c_N(0)&4&2&2\\
\wt(\Phi_N)&2&1&1 .
\end{array}
\]
The notation \(\kBKM(\Phi_N)\) has BKM-denominator meaning only after a
denominator algebra has been constructed.  On the three non-CHL rows
the displayed value is the Borcherds automorphic weight until the
denominator algebra and geometric host are supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Borcherds proves that the automorphic product obtained from the
singular theta lift has weight \(c(0)/2\).  Gritsenko-Nikulin and
Gritsenko apply this formula to the CHL K3 Jacobi inputs, giving
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))=(10,8,6,4,2).
\]
This proves the CHL denominator weights \((5,4,3,2,1)\).

For \(N=5,7,8\), Nikulin-Mukai supplies the symplectic K3 orders and
the frame shapes, while Gritsenko's paramodular construction supplies
the corresponding Borcherds products.  The zero-mode computation is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N&\text{frame shape}&T^{\mathrm{Jac}}_N&A_N&c_N(0)=T^{\mathrm{Jac}}_N-2A_N\\
\hline
5&1^4 5^4&8&2&4\\
7&1^3 7^3&6&2&2\\
8&1^2 2\,4\,8^2&6&2&2 .
\end{array}
\]
Here \(T^{\mathrm{Jac}}_N\) is the trace parameter in the twined K3
Jacobi input and \(A_N\) is the Gritsenko-Nikulin correction appearing
in the arithmetic lift normalisation.  Applying Borcherds' formula gives
\(\wt(\Phi_5)=2\), \(\wt(\Phi_7)=1\), and \(\wt(\Phi_8)=1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
Gritsenko and Clery classify the genus-two Siegel modular forms for
Hecke-type congruence subgroups whose divisor is the diagonal divisor
with multiplicity one.  Their product theorem gives the row-specific
Jacobi input, group, multiplier, and product chamber.  The table records
that theorem in triple order.  Since the labels
\((t,N_{\mathrm{lvl}};k)\) are paramodular diagonal-divisor labels,
equality with a Nikulin order row requires a theorem preserving the
input Jacobi form, group or cover, character, Weyl chamber, divisor
multiplicities, and product normalisation.  The argument remains inside
the two separated tables.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Geometric scope of the non-CHL orders]
\label{cor:n578-geometric-scope}
The primitive \(K3\times E\) CHL Calabi-Yau threefold scope is
\[
   N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
For \(N=5,7,8\), the automorphic weights \((2,1,1)\) are fixed, while
the geometric packages are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|p{0.72\linewidth}}
N & proof obligation\\ \hline
5 & construct a Borcea-Voisin or other compact CY$_3$ host with
reduced DT/BPS denominator comparison to \(\Phi_5^{\mathrm{Nik}}\);\\
7 & construct a smooth, stacky, or gerbe-corrected CY$_3$ package for
the order-\(7\) quotient and compare its protected index with
\(\Phi_7^{\mathrm{Nik}}\);\\
8 & construct a CY$_3$ or stacky CY package, or state a hyperkahler
Kummer-type endpoint as such, before comparing with \(\Phi_8^{\mathrm{Nik}}\).
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The elliptic-curve automorphism argument excludes \(5,7,8\) from the
origin-fixing diagonal \(K3\times E\) quotient.  At order \(5\), a
Borcea-Voisin threefold may provide compact CY$_3$ geometry only after
the commuting involution, reduced obstruction theory, and product
comparison are fixed.  At order \(7\), the diagonal quotient has
fixed-locus singularities; a smooth or stacky replacement must carry the
orientation and McKay data.  At order \(8\), the available Kummer-type
evidence is hyperkahler fourfold geometry; CY$_3$ construction requires
additional data.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Normalisations at \(K3\times E\).}
At \(N=1\) the primitive denominator is \(\DeltaFive\):
\[
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive,\qquad
  \PhiTen=\DeltaFive^2.
\]
The reduced Oberdieck-Pandharipande scalar convention is
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\DeltaFive^2.
\]
These scalar normalisations leave the five \(K3\times E\) constants
separate:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=24.
\]
These values come from distinct constructions.  The value \(5\) is the
Borcherds weight of \(\DeltaFive\); the compact Hodge supertrace is
\(0\), the Heisenberg-Mukai specialisation is \(3\), and the K3
Mukai-lattice rank is \(24\).

\paragraph{Chiral and Hall scope.}
For \(d\geq3\), the native output of \(\Phi_d\) is \(E_1\)-chiral.
Any \(E_2\) structure belongs to a centre, double, or representation
category after the required hypotheses are supplied.  The
Borcherds/Hall-Drinfeld branch is distinct from the Mukai Yangian
branch.  In the local toric test case,
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)=Y^+.
\]
The full \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) object appears only after a
Drinfeld double, derived centre, completion, or vertex-level evaluation.
