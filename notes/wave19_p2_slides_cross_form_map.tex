\section*{Automorphic form catalogues and the \(K3\times E\) invariants}

For a single-cusp Borcherds denominator product \(\Phi\), Borcherds'
singular theta lift computes the automorphic weight from the constant
term of the input:
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi)=\mathrm{wt}(\Phi)=\frac{c(0)}{2},
\]
where \(c(0)\) is the zeroth Fourier coefficient of the Jacobi or
vector-valued form at the cusp in the singular-theta normalisation.
For multi-cusp paramodular rows, Gritsenko--Cl\'ery replace \(c(0)\) by
the cusp-weighted sum of constant terms in their Borcherds-product
theorem.  The primitive CHL ladder, the non-CHL Nikulin boundary rows,
and the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue therefore share the
same weight principle, with the common entry the weight-\(5\) form
\(\Delta_5\).

\paragraph{The primitive CHL ladder.}
Let \(g_N\) be a symplectic K3 automorphism of order
\(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), with compatible elliptic automorphism on \(E\).
The diagonal quotient
\[
  X_N=(S\times E)/\langle(g_N,\epsilon_N)\rangle
\]
is the compact CHL \(K3\times E\)-type host.  The Borcherds product of
the \(g_N\)-twined weight-zero, index-one K3 Jacobi input gives
\[
  \phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)}
  \longmapsto
  \Phi_N=\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}
  \longmapsto
  \mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_N},
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
The constants are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \text{frame shape} & c_N(0) & \kBKM(\Phi_N) & \text{form} \\ \hline
1 & 1^{24} & 10 & 5 & \Delta_5 \\
2 & 1^8 2^8 & 8 & 4 & \Delta_4^{(2)} \\
3 & 1^6 3^6 & 6 & 3 & \Delta_3^{(3)} \\
4 & 1^4 2^2 4^4 & 4 & 2 & \Delta_2^{(4)} \\
6 & 1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2 & 2 & 1 & \Delta_1^{(6)}
\end{array}
\]
At \(N=1\),
\(\phi_{0,1}|_{q^0}=\zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta\), hence
\(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=10/2=5\).  The scalar normalisations used in the
reduced \(K3\times E\) DT literature are
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
  \qquad
  \ZOP=-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
       =-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The Igusa square has weight \(10\), twice the BKM denominator weight of
\(\Delta_5\).  The inverse-square scalar \(\ZOP\) is an
Oberdieck--Pandharipande reduced enumerative convention, leaving the
primitive Borcherds denominator and \(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\) unchanged.
The physical BPS/dyon reading is a heuristic attached to this scalar
convention: it interprets reciprocal-square coefficients as indexed
degeneracies after reduced integration.  The theorem in this paragraph
is the automorphic scalar identity; Hall multiplication, coproducts, and
Borcherds brackets belong to separate algebraic recognition data.

\paragraph{Nikulin boundary orders.}
The symplectic K3 orders \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) have automorphic Borcherds
targets.  A primitive compact \(K3\times E\) CHL quotient would require
an elliptic automorphism \(\epsilon_N\in\mathrm{Aut}(E,0)\) of the same
order, while over \(\mathbb C\) the possible orders in
\(\mathrm{Aut}(E,0)\) are \(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).  Thus these rows are
boundary automorphic rows, separate from the primitive compact CHL
ladder.
After denominator recognition their constants and weights are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \text{frame shape} & c_N(0) & \kBKM(\Phi_N) & \text{recognition scope} \\ \hline
5 & 1^4 5^4 & 4 & 2 & \text{Nikulin automorphic denominator} \\
7 & 1^3 7^3 & 2 & 1 & \text{Nikulin automorphic denominator} \\
8 & 1^2 2^1 4^1 8^2 & 2 & 1 & \text{Nikulin automorphic denominator}
\end{array}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  c_5(0)=4,\qquad c_7(0)=2,\qquad c_8(0)=2,
  \qquad
  \kBKM=(2,1,1)
\]
on the recognized boundary denominators.
The entries \((2,1,1)\) are BKM invariants only after the automorphic
target has been identified with a Borcherds denominator.  Before that
recognition they are automorphic weights of Nikulin-order targets.
The compact \(K3\times E\) CHL quotient geometry remains confined to
\(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).

\paragraph{The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue.}
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery classify the genus-two Siegel modular forms whose
divisor on \(\mathbb H_2/\Gamma_t(N)\) is the diagonal Humbert divisor
with vanishing order one.  The complete row set is indexed by
\[
 (t,N;k)\in
 \bigl\{(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
 (4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1)\bigr\}.
\]
Equivalently:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(t,N;k) & \text{space} & \text{name} & 2k \\ \hline
(1,1;5) & M_5(\Gamma_1,\nu_2) & \Delta_5 & 10 \\
(2,1;2) & M_2(\Gamma_2,\nu_4) & \Delta_2 & 4 \\
(1,2;3) & M_3(\Gamma_1(2),\nu_2) & \nabla_3 & 6 \\
(3,1;1) & M_1(\Gamma_3,\nu_6) & \Delta_1 & 2 \\
(1,3;2) & M_2(\Gamma_1(3),\nu_2) & \nabla_2 & 4 \\
(4,1;\tfrac12) & M_{1/2}(\Gamma_4,\nu_8) & \Delta_{1/2} & 1 \\
(1,4;\tfrac32) & M_{3/2}(\Gamma_1(4),\nu_4) & \nabla_{3/2} & 3 \\
(2,2;1) & M_1(\Gamma_2(2),\nu_4) & Q_1 & 2
\end{array}
\]
For each row,
\[
  \kBKM(F_{t,N})=\mathrm{wt}(F_{t,N})=k.
\]
Here \(\kBKM\) is again a denominator invariant: Gritsenko--Cl\'ery's
Borcherds-product theorem computes \(k\) by a cusp-weighted constant
term, and in the single-cusp case this reduces to \(c(0)/2\).
The tuple of weights is
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
and the tuple of twice-weights is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
This catalogue is a diagonal-divisor classification by the pair
\((t,N)\).  The primitive CHL ladder is a cyclic-order classification
by \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).  The unique common row is
\((t,N)=(1,1)\), namely \(\Delta_5\).  The twice-weight tuple
\((10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)\) is distinct from the primitive CHL constant tuple
\((10,8,6,4,2)\).

\paragraph{\(K3\times E\) invariants.}
For \(X=K3\times E\), the invariant assignments are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{invariant} & \text{value} & \text{construction} \\ \hline
\kcat(X) & 0 & \chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot 0 \\
\kch(X) & 0 & \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)=1-1+1-1 \\
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(X) & 3 &
  \text{Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, trace }2+1 \\
\kBKM(\fgDelta) & 5 &
  \text{Borcherds weight of }\Delta_5,\ c_1(0)/2=10/2 \\
\kfib(X) & 24 & \mathrm{rk}\,H^\bullet(K3,\mathbb Z)
\end{array}
\]
The value \(2\) belongs to \(\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\) on the K3 fibre.
The compact total-space values are
\(\kcat(K3\times E)=\kch(K3\times E)=0\).  The value \(3\) belongs to the
Heisenberg--Mukai chiral specialisation.  The value \(5\) belongs to the
Borcherds denominator.  The value \(24\) belongs to the Mukai lattice of
the K3 fibre.  These rows have independent definitions; the
incompatibility with an additive rule is visible in
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)\ne \kch(K3\times E)+\chi(\mathcal O_E),
  \qquad
  5\ne 0+0.
\]

\paragraph{Positive halves and centres.}
The \(K3\times E\) BKM object is reached through the Hall positive half,
its Drinfeld double, and a Borcherds recognition datum:
\[
  \YHall(K3\times E)
  \longmapsto
  D\bigl(\YHall(K3\times E)\bigr)
  \dashrightarrow
  \fgDelta .
\]
The dashed arrow records the Hall--Borcherds comparison.  The Mukai
self-mirror K3 Yangian is the separate \(d=2\) branch.  The local model
has
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)=Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
the positive Hall half.  The full \(\Winfty\) algebra appears only after
double, centre, completion, or evaluation operations.  At \(d=3\), the
native \(\Phi\)-output \(A\) on the elliptic curve is \(E_1\)-chiral.
The derived chiral centre \(Z_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{der}}(A)\) carries
the \(E_2\)-structure, as do the associated double and module-centre
layers.

\paragraph{Recognition data for row maps.}
A primitive CHL row consists of the diagonal \(K3\times E\) quotient
\(X_N\), the twined K3 Jacobi input \(\phi_{0,1}^{(g_N)}\), and the
Borcherds product \(\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}\) with
\(c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2)\).  A row at \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) consists of a
Nikulin-order automorphic denominator and a separate host-geometry
theorem.  A Gritsenko--Cl\'ery row consists of the triple \((t,N;k)\),
the subgroup \(\Gamma_t(N)\), the multiplier \(\nu_m\), and the
diagonal-divisor Borcherds product.  A scalar inverse-square identity
such as \(\ZOP=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}\) is partition-function data;
a BKM row map requires a recognized denominator product.

Borcherds 1998 Theorem 13.3 supplies the weight formula.
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part II supplies the primitive CHL ladder.
Gritsenko 1994 Proposition 3.1 supplies the non-CHL Nikulin automorphic
orders.  Gritsenko--Cl\'ery arXiv:0812.3962 Theorems 1.4 and 3.1 supply
the diagonal-divisor catalogue and the row-wise weight identity.
Oberdieck--Pandharipande arXiv:1411.1514 supplies the reduced
\(K3\times E\) scalar convention for the Igusa square.
