\section*{Humbert divisors on $\Amoduli$ and the $\DeltaFive$ divisor}

\subsection*{Humbert surfaces}
Write a point of the Siegel upper half-space as
\[
  Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau & z\\ z & \sigma\end{pmatrix}\in\Hsieg .
\]
For an integer $D>0$ with $D\equiv 0,1\pmod 4$, the Humbert surface
$H_D\subset\Amoduli=\Spfour\backslash\Hsieg$ is the image of the
primitive singular relations
\[
  a\tau+bz+c\sigma+d(z^2-\tau\sigma)+e=0,\qquad
  b^2-4ac-4de=D,
\]
with $(a,b,c,d,e)\in\mathbb Z^5$ primitive.  This is the classical
Humbert--van der Geer normalisation.  The divisor $H_1$ is the
decomposable locus: in the chart above it is represented by $z=0$ and
parametrises principally polarised products $E_\tau\times E_\sigma$,
modulo the involution interchanging the two factors.

\subsection*{The divisor of $\DeltaFive$}
The Gritsenko--Nikulin form $\DeltaFive$ is the genus-two Siegel modular
form of weight $5$ and theta character whose square is Igusa's scalar
weight-$10$ form in the usual unnormalised convention:
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\DeltaFive^2 .
\]
The monic denominator convention is \(\Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive\).
Equivalently, $\DeltaFive$ is the Borcherds product attached to the
weak Jacobi form
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=\zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta+O(q).
\]
Borcherds' divisor formula reads the weight from the constant term and
the reflective divisor from the negative-discriminant principal part:
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\DeltaFive)=\frac{c_1(0)}2=\frac{10}{2}=5,\qquad
  \operatorname{div}(\DeltaFive)=H_1 .
\]
The coefficient of the terms $\zeta^{\pm1}$ gives a simple reflective
component, so the vanishing order is
\[
  \operatorname{ord}_{H_D}(\DeltaFive)=
  \begin{cases}
    1,&D=1,\\
    0,&D\in\{4,5,8,9\}.
  \end{cases}
\]
This is a divisor statement about the automorphic form.  Monodromy
order, Atkin--Lehner orbits, and the Borcherds weight $\kBKM$ are
separate data.

\subsection*{Restriction of $H_D$ to the product locus}
Restrict the singular relation for $H_D$ to $H_1$ by setting $z=0$:
\[
  -d\tau\sigma+a\tau+c\sigma+e=0,\qquad
  b^2-4ac-4de=D.
\]
For fixed $b$ with $b^2\le D$ and $b^2\equiv D\pmod 4$, put
\[
  n=\frac{D-b^2}{4}.
\]
The remaining equation is the graph of a degree-$n$ modular
correspondence between the two elliptic factors:
\[
  \sigma=\frac{a\tau+e}{d\tau-c},\qquad
  \det\begin{pmatrix}a&e\\ d&-c\end{pmatrix}=n .
\]
Thus the support of $H_1\cap H_D$ in the interior of the product locus
is obtained from the Hecke correspondences $C_n$ indexed by the
primitive integer matrices above.  Stabiliser factors and stack
normalisations determine Chow multiplicities.  The support calculation
is independent of the divisor-order separation for $\DeltaFive$.

Here $C_1$ is the diagonal correspondence $E_\tau\simeq E_\sigma$, and
$C_2$ is the degree-$2$ Hecke correspondence.  The square-discriminant
boundary components of $H_9$ lie on the Satake boundary.  The interior
support remains the degree-\(2\) Hecke correspondence.

\subsection*{Monodromy, conductor, and Borcherds weight}
The order of vanishing of $\DeltaFive$ along $H_1$ is $1$.  The
Theorem-C $\mathcal B$-family conductor face records a different
integer:
\[
  K^{\kch}=8,\qquad
  \operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{mon}_{H_1}\mathcal L^{\DeltaFive}\bigr)=8,
  \qquad
  \hbar^2 K^{\kch}=-1 .
\]
The first equality is the Mukai-positive-signature calculation
$2c_+(\widetilde H(K3,\mathbb Z))=2\cdot4=8$.  The second is the
Humbert-$H_1$ monodromy order of the automorphic local system
$\mathcal L^{\DeltaFive}$.  The vanishing order of $\DeltaFive$ along
\(H_1\) remains \(1\).  The third equality is the Lusztig
eighth-root specialisation written in the normalisation
$\hbar^2=-1/8$.

The Borcherds weight is again separate:
\[
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5.
\]
On the CHL denominator ladder,
\[
  \bigl(c_N(0)\bigr)_{N=1,2,3,4,6}=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_N)\bigr)_{N=1,2,3,4,6}=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
These constants are weights of primitive BKM denominators.  Humbert
intersection multiplicities and Atkin--Lehner orbit counts are separate
arithmetic data.

\subsection*{The $K3\times E$ constants}
The $K3\times E$ values are four subscripted invariants from four
distinct constructions:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\DeltaFive})=5,\qquad
  \kfiber(K3)=24 .
\]
The value $5$ is the weight of the $\DeltaFive$ BKM denominator.  The value
$8$ is the conductor/monodromy integer in the $\mathcal B$-family.
The Humbert intersection table fixes the divisor and correspondence
geometry.  The Borcherds weight and the conductor/monodromy integer
come from the two displayed constructions above.

\subsection*{References}
Humbert's singular-relation definition and van der Geer's
\emph{Hilbert Modular Surfaces}, Ch.~IX, fix the convention
$D=b^2-4ac-4de$ and identify $H_1$ with the decomposable locus.
Igusa's genus-two theta-constant calculation gives
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\DeltaFive^2$ and the decomposable divisor.
Gritsenko--Nikulin's Borcherds-product construction identifies the
primitive denominator with $\DeltaFive$ and the simple $H_1$ divisor.
Borcherds' weight formula gives $\mathrm{wt}(\DeltaFive)=c_1(0)/2=5$.
Bruinier's Heegner Chern-class reciprocity is the source for the
separate order-$8$ Humbert-monodromy input used by the
$\mathcal B$-family conductor bridge.
