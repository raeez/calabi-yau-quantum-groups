\section*{Nikulin Boundary Orders \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\)}

\paragraph{Three indexing systems.}
The primitive \(K3\times E\) CHL denominator ladder has order set
\[
  \mathcal N_{\mathrm{CHL}}=\{1,2,3,4,6\},
  \qquad
  (c_N(0))_{N\in\mathcal N_{\mathrm{CHL}}}=(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
and Borcherds weights
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiN)=\wt(\PhiN)=\frac{c_N(0)}2=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
Here \(c_N(0)\) is the discriminant-zero coefficient of the weak Jacobi
input in the Borcherds product normalisation of Borcherds
\cite[Theorem~13.3]{Borcherds1998} and Gritsenko--Nikulin--Gritsenko
\cite{GritsenkoNikulin1998,Gritsenko1994,Gritsenko1999}.

The order-indexed Nikulin list of symplectic K3 automorphisms is
\[
  \mathcal N_{\mathrm{Nik}}=\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}
\]
by Mukai's classification \cite{Mukai1988}.  The three boundary orders
\[
  \mathcal N_{\partial}=\{5,7,8\}
\]
carry Borcherds product weights
\[
  (c_5(0),c_7(0),c_8(0))=(4,2,2),
  \qquad
  \bigl(\wt(\Phi_5),\wt(\Phi_7),\wt(\Phi_8)\bigr)=(2,1,1).
\]
These are order-indexed automorphic weights.  They become
\(\kBKM\)-invariants only after a denominator algebra, root datum, Weyl
chamber, character, and Calabi--Yau host have been supplied.

The Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue is a third indexing
system:
\[
 (t,N_{\mathrm{lvl}};k)=
 (1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
 (4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1).
\]
Its twice-weight tuple is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2),
\]
equivalently weights
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1).
\]
This catalogue is indexed by paramodular divisor data, with its own
covers, characters, and multiplier systems \cite{GritsenkoClery2013}.
It shares the \(\DeltaFive\) row with the CHL ladder and otherwise
requires an explicit row-map theorem before comparison with either
order-indexed list.

\paragraph{Boundary weight calculation.}
Write the frame shape of a symplectic K3 automorphism \(g_N\) as
\[
  \pi(g_N)=\prod_a a^{m_a}.
\]
The Lefschetz trace on the Mukai \(24\)-dimensional representation is
\[
  T_{H^\ast}(g_N)=\sum_a m_a,
\]
and the Gritsenko--Nikulin theta-component calculation gives
\[
  c_N(0)=T_{H^\ast}(g_N)-2A_N.
\]
For the boundary orders the odd theta-component coefficient is
\[
  A_5=A_7=A_8=2.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \pi(g_N) & T_{H^\ast}(g_N) & c_N(0) & \wt(\Phi_N) \\ \hline
5 & 1^4 5^4 & 8 & 4 & 2 \\
7 & 1^3 7^3 & 6 & 2 & 1 \\
8 & 1^2 2\,4\,8^2 & 6 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
The independent computational witness is
\texttt{compute/lib/diagonal\_siegel\_cy\_orbifolds.py}: its
\texttt{FRAME\_SHAPE\_DATA} table records the same three frame shapes,
discriminant-zero coefficients, and weights, and
\texttt{test\_diagonal\_siegel\_cy\_orbifolds.py} checks
\(\wt(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\) for all \(1\leq N\leq 8\).

\paragraph{Geometric scope.}
The primitive CHL denominator construction used here selects
\[
  \mathcal N_{\mathrm{CHL}}=\{1,2,3,4,6\}
\]
because the elliptic factor in that construction uses an
origin-preserving elliptic automorphism.  The automorphism group
\(\operatorname{Aut}(E,0)\) has finite cyclic orders \(1,2,3,4,6\):
after differentiating at the origin, it embeds into the unit group of
an imaginary quadratic order, whose finite unit groups have those
orders.  This proves the CHL selection in the present sense.

Translations by \(N\)-torsion on an elliptic curve are a different
operation.  They supply a free-action construction; the
origin-preserving elliptic automorphism, multiplier, and denominator
comparison package belong to the primitive CHL ladder.  Consequently
the weights \((2,1,1)\) at \(N=5,7,8\) are established automorphic
data, while a smooth \(K3\times E\) CHL denominator host for them
remains additional geometry.

\paragraph{Mukai boundary.}
The endpoint \(N=8\) is the maximal cyclic order of a symplectic K3
automorphism in the Mukai--Nikulin classification.  The orders \(5\) and
\(7\) are also K3-symplectic orders.  In this note their role is
geometric: they lie outside the primitive \(K3\times E\) CHL host
scope while still carrying order-indexed Borcherds weights.

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\(K3\times E\)}{K3 x E} normalisation.}
At the common \(N=1\) row the primitive denominator is \(\DeltaFive\),
with
\[
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=\frac{c_1(0)}2=\frac{10}{2}=5,
  \qquad
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive,
  \qquad
  \PhiTen=\DeltaFive^2.
\]
The Oberdieck--Pandharipande scalar convention uses
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\DeltaFive^2.
\]
The four \(K3\times E\) invariants used in Vol.~III remain distinct:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kfib(K3\times E)=24.
\]
The number \(5\) comes from the Borcherds weight of \(\DeltaFive\);
the compact chiral, categorical, and fibre invariants have their own
definitions and values.

\paragraph{Chiral and algebraic scope.}
For a Calabi--Yau threefold input, the native output of \(\Phi_3\) is
\(E_1\)-chiral.  \(E_2\)-structures occur after a centre, double, or
representation-category construction has been specified.  The
Hall--Drinfeld/BKM denominator branch considered here is separate from
the K3 Mukai self-mirror Yangian branch.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The three numerical rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{scope} & \text{orders or triples} & \text{weights} \\ \hline
K3\times E\ \text{CHL denominator ladder}
  & N=1,2,3,4,6 & 5,4,3,2,1\\
\text{Nikulin boundary orders}
  & N=5,7,8 & 2,1,1\\
\text{Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue}
  & (t,N_{\mathrm{lvl}};k)\ \text{as above}
  & 5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1
\end{array}
\]
The first row has the primitive \(K3\times E\) CHL denominator host used
in this note.  The second row supplies order-indexed Borcherds weights
at the Mukai boundary.  The third row is a separate paramodular
catalogue.
