\section*{Wave-12 B1: Szendroi--Morita for the conifold}

\subsection*{Cycle 1: the NCCR data of the conifold.}

Let $R = \bC[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw)$ be the affine cone on the quadric
$\bP^{1}\times\bP^{1}\subset\bP^{3}$; equivalently the affine toric
variety of the cone over the square. $R$ is a three-dimensional
Gorenstein normal Noetherian domain with an isolated singularity at the
origin (the node). Following Van den Bergh 2004 (\S 4.1, Definition~4.1;
arXiv:math/0207170), a \emph{non-commutative crepant resolution} of $R$
is an $R$-algebra $\Lambda=\End_{R}(M)$ with $M$ a finitely generated
reflexive $R$-module, such that
\[
  \mathrm{gldim}\,\Lambda<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad
  \Lambda\in\mathrm{CM}(R)
\]
(that is, $\Lambda$ is maximal Cohen--Macaulay over $R$). Van den Bergh
2004 Theorem~B proves: whenever $f\colon\cY\to\Spec R$ is a crepant
geometric resolution with one-dimensional fibres, there exists a tilting
bundle $\mathcal{P}=\cO_{\cY}\oplus\mathcal{L}$ on $\cY$ with
$\mathcal{L}$ of rank one, and
\[
  \Lambda:=\End_{\cY}(\mathcal{P})\cong\End_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{P})
\]
is an NCCR of $R$.

\subsection*{Cycle 2: the explicit conifold NCCR.}

For the conifold, $R$ has divisor class group $\mathrm{Cl}(R)\cong\mathbb Z$,
generated by the Weil divisor $D=\{x=z=0\}$; its ideal
$I:=(x,z)\subset R$ is the non-trivial rank-one reflexive module. Van den
Bergh 2004 \S 4.2 (Example; see also Wemyss 2018 arXiv:1404.0330
Theorem~2.17 for the MCM count) gives
\[
  M = R\oplus I,\qquad
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}:=\End_{R}(R\oplus I).
\]
Under the conifold flop of Bridgeland 2002 (arXiv:math/0009053,
Theorem~1.1) there are two crepant small resolutions $\cY_{\pm}$ with
exceptional $\bP^{1}$ and normal bundle $\cO(-1)\oplus\cO(-1)$; each
furnishes a tilting bundle $\cO_{\cY_{\pm}}\oplus\cO_{\cY_{\pm}}(\pm 1)$
whose endomorphism algebra is the same $\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}$ on the
base. The Bridgeland flop manifests as the two opposite MCM choices
$M=R\oplus I$ and $M^{\vee}=R\oplus I^{\vee}$, which yield isomorphic
NCCRs (an NCCR is insensitive to which side of the flop one picks).

\subsection*{Cycle 3: Szendroi's theorem --- Jacobi $=$ NCCR.}

The \emph{Klebanov--Witten quiver} $Q_{\mathrm{con}}$ has two vertices
$\{0,1\}$ and four arrows $a_{1},a_{2}\colon 0\to 1$ and
$b_{1},b_{2}\colon 1\to 0$; the superpotential is the cyclically
invariant quartic
\[
  W_{\mathrm{KW}}\;=\;a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2}\;-\;a_{1}b_{2}a_{2}b_{1}.
\]
The Jacobi algebra is
$J(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}}):=\bC Q_{\mathrm{con}}/\langle
\partial_{a_{i}}W,\partial_{b_{j}}W\rangle$; the cyclic derivatives read
$\partial_{a_{1}}W=b_{1}a_{2}b_{2}-b_{2}a_{2}b_{1}$ and the three
companions. Szendroi 2008 (Geom.~Topol.~12, Lemma~2.2 and Proposition
2.5; arXiv:0705.3419) proves
\[
  J(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}})\;\cong\;
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}\;=\;\End_{R}(R\oplus I)
\]
as $R$-algebras, with $R$ recovered as the centre
$Z(\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}})\cong R$. The isomorphism is given on
generators by
\[
  e_{0}\leftrightarrow \mathrm{id}_{R},\quad
  e_{1}\leftrightarrow \mathrm{id}_{I},\quad
  a_{1}\leftrightarrow (\cdot x),\;
  a_{2}\leftrightarrow (\cdot z),\;
  b_{1}\leftrightarrow (\cdot w),\;
  b_{2}\leftrightarrow (\cdot y),
\]
where the right-hand side reads as multiplication-by-element maps
$R\to I$ and $I\to R$; the quartic relations
$\partial_{a_{i}}W=\partial_{b_{j}}W=0$ are exactly the conifold
equation $xy=zw$ and its three cyclic rotations inside
$\End_{R}(R\oplus I)$. Generator-and-relation verification: the
$2\times 2$ block form of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}$ has
$\Hom_{R}(I,R)\cong(x,z)$ and $\Hom_{R}(R,I)=I=(x,z)$; both arrow-pairs
sit in these Hom-spaces as the four generators above, and the Jacobi
ideal closes under the Koszul-type conifold relation.

\subsection*{Cycle 4: derived equivalence and Bridgeland--King--Reid.}

Szendroi 2008 references but does not reprove the geometric derived
equivalence
\[
  \Db\Coh(\cY_{\pm})\;\simeq\;\Db(\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}\modd);
\]
this is Van den Bergh 2004 Theorem~B (Fourier--Mukai transform along the
tilting bundle $\cO_{\cY_{\pm}}\oplus\cO_{\cY_{\pm}}(\pm 1)$), the
Bridgeland--King--Reid type statement Bridgeland 2002 Theorem~1.1
specialised to the conifold flop diagram. Combining,
\[
  \Db\Coh(\cY_{+})\;\simeq\;\Db(\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}\modd)\;\simeq\;
  \Db\Coh(\cY_{-}),
\]
the two crepant resolutions are derived-equivalent via the NCCR as
common pivot. Szendroi's own contribution is orthogonal to the derived
equivalence: he uses the NCCR identification to \emph{define}
non-commutative Donaldson--Thomas invariants of the conifold as Euler
characteristics of moduli of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}$-modules, then
matches them to the Nekrasov--Okounkov commutative DT via the
isomorphism.

\subsection*{Cycle 5: what ``Morita equivalence'' means here.}

Szendroi 2008 uses the word ``Morita'' in a strong sense: the
identification $J(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}})\cong
\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}$ is an honest \emph{isomorphism of
$R$-algebras}, not merely an equivalence of their module categories.
Hence $J\modd$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}\modd$ are equal as
$R$-linear abelian categories (trivially Morita), and a fortiori
$\Db(J\modd)\simeq\Db(\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}\modd)$. The treatise
claim ``Jacobi algebra of the conifold quiver is Morita-equivalent to
the NCCR'' is correct and in fact understates Szendroi's statement,
which is isomorphism. The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\text{conifold})=1$
identification of
\texttt{chapters/examples/toric\_cy3\_coha.tex} Corollary
\texttt{cor:conifold-shadow} is consistent: the single compact curve
class $[\bP^{1}]\in K_{0}(\cY_{\pm})$ corresponds under the Morita
equivalence to the single non-trivial simple $\Lambda_{\mathrm{NCCR}}$
module supported at the node, and the CoHA of
$(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}})$ is Schiffmann--Vasserot's
CoHA of the Jacobi algebra of the resolved conifold.

\subsection*{Verdict.}

The treatise citation to Szendroi 2008 is correct and the
Jacobi--NCCR identification is \emph{stronger than Morita}: it is an
isomorphism of $R$-algebras, with the NCCR module
$M=R\oplus I$ and the Klebanov--Witten superpotential
$W_{\mathrm{KW}}=a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2}-a_{1}b_{2}a_{2}b_{1}$
matching generator-by-generator. The derived equivalence
$\Db\Coh(\cY_{\pm})\simeq\Db(J\modd)$ is Van den Bergh 2004
Theorem~B composed with Szendroi's isomorphism, with Bridgeland 2002
supplying the flop structure under which the two MCM choices
$I,I^{\vee}$ produce identified NCCRs.
