\section*{CHL Divisibility and Boundary Rows}

\paragraph{Primitive CHL ladder.}
For a symplectic K3 automorphism $g_N$ of order
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ and an order-$N$ translation $\sigma_N$ on an
elliptic curve $E$, the diagonal quotient
\[
X_N=(K3\times E)/\langle g_N\times\sigma_N\rangle
\]
is smooth. The translation supplies freeness of the threefold action.
The automorphic constant is supplied by the CHL Jacobi input and by
Borcherds' weight theorem, in the normalisation used by Gritsenko and
Nikulin:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \text{frame shape} & c_N(0) & \kBKM(\Phi_N) & \text{Borcherds form}\\
\hline
1 & 1^{24} & 10 & 5 & \Delta_5\\
2 & 1^8 2^8 & 8 & 4 & \Delta_4^{(2)}\\
3 & 1^6 3^6 & 6 & 3 & \Delta_3^{(3)}\\
4 & 1^4 2^2 4^4 & 4 & 2 & \Delta_2^{(4)}\\
6 & 1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2 & 2 & 1 & \Delta_1^{(6)} .
\end{array}
\]
The Borcherds-weight identity is
\[
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=\operatorname{wt}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}2
\]
row by row. The half-constant formula is the singular theta-lift
weight formula of Borcherds. The table is the five-row CHL ladder;
the Gritsenko and Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue is indexed by
paramodular data and multiplier systems.

\paragraph{The order-four quotient surface.}
For a symplectic automorphism $g_4$ of order $4$ on $K3$,
\[
\#\Fix(g_4)=4,\qquad \#\Fix(g_4^2)=8.
\]
The four points fixed by $g_4$ have stabiliser $\ZZ/4$ and give four
$A_3$ singularities on $K3/\langle g_4\rangle$. The remaining
$8-4=4$ points fixed by $g_4^2$ form two $\langle g_4\rangle$-orbits
with stabiliser $\ZZ/2$ and give two $A_1$ singularities. The Du-Val
type is
\[
4A_3+2A_1.
\]

\paragraph{The order-six quotient surface.}
For a symplectic automorphism $g_6$ of order $6$,
\[
\#\Fix(g_6)=2,\qquad \#\Fix(g_6^2)=6,\qquad \#\Fix(g_6^3)=8.
\]
The cyclic quotient $K3/\langle g_6\rangle$ has two stabiliser-$\ZZ_6$
orbits, two stabiliser-$\ZZ_3$ orbits, and two stabiliser-$\ZZ_2$
orbits. The Du-Val type is
\[
2A_5+2A_2+2A_1.
\]
The frame shape in the CHL row is $1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2$. The local
surface quotient datum above and the umbral Niemeier root system
$6D_4$ are different invariants.

\paragraph{Boundary symplectic orders.}
Nikulin's cyclic symplectic orders continue through $N=5,7,8$. They
have the following geometric scopes.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|l}
N & \text{frame shape} & c_N(0) & \kBKM & \text{geometric scope}\\
\hline
5 & 1^4 5^4 & 4 & 2 &
  \text{Borcea-Voisin CY}_3\text{ geometry}\\
7 & 1^3 7^3 & 2 & 1 &
  \text{singular quotient with gerbe and McKay correction data}\\
8 & 1^2 2^1 4^1 8^2 & 2 & 1 &
  \text{Kummer-type hyperk\"ahler geometry; CY}_3\text{ interpretation open}
\end{array}
\]
For $N=8$ the fixed-locus chain is
\[
\#\Fix(g_8)=2,\qquad
\#\Fix(g_8^2)=4,\qquad
\#\Fix(g_8^4)=8,
\]
so the quotient surface has Du-Val type $2A_7+A_3+A_1$. This is the
endpoint of the cyclic symplectic K3 list.

\paragraph{The $K3\times E$ invariant split.}
For the compact CHL total spaces,
\[
\kcat(X_N)=0,\qquad \kch(A_{X_N})=0.
\]
The first identity is $\chi(\mathcal O_{X_N})=0$. The second is the
Hodge supertrace
\[
\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X_N)=1-1+1-1=0,
\]
since the finite etale cover $K3\times E\to X_N$ preserves the
invariant $H^{0,\bullet}$ column. At the unquotiented
$K3\times E$ point, the four scalar invariants are
\[
\kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(A_{K3\times E})=3,\qquad
\kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=5,\qquad
\kfib(K3\times E)=24.
\]
They arise from the total-space holomorphic Euler characteristic, the
Heisenberg-Mukai chiral specialisation, the $\Delta_5$ Borcherds
weight, and the Mukai-lattice rank of the K3 fibre.
