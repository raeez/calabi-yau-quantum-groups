\section*{Nikulin orders beyond the CHL \texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E} slice}
\label{sec:nikulin-orders-beyond-chl-k3e}

\subsection*{Three index systems}

Three finite lists meet at the level-one denominator and separate
immediately after it.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}, leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=0.4ex]
\item The primitive CHL denominator branch on the $K3\times E$ slice is
indexed by
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
with Borcherds-input constants
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))
  =
  (10,8,6,4,2)
\]
and
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
\item The order-indexed Nikulin automorphic branch begins with a
symplectic K3 automorphism $g_N$ of order
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}.
\]
After the $g_N$-twined weak Jacobi input and its extended paramodular
Borcherds lift have been fixed, the weight computation has
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,4,2,2,2),
  \qquad
  \wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}})=c_N(0)/2=(5,4,3,2,2,1,1,1).
\]
Here $\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}}$ denotes the chosen automorphic product. A
compact $K3\times E$ Hall object requires additional data.
\item The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery dd-modular catalogue is indexed by
paramodular triples $(t,N;k)$. The second coordinate is a paramodular
level. The cyclic order of a K3 automorphism is separate data. Its eight triples are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(1,1;5),\ (2,1;2),\ (1,2;3),\ (3,1;1),\\
(1,3;2),\ (4,1;\tfrac12),\ (1,4;\tfrac32),\ (2,2;1).
\end{gathered}
\]
The weights are
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
with twice-weight tuple
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The common entry requiring no additional row map is the level-one form
$\Delta_5$ at $(t,N)=(1,1)$. A componentwise identification of the CHL
and Gritsenko--Cl\'ery lists gives the wrong constants already at the
next rows.

\subsection*{Level-one normalisation}

At $N=1$ the theta-normalised genus-two cusp form and the monic
Borcherds denominator differ by the leading theta coefficient:
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=\Dfive(2Z).
\]
The Jacobi input determines which automorphic line is being read:
\[
  \mathrm{BL}(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}})=\Dfive,\qquad
  \mathrm{BL}(2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}})=\Dfive^2.
\]
The first lift gives the primitive BKM denominator and the coefficient
$c_1(0)=10$ of $\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$, hence
$\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5$. The doubled K3 elliptic genus
$Z_{K3}=2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$ belongs to the scalar square line.
The square convention lives on the scalar Igusa line:
\[
  \Phiten^\theta=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  \Phiten^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
Thus $\Delta_5$ is the primitive denominator of weight $5$, while
$\Phiten$ is the scalar square of weight $10$ in the displayed
normalisation.

\subsection*{The CHL restriction}

The primitive $K3\times E$ CHL denominator construction uses the five
orders $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ because its elliptic factor carries an
origin-fixing cyclotomic automorphism of the same order. The condition is
\[
  \varphi(N)\mid 2,
\]
equivalently the realisability of the required $\mathbb Z[\zeta_N]$
linearisation on a complex elliptic curve. This gives
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.

An $N$-torsion translation on an elliptic curve exists for every
$N$. Such a translation can make a diagonal quotient free. The CHL
denominator specialisation also requires the elliptic cyclotomic
linearisation, the reduced DT comparison, and the Hall root datum. The
orders $5,7,8$ are Nikulin K3 orders whose automorphic Borcherds
products live on the extended paramodular side until a separate host
theorem is supplied.

\subsection*{Extended Nikulin orders}

For $N=5,7,8$ the Borcherds weight is the output of a specified
twined Jacobi input. Niemeier labels, fixed-point counts, and quotient
singularity types are auxiliary data; the constant is the coefficient
$c_N(0)$ of the twined Jacobi form.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \text{frame shape} & T_{H^*}(g_N) & c_N(0) &
\wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}}) \\ \hline
5 & 1^4 5^4 & 8 & 4 & 2 \\
7 & 1^3 7^3 & 6 & 2 & 1 \\
8 & 1^2 2^1 4^1 8^2 & 6 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
The row assignment is:
\[
  \text{K3 symplectic frame shape}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{twined weak Jacobi coefficient }c_N(0)
  \longrightarrow
  \wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}})=c_N(0)/2.
\]
The correction term in the twined Jacobi input is $A_N=2$ at
$N=5,7,8$, so $c_N(0)=T_{H^*}(g_N)-2A_N$ gives $(4,2,2)$. A BKM
interpretation of these three products requires root lattice, Weyl
vector, chamber, reflection character, and multiplicity data in
addition to the scalar automorphic product.

\subsection*{Relation to the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery catalogue}

Gritsenko--Cl\'ery classify dd-modular forms by paramodular triples
$(t,N;k)$ with diagonal Humbert divisor. This is an independent
classification from the CHL ladder. The catalogue contains two
half-integral weights, $1/2$ and $3/2$. It omits rows of weights $0$
and $1/4$. The six integer-weight entries live on paramodular
groups and their normal extensions; the two half-integral entries use
multiplier systems of orders $8$ and $4$.

The extended Nikulin entries $N=5,7,8$ have automorphic weights
$(2,1,1)$ in the order-indexed notation. Equal printed integers or
equal weights in the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery table carry separate row data.
The catalogue row fixes its paramodular group, divisor, multiplier
system, and Borcherds input. The Nikulin row fixes a K3 automorphism
and a twined Jacobi form.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E} invariants}

For $Y=K3\times E$,
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(Y)=(1,1,1,1),
  \qquad
  \kch(Y)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)=0,
\]
and K\"unneth multiplicativity gives
\[
  \kcat(Y)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, the Borcherds denominator, and
the K3 fibre rank are separate constructions:
\[
  \kHeis(Y)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=5,\qquad
  \kfib(Y)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\mathbb Z)=24.
\]
These numbers come from separate constructions. At the level-one denominator,
adding the compact chiral supertrace to the elliptic fibre Euler term
gives $0+0$, while the denominator weight is $5$. The valid identity is
the Borcherds weight formula
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2
\]
on a specified denominator product.

\subsection*{Algebraic branch boundaries}

The BKM/Hall--Drinfeld branch is controlled by Borcherds products,
Weyl chambers, Hall doubles, and compact Hall comparison data. The
Mukai/Yangian branch is controlled by the Mukai lattice, stable
envelopes, and root-of-unity Yangian or quantum-toroidal
specialisations. A theorem in one branch supplies no theorem in the
other branch.

The local positive-half convention is likewise fixed:
\[
  \CoHA(\bC^3)=Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1).
\]
The full $\mathcal W_{1+\infty}$ appears only after passing to a
double, centre, completion, or evaluation layer. For a CY$_3$ input
the specialised chiral output is $E_1$-chiral; an $E_2$ structure lives
on an associated centre, double, or module category.

\subsection*{What an extension would require}

Extending the geometric construction beyond the five CHL entries
requires four independent constructions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}, leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=0.4ex]
\item A CY$_3$ host theorem for $N=5,7,8$: either a $K3\times E$-type
quotient with the required CHL linearisation and reduced theory, or a
different K3-fibred host. Torsion freeness alone proves none of this.
\item A Borcherds-product theorem identifying the relevant twined
Jacobi form, its automorphic group, and the constant
$c_N(0)$ in the chosen normalisation. The automorphic values in the
extended Nikulin normalisation are
\[
  (c_5(0),c_7(0),c_8(0))=(4,2,2),
  \qquad
  \wt=(2,1,1).
\]
\item A root-data theorem giving the Lorentzian lattice, Weyl vector,
positive chamber, reflection character, and signed simple-root
multiplicities needed for a BKM or Hall--Drinfeld interpretation.
\item A fresh computation of $\kcat$, $\kch$, and any specialisation
invariants for the replacement CY$_3$ host. K\"unneth multiplicativity gives
$\kcat(K3\times E)=0$ on the product slice. A non-product or resolved
quotient geometry requires its own Hodge computation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Conclusion}

The primitive CHL denominator theorem remains a five-entry theorem on
the $K3\times E$ specialisation:
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},\qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The order-indexed Nikulin automorphic products extend the weight
calculation to $N=5,7,8$ with constants $(4,2,2)$ and weights
$(2,1,1)$ after the twined Jacobi input has been specified. The
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery catalogue is a separate eight-row
diagonal-divisor classification. A compact BKM or DT interpretation of
the non-CHL orders requires a host-and-root-data theorem beyond the
integer $N$.
