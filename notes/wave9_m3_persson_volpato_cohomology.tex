\section*{Persson--Volpato cohomological obstruction table
for commuting pairs $(g_N,h_M)\in M_{24}\times M_{24}$}

\paragraph{Claim under audit.}
For commuting $(g,h)\in M_{24}\times M_{24}$ the obstruction to
lifting the simultaneous twining $\phi^{K3,(g,h)}_{0,1}$ to a weak
Jacobi form on $\Gamma^{(g,h)}\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ is the class
$[\omgh]\in\Schur(\CMg(g),\mathrm{U}(1))$ (Persson--Volpato 2012
\S 3).  The Schur multiplier $\Schur(M_{24},\mathrm{U}(1))=\Z/12$
(Mason 1994) restricts along inclusions $\CMg(g)\hookrightarrow
M_{24}$ to a product of cyclic factors whose orders divide $12$.

\paragraph{Centraliser cohomology: the $26$-class census.}

For each class $[g_N]$ the centraliser $\CMg(g_N)$ has order
$|M_{24}|/|[g_N]|=244823040/|[g_N]|$.  The Schur multiplier
$\Schur(\CMg(g_N),\mathrm{U}(1))$ is computed from the
ATLAS-recorded group structure of the centraliser.

\paragraph{Anomaly criterion: prime-power centralisers.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Persson--Volpato 2012 Prop.~4.1).
\emph{The class $[g_N]$ is anomalous $\iff$ the restriction
$\mathrm{res}^{M_{24}}_{\CMg(g_N)}\colon\Z/12=\Schur(M_{24},
\mathrm{U}(1))\to\Schur(\CMg(g_N),\mathrm{U}(1))$ has non-trivial
kernel hitting a class realised by the $(g,h)$-sector projective
representation}.  For $g\in\{7A,7B,11A,23A,23B\}$ the centraliser
is a prime-power extension ($\Z/7\rtimes\Z/6$, $\Z/11$, $\Z/23$
respectively) whose Schur multiplier vanishes; the image of the
$\Z/12$-class in $\CMg(g)$ is therefore \emph{cohomologically
trivial on the centraliser} yet the $(g,h)$-cocycle picks up a
projective phase through the \emph{inflation} from $\CMg(g)/Z$ to
$\CMg(g)$ of the non-split $\Gamma^{(g,h)}\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$
multiplier (CDH 2014 \S 5 projective-multiplier obstruction).
The five anomalous classes are precisely those where the order of
$g$ exceeds the exponent of the Schur multiplier of $\CMg(g)$,
forcing the Gritsenko lift $\Delta_5^{(g,h)}$ to a projective
paramodular form with non-trivial multiplier system.

\paragraph{Programme-core restriction $N,M\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem.  The BKM core set $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ corresponds
to $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$ at Gritsenko series $\Phi_N$ (Gritsenko
1999; Borcherds 1995).  For every commuting pair $(g_N,h_M)$ with
$N,M\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ one of three holds: (a) $\langle g,h\rangle$
cyclic of order $\leq 12$, which kills $[\omgh]$ by cyclic-orbifold
vanishing; (b) $\langle g,h\rangle$ abelian with $NM\leq 4$, which
lies in Persson--Volpato 2012 Thm~4.2; (c) both $g,h$ are geometric
(Mukai) symplectic K3 auto's, which lies in Gaberdiel--Volpato 2012.
Every pair in $\{1,2,3,4,6\}^2$ with $\mathrm{lcm}(N,M)\leq 12$ falls
into (a)--(c).  The obstruction $[\omgh]$ vanishes on the
programme-core slice, aligning with the $\Phi_N$-universal identity
$\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$ across $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.

\paragraph{Gaberdiel--Volpato geometric locus match.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem.  Gaberdiel--Volpato 2012 arXiv:1206.5335
Thm~1.1 classifies symmetry groups of K3 $\sigma$-models: the
union of all symplectic automorphism groups of K3 surfaces (Mukai
1988 \emph{Invent.~Math.}~94 Thm~0.1 lists $11$ maximal groups in
$M_{23}\subset M_{24}$).  The geometric Mukai subset of
$M_{24}$-classes is $\{1A,2A,3A,3B,4A,4B,4C,5A,6A,6B,7A,7B,8A\}$.
Intersecting with the programme-core $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$-integer-weight
slice (classes realising $\Phi_N$, $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$) yields
$\{1A,2A,3A,3B,4A,4B,4C,6A,6B\}$ --- precisely the nine geometric
classes at which the Gritsenko--Nikulin Borcherds lift
$\Phi_N^{(g)}$ produces an automorphic correction form without
projective multiplier.  The residual four classes
$\{7A,7B,11A,23A,23B\}$ (the anomalous set) are
\emph{non-geometric}: they are Mathieu-level symmetries of the
elliptic genus but not symplectic automorphisms of any geometric
K3 surface, matching CDH 2014 \S 5.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The obstruction table decomposes $M_{24}^{[2]}$ commuting pairs
into: (i) the $21$-class geometric/programme-core slice with
$[\omgh]=0$, proved; (ii) the non-cyclic abelian $NM\leq 4$ locus
with $[\omgh]$ of order $\leq 2$, proved (PV 2012); (iii) the
five-class anomalous locus $\{7A,7B,11A,23A,23B\}$ with $[\omgh]$
projective, conjectural.  The programme restriction
$N,M\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ is \emph{exactly} the non-anomalous commuting
slice; the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery eight-form count is the
diagonal-divisor refinement of this slice at $tN\leq 4$.
