Fix $X=K3\times E$ with $L$ a primitive polarisation of degree $h$ and
$\beta_h\in H_2(K3,\mathbb Z)$ primitive effective with
$\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle=2h-2$.  Write
$\alpha=(n,\ell,m)\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{\mathrm{II}}$ for a positive root of
$\gDelta$ and set $h=nm$.

\begin{theorem}[The primitive $N=1$ denominator and scalar square]
\label{thm:lorgat-conj1-N1-complete}
Let
\[
  \phiEZ(\tau,z)=\sum_{a,b} f(a,b)q^a y^b,
  \qquad
  \phiEZ=y^{-1}+10+y+O(q).
\]
For every active positive root in the Gritsenko and Nikulin chamber with
$\gcd(n,\ell,m)=1$,
\[
  \operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\alpha}=f(nm,\ell).
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is the doubled input
\[
  Z_{K3}=2\phiEZ,\qquad c_{K3}(h,\ell)=2f(h,\ell),
\]
and the primitive reduced scalar exponent satisfies
\[
  c_{K3}(h,\ell)
  =
  n^{0}_{(\beta_h,\ell)}(X)
  =
  N^{\mathrm{DT}}_{(\beta_h,\ell)}(X).
\]
The scalar automorphic identity is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:OP-igusa-N1-normalized}
  \ZDTred(q,\tilde q,y)
  =
  -(\PhiOP)^{-1}
  =
  -\Dfive^{-2}
  =
  -4096\,\Delta_5^{-2},
  \qquad
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\quad
  \PhiOP=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\end{equation}
The Borcherds characteristic is
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=\left.c_N(0)/2\right|_{N=1}=10/2=5.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Gritsenko and Nikulin construct a rank-$3$ BKM Lie superalgebra $\gDelta$
whose real simple roots $\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3$ have Gram matrix
\[
  G=\begin{pmatrix}
  2&-2&-2\\
  -2&2&-2\\
  -2&-2&2
  \end{pmatrix}=2(2I-J_3).
\]
The Weyl vector is
\[
  \rho=\tfrac12(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
       =f_2-\tfrac12 f_3+f_{-2},
  \qquad
  (\rho,\delta_i)=-1,\quad
  (\rho,\rho)=-\tfrac32.
\]
The Weyl, Kac, and Borcherds denominator is the primitive product
\[
  \Dfive(Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z)
  =
  q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
  \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}.
\]
The product exponent is the signed root superdimension on the active
support, so the primitive denominator gives
\(\operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\alpha}=f(nm,\ell)\).

The input Jacobi form is Eichler and Zagier's form
\[
  \phiEZ=\sum f(n,\ell)q^ny^\ell,\qquad
  \phiEZ=(y^{-1}+10+y)+O(q),
\]
so $c_1(0)=10$.  Borcherds' weight theorem gives
$\kBKM(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=5$.

The full K3 elliptic genus is \(Z_{K3}=2\phiEZ\). Its coefficients are
\(c_{K3}(h,\ell)=2f(h,\ell)\), and these doubled exponents give the
Igusa square. In the Oberdieck and Pixton convention this square is
\[
  \PhiOP=\Dfive^2=(64^{-1}\Delta_5)^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
Thus $-(\PhiOP)^{-1}=-4096\Delta_5^{-2}$.

Oberdieck proves the Oberdieck and Pixton reduced series for $K3\times E$:
the absolute reduced theory is assembled from the relative theory of
$K3\times\mathbb A^1$ through the degeneration
$E\rightsquigarrow\mathbb P^1_{\mathrm{nodal}}$, with the rubber factor
$\eta^{24}$.  The Maulik, Pandharipande, and Thomas reduced
$\mathrm{GW}/\mathrm{DT}$ correspondence identifies the genus-zero
reduced Gromov-Witten and Donaldson-Thomas series in the primitive class,
and the OP scalar convention records the Behrend sign.

Pandharipande and Thomas prove the Katz, Klemm, and Vafa formula in
stable-pairs form. The genus-zero Gopakumar-Vafa integer in the
primitive class is the coefficient \(c_{K3}(h,\ell)\). The reduced
Donaldson-Thomas primitive logarithmic exponent is the same
coefficient by reduced MNOP in genus zero. Combining this with
\(c_{K3}=2f\) separates the primitive denominator equality from the
scalar-square equality and proves \eqref{eq:OP-igusa-N1-normalized}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Invariant split.}
For \(K3\times E\) the invariant readings are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \kcat(K3\times E)
    &=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\,\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0,\\
  \kch^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(K3\times E)
    &=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(K3\times E)=0,\\
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)
    &=3,\\
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)
    &=5,\\
  \kfib(K3)
    &=24.
\end{aligned}
\]
The value \(\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})=2\) belongs to the fibre calculation.
The total-space categorical value is \(0\). The Hodge, Heisenberg,
Borcherds, and fibre readings come from distinct constructions.

\paragraph{Boundary of the theorem.}
The proof uses the primitive $N=1$ chamber, identity monodromy, and the
OP normalisation \eqref{eq:OP-igusa-N1-normalized}.  Higher CHL orders
and nontrivial $h_M$ require their own twined denominator forms.
Gritsenko and Clery rows enter through an explicit row map and
normalisation.  For imprimitive roots the denominator side is
\[
  \operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\alpha}
  =
  \sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,\ell,m)}
  \mu(d)\,f(nm/d^2,\ell/d),
\]
which replaces the primitive exponent \(f(nm,\ell)\) by the divisor sum.
