\section{From reflection lattice to Siegel atlas}
\label{sec:delta5-reflection-siegel-atlas}

Fix an even Lorentzian $(2,1)$ lattice $\LamII$. Its reflection group
$\Wtwo(\LamII)$ and Weyl vector $\rho$ order the Fourier expansion of
$\Delaf$ into a Weyl denominator. The real roots give the Lorentzian
Kac--Moody skeleton. The weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$ supplies the
imaginary simple-root multiplicities. The completed denominator is the
Siegel cusp form $\Delaf$, equivalently the monic Borcherds product
$D_5=64^{-1}\Delaf$. This is the automorphic bootstrap behind the
primitive CHL identity
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2},
  \qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]

For $X=K3\times E$ the numerical invariants are separated:
\[
  \kcat(X)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0,
  \qquad
  \kch(X)=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(X)=0,
\]
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(X)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\gbkm)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=\mathrm{rank}\,H^\ast(K3,\ZZ)=24.
\]
The first two are total-space invariants, the third is a
Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, the fourth is the Borcherds weight of
$\Delaf$, and the fifth is the Mukai-lattice rank of the K3 fibre. No
additive law relates these numbers.

\subsection{The architectural frame in one diagram}
\label{subsec:atlas-diagram}

The construction has four layers:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{lattice data } (L, g_N, h_M) & \xrightarrow{\;\text{twisted elliptic genus}\;} & \phi^{(g_N, h_M)}_L(z_1, z_2) \\
\Big\downarrow\scriptstyle{\text{reflection}} & & \Big\downarrow\scriptstyle{\text{Borcherds lift}} \\
\Wtwo(L^{2,1}), \rho_L & \xrightarrow{\;\;\text{Weyl--Kac--Borcherds}\;\;} & \Phi(L, g_N, h_M)(z)
\end{array}
\]
Horizontal arrows: automorphic content. Vertical arrows: Lie-algebraic
content. The left column is finite-dimensional reflection geometry on
$L^{2,1}$ and the Gritsenko--Nikulin Weyl-vector computation; the
right column is a Siegel modular form of weight
$c_{(g_N,h_M)}(0)/2$ on $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\ZZ)$ or on a paramodular
congruence subgroup. When $\phi^{(g_N,h_M)}_L$ is a weight-$0$ weak
Jacobi form of index $1$, or the corresponding higher-index input for
composite twists, Borcherds' singular theta lift supplies the right
vertical arrow.

For $N=1$ the Borcherds lift of the K3 elliptic genus gives the inverse
Donaldson--Thomas partition function of $K3\times E$. For the twisted
quotients $(S\times E)/\ZZ_N$ the same assertion is a conjectural
arithmetic-geometric atlas: the twisted elliptic genus determines a
Borcherds denominator, and the reciprocal denominator is expected to be
the reduced DT partition function of the corresponding CY3 quotient.

\subsection{Layer 1: reflection architecture of $\LamII$}
\label{subsec:refl}

\begin{theorem}[Reflection structure of $\LamII$]
\label{thm:refl-LamII}
Let $\Lamtwoone = \Lambda^{1,1} \oplus [2] = \ZZ f_2 \oplus \ZZ f_3 \oplus \ZZ f_{-2}$
be the primitive hyperbolic sublattice with Gram matrix
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
and
$\LamII = \{m f_2 + l f_3 + n f_{-2} \in \Lamtwoone : m \equiv n \equiv 0 \pmod 2\}$.
Let $\Wtwo(\Lamtwoone) = \langle s_\delta : \delta \in \Lamtwoone,\ (\delta,\delta)=2,\ \delta\ \text{primitive}\rangle$
be the reflection group generated by primitive square-$2$ reflections.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W(\Lamtwoone) = \Wtwo(\Lamtwoone)$, every reflection is of
Type I $((\delta, \Lamtwoone) = \ZZ)$ or Type II $((\delta, \Lamtwoone) = 2\ZZ)$.
\item The sublattices $\LamI, \LamII$ generated by primitive square-$2$
vectors of each type yield normal subgroups $\Wtwo(\LamI)$,
$\Wtwo(\LamII)$ of $\Wtwo(\Lamtwoone)$ of indices $2$ and $6$.
\item $O(\Lamtwoone)_+ \simeq \Wtwo(\LamII) \rtimes S_3$.
\item The fundamental polyhedron $\PII \subset \mathcal{C}(\Lamtwoone)_+/\RR_{>0}$
has primitive orthogonal square-$2$ roots
$\PIIprim = \{\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3,\ \delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3,\ \delta_3 = f_3\}$
with Gram matrix
$(\delta_i, \delta_j) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
and stabiliser $A(\PII) \simeq S_3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Primary source]
Gritsenko--Nikulin, \emph{Siegel automorphic form corrections of some
Lorentzian Kac--Moody Lie algebras}, American J.\ Math.\ 119 (1997)
181--224. The Type I / Type II dichotomy and the $\wedge^2$
identification
$\mathrm{Sp}_4(\ZZ)/\{\pm I\} \simeq \mathrm{SO}_+(\Lambda^{3,2})$
are standard in this lattice model.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weyl vector of $\PII$]
\label{thm:weyl-vector-rho}
The Weyl vector $\rho$ for the reflection system
$\Wtwo(\LamII)$ acting on $\PII$ is
\[
  \rho \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_3
  \;=\; f_2 - \tfrac{1}{2}f_3 + f_{-2}.
\]
It satisfies $(\rho, \delta_i) = -1$ for each simple root and lies in
the dual cone $\Delta(\LamII)^{*}_+ \cap \LamII^{*}$; identifying
$\LamII \otimes \QQ \simeq \Lamtwoone \otimes \QQ$, one has
$\rho \in \Delta(\LamII)^{*}_+$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Weyl-vector normalisation]
The Weyl vector is the unique element of the simple-root dual cone
normalising each pairing to $-1$. Its fractional part in $f_3$ records
the parity condition $m \equiv n \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ defining $\LamII$.
The factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$ carries through the Fourier expansion and
fixes the $\exp(\pi i(z_1+z_2+z_3))$ prefactor of the infinite product.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Layer 2: automorphic invariance and the Fourier expansion}
\label{subsec:automorphic}

\begin{theorem}[Invariance / anti-invariance of $\Delaf$]
\label{thm:inv-anti-Delta5}
$\Delaf$ is either invariant or anti-invariant under every
reflection $s_\alpha$ of $O(\Lamtwoone)_+$. For $\alpha \in \{\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3\}$
(the $\PIIprim$ roots),
\[
  \Delaf(s_\alpha z) = -\Delaf(z).
\]
For $\alpha \in \{f_{-2} - f_2,\ f_2 - f_3,\ f_2 + f_3\}$ (the $\PIprim$ roots),
$\Delaf(s_\alpha z) = \Delaf(z)$. Under the full reflection group
$\Wtwo(\LamII)$,
\[
  \Delaf(w \cdot a z) = \det(w)\,\Delaf(z)\quad\text{for } w \in \Wtwo(\LamII),\ a \in \mathrm{Aut}(\PII).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Primary source]
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1997 and Maass's explicit multiplier formula for
$\mathrm{Sp}_4(\ZZ)$. The calculation uses the matrix representatives
$s_{f_{-2}-f_2}, s_{f_3}, s_{f_2-f_3}$ in
$\mathrm{GL}_2(\ZZ)/\{\pm I\}$ after the $\wedge^2$ identification.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fourier expansion reordered by $\rho$]
\label{prop:fourier-rho}
Let $z = z_1 f_{-2} + z_2 f_3 + z_3 f_2 \in \Lamtwoone \otimes \RR + i\mathcal{C}(\Lamtwoone)_+$.
For $(n, l, m) \in \ZZ^3$ with $n, m > 0$ and $n \equiv m \equiv l \equiv 1 \pmod 2$
and $4nm - l^2 > 0$, put
$a = (n-1)f_2 - (l-1)\tfrac{1}{2}f_3 + (m-1)f_{-2} \in \tfrac{1}{2}\Lamtwoone$
and $m(a) = -\tfrac{1}{64} f(n, l, m)$. Then:
\[
  \tfrac{1}{64} f(n,l,m) \exp(\pi i (n z_1 + l z_2 + m z_3))
  \;=\; m(a)\exp(-\pi i(\rho + a, z)),
\]
$\rho + a \in \mathcal{C}(\Lamtwoone)_+$, $m(a) \in \ZZ$ and
$m(0) = -1$. Combining with Theorem~\ref{thm:inv-anti-Delta5}:
\[
  \tfrac{1}{64}\Delaf(z)
  = \sum_{w \in \Wtwo(\LamII)} \det(w)\!\Bigl(\sum_{\rho + a \in (\LamII)^{*} \cap \LamII} m(a)\exp(-\pi i(w(\rho+a), z))\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Cone embedding and isotropic transitivity]
\label{thm:cone-embedding}
The cone embedding
$\Delta(\LamII)^{*}_+ \subset \mathcal{C}(\LamII)^{*}_+ = \mathcal{C}(\LamII)_+
\subset \Delta(\LamII)_+$ forces
\[
  a \in \LamII \cap \RR_{>0}\PII
  \quad\text{if } a \neq 0, \qquad m(0) = -1.
\]
$\mathrm{Aut}(\PII)$ is transitive on the three isotropic vertices
$\{2f_2,\ 2f_{-2},\ 2f_2 - 2f_3 + 2f_{-2}\}$ of $\overline{\PII}$
at infinity. Preservation of the Fourier expansion by $\mathrm{Aut}(\PII)$
yields, for $a_0 = 2f_2$ and every $t \in \NN$,
\[
  1 - \sum_{t \in \NN} m(t a_0) q^t
  = \prod_{t \in \NN} (1 - q^t)^{\tau(t a_0)},
\]
and the $\eta^9$ factor $A=\eta(z_1)^9\nu_{11}(z_1,z_2)$ gives
$\tau(a_0)=9$ for a primitive isotropic vertex.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Isotropic multiplicity]
The factor $\eta^9$ fixes the weight-$1/2$ prefactor of
$\psi_{5,1/2}e^{2\pi i t z_3}$ at the cusp of
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delaf^2$. The integer $9$ is Gritsenko's
isotropic simple-root multiplicity above the chosen vertex. It is
independent of the primitive CHL constants $(10,8,6,4,2)$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Layer 3: automorphic correction and the GBKM algebra}
\label{subsec:gbkm}

\begin{theorem}[Imaginary simple roots and the root system]
\label{thm:imag-roots}
Starting from the Kac--Moody generated by the real simple roots
$\PIIprim = \ZZ\delta_1 \oplus \ZZ\delta_2 \oplus \ZZ\delta_3$ with
Cartan matrix
$(\delta_i, \delta_j) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
define the imaginary simple-root multiset by
\[
  \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_0 = \{a : (a,a)=0,\ \tau(a)>0\}
  \quad\text{with multiplicity }\tau(a),
\]
\[
  \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_1 = \{a : (a,a)<0,\ m(a)<0\}
  \quad\text{as odd simple roots, with multiplicity }-m(a),
\]
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}} = \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_0 \cup \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_1$,
$\Delta^{\mathrm{re}} = \Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_0 = \PIIprim$.
Then $\Delta = \Delta^{\mathrm{re}} \cup \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}$ is a
Borcherds--Kac--Moody root system: $(\alpha,\alpha) > 0$ on
$\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}$, $(\alpha,\alpha) \leq 0$ on
$\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}$, $(\alpha, \alpha') \leq 0$ for distinct roots,
and $2(\alpha, \alpha')/(\alpha, \alpha) \in \ZZ$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[The generalised Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra $\gbkm$]
\label{def:gbkm}
$\gbkm$ is the Lie superalgebra generated by $\{e_\alpha, h_\alpha, f_\alpha : \alpha \in \Delta\}$
subject to: $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ relations
$[h_\alpha, e_{\alpha'}] = (\alpha, \alpha') e_{\alpha'}$,
$[h_\alpha, f_{\alpha'}] = -(\alpha, \alpha') f_{\alpha'}$,
$[e_\alpha, f_{\alpha'}] = \delta_{\alpha, \alpha'} h_\alpha$;
orthogonality $[e_\alpha, e_{\alpha'}] = [f_\alpha, f_{\alpha'}] = 0$
whenever $(\alpha, \alpha') = 0$; and Serre relations
\[
  (\mathrm{ad}\, e_\alpha)^{1 - 2(\alpha, \alpha')/(\alpha, \alpha)}\, e_{\alpha'}
  = (\mathrm{ad}\, f_\alpha)^{1 - 2(\alpha, \alpha')/(\alpha, \alpha)}\, f_{\alpha'} = 0
  \quad (\alpha \in \Delta^{\mathrm{re}}).
\]
The algebra is $\LamII$-graded; with integral simple-root cone
$\mathcal{C}_\Delta = \sum_{\alpha \in \Delta} \ZZ_+ \alpha \subset \LamII$,
there is a triangular decomposition
\[
  \gbkm = \bigl(\bigoplus_{\alpha \in \mathcal{C}_\Delta} \gbkm_\alpha\bigr) \oplus \LamII \oplus \bigl(\bigoplus_{\alpha \in -\mathcal{C}_\Delta} \gbkm_\alpha\bigr),
\]
and $\mult_\alpha = \dim \gbkm_{\alpha, 0} - \dim \gbkm_{\alpha, 1}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator identity]
\label{thm:bgn-denominator}
The Weyl--Kac--Borcherds character applied to the trivial $\gbkm$-module yields
\[
  \Phi(z) = \sum_{w \in \Wtwo(\LamII)} \det(w)\!\Bigl(e^{-2\pi i(w\rho, z)} - \!\!\!\!\sum_{a \in \LamII \cap \RR_{>0}\PII}\!\!\!\!\! m(a)\, e^{-2\pi i (w(\rho+a), z)}\Bigr)
  = e^{-2\pi i(\rho, z)}\!\!\!\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}\!\!(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha, z)})^{\mult_\alpha},
\]
valid on $\Omega(\LamII) = \LamII \otimes \RR + i \mathcal{C}(\LamII)_+$. One has
\[
  \tfrac{1}{64}\Delaf(2z) = \Phi(z):
\]
the automorphic correction of $\gbkm$ has denominator the Siegel cusp form of genus $2$, weight $5$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Primary source]
Borcherds, \emph{Automorphic forms on $O_{s+2,2}(\RR)$ and infinite products},
Invent.\ Math.\ 120 (1995); Gritsenko--Nikulin, Amer.\ J.\ Math.\ 119 (1997).
The infinite-product expression and the identification with $\tfrac{1}{64}\Delaf(2z)$
is the Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator normalisation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicity formula via $\phi_{0,1}$]
\label{thm:mult-phi01}
Let
\[
  \phi_{12,1}=(r^{-1}+10+r)q+O(q^2)
\]
be the weight-$12$ index-$1$ Jacobi cusp form coming from the
Fourier--Jacobi expansion of $\Delta_{12}$, and set
$\phi_{0,1} = \phi_{12,1}/\delta_{12}$ with
$\delta_{12} = q \prod_n (1 - q^n)^{24}$. Write
$\phi_{0,1} = \sum_{n \geq 0, l \in \ZZ} f(n, l)\, \exp(2\pi i(n z_1 + l z_2))$.
Then:
\[
  D_5(z):=\tfrac{1}{64}\Delaf(z)
  = \exp(\pi i (z_1+z_2+z_3))
    \prod_{(n,l,m)>0}
    (1-\exp(2\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)))^{f(nm,l)},
\]
with $(n,l,m) > 0$ meaning $n\geq0$, $m\geq0$ with $l$ arbitrary if
$n>0$ or $m>0$, and $l<0$ if $n=m=0$. The BKM character normalisation
uses $D_5(2z)=64^{-1}\Delaf(2z)$, and
$\mult_\alpha=f(nm,l)$ reads the root super-multiplicities from the
Jacobi-form coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Primary source]
The Fourier--Jacobi extraction of $\phi_{12,1}$ from $\Delta_{12}$ and
the normalisation by $\delta_{12}$ produce the Gritsenko--Nikulin
weight-$0$ index-$1$ weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$. Borcherds'
product theorem turns its coefficients into the imaginary-root
multiplicities of the denominator algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Automorphic correction]
The real-root Weyl denominator
$e^{-2\pi i(\rho, z)}\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_+}(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha, z)})$
is only the Lorentzian Kac--Moody skeleton. The imaginary-root product
$\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_+}(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha, z)})^{\mult_\alpha}$,
with multiplicities $f(nm,l)$ from $\phi_{0,1}$, completes the Siegel
modular form. The weight $5=c_1(0)/2$ is the automorphic weight of the
input at the cusp. For the primitive CHL denominators, the same
mechanism gives $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Layer 4: atlas of arithmetic-geometry CY3 quotients}
\label{subsec:atlas}

\begin{theorem}[$N=1$ Donaldson--Thomas / Siegel product identity]
\label{thm:dt-siegel-N1}
For $X = S \times E$ a smooth projective CY3 arising from an elliptically fibered K3 surface $(S, s \mapsto s + s_2(\pi(s)), \mathrm{id})$ and an elliptic curve $(E, e_0)$, let
\[
  Z^{S \times E}(q, t, p) = \sum_{h \geq 0, d \geq 0, n \in \ZZ} \mathrm{DT}_{n, (\beta_h, d)}\, q^{d - 1}\, t^{\tfrac{1}{2}\langle \beta_h, \beta_h\rangle}\, (-p)^n
\]
be the reduced elliptic Donaldson--Thomas partition function, with
$\beta_h\in H_2(S,\ZZ)$ primitive of square
$\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle=2h-2$ and
$\mathrm{DT}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}$ the Behrend-weighted reduced invariant
on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(X,(\beta_h,d))/E$. In the Oberdieck--Pandharipande
monic normalisation,
\[
  Z^{S\times E}(q,t,p)
  =
  -\frac{1}{\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}}
  =
  -D_5^{-2}
  =
  -\frac{4096}{\Delaf(q,t,p)^2}.
\]
In the square theta normalisation,
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delaf^2$. The geometric elliptic genus of
$K3$ equals $2\phi_{0,1}$, while the Borcherds input uses the
Gritsenko--Nikulin normalisation with $c_1(0)=10$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Primary source]
Oberdieck, \emph{Gromov--Witten theory, Hurwitz numbers, and
conjectures on the Donaldson--Thomas theory of $K3 \times E$},
Advances (2018); Bryan, \emph{The Donaldson--Thomas theory of
$K3 \times E$ via the topological vertex}, Geometry \& Topology
(2021); Bryan--Oberdieck--Pandharipande for the reduced DT product.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Twisted elliptic genus atlas]
\label{conj:twisted-atlas}
Let $(S, g_N, h_M)$ be a K3 surface with two symplectic automorphisms of orders $N, M \leq 8$. Let $L$ be a lattice polarization and $(E, e_0)$ an elliptic curve with an $N$-torsion point. For the CY3 quotient $X = (S \times E)/\ZZ_N$ acting by $(s, e) \mapsto (g_N s, e + e_0)$, there is an $L$-twisted DT series $Z^X_{L, h_M}(q, t, p)$.

For each admissible datum whose twisted elliptic genus
$F_L^{(g_N,h_M)}$ is a Borcherds input, the conjectural identity is
\[
  \operatorname{Bor}\bigl(F_L^{(g_N,h_M)}\bigr)
  =
  -Z^X_{L,h_M}(q,t,p)^{-1}
\]
up to the normalising scalar fixed at the cusp. The associated
denominator algebra $\gbkmLh$ is determined by the same twisted genus:
it has weight-$0$ index-$1$ input for $N\in\{1,2,3,5,7\}$ and
higher-index input for $N\in\{4,6,8\}$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Twisted-genus cases]
The explicit cases are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 7\}$: $A = \tfrac{1}{4}\phi_{0,1}^2,\ B = \phi_{-2,1} = \nu_1^2/\eta^6$, and for all $1 \leq s, r \leq N-1$, $0 \leq k \leq N-1$ the explicit $F_L^{(g_N^r, g_N^s)}$ is a Jacobi form of weight $0$ index $1$.
\item $N = 4$: for $s \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ the twisted genus is index $1$ or $2$ depending on $(r, s)$.
\item $N = 6$: analogous enumeration, index $\in \{1, 2, 3\}$.
\item $N = 8$: the elementary cases degenerate and additional lattice
polarization input $L$ is required.
\end{enumerate}
Each admissible case produces a sibling BKM superalgebra $\gbkmLh$
whose denominator is the corresponding twisted Siegel--Jacobi product.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[$\kBKM$ universality across $N \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$]
\label{thm:kBKM-universal}
Within the $\Phi_N$ hierarchy (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998, Scheithauer 2006),
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2
\]
with $c_N(0)$ the constant Fourier coefficient of the Borcherds--weight function associated to the $N$-twisted elliptic genus $F^{(g_N)}$. The values are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\\hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2 \\
\kBKM & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
At $N = 1$ this is Theorem~\ref{thm:bgn-denominator} (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1997 via Borcherds 1995). At $N \in \{2, 3\}$ this follows from Gritsenko--Nikulin's explicit Borcherds products for the $\Phi_N$ denominators. At $N \in \{4, 6\}$ the values are those of Scheithauer 2006 (the Scheithauer fake-$N=4,6$ superalgebras).
\end{theorem}

\begin{openproblem}[$N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$]
\label{prob:N-5-7-8}
The primitive smooth $K3\times E$ CHL ladder used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:kBKM-universal} has orders $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. Nikulin
orders $\{5,7,8\}$ belong to a different extension: the K3 side has
symplectic automorphisms of those orders, while the elliptic factor
lacks the corresponding origin-fixing CM quotient. A denominator
theorem for $N\in\{5,7,8\}$ must therefore specify a different quotient
geometry, multiplier system, and Borcherds input. The primitive
constant tuple remains $(10,8,6,4,2)$ for
$N=(1,2,3,4,6)$.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Framed specialisation of $\Phi_3$}
\label{subsec:two-stage-agreement}

\begin{proposition}[Borcherds atlas as framed $E_1$ specialisations]
\label{prop:atlas-stage-2}
Assume the framed CY$_3$ specialisation of $\Phi_3$ exists for
$D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3\times E)$ under the hypotheses of the
CY-to-chiral construction. For each atlas datum $(L,g_N,h_M)$ whose
twisted genus is a Borcherds input, the corresponding cycle
$\Sigma_2^{(L,g_N,h_M)}\subset K3\times E$ gives an $E_1$-chiral
shadow
\[
  A_3^{(L,g_N,h_M)}
  :=
  \Sp_{\Sigma_2^{(L,g_N,h_M)},E}
  \bigl(\Phihol_3(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3\times E))\bigr)
  \in \mathrm{ChirAlg}_{E_1}.
\]
After the Borcherds pushforward, the denominator attached to this
shadow is $\Phi(L,g_N,h_M)(z)$. The native output is $E_1$; any
$E_2$-structure belongs to a centre, double, or representation layer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The CY-to-chiral theorem at $d=3$ constructs
$\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}=\Sp_{\Sigma_2,C}\circ\Phihol_3$ as a framed
$E_1$-chiral specialisation. The twist pair $(g_N,h_M)$ and the lattice
polarisation $L$ choose the specialisation cycle and the Jacobi input.
The denominator comparison is then a separate Borcherds pushforward.
For the primitive CHL orders the automorphic weight is $c_N(0)/2$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:kBKM-universal}. This proves the typed comparison and
keeps any $E_2$-structure at the centre, double, or representation
layer.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Native output and automorphic specialisation]
\label{cor:native-output-automorphic-specialisation}
The Borcherds atlas separates the framed CY$_3$ output from the
automorphic denominator:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The reflection lattice, Weyl vector, and Jacobi input determine
the Borcherds denominator.
\item The datum $(L,g_N,h_M)$ determines the framed specialisation
cycle $\Sigma_2^{(L,g_N,h_M)}$.
\item The value $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$ records the constant term of
the chosen Borcherds input. It is independent of the compact
total-space value $\kch(K3\times E)=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Remaining mathematical questions}
\label{subsec:remaining-questions}

\begin{openproblem}[Full Mathieu twining completion]
\label{prob:mathieu-twining}
The construction above treats $(g_N,h_M)$ as a pair of commuting
symplectic automorphisms of $K3$. Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa (2010)
identify elliptic-genus coefficients with Mathieu $M_{24}$
representations; Gaberdiel--Hohenegger--Volpato (2012) prove the
twined Mathieu moonshine statement. The remaining problem is to
construct, for every admissible conjugacy-class pair $([g],[h])$ of
$M_{24}$, an explicit Jacobi form $F^{(g,h)}$ and a BKM superalgebra
$\gbkmLh$ whose denominator is the Borcherds lift of $F^{(g,h)}$.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Physical DT/BPS interpretation of the atlas]
\label{prob:physical-dt-bps}
For $N=1$, the reduced DT product is
$Z^{K3\times E}=-4096/\Delaf^2$ in theta normalisation. The physical
problem is to match each GBKM $\gbkmLh$ of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:twisted-atlas} to a partition function of the
CHL-type orbifold $(S\times E)/\ZZ_N$, with a dictionary between the
algebraic BPS invariants $\mathrm{DT}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}$, the
automorphic coefficients $m(a),\tau(a)$, and the Lie-superalgebra
multiplicities
$\mult_\alpha=\dim\gbkm_{\alpha,0}-\dim\gbkm_{\alpha,1}$.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Conjecture~\ref{conj:twisted-atlas} for $N = 8$ with generic $L$]
\label{prob:N-8-generic-L}
For $N=8$ the generic lattice-polarised input is missing. The problem
is to enumerate the irreducible components of the moduli of
lattice-polarised K3 surfaces with an order-$8$ symplectic automorphism
and determine the Jacobi-form index on each component.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Root-of-unity specialisations and Oberdieck--Pandharipande]
\label{prob:root-unity-OP}
Root-of-unity specialisations belong to the Mukai self-mirror Yangian
branch. The denominator algebras above belong to the Hall--Drinfeld
double and Borcherds branch. The problem is to construct a comparison,
if one exists, through a specified centre or double layer and then
match it to the Oberdieck--Pandharipande GW/DT correspondence at
$q^N=1$. Any identification must pass through that extra layer rather
than the native $\Phi_3$ output.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Automorphic bootstrap}
\label{subsec:most-load-bearing}

The automorphic bootstrap is the mechanism. A Cartan matrix supplies the
real-root skeleton. The weak Jacobi form $\phi_{0,1}$, or its twisted
cousin $F_L^{(g_N,h_M)}$, supplies the imaginary simple-root
multiplicities $\mult_\alpha=f(nm,l)$. The denominator character then
becomes the Siegel modular form because the imaginary roots are chosen
from the Borcherds input. Thus $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$: the
Borcherds weight is the automorphic weight of the chosen denominator,
separate from $\kcat$, compact $\kch$, Heisenberg-specialised
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}$, and $\kfib$.
