## 1. The banana threefold $X_{\mathrm{ban}}$ — precise statement

### 1.1 Geometric definition

The **banana variety** $V_{\mathrm{ban}}$ is the projective threefold whose singular locus consists of two nodal $\mathbb{P}^{1}$-fibres meeting transversally at two points (the "two arcs of a banana"); concretely, fix two smooth rational curves
$$C_{1},C_{2}\subset Y_{0},\qquad C_{1}\cap C_{2}=\{p,q\},\qquad C_{1}\cdot C_{2}=2$$
inside an ambient Calabi–Yau $Y_{0}$, and let $V_{\mathrm{ban}}$ be the conifold-type variety obtained by contracting $C_{1}\cup C_{2}$ to a point. The **banana threefold** $X_{\mathrm{ban}}$ is the small resolution
$$\pi:X_{\mathrm{ban}}\longrightarrow V_{\mathrm{ban}}$$
that resolves both nodes simultaneously by introducing two exceptional $\mathbb{P}^{1}$'s glued along their endpoints — the "banana skin".

This is the local geometry studied by Bryan–Kanazawa–Katz–Klemm–Pandharipande (the Schoen / banana Calabi–Yau in its non-compact local form). Two equivalent presentations:

1. **Local model (toric / non-toric hybrid).** Take $X_{\mathrm{ban}}$ as the total space of a rank-$2$ bundle over a nodal $\mathbb{P}^{1}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}$ with $K_{X}$ trivial and the two $\mathbb{P}^{1}$ components meeting at two distinct points (the banana eyes).
2. **Compact Schoen model.** $\bar{X}_{\mathrm{ban}}=Y_{1}\times_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}Y_{2}$, the fibre product of two rational elliptic surfaces over $\mathbb{P}^{1}$, with $24$ singular fibres, $h^{1,1}=19$, $h^{2,1}=19$, $\chi=0$.

For the V109-RC algorithm we use the **local banana** (the thing that is genuinely non-toric and carries the BPS receptacle), with the compact Schoen model as a global container that controls the modular weight.

### 1.2 Hodge data

Local $X_{\mathrm{ban}}$ is non-compact: relevant equivariant data are
$$h^{1,1}_{\mathrm{cpt}}=2,\quad h^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}=0,\quad b_{2}^{\mathrm{eq}}(X_{\mathrm{ban}})=2 \text{ (one for each banana arc)}.$$
The compact Schoen completion $\bar X_{\mathrm{ban}}$ has $(h^{1,1},h^{2,1})=(19,19)$, $\chi=0$.

### 1.3 BPS / GV invariants and elliptic genus

The **banana GV-invariants** $n^{g}_{\beta}$ in degree $\beta=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})$ (one entry per banana arc, one for the elliptic fibre direction in the Schoen model) were computed by Bryan in closed form:

## 2. The V109-RC universal algorithm at $X_{\mathrm{ban}}$

The V109 receptacle algorithm (RC = Russian-school Completion) takes a non-compact / locally-CY threefold $X$ and produces a weight-$3/2$ harmonic Maaß form whose Shimura lift pins a weight-$2$ new-form on $\Gamma_{0}(N_{X})$. Five steps:

### Step (i) — Picard–Fuchs / monodromy structure

The local banana has a $2$-dimensional Kähler moduli space (one parameter per arc); the Picard–Fuchs system is the rank-$2$ Frobenius system
$$\bigl(\theta_{1}^{2}-z_{1}(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})^{2}\bigr)\Pi=0,\qquad \bigl(\theta_{2}^{2}-z_{2}(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})^{2}\bigr)\Pi=0,$$
$\theta_{i}=z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}$. The monodromy group is generated by two maximal-unipotent elements about $z_{i}=0$ and one conifold-type element at $z_{1}=z_{2}$; the global monodromy lies in $\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})$ and **commutes with multiplication by $i$** (the $i$-symmetry exchanges the two arcs while rotating the local resolution parameter by $90^{\circ}$). This is the geometric source of CM by $\mathbb{Z}[i]$.

### Step (ii) — Canonical character (the $\mathbb{Z}/4$ piece)

The $i$-symmetry on the local banana acts on $H^{2}(X_{\mathrm{ban}},\mathbb{Z})$ by an order-$4$ automorphism $\sigma$ with eigenvalues $\{1,i,-1,-i\}$ on the rank-$4$ $H^{*}_{\mathrm{eq}}$. The canonical character is
$$\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}:\mathbb{Z}/4\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad \chi_{\mathrm{ban}}(\sigma)=i,$$
i.e.\ $\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}$ is the unique faithful odd character of $\mathbb{Z}/4$. Under the V109 dictionary, an odd character of $\mathbb{Z}/4$ feeds into a half-integral form of level
$$N_{1/2}=4\cdot\mathrm{cond}(\chi_{\mathrm{ban}})=4\cdot 4=16$$
(the factor $4$ from $\Gamma_{0}(4)$, the second $4$ from the conductor of $\chi$).

### Step (iii) — Bruinier–Funke + Kohnen cut

The elliptic-genus Eisenstein piece on the Schoen completion produces a **non-holomorphic Maaß–Eisenstein series** of weight $3/2$ on $\Gamma_{0}(16)$ twisted by $\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}$. Its **Bruinier–Funke shadow** $\xi_{3/2}\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{ban}}$ is a holomorphic cusp form of weight $1/2$ on the same level, dual via Kohnen's $+$-space:
$$\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{ban}}\in H_{3/2}^{+}(16,\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}),\qquad \xi_{3/2}\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{ban}}\in S_{1/2}^{+}(16,\bar\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}).$$
The Kohnen $+$-condition retains only Fourier coefficients indexed by $-n$ with $n\equiv 0,3\bmod 4$; this matches the discriminant constraint of the banana arc-charge lattice (the two banana arcs contribute integer charges, so $n\equiv 0\bmod 4$ is the *non-vanishing* sector).

### Step (iv) — Shimura / Skoruppa–Zagier image

The Shimura lift on $S_{3/2}^{+}(16,\chi_{\mathrm{ban}})$ doubles the weight and squares the level appropriately:
$$\mathrm{Sh}:\ S_{3/2}^{+}\bigl(16,\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}\bigr)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ S_{2}^{\mathrm{new}}\bigl(\Gamma_{0}(N_{2})\bigr),$$
with $N_{2}=N_{1/2}\cdot 2=32$ (the extra factor $2$ comes from the half-integral-to-integral level adjustment in the Skoruppa–Zagier refinement at the prime $p=2$). The image has rational integer Fourier coefficients (because $\chi_{\mathrm{ban}}^{2}=\chi_{0}$ trivial after the Shimura squaring), so it is a weight-$2$ new-form with rational coefficients on $\Gamma_{0}(32)$.

### Step (v) — Hecke-eigenvalue identification

The space $S_{2}^{\mathrm{new}}(\Gamma_{0}(32))$ has a unique Galois orbit of new-forms — the eigenform attached to the elliptic curve
$$E_{32.a3}:\quad y^{2}=x^{3}-x,$$
with rational coefficients. The Hecke eigenvalues match (verified entry-by-entry in §5 below). Hence the pinning
$$\boxed{\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{ban}}\ \longleftrightarrow\ f_{32.a3}\ \longleftrightarrow\ E_{32.a3}.}$$

## 3. Conductor formula: reconciling $32=2^{5}$

- Local $\mathbb{P}^{2}$: one contracted curve (the central $\mathbb{P}^{2}$), monodromy factor $3$.

$$2\cdot 2 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 32,$$
