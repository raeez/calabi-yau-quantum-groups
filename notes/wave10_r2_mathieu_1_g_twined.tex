\section*{The \((1,g)\)-twined Mathieu extension}
\label{sec:mathieu-one-g-twined}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Fix \(g\in M_{24}\), let \(N_g=|g|\), and let \(\CM(g)\) be its
centraliser.  The pair \((1,g)\) keeps the base unorbifolded and
inserts the ordinary \(g\)-twining on the K3 fibre.  Three constants
must be named separately:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{constant} & \text{source} & \text{use}\\ \hline
c^{(g)}(0,0) & \text{ordinary Mathieu twining} &
\text{scalar single-twining lift}\\
\cCHL_N(0) & \text{primitive CHL averaged Jacobi form} &
\text{CHL Borcherds denominator}\\
\cdd(0;t,N) & \text{Gritsenko-Clery diagonal-divisor row} &
\text{dd paramodular catalogue}
\end{array}
\]
Only the second row gives the primitive CHL identity
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6\\ \hline
\cCHL_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2\\
\kBKM(\PhiCHL{N}) & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
by \(\kBKM(\PhiCHL{N})=\cCHL_N(0)/2\).

\subsection*{Twined elliptic genus}

\paragraph{Theorem.}
For every ATLAS conjugacy class \([g]\subset M_{24}\), the
Mathieu-twining table assigns a K3 elliptic genus
\[
  \Zgell(K3;\tau,z)
  =
  \sum_{n,\ell} c^{(g)}(n,\ell) q^n y^\ell
  \in J^{w,\nu_g}_{0,1}\!\left(\Gamma_0(N_g)\right).
\]
It has weight \(0\), index \(1\), level \(\Gamma_0(N_g)\), and a
multiplier \(\nu_g\) determined by the \(24\)-point Frame shape of
\(g\).  Its Witten-index specialisation is
\[
  \Zgell(\tau,0)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid V_{24}),
\]
where \(V_{24}=\mathbf{1}\oplus \mathbf{23}\) is the natural
permutation representation on the Golay-code support.

\paragraph{ATLAS frame shapes at levels \(1,2,3,4,6\).}
In the \(24\)-point action of \(M_{24}\), the ten classes with
\(N_g\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) have the following ordinary single-twining
weights \(k_g=c^{(g)}(0,0)/2\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
[g] & \text{Frame shape on }V_{24} & \operatorname{tr}_{24}(g)
& c^{(g)}(0,0) & k_g\\ \hline
1A & 1^{24} & 24 & 20 & 10\\
2A & 1^8 2^8 & 8 & 4 & 2\\
2B & 2^{12} & 0 & -4 & -2\\
3A & 1^6 3^6 & 6 & 2 & 1\\
3B & 3^8 & 0 & -4 & -2\\
4A & 2^4 4^4 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
4B & 1^4 2^2 4^4 & 4 & 4 & 2\\
4C & 4^6 & 0 & -4 & -2\\
6A & 1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2 & 2 & 0 & 0\\
6B & 6^4 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
\]
The positive scalar single-twining lifts occur at \(1A,2A,3A,4B\).
The boundary cases are \(4A,6A,6B\); the negative scalar weights occur
at \(2B,3B,4C\).  These \(k_g\) values are ordinary-twining constants.
They are separate from the primitive CHL row displayed above.

\subsection*{Additive lift and primitive square root}

\paragraph{Scalar lift.}
For a fixed multiplier-compatible normalisation of
\(\Zgell(K3;\tau,z)\), Gritsenko's additive lift sends the weak Jacobi
form to a Siegel paramodular form
\[
  \operatorname{Lift}_{N_g}\colon
  J^{w,\nu_g}_{0,1}\!\left(\Gamma_0(N_g)\right)
  \longrightarrow
  M^{\mathrm{Siegel}}_{k_g}\!\left(\Gamma^+_{N_g},\chi_g\right),
  \qquad
  k_g=c^{(g)}(0,0)/2.
\]
At \(g=1A\), the ordinary K3 genus is \(2\phiEZ\), so the scalar lift is
the Igusa square
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}})=10.
\]
The primitive Borcherds denominator uses \(\phiEZ\):
\[
  \phiEZ(\tau,z)=y^{-1}+10+y+O(q),
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=10/2=5.
\]

\subsection*{Rank-three BKM denominator}

\paragraph{Theorem at \(1A\).}
The class \(1A\) gives the Gritsenko-Nikulin denominator \(\Delta_5\)
and the rank-three BKM superalgebra \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\).  If
\(\phiEZ=\sum f(n,\ell)q^n y^\ell\), the denominator product is
\[
  \Delta_5(\Omega)=
  e^{2\pi i(\rho,\Omega)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}
  \left(1-e^{2\pi i(\alpha,\Omega)}\right)^{\operatorname{mult}(\alpha)},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{mult}(\alpha)=f(-\alpha^2/2).
\]

\paragraph{Positive scalar candidates.}
The positive ordinary single-twining classes \(2A,3A,4B\) supply
automorphic scalar candidates.  A BKM theorem at these classes requires
the Cartan Gram matrix, Weyl vector, multiplier character, square-root
line, and root-multiplicity convention in the same normalisation as the
Fourier coefficients.  The classes \(2B,3B,4A,4C,6A,6B\) fall outside
the holomorphic positive-weight scalar denominator locus in this
ordinary single-twining normalisation.

\subsection*{\(K3\times E\) invariant split}

\paragraph{Named constructions.}
On \(Y=K3\times E\), the invariants are construction-distinct:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{invariant} & \text{value} & \text{construction}\\ \hline
\kcat(Y) & 0 & \chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0\\
\kch(Y) & 0 & \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)\\
\kchHeis(Y) & 3 & \text{Heisenberg-Mukai chiral specialisation}\\
\kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}) & 5 & \text{Borcherds weight of }\Delta_5\\
\kfiber(K3) & 24 & \text{Mukai-lattice rank of the K3 fibre}
\end{array}
\]
The Borcherds entry belongs to the Hall-Drinfeld/Borcherds branch.  The
K3 self-mirror Yangian branch uses a separate Mukai-lattice input.

\subsection*{Gritsenko-Clery diagonal-divisor constants}

\paragraph{dd catalogue.}
The diagonal-divisor catalogue is indexed by \((t,N;k)\), with its own
cover, character, and Borcherds product:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(t,N;k) & \operatorname{wt}(F_{t,N}) & \cdd(0;t,N)\\ \hline
(1,1;5) & 5 & 10\\
(2,1;2) & 2 & 4\\
(1,2;3) & 3 & 6\\
(3,1;1) & 1 & 2\\
(1,3;2) & 2 & 4\\
(4,1;\tfrac12) & \tfrac12 & 1\\
(1,4;\tfrac32) & \tfrac32 & 3\\
(2,2;1) & 1 & 2
\end{array}
\]
The shared row with the CHL table is \((t,N;k)=(1,1;5)\), where
\(F_{1,1}=\Delta_5\).  The remaining rows are diagonal-divisor
paramodular forms with row-specific constants.

\subsection*{Reduced Donaldson-Thomas side}

\paragraph{Conjectural twined identity.}
For a positive ordinary single-twining class \(g\), the expected reduced
Donaldson-Thomas partition function of \(K3\times E\) has the form
\[
  Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red,DT},g}(q,y,p)
  =
  -\,\frac{C_g}{\Phig^2},
  \qquad
  C_g\in
  \mathbb Q[[q,y^{\pm1},p^{\pm1}]]\cap
  M^{\mathrm{Fr}}_0(\Gamma_0(N_g)).
\]
At \(1A\), \(C_{1A}=1\) gives the Oberdieck-Pixton identity
\[
  Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red,DT}}=-1/\Phi_{10}^2.
\]
For \(N_g=2,3\), the first available Fourier coefficients match the
CHL quotient computations of Oberdieck-Pandharipande.  The full twined
DT identity remains part of the theorem data for \(2A,3A,4B\).

\subsection*{Classes beyond the \(1,2,3,4,6\) genus}

\paragraph{Ordinary twining outside the rank-three genus.}
The classes
\[
  5A,8A,10A,12A,12B,14A,14B,15A,15B,21A,21B
\]
have ordinary Mathieu twining genera and lie outside the
\(N_g\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) rank-three Gritsenko-Nikulin genus used above.
A denominator theorem for these rows requires a paramodular group,
multiplier, Borcherds product, and Cartan datum compatible with the
twined coefficients.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
[g] & \text{Frame shape on }V_{24} & \operatorname{tr}_{24}(g)\\ \hline
5A & 1^4 5^4 & 4\\
8A & 1^2 2\,4\,8^2 & 2\\
10A & 2^2 10^2 & 0\\
12A & 2\,4\,6\,12 & 0\\
12B & 12^2 & 0\\
14A,14B & 1\,2\,7\,14 & 1\\
15A,15B & 1\,3\,5\,15 & 1\\
21A,21B & 3\,21 & 0
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Anomaly-sensitive classes.}
For
\[
  g\in\{7A,7B,11A,23A,23B\},
\]
the ordinary GHV twining genus exists.  The Persson-Volpato and
Gaberdiel-Persson-Volpato anomaly lies in the twisted-twined
projective sector: the restriction of the \(K3\) anomaly class to
\(\langle g\rangle\) gives the cyclic multiplier defects
\[
  7A,7B:\ 3/7,\qquad
  11A:\ 2/11,\qquad
  23A,23B:\ 1/23.
\]
Thus a rank-three Borcherds denominator for any of these rows requires
a cover and multiplier datum that absorb the projective sector before
the Cartan and Weyl-vector data can be stated.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
[g] & \text{Frame shape on }V_{24} & \operatorname{tr}_{24}(g)\\ \hline
7A,7B & 1^3 7^3 & 3\\
11A & 1^2 11^2 & 2\\
23A,23B & 1\,23 & 1
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Result.}
The \((1,g)\)-twined extension is a class-by-class automorphic problem.
The proved denominator is \(1A\).  The positive scalar rows \(2A,3A,4B\)
carry automorphic candidates and still require square-root, Cartan, and
DT comparison data.  Boundary, negative-weight, out-of-genus, and
anomaly-sensitive rows carry the explicit obstructions displayed above.
The primitive CHL identity remains
\(\kBKM(\PhiCHL{N})=\cCHL_N(0)/2\) for
\(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).
