\section{Seven $\rCY$ witnesses on $K3\times E$}
\label{wn:sec:seven-faces-enumerate}

On the constructed framed \(d=3\) branch, set
\[
  X=S\times E,\qquad S=K3,\qquad
  \cF_X=\PhiFA_3(\mathrm{Perf}(X))\in E_3\text{-}\HolFA(X).
\]
The dimension-three specialisation
\[
  \Sp_{\Sigma_2,C}(\cF_X)\in E_1\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(C)
\]
is native \(E_1\).  The \(E_2\)-structure associated with a
dimension-three output belongs to the Drinfeld centre, the Hall--Drinfeld
double, or a representation category of this \(E_1\)-object.
Write \(\cA_X\) for the associated compact \(E_1\)-chiral output when
its compact scalar is evaluated by the Hodge supertrace.

A face of \(\rCY\) is a typed witness: an intrinsic CY datum, a
Stage-\(1\) invariant of \(\cF_X\), a boundary automorphic datum, or a
Stage-\(2\) specialisation.  The table at the end records these types.

\subsection{Constants fixed on $K3\times E$}
\label{wn:subsec:constants-fixed-k3e}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical \(K3\times E\) invariant readings]
\label{wn:prop:k3e-four-constants}
For \(X=K3\times E\),
\[
  \kcat(X)=0,\qquad
  \kch(\cA_X)=0,\qquad
  \kHeis(X)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})=5,\qquad
  \kfib(X)=24.
\]
The primitive CHL ladder and the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor
catalogue use separate constant lists:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)&5&4&3&2&1
\end{array}
\]
for the primitive CHL branch, while the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery catalogue has
twice-weight tuple
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
At \(N=1\) the denominator normalisations are
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Kunneth multiplicativity gives
\[
  \kcat(X)=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot 0=0.
\]
The compact Hodge column is
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(K3\times E)=(1,1,1,1),
\]
so
\[
  \kch(\cA_X)=\Xi(X)=1-1+1-1=0.
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation is additive on the
chiral-Heisenberg shadow and gives \(2+1=3\).  The K3 fibre contributes
\[
  \widetilde H(S,\ZZ)=H^0(S,\ZZ)\oplus H^2(S,\ZZ)\oplus H^4(S,\ZZ),
  \qquad \mathrm{rk}\,\widetilde H(S,\ZZ)=24.
\]
For the BKM face, the K3 weak Jacobi input has constant coefficient
\(c_1(0)=10\); Borcherds' weight formula gives
\(\wt(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=5\).  The CHL constants are the primitive
Gritsenko--Nikulin row.  The eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor
forms are indexed by their own paramodular triples and carry the
displayed twice-weight tuple.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_1=r_{\Muk}$: Mukai lattice}
\label{wn:subsec:r1-Mukai}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\Muk}$]
\label{wn:prop:r1-Mukai-witness}
For the specialisation datum \((\Sigma_2,C)=(S,E)\), the Mukai face is
the bilinear form on \(K_0(D^b(S))\otimes\QQ\) induced by
\[
  \widetilde H(S,\ZZ)=H^0(S,\ZZ)\oplus H^2(S,\ZZ)\oplus H^4(S,\ZZ),
\]
with pairing
\[
  \bigl((r,\xi,s),(r',\xi',s')\bigr)_{\Muk}
  =\xi\cdot \xi'-rs'-r's.
\]
This lattice has signature \((4,20)\) and rank \(24\).  The abelian
sector of \(\Sp_{S,E}(\cF_X)\) is
\[
  \Sp_{S,E}(\cF_X^{\mathrm{ab}})
  \simeq \Uchir(\heis_{\widetilde H(S,\ZZ)}),
\]
and its currents satisfy
\[
  J_v(z)J_w(0)\sim \frac{(v,w)_{\Muk}}{z^2},
  \qquad v,w\in \widetilde H(S,\ZZ).
\]
The scalar attached to this chiral Heisenberg specialisation is
\(\kHeis(X)=3\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Mukai's Riemann--Roch formula gives
\(\chi(F,G)=-(v(F),v(G))_{\Muk}\) for Mukai vectors
\(v(F),v(G)\).  The relative Dolbeault pushforward along \(S\) projects
the abelian BV zero modes to \(H^\bullet(S,\CC)\otimes\Omega_E^\bullet\);
the CY trace on \(S\) turns the Poincare form into the Mukai form.  Free
chiral quantisation of a lattice of zero modes gives the Heisenberg OPE
above.  The Vol III normalisation of the Heisenberg--Mukai scalar is the
dimension-three additive value \(2+1=3\), while the lattice rank remains
\(24\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_2=r_{\mathrm{BKM}}$: $\Delta_5$ denominator}
\label{wn:subsec:r2-BKM}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\mathrm{BKM}}$]
\label{wn:prop:r2-BKM-witness}
For \((\Sigma_2,C)=(S,E_\tau)\), the Borcherds denominator face is the
BKM algebra \(\fg_{\Delta_5}\) with monic denominator
\[
  D_5(Z)
  =
  q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
  \prod_{(n,l,m)>0}(1-q^nr^ls^m)^{f(nm,l)}
  =64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z),
\]
where
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n,l} f(n,l)q^ny^l,\qquad
  [q^0y^0]\phi_{0,1}=10.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \Delta_5=\operatorname{Borch}_{\theta}(\phi_{0,1})
  =\mathrm{Grit}(\eta^9\vartheta_1),
\]
with the first expression fixing the Borcherds product and the second
fixing the additive Gritsenko normalisation.  The face reads
\[
  \kBKM(r_{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =\kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})
  =\frac{c_1(0)}2
  =\frac{10}{2}
  =5.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Borcherds' singular-theta weight formula assigns weight
\(c_\phi(0,0)/2\) to the Borcherds product of a weak Jacobi input
\(\phi\).  For the K3 input \(\phi_{0,1}\), the constant term is \(10\).
Gritsenko--Nikulin identify the resulting genus-two product with the
weight-\(5\) form \(\Delta_5\), with monic product
\(D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5\).  The square \(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2\)
has weight \(10\); the primitive BKM weight is the unsquared value \(5\).
The compact Hall source recognition requires a positive half, a negative
half, a Hopf pairing, and a completion.  The denominator face above uses
the constructed Borcherds target.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_3=r_{\mathrm{fiber}}$: K3 fibre rank}
\label{wn:subsec:r3-fiber}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\mathrm{fiber}}$]
\label{wn:prop:r3-fiber-witness}
Choose an elliptic K3 surface \(\pi:S\to\PP^1\) with section.  The
factorisation
\[
  S\times E\longrightarrow\PP^1
\]
has generic fibre \(E_u\times E\), and the transverse K3 fibre datum
contributes
\[
  \kfib(r_{\mathrm{fiber}})
  =\mathrm{rk}\,\widetilde H(S,\ZZ)
  =24.
\]
On the semistable \(I_1^{24}\) locus this is also the Euler mass
\[
  e(S)=\sum_{p\in\PP^1}e(\pi^{-1}(p))=24.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every K3 surface,
\[
  b_0(S)+b_2(S)+b_4(S)=1+22+1=24,
\]
which is the rank of the Mukai lattice.  If \(S\) is elliptic, the
topological Euler number of the total K3 surface is the sum of the Euler
numbers of the singular fibres; in the semistable model this sum is
realised by \(24\) nodal \(I_1\)-fibres.  The invariant is therefore
read before any automorphic weight is introduced.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_4=r_{\mathrm{Hodge}}$: compact Hodge supertrace}
\label{wn:subsec:r4-Hodge}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\mathrm{Hodge}}$]
\label{wn:prop:r4-Hodge-witness}
The compact total-space face is
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(\cA_{K3\times E})=\Xi(K3\times E)=0.
\]
The K3 fibre has
\[
  \kcat(K3)=\chi(\cO_{K3})=2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The holomorphic Euler characteristic is Kunneth-multiplicative:
\[
  \chi(\cO_{K3\times E})=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
For the compact chiral supertrace, Kunneth gives
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(K3\times E)=(1,1,1,1),
\]
hence
\[
  \Xi(K3\times E)=1-1+1-1=0.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_5=r_{\Niem}$: Niemeier boundary labels}
\label{wn:subsec:r5-Niemeier}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\Niem}$]
\label{wn:prop:r5-Niemeier-witness}
The Niemeier face is a lattice-polarised boundary census.  For each of
the \(23\) non-Leech Niemeier root systems \(R_i\), the automorphic datum
is the Borcherds product whose principal part encodes \(R_i\), equivalently
the denominator attached to the Lorentzian lattice
\[
  II_{1,1}\oplus R_i(-1)
\]
in Borcherds' automorphic-product construction.  A K3 witness is a
marking of an ADE root sublattice of \(\mathrm{NS}(S_i)\) into the
Niemeier root system.  The full rank-\(24\) Niemeier lattice remains the
automorphic label; \(H^2(S_i,\ZZ)\) has rank \(22\), and
\(\mathrm{NS}(S_i)\) has rank at most \(20\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Niemeier's classification gives \(24\) positive-definite even unimodular
rank-\(24\) lattices, exactly \(23\) with roots.  Borcherds' theorem on
automorphic products constructs denominator forms from the corresponding
Lorentzian lattice and principal part.  A K3 surface cannot contain a
full rank-\(24\) positive-definite Niemeier lattice in \(H^2(S,\ZZ)\):
the K3 lattice has signature \((3,19)\), and the Neron--Severi lattice
has rank at most \(20\).  The K3 side therefore supplies lattice-polarised
ADE markings, while the \(23\)-fold enumeration belongs to the
automorphic boundary atlas.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_6=r_{\Hum}$: Humbert monodromy}
\label{wn:subsec:r6-Humbert}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\Hum}$]
\label{wn:prop:r6-Humbert-witness}
Let \(H_1,H_4\subset\overline{\mathcal A_2}\) be the Humbert divisors.
The \(\Delta_5\) holonomic \(\mathcal D\)-module
\[
  \cL^{\Delta_5}
  =
  \bigl(\cO_{\overline{\mathcal A_2}\setminus(H_1\cup H_4)}
  \cdot\log\Delta_5\bigr)\otimes_{\cO}\mathcal D_{\overline{\mathcal A_2}}
\]
has regular singularities along \(H_1\cup H_4\).  The monodromy face is
\[
  r_{\Hum}^{(n)}
  =
  \mathrm{Mon}_{H_n}(\cL^{\Delta_5}),
\]
with
\[
  \mathrm{ord}\,\mathrm{Mon}_{H_1}=8,\qquad
  \mathrm{ord}\,\mathrm{Mon}_{H_4}=16.
\]
The \(H_1\)-order is the \(\mathcal B\)-family conductor
\[
  K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=8,\qquad
  \hbar^2 K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=-1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Gritsenko--Nikulin compute the divisor of \(\Delta_5\): it vanishes to
order \(1\) on \(H_1\) and to order \(2\) on \(H_4\).  The logarithmic
connection \(d\Delta_5/\Delta_5\) is therefore regular singular along
these divisors.  Bruinier's Heegner-divisor Chern-class reciprocity,
applied to \(\Psi=\Delta_5\), gives torsion orders \(8\) and \(16\) for
the associated local monodromies.  On the \(H_1\) face, the Mukai lattice
has positive signature \(c_+=4\), and the Koszul conductor is
\(2c_+=8\).  Drinfeld's root-of-unity scaling gives
\(\hbar^2K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}}=-1\) at this order.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Face $r_7=r_{\mathrm{tw}}$: primitive CHL twining}
\label{wn:subsec:r7-twined}

\begin{proposition}[$r_{\mathrm{tw}}$]
\label{wn:prop:r7-twined-witness}
Let \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).  Let \(g_N\) be a symplectic K3 automorphism
of order \(N\), and let \(t_N\) be an order-\(N\) translation on \(E\).
The diagonal quotient
\[
  X_N=(S\times E)/\langle(g_N,t_N)\rangle
\]
is the primitive CHL geometry.  The orbifold specialisation datum is
\[
  (\Sigma_2^{(N)},C^{(N)})
  =
  ([S/\langle g_N\rangle],\,E/\langle t_N\rangle).
\]
For the CHL Borcherds input \(\phi_{0,1}^{(N)}\),
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
  \kBKM(r_{\mathrm{tw}}^{(N)})
  =\frac{c_N(0)}2
  =(5,4,3,2,1),
\]
with entries ordered by \(N=1,2,3,4,6\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The CHL input is the \(g_N\)-twined K3 weak Jacobi form, normalised in
the primitive Gritsenko--Nikulin branch.  Borcherds' weight formula gives
\(\wt(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\), and Gritsenko's CHL construction supplies the
displayed constants.  The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor rows have
their own paramodular indexing; a comparison with them requires a row
map and multiplier-system data.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Type separation}
\label{wn:subsec:consequence-enumerate}

The seven witnesses occupy three tiers.  The Mukai pairing and compact
Hodge face are intrinsic CY-datum measurements.  The Heisenberg current
algebra is the \((K3,E)\) reading of the Mukai pairing.  The K3 fibre
rank is a Stage-\(1\) measurement of the \(K3\)-factor retained by
\(\cF_X\).  The BKM, Niemeier, Humbert, and CHL rows use specialisation
or boundary automorphic data.  A comparison theorem between any two rows
must supply the relevant map: relative factorisation homology for Mukai,
a Borcherds product for \(\Delta_5\), Bruinier reciprocity for Humbert
monodromy, and a CHL row map for twining.

The BKM side of \(K3\times E\) is the Hall--Drinfeld/Borcherds branch.
The K3 self-mirror Yangian is the separate Mukai-current branch.  In the
toric comparison,
\[
  \CoHA(\CC^3)=Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
and the full affine Yangian or \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) appears after
the double, centre, Cartan, negative-half, and evaluation data are
specified.  The compact CY-C object \(G(X)\) is available only on
constructed Hall--Drinfeld double or centre strata; the generic global
construction remains a named proof obligation.
