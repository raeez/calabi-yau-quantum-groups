\section*{NCCR quiver covers and compact CY$_3$ Hall atlases}

An NCCR chart is an affine singularity datum.  Let \(R\) be a normal
Gorenstein affine threefold algebra.  In Van den Bergh's sense a
non-commutative crepant resolution is
\[
  \Lambda=\End_R(M),
  \qquad
  M\in\mathrm{refl}(R),
  \qquad
  \Lambda\in\mathrm{CM}(R),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{gldim}\Lambda_{\mathfrak p}=\dim R_{\mathfrak p}
  \quad(\mathfrak p\in\Spec R).
\]
For an isolated threefold singularity this last condition reads
\(\operatorname{gldim}\Lambda=3\).  The centre is affine throughout the
definition.
For a flopping contraction satisfying Van den Bergh's hypotheses, a
tilting bundle \(\cT\) on the crepant resolution gives
\[
  \Db\Coh(Y)\simeq\Db(\Lambda\text{-}\mathrm{mod}),
  \qquad
  \Lambda=\End_Y(\cT).
\]
The theorem is local over the affine centre.  A smooth affine chart has
the regular algebra \(R\) as its tautological NCCR.  It produces no
finite quiver presentation of a smooth compact Calabi-Yau threefold by
itself; singular charts, toric critical charts, or an explicit tilting
model are additional data.

A quiver-with-potential presentation is extra structure on the NCCR.
In the consistent toric dimer and quiver-polyhedron setting of Bocklandt,
the algebra is a Jacobi algebra
\[
  \Lambda\simeq\Jac(Q,W)
  =
  \bC Q\big/\langle\partial_a W\rangle_{a\in Q_1},
\]
and the completed Ginzburg dg algebra \(\Gamma(Q,W)\) is
bimodule \(3\)-Calabi-Yau.  On the exact hypotheses where the degree-zero
Jacobi algebra is an NCCR, the diagonal duality is the statement
\[
  \RHom_{\Lambda^e}(\Lambda,\Lambda^e)\simeq \Lambda[3].
\]
For a general compact non-toric threefold this Jacobi presentation is a
local input or a formal critical chart supplied in addition to
smoothness.

\subsection*{Critical positive half}

For a Jacobi chart \((Q_\alpha,W_\alpha)\), Kontsevich-Soibelman's
critical CoHA is
\[
  \cH_\alpha
  =
  \bigoplus_{\mathbf d\in\bZ^{Q_{\alpha,0}}_{\geq0}}
  H^{\mathrm{BM}}_*
  \bigl(\Crit(W_{\alpha,\mathbf d}),\phi_{W_{\alpha,\mathbf d}}\bigr),
\]
with multiplication given by the extension correspondence for short
exact sequences of \(\Jac(Q_\alpha,W_\alpha)\)-modules.  This
multiplication is the local \(\Eone\) Hall product at Calabi-Yau
dimension \(3\).

The Yangian name is justified only on charts where the comparison
theorem is available.  Schiffmann-Vasserot identify the smooth toric
germ with
\[
  \CoHA(\bC^3)=\Yplus(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
the positive half of the affine Yangian.  Rapcak-Soibelman-Yang-Zhao
prove the corresponding positive-half statement for toric CY$_3$
quivers whose compact \(4\)-cycle sector is empty.  The full
vertex-algebra object \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) enters after a
Drinfeld double, centre, completion, or evaluation construction.  The
local CoHA itself is the positive half; the full double is a later
construction.

\subsection*{Morita descent data}

Let \(\fU=\{U_\alpha\}\) be a family of affine singular or formal
critical charts with NCCRs \(\Lambda_\alpha\).  On an overlap
\(U_{\alpha\beta}\), two restrictions
\(\Lambda_\alpha|_{\alpha\beta}\) and
\(\Lambda_\beta|_{\alpha\beta}\) may differ as algebras.
The descent datum is a derived Morita kernel
\[
  {}_{\Lambda_\alpha}\cT_{\alpha\beta}{}_{\Lambda_\beta}
  \in
  \Db(\Lambda_\alpha\otimes\Lambda_\beta^{\mathrm{op}})
\]
which induces an equivalence of perfect module categories,
\[
  \cT_{\alpha\beta}\otimes^L_{\Lambda_\beta}(-)
  :
  \Perf(\Lambda_\beta|_{\alpha\beta})
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  \Perf(\Lambda_\alpha|_{\alpha\beta}).
\]
On triple overlaps one needs coherent identifications
\[
  \cT_{\alpha\beta}\otimes^L_{\Lambda_\beta}\cT_{\beta\gamma}
  \simeq
  \cT_{\alpha\gamma},
\]
with the usual higher associativity.  For toric dimer mutations these
kernels and coherences are explicit under the consistency hypotheses.
For compact non-toric CY$_3$ geometry they are part of the atlas data.

Derived Morita equivalence transports the dg category. Preservation of
dimension-vector gradings, stability chambers, and
Kontsevich-Soibelman orientation data is additional structure.  A Hall
atlas therefore includes compatible charge lattices, positive cones,
stability windows, and square roots of determinant lines.  CoHA gluing
requires this extra structure beyond the categorical Morita kernels.

\subsection*{Stack of objects}

From a coherent Morita diagram one forms the descended dg category
\[
  \cC_{\fU}
  :=
  \operatorname*{hocolim}_{\alpha\in N(\fU)}
  \Perf(\Lambda_\alpha),
\]
where \(N(\fU)\) is the nerve of the cover.  The intrinsic moduli object
is the derived stack of objects
\[
  \cM_{\fU}:=\mathrm{Obj}(\cC_{\fU}).
\]
In finite-type charge windows, Toen-Vaquie and Pantev-Toen-Vaquie-
Vezzosi give a derived Artin stack with tangent complex
\[
  T_E\cM_{\fU}\simeq \RHom_{\cC_{\fU}}(E,E)[1].
\]
When \(E\) is represented in a chart, this tangent complex agrees with
\(\Ext^*_{\Lambda_\alpha}(E,E)[1]\) under the Morita equivalence.  A
quotient stack of finite-dimensional representations is a local atlas
for these windows; the global object is the descent stack.

\subsection*{Positive half by \v{C}ech totalisation}

Assume the Morita kernels, charge data, stability windows, and
orientation data above are fixed.  The oriented local CoHAs
\(\cH_\alpha\) assemble as a cosimplicial diagram of complete
charge-filtered \(\Eone\)-algebras.  Its compactly supported positive
half is
\[
  \Yplus_{\fU}(X)
  :=
  \Tot\left(
  \prod_\alpha \cH_\alpha
  \rightrightarrows
  \prod_{\alpha\beta}\cH_{\alpha\beta}
  \rightthreetimes
  \prod_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\cH_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
  \cdots
  \right).
\]
Equivalently, this is \(R\Gamma(X,\underline{\Yplus}_{\fU})\) for the
descent sheaf of oriented local positive halves.  Hall integration,
where defined, sends its compactly supported Euler character to the
Joyce-Song or reduced Donaldson-Thomas character in the chosen chamber.

For compact \(X\), the chiral coefficient
\[
  \kch(A_X)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)
\]
is a global Hodge supertrace for the compact \(\Phi_3\) output.  It is
obtained from the global Hodge column.  The quiver atlas controls the
Hall positive half; the Hodge trace controls the compact chiral
coefficient.

\subsection*{Boundary tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item For \(X=\bC^3/\Gamma\), with
\(\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_3(\bC)\) finite, the skew group algebra
\(\bC[x,y,z]\rtimes\Gamma\) is the affine McKay NCCR.  The cover has
one chart, and \(\Yplus_{\fU}(X)\) is the McKay-quiver positive half.

\item Local \(\bP^2=\Tot(\cO_{\bP^2}(-3))\) is the crepant resolution of
\(\bC^3/\bZ_3\).  Bridgeland-King-Reid transports the
\(\bZ_3\)-equivariant skew-group model to the Beilinson tilting model
\(\cO\oplus\cO(1)\oplus\cO(2)\) on \(\bP^2\), pulled to the total
space.  The three toric \(\bC^3\)-charts and the orbifold chart compute
the same Jacobi CoHA after the age-graded and wheel-condition
corrections.  The chain-level chiral value is
\[
  \kch(\mathrm{loc}\,\bP^2)=\frac32 .
\]

\item The resolved conifold
\(\Tot(\cO(-1)^{\oplus2}\to\bP^1)\) is a genuine non-compact
CY$_3$, not a local surface.  Its Klebanov-Witten Jacobi algebra gives
the standard two-node NCCR, and the direct McKay calculation gives
\[
  \kch(\mathrm{conifold})=1 .
\]
This value belongs to the conifold quiver.  The local-surface formula
for \(\Tot(\omega_S\to S)\) applies to local surfaces only.

\item For a resolved toric CY$_3$ whose compact \(4\)-cycle sector is
empty, a consistent dimer supplies a single Jacobi model.  The
Rapcak-Soibelman-Yang-Zhao theorem identifies the resulting critical
CoHA with the positive half attached to the toric quiver.

\item For a compact non-toric CY$_3$, the totalisation above is a Hall
atlas construction.  It collapses to one finite quiver algebra only when
a global tilting/Jacobi model, Serre compatibility, and all descent data
are supplied.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E}}

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective K3 surface, let \(E\) be an elliptic
curve, and set \(X=S\times E\).  The Serre functor on \(\Perf(X)\) is
\([3]\).  Hence every nonzero \(T\in\Perf(X)\) satisfies
\[
  \Ext_X^3(T,T)
  =
  \Hom_X(T,T[3])
  \cong
  \Hom_X(T,T)^\vee
  \neq 0 .
\]
A classical tilting object would require
\(\Ext_X^i(T,T)=0\) for all \(i>0\).  Thus Serre duality excludes a
single ordinary finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension
as a classical-tilting model for \(\Perf(K3\times E)\).  A one-quiver
model for \(K3\times E\), if present, must therefore be dg, curved, or
Hall-theoretic rather than an ordinary global NCCR presentation of the
compact derived category.

Local ADE formal charts still exist on singular K3 degenerations.  If
\(\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\bC)\) is finite ADE and acts trivially on
the elliptic coordinate \(t\), then
\[
  R_{\Gamma,E}=\bC[[u,v,t]]^\Gamma,
  \qquad
  \Lambda_{\Gamma,E}
  =
  \End_{R_{\Gamma,E}}
  \left(\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat{\Gamma}}M_\rho\right)
  \cong
  \bC[[u,v,t]]\rtimes\Gamma
\]
is a local \(3\)-dimensional McKay NCCR.  These are formal singular
charts; the compact product \(S\times E\) requires a separate finite
atlas.  On overlaps, Seidel-Thomas spherical twists are derived
autoequivalences rather than algebra isomorphisms; their Fourier-Mukai
kernels carry higher Exts.  The transition theory is dg or
\(A_\infty\), with Serre compatibility built into the gluing.

The finite replacement for \(K3\times E\) is therefore a
Serre-equivariant quasi-NCCR/Hall atlas: local ADE and critical charts,
orientation data, reduced K3 obstruction theory, factorisation
pushforward, and completed Hall multiplication.  The compact
Hall-Drinfeld double and the BKM recognition require additional Hopf
pairing, coproduct, completion, and denominator data.

The separated invariants at this boundary are
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  \kcat(K3\times E)
  &=&
  \chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot0=0,\\[2pt]
  \kch(A_{K3\times E})
  &=&
  \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)=1-1+1-1=0,\\[2pt]
  \kHeis(K3\times E)
  &=&
  3,\\[2pt]
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})
  &=&
  c_1(0)/2=10/2=5,\\[2pt]
  \kfib(K3\times E)
  &=&
  \mathrm{rk}\,\widetilde H(K3,\bZ)=24 .
\end{array}
\]
The compact Hodge supertrace is the value of the compact chiral
coefficient.  The Heisenberg specialisation, Borcherds weight, and
Mukai fibre rank come from separate constructions.

The primitive CHL denominator rows use the Borcherds constant term:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2&5&4&3&2&1 .
\end{array}
\]
Rows at \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\), when present, belong to the
Nikulin/Gritsenko--Clery extension or to separate Gritsenko--Clery
triples rather than the primitive CHL ladder.  These denominator weights
are independent of additive expressions in \(\kch\), \(\kcat\), or
\(\kfib\).

\subsection*{Boundary theorem}

A local NCCR quiver cover of a CY$_3$ supplies oriented critical
\(\Eone\) positive halves on affine singular or formal critical charts.
Their descent is a completed \v{C}ech totalisation of CoHAs, conditional on
Morita coherences, charge compatibility, stability windows, and
orientation data.  The construction recovers the known positive halves
for \(\bC^3/\Gamma\), smooth toric CY$_3$ quivers in the established
range, and local ADE charts stretched along an elliptic direction.

For \(K3\times E\), Serre duality excludes an ordinary finite-dimensional
algebra model of finite global dimension.  The compact object is a
quasi-NCCR/Hall atlas with reduced
Donaldson-Thomas and Hall-Drinfeld data.  Since \(d=3\), every native
\(\Phi_3\) or Hall positive-half output in this whitepaper is \(\Eone\).
\(\Etwo\)-structures belong to centre, double, module, or
representation layers after the positive half has been constructed.
A global compact algebra presentation requires more than a quiver chart,
a scalar character, or a Borcherds denominator weight: the Serre
obstruction, coherent descent, Hopf pairing, and automorphic recognition
data are separate gates.
