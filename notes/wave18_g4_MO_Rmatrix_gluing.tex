\section*{Maulik-Okounkov \(R\)-matrices and positive-half gluing}

\subsection*{Stable-envelope layer}

Let \(\mathfrak M\) be a smooth Nakajima quiver variety, or the ADHM
Hilbert-scheme symplectic resolution used by the \(\bC^3\) affine-Yangian
module. Let \(T=\mathsf A\times\bC_{\hbar}^{\times}\) act with
\(\mathsf A\) preserving the symplectic form and \(\bC_{\hbar}^{\times}\)
scaling it with character \(\hbar\). Write
\[
  \mathfrak M^{\mathsf A}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}
\]
for the fixed locus. After choosing a polarization and slope, each
chamber \(C\subset\fa_{\bR}\) determines the Maulik-Okounkov stable
envelope
\[
  \Stab_C\colon
  H_T^\bullet(\mathfrak M^{\mathsf A})_{\mathrm{loc}}
  \longrightarrow
  H_T^\bullet(\mathfrak M)_{\mathrm{loc}},
\]
characterised by support, diagonal normalisation, and degree bound.
Localization makes \(\Stab_C\) invertible. The chamber-change operator is
\[
  R^{\mathrm{MO}}_{C_2\leftarrow C_1}(u)
  =
  \bigl(\Stab_{C_2}\bigr)^{-1}\circ\Stab_{C_1}
  \in
  \End\bigl(H_T^\bullet(\mathfrak M^{\mathsf A})_{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)
  \otimes\bQ(u).
\]
Under the tensor-product hypotheses of Maulik and Okounkov, these operators
satisfy the Yang-Baxter equation. With the standard scalar
normalisation,
\[
  R^{\mathrm{MO}}_{C_1\leftarrow C_2}(-u)\,
  R^{\mathrm{MO}}_{C_2\leftarrow C_1}(u)=\mathrm{id}.
\]
This is a statement about localized fixed-locus modules and their
Yangian evaluation representations.

\subsection*{Positive-half layer}

Let \(U,V\) be quiver charts with Ginzburg algebras
\[
  A_U=\Jac(Q_U,W_U),
  \qquad
  A_V=\Jac(Q_V,W_V).
\]
Assume a derived wall equivalence, with orientation data and
localization fixed, induces an \(E_1\)-algebra equivalence of positive
halves
\[
  \phi^+_{UV}(u)\colon
  Y^+(A_U)_{\mathrm{loc}}
  \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
  Y^+(A_V)_{\mathrm{loc}}.
\]
The parameter \(u\) is the additive spectral difference after choosing
equivariant characters. On triple overlaps the maps form a
\v{C}ech \(E_1\)-cocycle, with the spectral parameters composed by the
same character law.

The positive half is the native \(E_1\) object. For \(d\geq3\), the
output of \(\Phi_d\) is \(E_1\). Braiding enters after passing to a
centre, double, or module layer, for example
\[
  \mathcal Z\bigl(\Rep^{E_1}(Y^+(A_U))\bigr)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \Rep_{\mathrm{br}}\bigl(D_{\hbar}(Y^+(A_U))\bigr),
\]
where the Drinfeld double requires a non-degenerate Hopf pairing and a
completion. The Maulik-Okounkov \(R\)-matrix belongs to this completed
module layer.

\subsection*{Comparison operator}

Assume the charts \(U,V\) are Maulik-Okounkov accessible: they carry
symplectic-resolution modules \(\mathfrak M_U,\mathfrak M_V\), stable
envelopes for chambers \(C_U,C_V\), and evaluated modules
\[
  \rho_U\colon Y^+(A_U)_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\End(E_U),
  \qquad
  \rho_V\colon Y^+(A_V)_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\End(E_V).
\]
Assume further that \(\phi^+_{UV}(u)\) lifts to a rational module
transport
\[
  \tau_{UV}(u)\colon E_U\longrightarrow E_V
\]
intertwining the two positive-half actions:
\[
  \rho_V\bigl(\phi^+_{UV}(u)(a)\bigr)\,\tau_{UV}(u)
  =
  \tau_{UV}(u)\,\rho_U(a),
  \qquad
  a\in Y^+(A_U)_{\mathrm{loc}}.
\]
The induced fixed-locus comparison is
\[
  \mathcal R_{UV,C_V\leftarrow C_U}(u)
  =
  \bigl(\Stab^V_{C_V}\bigr)^{-1}
  \circ \tau_{UV}(u)\circ
  \Stab^U_{C_U}.
\]
For \(U=V\) and \(\tau_{UU}(u)=\mathrm{id}\), this is exactly
\[
  \mathcal R_{UU,C_2\leftarrow C_1}(u)
  =
  R^{\mathrm{MO}}_{C_2\leftarrow C_1}(u).
\]
For \(U\neq V\), \(\mathcal R_{UV,C_V\leftarrow C_U}\) is a transported
module operator. If \(\tau_{UV}\) has a simple pole at a wall
\(u=u_{\alpha}\), its singular coefficient is
\[
  \Res_{u=u_\alpha}\mathcal R_{UV,C_V\leftarrow C_U}(u)\,du
  =
  \bigl(\Stab^V_{C_V}\bigr)^{-1}
  \circ
  \Bigl(\Res_{u=u_\alpha}\tau_{UV}(u)\,du\Bigr)
  \circ
  \Stab^U_{C_U}.
\]
Thus wall residues are taken after passage to the evaluated
stable-envelope module. The scoped equality is an equality of transported
module operators.

On a triple overlap \(U,V,W\), the \(\v{C}\)ech cocycle for the
\(\phi^+\)'s gives the hexagon relation on transported modules once the
three comparison operators above are defined and the scalar
normalisations are chosen compatibly. In the one-chart case this is the
Maulik-Okounkov Yang-Baxter equation.

\subsection*{The \(\bC^3\) model}

For the one-vertex three-loop quiver with potential \(W=x[y,z]\), the
critical CoHA of \(\bC^3\) is the positive half
\[
  \CoHA(\bC^3)\cong Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1).
\]
This is the Schiffmann-Vasserot positive-half theorem. The full affine
Yangian and the \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) vertex algebra appear
after the double, centre, completion, and Fock evaluation steps.

Let \(h_1+h_2+h_3=0\) and set
\[
  g(z)=\prod_{a=1}^3\frac{z-h_a}{z+h_a},
  \qquad
  \sigma_2=h_1h_2+h_1h_3+h_2h_3,
  \qquad
  \sigma_3=h_1h_2h_3 .
\]
Then
\[
  g(z)g(-z)=1,\qquad
  g(z)=1-\frac{2\sigma_3}{z^3}
        +\frac{2\sigma_2\sigma_3}{z^5}
        +O(z^{-6}).
\]
The sign of the leading term follows directly from
\[
  \log g(z)
  =
  -2\sum_{m\geq0}\frac{h_1^{2m+1}+h_2^{2m+1}+h_3^{2m+1}}
  {(2m+1)z^{2m+1}} .
\]

On
\[
  \mathcal F=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}H_T^\bullet(\Hilb^n(\bC^2))_{\mathrm{loc}},
\]
let \(|\lambda\rangle\) be the partition basis and write
\[
  c(s)=h_1 i+h_2 j,\qquad s=(i,j)\in\lambda,
\]
with \(i,j\) zero-indexed. The diagonal stable-envelope prefactor is
\[
  R^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\lambda,\varnothing}(u)
  =
  \prod_{s\in\lambda}g\bigl(u+c(s)\bigr).
\]
For two Fock factors the diagonal scalar is
\[
  R^{\mathrm{diag}}_{\lambda,\mu}(u)
  =
  \prod_{s\in\lambda}\prod_{t\in\mu}
  g\bigl(u+c(s)-c(t)\bigr).
\]
At charge \(2\), the row and column diagonal entries are
\[
  R^{\mathrm{diag}}_{(2),\varnothing}(u)
  =
  g(u)g(u+h_2),
  \qquad
  R^{\mathrm{diag}}_{(1,1),\varnothing}(u)
  =
  g(u)g(u+h_1).
\]
These formulas give the diagonal part of the evaluated \(R\)-operator.
The full Maulik-Okounkov \(R\)-matrix in higher charge also contains
the off-diagonal stable-envelope matrix entries.

\subsection*{The conifold wall factor}

For the framed resolved-conifold quiver in a toric chamber, let the
charge lattice have primitive wall class \(\gamma\). In the completed
Hall torus, with orientation chosen so the wall factor is
\((1-e_{\gamma})^{-m}\), one has
\[
  K_{\gamma}(e_{\beta})
  =
  e_{\beta}(1-e_{\gamma})^{-m}
  =
  \sum_{k\geq0}
  \binom{m+k-1}{k}\,
  e_{\beta+k\gamma},
  \qquad
  m=\Omega(\gamma)\langle\gamma,\beta\rangle .
\]
This is a positive-half wall automorphism. It becomes an instanton
\(R\)-operator after an evaluated module, stable-envelope transport,
and an equivariant spectral character have been fixed.

\subsection*{Scope}

The comparison needs all four inputs: a torus-equivariant symplectic
resolution, localized stable envelopes, a positive-half wall
equivalence, and evaluated modules on which both sides act. Toric
\(\bC^3\), ADHM Hilbert schemes, and framed toric conifold chambers meet
these hypotheses. Generic compact \(K3\), \(K3\times E\), and non-toric
compact Calabi-Yau threefolds require different replacements for the
torus-equivariant stable-envelope input.

BKM denominator recognition is a separate construction. The Igusa
constant \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Delta_5)=5\) belongs to the Borcherds
product for \(K3\times E\). Maulik-Okounkov residues, positive-half
\(Y^+\) constants, and Mukai-Yangian structures carry different data.
