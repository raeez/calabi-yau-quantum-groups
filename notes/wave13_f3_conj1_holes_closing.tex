\section{Conjecture~1$'$ arithmetic and compact obstructions}
\label{sec:conj1-prime-arithmetic}

Conjecture~1$'$ separates into five mathematical questions: the
one-dimensionality of the weight-five theta-multiplier line, the scalar
normalisation of the primitive Igusa denominator, the passage from reduced
K3 invariants to reduced \(K3\times E\) invariants, the conversion from
additive paramodular automorphy to a Borcherds--Kac--Moody denominator, and
the Mathieu anomaly class.  The automorphic statements are classical after
the monic product is fixed.  The compact Hall, chiral, and holomorphic
Chern--Simons identifications require the hypotheses stated below.

\subsection*{The weight-five theta-multiplier line}
\label{subsec:conj1-weight-five-line}

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Let \(\nu_{\Delta_5}\) be the theta multiplier carried by the product of
the ten even genus-two theta constants.  Then
\[
        S_5\bigl(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5}\bigr)=\CC\,\Delta_5 .
\]
Equivalently, the weight-five cusp line with this multiplier is generated
by the primitive Igusa square root, and
\[
        \Delta_5^2=\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\chi_{10}
        \in S_{10}\bigl(\Sp_4(\ZZ)\bigr).
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Igusa's structure theorem for scalar genus-two modular forms with theta
multiplier gives the \(\nu_{\Delta_5}\)-isotypic component in the form
\[
        M_\bullet(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})
        =
        \Delta_5\,
        \CC[E_4,E_6,\chi_{10},\chi_{12}]
\]
in the weights occurring in the theta-constant character.  The degree-zero
part of the even ring is \(\CC\).  Hence the weight-five component is
\(\CC\Delta_5\).  Gritsenko--Nikulin construct the same non-zero form as
the primitive denominator attached to the hyperbolic rank-three chamber,
and Igusa identifies its square with the ordinary weight-ten cusp form
\(\chi_{10}\).  The square-map \(f\mapsto f^2\) by itself gives
set-theoretic injectivity; the dimension assertion uses Igusa's graded-ring
theorem.

\subsection*{The monic Igusa denominator}
\label{subsec:conj1-monic-denominator}

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Let \(D_5\) denote the monic Borcherds product attached to the
half-normalised Jacobi form \(\phi_{0,1}\), and let \(\Delta_5\) denote
the theta-constant normalisation.  At the cusp
\[
        T_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\[2pt]1/2&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
one has
\[
        [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64,\qquad
        D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5 .
\]
Consequently the Oberdieck--Pixton scalar square is
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}
        =D_5^2
        =4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
\]
while the unscaled Igusa square convention is
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2 .
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin product has leading monomial
\[
        q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
        \prod(1-q^nr^\ell s^m)^{c(nm,\ell)}
\]
with coefficient \(1\).  The theta-constant product normalisation of the
same automorphic form has coefficient \(64\) at that monomial.  Therefore
\(D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5\), and squaring gives the displayed OP scalar.
The scalar \(4096\) is a convention conversion; it carries no independent
Hall, chiral, or orientation datum.

\paragraph{Coefficient comparison.}
If four constructions \(R_i\) land in the line
\(\CC D_5\) and are normalised by the coefficient of
\(q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}\), then their comparison constants satisfy
\[
        C_i=1
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\,R_i=1
        \quad\text{in the monic \(D_5\) convention}.
\]
This is the exact scalar-free statement.  A construction whose leading
coefficient has been fixed by an invariant pairing, a CAP vector, a GSO
sign, or a regulator still requires the displayed coefficient
calculation before it can be identified with the monic Borcherds product.

\subsection*{Reduced K3 classes and the \(E\)-quotient}
\label{subsec:conj1-k3-classes}

\paragraph{Theorem on the K3 fibre.}
Pandharipande--Thomas prove the KKV formula for all curve classes on a K3
surface:
\[
        \sum_h n_h^0 q^{h-1}
        =
        \prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^n)^{-24},
        \qquad
        \beta\in H_2(K3,\ZZ),
\]
including imprimitive classes \(\beta=m\beta_0\).  This is a theorem about
the reduced K3 surface theory.

\paragraph{Compact \(K3\times E\) extension.}
The full reduced \(K3\times E\) partition function in imprimitive classes
requires three additional data:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(i)}&
\text{a reduced obstruction theory after quotienting by \(E\)-translation,}\\
\mathrm{(ii)}&
\text{rubber-integral compatibility with divisibility \(m\),}\\
\mathrm{(iii)}&
\text{the OP/DT scalar normalisation } \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2 .
\end{array}
\]
Oberdieck--Pandharipande formulate the corresponding all-class
\(K3\times E\) Igusa statement as Conjecture~A.  Thus the fibre theorem
supplies the K3-fibre input; the compact \(K3\times E\) imprimitive
theorem remains Conjecture~A.

\paragraph{CHL levels.}
For \(N\in\{2,3,4,6\}\) the required compact statement also needs a
CHL-equivariant reduced obstruction theory, a compatible
crepant-resolution or orbifold gluing theorem, and the chosen
twined-twisted Jacobi input.  The constants for the primitive CHL
Borcherds products are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\
\hline
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)&5&4&3&2&1 .
\end{array}
\]
These five constants are separate from the eight-row
Cl\'ery--Gritsenko diagonal-divisor catalogue.

\subsection*{Borcherds denominators}
\label{subsec:conj1-borcherds-denominators}

\paragraph{The \(N=1\) denominator.}
For the half-normalised Jacobi seed \(\phi_{0,1}\), Borcherds'
singular theta lift and the Gritsenko--Nikulin chamber give the monic
denominator
\[
        \operatorname{Borch}(\phi_{0,1})=D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5 .
\]
Its weight is
\[
        \mathrm{wt}(D_5)=\frac{c_1(0)}{2}=\frac{10}{2}=5 .
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is \(Z_{K3}=2\phi_{0,1}\).  With this doubled input,
the ordinary genus-two Siegel form is the square
\[
        \operatorname{Borch}(2\phi_{0,1})
        =
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}
        =
        \Delta_5^2 .
\]
Thus the primitive BKM denominator is \(\Delta_5\) on the multiplier cover,
whereas the full K3 elliptic genus produces the weight-ten square.

\paragraph{Conditional theorem at \(N\geq 2\).}
Let \(\phi_N\) be a weak Jacobi input for a primitive CHL level
\(N\in\{2,3,4,6\}\).  If the following four data are supplied:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(a)}&\text{integral polar Fourier coefficients giving root
supermultiplicities,}\\
\mathrm{(b)}&\text{a Lorentzian lattice and Weyl chamber,}\\
\mathrm{(c)}&\text{a Weyl vector and real-simple-root system,}\\
\mathrm{(d)}&\text{the Borcherds product identity in that chamber,}
\end{array}
\]
then the product is the denominator of a Borcherds--Kac--Moody
superalgebra and its invariant is
\[
        \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
Cl\'ery--Gritsenko additive lifts give paramodular automorphy.  The
denominator theorem requires the four multiplicative data above.

\paragraph{Catalogue separation.}
The eight Cl\'ery--Gritsenko diagonal-divisor products have weights
\[
        \left(5,2,3,1,2,\frac12,\frac32,1\right)
\]
and twice-weights
\[
        (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The primitive CHL table has constants
\[
        (10,8,6,4,2).
\]
The two catalogues meet at the \((1,1)\) row \(\Delta_5=\Phi_1\); all other
comparisons require a row map specifying the level, cover, multiplier,
normalisation, and branch.

\subsection*{The Mathieu anomaly class}
\label{subsec:conj1-mathieu-anomaly}

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Johnson-Freyd--Treumann prove
\[
        H^4(B\Mtf,\ZZ)\cong \ZZ/12 .
\]
For a finite group, the exponential sequence identifies
\[
        H^3(\Mtf,U(1))\cong H^4(B\Mtf,\ZZ)_{\mathrm{tors}},
\]
hence
\[
        H^3(\Mtf,U(1))\cong \ZZ/12 .
\]
The additional \(\ZZ/2\) summand has no source in this theorem.

\paragraph{Restriction problem.}
For a cyclic subgroup \(C_N=\langle g\rangle\), the target group is
\[
        H^3(C_N,U(1))\cong H^4(BC_N,\ZZ)\cong \ZZ/N .
\]
The restriction of the Mathieu class is determined by the
Johnson-Freyd--Treumann fractional Pontryagin generator
\(\frac{p_1}{2}(\mathrm{Perm}_{24}\otimes\mathbb R)\) and the conjugacy
class of \(g\).  Divisibility \(N\mid 12\) alone gives no vanishing
criterion: Johnson-Freyd--Treumann show that restriction to
\(\langle 12B\rangle\) is an isomorphism.  A vanishing assertion for a
chosen class \(g_N\) therefore requires four explicit inputs:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(i)}&\text{the conjugacy class of \(g_N\) in \(M_{24}\),}\\
\mathrm{(ii)}&\text{the restriction of \(\frac{p_1}{2}\) to
\(\langle g_N\rangle\),}\\
\mathrm{(iii)}&\text{the scalar multiple of the generator represented by
the K3 sigma-model anomaly,}\\
\mathrm{(iv)}&\text{the comparison with the GPV transgression convention.}
\end{array}
\]
Without these four inputs the restriction-to-\(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) claim is an
open proof obligation.

\subsection*{Compact hCS and Hall--chiral enhancement}
\label{subsec:conj1-compact-hcs}

\paragraph{Proof obligation.}
Any compact holomorphic Chern--Simons or Hall--chiral identification of the
\(K3\times E\) automorphic denominator with a chiral algebra on \(E\)
requires the following data:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(i)}&\text{a BV quantum master equation for the compact theory,}\\
\mathrm{(ii)}&\text{anomaly cancellation for the \(K3\times E\) orientation
line,}\\
\mathrm{(iii)}&\text{orientation and framing data for the CY\(_3\)
specialisation,}\\
\mathrm{(iv)}&\text{factorisation or TCFT descent from six dimensions to
the curve \(E\),}\\
\mathrm{(v)}&\text{counterterms compatible with the reduced
obstruction theory,}\\
\mathrm{(vi)}&\text{a completed Hall or chiral category carrying the
imaginary-root multiplicities.}
\end{array}
\]
The Borcherds product supplies the character-level automorphic target and
the root supermultiplicities.  The compact chiral algebra follows after
all six analytic and categorical inputs are constructed.

\paragraph{\(\kappa_\bullet\) separation for \(K3\times E\).}
The compact invariants are
\[
        \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
        \kch(K3\times E)=0.
\]
The three additional witnesses come from separate constructions:
\[
        \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
        \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=5,\qquad
        \kfib(K3)=24 .
\]
For the primitive CHL Borcherds products,
\[
        \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}2,\qquad
        c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
        N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
These identities are independent of the compact Hodge supertrace
\(\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(K3\times E)=0\).
