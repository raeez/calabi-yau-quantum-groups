\section*{Eichler--Zagier decomposition of twisted-twined K3 Jacobi inputs}
\label{wn:sec:EZ-twisted-twined}

\paragraph{Jacobi-form input.}
Let $(g_N,h_M)$ be a commuting pair of K3 symplectic automorphisms in
the Mukai-admissible Mathieu slice.  When the Persson--Volpato
discrete-torsion obstruction vanishes, the twisted-twined elliptic
genus is a weak Jacobi form of weight $0$, index $1$, and level
$L=\mathrm{lcm}(N,M)$:
\[
  \Fgh(\tau,z)\in J^{\mathrm w}_{0,1}(\Gamma_0(L),\nu_{g,h}).
\]
Eichler--Zagier 1985 Theorem 9.3, with the level-$L$ extension of Aoki
2000 and Gritsenko 1999, gives the coefficient decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wn-EZ-ansatz}
  \Fgh(\tau,z)
  =
  \agh(\tau)\,\phioo(\tau,z)
  +
  \bgh(\tau)\,\phimt(\tau,z),
  \qquad
  \agh\in M_0(\Gamma_0(L),\nu_{g,h}),\quad
  \bgh\in M_2(\Gamma_0(L),\nu_{g,h}).
\end{equation}
The generators are normalised by
\[
  \phioo=y^{-1}+10+y+O(q),\qquad
  \phimt=y^{-1}-2+y+O(q),\qquad
  \phioo(\tau,0)=12,\quad \phimt(\tau,0)=0.
\]
The coefficients in \eqref{eq:wn-EZ-ansatz} depend on the normalisation
of the Jacobi input.  The K3-normalised elliptic genus and the
Borcherds-normalised input differ by scalar factors in common use; the
scalar must be named before comparing constants.

\paragraph{Commuting pairs.}
Gaberdiel--Hohenegger--Volpato restrict commuting pairs to subgroups
preserving a positive four-plane in the Leech-lattice model.  On the
standard programme slice the admissible Mathieu classes are
\[
  \{1A,2A,2B,3A,3B,4A,4B,4C,6A,6B\}.
\]
For a pair $(g,h)$ the level of the Jacobi form divides
$\mathrm{lcm}(|g|,|h|)$ after the multiplier is included.  The
existence of the Jacobi form is a statement about the pair and its
multiplier. The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery atlas and the CHL ladder require
separate normalisation maps.

\paragraph{Coefficient pinning.}
Write the $q^0$-head of a chosen normalisation of $\Fgh$ as
\[
  \Fgh(\tau,z)\big|_{q^0}
  =
  p_{g,h}(y^{-1}+y)+m_{g,h}.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:wn-EZ-ansatz} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wn-EZ-pinning-equations}
  \agh(\infty)=\frac{m_{g,h}+2p_{g,h}}{12},
  \qquad
  \bgh(\infty)=p_{g,h}-\agh(\infty).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, evaluating at $z=0$ gives
\[
  \Fgh(\tau,0)=12\,\agh(\tau),
\]
and the polar coefficient gives
$p_{g,h}=\agh(\infty)+\bgh(\infty)$.  These two equations are the
normalisation-safe way to compare the Eichler--Zagier coefficients.

\paragraph{The class \(2A\).}
For the K3-normalised single twining at class $2A$, the Fourier head is
\[
  F_L^{(2A,\mathrm{id})}(\tau,z)\big|_{q^0}
  =
  2(y^{-1}+y)+4,
\]
as in the Mathieu twining table.  Hence
\[
  \agh(\infty)=\frac{4+2\cdot2}{12}=\frac23,\qquad
  \bgh(\infty)=2-\frac23=\frac43.
\]
If the Borcherds input is half-normalised, both constants are divided by
$2$:
\[
  \agh(\infty)=\frac13,\qquad
  \bgh(\infty)=\frac23.
\]
A formula for the full function $\bgh(\tau)$ requires matching further
Fourier coefficients against a specified basis of
$M_2(\Gamma_0(2),\nu_{2A})$.  The $q^0$-head alone does not determine an
Eisenstein correction such as a coefficient of
$E_2^{(2)}(\tau)=2E_2(2\tau)-E_2(\tau)$.

\paragraph{Untwined checks.}
For a K3-normalised single twining with polar coefficient
$p_g=2$ and Euler trace $\chi_g=F_L^{(g,\mathrm{id})}(\tau,0)$,
\[
  \agh(\infty)=\frac{\chi_g}{12},
  \qquad
  \bgh(\infty)=2-\frac{\chi_g}{12}.
\]
Thus the classes with
\[
  \chi_{1A}=24,\quad \chi_{2A}=8,\quad \chi_{3A}=6,\quad
  \chi_{4B}=4,\quad \chi_{6A}=2
\]
give
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
g & \agh(\infty) & \bgh(\infty)\\\hline
1A & 2 & 0\\
2A & 2/3 & 4/3\\
3A & 1/2 & 3/2\\
4B & 1/3 & 5/3\\
6A & 1/6 & 11/6
\end{array}
\]
in K3 normalisation.  Dividing the input by $2$ divides both columns by
$2$.

\paragraph{Borcherds weight.}
The universal Borcherds weight statement is
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2.
\]
Here $c_N(0)$ is the constant term of the Jacobi input in the
Borcherds-product normalisation for the chosen scope.  The
Eichler--Zagier decomposition gives a useful splitting of the Jacobi
input, but it does not by itself prove a second formula for the
Borcherds weight.  For a fixed Borcherds product one may define the
defect
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wn-EZ-refined-kBKM}
  \Delta_{\bgh}(\Phi_N)
  :=
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)-12\,\agh(\infty)
  =
  \frac{c_N(0)}2-12\,\agh(\infty).
\end{equation}
Identifying $\Delta_{\bgh}$ with a Hecke-minus contribution from
$\bgh\,\phimt$ is a proof obligation: it requires comparing the
Gritsenko multiplicative lift term-by-term with the decomposition
\eqref{eq:wn-EZ-ansatz}.  No numerical correction table follows from
\eqref{eq:wn-EZ-ansatz} alone.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Chain level: the four coefficients
\[
  c^{(g,h)}(-1,1),\quad c^{(g,h)}(0,0),\quad
  c^{(g,h)}(1,-1),\quad c^{(g,h)}(1,1)
\]
determine the first cusp constraints on $(\agh,\bgh)$, while higher
coefficients determine the weight-$2$ modular form $\bgh(\tau)$.
Categorical level: the pair $(\phioo,\phimt)$ is the two-generator
Jacobi-form basis in which the K3 elliptic-genus local system is read;
the Borcherds lift sees the same data only after the chosen
normalisation and multiplier have been fixed.
