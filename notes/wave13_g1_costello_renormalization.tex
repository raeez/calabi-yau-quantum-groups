\section{Costello Renormalization for Six-Dimensional Holomorphic Chern-Simons Theory}
\label{sec:costello-renormalization-6d-hcs}

Let \(X\) be a complex threefold with holomorphic volume form
\(\Omega_X\), and let
\((\fg,\langle-,-\rangle)\) be a finite-dimensional quadratic complex
Lie algebra.  On an open set \(U\subset X\), the local BV fields are
\[
  \cE_U=\Omega^{0,\bullet}(U,\fg)[1],
  \qquad
  Q=\dbar ,
  \qquad
  [\alpha,\beta]=[\alpha\wedge\beta]_{\fg}.
\]
The classical interaction is
\[
  I_{\mathrm{cl}}(\cA)
  =
  \int_U\Omega_X\wedge
  \left\langle
    \frac12\,\cA\,\dbar\cA+
    \frac16\,\cA[\cA,\cA],
    1
  \right\rangle .
\]
The BV pairing
\[
  \omega(\alpha,\beta)
  =
  \int_U\Omega_X\wedge\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
\]
pairs \(\Omega^{0,p}(U,\fg)\) with
\(\Omega^{0,3-p}_c(U,\fg)\) and has degree \(-1\) after the shift.
The \((0,0)\)-component is the ghost, the \((0,1)\)-component is the
connection field, and the \((0,2)\), \((0,3)\)-components are the BV
antifields under this pairing.  The classical master equation is the
Jacobi identity for the Dolbeault dg Lie algebra together with
invariance of \(\langle-,-\rangle\).

\paragraph{Effective interactions.}
A Costello perturbative quantization fixes a gauge-fixing operator
\(Q^{\mathrm{GF}}\), heat kernels \(K_t\), propagators
\[
  P_{\varepsilon,L}
  =
  \int_{\varepsilon}^{L}
  (Q^{\mathrm{GF}}\otimes1)K_t\,dt,
  \qquad 0<\varepsilon<L,
\]
regularized BV brackets \(\{-,-\}_L\), regularized BV Laplacians
\(\Delta_L\), and a completed complex
\(\Loc(\Fields)\) of local functionals.  The renormalized effective
interaction is obtained by the finite limit
\[
  I[L]
  =
  \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}
  W\bigl(P_{\varepsilon,L},
    I_{\mathrm{cl}}+I^{\mathrm{CT}}(\varepsilon)\bigr),
\]
where \(W(P,-)\) denotes the connected Feynman graph expansion and
\(I^{\mathrm{CT}}(\varepsilon)\in\Loc(\Fields)[[\hbar]]\) is a local
counterterm.  For \(0<L_1<L_2\), the family satisfies Costello's
renormalization group equation
\[
  I[L_2]=W(P_{L_1,L_2},I[L_1]).
\]
The scale \(L\) quantum master equation is
\[
  QI[L]+\frac12\bracket{I[L]}{I[L]}_L+\hbar\Delta_L I[L]=0.
\]
At order \(\hbar^r\), the obstruction to solving this equation lies in
\[
  H^1\bigl(\Loc(\Fields),\, Q+\bracket{I_{\mathrm{cl}}}{-}\bigr).
\]
This is the local BV obstruction complex of Costello,
\emph{Renormalization and Effective Field Theory}, Chapters 2-5, with
the locality axiom in Costello-Gwilliam, \emph{Factorization Algebras in
Quantum Field Theory}, Vol. I, Chapter 3.

\paragraph{Graph expansion and locality.}
For nonabelian \(\fg\), the interaction vertex is cubic.  Hence
\[
  W(P,I_{\mathrm{cl}})
  =
  \sum_{\Gamma\ \mathrm{connected}}
  \frac{\hbar^{b_1(\Gamma)}}{|\Aut(\Gamma)|}\,
  W_\Gamma(P,I_{\mathrm{cl}})
\]
ranges over connected trivalent graphs.  Edges carry the regulated
propagator and vertices carry the cubic Taylor coefficient of
\(I_{\mathrm{cl}}\).  Counterterms live on the small diagonal: they are
polynomials in jets of fields, integrated against \(\Omega_X\), and are
therefore elements of \(\Loc(\Fields)\).  General distributions cannot
serve as Costello counterterms.

\paragraph{Free abelian coefficient.}
If \(\fg\) is abelian, the cubic term vanishes.  The theory is Gaussian:
all connected interaction graphs with at least one cubic vertex have
zero amplitude, and the interacting counterterms vanish:
\[
  I[L]=I_{\mathrm{cl}},
  \qquad
  I^{\mathrm{CT}}_r=0\quad(r\geq1),
\]
after the determinant normalization of the free theory has been fixed.
The QME then reduces to the free BV identity.

\paragraph{Quartic anomaly.}
Let \(\rho:\fg\to\End(V)\) be a representation.  The invariant
polynomial governing the one-loop gauge anomaly in complex dimension
three is
\[
  I_4^\rho(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
  =
  \frac1{4!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}
  \Tr_V\!\left(
    \rho(x_{\sigma(1)})\rho(x_{\sigma(2)})
    \rho(x_{\sigma(3)})\rho(x_{\sigma(4)})
  \right),
  \qquad
  I_4^\rho\in\Sym^4(\fg^\vee)^\fg .
\]
The local descent representative is the four-linear functional
\[
  \Theta_U^\rho(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)
  =
  \int_U
  \left[
  I_4^\rho\bigl(
    \alpha_0,\partial\alpha_1,\partial\alpha_2,\partial\alpha_3
  \bigr)
  \right]_{(3,3)},
  \qquad
  \alpha_i\in\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(U,\fg),
\]
with the usual Chevalley-Eilenberg signs.  Its class
\[
  \kanom(X,\fg,\rho)
  =
  [\,\hbar\,\Theta^\rho\,]
  \in
  H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}
  \bigl(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\fg)[1],\cO_{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)
\]
is the first gauge obstruction to the QME.  In ghost-field notation for
\(\rho=\operatorname{ad}\), the same class is represented by the
Costello-Li descent expression
\[
  \int_X \Tr_{\operatorname{ad}}(c\,F_{\cA}^3).
\]
The relevant one-loop graph is the wheel with four external insertions:
the colour factor is the symmetrized trace of four Lie algebra inputs,
and the analytic factor supplies the three holomorphic derivatives
needed for a \((3,3)\)-density.

\paragraph{Quadratic two-point term.}
Let \(\{T_a\}\) be a basis of \(\fg\), with dual basis \(\{T^a\}\) for
\(\langle-,-\rangle\).  The quadratic Casimir coefficient in the
representation \(\rho\) is
\[
  C_2^\rho=\sum_a\rho(T_a)\rho(T^a).
\]
With the flat heat-kernel normalization used for the local
\(\mathbb C^3\) model, the two-point graph gives the degree-zero
field-strength counterterm
\[
  S_{\mathrm{ct}}^{(2)}
  =
  -\hbar\,\frac{C_2^\rho}{(4\pi)^3}\log(L/\varepsilon)
  \int_X\Omega_X\wedge\Tr_\rho(\cA\,\dbar\cA),
\]
up to the normalization of the invariant pairing and heat kernel.  This
renormalizes the kinetic representative and the field.  It has arity
two and degree zero in the BV local functional complex.  The QME anomaly
has arity four and degree one, and is the class \(\kanom(X,\fg,\rho)\).

\paragraph{Compact Calabi-Yau threefolds.}
A compact nonabelian hCS quantization theorem on a Calabi-Yau threefold
requires the following data:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a compact Calabi-Yau threefold \((X,\Omega_X)\), an orientation,
and a determinant-line framing compatible with the BV pairing;
\item a quadratic gauge dg Lie algebra or a bundle of such algebras,
with a representation \(\rho\) fixing \(I_4^\rho\) and \(C_2^\rho\);
\item a Costello elliptic gauge fixing, heat-kernel regulator, and
renormalization scheme;
\item a completed local functional and observable complex on which
\(\Delta_L\), \(\{-,-\}_L\), RG flow, and the QME are defined;
\item factorization or TCFT gluing data descending the local polydisk
construction to \(X\);
\item a trivialization of \(\kanom(X,\fg,\rho)\) by vanishing in local
BV cohomology, an explicit local counterterm, or a closed-sector
Green-Schwarz term.
\end{enumerate}
Under this package, vanishing of \(\kanom(X,\fg,\rho)\) gives the
one-loop QME correction by Costello's inductive obstruction theory.
Higher-loop quantization requires the corresponding higher obstruction
classes to vanish or to be killed by specified counterterms in the same
completed complex.  The equation \(c_1(X)=0\) supplies the holomorphic
volume form; anomaly trivialization for nonabelian coefficients requires
the BV data listed above.

\paragraph{Separation from categorical and Hall data.}
The hCS field complex
\(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\operatorname{ad}P)[1]\) is a BV field theory.
The Hochschild polyvector complex of a Calabi-Yau category is a separate
object:
\[
  \operatorname{HH}^\bullet(\operatorname{Perf}(X))
  \cong
  \bigoplus_{p,q}H^q(X,\wedge^pT_X).
\]
A finite gauge Lie algebra enters after a framing or Chan-Paton choice.
The numerical class \(\kanom\) is likewise separate from
\[
  \kch,\qquad
  \kcat=\chi(\cO_X),\qquad
  \kBKM=c_N(0)/2,\qquad
  \kfib .
\]
Any comparison between these invariants and hCS renormalization requires
an additional CY-to-chiral or Hall-side comparison theorem.

\paragraph{Boundary level at \(d=3\).}
The six-real-dimensional hCS factorization object is a stage-one
\(\Ethree\)-holomorphic object.  After specialization along a complex
surface \(\Sigma_2\) and restriction to a curve \(C\), the CY-to-chiral
output has native level
\[
  A_C
  =
  \operatorname{SpCh}_{\Sigma_2,C}
  \bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\mathcal C)\bigr)
  \in
  \operatorname{Alg}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Eone}(C).
\]
The braided \(\Etwo\) structure lives on a centre, double, or module
layer, for example on
\[
  \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_C)\bigr).
\]
This is the \(d\geq3\) specialization rule in
\texttt{chapters/theory/cy\_to\_chiral.tex}: the native output is
\(\Eone\), while \(\Etwo\) appears after the representation-centre
construction has been supplied.

\paragraph{Toric \(\mathbb C^3\) normalization.}
On the toric Hall side, Schiffmann-Vasserot identify the critical
cohomological Hall algebra of \(\mathbb C^3\) with the positive half
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)\cong
  \Yp(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1).
\]
The \(\Woneinf\) object is reached after Drinfeld doubling, centre
passage, completion, and evaluation on the Fock or vacuum module.
Borcherds denominator algebras enter through Borcherds products.
Yangian objects enter through Hall or stable-envelope constructions.
The local BV renormalization calculation supplies the QME obstruction
class before either representation-theoretic comparison.

\paragraph{Primary anchors.}
Costello, \emph{Renormalization and Effective Field Theory}, Chapters
2-5, supplies effective interactions, local counterterms, RG flow, and
the QME obstruction complex.  Costello-Gwilliam, \emph{Factorization
Algebras in Quantum Field Theory}, Vol. I, Chapter 3, supplies locality
and factorization gluing.  Costello-Li, \emph{Quantum BCOV theory on
Calabi-Yau manifolds and the higher genus B-model}, arXiv:1201.4501,
and Gwilliam-Williams, arXiv:2009.05037, identify the holomorphic hCS
anomaly slot in complex dimension three.  Schiffmann-Vasserot,
\emph{Publ. Math. IHES} 118 (2013), Theorem 9.1, identifies
\(\CoHA(\mathbb C^3)\) with the positive half
\(\Yp(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)\).
