\paragraph{The automorphic input.}
Let
\[
  X_N=(\mathrm{K3}\times E)/\Zmod{N},\qquad
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
be a primitive CHL quotient with the diagonal order-\(N\)
action fixed as part of the datum.  The Borcherds product attached to
the CHL denominator is \(\Phi_N\), and \(c_N(0)\) denotes the
\(q^0\zeta^0\)-coefficient of the weight-\(0\), index-\(1\) Jacobi input
in the Gritsenko--Nikulin normalisation.

\begin{proposition}[CHL Borcherds constants]
\label{prop:classS-CHL-borcherds-constants}
On the CHL ladder \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\),
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))
  =(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
and the BKM modular characteristic is
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2},\qquad
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_N)\bigr)_{N=(1,2,3,4,6)}
  =(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Borcherds' singular-theta weight formula
\emph{Invent. Math.} 132 (1998), Theorem 13.3, gives
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}
\]
for a Borcherds product whose input has \(q^0\zeta^0\)-coefficient
\(c_N(0)\).  The BKM characteristic used here is the denominator weight:
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N):=\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N).
\]
For \(N=1\), the Eichler--Zagier normalisation of the K3 Jacobi form is
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)\big|_{q^0}=\zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta,
\]
hence \(c_1(0)=10\) and \(\kBKM(\Phi_1)=5\); this is Gritsenko's
\(\Delta_5\).  In the theta-normalisation used for the Igusa square
root, the monic Borcherds product is
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5 .
\]
This normalisation changes the leading scalar and leaves
\(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\) unchanged.  For \(N=2,3,4,6\),
Gritsenko--Nikulin construct the CHL
paramodular denominator forms \(\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}\) with weights
\[
  k(2)=4,\qquad k(3)=3,\qquad k(4)=2,\qquad k(6)=1
\]
(Gritsenko--Nikulin, \emph{Automorphic forms and Lorentzian
Kac--Moody algebras II}, Theorem 2.1).  Applying Borcherds'
weight formula gives
\[
  c_N(0)=2k(N)=(8,6,4,2)
\]
for \(N=(2,3,4,6)\).  Together with the \(N=1\) coefficient this is the
displayed tuple.

These are CHL denominator constants.  The separate Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor catalogue has weights
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1)
\]
in triple order \((t,N)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(3,1),(1,3),(4,1),(1,4),(2,2)\),
so its twice-weight tuple is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The two tables share only the primitive \(\Delta_5\) row unless a
separate row-map theorem supplies the level, cover, Jacobi input, and
denominator chamber.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The four invariants of \(\mathrm{K3}\times E\).}
The categorical Euler invariant of the total space is
\[
  \kcat(\mathrm{K3}\times E)
  =\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{K3}\times E})
  =\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{K3}})\chi(\mathcal{O}_E)
  =2\cdot 0=0 .
\]
The compact chiral Hodge supertrace is also zero:
\[
  \kch(\mathrm{K3}\times E)
  =\sum_{q=0}^3(-1)^q h^{0,q}(\mathrm{K3}\times E)
  =1-1+1-1=0 .
\]
The value \(2=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{K3}})\) belongs to the K3 fibre.
The standard \(N=1\) spectrum
\[
  \{\kcat,\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kBKM,\kfib\}(\mathrm{K3}\times E)
  =\{0,3,5,24\}
\]
records four constructions: the categorical Euler invariant of the
total space, the Heisenberg--Mukai chiral specialisation, the Borcherds
denominator \(\Delta_5\), and the rank \(24\) Mukai lattice.  Since
\(\mathrm{K3}\times E\) is a Calabi--Yau threefold, the native
CY-to-chiral output is \(E_1\)-chiral; \(E_2\)-braiding appears through
the Drinfeld-centre or representation-category construction on loci
where that construction is available.  The Hall--Drinfeld/BKM branch
attached to \(\Delta_5\) is separate from the K3 self-mirror Yangian
branch.  The six routes to \(G(\mathrm{K3}\times E)\) are six distinct
constructions.

\paragraph{The \(A_1\) class-\(\mathcal{S}\) sphere.}
The class-\(\mathcal{S}\) field-theoretic parent with
\(\ctd=-214\) is
\[
  \TclassS{A_1}{\Sigma_{0,24}},
\]
the \(6d\) \((2,0)\) theory of type \(A_1\) compactified on a sphere with
twenty-four maximal regular \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) punctures.  A
pants-decomposition of \(\Sigma_{0,24}\) has \(22\) \(T_2\) trinions
and \(21\) \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) gauge tubes.  Since each \(T_2\) trinion
contributes \((n_v,n_h)=(0,4)\) and each gauge tube contributes
\((n_v,n_h)=(3,0)\), the effective vector and hypermultiplet counts are
\[
  (n_v,n_h)=(63,88).
\]
For a sphere with \(n\) maximal \(A_1\) punctures the same count gives
\[
  n_v(\Sigma_{0,n})=3(n-3),\qquad
  n_h(\Sigma_{0,n})=4(n-2),
\]
and hence
\[
  c_{\mathrm{4d}}(\Sigma_{0,n})
  =\frac{2n_v+n_h}{12}
  =\frac{5n-13}{6}.
\]
Shapere--Tachikawa normalisation then gives
\[
  \cfd=\frac{2n_v+n_h}{12}
       =\frac{2\cdot 63+88}{12}
       =\frac{107}{6},
  \qquad
  \afd=\frac{5n_v+n_h}{24}
       =\frac{5\cdot 63+88}{24}
       =\frac{403}{24}.
\]
The Beem--Lemos--Liendo--Peelaers--Rastelli--van Rees
\(4d/2d\) map gives
\[
  \ctd=-12\cfd=-214 .
\]
This computation uses the class-\(\mathcal{S}\) surface and puncture
types.  The Shapere--Tachikawa anomaly formula contains the effective
\((n_v,n_h)\) count; the Hodge data of \(\mathrm{K3}\times E\) enter the
\(\kappa_\bullet\)-invariants and Borcherds product above.  The closed
genus-\(2\) \(A_1\) curve has two \(T_2\) trinions and three gauge tubes,
so \((n_v,n_h)=(9,8)\), \(\cfd=13/6\), and \(\ctd=-26\).

\paragraph{What a CHL class-\(\mathcal{S}\) extension must specify.}
For \(N>1\), the CHL automorphism supplies a finite quotient of
\(\mathrm{K3}\times E\) and the automorphic denominator \(\Phi_N\).  A
class-\(\mathcal{S}\) extension at level \(N\) is determined only after
the following data are supplied:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \Sigma_N\ \text{as a marked Riemann surface},\qquad
  \text{the puncture type at every marked point},\\
  \text{the action of }\Zmod{N}\text{ on the marked surface},\qquad
  \text{a derivation of }(n_v,n_h)\text{ from a pants decomposition}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Riemann--Hurwitz applies after a covering
\(\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma_N\) and its ramification indices are
given.  Fixed-point numbers of a symplectic K3 automorphism live in the
K3 geometry. A Gaiotto curve needs its own ramification indices.
Since \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Dynkin}(A_1))=1\), the finite CHL action
has trivial outer-automorphism action on the \(A_1\) six-dimensional
theory. The marked curve, puncture types, and anomaly coefficients
remain independent data.

For a genus-zero CHL construction of type \(A_1\) with \(n_N\) maximal
punctures, the pants count gives
\[
  n_v(N)=3(n_N-3),\qquad n_h(N)=4(n_N-2),
\]
which gives
\[
  \cfd(N)=\frac{5n_N-13}{6},\qquad
  \ctd(N)=-12\cfd(N)=-2(5n_N-13),
\]
for \(n_N\ge 4\).  A table of \(\cfd(N)\), \(\ctd(N)\), or derived
numerators becomes a theorem only after the marked surface and puncture
data determine \(n_N\).

\paragraph{Orbifolds of the protected chiral algebra.}
The Beem--Rastelli functor associates a protected VOA to a \(4d\)
\(\mathcal{N}=2\) theory.  An equality
\[
  \chi(\TclassS{A_1}{\Sigma_N})
  \cong
  \chi(\TclassS{A_1}{\Sigma_{0,24}})^{\Zmod{N}}
\]
requires an action of \(\Zmod{N}\) on the \(4d\) theory and a theorem
identifying the Schur sector after the quotient or gauging.  Fixed-point
data in the CHL geometry are additional sector data for such a theorem;
the invariant subalgebra displayed above is only one possible term in a
gauged or orbifold Schur construction.  The automorphic constants alone
yield no protected-VOA orbifold theorem.
