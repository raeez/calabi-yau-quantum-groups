\subsection{BV datum and local conventions}

Let \(X=\CC^3\), let
\[
  \Omega=dz_1\wedge dz_2\wedge dz_3,
\]
and let \((\gfrak,\kappa_{\gfrak})\) be a finite dimensional complex
quadratic Lie algebra.  The BV fields are the shifted Dolbeault dg Lie
algebra
\[
  \cE_X=\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\gfrak)[1],
  \qquad
  Q=\dbar,
  \qquad
  [\alpha,\beta]=[\alpha\wedge\beta]_{\gfrak}.
\]
The unshifted \((0,0)\) part is the ghost, the \((0,1)\) part is the
holomorphic connection field, and the \((0,2)\) and \((0,3)\) parts are
BV antifields under the pairing
\[
  \omega_X(\alpha,\beta)=
  \int_X\Omega\wedge\kappa_{\gfrak}(\alpha,\beta).
\]
The classical action is
\[
 I_{\mathrm{cl}}(\cA)
 =
 \frac12\int_X\Omega\wedge\kappa_{\gfrak}(\cA,\dbar\cA)
 +
 \frac16\int_X\Omega\wedge\kappa_{\gfrak}(\cA,[\cA,\cA]).
\]
For a degree-one field \(A\in\Omega^{0,1}(X,\gfrak)\) and a trace
representation \(\rho\),
\[
 S_{\mathrm{cl}}(A)
 =
 \int_X\Omega\wedge\Tr_{\rho}\left(
   \frac12 A\,\dbar A+\frac13 A^3
 \right).
\]

Every graph coefficient below uses this fixed local datum:
Euclidean metric on \(\CC^3\), gauge fixing \(Q^{\mathrm{GF}}=\dbarstar\),
Costello heat-kernel propagator \(P_{\varepsilon,L}\), compactly
supported external sources, and the BV pairing \(\kappa_{\gfrak}\) for
all colour contractions.  Polynomial boundary conditions are mentioned
separately when the affine minimal model is used.  A scalar numerical
coefficient has meaning only after these choices and the trace
normalisation have been fixed.

\subsection{Flat heat-kernel propagator}

Let \(K_t(z,w)\) be the scalar heat kernel of the flat Dolbeault
Laplacian on \(\CC^3\).  For \(0<\varepsilon<L<\infty\),
\[
  P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w)
  =
  \int_{\varepsilon}^{L}
  (\dbarstar\otimes 1)K_t(z,w)\,dt,
  \qquad
  \dbar P_{\varepsilon,L}=K_L-K_{\varepsilon}.
\]
Off the diagonal, the limit \(\varepsilon\to0\), \(L\to\infty\) is the
Bochner-Martinelli homotopy kernel.  Writing \(\eta=z-w\), its scalar
Dolbeault part is
\[
 B_{\mathrm{BM}}(\eta)
 =
 \frac{2!}{(2\pi i)^3}
 \sum_{k=1}^{3}(-1)^{k-1}
 \frac{\overline{\eta_k}}{\|\eta\|^6}\,
 d\bar\eta_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{d\bar\eta_k}
 \wedge\cdots\wedge d\bar\eta_3 .
\]
The full BV kernel is \(B_{\mathrm{BM}}\) tensored with the inverse
gauge pairing and the holomorphic volume density in the integration
variable.  It has homogeneity \(r^{-5}\), \(r=\|\eta\|\), and gives the
Dolbeault homotopy
\[
  \dbar P_{\mathrm{BM}}+P_{\mathrm{BM}}\dbar=\mathbf 1
\]
on compactly supported forms on \(\CC^3\).  A single propagator has
scaling degree \(5<6\) in the real normal variable, hence is locally
integrable at the diagonal.

\subsection{Free and tree coefficients}

For homogeneous compactly supported sources
\(\alpha,\beta\in\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(X,\gfrak)\) of complementary
Dolbeault degrees, set
\[
  \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\Omega}
  =
  \int_X\Omega\wedge\kappa_{\gfrak}(\alpha,\beta).
\]
The free two-point coefficient is
\[
 \bigl\langle \cA^a(\alpha)\,\cA^b(\beta)\bigr\rangle_0
 =
 \hbar\,\kappa_{\gfrak}^{ab}\,
 \langle\alpha,P_{\mathrm{BM}}\beta\rangle_{\Omega}.
\]
For disjoint supports the integral is an ordinary absolutely convergent
integral.  Coincident supports are evaluated with
\(P_{\varepsilon,L}\) and then renormalised in Costello's local
functional complex.

Let \(\{T_a\}\) be \(\kappa_{\gfrak}\)-orthonormal and put
\[
  f_{abc}=\kappa_{\gfrak}(T_a,[T_b,T_c]).
\]
For \(\gfrak=\uone\), \(f_{abc}=0\), so the cubic vertex vanishes and
the odd connected Gaussian coefficients vanish.  For general
\(\gfrak\), the connected tree coefficient for compactly supported
sources away from total collision is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \bigl\langle
  \cA^{a_1}(\alpha_1)\cA^{a_2}(\alpha_2)\cA^{a_3}(\alpha_3)
 \bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{tree,conn}}
 &=
 \hbar^2 f^{a_1a_2a_3}
 \int_X\Omega\wedge
  (P_{\mathrm{BM}}\alpha_1)\wedge
  (P_{\mathrm{BM}}\alpha_2)\wedge
  (P_{\mathrm{BM}}\alpha_3).
\end{aligned}
\]
The power of \(\hbar\) is \(3-1=2\): three external contractions
contribute \(\hbar^3\), and one cubic vertex from
\(I_{\mathrm{cl}}/\hbar\) contributes \(\hbar^{-1}\).  The displayed
sign follows the orientation convention in the BV pairing above.

\subsection{Two-point one-loop local term}

The one-loop two-point graph has two cubic vertices and two internal
propagators.  Its colour contraction is
\[
  \sum_{b,c}f^{a_1bc}f^{a_2}{}_{bc}
  =
  C_2^{\ad}(\gfrak)\,\kappa_{\gfrak}^{a_1a_2},
  \qquad
  C_2^{\rho}=\sum_a\rho(T_a)\rho(T^a),
\]
where \(\{T^a\}\) is dual to \(\{T_a\}\) for the chosen pairing.  In the
physics basis \(t_a\) of \(\sun\) with
\(\Tr_{\mathrm{fund}}(t_at_b)=\frac12\delta_{ab}\) and index contraction
by \(\delta_{ab}\),
\[
  f_{acd}f_{bcd}=N\delta_{ab}.
\]
If the BV pairing itself is \(\Tr_{\mathrm{fund}}\), the inverse
pairing contributes the corresponding factor of \(2\).  Thus the
printed scalar \(N\) or \(2N\) records a normalisation choice, while the
invariant statement is the \(C_2^{\ad}\)-contraction above.

The raw diagonal singularity of the two internal kernels is
\[
  B_{\mathrm{BM}}(w_1-w_2)B_{\mathrm{BM}}(w_2-w_1)
  \sim
  \left(\frac{2!}{(2\pi i)^3}\right)^2 r^{-10}
\]
up to the angular form.  In the six real normal directions to the
diagonal,
\[
  \int_{\varepsilon}^{R} r^5r^{-10}\,dr
  =
  \frac14(\varepsilon^{-4}-R^{-4}).
\]
The product has scaling degree \(10\) in real dimension \(6\).  Its
extension across the diagonal is determined by the heat-kernel
regulator together with local distributions of derivative order at most
\(10-6=4\).  The local part of the one-loop effective interaction has
the form
\[
  \Gamma^{(1)}_{2,\mathrm{loc}}
  =
  \hbar\,C_2^{\ad}(\gfrak)
  \sum_{|\nu|\le4}c_{\nu}(\varepsilon,L,h,Q^{\mathrm{GF}})
  \int_X\Omega\wedge
  \kappa_{\gfrak}\!\left(A,D^{\nu}A\right),
\]
where \(D^{\nu}\) is a local holomorphic and Dolbeault differential
monomial.  The coefficient of
\[
  \int_X\Omega\wedge\kappa_{\gfrak}(A,\dbar A)
\]
is the two-point kinetic counterterm in the chosen subtraction scheme.
The coefficients \(c_{\nu}\) depend on the heat-kernel normalisation,
boundary condition, gauge fixing, and local coordinate convention.

\subsection{Quartic anomaly and QME}

For a representation \(\rho:\gfrak\to\End(V)\), define
\[
  I_4^{\rho}(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
  =
  \frac1{4!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}
  \Tr_V\!\left(
    \rho(x_{\sigma(1)})\rho(x_{\sigma(2)})
    \rho(x_{\sigma(3)})\rho(x_{\sigma(4)})
  \right).
\]
Then \(I_4^{\rho}\in\Sym^4(\gfrak^{\vee})^{\gfrak}\).  For the effective
interactions \(I[L]\), the scale-\(L\) quantum master equation is
\[
  QI[L]+\frac12\{I[L],I[L]\}_{L}
  +\hbar\Delta_LI[L]=0 .
\]
In complex dimension \(3\), the one-loop gauge obstruction is the
degree-one local functional
\[
  \Theta^{\rho}_U(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)
  =
  \int_U
  \left[
  I_4^{\rho}\bigl(
    \alpha_0,\partial\alpha_1,\partial\alpha_2,\partial\alpha_3
  \bigr)
  \right]_{(3,3)},
  \qquad
  \alpha_i\in\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(U,\gfrak).
\]
Its class
\[
  \kanom(X,\gfrak,\rho)
  =
  [\,\hbar\Theta^{\rho}\,]
  \in
  H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}
  \bigl(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\gfrak)[1],\cO_{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)
\]
is the first QME obstruction for the open hCS theory.  In ghost-field
notation the adjoint case is the Costello-Li descent representative
\[
  \int_X \Tr_{\ad}(c\,F_A^3).
\]
The arity-two, degree-zero tensor \(C_2^{\rho}\) controls the kinetic
counterterm.  The arity-four, degree-one tensor \(I_4^{\rho}\) controls
the QME anomaly.  Abelian hCS has no cubic vertex; a non-abelian datum
passes the one-loop QME precisely after the class above vanishes or is
trivialised by an explicit local or open-closed counterterm.

\subsection{Compact Calabi-Yau threefold package}

A compact hCS quantisation theorem on a Calabi-Yau threefold requires
the following data:
\begin{itemize}
\item a compact Calabi-Yau threefold \((X,\Omega_X)\);
\item orientation data and determinant-line framing for the BV pairing;
\item a quadratic gauge Lie algebra or dg Lie algebra
  \((\gfrak,\kappa_{\gfrak})\), plus \(\rho\) when traces are used;
\item elliptic Costello gauge fixing, heat-kernel regulator, and
  renormalisation scheme;
\item completed local functionals and observables carrying
  \(\Delta_L\), \(\{-,-\}_L\), and RG flow;
\item a factorisation or TCFT sewing structure descending from local
  polydisks to \(X\);
\item a vanishing or explicit trivialisation of
  \(\kanom(X,\gfrak,\rho)\).
\end{itemize}
Under these hypotheses the first quantum step is governed by Costello's
obstruction complex, and the obstruction is the class represented by
\(\Theta^{\rho}\).  Each higher-loop step requires its own
obstruction-vanishing or counterterm witness inside the same completed
complex.

\subsection{CY-to-chiral specialisation}

The local hCS factorisation algebra on a solved neighbourhood of a
curve \(C\subset X\) gives, after integration in the normal directions,
\[
  A_C=
  \int_{N_{C/X}/C}\Obs^q_{\hCS}
  \in
  \operatorname{Alg}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Eone}(C).
\]
This is the \(d=3\) specialisation layer of the CY-to-chiral
construction.  The braided \(\Etwo\) structure is a centre, double, or
module-layer structure, for example
\[
  \cZ\!\left(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_C)\right)
  \simeq
  \Rep^{\Etwo}\!\left(
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE},\mathrm{ch}}(A_C,A_C)
  \right)
\]
on the chiral BZFN locus.

For the toric chart,
\[
  \CoHA(\CC^3)\cong\Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1),
\]
the positive half in the Kontsevich-Soibelman and
Schiffmann-Vasserot normalisation.  The full affine-Yangian and
\(\Winf\) comparison uses the additional chain
\[
  \Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \longrightarrow
  D_{\hbar}\!\left(\Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\right)
  \longrightarrow
  \text{completed centre or evaluation module}
  \longrightarrow
  \Winf .
\]

\subsection{Separated objects}

The hCS fields, their BV antifields, Hochschild polyvectors, and
categorical Calabi-Yau data are distinct inputs:
\[
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\gfrak)[1],
  \qquad
  \Omega^{0,3-\bullet}_c(X,\gfrak)[2],
  \qquad
  \HH^\bullet(\Perf(\CC^3)),
  \qquad
  (\mathcal C,\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal C}).
\]
For the affine model,
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\Perf(\CC^3))
  \simeq
  \CC[x,y,z]\otimes
  \bigwedge\nolimits^\bullet
  \langle\partial_x,\partial_y,\partial_z\rangle .
\]
These polyvectors control categorical deformations and derived centres.
The finite dimensional hCS gauge algebra enters through an external
quadratic coefficient or framing object.  The local invariant used by
the toric hCS calculation is
\[
  \kch(A_{\CC^3})=\chi(\cO_{\CC^3})=1
\]
in the toric normalisation.  The invariants \(\kcat\), \(\kBKM\), and
\(\kfib\) belong to separate categorical, Borcherds, and fibre
constructions.

\subsection{Literature anchors}

Costello, \emph{Renormalization and Effective Field Theory}, AMS 2011,
Sections 2 through 9, supplies the heat-kernel RG formalism and the BV
QME obstruction complex.  Costello-Gwilliam,
\emph{Factorization Algebras in Quantum Field Theory}, Volumes 1 and 2,
construct the factorisation algebra of quantum observables.  Costello-Li,
\emph{Quantum BCOV theory on Calabi-Yau manifolds and the higher genus
B-model}, identifies the quartic open-string anomaly and the
open-closed Green-Schwarz cancellation mechanism.  Costello,
\texttt{arXiv:1303.2632}, Sections 4 and 9 through 10, gives the
holomorphic/topological Chern-Simons construction leading to affine
Yangian boundary algebras.  Kontsevich-Soibelman and
Schiffmann-Vasserot identify the critical \(\CC^3\) CoHA with
\(\Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\); the \(\Winf\) comparison uses the subsequent
double, centre, completion, and evaluation steps.
