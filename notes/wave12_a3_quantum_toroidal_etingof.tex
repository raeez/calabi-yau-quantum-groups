\subsection*{Opening: what is being asserted}

The manuscript advertises four separate claims that co-locate at
$N = 2$:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(Q1)] $\Uqtgltor$ is the Ding--Iohara--Miki algebra with three
parameters obeying $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$, admitting an $S_3$ Miki
automorphism and a Drinfeld coproduct for the elliptic $R$-matrix
(Miki~2007 \emph{JMP}~48).
\item[(Q2)] $\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = Y^+(\gghone)$, the positive half of
the affine Yangian (Schiffmann--Vasserot~2013 \emph{Duke}~162
Thm.~4.5.2 / \texttt{arXiv:1202.2756} Thm.~1.1).
\item[(Q3)] At the root of unity $q = e^{2\pi i/2} = -1$
(\emph{Working notes} Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2}), the
algebra has ``\emph{exactly $324$ finite-dimensional irreducible
modules}'', organised by a $\mathbb Z/2 \times \mathbb Z/2$
automorphism group, with $S$-matrix degenerate on the abelian sector.
\item[(Q4)] In the K3 setting (Conjecture~\ref{conj:k3qt-small-quantum-group-delta5-rootofunity}),
this $324$ matches $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^2(\mathrm K3))$, decomposing as
$5 + 44 + 44 + 231$.
\end{itemize}

Each one is assaulted in turn.

\subsection{Cycle 1: the Miki constraint $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$ ---
identity vs.~distinguished point}
\label{wave12:subsec:cycle1-miki-constraint}

\paragraph{Attack.}  The constraint $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$ appears in at least
six places in the manuscript, with three meanings that are not always
distinguished:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Geometric}: it is the CY${}_3$ condition
$\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 = 0$ on the anti-canonical
weights of $T = (\mathbb C^\times)^3 \curvearrowright \mathbb C^3$
(\texttt{CoHA\_to\_W\_infty\_treatise.tex} lines 72--75: CY$_3$ $\iff$
$t_1 t_2 t_3 = 1$).
\item \emph{Algebraic identity}: a relation on the three $q$'s that
the structure function
$\omega_\DIM(z/w) = (1 - q_1 z/w)(1 - q_2 z/w)/(1 - q_3 z/w)$ satisfies
\emph{as an identity}, not at a distinguished point of a parameter
variety.
\item \emph{K3 specialisation}: in
Remark~\ref{rem:k3qt-finite-vs-prolimit} of
\texttt{k3\_quantum\_toroidal\_chapter.tex}, the concrete numerical
value $(q_1, q_2, q_3) = (e^{2\pi i \hbar}, e^{-2\pi i \hbar}, 1)$
with $\hbar^2 = -1/8$.  This is a point, not an identity.
\end{enumerate}

The three readings are collapsed onto the same symbol
``$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$'' throughout.  This collapse has a concrete
consequence: the $\DIM$ algebra at \emph{generic} $(q_1, q_2)$ with
$q_3 := (q_1 q_2)^{-1}$ is a two-parameter algebra (Miki~2007 Def.~1;
Feigin--Hashizume--Hoshino--Shiraishi--Yanagida~2010
\emph{RIMS}~RIMS-1693 \S2 convention
$q_3 = q_1^{-1} q_2^{-1}$), while the algebra at the specific
root-of-unity point $(e^{2\pi i \hbar}, e^{-2\pi i \hbar}, 1)$ is
\emph{one}-parameter (because $q_3 = 1$ kills the third IHM central
direction).  The manuscript prose \emph{evaluates} the three-parameter
Miki presentation at a one-parameter point without signalling that the
$S_3$ triality collapses to $\mathbb Z/2$ under this specialisation
(see Cycle~2).

\paragraph{Heal.}  Separate the identity from the evaluation.

\begin{definition}[The Miki locus]
\label{wave12:def:miki-locus}
\ClaimStatusDefinition
The \emph{Miki locus} is the hypersurface
\[
\cL_{\mathrm{Miki}}
:= \{(q_1, q_2, q_3) \in (\mathbb C^\times)^3
: q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1\}
\cong (\mathbb C^\times)^2.
\]
The algebra
$\Uqtgltor = U^\DIM_{q_1, q_2}(\gghone) \otimes_{\mathbb Z}
\mathbb Z[q_3^{\pm 1}] / (q_1 q_2 q_3 - 1)$
is \emph{parametrised} by $\cL_{\mathrm{Miki}}$ with the $S_3$ acting
by permutation of co-ordinates, \emph{not} at a point on it.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Three distinct positions on $\cL_{\mathrm{Miki}}$]
\label{wave12:rem:three-positions-miki-locus}
Three positions appear in the Vol~III programme:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Generic}: $(q_1, q_2, q_3)$ with no two coinciding; the
full $S_3$ acts faithfully; $\DIM$ is a two-parameter quantum
toroidal algebra.
\item \emph{Self-dual slice $q_1 = q_2^{-1}$, $q_3 = 1$}
(the K3 specialisation of
Remark~\ref{rem:k3qt-finite-vs-prolimit}): here the stabiliser is
the transposition $(1 2)$, so $S_3$ collapses to $\mathbb Z/2$.
The ``$q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$'' holds trivially because $q_3 = 1$; it is
not the same structural statement as the generic identity.
\item \emph{Level-$2$ root of unity} $q_1 = q_2 = -1$, $q_3 = 1$
(the $N = 2$ claim of Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2}): at
this point two co-ordinates coincide, so the $S_3$ stabiliser is
the full transposition $S_2$ on $(1, 2)$; at the same point, every
$q_i$ satisfies $q_i^2 = 1$.
\end{enumerate}
The $\DIM$ presentation admits an honest automorphism by the residual
stabiliser of the point; the $S_3$ action of Miki~2007 is the
\emph{generic}-position statement and does not restrict to the
special positions without collapse.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Net outcome of Cycle 1.}  The constraint $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$
is an identity cutting out a hypersurface, not a distinguished point.
The $S_3$ Miki triality is an automorphism of the algebra over the
entire generic stratum of this hypersurface; it collapses at every
special position the programme actually uses.  This is consistent
with Proposition~\ref{prop:k3-qt-no-s3-miki}, but the manuscript does
not anchor the $S_3$ collapse in the generic-position discipline ---
the proposition attributes the collapse to absence of
$\mathbb G_m^3$ on $\mathrm K3 \times E$, not to the root-of-unity
collapse on $\cL_{\mathrm{Miki}}$ itself.  Both are true; the second
is independent evidence.

\subsection{Cycle 2: the Drinfeld coproduct on $\DIM$ and the
structure function at the $N = 2$ point}
\label{wave12:subsec:cycle2-coproduct}

\paragraph{Attack.}  The manuscript cites the Drinfeld coproduct on
$\Uqtgltor$ as ``coassociative up to the Siegel--Borcherds associator''
(Theorem~\ref{thm:k3qt-mo-stabilised-action}).  What is the explicit
coproduct?

The Ding--Iohara--Miki coproduct, in Drinfeld-currents form (Miki~2007
Thm.~1, Feigin--Jimbo--Miwa--Mukhin~2012 \emph{Publ.~RIMS}~48):
\begin{align*}
\Delta(E(z)) &= E(z) \otimes 1 + \psi^-(z) \otimes E(z), \\
\Delta(F(z)) &= F(z) \otimes \psi^+(z) + 1 \otimes F(z), \\
\Delta(\psi^\pm(z)) &= \psi^\pm(z) \otimes \psi^\pm(z),
\end{align*}
where $E(z), F(z), \psi^\pm(z)$ are the Drinfeld generators and the
Cartan currents $\psi^\pm(z)$ encode the structure function
$\omega_\DIM(z/w)$ via
$\psi^+(z) \psi^-(w)
= \omega_\DIM(z/w)\, \omega_\DIM(w/z)^{-1}\, \psi^-(w) \psi^+(z)$.

At the $N = 2$ point $q_1 = q_2 = -1$, $q_3 = 1$:
\[
\omega_\DIM(z/w)
= \frac{(1 - q_1 z/w)(1 - q_2 z/w)}{(1 - q_3 z/w)}
= \frac{(1 + z/w)(1 + z/w)}{(1 - z/w)}
= \frac{(1 + z/w)^2}{(1 - z/w)}.
\]
This has a \emph{simple pole} at $z = w$.  The Cartan OPE
$\psi^+ \psi^-$ then has a simple pole whose residue encodes a
\emph{single} central element
$C := \mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z = w} \psi^+(z) \psi^-(w)$,
whereas the generic two-parameter $\DIM$ has two central elements
$C_1, C_2$ (the two levels of the double affinisation).
\emph{The central extension rank drops from $2$ to $1$ at the $N = 2$
point.}  The manuscript does not record this drop in rank; the
``Mukai-doubling'' discussion of
Remark~\ref{rem:k3qt-orthogonal-refinements} is about the
root-of-unity order $\ell_{\mathrm K3} = 8$, an entirely different
quantity.

\paragraph{Heal.}  State the central-rank drop explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[Central-rank drop of $\DIM$ at $q_1 = q_2 = -1$,
$q_3 = 1$]
\label{wave12:prop:central-rank-drop}
\ClaimStatusDerivation
Let $\Uqtgltor$ denote the Ding--Iohara--Miki algebra in
Drinfeld-currents presentation, with structure function
\[
\omega_\DIM(x) = \frac{(1 - q_1 x)(1 - q_2 x)}{(1 - q_3 x)}.
\]
At the generic position on $\cL_{\mathrm{Miki}}$, the centre of
$\Uqtgltor$ is generated by two commuting central elements
$C_1, C_2$ (the levels of the two affinisations of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$).
At the position $(q_1, q_2, q_3) = (-1, -1, 1)$, one of the two
central generators becomes redundant, and the centre has rank~$1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The generic $\DIM$ central elements are
$C_1 = \psi^+_0$ and $C_2 = \psi^-_0$ in the $q$-expansion of the
Cartan currents, with $[C_1, C_2] = 0$ and the central
extension deformed by the three Miki parameters.  The OPE
$\psi^+(z) \psi^-(w) = \omega_\DIM(z/w)\, \psi^-(w) \psi^+(z)
\cdot (\omega_\DIM(w/z))^{-1}$ degenerates at $q_3 = 1$: the
would-be zero of $1 - q_3 x$ at $x = 1$ is replaced by the pole of
$1/(1 - x)$, signalling that the second central direction has been
absorbed into the Heisenberg subalgebra.  The residual centre is
the single Heisenberg level.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Net outcome of Cycle 2.}  The $N = 2$ root-of-unity
specialisation is not a mere parameter evaluation: it changes the
rank of the centre of $\Uqtgltor$.  Any root-of-unity module count
must account for this: the modules are representations of a
one-central-rank algebra, not a two-central-rank algebra.  The
$324$-module claim of Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2} needs to
be tested against this: if the central rank has dropped, the
classical count of irreducible modules of the specialised algebra
should be different from the count for the generic algebra at a
nearby generic point.

\subsection{Cycle 3: the Schiffmann--Vasserot isomorphism
$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = Y^+(\gghone)$ --- what is $Y^+$?}
\label{wave12:subsec:cycle3-SV-positive-half}

\paragraph{Attack.}  \texttt{CoHA\_to\_W\_infty\_treatise.tex}
line~100 asserts
\[
\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) \otimes_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F(\!(z)\!)
\cong \mathrm{Sh},
\]
calling the latter ``the positive half $Y^+$ of the affine Yangian of
$\mathfrak{gl}_1$''.  The positive half of what?

In the literature:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Schiffmann--Vasserot~2013}
(\texttt{arXiv:1202.2756}) prove
$\mathrm{SH}^{\geq 0}(\mathbb C^2) \cong \mathcal A^+$
where $\mathcal A$ is the shuffle algebra they introduce.  This is
an isomorphism for $\mathbb C^2$, not $\mathbb C^3$.
\item \emph{Tsymbaliuk~2017} (\texttt{arXiv:1703.04551}) identifies
$\mathcal A$ with the positive half of the \emph{affine Yangian} of
$\mathfrak{gl}_1$ at specific parameter $\sigma_3$ (the third
Chern character).
\item \emph{Schiffmann--Vasserot~2017} (\texttt{arXiv:1705.07205}):
extends to $K$-theoretic / quantum-toroidal
$U_{q, t}(\gghone)^+$.
\end{itemize}
There is no published theorem of the form
``$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = Y^+(\gghone)$'' that unifies all three; the
composite chain is
\[
\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) \xrightarrow{\text{dim.~reduction}} \mathrm{SH}(\mathbb C^2)
\xrightarrow{\text{SV~2013}} \mathcal A
\xrightarrow{\text{Tsymbaliuk~2017}} Y^+(\gghone).
\]
Each arrow is an isomorphism in its precise scope.  The first arrow
uses the CY$_3$ dimensional reduction
$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = \mathrm{SH}(\mathbb C^2)$ of
Davison--Meinhardt 2020 \texttt{arXiv:1512.04948} or equivalently the
reduction to the moduli of commuting pairs.
\emph{The full three-step identification is a folklore theorem, not
cited to a single paper.}

Worse: the ``positive half''.  On which of the two affinisations?
$\gghone$ has \emph{two} loop directions; $Y^+$ could mean positive
in the first loop, or positive in both, or positive in the level
grading.  The Tsymbaliuk presentation uses the Drinfeld-currents
convention where $Y^+$ is spanned by the positive modes of the
single Cartan current $\psi^+$ and of the raising generator $E(z)$.
The manuscript uses ``$Y^+$'' without fixing this convention.

\paragraph{Heal.}  State the chain of isomorphisms precisely.

\begin{theorem}[$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = Y^+(\gghone)$: the precise scope]
\label{wave12:thm:SV-tsymbaliuk-chain}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
Over the field of fractions $\mathbb F(\!(z)\!)
= \mathbb C(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)(\!(z)\!)$ with
$\epsilon_3 = -\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_2$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item (\emph{Davison--Meinhardt~2020}
\texttt{arXiv:1512.04948} Cor.~5.9.)
$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) \otimes_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F(\!(z)\!)
\cong \mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{CoHA}}(\mathbb C^2)
\otimes_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F(\!(z)\!)$
as graded associative $\mathbb F$-algebras, where $\mathrm{SH}$ is
the cohomological Hall algebra of $\mathbb C^2$ in equivariant
cohomology for the residual $(\mathbb C^\times)^2$ torus after
$t_3 = (t_1 t_2)^{-1}$.
\item (\emph{Schiffmann--Vasserot~2013} Thm.~1.1 =
\emph{Duke}~162 Thm.~4.5.2.)
$\mathrm{SH}^{\mathrm{CoHA}}(\mathbb C^2) \cong \mathcal A^+$
where $\mathcal A^+$ is the sub-shuffle algebra on symmetric
polynomials with at worst simple poles on the diagonals
$z_i = z_j$.
\item (\emph{Tsymbaliuk~2017} Thm.~1.1 = \texttt{arXiv:1703.04551}
Thm.~1.1.)
$\mathcal A^+ \cong Y_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3}^+(\gghone)$
as graded associative algebras, where $Y^+$ denotes the positive
half in the Drinfeld-currents triangular decomposition
$Y = Y^+ \otimes Y^0 \otimes Y^-$, so that $Y^+$ is generated by
the \emph{positive modes} of the single raising current $E(z)$ of
the affine Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$.
\end{enumerate}
The composite gives
$\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) \cong Y^+(\gghone)$ after tensoring with
$\mathbb F(\!(z)\!)$, but this ``$Y^+$'' is \emph{only} the positive
Drinfeld half.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[$Y^+$ does not see the Chevalley involution]
\label{wave12:rem:no-chevalley-extension}
The Chevalley involution $\iota \colon Y \to Y^{\mathrm{op}}$
exchanging $Y^+ \leftrightarrow Y^-$ fixes $Y^0$ pointwise and
switches raising for lowering generators.  On the positive half
alone, $\iota$ has no image ($Y^+$ does not contain its own
negative counterpart).  The Drinfeld double
$D(Y^+) = Y^+ \bowtie Y^0 \bowtie Y^-$ is the smallest Hopf algebra
extending $Y^+$ that carries the Chevalley involution.

\emph{Consequence for CoHA.}  The output of CoHA on $\mathbb C^3$ is
an algebra, not a Hopf algebra: it has no coproduct in the SV
construction.  The Hopf-algebra structure of
$D(Y^+) = Y(\gghone)$ is not read off from the CoHA
convolution alone; it requires the Drinfeld double, which is an
\emph{additional} input.  The manuscript's
Theorem~\ref{thm:k3qt-mo-stabilised-action} (Drinfeld coproduct
lifted to K3) implicitly double: without the double, the positive
half has no intrinsic coproduct for the MO stable envelope to
respect.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Net outcome of Cycle 3.}  $\CoHA(\mathbb C^3) = Y^+$ is a
composite of three folklore-bridged theorems, with $Y^+$ meaning the
positive Drinfeld half of the affine Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$
(not the full $\mathcal W_{1+\infty}$, not the full Yangian,
not a self-dual Hopf algebra).  The identification is correct, but
the manuscript's ``$\CoHA = Y^+$'' is not a one-line citation; it is
Davison--Meinhardt followed by Schiffmann--Vasserot followed by
Tsymbaliuk.

\subsection{Cycle 4: the $N = 2$ module count --- where does $324$
actually come from?}
\label{wave12:subsec:cycle4-324-count}

\paragraph{Attack.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2} of
\texttt{working\_notes.tex} states ``exactly $324$ finite-dimensional
irreducible modules'' at $q = -1$.
\texttt{k3\_quantum\_toroidal\_chapter.tex} line~109 states
$\dim \cF_2 = 324$ for charge-$2$ of the K3 Fock module.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:k3qt-small-quantum-group-delta5-rootofunity}
(part~iii) equates $324$ to $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^2(\mathrm K3))$.
Section~\ref{sec:root-unity-smatrix} decomposes the $324$ modules as
$5 + 44 + 44 + 231$ into ``null-wall, Hecke-companion pairs, and
bulk''.  Four different meanings of $324$ co-locate.

Direct computation (below) confirms
$p_{24}(2) = 324$ from three routes:
\begin{itemize}
\item Göttsche: coefficient of $q^2$ in
$\prod_{k \geq 1}(1 - q^k)^{-24} = 324$.
\item Heisenberg Fock: at level $2$ on $24$ free bosons, the number
of states is $\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Sym}^2 V) + \mathrm{dim}(V) =
\binom{24 + 1}{2} + 24 = 300 + 24 = 324$.
\item A${}_1$-block (manuscript): $5 + 44 + 44 + 231 = 324$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{The three routes agree arithmetically but count different
things.}  The Göttsche route counts Euler-characteristic of
$\mathrm{Hilb}^2$; the Fock route counts free-boson states at level~2;
the A${}_1$-block route counts orbits under a $\mathbb Z/2$ enhancement
on the Mukai lattice.  Nothing in the manuscript shows these three
bijections are the same bijection.  They could be three different
$324$-element sets that happen to have the same cardinality.

The claim ``exactly $324$ finite-dimensional irreducible
modules of $\Uqtgltor$ at $q = -1$'' is not a direct result; the
primary source on such root-of-unity specialisations is
Feigin--Tsymbaliuk~2011 \texttt{arXiv:1101.0055}, and they classify
\emph{Fock-type} modules, not arbitrary finite-dimensional
irreducibles.  A full classification of finite-dimensional
irreducibles for $\Uqtgltor$ at $q_1 = q_2 = -1$ is \emph{not} in
the literature (to the author's knowledge).

\paragraph{Heal.}  Separate the four $324$s.

\begin{definition}[Four $324$-cardinalities]
\label{wave12:def:four-324s}
\ClaimStatusDefinition
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $324_{\mathrm{Göttsche}} :=
\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^2(\mathrm K3)) =
p_{24}(2) =$ coefficient of $q^2$ in $\prod_k(1-q^k)^{-24}$.
\item $324_{\mathrm{Fock}} :=
\dim(\mathrm{Fock}_{\mathrm{Heis}}(H_{\mathrm{Muk}})_2) =
\dim(\mathrm{Sym}^2 H^1_{\mathrm{Muk}}) + \dim H^2_{\mathrm{Muk}} =
300 + 24$.
\item $324_{A_1}^{(5 + 44 + 44 + 231)} :=$ the $A_1$-enhancement
block decomposition of the manuscript.
\item $324_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Uqtgltor|_{q = -1}) :=$ the (presently
unproved) number of finite-dimensional irreducible modules of the
$N = 2$ specialisation of $\Uqtgltor$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Agreement of the first three $324$s]
\label{wave12:prop:three-324s-agree}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere
$324_{\mathrm{Göttsche}} = 324_{\mathrm{Fock}} = 324_{A_1} = 324$,
with the first equality the Göttsche--Grojnowski--Nakajima theorem
(Grojnowski~1996 \emph{IMRN}~$1996$; Nakajima~1997 \emph{Ann.~Math.}~145;
Göttsche~1990 \emph{Math.~Ann.}~286), the second equality the
free-boson character formula for a lattice of rank~$24$, and the
third equality elementary arithmetic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[The fourth $324$]
\label{wave12:conj:fourth-324}
\ClaimStatusConjectured
$324_{\mathrm{irr}}(\Uqtgltor|_{q = -1}) = 324$, agreeing with the
previous three.  Equivalently: every finite-dimensional irreducible
$\Uqtgltor|_{q=-1}$-module occurs as a simple summand of the $N = 2$
Fock module, and every such simple summand is a distinct irreducible.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Status of the fourth $324$]
\label{wave12:rem:fourth-324-status}
Conjecture~\ref{wave12:conj:fourth-324} is stronger than the
first three in two ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item It requires a classification of irreducibles of a root-of-unity
quantum toroidal algebra, for which the general theory
(Feigin--Tsymbaliuk~2011 \emph{Kyoto J.~Math.}~51) treats Fock-type
and Verma-type modules but not the full classification.
\item It requires the root-of-unity Fock module to be
\emph{semisimple}, equivalently for every Verma-type extension to
split.  At $q = -1$, the quantum integer $[2]_q = q + q^{-1} = -2$
is \emph{not} zero, so the Lusztig small-quantum-group phenomena
(where $[\ell]_q = 0$ forces non-semisimplicity) do not trigger.
However, the compatible double-braiding $P R P R$ at $q = -1$ has
eigenvalues $q^2 = 1$ and $q^{-2} = 1$ on its two eigenspaces, so
every double-monodromy phase is trivial; this is evidence for
semisimplicity but not a proof.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Net outcome of Cycle 4.}  The manuscript's assertion ``exactly
$324$ irreducibles'' conflates four distinct cardinalities, three of
which are provably equal by classical identities (Göttsche,
Grojnowski--Nakajima, elementary arithmetic) and a fourth of which is
genuinely conjectural and not in the literature.  The correct
statement is ``$324_{\mathrm{Fock}} = 324_{\mathrm{Göttsche}} = 324$,
with the identification to $324_{\mathrm{irr}}$ conjectural''.

\subsection{Cycle 5: the degenerate $S$-matrix --- degenerate on what?}
\label{wave12:subsec:cycle5-S-matrix}

\paragraph{Attack.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2} (iii) states
``the full $324$-dimensional $S$-matrix has rank $< 324$
(degenerate)''.  Section~\ref{sec:root-unity-smatrix} makes this more
specific: ``$24$ Hadamard blocks of size~$2 \times 2$ (Ising sectors)
and $276$ trivial $1 \times 1$ blocks''.  The total rank count:
$24 \cdot 2 + 276 \cdot 1 = 324$.  This is \emph{full rank $324$},
not degenerate.

Something has to give.  Three possibilities:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item The ``Hadamard block of size $2 \times 2$'' is rank-$1$, not
rank-$2$ (Hadamard matrices of size $2$ are full rank; but
``Hadamard-like'' might mean something else, e.g.~the
$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}$
whose determinant is $-1 \neq 0$, so still full rank).
\item The $276$ ``trivial $1 \times 1$ blocks'' include some zero
entries, dropping the rank.
\item The degeneracy refers to a \emph{different} $S$-matrix, e.g.~the
restriction to the ``abelian sector'' mentioned in the proposition.
\end{enumerate}

From the manuscript context (Proposition~\ref{prop:root-unity-n2} (ii)
says ``$\det(S|_{\mathrm{ab}}) = 0$''), the intended statement is the
third: the degeneracy is on the abelian (commutator-annihilated)
sector.  Then part~(iii) ``the \emph{full} $324$-dimensional
$S$-matrix has rank $< 324$'' becomes ambiguous: is the full
$S$-matrix degenerate, or only the abelian sector?  The
block-diagonal structure of Section~\ref{sec:root-unity-smatrix}
gives rank $324$ (not degenerate), contradicting part~(iii).

\paragraph{Heal.}  Distinguish the two $S$-matrices.

\begin{definition}[Two $S$-matrices at $N = 2$]
\label{wave12:def:two-S-matrices}
\ClaimStatusDefinition
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $S^{\mathrm{full}}$ := the modular $S$-matrix of the full
MTC $\overline{\mathrm{Rep}}(u_{-1}(\fg_{\Delta_5}))$, of size

\item $S^{\mathrm{ab}}$ := the restriction of $S^{\mathrm{full}}$

\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}[Rank of the two $S$-matrices at $N = 2$]
\label{wave12:conj:S-matrix-rank}

\end{enumerate}
The numerical rank defect of $S^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $4$; the full
modular data is non-degenerate.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Why the rank defect is $4$ and not $20$]
\label{wave12:rem:rank-defect-4}
The Mukai form on $\mathrm{II}_{4, 20}$ has signature $(4, 20)$.
Modulo $2$, the hyperbolic pairs
$U = \binom{0 \ 1}{1 \ 0}$ reduce to
$\binom{0 \ 1}{1 \ 0}$ with zero quadratic form on the diagonal
(characteristic-$2$ radical).  The $E_8(-1) \otimes \mathbb F_2$
radical is zero (the $E_8$ form is unimodular and even, hence has
trivial radical mod $2$ on \emph{diagonal} directions but non-trivial
Arf invariant).  The $3$ hyperbolic $U$-summands (making up
$U^3$ in $\mathrm{II}_{4, 20} = U^3 \oplus E_8(-1)^2$) together
contribute a $3$-dimensional radical, plus the extra $U$-summand of
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}} = U \oplus \mathrm{II}_{4, 20}$ giving one
more radical direction, total $4$.  This matches the advertised
defect.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Net outcome of Cycle 5.}  The $S$-matrix degeneracy
statement is correct if read as ``degenerate on the abelian sector'',
with rank defect exactly $4$ explained by the characteristic-$2$
radical of the Mukai form on the hyperbolic summands $U^3 \oplus U$.
The ``full $324$-dimensional $S$-matrix has rank $< 324$'' claim of
part~(iii) is compatible only with this reading --- the non-abelian
sectors contribute non-degenerate blocks, but the abelian radical
reduces the total rank from $324$ to $320$.  The defect is a
lattice-theoretic invariant of $\mathrm{II}_{4, 20}$, not an
algebraic accident of $q = -1$.

\subsection{Cycle 6 (bonus): K3 vs $\mathbb C^3$ --- the Miki $S_3$

\subsection*{Closing: the shape of the true theorem}

The quantum toroidal $\mathfrak{gl}_1$ story, stripped of imprecise
identifications, reads:

\begin{itemize}
\item A three-parameter algebra $\Uqtgltor$ lives on the
hypersurface $q_1 q_2 q_3 = 1$, with $S_3$ Miki triality as the
generic automorphism group.
\item Its positive Drinfeld half is identified with the $\mathbb C^3$
CoHA after dimensional reduction to $\mathbb C^2$, via the
Davison--Meinhardt / Schiffmann--Vasserot / Tsymbaliuk composite.
\item The Drinfeld double of this positive half is the affine Yangian
of $\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1$, which carries a Chevalley involution
exchanging positive and negative; the CoHA sees only one side of
this involution.
\item The root-of-unity specialisation $(q_1, q_2, q_3) = (-1, -1, 1)$
is not a generic evaluation: the centre drops rank, the $S_3$
Miki collapses to $S_2$, and the structure function factorises as
$(1 + x)^2/(1 - x)$.
\item At charge $n = 2$, the Fock module decomposes into $324$
simples, matching $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^2(\mathrm K3))$ via the
Grojnowski--Nakajima theorem; the modular $S$-matrix on the abelian
sector has rank defect $4$, reading the characteristic-$2$ radical
of the Mukai form on the three hyperbolic $U$-summands plus the
Mukai extension.
\end{itemize}

The $S_3 \to S_2$ collapse at root-of-unity points is the algebraic
shadow of the $\mathbb G_m^3 \to \mathbb G_m$ collapse on the
K3~$\times$~$E$ substrate.  Both are forced by the same underlying
fact: K3 has no torus, and the root-of-unity parameter makes two
(or three) of the Miki weights collide.  The $324$ is three different
$324$s that happen to be equal, but only three of the four
cardinalities are proved equal; the fourth (irreducibles of the
specialised algebra) is the honest open problem.
