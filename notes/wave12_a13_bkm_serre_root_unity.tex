\begin{abstract}
The Igusa denominator gives a proved Borcherds--Kac--Moody
superalgebra.  Its \(\Omega\)-deformed Serre presentation and the
root-of-unity finite tensor category attached to the quantum toroidal
boundary require additional data.  This note records the proved
denominator input, the conditional \(P_2=0\) mechanism for a linear
one-parameter twist, the exact representation-theoretic meaning of a
``\(324\)-module'' count, and the finite checks still required for a
theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Proved Denominator}
\label{sec:proved-denominator}

Let
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)
  =
  4\left(
  \frac{\theta_2(\tau,z)^2}{\theta_2(\tau,0)^2}
  +\frac{\theta_3(\tau,z)^2}{\theta_3(\tau,0)^2}
  +\frac{\theta_4(\tau,z)^2}{\theta_4(\tau,0)^2}
  \right)
  =
  \frac{1}{2}\chi(K3;\tau,z)
\]
be the Eichler-Zagier normalisation.  Its constant term is
\[
  \phi_{0,1}\big|_{q^0}=\zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta,\qquad c(0,0)=10 .
\]
The Gritsenko-Nikulin/Borcherds lift gives the weight-five Igusa form
\[
  \operatorname{Bor}(\phi_{0,1})=\Delta_5,\qquad
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=\frac{c(0,0)}{2}=5 .
\]
The primitive monic denominator convention is
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5 .
\]
The scalar Oberdieck-Pandharipande convention uses the square
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2 .
\]
The BKM denominator is the square-root lane; the scalar DT partition
function uses the square.

\section{Serre Data}
\label{sec:serre-data}

The real simple roots of \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) have Cartan matrix
\[
  A_{\mathrm{re}}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  2&-2&-2\\
  -2&2&-2\\
  -2&-2&2
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their real-root Serre relations are the usual Borcherds relations
\[
  (\mathrm{ad}\,e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j=0,\qquad i\neq j .
\]
The imaginary simple roots are read from the polar and light-cone part
of the Borcherds product.  Their multiplicities are coefficients of
\(\phi_{0,1}\) in the denominator formula.  A deformation of these
relations requires an \(\Omega\)-deformed BKM vertex algebra, a
specified deformed two-point function, and a cohomology class in
\[
  H^2(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5};\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})
\]
measuring the second-order source of the deformed imaginary-root
relations.

\begin{definition}[Second-order Serre obstruction]
\label{def:p2-obstruction}
Fix an \(\Omega\)-deformed BKM vertex algebra whose deformed
imaginary-root OPE exponent has expansion
\[
  P_\delta(\varepsilon)
  =
  P_{\delta,0}
  +\varepsilon P_{\delta,1}
  +\varepsilon^2P_{\delta,2}
  +O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
The second-order Serre obstruction is the image of
\(P_{\delta,2}\) in the depth-\(\delta\) summand of
\(H^2(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5};\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})\).
\end{definition}

This definition is the safe replacement for a scalar statement
\(P_2(D)=0\).  Depth, discriminant, and \(\Sp_4(\Z)\)-orbit agree in
small examples only after choosing the root parametrisation.  Beyond the
first chamber, the obstruction is orbit-indexed.

\begin{proposition}[Linear one-parameter twist]
\label{prop:linear-twist}
Assume the \(\Omega\)-deformation acts on every imaginary-root field by
the exact current twist
\[
  V_\delta(z)\longmapsto \exp(\varepsilon J_0)\,V_\delta(z)\,
  \exp(-\varepsilon J_0)
\]
and assume the deformed propagator has no quadratic correction.  Then
\[
  P_{\delta,2}=0
\]
for every imaginary root \(\delta\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the stated hypotheses the deformation is conjugation by a single
Lie algebra element \(J_0\).  Eigenvalues vary linearly in
\(\varepsilon\), and the two-point contraction defining the OPE
exponent is unchanged at order \(\varepsilon^2\).  The coefficient
\(P_{\delta,2}\) is therefore zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Proof obligation]
\label{rmk:linear-twist-obligation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-twist} is conditional.  A theorem for
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) must construct the \(\Omega\)-deformed BKM
vertex algebra, identify the current \(J_0\), prove absence of a
quadratic propagator correction, and show compatibility with imaginary
simple-root multiplicities in the Borcherds denominator.
\end{remark}

\section{Root-of-Unity Boundary Model}
\label{sec:root-unity}

Let \(U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_1)\) denote the DIM or
quantum toroidal algebra.  At a root of unity one must specify:
\[
  \text{integral form},\quad
  \text{central quotient},\quad
  \text{finite subcategory},\quad
  \text{Miki action},\quad
  \text{negligible quotient}.
\]
The notation \(U_{q=-1}\) alone determines none of these choices.

\begin{definition}[Finite root-of-unity model]
\label{def:finite-root-model}
A finite \(q=-1\) boundary model is a finite tensor category
\(\mathcal C_{-1}\) equipped with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a quotient of an integral form of
\(U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}}_1)\) at \(q=-1\);
\item a finite list of simple objects closed under tensor product;
\item a braiding or \(R\)-matrix on the quotient;
\item a stated negligible ideal \(\mathcal N\);
\item the induced \(S\)-pairing on \(K_0(\mathcal C_{-1})\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

In a particular pointed finite model, the count
\[
  324=18^2=2^2\cdot 3^4
\]
may occur as the number of simple objects.  This count is a theorem only
after Definition~\ref{def:finite-root-model} is supplied and the simple
objects are classified.  The number alone is a numerical target.

\begin{proposition}[Degeneracy criterion]
\label{prop:s-degeneracy-criterion}
For a pointed finite braided category with simple-object group \(G\) and
bicharacter \(b:G\times G\to\C^\times\), the \(S\)-matrix is degenerate
exactly when
\[
  \operatorname{Rad}(b)=
  \{g\in G: b(g,h)=1\ \text{for all }h\in G\}
\]
is nonzero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The \(S\)-matrix is the character table of the homomorphism
\[
  G\longrightarrow \widehat G,\qquad
  g\longmapsto b(g,-).
\]
Its rank is the cardinality of the image.  The determinant is nonzero
exactly when the homomorphism is an isomorphism, equivalently when the
radical is zero.
\end{proof}

Thus an ``abelian sector'' at \(q=-1\) has degenerate \(S\)-matrix
precisely when its braiding bicharacter has nontrivial radical.  A
finite abelian character table with a perfect bicharacter is
nondegenerate.  Degeneracy is a property of the chosen braiding and
requires a radical computation.

\section{Six-Dimensional Boundary Scope}
\label{sec:hcs-boundary-scope}

The \(d=3\) CY-to-chiral output is native \(E_1\).  The \(E_2\) or
braided structure belongs to a centre, double, or module category.  A
six-dimensional holomorphic Chern-Simons interpretation of the BKM or
quantum-toroidal boundary therefore needs:
\[
  \text{QME solution},\quad
  \text{orientation and determinant framing},\quad
  \text{anomaly cancellation},\quad
  \text{completion},\quad
  \text{descent or sewing}.
\]
For complex dimension three the local one-loop obstruction is the
quartic class \(I_4^\rho\).  The vanishing of a formal Serre
coefficient or the existence of a root-of-unity finite model supplies no
compact hCS quantisation theorem by itself.

\section{Obligations}
\label{sec:obligations}

The BKM denominator side is proved at the automorphic-product level:
\[
  \operatorname{Bor}(\phi_{0,1})=\Delta_5,\qquad \kBKM=5 .
\]
The following items remain separate mathematical obligations.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item Construct the \(\Omega\)-deformed BKM vertex algebra for
\(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\).
\item Prove that the deformation is the exact linear current twist of
Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-twist}, or compute the quadratic
propagator correction.
\item Index the second-order Serre obstruction by actual imaginary-root
orbits and by the scalar depth parameter.
\item Specify the integral form and central quotient of the
\(q=-1\) quantum toroidal boundary algebra.
\item Classify the finite simple objects in the chosen quotient and
derive the number \(324\) from that classification.
\item Compute the braiding bicharacter and its radical on the abelian
sector.
\item Relate the root-of-unity finite category to the \(E_1\) chiral
boundary and to its centre or double.
\end{enumerate}

\section{References}
\label{sec:references}

Borcherds' automorphic products are the source of the denominator
formalism.  Gritsenko and Nikulin construct the \(\Delta_5\) correction
for the relevant Lorentzian Kac-Moody algebra.  Miki, Feigin-Odesskii,
and Feigin-Jimbo-Miwa-Mukhin supply the quantum toroidal and finite-type
module background.  Costello-Yamazaki supply the gauge-theoretic
integrability framework; applying it to \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\)
