\paragraph{Cycle 3 --- additional sub-formulas.}
Three items not pulled into P3:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Root-system axioms as a four-line checklist} (slide
      631--645). For all $\alpha, \alpha' \in \Delta$:
      $(\alpha,\alpha)>0 \Leftrightarrow \alpha \in
      \Delta^{\mathrm{re}}$; $(\alpha,\alpha)\leq 0 \Leftrightarrow
      \alpha \in \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}$; $(\alpha,\alpha')\leq 0$
      whenever $\alpha \neq \alpha'$; and $2(\alpha,\alpha')/
      (\alpha,\alpha) \in \ZZ$ on the real part. The last is the
      integrality condition that makes $\gbkm$ a Borcherds--Kac--Moody
      Lie algebra (not merely a contragredient algebra).
\item \emph{Super-dimension from the grading}. Slide 693 reads
      $\mult_\alpha = \mult_0 + \mult_1 = \dim\mathfrak{g}_0 -
      \dim\mathfrak{g}_1$. The $+$ on the left and $-$ on the right
      is the super-multiplicity convention:
      $\mult_\alpha$ is the graded dimension, and only when $(\alpha,
      \alpha)<0$ does the odd part contribute with a sign. This is the
      step at which $\phi_{0,1}$-coefficients $f(n,l)$ become
      \emph{graded} dimensions and negative $f(n,l)$ become actual
      root multiplicities of the odd sector.
\item \emph{Validity domain.} $\Phi$ is valid on
      $\Omega(\LamII) = \LamII \otimes \RR + i\mathcal{C}(\LamII)_+$
      (slide 710--711). This is the Siegel upper half-plane realised
      as a tube domain over the positive cone $\mathcal{C}(\LamII)_+$;
      the product $\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}(1 - e^{-2\pi i
      (\alpha, z)})^{\mult_\alpha}$ converges absolutely there. The
      domain is the $d=3$ Baily--Borel hull inside which
      $\tfrac{1}{64}\Delaf(2z)$ admits the Borcherds product.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cycle 4 --- $\tau(a_0) = 9$ and the $\eta^9\nu_{11}$
structure.}
The slide 594 identity (one block before 600) is the single-variable
shadow of the full $\eta^9\nu_{11}$ factorisation:
\[
  1 + \tfrac{1}{64}\sum_{t\in\NN} f(1+2t, 1, 1)\, q^t
  = \prod_{k \in \NN} (1 - q^k)^9,
\]
and the general statement (slide 596--598)
\[
  1 - \sum_{t\in\NN} m(t a_0) q^t
  = \prod_{t \in \NN} (1 - q^t)^{\tau(t a_0)}
\]
implicitly defines $\tau(ta_0)$ along every isotropic multiple. The
transitivity of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pii)$ on the three vertices at
infinity $\{2f_2,\ 2f_{-2},\ 2f_2 - 2f_3 + 2f_{-2}\}$ (slide 586) and
the invariance of the Fourier expansion (slide 588) together force
$\tau(a_0) = 9$ at every isotropic $a_0$: the exponent on the RHS is
controlled by the $\eta(z_1)^9$ factor in $A = \psi_{5,1/2} \exp(2\pi
i t z_3) = \eta(z_1)^9 \nu_{11}(z_1, z_2)$, which at the $a_0 = 2f_2$
vertex pulls back to $\eta(q)^9 = \prod_{k}(1-q^k)^9$. Hence
$\tau(a_0) = 9$ is not an arithmetic surprise: it is the exponent of
$\eta$ in the multiplicative decomposition of the Jacobi
$\psi_{5,1/2}$ that appears in the Borcherds product input.
