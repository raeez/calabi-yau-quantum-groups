# Research Note: The Quiver-with-Potential as Core Combinatorial Datum

## Proposal under investigation

The core combinatorial datum of the quantum vertex chiral group G(X) is a
**quiver with superpotential (Q, W)**, or more generally the **Ginzburg CY3
dg-algebra Gamma(Q, W)**.

---

## 1. What the root datum R(X) contains (reference: theory_generalized_root_datum.tex)

The CY3 root datum R(X) = (Lambda, (.,.), Delta, mult, W, rho) has seven
axiom layers (CY1--CY7):

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| Lambda | Free Z-module, signature (p, q) with p,q >= 1, decomposition Lambda^hyp + Lambda^ell |
| (.,.) | Symmetric bilinear form, even lattice |
| Delta^re | Real roots, all norm 2 (simply-laced), lie in Lambda^hyp |
| Delta^im_0 | Even imaginary roots, mult > 0 (bosonic) |
| Delta^im_1 | Odd imaginary roots, mult < 0 (fermionic), only for negative-norm roots |
| mult | Multiplicity function controlled by a Jacobi form phi: mult(alpha) = c_phi(alpha^2/2, ell(alpha)) |
| W(X) | Weyl group, Coxeter, generated by reflections in real simple roots |
| rho | Weyl vector, (rho, alpha_i) = -1 for all real simple roots |
| Phi_R | Automorphic denominator product, weight k_R = (1/2)c_phi(0,0) |

The critical feature is axiom **(CY3)**: root multiplicities are Fourier
coefficients of a **modular object** (Jacobi form, mock modular form, or
higher-depth modular form). This is the automorphic content.

And axiom **(CY5)**: the denominator identity produces an **automorphic form**
for O(Lambda)_+. For K3 x E this is Delta_5; for toric CY3 this is the DT
partition function.

## 2. What (Q, W) captures

### 2a. The lattice and Gram matrix (partial)

From Construction 7.1 in theory_generalized_root_datum.tex, for a toric CY3
the quiver gives:

- **Lambda = Z^{Q_0}** (dimension vectors)
- **(d, d') = chi_Q(d, d') + chi_Q(d', d)** (symmetrized Euler-Ringel form)
- **Real simple roots** = standard basis vectors e_i with (e_i, e_i) = 2
- **Gram matrix** a_{ij} = (e_i, e_j), determined by arrow counts:
  - a_{ii} = 2 - 2*(loops at i)
  - a_{ij} = -(arrows i->j + arrows j->i) for i != j
- **Weyl group** generated by reflections in real simple roots

This is precisely the data of a **generalized Cartan matrix** of Borcherds type.
The quiver captures the Dynkin diagram of the real root system.

### 2b. The CY3 condition (via the Ginzburg algebra)

The Ginzburg dg-algebra Gamma(Q, W) is CY3 by construction: it has a
non-degenerate trace Tr: HH_3(Gamma) -> k. The potential W encodes the
**cubic relations** (Jacobian relations partial_a W = 0 for each arrow a).
The CY3 property is equivalent to the Jacobian algebra Jac(Q, W) being
3-Calabi-Yau.

### 2c. The positive half of G(X) (via critical CoHA)

From theory_coha_e1_sector.tex, Theorem 3.1 (RSYZ):

> CoHA(Q_X, W_X) = Y^+(hat{g}_{Q_X})

The critical CoHA, built from vanishing-cycle BM homology on the critical
locus of W, is isomorphic to the **positive half** of the affine super
Yangian. This is the E_1-sector: the associative (ordered) half.

The full quantum vertex chiral group is obtained by the **Drinfeld double**:

> Drin(CoHA(Q, W)) = Y(hat{g}_Q)

The R-matrix of the double provides the E_2-braiding.

### 2d. The tree-level shadow tower data

In the language of theory_automorphic_shadow.tex, the quiver data corresponds
to the **arity-2 shadow** Theta^{<=2}:

- The collision r-matrix r(z) has poles determined by the real root OPE,
  which is the Gram matrix from the quiver.
- The modular characteristic kappa(A_X) encodes the Weyl vector contribution.

## 3. What (Q, W) does NOT capture

### 3a. Imaginary root multiplicities

The imaginary roots Delta^{im} have multiplicities mult(alpha) = DT_alpha(X).
These are **not** visible in the quiver data (Q, W) itself. The quiver tells
you the lattice Lambda and the Gram matrix (the tree-level data), but the
DT invariants are computed from the **geometry of moduli of (Q, W)-representations**.
In other words, the multiplicities are derived from (Q, W) but are not part
of the finite combinatorial datum (Q, W).

**Assessment**: This is not a fundamental obstruction. The multiplicities are
**determined** by (Q, W) via the CoHA construction. They are not additional
data but computed invariants. The quiver datum is in principle sufficient;
the issue is extractability.

### 3b. The automorphic/modular structure (CY3 and CY5)

The CY3 axiom demands that multiplicities are **Fourier coefficients of a
Jacobi form** phi. The CY5 axiom demands that the denominator product is an
**automorphic form** for O(Lambda)_+.

For K3 x E: phi = phi_{0,1} (the K3 elliptic genus), Phi_R = Delta_5.

These modular/automorphic properties are **invisible** in the quiver datum.
The quiver encodes the Gram matrix (the real roots), but it does not encode:

1. **The Jacobi form phi**: this is external input from CY2 geometry
   (the K3 elliptic genus) or from the refined DT partition function.
2. **The Borcherds lift**: the mechanism that produces the automorphic form
   from the Jacobi form operates on lattice + modular data, not on quiver data.
3. **The lattice decomposition Lambda = Lambda^hyp + Lambda^ell**: the quiver
   gives Lambda = Z^{Q_0} but does not naturally decompose it into hyperbolic
   and elliptic parts.

**Assessment**: This is a **genuine gap**. The quiver datum lives at tree-level
(arity 2); the automorphic content is the full shadow tower (all arities).
The passage from (Q, W) to the automorphic form requires either:
- Computing all DT invariants (extracting them from geometry), or
- Supplying the Jacobi form as additional data.

### 3c. The BKM superalgebra structure

The BKM superalgebra g_R has:
- Even/odd imaginary roots (bosonic/fermionic generators)
- The super structure Delta^{im}_1 subset {alpha : alpha^2 < 0}
- The denominator identity with signed contributions

The quiver knows about the **real root** super structure (vertex i is odd if
a_{ii} < 0, i.e., more than one loop). But the imaginary root super structure
requires knowing the **sign** of DT invariants, which is again derived data.

### 3d. The Weyl vector rho

The Weyl vector rho satisfying (rho, alpha_i) = -1 for all real simple roots
is determined by the Gram matrix, hence by the quiver. **This IS captured.**

### 3e. K3 x E: rank mismatch

For K3 x E: Lambda^{3,2} has rank 5. The hyperbolic sublattice Lambda^{2,1}_{II}
has rank 3 with Gram matrix:

```
  ( 2  -2  -2)
  (-2   2  -2)
  (-2  -2   2)
```

The fibration adds Lambda^{1,1} (rank 2). There is no quiver with Q_0 = 5
vertices that naturally produces this lattice structure. The K3 x E geometry
is NOT toric (the K3 surface is not toric), so the quiver-from-toric-diagram
construction does not apply.

One could try to find a (non-toric) quiver whose symmetrized Euler form matches
the Gram matrix, but:
- The Gram matrix of Lambda^{2,1}_{II} has all off-diagonals = -2 (not the
  -1 typical of quivers with single arrows).
- The lattice decomposition Lambda^hyp + Lambda^ell is not a quiver feature.
- The elliptic fiber direction Lambda^{1,1} has signature (1,1), which would
  require a quiver vertex with (e, e) = 0, i.e., the lattice includes
  **isotropic** vectors not arising as simple roots.

**Assessment**: **Fundamental obstruction** for the K3 x E case. The quiver
datum does not naturally accommodate the lattice structure of fibered CY3s.

### 3f. Compact CY3s

The quintic X_5 in P^4 has h^{1,1} = 1, h^{2,1} = 101, chi = -200.
There is no known quiver description. The root datum (if it exists in the
BKM sense) would have Lambda extracted from K_0(X_5) with the Mukai pairing.
The multiplicity function might be governed by a mock Jacobi form (open
problem P2 in theory_generalized_root_datum.tex).

**Assessment**: **Fundamental obstruction** for compact CY3s. The quiver
approach is intrinsically tied to toric/non-compact geometry.

## 5. Analysis of the four enrichment proposals

### Proposal 1: Higher potentials W_3, W_4, ...

**Idea**: Replace the pair (Q, W_2) by a sequence (Q, W_2, W_3, W_4, ...)
where W_k is a "potential of arity k" (a formal sum of length-k cycles,
or more precisely, an element of the cyclic bar complex at arity k).

**Connection to the shadow tower**: This is precisely the shadow tower!
The arity-r shadow Theta^{<=r} captures roots of depth <= r-2.
- W_2 = the usual potential = tree-level data (real roots + Weyl vector)
- W_3 = cubic correction = first imaginary roots (depth 1)
- W_4 = quartic correction = depth-2 imaginary roots
- W_r = depth-(r-2) correction

In the A-infinity language: an A-infinity category has higher composition
maps m_2, m_3, m_4, ..., and the "higher potentials" are the cyclic
duals of these maps. A quiver with potential (Q, W) encodes only m_2
(the binary composition) and its cyclic dual. The higher potentials
encode the transferred L-infinity structure on bar cohomology.

**Verdict**: Mathematically correct and natural. The enriched datum is
**(Q, {W_r}_{r >= 2})**, which is equivalent to a curved A-infinity
category with finitely many objects (the vertices of Q). The full shadow
tower is the completed datum.

**But**: This replaces the finite datum (Q, W) with an infinite sequence.
The question becomes: is there a **finite** enrichment that determines
the full tower?

### Proposal 2: Lattice data (richer grading group)

**Idea**: Replace the grading Z^{Q_0} by a richer lattice Lambda with
additional structure: a decomposition Lambda = Lambda^hyp + Lambda^ell,
signature data, and a choice of positive cone.

**What this fixes**: The K3 x E obstruction (3e above). Instead of
Lambda = Z^{Q_0} (forced by the quiver), one specifies the full lattice
with its decomposition. The quiver Q is then a "presentation" of the
real root system inside this lattice.

**Concretely**: The enriched datum is (Lambda, (.,.), Q, W) where:
- Lambda is an even lattice of the right signature
- (.,.) is the bilinear form
- Q is a quiver whose vertices index the real simple roots
- The Gram matrix of Q matches the restriction of (.,.) to the simple roots
- W is the potential encoding cubic relations

**What this does NOT fix**: The automorphic content (phi, Phi_R). The lattice
tells you the arena but not the multiplicities.

**Verdict**: Necessary but not sufficient. This is the "geometric half"
of the enrichment.

### Proposal 3: Modular data (automorphic form on the lattice)

**Idea**: Add a choice of weak Jacobi form phi of weight 0 and appropriate
index, encoding the BPS counting function. This is the CY3 axiom directly.

**What this fixes**: The automorphic gap (3b above). With phi specified,
the imaginary root multiplicities are determined: mult(alpha) = c_phi(alpha^2/2, ell(alpha)).
The Borcherds lift then produces the automorphic denominator product.

**Concretely**: The enriched datum is (Lambda, (.,.), Q, W, phi) where phi
is the "CY counting form" -- a Jacobi form for the lattice Lambda.

**Connection to CY2 fibration**: For fibered CY3, phi = phi_S is the
elliptic genus of the CY2 surface S. The Borcherds lift then produces
the CY3 root datum. So the enriched datum is equivalent to the fibration
input (R_S, E).

**What this does NOT fix for non-fibered cases**: For the quintic, it is
unknown whether the multiplicity function is governed by any modular object.
Open problem P2.

**Verdict**: This is the correct "modular half" of the enrichment. Combined
with Proposal 2, the datum (Lambda, (.,.), Q, W, phi) captures all of R(X)
except possibly the super structure and the Weyl vector (both of which are
determined by the other data).

### Proposal 4: Quiver on a surface

**Idea**: Replace the abstract quiver Q by a "quiver on a surface" -- a
quiver embedded in a surface Sigma (the brane tiling / dimer model). The
surface carries additional topological data (genus, homology) that enriches
the combinatorics.

**What this fixes**: For toric CY3, the brane tiling on a torus T^2 already
encodes the quiver and potential. The torus provides:
- The two periodic directions = the two lattice vectors of the toric diagram
- The fundamental domain = the quiver
- The monodromy = the gluing data

For non-toric geometries, one could consider quivers on higher-genus surfaces
or surfaces with punctures. The surface provides a "spectral" direction.

**Connection to spectral curves**: The Hitchin system perspective
(theory_higgs_cy2_qvcg.tex, Construction 5.10) shows that the root datum
is enriched by "spectral data" -- for each point b in the Hitchin base,
the spectral curve C_b and its Jacobian contribute to the root space.
A "quiver on a surface" could be seen as a discrete approximation to this
spectral data.

**What this does NOT fix**: The modular content (phi, Phi_R) is still not
encoded by the surface topology alone. The surface gives a refined lattice
structure but not the multiplicities.

**Verdict**: Geometrically appealing and partially correct. The brane tiling
is already "a quiver on a torus" and it does carry more information than
the abstract quiver. But it is insufficient by itself -- it needs to be
combined with modular data.

## 6. Synthesis: the enriched datum

The analysis points to a **three-layer enrichment**:

### Layer 1: Lattice (Proposal 2)
**(Lambda, (.,.), Lambda^hyp + Lambda^ell)**

An even lattice with bilinear form and the CY1 decomposition. This is the
"arena" -- the charge lattice of the BPS spectrum.

### Layer 2: Quiver with potential (original datum)
**(Q, W) with Q_0 indexing real simple roots inside Lambda^hyp**

The Gram matrix of Q matches the restriction of (.,.) to the simple roots.
The potential W encodes the cubic (Jacobian) relations. This determines the
Weyl group, the Weyl vector, and the tree-level data.

### Layer 3: Modular datum (Proposal 3)
**phi: a weak Jacobi form of weight 0 for the lattice Lambda**

This determines the imaginary root multiplicities and, via the Borcherds
lift, the automorphic denominator product.

The combined datum is:

> **(Lambda, Q, W, phi)**

with compatibility conditions:
1. Q_0 indexes a subset of norm-2 vectors in Lambda^hyp
2. The Gram matrix a_{ij} = (alpha_i, alpha_j) matches the symmetrized
   Euler form of Q
3. W is a CY3 potential (Jac(Q, W) is 3-CY)
4. phi is a weight-0 Jacobi form for SL_2(Z) (or congruence subgroup)
   of appropriate index for Lambda
5. mult(alpha) = c_phi(alpha^2/2, ell(alpha)) >= 0 for isotropic roots
   and is integral

### Recovery of R(X) from (Lambda, Q, W, phi)

| R(X) component | Recovered from |
|---|---|
| Lambda, (.,.) | Layer 1 directly |
| Delta^re | Vertices of Q with norm 2 |
| Delta^im, mult | Fourier coefficients of phi (Layer 3) |
| W(X) | Reflections in real simple roots from Q |
| rho | Gram matrix of Q (Layer 2) |
| Phi_R | Borcherds lift of phi |

This is a **complete recovery**: (Lambda, Q, W, phi) determines R(X) entirely.

## 7. The higher-potential perspective (Proposal 1 revisited)

The shadow tower identification (theory_automorphic_shadow.tex, Theorem 1.1)
says:

> Automorphic correction = Shadow Postnikov tower

In this language:
- (Q, W_2) = arity-2 shadow = real roots + Weyl vector
- (Q, W_2, W_3) = arity-3 shadow = + first imaginary roots
- (Q, W_2, ..., W_r) = arity-r shadow = + depth-(r-2) roots
- (Q, {W_r}_{r>=2}) = full tower = complete root datum

The question is: **does phi determine {W_r}_{r >= 2}?**

For the fibered case (K3 x E), yes: phi = phi_{0,1} determines all root
multiplicities, hence all shadow components. The Jacobi form is a **finite**
piece of data (a modular form is determined by finitely many Fourier
coefficients, by the finite-dimensionality of spaces of Jacobi forms of
given weight and index).

For the toric case, the DT partition function (the analogue of phi) is in
principle determined by (Q, W) via the geometry of representation moduli.
So the higher potentials W_r are **derivable** from (Q, W_2) -- no extra
data is needed. But the derivation requires computing DT invariants, which
is a hard problem.

**Conclusion**: For toric CY3, (Q, W) is sufficient in principle -- phi is
derivable. For fibered CY3, phi must be supplied as additional input. For
compact CY3 (quintic), both the quiver and phi are unknown.

## 8. Obstructions and open problems

### 8a. The toric boundary

The toric case is the **only** case where (Q, W) is fully adequate. But toric
CY3s are non-compact and sit at the boundary of the CY moduli space. The
"no compact 4-cycles" condition (Remark in theory_coha_e1_sector.tex, 7.5)
is essential for the CoHA multiplication to be well-defined. When compact
4-cycles are present, wall-crossing modifies the CoHA structure.

### 8b. The Ginzburg algebra and formal CY3 structure

The Ginzburg dg-algebra Gamma(Q, W) is CY3 by construction, without any
geometry. It provides a CY3 category Perf(Gamma(Q, W)) for any (Q, W).
The CoHA of Gamma(Q, W) gives a positive half, and the Drinfeld double
gives a Yangian-type algebra, regardless of whether (Q, W) comes from
geometry.

**Question**: For which (Q, W) is the resulting "root datum" a CY3 root
datum in the sense of Definition 2.1 (theory_generalized_root_datum.tex)?
This is a non-trivial constraint: the DT invariants must satisfy the
modularity condition (CY3), the automorphic condition (CY5), and the
integrality conditions (CY7).

**Conjecture**: The (Q, W) that produce genuine CY3 root data are precisely
those arising from CY3 geometry (toric or otherwise). The Ginzburg algebra
is a universal construction; the CY3 root datum axioms select the geometric
ones.

### 8c. Mutations and wall-crossing

Mutations of (Q, W) (in the sense of Derksen-Weyman-Zelevinsky) correspond
to derived equivalences, hence to tilts of stability conditions, hence to
wall-crossings. In the root datum language, mutation should correspond to
changing the fundamental polyhedron P within the same Weyl group W (open
problem P4 in theory_generalized_root_datum.tex).

The quiver mutation datum is already present in (Q, W). But the
wall-crossing formula (KS) requires the full BPS spectrum (all DT invariants),
which goes beyond the quiver.

### 8d. The spectral enhancement

Construction 5.10 in theory_higgs_cy2_qvcg.tex introduces the notion of
"spectral enhancement" of the root datum: beyond the crude lattice, the
root datum is enriched by Hitchin spectral data (spectral curves, their
Jacobians, singular loci). This is the CY2 analogue of the modular datum phi.

For CY3, the spectral enhancement would involve: for each stability
condition sigma, the space of sigma-semistable objects in D^b(Coh(X)) of
class alpha. This is the "moduli side" of the root datum. The quiver (Q, W)
provides a presentation of these moduli (as critical loci), but the spectral
data goes beyond the quiver.

## 9. Verdict

### The quiver-with-potential (Q, W) is:

1. **The correct E_1-sector datum for toric CY3**: it determines the CoHA,
   which is the positive half of the quantum vertex chiral group. The full
   group is obtained by the Drinfeld double. The root datum R(X) is in
   principle recoverable from (Q, W) via DT invariant computation.

2. **The tree-level shadow (arity 2)**: in the shadow tower, (Q, W) captures
   the real root Gram matrix and the Weyl vector -- the arity-2 projection
   Theta^{<=2}. The higher arities (imaginary roots, modular structure) are
   the "automorphic correction" beyond the quiver.

3. **Insufficient for non-toric CY3**: the K3 x E lattice structure, the
   automorphic form Delta_5, the Jacobi form phi_{0,1} -- none of these are
   encoded in any quiver with potential. Compact CY3s like the quintic are
   completely out of reach.

### The correct enrichment is:

> **(Lambda, Q, W, phi)** = Lattice + Quiver with potential + Modular datum

This is the **minimal datum** that recovers the full CY3 root datum R(X).
It decomposes as:

- **Geometric half** (Lambda, Q, W): the lattice, real roots, and cubic
  relations. This is the Ginzburg/CoHA input.
- **Modular half** (phi): the BPS counting function. This is the automorphic
  input.

For toric CY3: phi is derivable from (Q, W), so (Lambda, Q, W) suffices.
For fibered CY3: phi = elliptic genus of the CY2 surface, supplied as input.
For compact CY3: both the quiver description and the nature of phi are open.

### Relation to the shadow tower:

The enriched datum (Lambda, Q, W, phi) is equivalent to specifying:
- The arity-2 shadow Theta^{<=2} (from Q, W)
- The "generating function" of all higher shadows (from phi)

The shadow tower is then determined: Theta^{<=r} for each r >= 2 is computed
from (Lambda, Q, W, phi) by the Borcherds lift (for the fibered case) or by
DT computation (for the toric case). The full MC element Theta_A is the
inverse limit.

### What the quiver proposal gets right:

The insight that (Q, W) is the E_1-sector -- the positive half, the
tree-level data, the "Dynkin diagram" of G(X) -- is entirely correct and
is the content of the RSYZ theorem. The Ginzburg algebra Gamma(Q, W) is the
correct CY3 dg-algebra for the toric setting. The critical CoHA is the
correct construction of U(n_+).

### What it misses:

The quiver is the skeleton; the automorphic form is the flesh. The BKM
superalgebra g_R is not a Kac-Moody algebra (which would be determined by
its Dynkin diagram = quiver). It is a **Borcherds algebra**, which requires
additional data: the imaginary simple roots and their multiplicities. This
additional data is the Jacobi form phi -- the modular datum.

The programme's central identification "automorphic correction = shadow tower"
(theory_automorphic_shadow.tex) makes this precise: the quiver is the base
of the tower, and the modular datum generates the infinite tower of
corrections that complete it to the full automorphic form.
