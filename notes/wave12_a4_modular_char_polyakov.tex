\subsection*{Cycle 1: the claim ``$\chitop/24 \neq \kch$ in general''}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{ATTACK (Polyakov).} At the four explicit ``No'' rows of the
table (\texttt{wn:thm:chi-neq-kappa}) the discrepancy is driven by a
BCOV genus-$1$ free-energy formula $F_1 = -\chitop(X)/24$ restricted
to \emph{compact} CY$_3$. Apply this uncritically to the elliptic
curve $E$ (CY$_1$, $\chitop=0$, $F_1=0$) and one would predict
$\kch(E)=0$; the manuscript writes $\kch(E)=1$. Same for K3 (CY$_2$,
$\chitop=24$, $F_1=-1$, prediction $\kch=-1$; manuscript writes $12$).
This is dimension-crossing: the BCOV $-\chitop/24$ is the $d=3$
tree-level genus-$1$ free energy in a specific B-model normalisation,
and is not the definition of $\kch$ on the chiral side. At $d=1,2$
the identification breaks on purely dimensional grounds, not through
any failure of categoricality.

Second attack surface: the three ``Yes'' rows (quintic, $\bP^5[3,3]$,
$\bP^5[2,4]$) sit at $\kch=\chitop/24$ via the BCOV tree-level identity.
This is the CY$_3$ compact B-model identification, and it rests on
\emph{one-loop BCOV anomaly cancellation} (BCOV 1993/1994 Eq.~(4.16)).
Declaring $\chitop/24 \neq \kch$ ``in general'' with three compact-B-model
hits and four mis-scoped failures is a category-error over the table:
the formula $\kch = \chitop(X)/24$ holds in the compact CY$_3$ smooth
B-model scope and fails elsewhere because the scope fails, not because
the equation fails.

Third attack surface: resolved conifold row with $\chitop/24 = 1/12$
versus $\kch=1$. This row is not a disagreement between two definitions
applied to the same object; it is an application of the \emph{compact}
BCOV formula to a \emph{local} toric CY$_3$, precisely AP-CY symbol-overload
cache entry \#1 (bare $\kch$ labelling two different constructions
without a scope gate).

\paragraph{HEAL (first principles).} The table's mathematical content
is right; its framing is wrong. The precise statement is:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(compact smooth CY$_3$, B-model).}
   For compact CY$_3$ complete intersections in products of projective
   spaces, $\kch(X) = \chitop(X)/24$; this is the BCOV 1993/1994
   tree-level genus-$1$ free-energy identity $F_1 = -\chitop/24$ with
   the normalisation $\kch := -24\,F_1$. Verified at quintic
   ($-200/24 = -25/3$), $\bP^5[3,3]$ ($-144/24=-6$), $\bP^5[2,4]$
   ($-176/24=-22/3$). No pretence of holomorphic modular lift; these
   $\kch$-values are non-positive and non-integral, correctly flagged
   as outside the BKM window at locus (B).
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(local CY$_3$ over compact surface $S$).}
   For $X=\mathrm{Tot}(K_S\to S)$, the correct read is
   $\kch^{\mathrm{loc}}(X) = \chitop(S)/2$ (surface-reduced). Resolved
   conifold $S=\bP^1\times\bP^1$ broken to a single curve gives
   $\kch=1$; applying $/24$ to the $\chitop$ of the total space produces
   the irrelevant number $1/12$.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(compact CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0}\geq 1$).}
   For K3-fibered $X\to E$ with $\chitop(X) = \chitop(K3)\cdot \chitop(E)=0$,
   BCOV 1993 Eq.~(4.14) corrects the tree-level identity by a
   \emph{Ray-Singer holomorphic-torsion} contribution
   $\tau_{\mathrm{BCOV}}(X)$ that does not vanish with $\chitop$.
   The invariant fed to the chiral side is the full BCOV anomaly
   polynomial, whose integral over $X$ reconstitutes
   $\kch(K3\times E) = 3$ from the $E$-holomorphic-torsion row, not
   from the naive $\chitop/24 = 0$.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(lower $d$, no BCOV read at all).}
   At $d=1$: $\kch(E) = 1 = h^{0,1}(E)$ is the Hodge-supertrace
   identification on compact CY$_1$. At $d=2$: $\kch(K3) = 12$ is the
   Mukai-lattice rank divided by $2$ (rank 24 Mukai pairing), also
   the $c/2$ for the chiral-side Heisenberg twist at $c=24$. The formula
   $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$ is a $d=3$ compact-B-model identity; it
   has no domain of definition at $d=1,2$.
\end{itemize}
The claim is thus ``$\chitop/24 = \kch$ only in the compact-smooth-CY$_3$
B-model scope; elsewhere the invariant categorising the chiral side is
the BCOV-corrected Ray-Singer torsion integral, the surface-reduced
$\chitop(S)/2$ on local toric CY$_3$, or a Hodge supertrace at
$d\neq 3$.'' The categorical nature is the claim that $\kch$ is built
from the bar complex of the CY-side chiral algebra $A_X := \Phi(X)$,
which in each case reproduces the correct dimension-$d$-specific
formula. Not every CY reads as $/24$; the ones that do are a scope.

\paragraph{OPE derivation (K3 $\times E$).} The chiral-side computation:
the bar complex $B^\bullet(A_{K3\times E})$ has genus-$1$ obstruction

\subsection*{Cycle 2: ``modular characteristic is categorical'' vs
``modular characteristic is chiral-algebraic''}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{ATTACK.} The section heading at locus (B) asserts that
``the modular characteristic is categorical.'' A hostile reader
parses this as: $\kch$ is recovered from the derived category
$D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ alone, independent of the chiral algebra
$A_X = \Phi(X)$. But the five-path verification list
(\texttt{wn:prop:categorical-euler}) names (a) Igusa weight,
(b) DMVV exponent, (c) bar Euler product, (d) relative DT,
(e) worldsheet anomaly. Four of the five are chiral-side (bar complex,
relative chiral, worldsheet). Only (d) passes through DT, which is
B-side categorical. So the heading oversells categoricality: the
mathematics is \emph{chiral-algebraic} with one categorical consistency
check, not categorical with one chiral consistency check.

Second attack: ``categorical Euler characteristic'' $\chi^{\CY}$ is
introduced at \texttt{wn:prop:categorical-euler} as the invariant
controlling $\kch$, with $\chi^{\CY}(K3\times E)=5$. No definition is
given on that line. The cache (row \#1) documents that $\kch$-labels
split into
$\kch^{\mathrm{cpt}}=\chitop/24$ vs $\kch^{\mathrm{loc}}=\chitop(S)/2$;
introducing a third label $\chi^{\CY}$ without tying it to one of these
creates latent AP113 exposure.

\paragraph{HEAL.} The correct relationship: $\kch$ is an
$A_\infty$-algebraic invariant of the chiral algebra $A_X$, computed
as the leading coefficient of $\log \chi_{\mathrm{bar}}(q)$ where
$\chi_{\mathrm{bar}}(q) = \sum_k (-1)^k \mathrm{ch}(B^k(A_X))$. The
categorical content is the identification of this bar invariant with
a Hodge supertrace on the $\CY_d$-category $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ at
smooth compact $X$, as proved in \texttt{cy\_d\_kappa\_stratification.tex}
Theorem \texttt{thm:hodge-supertrace-compact}:
\[
  \kch(A_X) \;=\; \sum_q (-1)^q\, h^{0,q}(X)
  \quad(\text{compact smooth CY}_d).
\]
On $K3\times E$: $h^{0,0}=h^{0,3}=1$, $h^{0,1}=h^{0,2}=1$, Hodge
supertrace $= 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 = 0$. This is $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3\times E}) =
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3})\chi(\mathcal{O}_E) = 2\cdot 0 = 0 = \kcat$, not
$\kBKM=5$ nor $\kch=3$. So the Hodge supertrace reads
$\kcat$, \emph{not} the $\kBKM$ or $\kch$-BKM-window values. The
remaining numerical gap ($5$ and $3$) comes from the BKM extension
via the Borcherds lift and from the relative-chiral-algebra depth
upgrade respectively; neither is visible to
$D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3\times E)$ alone.

The categorical story is therefore the exact opposite of the heading:
$\kcat$ is the purely categorical read, equal to $0$ on $K3\times E$,
distinct from both $\kBKM=5$ and $\kch=3$. The \emph{chiral} invariants
$\kBKM$ and $\kch$ require the chiral algebra $A_{K3\times E}$ (with
its factorisation over $E$ and its Heisenberg-fibre rank), not the
derived category alone.

Reformulation: ``the modular characteristic is chiral-algebraic, not
categorical; on compact smooth CY$_d$ its Hodge-supertrace shadow is
$\kcat = \chi(\mathcal{O}_X)$, multiplicative under Künneth; the
difference $\kch - \kcat$ records the BKM / relative-chiral-algebra
depth upgrade.'' This is how the table should be re-headed.

\paragraph{A-side versus B-side Hochschild.} The $E$-direction bar
complex at locus (A) localises on $E$ (one complex direction,
$E_1$-native per Costello-Gwilliam 2017 \S5.3, cf.\ cache row 16F).
Its Hochschild homology $HH_\bullet(A_{K3\times E}) =
\mathrm{HH}_\bullet(\Phi_{K3\times E})$ is the \emph{A-side}
(factorisation-homology, $E_1$-chiral) Hochschild; this carries the
$\kch$ data. The \emph{B-side} Hochschild $HH_\bullet(D^b\mathrm{Coh}(X))
= H^\bullet(X, \Omega_X^\bullet)$ carries the $\kcat$ data, via the
HKR isomorphism $HH_n = \bigoplus_{p-q=n} H^q(X,\Omega_X^p)$; the
Euler characteristic of this reads $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X)$ by Serre
duality. The ``A-side vs B-side Hochschild'' split, not ``categorical
vs topological,'' is the correct framing of the $\kch\neq\kcat$
discrepancy.

\subsection*{Cycle 3: modularity constraints on $\kch$ (integrality,
positivity, Igusa weight match)}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{ATTACK.} Proposition \texttt{prop:modularity-constraints}
states ``$\kch \in \Z_{>0}$ for a holomorphic Siegel modular form
to exist as denominator.'' This is applied as a BKM-existence filter:
quintic ($\kch=-25/3$) and Schoen $K3\times E$-style $\chi=0$ families
are ruled out from naive BKM interpretation. The hostile read: this
treats $\kch$ and $\kBKM$ as the same number. They are not. Quintic
has $\kch = -25/3$ \emph{and} no $\kBKM$ at all (no Borcherds lift
known); the $\kch$ non-integrality is the B-side BCOV
\emph{anomaly coefficient}, not the candidate weight of a BKM
denominator. Saying the quintic is ``ruled out from naive BKM'' is
correct; saying it is ruled out \emph{because $\kch$ is non-integral}
is wrong: $\kch^{\mathrm{BCOV}}$ is \emph{never} the BKM-weight
candidate on compact CY$_3$ without a K3 fibration.

Second attack: the Schoen CY$_3$ has $\chi(X)=0$, $\kch = 0$; the
line ``rules out'' with $\kch=0$ fails integrality marginally (as $0$
\emph{is} a non-negative integer, in the wrong sign/strictness box).
The sharper criterion: $\kBKM$ must be a \emph{positive} integer
\emph{and} the Fourier coefficients of the Borcherds-lift input
$\phi$ must satisfy sign-coherence on the K3-Humbert locus. The
$\kch$-level integrality test is necessary but far from sufficient;
the programme's own N=1..6 table (Gritsenko-Nikulin 1998 Duke 94) shows
that only $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ produce rank-$3$ BKMs, and the sieve
that selects $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ is the Humbert--Heegner admissibility
(cache AP-CY142), \emph{not} $\kch>0$ integrality.

Third attack: ``row-sum vanishing of $\phizeroone$''
(\texttt{prop:modularity-constraints} sixth constraint) is a Jacobi-form
theta-decomposition identity (Eichler-Zagier 1985), not a $\kch$-level
constraint. Listing it as a ``structural constraint on $\kch$'' mixes
Jacobi-form combinatorics with chiral-algebra invariants.

\paragraph{HEAL.} The correct statement: for $X$ to admit a BKM
denominator, five separable conditions must hold, only one of which
is a $\kch$-level integrality.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(Borcherds lift input, Gritsenko
   1999 Algebra i Analiz 11 Thm 2.1).} A weak Jacobi form
   $\phi\in J^{w}_{k,m}$ of weight $k\in\{-2,0\}$ and index $m\geq 1$
   with Fourier coefficients $c(n,\ell)$ satisfying $4nm-\ell^2\geq -1$
   for non-vanishing; \emph{paramodular weight} $c(0,0)/2$ becomes the
   Siegel weight of the Borcherds lift.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(integrality of $c(0,0)/2$).} The
   Borcherds lift produces a Siegel modular form on $\mathbb H_2$; the
   weight is $c(0,0)/2\in\tfrac12\Z$. When integral, it lifts to a
   genuine Siegel-holomorphic form; at half-integral, to a
   Siegel-half-integral-weight form on the paramodular double cover.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(Gritsenko-Nikulin 1998 rank-$3$ BKM
   criterion).} For the lift to produce a rank-$3$ BKM via hyperbolic
   reflection on $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, the Humbert divisor structure
   on $\mathbb H_2$ must carry a Weyl vector $\rho_g$ with $(\rho_g,\rho_g)<0$
   and reflection sublattice of rank $3$. This selects $N_g\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(row-sum vanishing).} $\sum_\ell
   c(4nm-\ell^2) = 0$ for $n\geq 1$ is an Eichler-Zagier 1985 Thm 3.4
   Jacobi-form identity, equivalent to the absence of holomorphic
   weight-$k$, index-$m$ cusp forms in the Saito-Kurokawa lift image.
   Structural, not at the $\kch$ level.
 \item \ClaimStatusTheorem\ \emph{(Hecke eigenform at the Igusa weight).}
   $\PhiN$ at paramodular level $N$ is a Hecke eigenform for the
   paramodular Hecke algebra (Pitale-Schmidt 2014 at prime $N$,
   extended to $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ by Ibukiyama 2012).
\end{itemize}
The chain is $\phi \to c(0,0)/2 \to \PhiN \to \mathfrak{g}_{\PhiN} \to
\kBKM$; the $\kch$-level integrality is a single interior node,
not the outer filter. Reformulating
\texttt{prop:modularity-constraints} as ``positive integral $\kBKM$
is necessary for BKM existence'' is sharper than ``$\kch\in\Z_{>0}$'',
and honest about the cache row \#1 symbol split.

\paragraph{Worked Jacobi decomposition at $N=1$.} The K3 elliptic
genus $\Zell(K3;\tau,z) = 2\phizeroone(\tau,z)$ decomposes via

\[
