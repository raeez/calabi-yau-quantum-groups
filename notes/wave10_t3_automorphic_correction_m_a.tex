\section*{Automorphic correction for the $\Delta_5$ denominator algebra}

\paragraph{Type-II chamber.}
Work on the hyperbolic lattice
\[
  \LambdaIIone=2\Z f_2\oplus \Z f_3\oplus 2\Z f_{-2}.
\]
The type-II chamber has primitive real walls
\[
  \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_3=f_3,
\]
and Weyl vector
\[
  \rho=f_2-\frac12 f_3+f_{-2},
  \qquad
  (\rho,\delta_i)=-1.
\]
The symmetric Cartan matrix is
\[
  \bigl((\delta_i,\delta_j)\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq3}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
  2&-2&-2\\
  -2&2&-2\\
  -2&-2&2
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The real simple roots of the corrected BKM superalgebra are the three
walls above, and the real roots are their
\(\WII\)-orbit.

\paragraph{Coefficient normalisation.}
Put
\[
  q=e^{2\pi i z_1},\qquad r=e^{2\pi i z_2},\qquad
  s=e^{2\pi i z_3}.
\]
Write the Siegel coefficients of Igusa's square root in the theta
normalisation as
\[
  \Delta_5(Z)
  =\sum c_\Delta(n,l,m)\,
    e^{\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)} .
\]
The monic Borcherds product is
\[
  D_5(Z):=64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z)
  =
  q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
  \prod_{(n,l,m)>0}
  (1-q^nr^ls^m)^{f(nm,l)},
\]
where
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\geq0,\ l\in\Z}f(n,l)q^nr^l
\]
is the Eichler-Zagier weak Jacobi form. Thus
\[
  [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64,
  \qquad c_\Delta(1,1,1)=64.
\]
The notation separates two coefficient systems: \(c_\Delta(n,l,m)\) is
a Siegel coefficient of \(\Delta_5\), while \(f(nm,l)\) is the Jacobi
coefficient used as a product exponent.

The first Jacobi rows are
\[
  \phi_{0,1}
  =
  r^{-1}+10+r
  +q(10r^{-2}-64r^{-1}+108-64r+10r^2)+O(q^2).
\]
Consequently
\[
  f(0,-1)=f(0,1)=1,\quad f(0,0)=10,
  \quad f(1,\pm1)=-64,\quad f(1,0)=108,\quad
  f(1,\pm2)=10.
\]

\paragraph{Negative-norm correction coefficient.}
Let \(n,m,l\) be odd integers with \(n,m>0\) and \(4nm-l^2>0\), and set
\[
  a=(n-1)f_2-\frac{l-1}{2}f_3+(m-1)f_{-2}\in\LambdaIIone.
\]
Then
\[
  \rho+a=n f_2-\frac l2f_3+m f_{-2},
\]
and the Gritsenko-Nikulin additive correction coefficient is
\[
  m(a)=-\frac{c_\Delta(n,l,m)}{64}.
\]
The integrality assertion is a statement in this fixed theta
normalisation: the chamber coefficients \(c_\Delta(n,l,m)\) that enter
the additive correction are divisible by \(64\). In particular
\[
  m(0)=-\frac{c_\Delta(1,1,1)}{64}=-1.
\]
For negative-norm \(a\), the sign of \(m(a)\) is the parity datum:
positive \(m(a)\) contributes even imaginary simple roots, and negative
\(m(a)\) contributes odd imaginary simple roots with multiplicity
\(-m(a)\).

\paragraph{Isotropic correction coefficient.}
The primitive isotropic rays of the chamber boundary are generated by
\[
  2f_2,\qquad 2f_{-2},\qquad 2f_2-2f_3+2f_{-2}.
\]
For \(a_0=2f_2\), the Jacobi identity
\(\psi_{5,1/2}=\eta^9\nu_{11}\) gives
\[
  1+\frac{1}{64}\sum_{t\geq1}c_\Delta(1+2t,1,1)q^t
  =
  \prod_{k\geq1}(1-q^k)^9 .
\]
Since \(m(t a_0)=-c_\Delta(1+2t,1,1)/64\), this is exactly
\[
  1-\sum_{t\geq1}m(t a_0)q^t
  =
  \prod_{t\geq1}(1-q^t)^{\tau(t a_0)},
  \qquad
  \tau(t a_0)=9\quad(t\geq1).
\]
The \(S_3\)-symmetry of the chamber carries the same value to the other
two primitive isotropic rays. The integer \(9\) is the \(\eta^9\)
exponent in the isotropic correction. The Borcherds weight is the
separate scalar \(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\).

\paragraph{Imaginary simple roots.}
For \(a\in\LambdaIIone\cap\R_{>0}\PII\), define the even and odd
imaginary multiplicities by
\[
  \mu_{\bar 0}(a)=
  \begin{cases}
  m(a),& (a,a)<0\ \text{and}\ m(a)>0,\\
  \tau(a),& (a,a)=0\ \text{and}\ \tau(a)>0,\\
  0,& \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\]
and
\[
  \mu_{\bar 1}(a)=
  \begin{cases}
  -m(a),& (a,a)<0\ \text{and}\ m(a)<0,\\
  -\tau(a),& (a,a)=0\ \text{and}\ \tau(a)<0,\\
  0,& \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
The imaginary simple-root multisets are \(a\) with multiplicity
\(\mu_{\bar0}(a)\) in parity \(\bar0\) and \(a\) with multiplicity
\(\mu_{\bar1}(a)\) in parity \(\bar1\). This sign split is part of the
Gritsenko-Nikulin denominator algebra. The product exponent
\(f(nm,l)\) gives the signed root supermultiplicity after the BKM
presentation and its radical quotient have been imposed.

\paragraph{Denominator identity.}
The corrected Weyl denominator is
\[
\begin{aligned}
  64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)
  &=
  \sum_{w\in\WII}\det(w)\,
  \left(
  e^{-2\pi i(w\rho,z)}
  -
  \sum_{a\in\LambdaIIone\cap\R_{>0}\PII}
  m(a)e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a),z)}
  \right) \\
  &=
  e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}
  \left(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\right)^{\smult(\alpha)} .
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \operatorname{den}(\gDeltaFive)=D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
On the visible active support
\[
  \alpha(n,l,m)=2nf_2-lf_3+2mf_{-2},
\]
the signed multiplicity is
\[
  \smult\,\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5,\alpha(n,l,m)}=f(nm,l).
\]
This is the denominator-algebra statement. A compact Hall bracket or
microscopic BPS operator product requires independent source data.

\paragraph{First timelike coefficient.}
The first negative-norm additive coefficient beyond \(m(0)\) occurs at
\[
  \delta_{123}:=\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3
  =2f_2-f_3+2f_{-2}.
\]
It corresponds to \((n,l,m)=(3,3,3)\), hence
\[
  m(\delta_{123})=-\frac{c_\Delta(3,3,3)}{64}.
\]
Removing the leading monomial from \(D_5\) gives
\[
  \left[qrs\right]
  \prod_{(n,l,m)>0}
  (1-q^nr^ls^m)^{f(nm,l)}=93,
\]
so
\[
  c_\Delta(3,3,3)=64\cdot93,
  \qquad
  m(\delta_{123})=-93.
\]
The same root has signed product multiplicity \(f(1,1)=-64\). In the
Gritsenko-Nikulin target presentation this signed value is realised as
\[
  29-93=-64:
\]
there are \(29\) even presentation directions and \(93\) odd
negative-norm imaginary simple directions. The coefficient \(93\) is
therefore an additive-correction coefficient; the Jacobi product
exponent at the same signed root is \(f(1,1)=-64\).

\paragraph{Borcherds weights and catalogue separation.}
For the primitive CHL Borcherds products \(\Phi_N\), the symbol
\(c_N(0)\) is the \(\zeta^0q^0\)-coefficient of the normalised twined
K3 Jacobi input after the cusp and product normalisation are fixed. In
the standard Gritsenko-Nikulin CHL normalisation,
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))
  =(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
Borcherds' weight theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\wt(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2},
  \qquad
  (\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
  \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6))=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
At \(N=1\), \(\Phi_1=\Delta_5\) and \(c_1(0)=10\).

Let \(Y=K3\times E\). The compact total-space Hodge numbers are
\[
  (h^{0,0}(Y),h^{0,1}(Y),h^{0,2}(Y),h^{0,3}(Y))=(1,1,1,1),
\]
hence
\[
  \kCH^{\mathrm{comp}}(Y)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)=0.
\]
The categorical invariant is Kunneth-multiplicative:
\[
  \kCat(Y)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
The Heisenberg-Mukai specialisation gives
\[
  \kCH^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y)=\kCH(K3)+\kCH(E)=2+1=3.
\]
The Borcherds denominator gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=\wt(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=10/2=5,
\]
and the fibre scalar is the Mukai-lattice rank
\[
  \kFiber(Y)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=1+22+1=24.
\]
Thus
\[
  \{\kCat(Y),\kCH^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y),
  \kBKM(\Delta_5),\kFiber(Y)\}=\{0,3,5,24\},
\]
while \(\kCH^{\mathrm{comp}}(Y)=0\) records the compact Hodge-supertrace
reading. The equality \(\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\) is a Borcherds weight
identity. The additive expression
\(\kBKM=\kCH+\chi(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{fiber}})\) evaluates to left side
\(5\) and right side \(0+0\) at the compact \(N=1\) total-space
reading; the CHL ladder is governed by the Borcherds denominator
theorem.

The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue is the distinct
eight-row diagonal-divisor family indexed by triples \((t,N;k)\). Its
weights are
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
with twice-weight tuple
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The common row with the CHL normalisation is \((t,N;k)=(1,1;5)\). The
Fake-Monster product belongs to a third lattice:
\(\eta(\tau)^{-24}=q^{-1}+24+\cdots\) gives
\(\kBKM(\Phi_{12}^{\mathrm{FM}})=24/2=12\) on the orthogonal
rank-\(26\) Fake-Monster lattice, whereas the \(\Delta_5\) type-II
denominator algebra is built from the rank-\(3\) lattice \(\LambdaIIone\).

\paragraph{Hall source-recognition data.}
The automorphic theorem supplies the monic product, the corrected Weyl
sum, the signed root supermultiplicities, and the simple-imaginary-root
coefficients \(m(a)\) and \(\tau(a)\) for \(\gDeltaFive\). A compact
\(K3\times E\) Hall or BPS realisation requires source-side protected
primitive bases, parity dimensions, Hall brackets, Serre relations, a
Hopf pairing, its closed radical quotient, and PBW compatibility.

For \(N\geq2\), a CHL denominator algebra with explicit correction
coefficients requires its own Jacobi input, chamber lattice, Weyl
vector, multiplier character, product normalisation, and additive
coefficient formula. For a Gritsenko-Cl\'ery row, the proof names
the row \((t,N;k)\), the relevant cusp-weighted coefficient sum, the
multiplier system, and the row-wise denominator theorem. A comparison
using weights without these data records a numerical equality of scalar
invariants.
