\section*{Jatkar-Sen, Primitive BKM, and Gritsenko-Cl\'ery Towers}
\label{sec:two-tower-unification}

Three automorphic families use variants of the symbol $\Phi_N$.
The superscript is part of the datum:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{JS}}&=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},&
\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{JS}})&=24/(N+1)-2
=(10,6,4,2,1,0),\\
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{BKM}}&=\{1,2,3,4,6\},&
(c_N(0))_{N\in\mathcal N_{\mathrm{BKM}}}&=(10,8,6,4,2),\\
&&\bigl(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})\bigr)_{N\in\mathcal N_{\mathrm{BKM}}}
&=(5,4,3,2,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The diagonal divisor catalogue is
\[
\mathsf{GC}_{\mathrm{dd}}=
\{(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
(4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1)\},
\]
with weight row \(k=(5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1)\).
The two order towers are the Jatkar-Sen physical CHL tower and the
primitive BKM/CHL denominator tower. The second row above is the
primitive CHL constant row in BKM denominator normalisation. Their
common orders are \(\{1,2,3\}\), and their union is
\(\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,11\}\).
The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery diagonal divisor catalogue is a third,
triple-indexed family.

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic origin of the order towers]
\label{thm:two-tower-unification}
Let \(L_{\mathrm{het}}=\mathrm{II}_{6,22}\) be the Narain lattice of
the heterotic string on \(T^6\), and let
\(\widetilde H(K3,\ZZ)=H^0\oplus H^2\oplus H^4=\mathrm{II}_{4,20}\)
be the Mukai lattice.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item \textup{(Jatkar-Sen physical CHL tower.)}
The genus two dyon form for the \((K3\times T^2)/\ZZ_N\) CHL model has
\[
  N\in \{1,2,3,5,7,11\},\qquad
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{JS}})=\frac{24}{N+1}-2
  =(10,6,4,2,1,0).
\]
These are exactly the positive weight and weight zero solutions of
\(N+1\mid 24\) with \(24/(N+1)\ge 2\), with the balanced \(M_{23}\)
condition of Chaudhuri, Hockney, and Lykken.

\item \textup{(Primitive BKM/CHL denominator tower.)}
The compact \(K3\times E\) diagonal host admits an origin fixing
elliptic factor automorphism only in orders
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
For the corresponding K3 twined weak Jacobi input,
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =\frac{c_N(0)}2=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]

\item \textup{(Intersection and square at the identity.)}
The intersection of the two order towers is \(\{1,2,3\}\). At \(N=1\)
one has
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2
  =(\Phi_1^{\mathrm{BKM}})^2,\qquad
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}})=10=2\cdot 5.
\]
At \(N=2\) the JS weight is \(6\) while
\(2\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_2^{\mathrm{BKM}})=8\). At \(N=3\) the
JS weight is \(4\) while
\(2\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_3^{\mathrm{BKM}})=6\).

\item \textup{(Boundary orders.)}
The order indexed Nikulin extension has \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) with
\[
  (c_5(0),c_7(0),c_8(0))=(4,2,2),\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=(2,1,1).
\]
These rows require the extended Borcherds input and an explicitly
named host. The compact diagonal \(K3\times E\) host supplies no
origin fixing elliptic automorphism of orders \(5,7,8\).

\item \textup{(Gritsenko-Cl\'ery diagonal divisor catalogue.)}
The eight diagonal divisor forms are indexed by
\[
\mathsf{GC}_{\mathrm{dd}}=
\{(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
(4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1)\}.
\]
Their twice weight row is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2),
\]
attached to the triples above. This row is separate from the primitive
BKM constants \((10,8,6,4,2)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the JS tower, Jatkar and Sen identify the dyon partition function
with \(1/\Phi_N^{\mathrm{JS}}\), where
\(\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{JS}})=24/(N+1)-2\) on the CHL
paramodular group. The arithmetic check is immediate:
\[
  N+1\in\{2,3,4,6,8,12\}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  N\in\{1,2,3,5,7,11\},
\]
and the displayed weight row follows by substitution. The balanced
class condition is the Chaudhuri, Hockney, and Lykken \(M_{23}\)
selection recorded in Jatkar and Sen 2005, Section 2.3 and Section 3.

For the primitive BKM/CHL tower, the finite origin fixing automorphism
group of an elliptic curve is the unit group of its endomorphism
lattice. A generic elliptic curve contributes the subgroup generated
by \(-1\); the square lattice \(j=1728\) contributes order \(4\); the
hexagonal lattice \(j=0\) contributes order \(6\). Taking subgroups
gives precisely \(\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) (Silverman, Appendix A, Section 1).
For these five orders the Gritsenko and Nikulin exponential lift of
the K3 twined weight zero index one Jacobi form gives the primitive
denominator forms \(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}}\). Borcherds' weight theorem
gives
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =c_N(0)/2.
\]
Gritsenko 1999 and Gritsenko and Nikulin 1995 give
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2)
  \quad\text{for}\quad N=(1,2,3,4,6),
\]
hence \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=(5,4,3,2,1)\).

Igusa's identity at level \(1\) gives the unnormalised square
\(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2\). At \(N=2,3\), the weight
calculation separates the two families. The JS forms have weights
\((6,4)\), while the squares of the primitive BKM denominators have
weights \((8,6)\).

Mukai's symplectic K3 automorphism classification supplies the
additional orders \(5,7,8\). Gritsenko 1994 and Gritsenko with
Cl\'ery give the extended Borcherds weights
\((2,1,1)\), equivalently constants \((4,2,2)\). The obstruction at
these three orders is geometric: the elliptic factor has no origin
fixing automorphism of order \(5\), \(7\), or \(8\), so the compact
diagonal \(K3\times E\) host used by the five primitive BKM rows is
absent.

Gritsenko and Cl\'ery 2008, Theorem 1.4, classifies the diagonal
divisor forms by triples \((t,N;k)\), giving the eight row
\(\mathsf{GC}_{\mathrm{dd}}\) displayed above. The common element with
the primitive BKM tower is \(F_{1,1}^{\mathrm{GC,dd}}=\Delta_5\).
The remaining seven rows live on their own paramodular groups and
multiplier systems, with the displayed twice weight row
\((10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two actions on lattices]
\label{rem:two-m24-actions}
The JS tower is selected by the Narain lattice action
\[
  M_{24}\hookrightarrow \Aut(\mathrm{II}_{6,22})
\]
through the Leech lattice embedding and the heterotic level matching
condition. The primitive BKM/CHL tower is selected by K3 cohomology and the
elliptic factor:
\[
  M_{24}\curvearrowright \widetilde H(K3,\ZZ)=\mathrm{II}_{4,20},
  \qquad
  \ZZ_N\subset \Aut(E,0).
\]
The first selection gives \(N=\{1,2,3,5,7,11\}\). The second gives
\(N=\{1,2,3,4,6\}\). The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery diagonal divisor catalogue
is parametrised by \((t,N)\), so it enters only after the
triple-indexed row is fixed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The \(K3\times E\) invariant split]
\label{rem:k3e-invariant-split-two-tower-note}
For \(Y=K3\times E\), the four invariants are separate.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \(\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(Y)=0\), since
\(\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot 0\).
\item \(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y)=3\), since
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3)+
\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(E)=2+1\).
\item \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=5\), since
\(c_1(0)/2=10/2\).
\item \(\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}=24\), the rank of
\(\widetilde H(K3,\ZZ)\).
\end{enumerate}
The BKM branch is the Borcherds denominator and Hall-Drinfeld
comparison target. The K3 Yangian branch is the Mukai Heisenberg
stable envelope branch. The two branches remain disjoint throughout
the tower comparison.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Boundary orders \(5,7,8\)]
\label{rem:union-and-n-equal-5}
The order tower union is
\[
  \{1,2,3,4,6\}\cup\{1,2,3,5,7,11\}
  =\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,11\}.
\]
The boundary order \(N=5\) is Mukai admissible and JS selectable:
the class \(5A\) has frame shape \(1^4 5^4\), and
\(24/(5+1)-2=2\). Its extended Borcherds constant is
\(c_5(0)=4\), hence \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=2\), after the order \(5\)
Borcherds input is specified. The equality of the JS weight and the
extended BKM weight at \(N=5\) is numerical; the constructions and
groups are distinct.

The same separation holds at \(N=7\): the JS weight is \(1\), and the
extended BKM constant \(c_7(0)=2\) gives \(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=1\).
At \(N=8\), the extended BKM constant is \(c_8(0)=2\), giving
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=1\), while the JS tower has no order \(8\)
entry.
\end{remark}

\begin{problem}[Boundary source packages]
\label{prob:boundary-source-packages-two-tower-note}
For \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\), construct a compact source package carrying the
specified extended Borcherds input, identify the automorphic group of
the denominator, and build the Hall-Drinfeld comparison object. The
data must reproduce \(c_N(0)=(4,2,2)\) and
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}=(2,1,1)\) without using the compact diagonal
\(K3\times E\) host.
\end{problem}
