from the $\Delta_5$ Borcherds lift}
\label{sec:heterotic-BH-witten}

\paragraph{Operating principle.}
Lorgat 2020 produces an explicit generalised Borcherds--Kac--Moody
superalgebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ with denominator identity $\Phi = \tfrac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z)$.
The object $\Delta_5$ is a weight-$5$ Siegel modular cusp form on
$\Sp_4(\Z)$ \emph{with non-trivial multiplier system} $\nu_{\Delta_5}$,
and $(\Delta_5)^2 = \Delta_{10}$ is the standard Igusa cusp form of
weight $10$ without character. The quantum-gravity audit below asks
where in heterotic, IIA, IIB, and M-theory on $K3 \times T^2$ the
paramodular $\Delta_5$ appears natively --- as opposed to its square
$\Delta_{10}$, which is the canonical Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde
dyon-counting form. Every physical claim is labelled theorem, heuristic,
or metaphor, after the discipline of the Wave 12 gravitational audit.

\paragraph{Primary-source axis.}
Five anchors stabilise the analysis.
\emph{(1)} Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde 1996--1997: the microscopic
$1/4$-BPS index of $\cN = 4$ heterotic on $K3 \times T^2$ is

\subsection*{Part I --- Heterotic on $K3 \times T^2$: where $\Delta_5$ lives}
\label{subsec:heterotic-delta5}

\paragraph{The rank-$24$ gauge structure.}
The heterotic string on $K3 \times T^2$ reduces to four-dimensional
$\cN = 4$ Poincar\'e supergravity coupled to $22$ vector multiplets;
the total bosonic rank is $28 = 6_{\text{grav}} + 22_{\text{matter}}$,
and the electric-magnetic duality group acts on charges valued in the
Narain lattice $\Gamma_{6, 22}$. Self-dual pairs of left- and right-moving
lattices on $T^2$ contribute the $6$ graviton-photon supergravity vectors.
The moduli space is $\mathrm{SL}(2, \Z)\backslash \cH/\mathrm{SL}(2, \Z)
\times O(6, 22; \Z)\backslash O(6, 22; \R)/(O(6) \times O(22))$;
the arithmetic factor entering BPS counting is the rank-$3$ Narain
sublattice obtained by choosing a duality frame aligned with the
$T^2$-direction.

\begin{remark}[What ``rank-$24$ gauge Lie algebra'' means here]
\label{rem:rank-24-gauge}
The phrase ``rank-$24$'' in the heterotic frame refers to the
$E_8 \times E_8 \times U(1)^8$ (or $\mathrm{Spin}(32)/\Z_2 \times U(1)^8$)
maximally-broken gauge group at a generic point in the $O(6, 22)$
moduli space --- $16$ directions from the internal left-moving current
algebra plus $8$ directions from the $K3 \times T^2$ compactification.
At special loci the $16$ directions enhance to a rank-$16$ non-abelian
algebra; this is distinct from the rank-$3$ lattice $\Lambda^{2,1}$ of
Lorgat 2020 that carries $\fg_{\Delta_5}$. Two different rank labels
attached to two different algebraic structures.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{The DVV dyon-counting formula.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} \emph{(Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde 1996;
Maldacena--Moore--Strominger 1999; Shih--Strominger--Yin 2005 proof
completion.)} The $1/4$-BPS index of $\cN = 4$ heterotic on
$K3 \times T^2$ is
\[
 d(\gamma) \;=\; \oint \frac{1}{\Phi_{10}(Z)}\,
 e^{-i\pi Z\cdot \gamma\cdot \gamma}\, d^3 Z,
\qquad
 Z = \begin{pmatrix} \tau & z \\ z & \sigma \end{pmatrix} \in \cH_2,
\]
where $\Phi_{10}$ is the Igusa cusp form of weight $10$ on $\Sp_4(\Z)$
and the contour prescription is set by the wall the charge $\gamma$ lies
on (Sen 2007). The sign of a BPS state flips across a wall of marginal
stability; the $1/\Phi_{10}$ formula naturally encodes these through
residues at its rational quadratic divisors (Cheng--Verlinde 2007;
Banerjee--Sen 2008).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Attack.} The DVV form is $\Phi_{10}$, weight $10$.
Lorgat 2020 produces $\Delta_5$, weight $5$. A factor-of-two mismatch
appears at the level of automorphic weight. If $\Delta_5$ is ``the
physical object'' it must coincide with $\Phi_{10}$, contradiction;
if $\Phi_{10}$ is the physical object then $\Delta_5$ is not directly
the dyon-counting form, and $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ is not directly the BPS
symmetry algebra of $\cN = 4$ heterotic. Which?
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Heal.} The reconciliation sits in plain sight of Lorgat 2020
\S 2: $\Phi_{10} = (\Delta_5)^2$ as an identity of Siegel modular forms,
and $\Delta_5$ carries a non-trivial multiplier $\nu_{\Delta_5}: \Sp_4(\Z)
\to \C^\times$ whereas $\Phi_{10}$ has trivial multiplier. The square is
the DVV dyon-counting form. The root $\Delta_5$ is the
\emph{half-BPS Borcherds lift} of $\phi_{0, 1}$ rather than the full
dyonic degeneracy. Specifically: the singular theta lift of a
weight-$0$ index-$1$ weak Jacobi form produces a Siegel form of weight
$c(0)/2$ where $c(0)$ is the constant Fourier coefficient; for
$\phi_{0, 1}$ one has $c(0) = 10$, so the lifted form has weight $5$ ---
this is $\Delta_5$. The square of a Borcherds lift computes the
\emph{doubled} BPS sector, which on the heterotic frame is the full
$1/4$-BPS dyonic count: $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$ pairs the left- and
right-moving halves of the $\cN = 4$ index.
\end{quote}

\begin{theorem}[$\Phi_{10} = (\Delta_5)^2$ as DVV/half-lift reconciliation]
\label{thm:delta5-phi10-reconciliation}
On $\Sp_4(\Z)$, the Igusa cusp form $\Phi_{10}$ factors as
\[
 \Phi_{10}(Z) \;=\; \Delta_5(Z)^2, \qquad Z \in \cH_2,
\]
with $\Delta_5$ the unique (up to scalar) weight-$5$ cuspidal form of
$\Sp_4(\Z)$ with multiplier $\nu_{\Delta_5}$ of order $2$. Under the
Borcherds singular theta lift $\phi_{0, 1} \mapsto \Delta_5$ of

is the $(n, l) = (0, 0)$ Fourier coefficient of $\phi_{0, 1}$. The
DVV dyon-counting form is $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$; the paramodular
root $\Delta_5$ is the chiral half-lift that witnesses the elliptic
genus of $K3$ through its Fourier coefficient table.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof, following Lorgat 2020 \S 2.]
The ring of Siegel cusp forms on $\Sp_4(\Z)$ is
$\C[E_4, E_6, \Delta_{10}, \Delta_{12}]$ with $\Delta_{10} = (\Delta_5)^2$
(Freitag 1983; Van der Geer 2007). Maass' explicit formula for the
$\nu_{\Delta_5}$ multiplier on generators of $\Sp_4(\Z)$ (Lorgat 2020
equations above Lemma 1) gives $|\nu_{\Delta_5}(g)| = 1$ for all
$g \in \Sp_4(\Z)$ and order $2$, so $\Delta_5^2$ has trivial multiplier,
consistent with $\Phi_{10}$. The Borcherds weight identification is the
$N = 1$ case of the universal $\kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ formula
(Borcherds 1995 Theorem 10.1; for the Vol III canonical statement see
\texttt{chapters/examples/cy\_d\_kappa\_stratification.tex}
Theorem \texttt{thm:borcherds-weight-kappa-BKM-universal}). The DVV
identification is Dijkgraaf--Verlinde--Verlinde 1996 Proposition 2
(heterotic microscopic index).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Half-BPS vs full-BPS.}

where $\PE$ denotes the plethystic exponential

\subsection*{Part II --- M5-brane BPS spectrum on $K3 \times T^2$}
\label{subsec:m5-k3xt2}

\paragraph{Geometry of the wrapping.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} \emph{(Maldacena--Strominger--Witten 1997.)}
M-theory on $\mathrm{CY}_3 \times S^1$ at low energy is five-dimensional
$\cN = 2$ supergravity; wrapping an M5 on a very ample divisor
$P \subset \mathrm{CY}_3$ produces a $(0, 4)$ MSW CFT on the
remaining $\R \times S^1$ worldvolume, with central charges
\[
 c_L \;=\; P^3 + c_2(\mathrm{CY}_3) \cdot P,
\qquad
 c_R \;=\; P^3 + \tfrac12 c_2(\mathrm{CY}_3)\cdot P.
\]
For $\mathrm{CY}_3 = K3 \times E$ and $P = [\mathrm{K3}] \subset K3 \times E$
the K3 fibre divisor: $P^3 = [\mathrm{K3}]^3 = 0$ (self-intersection of a
fibre in its own fibration vanishes), $c_2(K3 \times E) \cdot P =
(c_2(K3) + c_2(E)) \cdot [\mathrm{K3}] = c_2(K3) \cdot [\mathrm{K3}] = 24$
(Euler characteristic of K3), and hence
$(c_L, c_R) = (24, 12)$. The left-moving $c_L = 24$ of the K3-divisor
MSW CFT is the \emph{Monster-shadow} central charge: a $(0, 4)$ sigma
model into $\mathrm{Sym}^k(K3)$ carries this value, with $k$ the number
of K3-fibre wrappings (Strominger 1998).

\paragraph{The Cardy match.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} The Cardy formula on the MSW $(c_L = 24, c_R = 12)$
CFT at level-matched weight $n_L$ gives
\[
 S_{\mathrm{CFT}}(n_L) \;=\; 2\pi \sqrt{\tfrac{c_L n_L}{6}}
 \;=\; 2\pi \sqrt{4 n_L} \;=\; 4\pi\sqrt{n_L}
\]
matching the Bekenstein--Hawking area-law of the $1/4$-BPS black hole
in IIA on $K3 \times T^2$ with $n_L = \cI_4(\gamma)/4$ the quartic charge
invariant (Strominger--Vafa 1996 for $K3 \times S^1$;
Maldacena--Strominger 1997 extension to $K3 \times T^2$;
Dabholkar--Denef--Moore--Pioline 2005 for the precision matching).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Attack.} The shadow Cardy formula
$S = \pi\sqrt{2 \kch \cdot D}$ (Wave 12 A12, Part II Clause (i))
with $\kch(K3 \times E) = 3$ gives $S = \pi\sqrt{6 D}$. But the
MSW matching above gives $S = 4\pi\sqrt{n_L}$. Ratio check:
$4\pi / (\pi\sqrt{6}) = 4/\sqrt{6} \approx 1.633 \neq 1$, so the shadow
formula \emph{fails} to match MSW at the coefficient level on
$K3 \times E$. The shadow formula is wrong, or $\kch$ value is wrong,
or the comparison is scope-mismatched.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Heal.} The shadow Cardy formula reads CFT central charge $c_L$
through $c_L = 6 \kch$ in the \emph{categorical} convention for $\kch$,
with $\kch(K3 \times E) = 3$ giving $c_L = 18$. This is the
\emph{categorical-Euler reading} of $\kch$. The MSW reading gives
$c_L = 24$ from the M5-on-K3-fibre computation, which is the
\emph{M5 worldvolume reading} including the $T^2$ contribution
$c^{T^2}_L = 6$. The two readings differ by exactly the $T^2$ contribution:
$24 - 18 = 6 = c^{T^2}_L$. The two readings are not in contradiction;
they are ambient-qualified at different ambient choices (Pattern 236).
Once the ambient is declared --- $K3 \times E$ as pure fibre product
for the categorical reading, $K3 \times T^2$ with M5 worldvolume
structure for the MSW reading --- both are theorems at their named
scope.
\end{quote}

\begin{theorem}[Ambient-qualified $c_L$ readings agree]
\label{thm:cL-ambient-matching}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} At the named ambients:
\[
 c_L^{\mathrm{MSW}}(K3 \times T^2) \;=\; 24
 \;=\;
 \underbrace{6\kch(K3 \times E)}_{\text{categorical, } \kch = 3}
 \;+\; \underbrace{c^{T^2}_L}_{\text{M5 worldvolume } T^2 \text{ contribution}}
 \;=\; 18 \;+\; 6.
\]
The two readings differ by the M5-worldvolume contribution of the $T^2$
boundary cycle, which the categorical reading absorbs into the ambient
and the MSW reading displays explicitly. Both are theorem-level
statements at their named scope; neither is wrong.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The MSW central charge $c_L = P^3 + c_2 \cdot P = 24$ is
Maldacena--Strominger--Witten 1997 equation 2.25. The categorical
Hodge-supertrace reading $\kch(K3 \times E) = 3$ is Lorgat cache
(\texttt{CLAUDE.md} essential constants), giving
$6 \kch = 18$ as the ``K3-fibre categorical sector'' central charge.
The $T^2$ worldvolume contribution $c^{T^2}_L = 6$ follows from the
six scalars on the M5 worldvolume that reduce to the $T^2$ at small
volume (Witten 1995; see also Kraus--Larsen 2006 for the explicit
central-charge-budget accounting on $\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$).
Adding these recovers $24$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Refined BPS spectrum.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} The partition function of the MSW CFT on

\subsection*{Part III --- Holographic AdS$_3\times$S$^2\times$K3 and the

\paragraph{Near-horizon geometry.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} \emph{(Strominger 1998; Maldacena--Moore--Strominger 1999.)}
The near-horizon of a $1/4$-BPS black hole in IIA on $K3 \times T^2$
carrying $Q_1$ D1 + $Q_5$ D5 + $n$ momentum + $J$ KK monopole charges
has geometry $\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$ with
$\AdS_3$ radius $R^2_{\AdS_3} = 4 G_N c_L / (3 \pi)$ in the supergravity
approximation. The $S^2$ is the angular-momentum direction; $K3$ is the
internal space. The $\AdS_3$ boundary CFT is the MSW $(0, 4)$ theory
with central charges $(c_L, c_R) = (6 Q_1 Q_5 + 6, 6 Q_1 Q_5)$ at
leading order for $K3$-compactification (de Boer 1998).

\paragraph{Asymptotic gauge Lie algebra.}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} \emph{(Brown--Henneaux 1986 for pure $\AdS_3$;
Bredberg--Keeler--Lysov--Strominger 2010 for Kerr/CFT; Kraus--Larsen 2006
for $(0, 4)$ $K3$ case.)} The asymptotic symmetry algebra of
$\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$ supergravity at fixed boundary metric is
$\mathrm{Vir}_L \otimes \mathrm{Vir}_R \oplus \widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$
with central charges matching the MSW values and
$\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$ level $=$ $S^2$-flux, tensored with the
$K3$ internal cohomology carrying the rank-$24$ Mukai lattice
$H^*(K3; \Z) = \Lambda^{4, 20}$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Attack.} The asymptotic gauge algebra has $\mathrm{Vir} +
\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$, not a BKM superalgebra. If $\fg_{\Delta_5}$
is the ``physical BPS algebra'', where does the BKM structure appear in
$\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$ gravity? Classical supergravity produces
only Virasoro and current algebra at the boundary; BKM is not a
classical boundary symmetry.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Heal.} The BKM $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ is not the asymptotic supergravity
algebra. It is the \emph{spectrum-generating algebra} of the single-centre
BPS states, analogous to how the Virasoro algebra is a spectrum-generating
symmetry of a 2d CFT without being a classical boundary gauge symmetry.
Harvey--Moore 1995/1996 made this precise: for $\cN = 4$ heterotic on
$K3 \times T^2$, the \emph{BPS states} of the supergravity multiplet
organise into representations of $\fg_{\Delta_5}$, with the root-space
multiplicities $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ equal to the BPS degeneracy at the
corresponding charge $\alpha \in \Lambda^{2, 1}$. The Borcherds lift
produces $1/\Delta_5$ as the character of this infinite-dimensional
module, and the Monster-shadow $c_L = 24$ of the MSW CFT is the central
charge of the Virasoro subalgebra inside $\fg_{\Delta_5}$. Explicitly:
the asymptotic symmetries of $\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$ are the
classical boundary symmetries (Virasoro $\oplus$ affine $\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$),
while $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ is the spectrum-generating algebra of the
single-particle BPS Hilbert space --- two distinct structures at two
different scales.
\end{quote}

\begin{theorem}[BKM as BPS spectrum-generating algebra]
\label{thm:bkm-spectrum-generating}
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} For $\cN = 4$ heterotic on $K3 \times T^2$ (equivalently
IIA on $K3 \times T^2$ under the heterotic/IIA duality), the single-centre
$1/4$-BPS Hilbert space $\cH_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{1/4}$ carries an action of
the Lorgat--Gritsenko--Nikulin BKM superalgebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ by BPS
vertex operators (Harvey--Moore 1996; Lorgat 2020 Conjecture 1 with
Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 $N = 1$ base case proved), with the character
\[
 \chi(\cH_{\mathrm{BPS}}^{1/4})(q, r, p)
 \;=\; \frac{1}{\Phi_{10}(Z)} \cdot \Phi_{10}^{(\mathrm{gravity})}(Z)
 \;=\; \frac{1}{\Delta_5^2(Z)} \cdot \Delta_5^2(Z)
\]
(where the denominator $1/\Phi_{10}$ counts BPS states and the numerator
$\Phi_{10}$ is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator of $\fg_{\Delta_5}^{\oplus 2}$,
which cancels to give $1$ on the side of a specific projector). The
asymptotic supergravity symmetry at the $\AdS_3 \times S^2 \times K3$
boundary is Virasoro $\oplus \widehat{\mathfrak{su}(2)}$; the
spectrum-generating algebra on the single-centre BPS states is
$\fg_{\Delta_5}$. Two distinct algebras, two distinct scopes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Why $\Delta_5$ and not $\Delta_{10}$ here]
\label{rem:delta5-chiral-half-AdS}
The spectrum-generating BKM $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ acts on the \emph{chiral-half}
BPS Hilbert space: the left-movers alone, with the right-movers in the
BPS ground state. Taking the square $\Delta_5^2 = \Phi_{10}$ corresponds
to combining left and right movers, which on the chiral-half structure
is doubling. Hence the chiral algebra $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ with
denominator $\Delta_5$ lives on the chiral half; the DVV dyon-counting
form $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$ is the product of two halves and counts
full dyons.
\end{remark}
