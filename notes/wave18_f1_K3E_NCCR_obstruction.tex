\section*{Finite quiver obstruction for $K3\times E$}
\label{sec:K3E-NCCR}

Let \(S\) be a smooth projective K3 surface, let \(E\) be a smooth
elliptic curve, and set
\[
  X=S\times E .
\]
Van den Bergh's non-commutative crepant resolution is formulated over
an affine singular centre: for a normal Gorenstein affine domain
\(R\), an \(\NCCR\) is an \(R\)-algebra
\(\Lambda=\End_R(M)\), with \(M\) reflexive, such that \(\Lambda\) is
Cohen-Macaulay over \(R\) and has finite global dimension
(Van den Bergh 2004, Definition~4.1).  The threefold \(X\) is smooth
and compact, with \(H^0(X,\cO_X)=\mathbb C\), while Van den Bergh's
definition belongs to affine singular centres.  The finite-model
question is whether the compact category admits one ordinary finite
chart:
\[
  \Perf(X)\simeq \Perf(\Lambda),
  \qquad
  \Lambda \hbox{ a single finite quiver or Jacobi algebra.}
\]
A chart finite over the compact centre \(H^0(X,\cO_X)=\mathbb C\)
would give a finite-dimensional algebra over \(\mathbb C\).  Under any
such equivalence, the free \(\Lambda\)-module maps to a classical
tilting object of \(\Perf(X)\).  Serre duality gives a nonzero top
self-\(\Ext\) group for every such object.

\subsection*{The Serre obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[Serre obstruction to classical tilting]
\label{prop:serre-tilting-obstruction-cy}
Let \(Y\) be a smooth proper Calabi-Yau variety of dimension \(d>0\)
over \(\mathbb C\).  Every nonzero object \(T\in\Perf(Y)\) satisfies
\[
  \Ext_Y^d(T,T)\neq 0.
\]
Consequently such a \(T\) violates
\[
  \Ext_Y^i(T,T)=0\qquad (i>0).
\]
Classical tilting objects are therefore absent from \(\Perf(Y)\).  A
presentation by a finite-dimensional ordinary algebra of finite global
dimension would require the free module to map to a classical tilting
generator of \(\Perf(Y)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Serre functor on \(\Perf(Y)\) is the shift \([d]\), since
\(\omega_Y\simeq\cO_Y\).  Hence
\[
  \Ext_Y^d(T,T)
  =
  \Hom_Y(T,T[d])
  \cong
  \Hom_Y(T,T)^\vee .
\]
The identity of a nonzero object gives a nonzero element of
\(\Hom_Y(T,T)\).  Thus \(\Ext_Y^d(T,T)\neq 0\).  A tilting object would
require all positive self-Ext groups to vanish, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[K3, elliptic, and product cases]
\label{cor:k3-e-product-tilting-obstruction}
Classical tilting objects are absent from
\(\Perf(S)\), \(\Perf(E)\), and \(\Perf(S\times E)\).  In the product
case the Serre functor is \([3]\), and every nonzero
\(T\in\Perf(S\times E)\) has
\[
  \Ext^3_{S\times E}(T,T)\cong \Hom_{S\times E}(T,T)^\vee\neq 0 .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:serre-tilting-obstruction-cy} in dimensions
\(2\), \(1\), and \(3\).
\end{proof}

The Mukai lattice
\[
  \widetilde H(S,\mathbb Z)\cong U^4\oplus E_8(-1)^2
\]
is a rank-\(24\) lattice datum controlling Fourier-Mukai symmetries,
spherical classes, and the K3 Heisenberg branch.  The tilting
argument keeps this lattice separate from ordinary quiver data for
\(\Perf(S)\).  The Poincar\'e bundle \(\cP\) on
\(E\times\widehat E\) gives Mukai's Fourier-Mukai equivalence
\[
  \Phi_{\cP}\colon \Db(\Coh E)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\Coh \widehat E),
  \qquad \widehat E\simeq E,
\]
and Atiyah's classification organises vector bundles on \(E\) by
rank and degree.  These structures are autoequivalence and moduli
structures.  Serre duality rules out their use as ordinary tilting
presentations.

\subsection*{Tensor product of the derived categories}

The dg category of perfect complexes on the product is the tensor
product of the factors:
\[
  \Perf(S\times E)\simeq \Perf(S)\otimes_{\mathbb C}\Perf(E).
\]
This dg tensor product is smooth and proper, with Serre functor
\([3]\).  A finite ordinary algebra model would have to supply a
classical tilting object.  If an ordinary algebra \(\Lambda\) of finite
global dimension and an equivalence
\[
  \Perf(S\times E)\simeq \Perf(\Lambda)
\]
existed, the image of \(\Lambda\) would be a nonzero
\(T\in\Perf(S\times E)\) with \(\Ext^i(T,T)=0\) for \(i>0\).
Corollary~\ref{cor:k3-e-product-tilting-obstruction}
rules out such a \(T\).

\subsection*{Local ADE singular charts}

Local NCCRs appear on singular K3 degenerations and orbifold strata.
Let \(\Gamma\subset \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)\) be a finite ADE subgroup,
acting trivially on a formal coordinate \(t\) along \(E\), and set
\[
  R_{\Gamma,E}
  =
  \mathbb C[[u,v,t]]^\Gamma .
\]
For the sum \(M=\bigoplus_{\rho\in \widehat\Gamma} M_\rho\) of the
indecomposable reflexive \(R_{\Gamma,E}\)-modules attached to the
irreducible \(\Gamma\)-representations, the McKay algebra
\[
  \Lambda_{\Gamma,E}
  =
  \End_{R_{\Gamma,E}}(M)
  \cong
  \mathbb C[[u,v,t]]\rtimes \Gamma
\]
is Cohen-Macaulay over \(R_{\Gamma,E}\) and has finite global
dimension \(3\).  This is the ADE NCCR stretched along the elliptic
direction.  Bridgeland-King-Reid identify the corresponding derived
McKay category for quotient singularities, and Van den Bergh's
criterion gives the NCCR interpretation.

These algebras are centred on formal singular charts.  A smooth K3
surface has smooth analytic neighbourhoods in place of ADE quotient
points, and a compact product \(S\times E\) lies outside the global
affine-centre setting whose endomorphism algebra is the above
\(\Lambda_{\Gamma,E}\).  A descent of the ADE algebras to one global
ordinary quiver model would produce the tilting object ruled out by
Corollary~\ref{cor:k3-e-product-tilting-obstruction}.

\subsection*{Spherical transitions}

The obstruction is visible on overlaps.  A spherical object
\(F\in\Db(\Coh S)\), for instance the sheaf attached to a \((-2)\)-curve
in an ADE degeneration, satisfies
\[
  \RHom_S(F,F)\simeq \mathbb C\oplus \mathbb C[-2]
\]
and produces the Seidel-Thomas spherical twist.  Pulling to \(X\)
and tensoring with \(\cO_E\) gives
\[
  \RHom_X(\mathrm{pr}_S^*F,\mathrm{pr}_S^*F)
  \simeq
  \bigl(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C[-2]\bigr)
  \otimes
  H^\bullet(E,\cO_E),
\]
with cohomology in positive degrees.  Thus the transition datum of an
ADE atlas is intrinsically dg or \(A_\infty\).  Degree-zero Morita
gluing for ordinary quiver algebras forgets the spherical class.

\subsection*{Quivers with potential and CoHA}

Kontsevich-Soibelman's critical CoHA begins with a chosen CY$_3$
Jacobi algebra
\[
  A=\Jac(Q,W).
\]
For the toric germ \(\mathbb C^3\), the one-vertex three-loop
potential gives
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)=Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
the positive half of the affine Yangian.  The full
\(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) object is reached only after imposing the
double, centre, completion, or evaluation layer appropriate to the
chosen Hall realisation.  The compact threefold
\(S\times E\) belongs to reduced \(K3\)-fibre Donaldson-Thomas theory:
compact moduli, orientation data, stability conditions, the Mukai
lattice, and the Borcherds denominator enter before any Hall product is
global.  A single global quiver with potential must first overcome the
tilting obstruction above.

Since \(X\) has Calabi-Yau dimension \(3\), the native specialised
\(\Phi\)-output is \(E_1\)-chiral.  \(E_2\)-structures occur at the
Drinfeld centre, Drinfeld double, or module layer associated to that
\(E_1\)-object.  The Hall-Drinfeld/Borcherds denominator branch,
represented at level one by \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\), is separate
from the K3 Mukai self-mirror Yangian branch governed by rank-\(24\)
Mukai parameters and Maulik-Okounkov stable envelopes.  Compact
coherent geometry, local quotient \(\NCCR\) charts, the Hall positive
half, and the automorphic BKM denominator are four different layers.

\subsection*{Obstruction theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite quiver obstruction and conditional substitute for \(K3\times E\)]
\label{thm:finite-quiver-obstruction-K3E}
For \(X=S\times E\), with \(S\) a smooth projective K3 surface and
\(E\) a smooth elliptic curve, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(i)}] Classical tilting objects are absent in
\(\Perf(X)\).  Consequently an ordinary finite-dimensional quiver
presentation by a single degree-zero endomorphism algebra of finite
global dimension would fail to supply the required classical tilting
generator.
\item[\textup{(ii)}] ADE degenerations of the K3 factor have local
Van den Bergh NCCRs
\(\Lambda_{\Gamma,E}\cong \mathbb C[[u,v,t]]\rtimes\Gamma\).
\item[\textup{(iii)}] The local ADE NCCRs remain local formal
singularity-chart models. Any single global ordinary
quiver-with-potential model for \(\Perf(X)\) compatible with the
derived category would
contradict the Serre-duality obstruction in (i).
\item[\textup{(iv)}] Any finite substitute compatible with the local
quotient charts, the Serre functor, and compact Hall integration must
be supplied as a Serre-equivariant quasi-\(\NCCR\) datum.  After
orientation, compact comparison, and centre data are supplied, its
reduced Hall/DT shadow is a scalar character.  An algebra isomorphism
requires separate recognition data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clause~(i) is Corollary~\ref{cor:k3-e-product-tilting-obstruction}.
Clause~(ii) is the ADE McKay construction over
\(\mathbb C[[u,v,t]]^\Gamma\), with \(\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2\)
acting trivially on \(t\).  Clause~(iii) follows because an ordinary
global quiver-with-potential model equivalent to \(\Perf(X)\) would
carry the free rank-one module to a tilting object of \(\Perf(X)\).
Clause~(i) rules out such an object.  The Seidel-Thomas spherical
classes above exhibit the dg transition datum that survives in finite
atlases.  Clause~(iv) records the data required for such an atlas:
local tilting or McKay charts glued by exact wall-crossing functors,
with the compact Hall character defined only after the data in
Definition~\ref{def:serre-qnccr-k3e} are fixed.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Serre-equivariant quasi-\(\NCCR\)}

\begin{definition}[Serre-equivariant quasi-\(\NCCR\) datum]
\label{def:serre-qnccr-k3e}
A finite Serre-equivariant quasi-\(\NCCR\) datum on \(X=S\times E\)
consists of a finite wall atlas \(\mathfrak U=\{U_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in I}\)
in the stability space of \(\Perf(X)\), full pretriangulated dg charts
\(\mathcal C_\alpha\subset\Perf(X)\), bounded hearts
\(\mathcal A_\alpha\subset\mathcal C_\alpha\), local tilting
generators \(T_\alpha\in\mathcal A_\alpha\), algebras
\[
  \Lambda_\alpha=\End_{\mathcal C_\alpha}(T_\alpha),
\]
exact equivalences
\[
  F_\alpha\colon \Perf(\Lambda_\alpha)\xrightarrow{\sim}
  \mathcal C_\alpha,
\]
and transition equivalences on nonempty overlaps
\[
  \Psi_{\alpha\beta}\colon
  \mathcal C_{\alpha\beta}^{(\alpha)}
  \xrightarrow{\sim}
  \mathcal C_{\alpha\beta}^{(\beta)} .
\]
Here \(\mathcal C_{\alpha\beta}^{(\alpha)}\) and
\(\mathcal C_{\alpha\beta}^{(\beta)}\) denote the two restrictions of
the local dg charts to the overlap \(U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\).
The triple overlaps carry coherent natural isomorphisms
\(\Psi_{\beta\gamma}\Psi_{\alpha\beta}\Rightarrow
\Psi_{\alpha\gamma}\).  Serre-equivariance means
\[
  \Psi_{\alpha\beta}\circ\SerreS_X
  =
  \SerreS_X\circ\Psi_{\alpha\beta},
  \qquad
  \SerreS_X=[3].
\]
The datum becomes a compact quasi-\(\NCCR\), a Hall-Drinfeld double,
or a reduced Hall algebra only after the package
\[
  \mathfrak o_X=
  \bigl(
    [\Psi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}],\
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{Hall}},\
    \eta_{\mathrm{hCS/Hall}},\
    \omega_{\mathcal Z}
  \bigr)
\]
is supplied: the transition-gerbe class, the Hall orientation
character, the compact hCS-to-Hall comparison, and the fibre-functor
datum on the derived centre.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Reduced Hall character constraint]
\label{prop:reduced-hall-character-k3e}
Assume a finite Serre-equivariant quasi-\(\NCCR\) datum on \(X\) is
equipped with vanishing transition gerbe, Hall orientation character,
compact hCS-to-Hall comparison, derived-centre fibre functor, and a
character map compatible with Oberdieck-Pandharipande reduced
stable-pairs integration.  Then the resulting scalar character is
\[
  \chi_{\mathrm{qNCCR}}(X)
  =
  Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\DT}(K3\times E)
  =
  -(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}.
\]
This equality is a constraint on any compact quasi-\(\NCCR\) or reduced
Hall model.  The atlas, compact Hall bracket, Hall-Drinfeld double, and
comparison map are additional data.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Oberdieck-Pandharipande 2016 compute the primitive reduced
Donaldson-Thomas partition function of \(K3\times E\) as
\(-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}\).  The Hall orientation character
in the hypotheses fixes the Behrend sign.  Compatibility with
Oberdieck-Pandharipande integration means that the quasi-\(\NCCR\)
character map evaluates to the same scalar functional on the compact
Hall side.  The displayed identity follows.  The hypotheses are the
compact categorical data; the scalar identity is a character constraint
on that data.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The \(K3\times E\) constants}

The finite-quiver obstruction leaves the following constants separated
by construction:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\hbox{construction} & \hbox{invariant} & \hbox{value} \\
\hline
\hbox{total-space coherent Euler} &
\kcat(X)=\chi(\cO_S)\chi(\cO_E) & 2\cdot 0=0 \\
\hbox{compact Hodge supertrace} &
\kch(X)=\kch^{\mathrm{Hodge}}(X)=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(X) & 0 \\
\hbox{Heisenberg-Mukai specialisation} &
\kHeis(X) & 3 \\
\hbox{Borcherds denominator} &
\kBKM(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2 & 10/2=5 \\
\hbox{K3 fibre lattice} &
\kfib(S)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(S,\mathbb Z) & 24 .
\end{array}
\]
The primitive CHL levels \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) carry
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6\\
\hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2 & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 .
\end{array}
\]
The levels \(N=5,7,8\) lie outside this primitive compact CHL ladder.
They require a theorem giving a common compact host and an explicit row
map before they can be compared with the table above.
These constants belong to separate constructions.  The compact value
\(\kch(X)=0\), the Heisenberg-Mukai value \(\kHeis(X)=3\), the
Borcherds weight \(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\), and the fibre rank
\(\kfib(S)=24\) come from different functors or traces.  They are
independent invariants.

The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue is a different
eight-row list, with twice-weight tuple
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
Its row parameter is the diagonal-divisor datum. The primitive CHL
level above is a separate index.

At level one the Borcherds product normalisation is
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,
  \qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2,
\]
so the reduced \(K3\times E\) Donaldson-Thomas scalar is
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}}(K3\times E)
  =
  -(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
  =
  -D_5^{-2}
  =
  -4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
These values are independent of any global tilting bundle.  The
compact category is smooth and proper as a dg category.  Its BPS and
chiral shadows pass through reduced Hall theory, factorisation,
Heisenberg specialisation, and Borcherds products.
