\section*{Positive Geometry of Quiver Gluing}
\label{sec:positive-geometry-cluster}

\paragraph{Thesis.}
Let $\cC$ be a $3$-Calabi--Yau dg category with numerical charge
lattice $\Gamma=N(\cC)$, skew Euler form
$\langle-,-\rangle$, a Bridgeland stability component
$\Stab^\dagger(\cC)$, and an oriented critical-Hall theory. A
quiver-with-potential chart $(Q_s,W_s)$ on a chamber $s$ supplies a seed
torus
\[
  \cX_s=\Hom(\Gamma_s,\C^\times),
  \qquad
  B_{ij}=\langle e_i,e_j\rangle ,
\]
with basis $\{e_i\}$ given by the stable simples of the heart in that
chamber. Mutation of hearts, Kontsevich--Soibelman wall crossing, and
cluster scattering become the same cocycle after the following
comparison data are fixed:
\[
  \text{seed mutation}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{consistent scattering diagram}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{Hall positive half}
  \longrightarrow
  \Phi_3\text{-specialised }E_{1}\text{-chiral output}.
\]
Positive geometry enters at the finite-window level: a chosen finite
wall window in $\Gamma_\R$ admits an algebraic compactification with a
canonical logarithmic form whose residues are Donaldson--Thomas
invariants. The global slogan ``stability space equals positive
geometry'' is replaced by this finite-window theorem plus the stated
descent hypotheses.

\subsection*{Cluster Variety and Scattering Diagram}
\label{sec:cluster-variety-scattering}

Fix a chamber $s$ with heart $\cA_s\subset \Db(\cC)$ and stable simples
$S_1,\ldots,S_n$. The associated lattice is
$\Gamma_s=\bigoplus_i\Z[S_i]$, and the exchange matrix is
\[
  B_s=(B_{ij}),\qquad B_{ij}=\chi(S_i,S_j)-\chi(S_j,S_i).
\]
The Fock--Goncharov cluster $\cX$-torus attached to $s$ is
\[
  \cX_s=\Spec \C[X_\gamma^{\pm1}:\gamma\in \Gamma_s],
  \qquad
  X_\alpha X_\beta=X_{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
For a mutation at a simple class $e_k$, the birational gluing map on
the classical cluster $\cX$-torus is
\[
  \mu_{e_k}^{*}(X_\beta)
  =
  X_\beta\,
  (1+X_{e_k})^{\langle e_k,\beta\rangle},
  \qquad \beta\in\Gamma_s,
\]
up to the sign convention chosen in the orientation data. These tori
and birational mutations define the cluster variety
$\cX_{\Gamma,\mathbf{s}}$ over the exchange groupoid
$\mathbf{s}$ of reachable hearts.

The scattering diagram attached to the same data is a collection
\[
  \mathfrak D_\sigma
  =
  \{(\gamma^\perp,\Theta_\gamma):
      \gamma\in \Gamma^+,\ \Omega_\sigma(\gamma)\ne0\}
  \subset \Gamma_\R^\vee ,
\]
where $\Omega_\sigma(\gamma)$ is the numerical DT invariant and
$\Theta_\gamma$ is the automorphism of the completed torus algebra
\[
  \Theta_\gamma(X_\beta)
  =
  X_\beta
  (1-X_\gamma)^{\langle\gamma,\beta\rangle\Omega_\sigma(\gamma)}.
\]
In the quantum refinement one replaces the commutative torus by
$\widehat{\mathbb T}_{\Gamma,q}$ with
$X_\alpha X_\beta=q^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle/2}X_{\alpha+\beta}$ and
uses the quantum dilogarithm factor
\[
  \mathbb E_q(X_\gamma)^{\Omega_\sigma(\gamma)}.
\]
Consistency means that the ordered product of wall automorphisms around
each joint is the identity. This is precisely the
Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing equation in the charge lattice
normalisation above.

\subsection*{Positive Geometry Window}
\label{sec:positive-geometry-window}

Arkani-Hamed--Bai--Lam positive geometry is a pair $(P,\partial P)$
inside an algebraic ambient space, with a meromorphic top form whose
iterated residues are the canonical forms of boundary strata. A
Bridgeland component is a complex manifold, while a cluster scattering
chart is an algebraic torus. The positive-geometry statement therefore
lives on finite algebraic windows of the scattering atlas.

\paragraph{Finite-window hypothesis.}
Choose a finite saturated set of effective classes
$\Gamma_W^+\subset \Gamma^+$ and the finite set of walls carrying
classes in $\Gamma_W^+$. Assume:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(PG1)}&
  \text{the chamber fan of } W \text{ is rational polyhedral;}\\
\mathrm{(PG2)}&
  \text{the seed tori glue to a finite-type algebraic space }
  \cX_W;\\
\mathrm{(PG3)}&
  \text{there is a projective compactification }
  \overline{\cX}_W \text{ with normal-crossing boundary;}\\
\mathrm{(PG4)}&
  \text{the DT series restricted to } \Gamma_W^+
  \text{ is locally finite on every chamber.}
\end{array}
\]
The finite positive domain $P_W$ is the closure in
$\overline{\cX}_W(\R_{\ge0})$ of the locus
$X_\gamma>0$ for all effective $\gamma$ in the window. Its logarithmic
DT form is
\[
  \Omega_W^{\mathrm{DT}}
  =
  \sum_{\sigma}
  \sum_{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r\in\Gamma_W^+}
  c_\sigma(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r)\,
  d\log X_{\gamma_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\log X_{\gamma_r},
\]
where $r=\dim \cX_W$ and the coefficient is the chamber-ordered product
of the DT weights entering the KS factorisation. The residue along the
wall $\gamma^\perp$ is multiplication by $\Omega_\sigma(\gamma)$ and
then restriction to the boundary torus $X_\gamma=0$. The consistency of
$\mathfrak D_\sigma$ gives the equality of residues computed from
adjacent chambers. Hence $(P_W,\partial P_W,\Omega_W^{\mathrm{DT}})$ is
an AHL positive geometry whenever (PG1)--(PG4) hold.

The DT partition function is the coefficient series behind this form:
\[
  Z_W^{\mathrm{DT}}(X)
  =
  \prod_{\ell}^{\curvearrowright}
  \prod_{\gamma\in \ell\cap\Gamma_W^+}
  \mathbb E_q(X_\gamma)^{\Omega_\sigma(\gamma)}.
\]
The canonical form is obtained from the logarithmic differential and
top-degree projection of this chamber product, with the chosen
orientation data fixing signs.

\subsection*{Mutation, Tilting, and Wall Crossing}
\label{sec:mutation-tilting-wall-crossing}

Let $(Q,W)$ be a Jacobi-finite quiver with potential, let
$A=\Jac(Q,W)$, and let $\cA=\mod A$ be the standard heart. Mutation at a
loop-free vertex $k$ has three compatible meanings.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item Derksen--Weyman--Zelevinsky mutation transforms $(Q,W)$ into
  $(\mu_kQ,\mu_kW)$.
  \item Keller--Yang mutation gives a derived equivalence between the
  corresponding Ginzburg dg algebras.
  \item Tilting $\cA$ at the simple $S_k$ crosses the Bridgeland wall
  on which $S_k$ aligns with the remaining semistable factors.
\end{enumerate}

Nagao's DT/cluster theorem identifies the resulting cluster
transformation with the KS wall-crossing transformation for
Jacobi-finite quivers with potential. For a general CY$_3$ category the
same formula requires a stability datum, orientation data, support
property, and completed quantum torus. Compact examples such as
$K3\times E$ enter through this completed stability-datum formalism or
through finite Hall windows, since a single global Jacobi-finite tilting
object is extra structure.

\subsection*{Hall Positive Half}
\label{sec:hall-positive-half}

For a quiver with potential, the cohomological Hall algebra is
\[
  \CoHA(Q,W)
  =
  \bigoplus_{\mathbf d\in\N^{Q_0}}
  H^{\bullet}_{G_{\mathbf d}}
  \bigl(\Rep_{\mathbf d}(Q),\varphi_{\Tr W}\bigr),
\]
with multiplication defined by the extension correspondence. The
orientation data make this an associative $E_{1}$ Hall algebra. A
mutation equivalence
\[
  \mu_k:\Db(\Gamma(Q,W))\xrightarrow{\sim}
        \Db(\Gamma(\mu_kQ,\mu_kW))
\]
induces an $E_{1}$ quasi-isomorphism of completed Hall algebras once the
vanishing-cycle sheaves and orientation lines are transported through
the derived equivalence:
\[
  \mu_k^*:
  \widehat{\CoHA}(Q,W)
  \xrightarrow{\simeq_{E_{1}}}
  \widehat{\CoHA}(\mu_kQ,\mu_kW).
\]
The positive half $\Yplus_{\sigma}(Q,W)$ is the completed subalgebra
generated by stable effective classes in the chamber $\sigma$. Across a
wall it is transported by the same KS automorphism. Thus the quiver
atlas gives a diagram
\[
  \mathbf{s}\longrightarrow E_{1}\text{-}\mathrm{Alg},
  \qquad
  s\longmapsto \Yplus_{\sigma_s}(Q_s,W_s),
\]
whose homotopy colimit is the Hall-side gluing object of the chosen
window.

For the smooth toric chart $\C^3$, Schiffmann--Vasserot identify
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)\cong
  \Yplus(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
the positive half of the affine Yangian. The full
$\mathcal W_{1+\infty}$ object enters after Drinfeld doubling, Cartan
completion, and a Fock or evaluation representation:
\[
  \Yplus
  \longrightarrow
  D_\hbar(\Yplus)
  \longrightarrow
  Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1)
  \longrightarrow
  \mathcal W_{1+\infty}[\lambda].
\]
This four-step path is the required layer separation for every toric
chart used below.

\subsection*{Chiral Transfer Hypotheses}
\label{sec:chiral-transfer-hypotheses}

The cluster and Hall constructions produce algebraic wall-crossing
data. A chiral output requires additional transfer maps. For a
CY$_3$ category $\cC$ the typed hypotheses are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(C1)}&
  \PhiFA_3(\cC) \text{ is constructed as an } E_3
  \text{ holomorphic factorisation algebra;}\\
\mathrm{(C2)}&
  \text{a framed specialisation datum }(\Sigma_2,C)
  \text{ is fixed;}\\
\mathrm{(C3)}&
  A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma_2,C)}
  :=
  \SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(\cC)\bigr)
  \text{ is an } E_{1}\text{-chiral algebra on } C;\\
\mathrm{(C4)}&
  \text{there is an oriented hCS-to-Hall comparison }
  \eta:
  \Yplus_{\mathrm{Hall}}(\cC)
  \to A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma_2,C)}
  \text{ of } E_{1}\text{-algebras;}\\
\mathrm{(C5)}&
  \text{braided or full vertex output is taken through }
  \cZ(\Rep^{E_{1}}(A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma_2,C)}))
  \text{ or }D_\hbar(\Yplus_{\mathrm{Hall}}).
\end{array}
\]
At dimension $d\ge3$, the native specialisation is $E_{1}$-chiral. The
braided $E_{2}$ layer belongs to the centre, double, module, or
representation enhancement. Positive geometry and cluster charts supply
inputs to (C4); construction of (C1)--(C5) requires the transfer maps
listed above.

\subsection*{Conifold Window}
\label{sec:conifold-window}

The resolved conifold has the Klebanov--Witten quiver with two vertices,
two arrows in each direction, and potential
\[
  W_{\mathrm{KW}}
  =
  a_1b_1a_2b_2-a_1b_2a_2b_1.
\]
The exchange lattice is rank two with
\[
  B_{\mathrm{con}}=
  \begin{pmatrix}0&2\\-2&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the affine $A_1^{(1)}$ rank-two cluster pattern. A single flop window is
the two-chamber sector generated by the two adjacent hearts on either
side of the conifold wall; iterating mutation gives the full affine
rank-two scattering pattern. The KS automorphisms are
\[
  \Theta_{S_i}(X_\beta)
  =
  X_\beta(1-X_{S_i})^{\langle S_i,\beta\rangle},
  \qquad i=0,1,
\]
with DT weights equal to the non-commutative conifold invariants of
Szendr\H{o}i in the chosen chamber.

The finite flop window satisfies (PG1)--(PG4). Its positive domain is an
interval in the tropical exchange line after quotienting by the common
scaling direction. The logarithmic canonical form has residues equal to
the two simple DT wall weights. The Hall object is the positive conifold
CoHA of the Klebanov--Witten potential. The chiral transfer to a
CY$_3$ output further requires (C1)--(C5).

The invariant used by the CY-to-chiral table is
\[
  \kch(\widetilde Y)=1
\]
for the direct McKay conifold branch. This is separate from the
degree-zero MacMahon exponent and from the affine rank-two cluster
matrix.

\subsection*{Local \texorpdfstring{$\bP^2$}{P2} Window}
\label{sec:local-p2-window}

For $Y=\mathrm{Tot}(\omega_{\bP^2})$, the Beilinson exceptional
collection $(\cO(-2),\cO(-1),\cO)$ gives the three-node Markov quiver
with exchange matrix
\[
  B_{\bP^2}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0&3&-3\\
    -3&0&3\\
    3&-3&0
  \end{pmatrix}
\]
and cubic potential. The full seed pattern is the Markov local-surface
cluster pattern. The affine $A_2^{(1)}$ label applies to the toric fan
and to quotient windows in which the three cyclic walls are projected
to the rank-two affine surface slice.

The scattering diagram is the toric DT scattering diagram of the local
surface chamber, with walls labelled by effective triples
$\gamma=(r,c_1,c_2)$ and automorphisms
\[
  X_\beta\longmapsto
  X_\beta
  (1-X_\gamma)^{\langle\gamma,\beta\rangle
  \Omega_Y(\gamma)}.
\]
In a finite slope-and-discriminant window, the chamber fan is finite and
the AHL positive-geometry package is the polygonal cluster window cut
out by those walls. The canonical form is the top logarithmic form whose
wall residues are the local-surface DT invariants in that window.

The Vol III invariant is
\[
  \kch(\mathrm{Tot}(\omega_{\bP^2}))=\frac32,
\]
the local-surface CY$_3$ value from the McKay/direct-shadow
normalisation. It is recorded independently of the Markov cluster
matrix and independently of the topological degree-zero partition
function.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E} Lattice Window}
\label{sec:k3e-lattice-window}

For $X=K3\times E$, the strict quiver-with-potential atlas is replaced
by a lattice-polarised Hall and scattering package. Fix a primitive
lattice polarisation $L\subset H^2(K3,\Z)$ and the corresponding period
domain $\Omega_L$. The Mukai lattice of the K3 fibre is
\[
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{Mukai}}(K3)
  =
  H^*(K3,\Z)
  \cong U^{\oplus4}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus2},
\]
of signature $(4,20)$. The elliptic factor supplies an additional
hyperbolic plane in the charge lattice. The Hall scattering diagram is
formed on the completed effective cone in the lattice
\[
  \Gamma_L^+
  \subset
  \Lambda_{\mathrm{Mukai}}(K3)\oplus H^*(E,\Z),
\]
with walls $\gamma^\perp$ carrying reduced DT weights
$\Omega^{\mathrm{red}}_{K3\times E}(\gamma)$.

The finite-window cluster object is a Fock--Goncharov/GHKK
cluster-ensemble chart only after choosing a finite root window
$R_W\subset \Gamma_L^+$. Its seed tori are the algebraic tori
$\Hom(\Z R_W,\C^\times)$; its mutation maps are the simple-reflection
KS automorphisms for spherical or real-root classes in $R_W$; its
scattering diagram is the restriction of the reduced DT scattering
diagram to $\R R_W$. The comparison with the Gritsenko--Nikulin
automorphic side is the map
\[
  \Theta_L^{\mathrm{GHKK}}
  :
  \mathrm{Span}\{\vartheta_v:v\in \cA_L^{\mathrm{trop}}(\Z)\}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathrm{Span}\{c_L(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma_L^+\},
\]
which sends theta functions of the cluster ensemble to Fourier--Jacobi
coefficients of the Borcherds product when the lattice-polarised mirror
identification is constructed. This map is a hypothesis for the full
K3-fibred comparison; the automorphic product itself is supplied by the
Gritsenko lift.

The reduced DT character is
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{DT},\mathrm{red}}_{K3\times E}
  =
  \Phi_{10}^{-1}
  =
  \Delta_5^{-2}
\]
in the Igusa-square normalisation. The Borcherds invariant attached to
the denominator algebra is read in the $\Delta_5$ normalisation:
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=\frac{c_1(0)}2=\frac{10}{2}=5.
\]
The square $\Phi_{10}$ has weight $10$ as a scalar automorphic
character. The compact and fibre invariants remain distinct:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{invariant} & \text{value} & \text{construction}\\
\hline
\kcat(K3\times E) & 0 & \chi(\cO_{K3\times E})\\
\kch(K3\times E) & 0 & \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)\\
\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E) & 3 & \text{Mukai--Heisenberg Stage-2 shadow}\\
\kBKM(\Delta_5) & 5 & \text{Borcherds weight }c_1(0)/2\\
\kfib(K3) & 24 & \text{K3 Mukai-lattice rank witness}
\end{array}
\]
The Hall--Drinfeld/Borcherds branch uses the completed double
$D_\hbar(\Yplus_{\mathrm{Hall}}(K3\times E))$ on its recognition locus.
The K3 self-mirror current branch is a separate construction.

\subsection*{Comparison Diagram}
\label{sec:comparison-diagram}

For every finite wall window satisfying the hypotheses above, the
comparison has the following typed form:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
  \text{cluster seed tori}
    \arrow[r,"\mu_k"]
    \arrow[d]
  & \text{cluster seed tori}
    \arrow[d]
  \\
  \mathfrak D_\sigma
    \arrow[r,"\Theta_\gamma"]
    \arrow[d]
  & \mathfrak D_{\sigma'}
    \arrow[d]
  \\
  \Yplus_\sigma(Q,W)
    \arrow[r,"\mu_k^*"]
    \arrow[d,"\eta"']
  & \Yplus_{\sigma'}(Q',W')
    \arrow[d,"\eta'"]
  \\
  A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma_2,C)}
    \arrow[r,"\text{wall transport}"]
  & A_{\cC}^{(\Sigma_2,C)} .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The first square is Fock--Goncharov mutation written as a KS
automorphism. The second square is the Hall realisation through
vanishing cycles and extension correspondences. The third square is the
chiral transfer, present only under (C1)--(C5). Passing to the centre or
the Drinfeld double is an additional operation applied after the
$E_{1}$ Hall or chiral algebra is built.

\paragraph{Result.}
The quiver atlas supplies a cluster-scattering skeleton. The DT
invariants supply the logarithmic residues of finite positive-geometry
windows. The CoHA supplies the $E_{1}$ Hall positive half. The
CY-to-chiral output follows only after the framed specialisation and
Hall-to-chiral comparison maps are supplied. This is the precise
content carried by the conifold, local $\bP^2$, and $K3\times E$
examples.
