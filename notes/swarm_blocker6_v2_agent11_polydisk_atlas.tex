\section*{Swarm Blocker 6 v2 / Agent 11: Polydisk atlas and
DWR/Ran basis for \texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E}}
\label{sec:swarm-blocker6-v2-agent11-polydisk-atlas}

\paragraph{Target.}
The local input in
\verb|chapters/theory/cy_to_chiral.tex|
\verb|subsubsec:pre-fa-polydisk-k3xe|
needs a holomorphic-polydisk source for the perturbative hCS
pre-factorisation algebra on $X=S\times E$, where $S$ is a projective
$K3$ surface and $E$ is an elliptic curve. The stable replacement is
not a canonical $8$-bidisk or $32$-polydisk atlas. The stable
replacement is:
\[
\text{a noncanonical finite coordinate-polydisk atlas}
\quad+\quad
\text{the cofinal DWR/Ran basis of finite disjoint polydisk families}.
\]
The finite atlas supplies local coordinates. The DWR/Ran basis supplies
Weiss descent. These are different objects.

\paragraph{Falsified attacks.}
\ClaimStatusFalsified{}
Four claims must not be used.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Not every projective $K3$ surface is a smooth quartic in
$\bP^3$. A smooth quartic is a genus-$3$ polarised $K3$ model, not a
canonical model for all projective $K3$ surfaces.
\item There is no canonical uniform $8$-bidisk cover of a $K3$, hence
no canonical uniform $32$-polydisk cover of $K3\times E$.
\item On $E=\C/(\Z+i\Z)$, the four radius-$2/3$ corner disks are not a
coordinate disk cover. The quotient map is injective only on disks of
radius $<1/2$, and the four corners represent the same point of $E$.
\item All finite unions of a finite ordinary atlas do not form a
DWR/Ran polydisk Weiss basis. A finite ordinary subcover is not a
finite Weiss subcover.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Finite coordinate-polydisk atlas, noncanonical]
\label{thm:finite-coordinate-polydisk-atlas-k3xe-healed}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
Let $S$ be a compact complex $K3$ surface and let $E$ be an elliptic
curve. Then $X=S\times E$ admits a finite open cover
\[
 \mathfrak A=\{D_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in I}
\]
such that each $D_\alpha$ is biholomorphic to a polydisk in $\C^3$ and
is Stein. The index set $I$ depends on the chosen coordinate charts,
metric, and finite subcover; it has no canonical cardinality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every point $s\in S$ has a holomorphic coordinate neighbourhood
$U_s\simeq \Delta^2_{\epsilon_s}$ by the definition of a complex
surface. Every point $e\in E$ has a coordinate disk
$V_e\simeq \Delta_{\rho_e}$. The products
$U_s\times V_e$ form an open cover of the compact manifold
$S\times E$ by coordinate polydisks in $\C^3$. Compactness gives a
finite subcover. A polydisk is Stein, and biholomorphism preserves the
Stein property. No algebraic embedding or quartic model is required.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Algebraic coordinate source.}
\ClaimStatusVerified{}
If a projective $K3$ is embedded as $S\hookrightarrow\bP^N$, the
standard affine charts of $\bP^N$ and the holomorphic implicit function
theorem give local bidisk coordinates on $S$ around each smooth point.
A finite subcover exists by compactness. The number of charts depends
on the embedding and the chosen Lebesgue number. The quartic
$S=\{F_4=0\}\subset\bP^3$ is one source of such charts only on the
quartic locus.

\begin{lemma}[Square elliptic curve disk correction]
\label{lem:square-elliptic-disk-correction}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
For $E_i=\C/(\Z+i\Z)$ a valid explicit coordinate disk cover is given
by the images of the nine disks
\[
 B\!\left(\frac{a}{3}+\frac{b}{3}i,\frac{1}{3}\right),
 \qquad a,b\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]
Each disk maps injectively to $E_i$, and the nine images cover $E_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The injectivity radius of the square flat torus is $1/2$, so the
quotient map is injective on every Euclidean disk of radius $<1/2$.
The radius $1/3$ disks above satisfy this bound. Every point of the
unit square is within Euclidean distance at most $\sqrt2/6<1/3$ from
one of the nine grid points, with distance computed in the quotient
metric. Hence the nine images cover the torus. The same argument for an
arbitrary lattice $\Lambda$ uses any radius below half the systole and a
finite net in a fundamental parallelogram.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[DWR/Ran polydisk basis]
\label{def:dwr-ran-polydisk-basis-healed}
\ClaimStatusConstruction{}
For an open $U\subset X$, let
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{DWR}}(U)$ be the category whose objects are finite
pairwise disjoint families
\[
 \sigma=(S,\{P_s\Subset U\}_{s\in S})
\]
of relatively compact holomorphic coordinate polydisks in $U$. The
associated open is $|\sigma|=\bigsqcup_{s\in S}P_s$. Morphisms are
refinements by inclusions over maps of finite sets, and Ran
multiplication is disjoint union of families.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Weiss cofinality of the DWR/Ran basis]
\label{prop:dwr-ran-weiss-cofinality-healed}
\ClaimStatusProvedHere{}
The collection
\[
 \{\,|\sigma|:\sigma\in \mathcal B_{\mathrm{DWR}}(U)\,\}
\]
is a Weiss cover of $U$. Moreover it is cofinal among
holomorphic-polydisk refinements of any Weiss cover of $U$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $F\subset U$ be finite. Since $X$ is Hausdorff and complex
manifold charts form a basis, choose pairwise disjoint relatively
compact coordinate polydisks around the distinct points of $F$, all
contained in $U$. Their finite union is $|\sigma|$ for an object
$\sigma\in\mathcal B_{\mathrm{DWR}}(U)$ and contains $F$; this is the
Weiss condition.

If $\cW$ is a Weiss cover of $U$, then for every finite set $F$ there
is $W\in\cW$ with $F\subset W$. Repeating the preceding construction
inside $W$ gives an object $\sigma$ with $F\subset |\sigma|\Subset W$.
Thus the DWR/Ran basis refines the Weiss data without defining its
objects by descent. The basis is infinite in general; compactness of
$X$ gives finite ordinary subcovers, not finite Weiss subcovers.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Cosheaf variance for hCS observables.}
\ClaimStatusVerified{}
The hCS factorisation algebra is covariant for inclusions of
admissible opens. In the compact-support model, an inclusion
$i:U\hookrightarrow V$ gives extension by zero on compactly supported
fields or test insertions,
\[
 i_!:\Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(U,\frakg)\longrightarrow
 \Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(V,\frakg),
\]
and hence the corresponding structure map on renormalised local and
multilocal observables. For pairwise disjoint opens
$U_1,\ldots,U_n\subset V$ the factorisation product is the completed
tensor product followed by the extension of supports into $V$.
Consequently Weiss descent is a homotopy-colimit/cosheaf statement on
the DWR/Ran nerve, not a sheaf restriction or homotopy-limit Cech
statement.

\paragraph{Calabi--Yau form on overlaps.}
\ClaimStatusVerified{}
On a coordinate polydisk $D_\alpha$ with coordinates
$z_\alpha=(z_{\alpha,1},z_{\alpha,2},z_{\alpha,3})$ one can write
\[
 \Omega_X|_{D_\alpha}
 = c_\alpha(z_\alpha)\,
 dz_{\alpha,1}\wedge dz_{\alpha,2}\wedge dz_{\alpha,3},
 \qquad c_\alpha\in\cO(D_\alpha)^\times.
\]
On an overlap $D_\alpha\cap D_\beta$ the transition determinant obeys
\[
 \det\!\left(\frac{\partial z_\beta}{\partial z_\alpha}\right)
 = \frac{c_\alpha}{c_\beta}.
\]
Thus a global holomorphic $3$-form trivialises $K_X$; it does not make
arbitrary coordinate transitions volume-preserving. Volume-adapted
coordinates may be chosen locally, but the choice is noncanonical and
requires its own compatibility data on overlaps.

\paragraph{Propagator compatibility.}
\ClaimStatusOpen{}
The restricted global propagator
$P_X|_{P\times P}$ and the flat Costello--Li propagator on a coordinate
polydisk $P\subset\C^3$ have the same diagonal singularity, but they
need not be equal. Their difference is the place where the
Costello--Gwilliam renormalisation-group homotopy, local counterterms,
and overlap compatibility enter. The atlas alone does not prove this
compatibility. The correct hCS input is a scale-dependent quantum
package whose effective interactions and counterterms restrict
coherently along DWR/Ran refinements.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
\ClaimStatusVerified{}
The atlas and DWR/Ran basis are hCS-side inputs. They do not construct
the hCS-to-Hall comparison. The latter requires the oriented
DWR/Ran natural transformation, orientation data, shifts, Tate twists,
completion choices, and Thom--Sebastiani coherences of
\verb|chapters/theory/cy3_chain_level_bridge.tex|.
