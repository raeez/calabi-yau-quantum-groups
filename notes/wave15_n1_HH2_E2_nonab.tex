\section{Hochschild Deformations and Nonabelian hCS Boundary Quantisation}
\label{sec:hochschild-nonabelian-hcs}

\subsection{Typed deformation complexes}

Let $\cC$ be a smooth proper Calabi-Yau $d$-category. The
CY-to-chiral construction has two stages
\[
  \Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}
  =
  \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ \PhiFA_d,
  \qquad
  \PhiFA_d(\cC)\in \mathrm{Alg}_{\Ed}^{\mathrm{hol}}(X),
\]
and
\[
  \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\bigl(\PhiFA_d(\cC)\bigr)
  \in
  \mathrm{Alg}_{E_{n(d)}}^{\mathrm{ch}}(C),
  \qquad
  n(d)=
  \begin{cases}
    \infty, & d=1,\\
    2, & d=2,\\
    1, & d\geq 3.
  \end{cases}
\]
The Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ supplies
the Stage 1 Hochschild input. It is categorical Hochschild cohomology.
It is separate from topological Hochschild cohomology of an $E_1$
algebra and from chiral Hochschild cohomology of a chiral algebra on
the curve $C$.

For $d\geq 3$ the specialised object
\[
  A_C=\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\bigl(\PhiFA_d(\cC)\bigr)
\]
is an $\Eone$-chiral algebra. Set $Z_C=\Zchder(A_C)$. The braided
$\Etwo$ structure appears on centre, double, or module layers, for
example on the verified Drinfeld-centre locus
\[
  Z\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_C)\bigr)
  \simeq
  \Rep^{\Etwo}\bigl(Z_C\bigr).
\]
Thus an $\Etwo$-Hochschild tangent complex is available only after an
$\Etwo$ object such as $Z_C$, a double, or a module category has been
specified.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
An element of $\HH^2_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)$ controls a first order
deformation of the Calabi-Yau category $\cC$. It lies outside the
intrinsic deformation theory of the native $d\geq 3$ specialised
chiral algebra. Its $\Etwo$ meaning requires the centre, double, or
module layer.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The Hochschild complex of $\cC$ is the cyclic bar or cochain complex of
the category. Its degree two classes deform the dg or $A_\infty$ product
on $\cC$, with obstruction in degree three through the Gerstenhaber
square. The object $A_C$ is obtained only after Stage 1 factorisation
and Stage 2 specialisation. At $d\geq 3$ that specialisation lands in
$\Eone$-chiral algebras. Passing from $A_C$ to $\Zchder(A_C)$ is an
additional centre construction with its own deformation complex. A
comparison map
\[
  \HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{cat}}(\cC)
  \longrightarrow
  \Def^\bullet_{\Etwo}\bigl(Z_C\bigr)
\]
is therefore extra structure beyond the notation $\Phi_d$.

\subsection{Finite-dimensional Lie cohomology and chiral deformation}

Let $\fg=\sln$ and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $\fg$-module. The
Whitehead lemmas give
\[
  H^1_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\fg,V)=0,
  \qquad
  H^2_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\fg,V)=0.
\]
These statements control finite-dimensional colour-sector
deformations. Their hypotheses exclude the completed loop algebra, the
central extension, BRST ghosts, OPE singularities, factorisation
modules, and chiral completions.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
The vanishing
$H^2_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\sln,V)=0$ for finite-dimensional $V$ has no
implication
\[
  \HH^2_{\Etwo}\bigl(\partial\hCS_5(\sln),M\bigr)=0
\]
for arbitrary chiral bimodules or factorisation modules $M$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The left side is computed in the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
of the semisimple finite-dimensional Lie algebra $\sln$. The right side
is an operadic Hochschild or chiral tangent complex for a boundary
factorisation algebra. It contains the loop variable, the
Kac-Moody cocycle, the BRST differential, distributional OPE kernels,
completed tensor products, and coefficient objects $M$ whose underlying
representation is usually infinite-dimensional. A reduction from the
right side to the left side requires a filtration, an associated graded
identification, convergence, compatibility with BRST reduction, and a
proof that the only surviving coefficient systems satisfy Whitehead's
finite-dimensional hypotheses. Simplicity of a generic vacuum
Kac-Moody vertex algebra controls vertex ideals; formality of its full
$L_\infty$ deformation complex is a separate theorem.

At the critical level, the Feigin-Frenkel centre already changes the
tangent problem: the centre is the algebra of functions on opers, and
deformations of the centre are governed by the derived geometry of the
oper space. Generic and critical levels therefore require different
arguments.

\subsection{BV quantisation of nonabelian hCS}

Let $X$ be a complex Calabi-Yau threefold with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega_X$, and let $(\fg,\langle-,-\rangle)$ be a quadratic complex Lie
algebra. The local holomorphic Chern-Simons fields on $U\subset X$ are
\[
  \cL_{\hCS}(U)=\Omega^{0,\bullet}(U,\fg)[1],
  \qquad
  \ell_1=\dbar,
  \qquad
  \ell_2(\alpha,\beta)=[\alpha\wedge\beta]_\fg .
\]
In unshifted Dolbeault grading the $(0,1)$ component is the connection
field, the $(0,0)$ component is the ghost, and the $(0,2)$ and $(0,3)$
components are BV antifields under the pairing
\[
  \omega(\alpha,\beta)
  =
  \int_U \Omega_X\wedge \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle .
\]

A compact or boundary nonabelian hCS statement requires a Costello
renormalisation datum: orientation and determinant-line framing for the
BV pairing, elliptic gauge fixing $Q^{\mathrm{GF}}$, heat kernels
$K_t$, propagators $P_{\varepsilon,L}$, completed local functionals
$\cO_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cE_X)[[\hbar]]$, counterterms, and factorisation
or TCFT sewing. The effective interaction must satisfy the scale $L$
quantum master equation
\[
  QI[L]
  +\frac12\{I[L],I[L]\}_L
  +\hbar\Delta_L I[L]
  =
  0.
\]

For a representation $\rho:\fg\to\operatorname{End}(V)$ the quartic
invariant is
\[
  I_4^\rho(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)
  =
  \frac1{4!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}
  \Tr_V\!\left(
    \rho(x_{\sigma(1)})\rho(x_{\sigma(2)})
    \rho(x_{\sigma(3)})\rho(x_{\sigma(4)})
  \right).
\]
The corresponding one-loop local functional is
\[
  \Theta^\rho_U(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)
  =
  \int_U
  \left[
    I_4^\rho(
      \alpha_0,\partial\alpha_1,\partial\alpha_2,\partial\alpha_3
    )
  \right]_{(3,3)} .
\]
Its cohomology class is the degree one obstruction to the quantum
master equation. A quantisation through this obstruction requires an
explicit local counterterm $J$ with
\[
  QJ+\Theta^\rho_U=0
\]
in the completed local BV complex, followed by the higher order
compatibilities imposed by the renormalisation group.

The quadratic Casimir
\[
  C_2^\rho=\sum_a \rho(T_a)\rho(T^a)
\]
has arity two and degree zero. It is the coefficient of a two-point
kinetic or field-strength counterterm. It is separate from the quartic
QME anomaly $I_4^\rho$.

\subsection{Objects that remain distinct}

The following objects have different definitions and functorial roles:
\[
  A,\qquad B(A),\qquad A^i,\qquad A^!,\qquad \Zchder(A).
\]
Here $A$ is the specialised $\Eone$-chiral algebra, $B(A)$ is its bar
coalgebra, $A^i$ is the Koszul dual coalgebra, $A^!$ is the Verdier or
Koszul dual algebra in the specified duality context, and
$\Zchder(A)$ is the derived chiral centre. The identity
$\Omega B(A)\simeq A$ is bar-cobar inversion. It says nothing about
$A^!$ or about the bulk centre.

Hochschild polyvectors are another layer. For a smooth variety $X$, HKR
identifies
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\operatorname{Perf}(X))
  \simeq
  \bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X,\wedge^p T_X)
\]
after the completed diagonal is used. Product, module, and Mukai-pairing
comparisons carry the Duflo-Todd correction generated by the Atiyah
class. The hCS fields $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\fg)[1]$ and their BV
antifields belong to the gauge theory layer. Hochschild polyvectors
belong to the HKR layer.

\subsection{The toric Hall comparison}

For the toric affine threefold $\C^3$, the critical CoHA of the tripled
Jordan quiver gives the positive half
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)\cong \Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1).
\]
The complete $\Woneinf$ comparison passes through the double, centre,
completion, and evaluation layers:
\[
  \Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \hookrightarrow
  D\bigl(\Yp(\widehat{\fgl}_1)\bigr)
  =
  \widehat{Y}(\widehat{\fgl}_1)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{End}\bigl(\Woneinf[\lambda]\text{-vacuum}\bigr).
\]
This is the typed route from Hall algebra to vertex algebra. The direct
Hall output is the positive half.

\subsection{Corrected conclusion}

For $\fg=\sln$, finite-dimensional Whitehead vanishing removes the
ordinary colour-sector first order deformations. It has no force as an
all-coefficient $\Etwo$-Hochschild vanishing theorem for
$\partial\hCS_5(\sln)$.

A theorem identifying a nonabelian hCS boundary algebra with an affine
Yangian or Yangian vertex algebra must provide the following witnesses:
the BV quantisation satisfying the scale $L$ QME, trivialisation of the
quartic class $I_4^\rho$,
a distinct treatment of the two-point counterterm $C_2^\rho$,
BRST reduction compatible with the chosen Kac-Moody level,
completion and coefficient control for the chiral tangent complex, PBW
or no-extra-relation comparison, and sewing for compact or boundary
geometries. The Hochschild calculation supplies one input to this
construction. The construction requires the remaining witnesses.
