\section*{The Borcherds denominator of \texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta5} as a
specialised \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3$}{Phi3} output}
\label{sec:lorgat-two-stage-kapranov}

Let
\[
  X = K3\times E,\qquad
  \Omega_X = \Omega_{K3}\wedge dz_E,
\]
and let \(\Perf(X)\) carry its Kontsevich--Soibelman
\(3\)-Calabi--Yau structure.  The \(d=3\) CY-to-chiral construction is
the tagged assignment
\[
  \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\mathcal C)
  :=
  \SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(\mathcal C)\bigr).
\]
The tag \((\Sigma_2,C)\) is part of the mathematical datum.  The
ambient object \(\PhiFA_3(\mathcal C)\) and the specialised object
\(\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\mathcal C)\) answer different questions:
\(\PhiFA_3(\mathcal C)\) is the holomorphic factorisation object on
the \(3\)-fold, while
\(\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\mathcal C)\) is an \(\Eone\)-chiral algebra
on the reference curve \(C\).

For the K3-fibre datum \((\Sigma_2,C)=(K3,E)\), write
\[
  A_{K3,E}
  :=
  \SpCh_{K3,E}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(\Perf(K3\times E))\bigr)
  \in \Eone\text{-}\ChirAlg(E).
\]
The Borcherds--Gritsenko--Nikulin algebra \(\fg_{\Delta_5}\) enters
after the Mukai--Heisenberg reduction and the Borcherds pushforward of
this \(\Eone\)-chiral algebra.  Its denominator is the function
constructed by Lorgat from the weak Jacobi form \(\phi_{0,1}\):
\[
  \frac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z)
  =
  \Phi(z)
  =
  e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+}
  \bigl(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}.
\]
This is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity for
\(\fg_{\Delta_5}\), with \(z\) in the tube domain of
\(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\) and with root multiplicities read from
\(\phi_{0,1}=\sum f(n,\ell)q^n r^\ell\) through
\(\mathrm{mult}(n,\ell,m)=f(nm,\ell)\).

\subsection*{The functorial scope}

The formula
\[
  \Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}
  =
  \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ\PhiFA_d
\]
is a scope declaration.  It fixes the source, target, and
specialisation datum of the construction.  Chain-level constructions
supply explicit factorisation algebras, homotopies, BV observables, and
denominator expansions.  \((\infty,1)\)-categorical constructions
supply functoriality, kernels, centres, and stable categorical descent.
The \(d=3\) K3-fibre comparison uses both constructions.

The native specialised output obeys the dimensional rule
\[
  d\leq 2:\ \text{higher chiral, locally \(\Etwo\) on the constructed locus},
  \qquad
  d\geq 3:\ \text{\(\Eone\)-chiral after specialisation}.
\]
At \(d=3\), any \(\Etwo\)-structure belongs to enhancement layers such
as
\[
  \cZ\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_{K3,E})\bigr),\qquad
  Z_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{der}}(A_{K3,E}),\qquad
  D\bigl(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_{K3,E})\bigr),
\]
and \(A_{K3,E}\) itself remains \(\Eone\)-chiral.

\subsection*{The denominator theorem}

The framed H1--H4 hypotheses mean: \(\Perf(X)\) is smooth and proper
over \(\C\); \(\HH^0(\Perf(X))=\C\); the negative-cyclic
Calabi--Yau class gives a non-degenerate cyclic pairing of degree
\(-3\); \(X\) is a smooth Calabi--Yau threefold with the chosen
holomorphic volume form \(\Omega_X\), together with the \(d=3\)
chain-level framing and Costello--Li holomorphic Chern--Simons witness.

\begin{theorem}[K3-fibre specialisation and the \(\Delta_5\) denominator]
\label{thm:lorgat-k3e-denominator}
Assume the H1--H4 framed \(d=3\) hypotheses for
\(\Phi_3^{(K3,E)}(\Perf(K3\times E))\).  Then
\[
  A_{K3,E}
  =
  \SpCh_{K3,E}\bigl(\PhiFA_3(\Perf(K3\times E))\bigr)
\]
is an \(\Eone\)-chiral algebra on \(E\).  Its
Mukai--Heisenberg reduction followed by the Borcherds pushforward has
denominator
\[
  \operatorname{den}\bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{Borch}}_* A_{K3,E}\bigr)
  =
  \frac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z).
\]
The associated generalized Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra is
\(\fg_{\Delta_5}\), and
\[
  \kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})
  =
  \mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)
  =
  \frac{c_1(0)}{2}
  =
  \frac{10}{2}
  =
  5.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The H1--H4 hypotheses provide the framed object-level construction
\(\Phi_3^{(K3,E)}=\SpCh_{K3,E}\circ\PhiFA_3\), hence
\(A_{K3,E}\in\Eone\)-\(\ChirAlg(E)\).  The \(K3\) factor contributes
the Mukai lattice and the K3 elliptic genus.  The relevant hyperbolic
lattice in Lorgat's construction is
\[
  \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}
  =
  \Lambda(1,1)\oplus[2]
  \cong
  \mathbb Z f_2\oplus\mathbb Z f_3\oplus\mathbb Z f_{-2}
  \subset \Lambda^{3,2}.
\]
The three real simple roots are
\[
  \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_3=f_3,
\]
and \(\Delta_5(s_{\delta_i}Z)=-\Delta_5(Z)\) for their reflections.
The Weyl chamber in the cone of \(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\) gives the
positive-root set \(\Delta^+\).

Lorgat's denominator function is
\[
  \Phi(z)
  =
  \sum_{w\in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})}
  \det(w)
  \left(
    e^{-2\pi i(w\rho,z)}
    -
    \sum_{a\in \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap \mathbb R_{>0}\mathcal P_{II}}
    m(a)e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a),z)}
  \right),
\]
and the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds product expansion gives
\[
  \Phi(z)
  =
  e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+}
  \bigl(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}.
\]
Lorgat's Theorem 3 identifies this function with
\(\frac{1}{64}\Delta_5(2Z)\).  Lorgat's Theorem 4 expresses the same
product through the weak Jacobi form
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)
  =
  (r^{-1}+10+r)
  +
  q(10r^{-2}-64r^{-1}+108-64r+10r^2)+\cdots ,
\]
so that the coefficient \(f(nm,\ell)\) supplies the superdimension of
the \((n,\ell,m)\)-root space.

Borcherds' weight formula for singular theta products gives
\(\mathrm{wt}(\Phi)=c(0)/2\).  In the \(\Delta_5\) normalisation,
\(c_1(0)=10\), hence \(\kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})=5\).  The denominator of
the Borcherds pushforward of \(A_{K3,E}\) is therefore exactly the
Lorgat denominator, with the algebra \(\fg_{\Delta_5}\) determined by
the Borcherds root datum and product formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The five algebraic objects}

For \(A=A_{K3,E}\), the following objects occupy different categories:
\[
  A,\qquad B(A),\qquad A^i,\qquad A^!,\qquad
  Z_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{der}}(A).
\]
Here \(A\) is the \(\Eone\)-chiral algebra on \(E\).  The object
\(B(A)\) is its bar coalgebra.  The equivalence
\[
  \Omega B(A)\simeq A
\]
is bar--cobar inversion under the usual conilpotence and cofibrancy
hypotheses.  The object \(A^i\) is the Koszul dual coalgebra selected
by the bar construction, while \(A^!\) is the Verdier/Koszul dual
algebra after dualisation.  The derived chiral centre
\(Z_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{der}}(A)\) is the bulk object computed
through Hochschild cochains of the \(A\)-module category.  In the
\(d=3\) comparison, the \(\Etwo\)-layer lives on this centre or on the
Drinfeld centre of \(\Rep^{\Eone}(A)\).

\subsection*{The \texorpdfstring{\(\kappa_\bullet\)}{subscripted} invariants}

The \(K3\times E\) constants used here are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \kcat(K3\times E)
    &= \chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)
     = 2\cdot 0
     = 0,\\
  \kch(K3\times E)
    &= \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)
     = 0,\\
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)
    &= 3,\\
  \kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})
    &= c_1(0)/2
     = 10/2
     = 5,\\
  \kfib(K3)
    &= \operatorname{rank}\mathrm{II}_{4,20}
     = 24.
\end{aligned}
\]
The five displayed values come from distinct constructions: the
total-space categorical Euler
characteristic, the compact chiral Hodge supertrace, the
Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, the Borcherds denominator weight, and
the Mukai-lattice fibre rank.  The formula
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}
\]
is the BKM-denominator formula.  On the CHL ladder
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},\qquad
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
so the Borcherds weights are
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
These constants are independent of the compact total-space value
\(\kch(K3\times E)=0\).

\subsection*{The CoHA boundary}

The toric local model obeys
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)\simeq Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1).
\]
The full \(\cW_{1+\infty}\) object is recovered through the
Drinfeld-double, centre, completion, or evaluation passage appropriate
to the chosen comparison.  Thus the local toric computation supplies
the positive-half Hall algebra; the \(d=3\) \(\Etwo\)-braiding resides
on the centre or double layer associated to the \(\Eone\)-chiral output.

\subsection*{Sibling denominator constructions}

Different constructed specialisation data \((\Sigma_2,C)\) for the
same ambient \(\PhiFA_3(\Perf(K3\times E))\) can produce different
\(\Eone\)-chiral algebras on \(E\).  A Borcherds denominator conclusion
requires a matching automorphic input and a verified comparison map.
For the CHL cases, the verified constants are attached to the
frame-shape constructions and quotient data:
\[
  \Delta^{(N)}_{k(N)},\qquad
  k(N)=(5,4,3,2,1),\qquad
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
The \(N=2\) row has \(c_2(0)=8\) and
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_2)=c_2(0)/2=4,
\]
the weight of the corresponding CHL paramodular denominator.  Arbitrary
cycles in the smooth product \(K3\times E\) carry no BKM conclusion
until the cycle, the equivariant Mukai lattice, the denominator form,
and the Hall/Borcherds comparison are specified.
