\section*{Reduced DT and the positive effective geometry of $K3\times E$}

\subsection*{Attack–heal 1: the reduced virtual class}

On a compact holomorphic-symplectic surface $S = K3$, the
Pandharipande–Thomas and Donaldson–Thomas moduli spaces
$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DT}}(S,\beta,n)$ carry a perfect obstruction
theory whose virtual class vanishes on every nonzero curve class:
the symplectic form $\sigma \in H^{0}(K3,\Omega^{2})$ pairs with the
obstruction sheaf to produce a trivial quotient line bundle
$\mathcal{O}$, and integration against $\virred$ yields $0$.
\textbf{Flaw.} A vanishing class computes no enumerative invariant;
the naive deformation theory is over-determined on symplectic surfaces.
\textbf{Heal (Maulik–Pandharipande–Thomas 2010, arXiv:1001.2719).}
Kill the trivial quotient: write the obstruction sheaf as
$\mathrm{Ob} \twoheadrightarrow \mathcal{O}$ and reduce to
$\mathrm{Ob}^{\mathrm{red}} := \ker(\mathrm{Ob} \to \mathcal{O})$.
The reduced perfect obstruction theory of virtual dimension
$\mathrm{vd}^{\mathrm{red}} = \mathrm{vd} + 1$ produces
$\virred \in A_{\mathrm{vd}+1}(\Mred_{\mathrm{DT}}(S,\beta,n))$,
recovering the Kawai–Yoshioka 2000 formula for $\chi(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n} S)$
generating series and extending to all primitive classes
$\beta \in H_{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})$ with $\beta^{2} = 2n-2$.
Pass now to $X = K3\times E$. The projection $\pi_{E} : X \to E$ makes
every K3-primitive class $\gamma \in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})$ pushforward-trivial
on $E$, and the $(3,0)$-form on $X$ splits as
$\sigma_{K3}\wedge dz_{E}$. The K3 symplectic anomaly persists in
$X$-moduli on K3-primitive classes; the same reduction procedure
produces $\virred_{X}$ of virtual dimension $1$ rather than $0$.
\textbf{Resolution.} On K3-primitive classes of $X$, the reduced
virtual class is the only class supporting a nonzero curve count.

\subsection*{Attack–heal 2: $\Cstar_{E}$-equivariance}

The elliptic direction carries a free translation action
$\Cstar_{E} \curvearrowright E$ by the identity-component
$\mathrm{Aut}^{0}(E)$; lift to $\Cstar_{E} \curvearrowright X = K3\times E$
fibrewise-trivially on $K3$. The $\Cstar_{E}$-action is free on $E$
but fixes the zero-section and therefore descends to moduli.
\textbf{Flaw.} Free action has no fixed locus, so naive
Atiyah–Bott localisation is vacuous on the total space.
\textbf{Heal.} Use the fibre-over-$E$ structure of K3-primitive
curves: the stable pair $I_{\gamma,n}^{\bullet} \in D^{b}(X)$
supported in K3-fibres is $\Cstar_{E}$-translated among isomorphic
stable pairs; the quotient moduli $\Mred_{\mathrm{DT}}(X;\gamma)/\Cstar_{E}$
has virtual dimension $0$ and supports the reduced integration.
The equivariant Euler class $e_{\Cstar_{E}}(\mathrm{Nor}^{\mathrm{vir}})$
of the fibrewise normal bundle encodes the elliptic weight, and
its inversion introduces the $t$-variable in which Oberdieck–Pixton
expand $Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}}$. \textbf{Resolution.} $\Cstar_{E}$
is not a Białynicki-Birula localisation torus but an equivariant
weight bookkeeping device; the reduced integration is residual
with respect to this weight.

\subsection*{Attack–heal 3: discrete $\Auts(K3)$ and CHL orbifolds}

The symplectic automorphism group $\Auts(K3)$ acts on the K3-factor
through Mukai's eleven maximal subgroups of $M_{23}$; for the
Borcherds $\PhiTen$ setting the relevant symmetry is the Nikulin
involution lattice $N_{0}$ of rank $8$ with discriminant-form
$A_{1}^{8}(\tfrac{1}{2})$. \textbf{Flaw.} Without a discrete group,
the $\PhiTen$ denominator-formula genus-2 Jacobi structure has no
categorical origin in pure reduced DT. \textbf{Heal (CHL orbifold).}
Quotienting $K3\times E$ by the diagonal action
$(g,\tau) \in \Auts(K3)\times E[N]$ produces the Chaudhuri–Hockney–Lykken
threefolds of orbifold level $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The reduced DT
partition function on the CHL quotient specialises to the Borcherds
product of level $N$, and Oberdieck–Pixton 2017 (Invent Math 222,
Theorem~1) establish for $N=1$ that
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}}(K3\times E; \gamma \text{ primitive on } K3)(q,t,p)
  = -\frac{1}{\PhiTen(q,t,p)}
\]
with $\PhiTen$ the Igusa cusp form of weight $10$ on $\mathrm{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z})$.
\textbf{Resolution.} Discrete equivariance is not required for the
base $K3\times E$; it becomes the orbifold covariance of the
KKV/Oberdieck–Pandharipande wall-crossing and produces the
higher-$N$ Borcherds products through the same mechanism.

\subsection*{Attack–heal 4: contrast with toric CY$_{3}$}

In the toric case $X = \text{local } \mathbb{P}^{2}, \mathbb{C}^{3},
\text{resolved conifold}$, a $T^{3}$-action with isolated fixed
points reduces DT integration to lattice sums (topological vertex,
Iqbal–Kashani–Poor–Vafa). \textbf{Flaw for $K3\times E$.} A generic
K3 admits no nontrivial global torus action: $T^{r}\curvearrowright K3$
forces $r=0$ since $h^{1,0}(K3) = 0$ obstructs Hamiltonian vector
fields and symplectic automorphisms are finite. The $\Cstar_{E}$
translation is the entire continuous symmetry available.
\textbf{Heal.} Replace the toric triple
\[
  \text{fixed points} \;\to\; \text{integration} \;\to\; \text{vertex}
\]
by the reduced triple
\[
  \virred \;\to\; \Cstar_{E}\text{-residual integration} \;\to\;
  \Auts(K3)\text{-orbit averaging}.
\]
The first step kills the anomaly; the second introduces the
elliptic weight variable $t$; the third produces Mathieu-type
twinings for each Nikulin group element. The Igusa denominator
$\PhiTen$ is the generating-function consequence of all three
operations composed. \textbf{Resolution.} Toric vertex and reduced
DT are \emph{not} two instances of a common localisation; they are
structurally distinct because the K3 symmetry type admits no
continuous symplectic torus. The fixed-point paradigm is replaced,
not specialised.

\subsection*{Attack–heal 5: the positive effective geometry}

The positive half $\Ypos(K3\times E)$ is the cohomology of reduced
DT moduli, equivariant for $\Cstar_{E}$ and for the symplectic
discrete group: state
\[
  \boxed{\;
  \Ypos(K3\times E)
  \;=\;
  H^{\bullet}_{\Cstar_{E}\times \Auts(K3)}
    \bigl(\Mred_{\mathrm{DT}}(K3\times E;\gamma\text{ primitive on }K3)\bigr)
  \;}
\]
with character formula, on the K3-primitive sector,
\[
  \chi_{\Ypos}(q,t,p) \;=\; \frac{1}{\PhiTen(q,t,p)}
\]
per Oberdieck–Pixton 2017 (sign convention
$Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}} = -1/\PhiTen$ absorbed into the
standard grading). The four subscripted invariants
$\kch,\kcat,\kBKM,\kfib$ read off this geometry sit in the
Vol III spectrum $\{2,3,5,24\}$ through four distinct constructions:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3\times E}) = 0,
  \qquad
  \kch(K3\times E) = 2 \text{ on the K3 fibre shadow},
\]
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiTen) = c_{1}(0)/2 = 5 \text{ (Borcherds 1995; Gritsenko 1999)},
\qquad
  \kfib(K3) = 24.
\]
\textbf{Flaw.} The positive half alone does not recover $\PhiTen$;
the denominator identity requires the full bar–cobar and the BKM
Cartan generator. \textbf{Heal.} $\PhiTen$ is the Borcherds product
$\prod_{(m,n,r)>0}(1 - q^{m}p^{n}t^{r})^{c(4mn-r^{2})}$ with exponents
$c(k)$ from the Jacobi form $\phi_{10,1}$ of weight $10$ index $1$
(Gritsenko–Nikulin); the Koszul dual $B(\Ypos)$ contributes the
Cartan and negative generators and produces $1/\PhiTen$ as the full
denominator. \textbf{Resolution.} Reduced DT on $K3\times E$ is
the positive effective geometry; Oberdieck–Pixton 2017 is the
character statement; the full BKM algebra appears after bar–cobar.
