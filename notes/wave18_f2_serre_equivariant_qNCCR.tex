\section*{Serre-Compatible quasi-NCCR Hall Atlas}

\paragraph*{The Calabi-Yau Serre datum.}
Let \(X=S\times E\), with \(S\) a K3 surface and \(E\) an elliptic
curve, and set \(\cD=\Db(X)\). A choice of holomorphic volume form
\(\Omega_X=\Omega_S\wedge dz_E\) gives a trivialisation
\(\theta\colon \omega_X\xrightarrow{\sim}\cO_X\). Hence the Serre
functor is
\[
  \bS_X(-)=(-)\otimes \omega_X[3],
  \qquad
  \theta\colon \bS_X\xRightarrow{\sim}[3].
\]
The isomorphism \(\theta\) is part of the CY datum. It trivialises the
canonical-bundle tensor factor in the Serre functor; objects of \(\cD\)
remain distinct from their threefold shifts. If a Bridgeland
slicing \(\mathcal P_\sigma\) is fixed,
then \([1]\mathcal P_\sigma(\phi)=\mathcal P_\sigma(\phi+1)\), so
\(\bS_X\) carries stability angle \(\phi\) to stability angle
\(\phi+3\). A central charge
\(Z\colon K_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\to\bC\) is a homomorphism to a
two-dimensional real vector space. The Mukai pairing is a bilinear form
on the numerical lattice and gives a different datum from injectivity of
\(Z\).

\begin{definition}[Serre-compatible transition]
Let \(\mathcal A_\alpha,\mathcal A_\beta\subset\cD\) be hearts in a
finite wall-chamber atlas. A transition
\(\Psi_{\alpha\beta}\colon D^b(\mathcal A_\alpha)\to
D^b(\mathcal A_\beta)\) is \emph{Serre-compatible} if it is equipped
with a coherent natural isomorphism
\[
  \eta_{\alpha\beta}\colon
  \Psi_{\alpha\beta}\circ\bS_X
  \xRightarrow{\sim}
  \bS_X\circ\Psi_{\alpha\beta}.
\]
After the volume-form trivialisation \(\theta\), this is compatibility
with the shift \([3]\), together with the triple-overlap coherence for
the \(\eta_{\alpha\beta}\).
\end{definition}

\paragraph*{The obstruction to a global NCCR.}
The usual Van den Bergh \(\NCCR\) is an affine normal Gorenstein
singularity construction. On the smooth proper CY threefold \(X\), the
closest compact analogue would be a strong tilting generator \(T\) with
\(D^b(\End(T)\text{-mod})\simeq\cD\). Serre duality excludes such a
\(T\).

\begin{lemma}[Compact Serre obstruction]
\label{lem:compact-serre-obstruction}
Every nonzero \(T\in \Perf(X)\) satisfies
\[
  \Ext^3_X(T,T)\cong\Hom_X(T,T)^\vee ,
\]
so \(\Ext^3_X(T,T)\neq0\). Therefore classical tilting objects are
absent from \(\Perf(X)\), and a presentation by one finite-dimensional
ordinary algebra of finite global dimension cannot send its free module
to a classical tilting generator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Serre functor is \([3]\) after the volume-form trivialisation.
Serre duality gives the displayed perfect pairing. The identity of a
nonzero object gives a nonzero element of \(\Hom_X(T,T)\), hence a
nonzero top self-extension. A classical tilting object would require
\(\Ext^i_X(T,T)=0\) for all \(i>0\).
\end{proof}

The compact replacement for \(K3\times E\) is an equivariant
quasi-\(\NCCR\) Hall atlas: finite local algebra charts,
Serre-compatible Morita transitions, and reduced Hall integration data.

\paragraph*{Local quotient charts.}
Local \(\NCCR\) algebras still occur on singular charts. If
\(\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\bC)\) is an ADE subgroup acting on
\(\bC^2\) and trivially on a formal elliptic coordinate \(t\), then
\[
  R_{\Gamma,E}=\bC[[u,v,t]]^\Gamma,\qquad
  \Lambda_{\Gamma,E}
  =
  \End_{R_{\Gamma,E}}\!\left(\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat\Gamma}M_\rho\right)
  \cong
  \bC[[u,v,t]]\rtimes\Gamma
\]
is the stretched McKay \(\NCCR\) of the local quotient singularity.
These algebras are local affine-centre models. The compact coherent
input is \(\Perf(X)\); its finite charts may remember such local
quotient algebras, but Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-serre-obstruction}
prevents their descent to a single global Van den Bergh \(\NCCR\).

\begin{definition}[Finite Serre-compatible quasi-\(\NCCR\) Hall atlas]
Fix a component of the stability space on which a finite bounded
wall-chamber atlas \(\mathfrak U=\{U_\alpha\}\) has been chosen, and fix
a finite bound
\[
  B=(\text{charge height},\text{jet order},\text{Ran arity}).
\]
A finite Serre-compatible quasi-\(\NCCR\) Hall atlas of height \(B\) on
\(X=K3\times E\) consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a bounded heart window \(\mathcal A_{\alpha,B}\subset\cD\) over
  each \(U_\alpha\);
  \item a finite object \(T_{\alpha,B}\) split-generating the
  pretriangulated dg subcategory \(\cC_{\alpha,B}\subset\Perf(X)\)
  cut out by that window, and the degree-zero finite algebra
  \[
    \Lambda_{\alpha,B}:=
    H^0\End_{\cC_{\alpha,B}}(T_{\alpha,B});
  \]
  \item the cyclic minimal \(A_\infty\)-algebra
  \[
    \cE_{\alpha,B}:=\Ext_X^\bullet(T_{\alpha,B},T_{\alpha,B})
  \]
  with its CY\(_3\) trace pairing, potential \(W_{\alpha,B}\), and
  finite critical chart
  \((\Rep(\Lambda_{\alpha,B}),W_{\alpha,B})\);
  \item invertible \(A_\infty\)-bimodules \(P_{\alpha\beta,B}\)
  inducing derived Morita transitions
  \[
    \Psi_{\alpha\beta,B}\colon
    D^b(\Lambda_{\alpha,B}\text{-mod})
    \longrightarrow
    D^b(\Lambda_{\beta,B}\text{-mod});
  \]
  \item Serre-compatible natural isomorphisms
  \(\eta_{\alpha\beta,B}\) for these transitions;
  \item a specified null-homotopy of the triple-overlap gerbe
  \[
    \mathfrak o_{\alpha\beta\gamma,B}
    \in H^2\!\left(N(\mathfrak U),
    \mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(D^b\Coh(X))\right).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Finiteness refers to the truncation \(B\) and to the chosen atlas.
The symbol \((\Rep(\Lambda_{\alpha,B}),W_{\alpha,B})\) denotes the
Jacobi critical chart extracted from the cyclic \(A_\infty\)-model.
The compact moduli stack of coherent sheaves on \(X\) enters at the
compact Hall stage.
Compact Hall theory begins after the Mittag-Leffler limit in \(B\),
orientation transport, compact-support Beck-Chevalley, and compatibility
with the reduced integration map.
\end{definition}

\paragraph*{Positive-half Hall charts.}
For a finite datum \((\mathfrak U,B)\), define the chartwise critical
cohomology
\[
  \mathcal H_{\alpha,B}:=
  H^\bullet_{\bcrit}\bigl(\Rep(\Lambda_{\alpha,B}),W_{\alpha,B}\bigr).
\]
The Serre-compatible bimodules identify the \(\mathcal H_{\alpha,B}\)
on overlaps. The finite positive half is the Cech totalisation
\[
  \Yplus_{\mathfrak U,B}(X):=
  \Tot_{\alpha\in N(\mathfrak U)}
  \mathcal H_{\alpha,B}^{\mathrm{or},+},
\]
where \(\mathrm{or}\) records the orientation local system and \(+\)
selects the effective charge cone in a stability-angle window of
length \(1\).
The product is the Kontsevich-Soibelman critical Hall product on the
finite representation stacks, transported by the Morita bimodules.
The integration map carries the K3 cosection reduction and the quotient
by elliptic translation; finite charts carry only local algebra
generators.

This construction is a positive-half object. It is the compact analogue
of the local identity \(\CoHA(\bC^3)=Y^+\). The vertex algebra
\(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\), a Hall-Drinfeld double, and a BKM
denominator require the negative half, the Cartan torus, a
nondegenerate Hall-Serre pairing, and the radical quotient. The word
``Serre'' in the quasi-\(\NCCR\) datum refers to the categorical Serre
functor; Borcherds-Serre relations belong to the doubled BKM
recognition datum.

\begin{proposition}[Character constraint]
\label{prop:qnccr-character-constraint}
Assume that the finite Serre-compatible quasi-\(\NCCR\) data form a
Mittag-Leffler compatible system in \(B\), that the compact orientation
local system is trivialised, and that there is a Behrend-compatible
character map
\[
  \chi_{\mathrm{qNCCR}}\colon
  \varprojlim_B\Yplus_{\mathfrak U,B}(X)
  \longrightarrow
  \bC[[q,t,p]][(\PhiTenOP)^{-1}]
\]
whose scalar integration agrees with the reduced
Oberdieck-Pandharipande stable-pairs integration on the primitive K3
sector after quotient by the \(E\)-translation direction. Then
\[
  \chi_{\mathrm{qNCCR}}\!\left(
  \varprojlim_B\Yplus_{\mathfrak U,B}(K3\times E)\right)
  =
  Z^{\red,\prime}_{\DT}(K3\times E)
  =
  -\bigl(\PhiTenOP\bigr)^{-1}
  =
  -\Dfive^{-2}
  =
  -4096\,\DeltaFive^{-2}.
\]
Here
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive,
  \qquad
  \PhiTenOP=\Dfive^2,
  \qquad
  \PhiTenUn=\PhiTen=\DeltaFive^2.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  Z^{\red,\prime}_{\DT}(K3\times E)
  =
  -4096\,(\PhiTenUn)^{-1}
\]
in the unnormalised Igusa convention.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The assumptions produce a compact positive-half limit and a scalar
character. Oberdieck and Pandharipande identify the reduced primitive
stable-pairs/DT scalar of \(K3\times E\), with the Behrend sign and the
\(E\)-translation quotient, with
\(-(\PhiTenOP)^{-1}\). The OP convention uses the monic Borcherds
product \(\Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive\), so
\[
  \PhiTenOP=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\DeltaFive^2.
\]
The assumed character map is required to commute with that reduced
integration. Therefore its scalar value is the displayed OP scalar. This
proposition asserts the scalar constraint on the compact positive-half
limit. The Hall-Serre pairing, negative half, radical quotient, and
Hall-Drinfeld double belong to the later BKM recognition datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar character versus Hall recognition]
\label{prop:scalar-character-boundary}
The identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:qnccr-character-constraint} is a
necessary scalar constraint on a compact positive-half Hall atlas. An
algebra isomorphism
\[
  \varprojlim_B\Yplus_{\mathfrak U,B}(X)
  \cong
  U(\mathfrak g_{\DeltaFive})^{+},
\]
and the construction of the Hall-Drinfeld double are additional Hall
recognition theorems.

A Hall recognition theorem would require, in addition to the scalar
character, a nondegenerate Hall-Serre pairing, a Cartan lattice
identified with the Mukai lattice, an explicit negative half, a radical
quotient, and a comparison of the resulting denominator identity with
the Borcherds product \(D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\DeltaFive(2Z)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A character map forgets extension products, coproducts, pairings,
primitive classes, and radicals. Distinct graded algebras can have the
same graded scalar character. The Borcherds denominator theorem
recognises a generalized Kac-Moody algebra from a denominator identity
with root lattice, Weyl vector, signs, and multiplicities. The OP
scalar fixes the reciprocal square
\(-(\PhiTenOP)^{-1}\); doubled Hall recognition requires the data
listed above.
\end{proof}

\paragraph*{Constants and branch separation.}
The primitive CHL constants on the Borcherds-denominator side are
\[
  \bigl(c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0)\bigr)
  =
  (10,8,6,4,2),
  \qquad
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
  \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6)\bigr)
  =
  \frac12\bigl(c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0)\bigr)
  =
  (5,4,3,2,1).
\]
At \(N=1\), the primitive form is \(\DeltaFive\), \(c_1(0)=10\), and
\(\kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5\). These are Borcherds weights from the
Gritsenko-Nikulin and Gritsenko lift inputs. Hodge
supertraces and compact quasi-\(\NCCR\) scalars are separate rows.
The orders \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) lie outside this primitive compact CHL
ladder; when they are used, they belong to the non-CHL
Nikulin/Gritsenko or Gritsenko-Cl\'ery catalogues with their own
paramodular groups and row parameters.

The \(K3\times E\) values used here are
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=24.
\]
The compact Hodge supertrace is
\[
  \sum_{q=0}^3(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)
  =
  1-1+1-1=0.
\]
This atlas keeps \(\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}\) separate from the fibre Euler
term, from the compact supertrace, and from the Borcherds weight. There
are independent definitions for these invariants. The CHL formula is
\(\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\).

The symbol \(q\) in quasi-\(\NCCR\) abbreviates ``quasi''. Root-of-unity
specialisation is separate input. For \(N>1\), the constants above are
target Borcherds weights contingent on an equivariant hCS-to-Hall
comparison, a \(g_N\)-equivariant
\((\Sigma_2,C)\)-specialisation, and the Hall-Borcherds denominator
comparison.

For \(d=3\), the output of \(\Phi_3\) is \(E_1\)-chiral. Braided
\(E_2\) structure belongs to the Drinfeld centre, the double, or module
layers. The BKM/Hall-Drinfeld branch for \(K3\times E\) uses compact
Hall primitives and automorphic denominators. The Mukai self-mirror
branch uses stable envelopes and the rank-\(24\) Mukai current algebra.
The local equality \(\CoHA(\bC^3)=Y^+\) is the positive-half statement.
The \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\) identification requires the double,
Cartan currents, completion, and evaluation module.

\paragraph*{References and boundaries.}
The Serre functor statement is the smooth proper Serre-duality theorem.
The absence of a strong compact tilting generator follows from the
CY threefold Ext pairing of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact-serre-obstruction}.
Van den Bergh's \(\NCCR\) definition applies to affine normal
Gorenstein centres; Bridgeland-King-Reid and Van den Bergh supply the
local quotient/NCCR models. The wall-chamber language is Bridgeland
stability theory. The critical Hall product is the Kontsevich-Soibelman
and Davison-Meinhardt construction on finite critical charts. The OP
scalar is Oberdieck-Pandharipande reduced stable-pairs theory,
normalised by the monic product \(D_5\). The identities
\(\PhiTenUn=\DeltaFive^2\), \(\Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive\), and
\(\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2\) are the Gritsenko-Nikulin, Gritsenko, and
Borcherds weight normalisations used in the Vol III invariant table.
The scalar proposition assumes the compact
positive-half limit and character map. Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar-character-boundary}
records the remaining Hall recognition obligations.
