\paragraph{Finite etale CHL quotients.}
Let \(N\in\{4,6\}\).  Choose a symplectic automorphism
\(g_N\in\mathrm{Aut}_s(K3)\) of order \(N\) and an exact
\(N\)-torsion translation \(t_N\in E[N]\).  The diagonal action
\[
  \sigma_N=(g_N,t_N)
\]
on \(K3\times E\) is free because \(t_N\) is free on \(E\).  Thus
\[
  X_N=(K3\times E)/\langle\sigma_N\rangle
\]
is a smooth finite etale quotient of \(K3\times E\).  The quotient is a
Calabi--Yau threefold in the holomorphic-volume sense: \(g_N\) fixes the
K3 holomorphic two-form and \(t_N\) fixes the elliptic one-form.

The Hodge column is computed on invariants.  Translation acts trivially
on \(H^{0,\bullet}(E)\), and a symplectic K3 automorphism acts trivially
on \(H^{0,0}(K3)\oplus H^{0,2}(K3)\).  Therefore
\[
  H^{0,\bullet}(X_N)
  \cong
  \bigl(H^{0,\bullet}(K3)\otimes H^{0,\bullet}(E)\bigr)^{\langle\sigma_N\rangle}
  =
  (1,1,1,1),
\]
and
\[
  \kcat(X_N)=\chi(\mathcal O_{X_N})=0,\qquad
  \kch(X_N)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X_N)=0.
\]

\paragraph{Primitive CHL constants.}
The primitive CHL Borcherds ladder is indexed by
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
For its Jacobi input \(\phi^{(g_N)}_{0,1}\), let \(c_N(0)\) denote the
discriminant-zero coefficient.  Borcherds' weight theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2},
\]
with
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)&5&4&3&2&1 .
\end{array}
\]
Thus \(N=4\) has \(c_4(0)=4\) and \(\kBKM(\Phi_4)=2\), while
\(N=6\) has \(c_6(0)=2\) and \(\kBKM(\Phi_6)=1\).

\paragraph{Separation from the Gritsenko--Clery catalogue.}
The Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue is an eight-row
paramodular classification with twice-weight tuple
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
It shares the level-one \(\Delta_5\) row with the primitive CHL ladder.
The remaining rows have their own paramodular group, diagonal divisor,
multiplier, and Borcherds input.  A CHL-to-Gritsenko--Clery comparison
therefore requires a row map as part of the theorem.

The level-one normalisation is
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\DeltaFive^2,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\DeltaFive^2.
\]
The scalar-square convention at \(N=1\) is separate from the primitive
Borcherds weight
\[
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=10/2=5.
\]

\paragraph{Surface fixed data.}
The surface quotient \(K3/\langle g_N\rangle\) supplies useful local
checks, while \(X_N\) itself is smooth because of the elliptic
translation.  At \(N=4\), a symplectic automorphism has
\[
  \#\mathrm{Fix}(g_4)=4,\qquad \#\mathrm{Fix}(g_4^2)=8.
\]
The \(g_4\)-fixed points give four \(A_3\) surface singularities, and
the remaining \(g_4^2\)-fixed orbits give two \(A_1\) singularities on
the surface quotient.  At \(N=6\),
\[
  \#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6)=2,\qquad
  \#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6^2)=6,\qquad
  \#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6^3)=8,
\]
so
\[
  K3/\langle g_6\rangle
  \quad\text{has type}\quad
  2A_5+2A_2+2A_1.
\]
The \(N=6\) umbral anchor is the Niemeier root system \(6D_4\).  The
Niemeier lattice \(4A_5+D_4\) is a different Coxeter-six entry.

\paragraph{Reduced Donaldson--Thomas input.}
The \(N=1\) reduced \(K3\times E\) scalar is the Oberdieck--Pixton and
Oberdieck--Pandharipande theorem in the OP normalisation:
\[
  \DTred(K3\times E)=-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
  =-\Dfive^{-2}
  =-4096\,\DeltaFive^{-2}.
\]
For \(N=4,6\), the CHL reduced-pairs extension is a separate input.  A
formula for \(\DTred(X_N)\) must specify:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \text{the equivariant reduced obstruction theory on }K3\times E,\\
  \text{descent through the free finite quotient }X_N,\\
  \text{rubber and degeneration calculus compatible with }g_N,\\
  \text{the scalar convention: primitive }\Phi_N\text{ or a square line.}
\end{gathered}
\]
Bryan--Cadman--Young and Bryan--Steinberg provide orbifold DT technology
for local and wall-crossing settings.  A global CHL reduced-pairs
identity on \(X_N\) additionally requires the four data just displayed.

\paragraph{BKM and Hall scope.}
The automorphic theorem proves
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2.
\]
It supplies the denominator weight.  A compact Hall or BPS algebra
interpretation requires an oriented reduced critical CoHA, coproduct,
Hall pairing, radical quotient, parity convention, PBW theorem, and
Borcherds bracket comparison.

\paragraph{Invariant summary.}
For the untwisted \(K3\times E\) reference,
\[
  \kcat=0,\qquad
  \kch=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \kfib=24.
\]
For the smooth finite etale CHL quotients \(X_N\), \(N=4,6\),
\[
  \kcat(X_N)=0,\qquad
  \kch(X_N)=0,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_4)=2,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_6)=1.
\]
Any fibre invariant for \(X_N\) must name the invariant Mukai lattice or
the chosen quotient fibre datum.
