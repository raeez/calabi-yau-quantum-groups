\section*{Low-discriminant denominator exponents of
\texorpdfstring{$\gDeltaFive$}{g Delta5}}

Put \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\) and \(r=e^{2\pi iz}\).  The generator of
\(J^{\mathrm{weak}}_{0,1}\) in the Eichler and Zagier normalization is
\[
  \phizo(\tau,z)
  =
  4\sum_{i=2}^{4}
  \left(\frac{\theta_i(\tau,z)}{\theta_i(\tau,0)}\right)^2
  =
  \sum_{a\geq 0,\ \ell\in\Z} f(a,\ell)q^a r^\ell .
\]
Elliptic translation for index \(1\) gives
\[
  f(a,\ell)=c(4a-\ell^2),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{supp}(c)\subset
  \{D\in\Z:D\geq -1,\ D\equiv 0,3 \pmod 4\}.
\]
Exact expansion of the theta-ratio identity through \(q^4\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\phizo
={}& r^{-1}+10+r \\
&+q(10r^{-2}-64r^{-1}+108-64r+10r^2)\\
&+q^2(r^{-3}+108r^{-2}-513r^{-1}+808-513r+108r^2+r^3)\\
&+q^3(-64r^{-3}+808r^{-2}-2752r^{-1}+4016-2752r+808r^2-64r^3)\\
&+q^4(10r^{-4}-513r^{-3}+4016r^{-2}-11775r^{-1}
      +16524-11775r+4016r^2-513r^3+10r^4)+O(q^5).
\end{aligned}
\]
The required discriminant coefficients are
\[
  \begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
    D & -1 & 0 & 3 & 4 & 7 & 8 & 11 & 12 & 15 \\ \hline
    c(D) & 1 & 10 & -64 & 108 & -513 & 808 & -2752 & 4016 & -11775 .
  \end{array}
\]
They are Jacobi input coefficients.  The Gritsenko and Nikulin product
below reads them as denominator exponents.

Let \(Z=(\tau,z,\omega)\), \(s=e^{2\pi i\omega}\), and use the
Gritsenko and Nikulin positive chamber
\[
  (n,\ell,m)>0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  m>0,\quad\hbox{or}\quad m=0,\ n>0,\quad\hbox{or}\quad m=n=0,\ \ell<0 .
\]
In this chamber the monic Borcherds product is
\[
  D_5(Z)
  =
  q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
  \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}
  (1-q^n r^\ell s^m)^{f(nm,\ell)}
  =
  64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z).
\]
The Weyl-Kac-Borcherds denominator of the automorphic correction is
\(D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)\).  For the root
\[
  \alpha(n,\ell,m)=2nf_2-\ell f_3+2mf_{-2}
\]
one has
\[
  (\alpha,\alpha)=2(\ell^2-4nm)=-2D,
  \qquad D=4nm-\ell^2,
\]
and the signed root supermultiplicity is the product exponent
\[
  \smult_{\gDeltaFive}(\alpha(n,\ell,m))
  =
  \dim\gDeltaFive_{\alpha,\bar 0}
  -
  \dim\gDeltaFive_{\alpha,\bar 1}
  =
  f(nm,\ell)
  =
  c(4nm-\ell^2).
\]
The greatest common divisor of \((n,\ell,m)\) is part of the charge; the
already normalized product exponent is read directly from \(f(nm,\ell)\).
Divisor terms enter the separate problem of recovering exponents from
ordinary product coefficients or from logarithmic coefficients:
\[
  \log\prod_{\beta>0}(1-X^\beta)^{\smult(\beta)}
  =
  -\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\smult(\beta)}{j}X^{j\beta}.
\]
Thus the imprimitive charge \((2,2,2)\) has product exponent
\(c(12)=4016\).  The expression \(4016-\frac12c(3)=4048\) belongs to a
Mobius recovery calculation for a different coefficient convention.

\[
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
  \begin{array}{r|ccc|c|c|c|l}
   k & n & \ell & m & D=4nm-\ell^{2} & \gcd(n,\ell,m)
     & \smult_{\gDeltaFive}(\alpha) & \text{root character}\\ \hline
   1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 1 & c(-1)=1
     & \text{real}\\
   2 & 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & c(0)=10
     & \text{isotropic, positive signed superdimension}\\
   3 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 3 & 1 & c(3)=-64
     & \text{imaginary, negative signed superdimension}\\
   4 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 4 & 1 & c(4)=108
     & \text{imaginary, positive signed superdimension}\\
   5 & 2 & 1 & 1 & 7 & 1 & c(7)=-513
     & \text{imaginary, negative signed superdimension}\\
   6 & 2 & 0 & 1 & 8 & 1 & c(8)=808
     & \text{imaginary, positive signed superdimension}\\
   7 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 11 & 1 & c(11)=-2752
     & \text{imaginary, negative signed superdimension}\\
   8 & 2 & 2 & 2 & 12 & 2 & c(12)=4016
     & \text{imprimitive charge, normalized product exponent}\\
   9 & 3 & 0 & 1 & 12 & 1 & c(12)=4016
     & \text{imaginary, positive signed superdimension}\\
   10 & 4 & 1 & 1 & 15 & 1 & c(15)=-11775
     & \text{imaginary, negative signed superdimension}
  \end{array}
\]

The constant \(c(0)=10\) appears in two places.  It is the signed
supermultiplicity of the isotropic exponent \(f(1,2)=c(0)=10\), and
Borcherds weight theorem gives
\[
  \kappaBKM(\Delta_5)
  =
  \operatorname{wt}(\Delta_5)
  =
  \frac{c(0)}{2}
  =
  5 .
\]
The formula \(\kappaBKM(\Phi)=c(0)/2\) is meaningful after fixing the
Borcherds input and the product normalization.  For the primitive CHL
denominator ladder \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\),
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
  \qquad
  \kappaBKM(\Phi_N)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]

For \(X=K3\times E\), the compact Hodge column is
\[
  (h^{0,0},h^{0,1},h^{0,2},h^{0,3})(X)=(1,1,1,1),
  \qquad
  \sum_{q=0}^{3}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)=0.
\]
The invariant lanes are therefore
\[
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
  \begin{array}{c|c|l}
  \text{invariant} & \text{value} & \text{construction}\\ \hline
  \kappaCat(X) & 0 &
    \chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot 0\\
  \kappaCh(X) & 0 &
    \text{compact Hodge supertrace}\\
  \kappaHeis(X) & 3 &
    \kappaCh(K3)+\kappaCh(E)=2+1\\
  \kappaBKM(\gDeltaFive) & 5 &
    \operatorname{wt}(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2\\
  \kappaFiber(K3) & 24 &
    \operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=1+22+1 .
  \end{array}
\]
These five entries come from the total-space holomorphic Euler
characteristic, the compact chiral Hodge supertrace, the Heisenberg
specialization, the BKM denominator weight, and the K3 Mukai lattice.

The Cl\'ery and Gritsenko diagonal-divisor catalogue is the eight-row
\(dd\)-modular system
\[
\begin{gathered}
  (1,1;5),\ (2,1;2),\ (1,2;3),\ (3,1;1),\ (1,3;2),\\
  (4,1;\tfrac12),\ (1,4;\tfrac32),\ (2,2;1),
\end{gathered}
\]
with row weight equal to the third entry.  It intersects the primitive
CHL ladder at the \(\Delta_5\) row \((1,1;5)\), and its row
normalization and indexing are those of the diagonal-divisor theorem.

The ordinary Mathieu table is the ATLAS \(26\)-class table
\[
\{1A,2A,2B,3A,3B,4A,4B,4C,5A,6A,6B,7A,7B,
8A,10A,11A,12A,12B,14A,14B,15A,15B,21A,21B,23A,23B\}.
\]
The rows needed for the low-order twining comparison are
\[
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
  \begin{array}{c|c}
  [g] & \text{\(24\)-point cycle shape}\\ \hline
  3B & 3^8\\
  4A & 2^4 4^4\\
  4B & 1^4 2^2 4^4\\
  6B & 6^4\\
  10A & 2^2 10^2\\
  12A & 2\,4\,6\,12\\
  12B & 12^2 .
  \end{array}
\]
A denominator theorem for an ordinary Mathieu row requires, in addition
to this cycle shape, a Jacobi input, a multiplier, a paramodular group,
and a Cartan datum.

The scalar square is a third normalization:
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2 .
\]
The first equality squares the universal Siegel product and doubles the
weight from \(5\) to \(10\).  The second is the Oberdieck and Pixton
scalar product.  The scalar \(4096^{-1}\) changes neither the weight nor
the exponents of \(D_5\).  The Fake Monster product uses the input
\(j(\tau)-744\), constant term \(24\), and weight \(12\).

The coefficient table determines exactly the signed product exponents of
\(\gDeltaFive\).  Parity-refined root-space dimensions, bracket
constants, Chevalley-Eilenberg differentials, compact Hall products, and
source-to-target comparison maps require algebraic data beyond these
Fourier coefficients.
