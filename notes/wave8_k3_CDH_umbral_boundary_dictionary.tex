\section{K3 Umbral Boundary Data and Diagonal-Divisor Rows}
\label{sec:cdh-umbral-boundary-dictionary}

\paragraph{Three index sets.}
Three arithmetic lists meet near the integers $5,7,8$.

The Nikulin--Mukai classification of finite symplectic automorphisms of
a K3 surface permits cyclic orders
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}.
\]
The diagonal compact CHL construction on $K3\times E$ also needs an
automorphism of the elliptic curve fixing the origin.  For a complex
elliptic curve,
\[
 \mathrm{Aut}(E,0)=\mathcal O_{\mathrm{End}(E)}^\times,
\]
so its possible finite orders are
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
Indeed the generic unit group is $\{\pm1\}$, the $j=1728$ curve has
Gaussian units of order $4$, and the $j=0$ curve has Eisenstein units
of order $6$.

Cheng--Duncan--Harvey umbral moonshine is indexed by Niemeier root
systems $X$ with Coxeter number $m(X)$.  The three K3 orders outside
the elliptic slice match the following Niemeier Coxeter numbers:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
N & 5 & 7 & 8 \\ \hline
X & A_4^6 & A_6^4 & A_7^2D_5^2 \\
m(X) & 5 & 7 & 8 .
\end{array}
\]
This table is a Coxeter-number comparison.  It supplies a boundary
surface between K3 automorphism data and CDH umbral data.  A row theorem
requires a paramodular group, Borcherds input, Weyl chamber, and CY$_3$
host as additional data.

The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue is indexed by
paramodular triples $(t,N_{\mathrm{dd}};k)$.  Its complete row data are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{row} & (t,N_{\mathrm{dd}};k) & 2k \\ \hline
1 & (1,1;5) & 10 \\
2 & (2,1;2) & 4 \\
3 & (1,2;3) & 6 \\
4 & (3,1;1) & 2 \\
5 & (1,3;2) & 4 \\
6 & (4,1;\tfrac12) & 1 \\
7 & (1,4;\tfrac32) & 3 \\
8 & (2,2;1) & 2 .
\end{array}
\]
Thus the GC twice-weight tuple is
\[
 (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
This tuple is independent of both the primitive CHL ladder and the
CDH/K3 order-indexed boundary list.

\paragraph{Borcherds constants.}
For a Borcherds product $\Phi$ with input constant term $c(0)$,
Borcherds' singular-theta theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi)=\wt(\Phi)=\frac{c(0)}{2}.
\]
On the primitive CHL denominator ladder $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ this gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2 \\
\kBKM(\Phi_N) & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 .
\end{array}
\]
The $N=1$ primitive denominator is the Gritsenko--Nikulin square root
$\Delta_5$ of weight $5$.  In the Oberdieck--Pandharipande
normalisation the monic Borcherds product is
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2.
\]

For the K3 order-indexed twined inputs at $N=5,7,8$, once the twined
Jacobi input, cover, character, chamber, and product normalisation are
fixed, the constants are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
N & 5 & 7 & 8 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 4 & 2 & 2 \\
\kBKM(\Phi_N) & 2 & 1 & 1 .
\end{array}
\]
These are the extended Nikulin/K3 twining constants.  The
half-integral GC rows $(4,1;\tfrac12)$ and $(1,4;\tfrac32)$ live on
multiplier systems in the diagonal-divisor catalogue and carry their
own row data.

\paragraph{Umbral comparison surface.}
The CDH systems $A_4^6$, $A_6^4$, and $A_7^2D_5^2$ lie outside the
$24A_1$ Mathieu root system in the Niemeier list. Their Coxeter
numbers match the K3 symplectic orders $5,7,8$, giving an umbral
comparison surface.  A theorem identifying any one CDH/K3 boundary
entry with a GC row must name the automorphic group, cover, character,
diagonal divisor, Jacobi input, Weyl chamber, leading coefficient, and
denominator identity.  The Coxeter-number table supplies only the first
integer label.

\paragraph{Geometric boundary.}
The compact diagonal quotient $(K3\times E)/\mathbb Z_N$ used in the
primitive CHL slice has a fixed-origin model only at
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.  An $N$-torsion translation on $E$ is useful for a
free quotient problem; it acts trivially on $H^{1,0}(E)$ and supplies a
different automorphic linearisation from the CM action.  Orders
$5,7,8$ therefore remain boundary automorphic rows until a replacement
host theorem is constructed.

Such a host theorem consists of:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(i)}&\text{a Calabi--Yau quotient or stack with orientation data;}\\
\text{(ii)}&\text{a reduced stable-pairs or Donaldson--Thomas theory on it;}\\
\text{(iii)}&\text{a protected partition function equal to the chosen product;}\\
\text{(iv)}&\text{a Hall or chiral primitive object with the same Jacobi input.}
\end{array}
\]
If such a host is a CY$_3$ input to $\Phi$, its native chiral output is
an $E_1$-chiral algebra; the $E_2$ structure lives on the Drinfeld
centre of its representation category.

\paragraph{K3 \texorpdfstring{$\times$}{x} E invariant split.}
Let $Y=K3\times E$.  K\"unneth gives
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(Y)=(1,1,1,1),\qquad
  \kch(Y)=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(Y)=0.
\]
The total-space categorical invariant is
\[
  \kcat(Y)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
The construction-distinct invariants on the canonical K3$\times E$
surface are
\[
  \kcat(Y)=0,\qquad
  \kch(Y)=0,\qquad
  \kHeis(Y)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\mathbb Z)=24.
\]
The compact supertrace $0$, the Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation $3$,
the Borcherds weight $5$, and the fibre Mukai rank $24$ are values of
different constructions.  The rank $24$ attached to a Niemeier root
system is a lattice rank; it becomes a $\kfib$ value only after a fibre
geometry has been specified.  The GC weights $1/2$ and $3/2$ are modular
weights on multiplier systems.

\paragraph{Branch separation.}
The BKM-side K3 object is governed by Borcherds products, Hall positive
halves, and the Hall--Drinfeld double once the compact Hall comparison
exists.  The Mukai self-mirror K3 Yangian branch is a separate current
algebra and stable-envelope construction.  This note uses only the
Borcherds/Hall boundary data.

diagonal-divisor rows and the tuple $(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)$.
