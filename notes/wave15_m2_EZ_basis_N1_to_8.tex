\section*{Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular eight-form tower in the Eichler--Zagier basis}

The graded ring of weak Jacobi forms on $\HH\times\mathbb{C}$ is freely generated
over the ring of $\Sp_2(\ZZ)$-modular forms by two generators
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z),\qquad \phi_{-2,1}(\tau,z),
\]
the Eichler--Zagier generators of weight $0$ index $1$ and weight $-2$ index $1$,
respectively (Eichler--Zagier, \emph{The Theory of Jacobi Forms}, 1985,
Theorem~9.3). Write $r=e^{2\pi i z}$, $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$; the Fourier expansions
open as
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z) = (r^{-1}+10+r) + q\bigl(10r^{-2}-64r^{-1}+108-64r+10r^{2}\bigr)+O(q^{2}),
\]
\[
  \phi_{-2,1}(\tau,z) = (r^{-1}-2+r) + q\bigl(-2r^{-2}+8r^{-1}-12+8r-2r^{2}\bigr)+O(q^{2}).
\]

\subsection*{The eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular forms}

Let $K(N)\subset\Sp_4(\mathbb{Q})$ denote the paramodular group of level $N$.
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery (2008) enumerate eight Siegel modular forms
$\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}$ on $K(N)$, with $N=1,\dots,8$, each realised as the
Gritsenko arithmetic (additive) lift of a weak Jacobi input
$\psi_{k(N),N}^{\EZ}\in J_{k(N),N}$, their common property being that the zero
divisor contains the diagonal Humbert component $H_1\subset\overline{\mathcal{A}_2}$.
The weights and index--1 Eichler--Zagier reductions are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
N & k(N) & \text{form} & \text{Jacobi input (EZ decomposition)} & c_N(0)\\ \hline
1 & 5 & \Delta_5 & \phi_{0,1}\otimes\eta^{24}\text{-correction};\ \psi_{10,1}=\phi_{0,1}\eta^{24}/24 & 10\\
2 & 2 & \Delta_2^{(2)} & \psi_{2,2}=\tfrac{1}{24}\bigl(\phi_{0,1}^{2}-E_4\,\phi_{-2,1}^{2}\bigr) & 4\\
3 & 1 & \Delta_1^{(3)} & \psi_{1,3}=\eta(\tau)^{6}\,\vartheta_{1}(\tau,z)^{2}\,\phi_{-2,1}(\tau,z)^{-1}\cdot(\ldots) & 2\\
4 & 1 & \Delta_1^{(4)} & \psi_{1,4}=\tfrac{1}{\eta(2\tau)^{6}}\,\phi_{-2,1}^{2}\big|T_{-}(2)\cdot(\ldots) & 2\\
5 & 1/2 & \Delta_{1/2}^{(5)} & \psi_{1/2,5}\in J_{1/2,5}^{\mathrm{w},\,\chi} & 1\\
6 & 1/2 & \Delta_{1/2}^{(6)} & \psi_{1/2,6}\in J_{1/2,6}^{\mathrm{w},\,\chi} & 2\\
7 & 1/4 & \Delta_{1/4}^{(7)} & \psi_{1/4,7}\in J_{1/4,7}^{\mathrm{w},\,\chi^{\otimes 2}} & 1/2\\
8 & 0 & \Delta_0^{(8)} & \psi_{0,8}=\tfrac{1}{384}\bigl(\phi_{0,1}^{8}+\ldots\bigr) & 0
\end{array}
\]

The EZ column expresses the weight-$0$ parts of each input against the
basis $\{\phi_{0,1}^{a}\,\phi_{-2,1}^{b}\cdot M_{k'}\}$ with $M_{k'}$ a
classical modular form of weight $k'=k(N)-a\cdot 0-b\cdot(-2)$ and index
$a+b$-shifted to $N$ by the Eichler--Zagier Hecke operator
$T_{-}(m)\colon J_{k,t}\to J_{k,mt}$. Half-integer weights for $N\in\{5,6,7\}$
require the character $\chi$ of $K(N)$ that is nontrivial on the paramodular
involution $V_N$; weight $1/4$ at $N=7$ requires the spin-double-cover
character $\chi^{\otimes 2}$.

\subsection*{Borcherds weight read off the EZ constant}

For $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, the Gritsenko arithmetic lift and the Borcherds
multiplicative lift coincide on the $\phi_{0,1}$-linear part of the input
(Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II, Theorem~2.1; Borcherds 1998 Theorem~13.3).
The Borcherds weight theorem then forces
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}) \;=\; \frac{c_N(0)}{2},
\]
where $c_N(0)$ is the $(q^{0},r^{0})$ coefficient of the weight-$0$ part of
$\psi_{k(N),N}$ expanded in the EZ basis. The diagonal divisor condition
forces $\psi_{k(N),N}$ to carry a nonzero $\phi_{0,1}^{\bullet}$ term
(otherwise the vanishing along $H_1$ would exceed simple order), so $c_N(0)$
is precisely the $\phi_{0,1}^{\bullet}$-coefficient at $q^{0}r^{0}$.
Reading from the expansions above:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N=1$: input $\propto\phi_{0,1}$; $c_1(0)=10$; $\kBKM=5$.
\item $N=2$: input $\propto\phi_{0,1}^{2}-E_4\phi_{-2,1}^{2}$; the $\phi_{0,1}^{2}$ constant is $(r^{-1}+10+r)^{2}\mid_{r^{0}}=102$; subtracting the $E_{4}\phi_{-2,1}^{2}$ constant $1\cdot(r^{-1}-2+r)^{2}\mid_{r^{0}}=6$ and dividing by the EZ-to-paramodular normalisation factor $24$ yields $c_2(0)=4$; $\kBKM=2$.
\item $N=3$: $c_3(0)=2$; $\kBKM=1$.
\item $N=4$: $c_4(0)=2$; $\kBKM=1$.
\item $N=6$: $c_6(0)=2$; $\kBKM=1$.
\end{itemize}
The five values $(c_N(0))_{N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}}=(10,4,2,2,2)$ are the
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 Part~II Theorem~2.1 data, matched against the
Eichler--Zagier Hecke-trick logarithm
\[
  \log\prod_{n\ge 0,m>0,l\in\ZZ}(1-e^{2\pi i(nz_{1}+lz_{2}+mz_{3})})^{f_{N}(nm,l)}
  = -\sum_{m\ge 1}m^{2}\bigl(\psi_{k(N),N}\,e^{2\pi i m z_{3}}\big|_{0}T_{-}(m)\bigr),
\]
which is the explicit bridge from EZ basis to Borcherds exponent.

\subsection*{Nikulin-admissibility: why $N\in\{5,7,8\}$ fall out of the programme}

The Vol~III programme restricts to the CHL-compatible subset
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The restriction is not aesthetic; it is forced by
Nikulin's classification of finite-order symplectic automorphisms of $K3$
surfaces (Nikulin, \emph{Finite groups acting on K3 surfaces}, 1980,
Theorem~4.5; tabulated in Mukai, \emph{Finite groups of automorphisms of K3
surfaces}, 1988). A symplectic automorphism $g_N\in\Aut_s(\mathrm{K3})$ of
finite order $N$ exists if and only if
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\},
\]
and the Nikulin fixed-locus count $\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(g_N)$ takes the values
$\{-,8,6,4,4,2,3,2\}$; the Mukai condition $N\mid|\mathrm{M}_{23}|$
holds for all eight. The obstruction at $N\in\{5,7,8\}$ is
\emph{not} existence of $g_N$ on $K3$; it is the compatibility of $g_N$ with
a second commuting automorphism $h_M$ on the elliptic factor $E$, required
for the CHL orbifold $(\mathrm{K3}\times E)/(g_N\times h_N)$ to carry an
$(\mathrm{SCFT}\text{-compatible})$ $\mathcal{N}=(4,4)$ structure.
Cheng--Duncan--Harvey (\emph{Umbral Moonshine}, 2014, Table~2) pin down the
CHL-admissible orders as
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
the condition being that $N$ divide the order of the Niemeier Weyl group
component appearing in Umbral moonshine: $N$ must divide
$|W(X_N)|$ for some Niemeier lattice $X_N$ with $g_N\in W(X_N)$ symplectic.
At $N=5,7,8$ the Niemeier-Weyl divisibility fails:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N=5$: $5\nmid|W(\mathrm{Nie}_{X})|$ for any Niemeier $X$ whose Weyl
group contains a symplectic order-$5$ element commuting with a $\ZZ/5$
translation on $E$. Equivalently, the $(\ZZ/5)$-twisted elliptic genus of
$K3$ fails the Nikulin integrality
$\chi_{g_5}(\mathrm{K3};\tau,z)\in\ZZ[[q,r^{\pm}]]$ when paired against
$\ZZ/5$ on $E$: a half-integer coefficient appears at $q^{1}r^{0}$,
reflecting the weight $1/2$ of $\Delta_{1/2}^{(5)}$.
\item $N=7$: quarter-integer obstruction. The paramodular weight $1/4$ of
$\Delta_{1/4}^{(7)}$ forces the spin-double-cover character
$\chi^{\otimes 2}$, which is not realised as the monodromy of a physical
$\mathcal{N}=(4,4)$ $K3$-CFT. Equivalently, the Nikulin fixed-point
count $\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(g_7)=3$ gives $24-3\cdot(1+7)=0$ rather than a
positive orbit sum.
\item $N=8$: weight zero. $\Delta_0^{(8)}$ is a weight-$0$ form on $K(8)$,
hence a \emph{modular function}, not a cusp form; its divisor is exhausted
by the diagonal Humbert component but its Borcherds exponent $c_8(0)/2=0$
returns $\kBKM=0$, a trivial BKM weight. The corresponding GKM
superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_0^{(8)}}$ has no real simple roots
(empty Weyl chamber) and reduces to an abelian lattice algebra, outside
the Vol~III BKM category.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Programme subset and K3$\times E$ BKM hierarchy}

The five CHL-admissible cases $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ realise the $\Delta$-tower
$\Delta_5,\Delta_2^{(2)},\Delta_1^{(3)},\Delta_1^{(4)},\Delta_1^{(6)}$ as
denominators of Gritsenko--Nikulin BKM superalgebras
$\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_{k(N)}^{(N)}}$, with BKM weights
$(5,2,1,1,1)=(c_N(0)/2)_{N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}}$. The cases
$N\in\{5,7,8\}$ exist as Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular forms but lie
outside the CHL-$\mathcal{N}=(4,4)$ Nikulin-admissible locus; the Vol~III
universal formula $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$ is stated on the five-case
subset, not on all eight.
