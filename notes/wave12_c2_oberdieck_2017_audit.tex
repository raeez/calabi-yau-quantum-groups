\subsection*{Paper identification}

arXiv:1706.10100 is \emph{``Holomorphic anomaly equations and the Igusa
cusp form conjecture''} by Georg Oberdieck and Aaron Pixton (submitted
2017-06-30, v2 2018-02-21; published Invent.\ Math.\ \textbf{222}
(2020), \textsc{doi}:10.1007/s00222-018-0794-0).  The paper is
\emph{not} single-authored: every treatise citation ``Oberdieck 2017''
for this result must read ``Oberdieck--Pixton''.  The genuine
Oberdieck-solo 2018 references (\emph{Geom.\ Topol.} 22 \S5, \emph{Duke
Math.\ J.} 167) are a different corpus (Gromov--Witten of $K3$, not of
$K3\times E$) and must not be conflated (AP-CY-type scope slip;
cf.\ the Wave-10 BOP-2019 fabrication caught by Wave-11 U2).

\subsection*{Main theorem, verbatim}

Let $S$ be a non-singular projective K3 surface, $E$ an elliptic curve,
$X = S\times E$.  For $\beta\in H_2(S,\mathbb Z)$ \emph{primitive} with
$\langle\beta,\beta\rangle=2h-2$, and $d\ge 0$, the reduced
Gromov--Witten invariant $\Nred_{g,\beta,d}^{X}$ is defined via the
one-dimensional reduced virtual class on $\Mbar_g^{\bullet}(X,(\beta,d))$
and a point insertion dual to the $E$-translation orbit (Oberdieck
2017, eq.~(1)).  Set
\[
  \Zred(u,q,\tilde q)
  \;=\; \sum_{g\ge 0}\sum_{h\ge 0}\sum_{d\ge 0}
        \Nred_{g,h,d}\, u^{2g-2} q^{h-1}\tilde q^{d-1}.
\]

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Oberdieck--Pixton, arXiv:1706.10100).} \emph{Under
the substitution $p=e^{iu}$,}
\[
  \boxed{\;\Zred(u,q,\tilde q)\;=\;-\,\dfrac{1}{\chi_{10}(p,q,\tilde q)}\;}
\]
\emph{as a Laurent expansion of $-1/\chi_{10}$ in the non-empty open
region $|p^k q^h \tilde q^d|<1$ whenever $4hd-k^2\ge -1$.}

Here $\chi_{10}=\Phi_{10}$ is the weight-10 Igusa--Siegel cusp form of
genus $2$, realised as the Borcherds/Gritsenko lift (Gritsenko--Nikulin
1998)
\[
  \chi_{10}(p,q,\tilde q) \;=\; pq\tilde q\prod_{k,h,d} (1 - p^k q^h
  \tilde q^d)^{c(4hd-k^2)},
\]
where $c(n)$ are the Fourier coefficients of the Jacobi form of weight
$-1/2$ for $\Gamma_0(4)$ given by $\sum c(n)q^n =
(40\theta_3^4-8\theta_4^4)/(\theta_3\theta_2^4)
= 2q^{-1}+20-128q^3+216q^4-\cdots$.

\subsection*{Scope: what ``reduced'' means and what is (not) proved}

\emph{Reduced = reduced virtual fundamental class.}  Oberdieck--Pixton
p.~3 footnote~2: \emph{``Since $S$ is holomorphic symplectic the
(ordinary) virtual fundamental class vanishes.  The theory is
non-trivial only after reduction.''}  The reduction is the standard
Maulik--Pandharipande--Thomas reduction of the obstruction theory on
$S$ by the $(2,0)$-form; it lifts to $X=S\times E$ via pullback along
$\pi_1$.  This is \emph{not} reduced cohomology, \emph{not} reduced
homology, and \emph{not} a motivic reduction; it is the one-dimensional
class $[\Mbar_g^{\bullet}(X,(\beta,d))]^{\mathrm{red}}$.

\emph{Scope I: primitive K3 class only.}  The theorem fixes $\beta$
\emph{primitive in $H_2(S,\mathbb Z)$}, i.e.\ not a proper multiple of
another integral class.  Oberdieck--Pixton p.~2: \emph{``We solve the
reduced Gromov--Witten theory of the Calabi--Yau threefold
$S\times E$\emph{} for all curve classes which are primitive in the K3
factor.''}  Imprimitive cases ($\beta = m\beta_0$, $m\ge 2$) are
\emph{not} proved; p.~5 states: \emph{``A conjecture in all curve
classes has been proposed in Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2014
[arXiv:1411.1514, Conj.~A]\ldots the intermediate cases remain open.''}

\emph{Scope II: Gromov--Witten, not Donaldson--Thomas, in the statement.}
The theorem is stated for $\Nred^X_{g,\beta,d}$, the reduced
\emph{GW} invariant.  The DT reading used by the treatise follows from
the Pandharipande--Thomas GW/PT correspondence, stable in this regime
via Pandharipande--Thomas 2014 and Pandharipande--Pixton 2017 (the
paper's refs.\ [41,42]); at the \emph{unreduced} DT level the
translation is direct, at the reduced level it relies on a parallel
reduced GW/PT correspondence that for $X=S\times E$ is part of the
main proof (Oberdieck--Pixton \S 3).

\emph{Scope III: constant $C=-1$, not ``an explicit constant''.}  The
sign on the right-hand side is \emph{determined}: $C=-1$, not a free
parameter.  The treatise phrasing in
\texttt{modular\_trace.tex}~l.1486--1492 (``an explicit constant $C$'',
then ``$C$ reconstructed as the Gritsenko--Nikulin Siegel--Borcherds
lift constant'') is loose: the $C$ is pinned to $-1$ by the Laurent
expansion convention (expand $-1/\chi_{10}$ in the fundamental chamber
$4hd-k^2\ge -1$).  Any nontrivial ``$C$'' in the treatise must arise
from a \emph{different} normalisation convention (Weyl-vector shift
$\rho = f_2 - \tfrac12 f_3 + f_{-2}$, the $64$ normalisation from
$f(1,1,1)=64$ in the $\Delta_5$ Fourier expansion, or the
$\Phi_{10}=\Delta_5^2$ square-root ambiguity); the underlying
Oberdieck--Pixton theorem supplies the sign, not a free $C$.

\subsection*{Relation to Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2014 (arXiv:1411.1514)}

OP-2014 \emph{``Curve counting on $K3\times E$, the Igusa cusp form
$\chi_{10}$, and descendent integration''} (Oberdieck--Pandharipande,
v4 2015-07-13) formulates Conjectures~A--G for reduced GW and reduced
PT of $S\times E$ in \emph{all} curve classes $(\beta,d)$.
Oberdieck--Pixton 2017 proves \emph{only} the primitive-$\beta$
specialisation of Conjecture~A (``the Igusa cusp form conjecture''),
plus the matching primitive specialisations of the GW/PT-correspondence
Conjecture D and the holomorphic anomaly predictions.
Conjectures~B (imprimitive reduction rule), C (descendent reduction),
E, F, G, and the motivic refinement remain \emph{open} as of the
paper's publication (paper's~\S 0.2, \S 4).

\subsection*{Verdict against treatise claims}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reference genuine.}  arXiv:1706.10100 exists, is by
Oberdieck \emph{and} Pixton, and proves what the treatise attributes
to ``Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 \emph{Invent.}~213 Thm.~3''.  The
\emph{Inventiones} volume is \textbf{222} (2020), not \textbf{213}
(2018): verify every occurrence of ``\emph{Invent.}~213'' in
\texttt{hochschild\_calculus.tex}~l.1700,
\texttt{modular\_trace.tex}~l.1486, and downstream.
\item \textbf{Formula correct, up to sign convention.}  The core
identification $\Zred^{GW}(K3\times E) = -1/\chi_{10}$ is Theorem~1;
the GW/PT-translated DT statement $Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}} =
-1/\Phi_{10}$ is immediate.  The ``$-C/\Phi_{10}$'' treatise form is
compatible only with the reading $C=+1$ and the $-$ in front extracted;
the ``$C/\Phi_{10}$'' reading in
\texttt{bar\_cobar\_bridge.tex}~l.757 has the wrong sign unless $C$
absorbs it.
\item \textbf{Primitive-only scope missing.}  Every treatise citation
of Oberdieck--Pixton 2017 for $K3\times E$ reduced DT/GW must carry the
qualifier \emph{$\beta$ primitive in $H_2(S,\mathbb Z)$}.  The
six-routes-to-$G(K3\times E)$ story depends on this qualifier: the
imprimitive extension is Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2014 Conjecture~B,
still conjectural.  \texttt{cy\_to\_chiral.tex}~l.354 currently writes
``the generating series of $K3\times E$ DT invariants'' with no
primitivity scope; this is a scope overstatement.
\item \textbf{Attribution ``Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016'' in
\texttt{cy\_c\_beyond\_k3e\_existence\_obstruction.tex}~l.3121--3123 is
a separate paper} (arXiv:1607.05220, \emph{Shifted symplectic structures
on\ldots}).  The $Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{DT}}(K3\times E) =
1/\Phi_{10}$ identification is Oberdieck--Pixton 2017/2018, not
Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016.
\end{enumerate}
