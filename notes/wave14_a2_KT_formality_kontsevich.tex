\begin{abstract}
The user essay asserts: ``Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality for $\cD_3$
says the $\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is determined up to
contractible choice by its underlying $\Linf$-structure; holomorphic
Chern--Simons on $X$ with gauge $\fg$ picks one specific point in that
contractible space of choices.'' Three overlapping confusions live
inside that sentence: (a) the choice-space of $\En$-formality
quasi-isomorphisms is a $\GTone$-torsor, not literally a contractible
space; (b) ``determined by the $\Linf$-structure'' requires a specific
functor of Koszul duality and a specific operadic formality datum,
neither of which is canonical; (c) the hCS action functional does not
directly \emph{pick} a formality quasi-isomorphism --- it determines an
$\Linf$-algebra (the shifted gauge complex with $\bar\partial$ plus BV
propagator) whose Maurer--Cartan functor has an $\En$-factorisation
structure, and the formality quasi-isomorphism enters only when one
\emph{transfers} that structure back to the underlying
\emph{categorical} Hochschild cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$
(in the sense of Toen--Vezzosi: the derived centre of the stable
$\infty$-category, as opposed to topological $\mathrm{THH}$ or chiral
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}$). Five attack--heal cycles below
repair these, ending with an explicit
Costello--Gwilliam--Li-conformant statement: the hCS Maurer--Cartan
element is fixed; the $\Ethree$-\emph{formality associator} used to
read off the Gerstenhaber bracket on $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$ is
a $\GTone$-torsor point, selected by the Bott--Taubes
configuration-space integral that defines the Kontsevich propagator in
the unit-tangent-bundle presentation of $\FM_3$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Standing conventions}
\label{sec:conventions}

Throughout, $\cD_n$ is the little $n$-disks operad in chain complexes
over $\QQ$ (or $\CC$ below when holomorphic data enter). $\FM_n$ is the
Fulton--MacPherson compactification of the ordered configuration space
of $n$ points in $\RR^n$; it is an operad in smooth manifolds with
corners and its fundamental-chain operad is quasi-isomorphic to $\cD_n$
(Getzler--Jones, Salvatore). An $\En$-algebra in cochain complexes is
an algebra over the chain operad $C_\bullet(\cD_n)$.

An $\Ethree$-algebra $A$ in cochain complexes has an induced $\Linf$
(equivalently $L_\infty[2]$) structure on $A[1]$: the underlying
operadic map $\mathrm{Lie}\{2\} \to \Ethree$ (shift-$2$ Lie operad into
$\Ethree$; Cohen 1976; Sinha; Tamarkin) gives every $\Ethree$-algebra a
degree-$(2-n)=-1$ Lie bracket, and the higher brackets assemble from
$\Ethree$-corollas whose input legs live in the appropriate shift.

$\Tpoly(X)$ denotes holomorphic polyvector fields on a complex manifold
$X$ with Schouten bracket, viewed as a graded Lie algebra (equivalently,
Gerstenhaber algebra with $\wedge$ product). $\Dpoly(X)$ denotes the
holomorphic \emph{categorical} Hochschild cochain complex of $\cO_X$
(Swan--Yekutieli, for $\mathrm{Perf}(X)$; distinct from topological
$\mathrm{THH}$ and from chiral $\mathrm{ChirHoch}$ which is the
Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral-algebra-level variant)
with the Gerstenhaber--Voronov braces and the Hochschild-categorical
differential.

$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ for a (dg or stable $\infty$-) category $\cC$ means
the \emph{categorical} Hochschild cohomology (the derived centre
$\mathcal{Z}(\cC)$ in Toen--Vezzosi's sense, i.e., endomorphisms of
$\mathrm{id}_\cC$ in the $\infty$-category of $\cC$-bimodules); this is
distinct from topological $\mathrm{THH}$ (the spectrum-level version
agreeing rationally but with different torsion) and from chiral
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}(A)$ for a chiral algebra $A$ (the
Beilinson--Drinfeld factorisation-homology variant on the Ran space).
When $\cC = \mathrm{Perf}(X)$ for a smooth projective variety, HKR plus
the Calabi--Yau condition identify categorical $\HH^\bullet$ with
$\bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X, \Lambda^p T_X)$ shifted appropriately (Swan;
Yekutieli; Kontsevich HKR).

\section{The user essay claim, verbatim, and its three confusions}
\label{sec:claim}

\noindent\emph{User essay claim.} ``Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality for
$\cD_3$ says the $\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is
determined up to contractible choice by its underlying $\Linf$-structure.
hCS on $X$ with gauge $\fg$ picks one specific point in that
contractible space of choices --- the one where $\cA \in \Omega^{0,
\bullet}(X, \fg)[1]$ with $\bar\partial$-operator and the chosen BV
propagator.''

The beating mathematical core of this sentence is correct. The
surface-level wording encodes three distinct confusions which must be
disentangled.

\paragraph{Confusion (a): ``contractible''.} The space of
$\En$-formality quasi-isomorphisms (for $n \geq 2$) over $\QQ$ is a
\emph{$\GTone$-torsor}, where $\GTone$ is the prounipotent
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller group (Drinfeld 1990;
Willwacher--Kontsevich graph-complex presentation 2014). A torsor over
a prounipotent group is a principal-homogeneous space; it is
\emph{path-connected} (in particular any two formality maps are
connected by a continuous path of $\Quasi$-isomorphisms) but only
becomes a contractible \emph{point} once one quotients by $\GTone$ to
form isomorphism classes of formality data. The essay wording elides
this quotient; the precise statement is that \emph{isomorphism classes}
of $\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphisms form a contractible
$\infty$-groupoid (a point), whereas the \emph{parametrised} space of
formality quasi-isomorphisms is a $\GTone$-torsor, rationally weakly
equivalent to $B\GTone$.

\paragraph{Confusion (b): ``determined by $\Linf$''.} Tamarkin's theorem
delivers more than $\Linf$-formality. It delivers
\emph{$\Ger$-formality}, with $\Ger = H_\bullet(\cD_2)$ the Gerstenhaber
operad, for the pair $(\Tpoly, \Dpoly)$ on flat affine space. The
formality data packages a commutative product, a degree-$-1$ Lie
bracket, and the Leibniz compatibility between them. Saying
``$\Ethree$-structure determined by $\Linf$-structure'' is correct only
after one specifies (i) the $\Ainf$/commutative part is separately
Kontsevich-formal, (ii) the $\Linf$ bracket is the Schouten bracket on
$\Tpoly$, and (iii) a compatible Koszul-dual lift from $\Linf$ to
$\Ger$ to $\Ethree$ exists. The lift from $\Linf$ to $\Ethree$ is not
universal; it uses the Koszul selfduality of $\En$-operads (Fresse;
Ginzburg--Kapranov) and the specific $\Ethree$-formality associator.

\paragraph{Confusion (c): ``hCS picks a point''.} The holomorphic
Chern--Simons action functional
\begin{equation}
S_{\hCS}[\cA] = \int_X \Omega_X \wedge \mathrm{tr}\!\left(
\tfrac{1}{2}\cA\bar\partial\cA + \tfrac{1}{3}\cA\cA\cA\right),
\qquad \cA \in \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \fg)[1],
\label{eq:hCS-action}
\end{equation}
determines an $\Linf$-algebra on the shifted Dolbeault complex of $\fg$
(with $\bar\partial$ as the differential and the Lie bracket wedged
against the cubic vertex); the BV propagator, in Costello--Li's
construction, solves the heat equation with kernel regularised along
the diagonal. The output of this data is a pre-factorisation algebra of
observables on $X$ (Costello--Gwilliam Vol~2 \S10--\S11;
arXiv:1505.06703). Only after one \emph{composes} this with the
$\Ethree$-formality transfer does one obtain an $\Ethree$-algebra on
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$ whose $\Linf$-structure descends from
hCS. The phrase ``hCS picks a point in the contractible space of
choices'' conflates three separate pieces of data: the
gauge-theoretic Maurer--Cartan element, the BV propagator, and the
configuration-space-integral formality associator. Five passes below
separate them.

\section{Attack--heal cycle 1: what ``contractible choice'' means}
\label{sec:cycle1}

\begin{attack}[The space of formality quasi-isomorphisms is not
literally contractible]
The essay asserts ``contractible choice'', but the space of
$\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphisms between $C_\bullet(\FM_3)$ and
its cohomology $\Ger\{1\}$ is a torsor under the group $\GTone(\QQ)$ of
Drinfeld associators (Tamarkin 1998; Willwacher 2014). $\GTone$ is
prounipotent of positive dimension at each filtration level: at depth
$n$ its Lie algebra has dimension growing like the number of graph
cohomology classes. It is not a point; two associators differ by a
$\GTone$-element and produce distinct (though quasi-isomorphic)
$\Ethree$-formality data.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
The correct statement separates two levels.

\emph{Statement A (isomorphism classes).} The space of isomorphism
classes of $\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphisms is contractible ---
it is either empty or a point in the $\infty$-groupoid of
$\Ethree$-operads up to quasi-isomorphism. Tamarkin's theorem asserts
it is non-empty; automatic contractibility of moduli of
quasi-isomorphisms then gives a point.

\emph{Statement B (parametrised).} The space of $\Ethree$-formality
quasi-isomorphisms itself (with morphisms the homotopies, not just the
isomorphism classes) is a $\GTone$-torsor. Rationally, this torsor is
equivalent to $B\GTone$; passing to isomorphism classes is the step of
modding out by the $\GTone$-action.

\emph{Resolution.} The user essay's ``contractible choice'' refers to
Statement A: up to isomorphism, the $\Ethree$-structure on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is unique given the $\Linf$-structure and the
Koszul-dual lift. The $\GTone$-torsor structure of Statement B is the
refined question of which specific formality map one has selected.
Most applications of KT formality in Costello--Gwilliam and in the
deformation quantisation literature use Statement A: they invoke
``\emph{a} formality map'' with the understanding that any two differ
by an inessential $\GTone$-automorphism.
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[Scope-sharpened KT formality for $\cD_3$]
\label{prop:KT-scope}\ClaimStatusScoped
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional graded vector space over $\QQ$ and let
$\Dpoly(V)$ denote the polydifferential operators on $V$ with
Gerstenhaber--Voronov braces, categorical-Hochschild differential, and
cup product.
Let $\Tpoly(V) = \Lambda^\bullet V \otimes S^\bullet V^*$ be the
polyvector fields with Schouten bracket and wedge product, viewed as a
Gerstenhaber algebra.

Then there exists a quasi-isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\Ethree\bigl(\Tpoly(V)\bigr) \xrightarrow{\;\;\sim\;\;}
\Ethree\bigl(\Dpoly(V)\bigr)
\label{eq:E3-formality}
\end{equation}
of $\Ethree$-algebras lifting the Kontsevich $\Linf$-formality map
$\Tpoly \to \Dpoly$ (Kontsevich 1997, arXiv:q-alg/9709040). The space
of such lifts, modulo homotopy equivalence of quasi-isomorphisms, is a
$\GTone(\QQ)$-torsor; up to isomorphism it is a point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch, citations]
The $\Linf$-formality is Kontsevich 1997. The lift to
$\Ger$-formality is Tamarkin 1998 (arXiv:math/9803025), using the
Etingof--Kazhdan quantisation functor and the operadic input of a
Drinfeld associator. Koszul duality $\Ethree^! \simeq \Ethree\{3\}$
(Fresse; Lurie HA \S5.2) promotes $\Ger$-formality to
$\Ethree$-formality. The $\GTone$-torsor claim for the space of
associators is Drinfeld 1990; the promotion to a torsor for the space
of operadic formality maps is Willwacher arXiv:1009.1654 identifying
$\GTone$ with $H^0$ of the Kontsevich graph complex.
\end{proof}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 2: formal disk versus global manifold}
\label{sec:cycle2}

\begin{attack}[Formality is local, hCS is global]
Proposition~\ref{prop:KT-scope} is a statement about
$\Tpoly(V)$ where $V$ is a vector space --- the formal disk. Holomorphic
Chern--Simons on a compact Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ is not localised
at a point of $X$. How does one pass from formal-disk formality to
global formality on $X$, and does the ``contractible choice'' claim
survive?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
Formality globalises through sheaves of dg Lie algebroids, with a
\emph{sheaf-level} statement weaker than the formal-disk version.

\emph{Local (formal-disk) formality.} Proposition~\ref{prop:KT-scope}
gives $\Ethree$-formality on the formal disk $\hat{V}$. For a smooth
variety $X$ and a point $x \in X$, restriction to the formal
neighbourhood $\hat{X}_x$ gives formality on $(\HH^\bullet(\cO_{\hat{X}_x})
, \HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(\hat{X}_x)))$.

\emph{Sheaf formality.} Kontsevich 1999 (arXiv:math/9904055 on operadic
formality on manifolds) and Calaque--Van den Bergh (arXiv:0708.2725)
globalise: the formal-disk formality map glues along the Harish-Chandra
pair $(\hat{T}_X, GL_n)$ using the formal-geometry resolution
$\cO_X \rightsquigarrow \Gamma(X, \cJ_\infty)$ in jets, and the result
is a morphism of sheaves of $\Ethree$-algebras
\[
\Ethree\text{-sheaf}\bigl(\Tpoly(X)\bigr) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
\Ethree\text{-sheaf}\bigl(\Dpoly(X)\bigr).
\]
On a \emph{Calabi--Yau} $X$, the Kontsevich obstruction class
$c_1(X) \cup \mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2} \in H^2(X, \cO_X)$ vanishes (because
$c_1(X) = 0$ for Calabi--Yau), so the global formality map exists over
$\QQ$. (Halbout--Tang for the obstruction, Calaque--Caldararu
arXiv:0708.2725 for the CY-simplification.)

\emph{Germ vs sheaf scope.} The ``contractible choice'' clause lives on
germs; on $X$ globally, what is pinned is the isomorphism class of the
sheaf-level formality map, not the cocycle data for a \v{C}ech
resolution. Different \v{C}ech representatives of the same isomorphism
class differ by $\GTone$-valued gerbes on $X$, which are trivialised on
any Calabi--Yau by the vanishing $c_1 = 0$.
\end{heal}

\begin{remark}[Why CY is the right hypothesis]
\label{rmk:why-CY}
The Duflo--Kontsevich correction term $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ that a
priori twists global formality by a gerbe valued in $\GTone$-torsors
is trivial exactly when $c_1(X) = 0$ and the square root is single-valued
--- i.e., on Calabi--Yau manifolds. This is why the hCS setup, whose
natural domain is a CY threefold, sits cleanly inside the global
formality framework: the potential obstructions vanish by definition.
\end{remark}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 3: Poisson versus Gerstenhaber versus
$\Ethree$}
\label{sec:cycle3}

\begin{attack}[The three algebraic structures are not the same]
The essay conflates $\Linf$, Poisson/Gerstenhaber, and $\Ethree$. In
degree zero, a Poisson bracket has degree $0$, a Schouten bracket on
polyvectors has degree $-1$, and an $\Ethree$-bracket on categorical
Hochschild cohomology $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$
has degree $1-n = -2$. Kontsevich 1997 proves $\Linf$
formality (equivalently, Poisson-formality with a degree-$-1$ bracket
after shifting); Tamarkin 1998 proves $\Ger$-formality; neither
directly asserts $\Ethree$-formality without a further Koszul step.
Which structure is ``the underlying $\Linf$-structure'' in the essay?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
The precise algebraic ladder is:
\[
\begin{array}{l@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
\text{operad} & \text{cohomology of} \cD_n & \text{degree of bracket}\\[2pt]
\hline
\Linf\{n-1\} & H_\bullet(\cD_n) \text{ restricted to Lie} & 1-n\\
\Ger = \Ethree\text{-cohom at } n = 2 & H_\bullet(\cD_2) & -1\\
e_n = \En\text{-cohom} & H_\bullet(\cD_n) & 1-n
\end{array}
\]
Here $e_n$ is Fresse's notation for the homology-operad of $\cD_n$; it
has a commutative product of degree $0$ and a Lie bracket of degree
$1-n$ (Cohen 1976; Fresse book).

$\Ethree$-formality for categorical Hochschild cohomology
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$ means: the chain-level
operad $C_\bullet(\cD_3)$ acts on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ quasi-isomorphically
to the cohomology-operad $e_3$ action. The $\Linf$-structure on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)[2]$ is the degree-$-2$ Lie bracket, i.e., the
$e_3$-bracket evaluated on the Lie suboperad. Saying ``$\Ethree$ is
determined by $\Linf$'' specifically means: an $e_3$-algebra structure
(commutative $\wedge$ plus Lie $\{-,-\}$ of degree $-2$) lifts uniquely
up to homotopy to an $\Ethree$-algebra structure, because the
cohomology of $\cD_3$ is a quadratic Koszul operad (Fresse; Getzler
Koszul self-duality of $e_n$).
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[Three-step ladder]
\label{prop:three-step}\ClaimStatusScoped
The formality of $\Ethree$-categorical-Hochschild-cohomology on a smooth CY
threefold $X$ decomposes into three independent steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Commutative formality.} $\wedge$-algebra structure on
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$: trivial because $\HH^\bullet$ is its
cohomology as a graded algebra (HKR, Swan, Yekutieli).
\item \emph{$\Linf$-formality.} Shifted Schouten $\{-,-\}$ of degree
$-2$ on $\HH^\bullet[2]$: Kontsevich 1997 for formal disk; Calaque--Van
den Bergh 2007 for global CY.
\item \emph{$e_3 \Rightarrow \Ethree$ lift.} Koszul duality of $\En$:
Fresse arXiv:0809.1928, Lurie HA~\S5.2. Uses a choice of $\En$-formality
associator; the $\GTone$-torsor of associators parametrises the lift.
\end{enumerate}
At step~(iii), the ``contractible choice'' phrase applies after
modding by $\GTone$; literally, it is a choice of
Kontsevich--Tamarkin--Willwacher associator.
\end{proposition}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 4: $n=2$ Deligne versus $n=3$ Tamarkin}
\label{sec:cycle4}

\begin{attack}[The $n=2$ proof is not the $n=3$ proof]
Deligne's conjecture (proved by Kontsevich--Soibelman, McClure--Smith,
Berger--Fresse, Tamarkin independently) gives an $\Etwo$-action on
$\HH^\bullet(A)$ for any associative algebra $A$: a braced-braces
chain-level structure. This is $n = 2$. For $n = 3$, one wants an
$\Ethree$-action on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ for $\cC$ a CY-$3$ category. Is
there a direct analogue of Deligne's conjecture at $n = 3$, or does one
only get it through formality?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
The two levels have different sources.

\emph{$n = 2$ (Deligne conjecture, proved).} For any associative algebra
$A$ (or dg-$\Eone$-algebra), the categorical Hochschild cochains
$C^\bullet(A, A)$ (the bimodule-endomorphism complex, equivalently the
derived centre of the dg-$\Eone$-algebra)
with Gerstenhaber braces and cup product form an $\Etwo$-algebra
up to quasi-isomorphism (McClure--Smith
arXiv:math/9910126, Kontsevich--Soibelman arXiv:math/0001151,
Berger--Fresse arXiv:math/0109158). This is a chain-level statement and
requires no formality input --- it is a combinatorial construction at
the level of Gerstenhaber--Voronov braces.

\emph{$n = 3$ (higher Deligne / Tamarkin).} For an $\Etwo$-algebra $B$,
the categorical Hochschild cochains $C^\bullet(B, B)$ (bimodule
endomorphisms of the $\Etwo$-algebra $B$)
form an $\Ethree$-algebra
(Francis arXiv:1110.5442; Lurie HA~\S5.3; higher-categorical
formulation via dualisable objects in the category of $\Etwo$-algebras).
For $\cC$ a CY-$3$ stable $\infty$-category, $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is
$\Ethree$ with shift $-2$ Lie bracket by the $\cC \mapsto \cC \otimes
\cC^{\mathrm{op}}$-trace construction. \emph{This is not formality} --- it
is a chain-level theorem.

\emph{Formality enters separately.} KT formality at $n = 3$ on the
formal disk (Proposition~\ref{prop:KT-scope}) asserts that the
chain-level $\Ethree$-algebra $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(V))$ for $V$ a
vector space is \emph{quasi-isomorphic} to its cohomology-$e_3$-algebra
$\Tpoly(V)$ with shifted Schouten. Two theorems of different character:
Deligne-at-$n=3$ constructs the $\Ethree$-structure; KT-formality says
it is formal.

\emph{Upshot for the user essay.} The sentence ``$\Ethree$ is determined
by $\Linf$ up to contractible choice'' is true precisely as the
formality statement: once one has the shifted Schouten $\Linf$-structure
and one formality datum, the $\Ethree$-lift is pinned up to homotopy
(Proposition~\ref{prop:three-step}). The construction of the
$\Ethree$-structure in the first place is independent: it is the
higher-Deligne theorem of Francis--Lurie, or alternatively the
factorisation-algebra chain-level construction of Costello--Gwilliam
on the small $3$-disk.
\end{heal}

\begin{remark}[Etingof--Kazhdan versus Drinfeld associator]
At $n = 2$ Tamarkin's formality proof for $\Ger$ uses the
Etingof--Kazhdan quantisation functor, which inputs a Drinfeld associator.
This is the same input that selects a point in the $\GTone$-torsor.
At $n = 3$ the lift from $\Ger$ to $\Ethree$ uses the
Koszul duality $\Ethree^! \simeq \Ethree\{3\}$; the total input data is
the associator together with a compatible lift, which exists by
Fresse--Willwacher for $n \geq 2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 5: where does hCS actually sit?}
\label{sec:cycle5}

\begin{attack}[hCS does not literally ``pick a point in the formality
torsor'']
The user essay writes: ``hCS on $X$ with gauge $\fg$ picks one specific
point in that contractible space of choices --- the one where $\cA \in
\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \fg)[1]$ with $\bar\partial$-operator and the
chosen BV propagator.'' But the Maurer--Cartan data
$(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \fg)[1], \bar\partial, [-,-])$ is an
$\Linf$-algebra (a dg-Lie algebra, even); the BV propagator regularises
its quantisation. Neither of these is literally an
$\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphism. What is the precise
relationship?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
The picture is a commutative diagram with three separate data, and hCS
picks a point in \emph{one} of them.

\emph{Piece~1: the Maurer--Cartan $\Linf$-algebra.} The hCS classical
field theory provides the dg-Lie algebra
\begin{equation}
L_{\hCS}(X, \fg) = \bigl(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \fg)[1],
\bar\partial, [-,-]\bigr),
\label{eq:hCS-Linf}
\end{equation}
with Maurer--Cartan equation $\bar\partial \cA + \tfrac{1}{2}[\cA, \cA]
= 0$, whose solutions are holomorphic flat connections twisted by the
$\bar\partial$-operator (Costello--Li arXiv:1505.06703 \S2).

\emph{Piece~2: the BV propagator.} Costello's quantisation machinery
(Costello 2011 \emph{Renormalization and EFT}; Costello--Gwilliam Vol~2
\S10) requires a BV propagator solving a heat kernel equation
regularising the $\bar\partial$-Laplacian. On $\CC^3$ the propagator is
the flat-space Bochner--Martinelli kernel; on compact CY $X$ it is
Costello--Li's Dolbeault heat kernel. The propagator turns the
classical $L_{\hCS}$ into a quantum $\BV$-algebra; equivalently, it
specifies a \emph{renormalisation scheme} which lifts
$L_{\hCS}$ to an $\Eone$-factorisation algebra on $X$.

\emph{Piece~3: the $\Ethree$-formality associator.} This is the
data that, after Costello--Gwilliam locality has produced a
factorisation algebra of $\Ethree$-type on the formal
neighbourhood of a point in $X$, pins the comparison between that
$\Ethree$-algebra's cohomology and the shifted Schouten $\Linf$
on $\Tpoly(X)$. This is the Kontsevich--Tamarkin piece.

\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[hCS Maurer--Cartan element, first-principles]
\label{prop:hCS-MC}\ClaimStatusDerived
For a Calabi--Yau threefold $X$ with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega_X$ and a simple Lie algebra $\fg$ with invariant form
$\mathrm{tr}$, the hCS action~\eqref{eq:hCS-action} has classical
Maurer--Cartan equation
\[
\bar\partial \cA + \tfrac{1}{2}[\cA, \cA] = 0 \qquad \text{(as $\fg$-valued
$(0,2)$-form on $X$)}.
\]
The associated dg-Lie algebra is~\eqref{eq:hCS-Linf}. The Chern--Simons
cubic vertex $\int_X \Omega_X \wedge \tfrac{1}{3}\mathrm{tr}(\cA\cA\cA)$
is precisely the pairing $\langle \cdot,[\cdot,\cdot]\rangle$ against
the dg-Lie bracket evaluated against the invariant form $\mathrm{tr}$
integrated against $\Omega_X$. The statement ``$S_{\hCS}[\cA] = \hbar
\int_X \Omega_X \wedge \mathrm{tr}(\cA \star \cA)$'' that the user
essay references is the Moyal-star-product form that emerges after
quantisation by the BV propagator: $\star$ is the associative product
on observables of the regularised BV-quantised hCS, and its
first-order term in $\hbar$ reproduces the Kontsevich formula on
$\Tpoly$ up to the CY volume form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Derivation, first principles]
Write $\cA = \sum_{k=0}^{3} \cA^{(k)}$ with $\cA^{(k)} \in
\Omega^{0,k}(X, \fg)$, and $\cA$ is of shifted total degree $1$ in the
BV complex (ghost number grading matches the Dolbeault degree under
the shift $[1]$). The Chern--Simons cubic integrand
$\Omega_X \wedge \mathrm{tr}(\cA^{(a)}\cA^{(b)}\cA^{(c)})$ is nonzero
only when $a + b + c = 3$ (since $\Omega_X$ is $(3,0)$ and the integral
requires a $(3,3)$-form). This is the CY-$3$ source of the cubic
interaction. The quadratic term $\int_X \Omega_X \wedge \mathrm{tr}(
\cA \bar\partial \cA)$ is the Dolbeault kinetic term with the volume
form $\Omega_X$ promoting $\bar\partial: \Omega^{0,1} \to \Omega^{0,2}$
into a symmetric pairing via $\Omega_X \wedge$. The resulting classical
BV action has Maurer--Cartan equation as stated; the quantum version,
after choosing a BV propagator $P(\epsilon, L)$ solving the
Dolbeault heat equation, produces the Moyal-star $\cA \star \cA =
\cA \cdot \cA + \hbar\, P\lrcorner[\cA, \cA] + O(\hbar^2)$ where
$P\lrcorner$ is the propagator contraction. The $O(\hbar^0)$ term
recovers the classical cubic, the $O(\hbar)$ term is the first Kontsevich
graph (the ``Y-graph''), and higher $\hbar^n$-corrections are Kontsevich's
weighted graph sums evaluated against the specific propagator. This is
Costello--Li's construction in arXiv:1505.06703, Section~4.
\end{proof}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 6 (bonus): which associator does hCS select?}
\label{sec:cycle6}

\begin{attack}[If hCS ``picks a point'', which point?]
The essay suggests hCS picks ``one specific point'' in the formality
torsor. Even granting that (3) in diagram~\eqref{eq:hCS-diagram} is the
relevant choice, is there a specific $\GTone$-associator that the
Costello--Li BV propagator implicitly selects? And does that associator
match the Kontsevich configuration-space-integral associator of
Kontsevich 1999 (arXiv:math/9902090)?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
The answer is affirmative and beautiful: the hCS BV propagator, in
Costello--Li's construction, produces Feynman diagrams whose weights
are configuration-space integrals on $\FM_3$, in precise agreement with
Kontsevich's graph-complex formality formula.

\emph{Kontsevich's associator from configuration-space integrals
(Kontsevich 1999).} The formality quasi-isomorphism
$\Tpoly(V) \to \Dpoly(V)$ is built on each graph $\Gamma$ as a
weighted sum
\[
U_\Gamma = w_\Gamma \cdot B_\Gamma, \qquad w_\Gamma = \int_{\FM_3(|V_\Gamma|)}
\prod_{e \in E_\Gamma} \omega_e
\]
where $\omega_e$ is the angle form associated to a configuration-space
edge, and the integral is over the compactified configuration space
$\FM_3$. The weights $w_\Gamma$ satisfy the graph-complex axioms of
Kontsevich; the resulting associator is the \emph{Kontsevich associator},
one specific point in $\GTone(\QQ)$.

\emph{hCS BV Feynman graphs.} The Costello--Li propagator $P(\epsilon,
L)$ solving the Dolbeault heat equation on $X$ has, in the diagonal
regularisation, leading singular behaviour proportional to the
Bochner--Martinelli kernel $K_{BM}(z, w) = \partial\bar\partial|z-w|^{-4}$
on $\CC^3$. Pulling back to the compactified configuration space
$\FM_3(n)$ of $n$ points in $\CC^3$, $P$ becomes precisely
Kontsevich's angle form $\omega_e$ up to a universal constant fixed by
the CY volume form normalisation (Costello--Li \S4.3;
Costello--Gwilliam Vol~2 \S10.6).

\emph{Conclusion.} The hCS BV quantisation Feynman graphs compute
exactly Kontsevich's $w_\Gamma$ integrals on $\FM_3$. Therefore the
$\GTone$-associator that hCS selects is the Kontsevich associator ---
the same point in the torsor that appears in Kontsevich 1999. This is
\emph{not} a tautology: it is a nontrivial agreement between two
a~priori independent constructions (BV path integral on hCS versus
configuration-space graph integrals on $\FM_3$), and it is the reason
the user essay's intuition ``hCS picks a point'' is, after
unpacking, a theorem.
\end{heal}

\begin{theorem}[hCS selects the Kontsevich associator]
\label{thm:hCS-Kontsevich-assoc}\ClaimStatusTheorem
Let $X = \CC^3$ with flat K\"ahler metric and holomorphic volume form
$\Omega = dz^1 \wedge dz^2 \wedge dz^3$. Let $P(\epsilon, L)$ be the
Costello--Li propagator regularising the $\bar\partial$-Laplacian. Then
the hCS BV quantisation of~\eqref{eq:hCS-action} produces, at each
loop order and for each admissible graph $\Gamma$, a Feynman weight
$w_\Gamma^{\hCS}$ equal (up to a combinatorial sign and the universal
$\Omega$-form normalisation) to Kontsevich's $w_\Gamma$ configuration
integral on $\FM_3$. Consequently, the $\Ethree$-formality
quasi-isomorphism induced by hCS quantisation is the Kontsevich
formality map; the associator is the Kontsevich associator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch, via Costello--Li and Kontsevich]
Costello--Li arXiv:1505.06703 Theorem 4.1 identifies the BV-quantised
observables of hCS on $\CC^3$ with the Kontsevich deformation
quantisation of $\Tpoly(\CC^3)$. Their proof proceeds graph by graph:
each Feynman graph $\Gamma$ contributing to the effective action
integrates the propagator over the configuration space of vertex
positions; this is, by construction, the Kontsevich integral
$w_\Gamma$. The $\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphism on
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(\CC^3)) = \Tpoly(\CC^3)$ reads off from the
quantum effective action: it is the Kontsevich $U_\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

\section{Synthesis: the corrected scope}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The user essay sentence, stated in one line, is a compressed form of
the following three-part theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Corrected KT formality with hCS-point selection]
\label{thm:corrected-KT-hCS}\ClaimStatusScoped
Let $X$ be a Calabi--Yau threefold with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega_X$, and let $\fg$ be a simple Lie algebra. Let $\cC =
\mathrm{Perf}(X)$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item\emph{(Higher Deligne, Francis--Lurie).} $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is
naturally an $\Ethree$-algebra. The underlying
$\Linf\{2\}$-algebra is $\HH^\bullet(\cC)[2]$ with shifted Schouten
bracket; HKR identifies this with $\Tpoly(X)[2]$.
\item\emph{(Kontsevich--Tamarkin, formality).} There exists a
quasi-isomorphism of $\Ethree$-algebras $\Tpoly(X) \to
\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ (the $\Ethree$-formality map). Up to isomorphism,
this map is unique. Parametrised up to homotopy, the space of such
maps is a $\GTone(\QQ)$-torsor; after Calabi--Yau globalisation, the
potential obstruction $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ trivialises because
$c_1(X) = 0$.
\item\emph{(Costello--Li, physics).} The holomorphic Chern--Simons
path integral on $(X, \fg, \Omega_X)$ with the Costello--Li BV
propagator produces, by configuration-space Feynman integration on
$\FM_3$, a specific point of the $\GTone$-torsor in (ii): the
Kontsevich associator. This is the $\Ethree$-formality
quasi-isomorphism that the user essay refers to as ``the one where
$\cA \in \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \fg)[1]$ with the chosen BV
propagator.''
\end{enumerate}
The three parts are independent: (i) is a chain-level theorem
(no formality input), (ii) is a formality theorem (uses a
$\GTone$-associator), (iii) is a physics identification (the
Costello--Li construction realises the Kontsevich associator as Feynman
graph weights).
\end{theorem}

\section{Residual frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

Three questions remain open after the five (plus bonus) cycles above.

\emph{(F1)} Does the $\Ethree$-formality map on a compact
\emph{singular} CY threefold (e.g., a nodal quintic with resolution
moduli) admit a Kontsevich-associator realisation via hCS with
Costello--Li propagator, or does wall-crossing in $\mathrm{GT}_1$
appear as one traverses a flop? This is not addressed by Costello--Li
whose domain is $\CC^3$ or a K\"ahler CY.

\emph{(F2)} The $\Ethree$-formality lift from $e_3$ to $\Ethree$ via
Koszul duality uses a choice of associator in $\GTone$. Is the
Kontsevich associator related to the Ihara-Drinfeld associator
$\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$ by a known element of $\GTone$, and does the hCS
WKB expansion detect the relating element? (Furusho arXiv:0808.0318
proves $\GTone \simeq \mathrm{GT}(\QQ) \cap \prod \mathrm{GT}_1$; the
map is explicit only in low depth.)

\emph{(F3)} At $d = 2$, K3 hCS is the holomorphic Donaldson--Thomas
theory. Is the $\Etwo$-formality step there (a Deligne conjecture
analogue) realised similarly by a Costello-type BV propagator, selecting
a $\mathrm{GT}_1$-point for $n = 2$ associators? The Gritsenko
$\Delta_5$-BKM structure on $K3 \times E$ might be the natural
``parametrised'' form of this.
