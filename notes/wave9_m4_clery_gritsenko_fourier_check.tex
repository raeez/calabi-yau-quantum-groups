\section{Cl\'ery--Gritsenko diagonal-divisor forms and CHL Borcherds constants}
\label{wn:sec:clery-gritsenko-fourier-check}

Let
\[
  q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\qquad r=e^{2\pi iz},\qquad
  s=e^{2\pi i\omega},\qquad
  Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&z\\ z&\omega\end{pmatrix}\in\HH_2 .
\]
For an index-one weak Jacobi form
\[
  \phi(\tau,z)=\sum_{n,\ell} f(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell
\]
the discriminant-zero coefficient is the coefficient \(c(0)\) of the
Eichler--Zagier discriminant variable \(D=4n-\ell^2\).  This differs
from the constant term of \(\phi(\tau,0)\).  For the standard generator,
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=r^{-1}+10+r+O(q),
\]
so \(c(-1)=1\), \(c(0)=10\), and
\(\phi_{0,1}(\tau,0)|_{q^0}=12\).  A direct evaluation of the theta-ratio
formula
\[
  \phi_{0,1}
  =4\sum_{i=2}^{4}\left(\frac{\theta_i(\tau,z)}
  {\theta_i(\tau,0)}\right)^2
\]
in \texttt{compute/lib/phi01\_fourier.py} gives the same row and the next
discriminant coefficient \(c(3)=-64\).

\paragraph{The CHL denominator row.}
For the CHL Borcherds denominators attached to the twined K3 input,
write \(c_N^{\CHL}(0)\) for the discriminant-zero coefficient after the
CHL input normalization has been fixed.  Borcherds' weight theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N^{\CHL})
  =\wt(\Phi_N^{\CHL})
  =\frac{c_N^{\CHL}(0)}{2},
  \qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
In this normalization the row is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
N & \text{K3 frame shape} & c_N^{\CHL}(0) &
\kBKM(\Phi_N^{\CHL})\\
\hline
1 & 1^{24} & 10 & 5\\
2 & 1^{8}2^{8} & 8 & 4\\
3 & 1^{6}3^{6} & 6 & 3\\
4 & 1^{4}2^{2}4^{4} & 4 & 2\\
6 & 1^{2}2^{2}3^{2}6^{2} & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
These are the standard twined-K3 constants in the CHL normalization.
They are also checked by
\texttt{compute/lib/diagonal\_siegel\_cy\_orbifolds.py}.  The level-one entry is
the primitive Gritsenko--Nikulin form \(\Delta_5\), whose weight is
\(10/2=5\).  The Igusa form \(\Psi_{10}=\Delta_5^2\) has weight \(10\);
the primitive BKM denominator remains the weight-\(5\) form
\(\Delta_5\), with \(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\).  In the monic denominator
normalization,
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2 .
\]
For \(Y=K3\times E\), this automorphic value sits in the subscripted
list
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(Y)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{compact}}(Y)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(Y)=24 .
\]

\paragraph{The Cl\'ery--Gritsenko diagonal-divisor catalogue.}
Cl\'ery--Gritsenko prove that the Hecke subgroups \(\Gamma_t(N)\) of
the genus-two paramodular groups carry exactly eight
diagonal-divisor modular forms.  Their Theorem~1.4 indexes the rows by
triples \((t,N;k)\).  In the order of that classification the rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{form} & (t,N) & \text{group} & \text{character or multiplier}
& \wt \\
\hline
\Delta_5 & (1,1) & \Gamma_1=\Sp_4(\Z) & \chi_2 & 5\\
\Delta_2 & (2,1) & \Gamma_2 & \chi_4 & 2\\
\nabla_3 & (1,2) & \Gamma_0^{(2)}(2) & \chi_2 & 3\\
\Delta_1 & (3,1) & \Gamma_3 & \chi_6 & 1\\
\nabla_2 & (1,3) & \Gamma_0^{(2)}(3) & \chi_2 & 2\\
\Delta_{1/2} & (4,1) & \Gamma_4 & \chi_8 & \frac12\\
\nabla_{3/2} & (1,4) & \Gamma_0^{(2)}(4) & \chi_4 & \frac32\\
Q_1 & (2,2) & \Gamma_2(2) & \chi_4 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Thus the Cl\'ery--Gritsenko weight tuple is
\[
  \left(5,2,3,1,2,\frac12,\frac32,1\right).
\]
After the Borcherds product input and cusp normalization of their
Theorem~3.1 are fixed, the corresponding twice-weight product constants
are
\[
  \left(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2\right).
\]
The possible ninth triple \((2,4;\frac12)\) is excluded in their proof,
so there is no additional diagonal-divisor row on \(\Gamma_2(4)\).

\paragraph{Fourier coefficients in the Cl\'ery--Gritsenko products.}
For a nearly holomorphic Jacobi form \(\phi\in
J^{\mathrm{nh}}_{0,t}(\Gamma_0(N))\), Cl\'ery--Gritsenko's product
theorem writes the Borcherds product as
\[
  B_\phi(Z)=q^Ar^Bs^C
  \prod_{f/e}
  \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}
  \left(1-(q^nr^\ell s^{tm})^{N_e}\right)^{
  (h_e/N_e)c_{f/e}(nm,\ell)}
\]
and gives its weight as
\[
  \wt(B_\phi)
  =
  \frac12\sum_{f/e}\frac{h_e}{N_e}c_{f/e}(0,0).
\]
This is the normalization in which a statement of the form
\(\wt(B_\phi)=c(0)/2\) is meaningful.  The symbol \(c(0)\) denotes the
cusp-weighted product input on the chosen group.

For the four congruence-subgroup rows constructed in that theorem, the
Fourier data at the cusps are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{row} & \text{Jacobi input} & \text{leading cusp expansions}
& \wt\\
\hline
\nabla_3 &
\phi_2=4(\xi_{1,0}^{(2)})^2 &
\begin{array}{l}
\phi_2=r^{-1}+2+r+O(q)\\
\phi_2|S=4+O(q^{1/2})
\end{array}
& 3\\[2ex]
\nabla_2 &
\phi_3=3\xi_{3,1}^{(6)}\xi_{3,5}^{(6)} &
\begin{array}{l}
\phi_3=r^{-1}+1+r+O(q)\\
\phi_3|S=3+O(q^{1/3})
\end{array}
& 2\\[2ex]
\nabla_{3/2} &
\phi_4=2\xi_{2,1}^{(4)}\xi_{2,3}^{(4)} &
\begin{array}{l}
\phi_4=r^{-1}+r+O(q^2)\\
\phi_4|S=2+O(q^{1/4})\\
\phi_4|M=2+O(q)
\end{array}
& \frac32\\[3ex]
Q_1 &
\psi=2\xi_{1,0}^{(2)}(\tau,2z) &
\begin{array}{l}
\psi=r^{-1}+r+O(q)\\
\psi|S=2+O(q^{1/2})
\end{array}
& 1
\end{array}
\]
The symbols \(S\) and \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\2&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
are the cusp representatives used in the paper.  The displayed constants
are exactly the constants entering the cusp-weighted formula above.

\paragraph{Separation of the three rows.}
The CHL row, the Cl\'ery--Gritsenko diagonal-divisor catalogue, and the
Igusa square are distinct normalizations.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{normalization} & \text{indexing} & \text{weights}\\
\hline
\text{CHL denominators} & N=1,2,3,4,6 & (5,4,3,2,1)\\
\text{Cl\'ery--Gritsenko dd forms} & (t,N;k)\ \text{eight rows}
& (5,2,3,1,2,\frac12,\frac32,1)\\
\text{Igusa square} & \Psi_{10}=\Delta_5^2 & 10
\end{array}
\]
The only shared primitive entry is \(\Delta_5\).  Consequently the CHL
constant \(c_2^{\CHL}(0)=8\) belongs to the CHL denominator row, while
the \(\Delta_2\), \(\nabla_3\), and \(Q_1\) constants are
row-specific Cl\'ery--Gritsenko product inputs.  The CHL value
\(\kBKM(\Phi_6^{\CHL})=1\) belongs to the \(N=6\) primitive
denominator row.
