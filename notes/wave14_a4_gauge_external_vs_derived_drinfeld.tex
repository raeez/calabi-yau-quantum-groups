\section*{External Gauge Coefficients and Derived CY$_3$ Data}
\label{sec:gauge-external-derived-drinfeld}

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex Calabi-Yau threefold with holomorphic
volume form \(\Omega_X\), and let
\(\cC=\Perf^{\mathrm{dg}}(X)\) carry a negative-cyclic CY$_3$ class
\(\eta\). Holomorphic Chern-Simons theory and the CY category attach
different objects to this input. The finite-dimensional gauge Lie
algebra of hCS is a coefficient. The external gauge groupoid, the
derived quotient stack of solutions, the BV complex, the BRST complex,
the Drinfeld centre or double, and the chiral output occupy separate
layers. The derived category supplies framed endomorphism algebras,
Hochschild cochains, and autoequivalence mapping spaces. These
structures interact after a framing object is chosen.

\subsection*{The coefficient Lie algebra of hCS}

Fix a reductive complex Lie algebra \(\fg\), an invariant trace
\(\Tr_{\rho}\) coming from a representation \(\rho\), and a principal
\(G\)-bundle \(P\) on \(X\). The deformation dg Lie algebra is
\[
  \ell_P
  =
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}\!\left(X,\operatorname{ad}P\right),
  \qquad
  d=\dbar_P,
\]
with bracket induced by the Lie bracket on \(\fg\) and the wedge
product of forms. The classical hCS field complex is the shifted
complex
\[
  \cL_{\hCS}(X,P,\fg)
  =
  \ell_P[1].
\]
In a local trivialisation \(P=X\times G\) this becomes
\[
  \cL_{\hCS}(X,\fg)
  =
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)\otimes\fg[1].
\]
For \(a\in\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\operatorname{ad}P)\), the classical
functional is
\[
  S_{\hCS}(a)
  =
  \int_X \Omega_X\wedge
  \Tr_{\rho}\!\left(
    \frac{1}{2}a\,\dbar_P a
    +
    \frac{1}{6}a\,[a,a]
  \right).
\]
This formula uses the CY volume form, the trace pairing, and the chosen
gauge coefficient. The finite-dimensional \(\fg\) is an added datum of
the field theory.

For a compact nonabelian hCS claim, the classical formula supplies the
first datum. A quantum factorisation algebra of observables requires a
Costello QME solution, an anomaly cancellation or anomaly
trivialisation in the local BV functional complex, an orientation and
framing datum, descent and sewing data, local counterterms, analytic
OPE completion, and an RG-compatible gauge fixing. In complex dimension
\(3\), the local gauge anomaly is governed by a quartic invariant
polynomial
\[
  I^\rho_4\in\Sym^4(\fg^\vee)^\fg .
\]
The quadratic Casimir \(C^\rho_2(\fg)\) controls the two-point
field-strength counterterm and the kinetic normalisation. It is a
renormalisation coefficient. The QME obstruction is the quartic class.

\subsection*{Gauge groupoid, derived stack, BV, and BRST}

The external gauge group is
\[
  \cG_P=\Gamma(X,\operatorname{Ad}P),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Lie}(\cG_P)=\Omega^{0,0}(X,\operatorname{ad}P).
\]
It acts on the affine space of Dolbeault connections
\[
  \cA_P=\dbar_P+\Omega^{0,1}(X,\operatorname{ad}P),
  \qquad
  \cA_P//\cG_P\rightrightarrows \cA_P .
\]
The action groupoid records classical gauge equivalence before the
equation of motion. The Maurer-Cartan equation
\[
  \dbar_P a+\frac{1}{2}[a,a]=0
\]
cuts out the derived solution stack
\[
  \mathbf{R}\cM_{\hCS}(X,P)
  =
  [\MC(\ell_P)/\cG_P].
\]
Equivalently, on the hCS field space with the \((-1)\)-shifted
symplectic form induced by \(\Omega_X\) and \(\Tr_\rho\),
\[
  \mathbf{R}\cM_{\hCS}(X,P)
  \simeq
  [\Crit(S_{\hCS})/\cG_P].
\]
At a solution \(a\), its tangent complex is
\[
  \bigl(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\operatorname{ad}P),\dbar_P+[a,-]\bigr)[1].
\]

The BRST complex is the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the gauge action
on functions:
\[
  \Obs_{\BRST}(\cA_P)
  =
  \CE^\bullet\!\left(
    \Omega^{0,0}(X,\operatorname{ad}P),
    \cO(\cA_P)
  \right).
\]
It adds ghosts and the infinitesimal gauge differential. The BV complex
adds the odd symplectic pairing and antifield directions; in the compact
hCS package it is represented by \(\ell_P[1]\) together with
\[
  \omega_{\BV}(\alpha,\beta)
  =
  \int_X \Omega_X\wedge\Tr_\rho(\alpha\wedge\beta).
\]
The BV differential is generated by \(S_{\hCS}\) through the
antibracket. Thus the underlying shifted Dolbeault complex may be the
same, while the BV object is the triple
\((\ell_P[1],\omega_{\BV},S_{\hCS})\). The BRST complex presents the
quotient by gauge symmetry; the BV complex presents the shifted
critical theory with its symplectic and QME data.

\subsection*{Framed derived endomorphisms}

The CY category \(\cC\) has no preferred finite-dimensional gauge Lie
algebra. A choice of object \(\cE\in\cC\) produces the dg algebra
\[
  A_{\cE}
  =
  \REnd_{\cC}(\cE)
  =
  \RHom_{\cC}(\cE,\cE),
\]
and its commutator dg Lie algebra \(A_{\cE}^{\mathrm{Lie}}\). For a
vector bundle \(E\) on \(X\), a Dolbeault model is
\[
  A_E^{\mathrm{Dolb}}
  \simeq
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}\!\left(X,\End(E)\right),
  \qquad
  d=\dbar_E .
\]
If \(E=\cO_X^{\oplus r}\) on a trivialising chart, then
\[
  A_E^{\mathrm{Dolb}}
  \simeq
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_r .
\]
Thus the hCS coefficient \(\mathfrak{gl}_r\) is recovered from the
framed pair \((\cC,E)\) as the degree-zero fibre of the derived
endomorphism algebra in the trivial local model. The recovery uses the
framing; the unframed CY category determines the space of possible
framings and their endomorphism algebras.

Hochschild cochains are a separate intrinsic object:
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\cC)
  =
  \REnd_{\cC\otimes\cC^{\mathrm{op}}}(\cC).
\]
For \(\cC=\Perf(X)\), the Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg map identifies
the associated graded object with
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\Perf(X))
  \simeq
  \bigoplus_{p,q} H^q(X,\wedge^p T_X)
  =
  \PV^\bullet(X)
\]
after the usual filtration and Todd-class normalisations. These
polyvector fields govern deformations and the intrinsic centre of the
CY category. They are distinct from the \(\End(E)\)-valued Dolbeault
forms that make the hCS gauge field complex.

\subsection*{Mapping spaces and categorical gauge symmetry}

The symmetry object of the CY category is the mapping space of
\(\C\)-linear dg autoequivalences
\[
  \Autinf(\cC)
  =
  \Map_{\mathrm{dgCat}_{\C}}(\cC,\cC)^{\simeq}.
\]
It is an \(\infty\)-group. Its connected components form the ordinary
autoequivalence group \(\pi_0\Autinf(\cC)\). At the identity,
\[
  \pi_1\bigl(\Autinf(\cC),\operatorname{id}_{\cC}\bigr)
  =
  \Aut(\operatorname{id}_{\cC})
  =
  \cZ(\cC)^\times ,
\]
the units of the derived centre. Higher homotopy is controlled by the
connective units in the derived centre. The phrase ``2-groupoid of
autoequivalences'' is the truncation \(\tau_{\leq 2}\Autinf(\cC)\).
The full mapping space is the object needed for coherent functoriality.

For smooth projective varieties, Orlov representability identifies
exact equivalences \(D^b\operatorname{Coh}(X)\to D^b\operatorname{Coh}(Y)\)
with Fourier-Mukai kernels under the standard hypotheses. A CY$_3$
autoequivalence group can contain geometric automorphisms, tensoring by
line bundles, shifts, spherical twists, and other Fourier-Mukai
transformations. A formula such as
\((\Aut(X)\ltimes\operatorname{Pic}(X))\times\Z\) belongs to special
geometric regimes. General CY$_3$ derived autoequivalences require the
full Fourier-Mukai groupoid.

\subsection*{Morita invariance and functorial direction}

Morita equivalence of smooth dg categories preserves Hochschild
homology, Hochschild cohomology, and the derived centre. If
\(F:\cC\to\cD\) is a CY-compatible dg functor and \(\cE\in\cC\), then
there is a natural dg-algebra morphism
\[
  \REnd_{\cC}(\cE)
  \longrightarrow
  \REnd_{\cD}(F\cE).
\]
For a Morita equivalence this morphism is a quasi-isomorphism after
transporting the object. The coefficient algebra used by hCS follows
the chosen framing object through the equivalence.

The functorial direction is therefore:
\[
  (\cC,\eta,\cE)
  \longmapsto
  \REnd_{\cC}(\cE)^{\mathrm{Lie}}
  \longmapsto
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\End(E))[1]
\]
on geometric framed inputs. The unframed datum \((\cC,\eta)\) supplies
\(\HH^\bullet(\cC)\), the derived centre, and the autoequivalence
mapping space. It supplies a finite-dimensional external \(\fg\) after
the framing has been specified.

\subsection*{The \(E_3\) structure and the \(E_1\) chiral output}

The Hochschild cochain complex of any dg category carries the
\(\Etwo\) structure of the Deligne conjecture. A CY$_3$ trace adds the
shifted symplectic and cyclic data used by the Kontsevich-Tamarkin and
Calaque-Van den Bergh formality package. On the verified CY$_3$ loci,
after choosing the required formality, CY framing, and Costello-Li
holomorphic BV data, this yields the stage-one holomorphic
factorisation algebra
\[
  \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\cC)\in \Ethree\text{-}\mathrm{HolFA}(X).
\]
This is the intrinsic side. It is built from the CY category and its
Hochschild calculus.

The specialised CY-to-chiral output uses a complex surface
\(\Sigma_2\subset X\), a reference curve \(C\), and the analytic
specialisation datum:
\[
  \Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\cC)
  =
  \operatorname{SpCh}_{\Sigma_2,C}\bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\cC)\bigr).
\]
For \(d\geq 3\) the specialised output on \(C\) is an \(\Eone\)-chiral
algebra. Braided \(\Etwo\) structure belongs to the representation
centre
\[
  \cZ\!\left(\operatorname{Rep}^{\Eone}
  \bigl(\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\cC)\bigr)\right)
\]
or, in Hopf algebra presentations, to the Drinfeld double after the
Hopf pairing, completion, and module category have been constructed.
The \(\Etwo\) layer is a centre or double layer over the \(\Eone\)
output.

\subsection*{Local model on \(\C^3\)}

For \(X=\C^3\) and the trivial rank-\(r\) framing \(E=\cO_{\C^3}^{\oplus r}\),
the three complexes have concrete forms:
\[
  A_E^{\mathrm{Dolb}}
  =
  \Omega^{0,\bullet}(\C^3)\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_r,
  \qquad
  \cL_{\hCS}(\C^3,\mathfrak{gl}_r)
  =
  A_E^{\mathrm{Dolb}}[1],
\]
and
\[
  \HH^\bullet(\Perf(\C^3))
  \simeq
  \C[x,y,z]\otimes
  \wedge^\bullet
  \langle \partial_x,\partial_y,\partial_z\rangle .
\]
The first two formulas describe the framed gauge field complex. The
third describes intrinsic polyvector deformations of the local CY
category. The BV anti-fields of hCS live in the shifted cotangent
theory to the field complex, paired by
\(\int_{\C^3}\Omega_{\C^3}\wedge\Tr_\rho(-\, -)\). The
\(p>0\) polyvector summands of \(\HH^\bullet(\Perf(\C^3))\) describe
intrinsic category deformations instead.

The toric Hall algebra attached to the critical Hilbert scheme of
\(\C^3\) is
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)\simeq \Yp(\gglone),
\]
the positive half of the affine Yangian of \(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1\)
in the Schiffmann-Vasserot normalisation. A full
\(\Wilpha\)-type vertex algebra appears after passing through the
Drinfeld double or centre, completing the algebra, and applying the
appropriate evaluation representation. The CoHA identification stops
at the positive half.

\subsection*{\(K3\times E\) invariants and Hall layers}

For \(X=K3\times E\), compact CY data, specialised chiral data,
Borcherds data, and fibre data give separate constants:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3\times E) &= 0,\\
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3\times E) &= 0,\\
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E) &= 3,\\
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}) &= 5,\\
  \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(K3) &= 24.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first two values are compact total-space invariants. The
Heisenberg value is a specialised chiral witness. The Borcherds value
is the weight of \(\Delta_5\), equivalently \(c_1(0)/2=10/2\). The
fibre value is the Mukai-lattice rank.

The Hall-Drinfeld construction on \(K3\times E\) has the form
\[
  \mathcal D_\hbar(\CoHA_{K3\times E})
  \leadsto
  \mathfrak g_{\Delta_5},
  \qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=5.
\]
It belongs to the BPS/Borcherds layer. The Mukai self-mirror
construction uses K3 stable-envelope and Yangian data. It belongs to
the separate K3 branch. A comparison between the branches needs an
explicit functor, character map, or representation-theoretic
degeneration.

\subsection*{Separation statement}

For a smooth CY$_3$ category \((\cC,\eta)\), a framing object
\(\cE\in\cC\), and a geometric realisation by a vector bundle \(E\) on
\(X\), the following data are separate and functorially related.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{object} & \text{definition} & \text{role}\\
\hline
\text{external hCS coefficient} & \fg,\ \Tr_\rho & \text{finite-dimensional gauge input}\\
\text{external gauge groupoid} &
\cA_P//\cG_P &
\text{classical gauge equivalence}\\
\text{derived solution stack} &
[\MC(\ell_P)/\cG_P] &
\text{equations of motion modulo gauge}\\
\text{hCS deformation dg Lie algebra} &
\ell_P &
\text{Maurer-Cartan deformation theory}\\
\text{hCS field complex} &
\ell_P[1] &
\text{shifted classical fields}\\
\text{BRST complex} &
\CE^\bullet(\Omega^{0,0}(X,\operatorname{ad}P),\cO(\cA_P)) &
\text{infinitesimal quotient observables}\\
\text{BV complex} &
(\ell_P[1],\omega_{\BV},S_{\hCS}) &
\text{shifted critical theory}\\
\text{framed derived algebra} &
\REnd_{\cC}(\cE) &
\text{endomorphisms of a chosen brane}\\
\text{intrinsic centre} &
\HH^\bullet(\cC) &
\text{Morita-invariant CY deformation complex}\\
\text{categorical symmetry} &
\Autinf(\cC) &
\text{mapping space of dg autoequivalences}\\
\text{specialised output} &
\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(\cC) &
\Eone\text{-chiral algebra on }C\\
\text{chiral centre} &
\cZ(\operatorname{Rep}^{\Eone}(-))\text{ or }D(-) &
\Etwo\text{ centre or double}
\end{array}
\]

The CY category controls the intrinsic Hochschild and Morita-invariant
structure. A framing object evaluates this structure to a derived
endomorphism algebra. A geometric trivialisation of that framed algebra
recovers the finite-dimensional coefficient \(\fg\) used in hCS.
Quantising compact nonabelian hCS adds QME, anomaly cancellation,
orientation, framing, descent, sewing, counterterms, completion, and
gauge-fixing data. The CY-to-chiral specialisation at \(d=3\) produces
an \(\Eone\)-chiral algebra; the \(\Etwo\) structure is carried by the
centre or double built from that output.
