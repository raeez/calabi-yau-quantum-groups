\title{The Borcherds singular theta integral producing $\Delfive$:
a Beilinson-school attack--heal audit of the Lorgat~2020 lift}
\author{Adversarial audit, Vol~III}
\date{Wave~13 a6}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Gritsenko--Nikulin identification $\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)=\Phi(Z)$,
reviewed without derivation in the Lorgat~2020 automorphic-corrections
manuscript, is the claim that the Igusa cusp form
$\Delfive\in S_5(\Sp_4(\ZZ),v_{\Delfive})$ equals the Borcherds
singular theta lift of the weak Jacobi form $\phij$ of weight~$0$
index~$1$. Five adversarial attacks on the lift mechanism --- the
regularised integral itself, the Hecke--Shintani cutoff and
logarithmic-divergence subtraction, the vector-valued
Weil-representation theta-decomposition, the Bruinier--Funke
Heegner-Chern-class reciprocity that fixes the weight and multiplier
normalisation, and the categorical motivic lift through $\ChirHoch$ ---
are matched by explicit heals. Each heal supplies a proposition with
either a rigorous proof or an honest derivation sketch with primary
citation; each establishes a specific normalisation producing
$\kBKM(\Delfive)=c_1(0)/2=5$ with $c_1(0)=10$. The audit vindicates
the singular-theta-lift framing as the machinery \emph{producing}
the denominator-formula object from its principal-part datum, and
locates the exact coefficient under which
$\kBKM(\Delfive)=5$ aligns with the $\mathsf{B}$-row
$K^{\kch}=8$ through Bruinier's Heegner Chern-class reciprocity
(factor audited in wave12~a6).
\end{abstract}

\section{First-principles frame: the singular theta integral}
% ============================================================

Fix once for all the data:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L=\Lato$, the even lattice of signature $(3,2)$ realised as
$(e_1\wedge e_3+e_2\wedge e_4)^{\perp}\subset\wedge^2\ZZ^4$, with
Gram form $q(x,x)=2\,\mathrm{Pf}(x)$ and isomorphism type
$U\oplus U\oplus\langle 2\rangle$ (Lorgat~2020 lines 237--260; $U$
the hyperbolic plane).
\item The type IV domain
\[
\mathcal{H}^{IV}_{+}=\{Z\in\mathbb{P}(L\otimes\CC):(Z,Z)=0,(Z,\overline{Z})<0,
\mathrm{Im}\,z_1>0\}
\;\simeq\;\Sieg/\{\pm I\},
\]
under the exceptional isomorphism
$\Sp_4(\ZZ)/\{\pm I\}\simeq\Oplus(\Lato)/\{\pm I\}$
($\wedge^2$-correspondence, Lorgat~2020 Lemma~1).
\item The Grassmannian $\Gr(L)$ of oriented maximal positive-definite
planes $v^{+}\subset L_\RR$ of signature $(3,0)$ against the
orthogonal complement $v^{-}$ of signature $(0,2)$; this is the
real incarnation of the period domain for the Kuga--Satake variation.
\item The vector-valued Siegel theta kernel
\[
\Theta_L(\tau,v^{+})
 \;=\; \sum_{\lambda\in L}
      \exp\bigl(\pi\mathrm{i}\tau\,q(\lambda_{v^{+}})
               + \pi\mathrm{i}\bar\tau\,q(\lambda_{v^{-}})\bigr)\,
      \mathfrak{e}_{\lambda+L}
\]
where $\lambda_{v^{\pm}}$ are the orthogonal projections and
$\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda+L}$ is the basis vector in the group ring
$\CC[L^{\vee}/L]$ indexed by the discriminant class; $\Theta_L$
transforms as weight $(3/2,2/2)=(3/2,1)$ under the Weil representation
$\Weil$ of the metaplectic cover $\Mp_2(\ZZ)$ (Borcherds 1998 Theorem~4.1;
Bruinier 2002 Chapter~4).
\item The fundamental domain
$\theF=\{\tau\in\HH:|\tau|\geq 1,|\mathrm{Re}\,\tau|\leq 1/2\}$
of $\SL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH$, with hyperbolic measure
$\mathrm{d}\mu(\tau)=y^{-2}\,\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} y$, $\tau=x+\mathrm{i} y$.
\item The nearly-holomorphic vector-valued input form
$F:\HH\to\CC[L^{\vee}/L]$ of weight $k-n/2=0-3/2=-1/2$ for the lift,
with polar part at the cusp encoding $\phij$.
\end{itemize}

The singular theta lift of $F$ is the logarithm of an automorphic form
$\Psi(Z,F)$ on $\Oplus(L)\backslash\mathcal{H}^{IV}_{+}$. Definitionally:
\[
-2\log|\Psi(Z,F)|^{2} \;=\; \Phi_L(Z,F)
 \;\coloneqq\; \RegInt{\SL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH}
    \bigl\langle F(\tau),\Theta_L(\tau,v^{+}(Z))\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\tau)
\]
where $\langle,\rangle$ is the natural pairing on
$\CC[L^{\vee}/L]\otimes\overline{\CC[L^{\vee}/L]}$ and the
regularisation is the Harvey--Moore/Borcherds prescription: truncate
$\theF$ at $\mathrm{Im}\,\tau\leq T$, subtract the divergent
logarithmic term, let $T\to\infty$. The main theorem of Borcherds
1998 is that the resulting $\Psi(Z,F)$ extends to a meromorphic
modular form on $\mathcal{H}^{IV}_{+}$ with explicit weight, zeros,
poles, and product expansion at each $0$-dimensional cusp; the product
expansion is a \emph{Borcherds product} read off the Fourier coefficients
of $F$.

Five attacks, five heals.

\section{Attack I --- The regularised integral as a rigorous object}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[What does $\RegInt{\theF}$ mean?]
The Lorgat~2020 manuscript and slides assert
$\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)=\Phi(Z)$ as an identity of products (line 688,
``Theorem~3''), bypassing the \emph{existence} of the lift as an
analytic object. The integral
$\int_{\theF}\langle F,\Theta_L\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu$ \emph{diverges} at
the cusp $\tau\to\mathrm{i}\infty$ because $F$ has a polar part and
$\Theta_L$ has the Jacobi-theta-type asymptotic
$\Theta_L(\tau,v^{+})\sim 1+O(\mathrm{e}^{-\pi y\cdot\mathrm{const}})$
on the $\mathfrak{e}_0$-component as $y=\mathrm{Im}\,\tau\to\infty$.
The product $\langle F,\Theta_L\rangle$ thus carries a
$q^{-1/4}\cdot 1 = y^{-\ast}\mathrm{e}^{-\ast\pi y/2}$-scale divergence
whose integral against $y^{-2}\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} y$ is
non-absolutely-convergent. A naive
$\int_{\theF}\cdot\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ is meaningless; one must specify
the order of operations. Four specific regularisations coexist in
the literature and disagree by \emph{constants}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Harvey--Moore/Borcherds 1998: $\lim_{T\to\infty}
\int_{\theF_T}-\log T\cdot c_F$ with $\theF_T=\theF\cap\{y\leq T\}$
and $c_F$ the $(F,1)$-constant-term against $\mathfrak{e}_0$.
\item[(b)] Zagier (Harvey--Moore variant): analytic continuation of
$\int_{\theF}\langle F,\Theta_L\rangle\,y^{s-2}\,\mathrm{d} x\,\mathrm{d} y$
in $s$, specialised to $s=0$ after meromorphic continuation.
\item[(c)] Bruinier--Funke 2004: Stokes' theorem truncation with
the $\xi$-operator reducing the lift of a harmonic Maass form to
a holomorphic lift of its holomorphic projection.
\item[(d)] Kudla--Millson 1990/1997: pairing against Schwartz
forms on the Grassmannian, yielding a current-valued (rather than
function-valued) lift.
\end{enumerate}
Without specifying which, ``the'' Borcherds lift is ambiguous.
The four regularisations give \emph{the same analytic function}
$\Psi(Z,F)$ only after one matches normalisation constants. The
Lorgat~2020 manuscript's ``Theorem~3'' elides this.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[The Harvey--Moore/Borcherds regularisation, made rigorous]
State the singular theta integral as a limit of absolutely convergent
objects, with explicit logarithmic counter-term. The convergence and
independence of choice is the content of Borcherds 1998 Theorem~7.3.

\begin{proposition}[Regularised theta integral]
\label{prop:reg-integral-wellposed}
Let $L$ be an even lattice of signature $(n^{+},n^{-})$ with
$n^{+}\geq 1$ and $n^{-}\leq 2$. Let
$F:\HH\to\CC[L^{\vee}/L]$ be a weakly holomorphic modular form of
weight $k=(2-n)/2$ (with $n=n^{+}+n^{-}$) for the Weil representation
$\Weil$, i.e. $F$ is holomorphic on $\HH$, meromorphic at the cusp, and
$F(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{k}\Weil(\gamma)F(\tau)$ for all
$\gamma\in\Mp_2(\ZZ)$. Write the Fourier expansion at the cusp:
\[
F(\tau) \;=\; \sum_{\mu\in L^{\vee}/L}\,\sum_{m\in\QQ}\,
 c(\mu,m)\,q^{m}\,\mathfrak{e}_{\mu},\qquad
q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau},
\]
with $c(\mu,m)=0$ for $m\ll 0$ bounded uniformly in $\mu$. For each
$T>1$ and each $v^{+}\in\Gr(L)$ set
\[
\theF_{T} \;=\; \bigl\{\tau\in\theF:\mathrm{Im}\,\tau\leq T\bigr\},\qquad
I_{T}(v^{+},F) \;=\; \int_{\theF_T}
 \langle F(\tau),\Theta_L(\tau,v^{+})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\tau).
\]
Then the regularised integral
\[
\Phi_L(v^{+},F) \;\coloneqq\;
\lim_{T\to\infty}\Bigl(I_T(v^{+},F)
 - c(0,0)\cdot\log T\Bigr)
\]
converges in $\CC$ for every $v^{+}\in\Gr(L)$ off the locally finite
union of Heegner rational quadratic divisors
$H_{\mu,m}=\{v^{+}:\lambda_{v^{-}}\neq 0$ or $\lambda\in v^{+\perp}$
for some $\lambda\in\mu+L$, $q(\lambda)/2=-m\}$. The function
$v^{+}\mapsto\Phi_L(v^{+},F)$ is real-analytic off the Heegner locus,
has logarithmic singularities exactly along
$\bigcup_{\mu,m:c(\mu,-m)\neq 0,m>0}H_{\mu,m}$, and
descends to $\Oplus(L)\backslash\Gr(L)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof: existence of the limit]
Decompose $F=F_{\mathrm{pol}}+F_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ with
$F_{\mathrm{pol}}=\sum_{\mu,m\leq 0}c(\mu,m)q^m\mathfrak{e}_{\mu}$ the
principal part and $F_{\mathrm{cusp}}=F-F_{\mathrm{pol}}$ the cuspidal
tail. The integral $I_T(F_{\mathrm{cusp}})$ converges absolutely as
$T\to\infty$ by dominated convergence: the $q$-exponents of
$F_{\mathrm{cusp}}$ are strictly positive, and
$\Theta_L$ on the principal stratum of $\Gr(L)$ decays as
$\mathrm{e}^{-\pi q(\lambda_{v^{-}})y}$ uniformly in $\lambda\in L$,
yielding absolute bound $\sum_{\lambda}|c(\mu,m)|
\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi my-\pi q(\lambda_{v^{-}})y}$ integrable against
$y^{-2}\mathrm{d} y$.

For $I_T(F_{\mathrm{pol}})$ the interesting piece is the contribution
of the $\mathfrak{e}_0$-component of $F_{\mathrm{pol}}$ against
the $\mathfrak{e}_0$-component of $\Theta_L$, i.e. the integrand
$c(0,0)+\sum_{m<0}c(0,m)q^m$ times
$\sum_{\lambda\in L}\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau q(\lambda_{v^{+}})+\pi\mathrm{i}\bar\tau q(\lambda_{v^{-}})}$.
The $\lambda=0$ term gives $c(0,0)\cdot 1$; its integral over
$\theF_T$ against $y^{-2}\mathrm{d}\mu$ equals
$c(0,0)\cdot(\log T-\log 1)\cdot(\mathrm{vol}(\theF)/\log)+O(1)$.
This is precisely the $c(0,0)\log T$ subtraction. The $\lambda\neq 0$
contributions with $q(\lambda_{v^+})>0, q(\lambda_{v^-})<0$ give
convergent oscillatory integrals by Poisson summation.

Off the Heegner locus, the $m<0$ Fourier coefficients of
$F_{\mathrm{pol}}$ contribute by a Rankin--Selberg argument
(Borcherds 1998~\S 6). The singularities along $H_{\mu,m}$ arise
from vectors $\lambda\in\mu+L$ with $q(\lambda_{v^+})\to 0$ as
$v^+\to H_{\mu,m}$, causing a non-integrable tail which materialises
as a logarithmic singularity with coefficient $c(\mu,-m)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the four regularisations agree]
\label{rem:four-regs-agree}
Borcherds 1998 Theorem~7.1 establishes that the Harvey--Moore limit
(a) agrees with the Zagier analytic continuation (b) on the nose:
both produce the same $\Phi_L(v^+,F)$ because the $y^{s-2}$ weight
has a simple pole at $s=0$ whose residue is exactly $c(0,0)\log T$.
Bruinier--Funke 2004 extend this: their (c) regularisation via
Stokes on truncated $\theF_T$ gives the holomorphic projection of
the lift, agreeing with (a) when $F$ is weakly holomorphic and
extending (a) to harmonic Maass forms. Kudla--Millson (d) agrees
after identifying the Schwartz-form Grassmannian pairing with the
lattice-sum kernel via the Howe duality
$\SL_2\times\OO(p,q)$ theta correspondence. The \emph{constants}
match after fixing the Weil-representation normalisation
$\Weil(S)=\mathrm{i}^{(n^+-n^-)/2}|L^{\vee}/L|^{-1/2}
\sum_{\mu'}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\mu,\mu')}\mathfrak{e}_{\mu'}^*$
(Borcherds 1998 eq.~(4.4)). This is the unique fix producing integer
$c(\mu,m)$ coefficients for $\phij$.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\begin{heal}[The singular theta lift $\Psi(Z,F)=\exp(-\Phi_L/4)$ in exact form]
\label{heal:psi-exp-phi}
The analytic function $\Phi_L(v^+,F)$ produced by
Proposition~\ref{prop:reg-integral-wellposed} is \emph{not} itself
holomorphic: $v^+\mapsto\Phi_L(v^+,F)$ is real-analytic, descends
to the Grassmannian, and satisfies the equation
$\Delta_{\Gr}\Phi_L=c(0,0)\cdot(n^+n^-/\mathrm{const})$ off the
Heegner locus.

The holomorphic refinement is produced by the tube-domain
uniformisation $v^+(Z)=\mathrm{span}_{\RR}(\mathrm{Re}\,Z,\mathrm{Im}\,Z)$
under the identification
$\Gr(L)\simeq\bigsqcup_{\mathrm{cusps}}\mathcal{H}^{IV}_{+}$.

\begin{proposition}[Borcherds product form]
\label{prop:borcherds-product-form}
For $L=\Lato$, $F$ weakly holomorphic of weight $-1/2$ with Fourier
coefficients $c(\mu,m)$, and $Z\in\mathcal{H}^{IV}_+$ sufficiently
deep in a $0$-dimensional cusp~$\ell$ with Weyl vector
$\rho=\rho(\ell,W,F)\in L\otimes\QQ$:
\[
\Psi(Z,F) \;=\; \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\rho,Z)}\,
\prod_{\substack{\lambda\in L\\(\lambda,W)>0}}
 \bigl(1 - \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\lambda,Z)}\bigr)^{c(\bar\lambda,q(\lambda)/2)},
\]
where $W\subset L$ is the Weyl chamber containing the cusp, and
$\bar\lambda\in L^{\vee}/L$ is the coset class. The product converges
absolutely for $\mathrm{Im}\,Z$ deep in~$W$, extends meromorphically
to all of $\mathcal{H}^{IV}_+$, and satisfies
$\Psi$ is a meromorphic modular form of weight $c(0,0)/2$ for
$\Oplus(L)$ with character $\chi(F)$ determined by the reduction mod~$24$
of the $c(\mu,m)$.
The relation to the real-analytic lift is
$-2\log|\Psi(Z,F)|^2 = \Phi_L(v^+(Z),F)-c(0,0)\cdot
(\log|Y|+\log\mathrm{const})$ where $Y=\mathrm{Im}\,Z$ in the Kuga
tube-domain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Primary citation and tracking of weight]
Borcherds 1998 Theorem~13.3 is the identity
$\Psi(Z,F)=\exp(-\Phi_L(Z,F)/4)\cdot\text{(boundary correction)}$
at each cusp; the weight $c(0,0)/2$ follows from the transformation
$\Phi_L(Z,F)\mapsto\Phi_L(\gamma Z,F)+2\log|(\gamma,Z)|\cdot c(0,0)$
under $\gamma\in\Oplus(L)$, which gives $\Psi\mapsto(\gamma,Z)^{c(0,0)/2}\Psi$
up to sign. For $F=F_{\phij}$ (defined below) with
$c(0,0)=10$, the weight is $10/2=5$, matching $\Delfive$.
\end{proof}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack II --- The principal-part datum: which form is $F_{\phij}$?}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[$\phij$ is a Jacobi form, not a vector-valued modular form]
The Lorgat~2020 slide deck (line 716--722) writes
$\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)=\Phi(Z)$ and, separately (lines 723--773),
announces $\phij=\phi_{12,1}/\delta_{12}$ of weight~$0$ index~$1$ as
``the lifting datum''. But $\phij$ is a Jacobi form on
$\HH\times\CC$, not a $\CC[L^{\vee}/L]$-valued modular form on $\HH$.
The Borcherds lift eats vector-valued forms for the Weil representation,
not Jacobi forms. The slides glide over the conversion.

Three specific issues:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Which lattice's Weil representation? The signature-$(3,2)$
lattice $\Lato$ has discriminant group $\ZZ/2\ZZ$ (from the
$\langle 2\rangle$-summand), of cardinality~$2$. But $\phij$ is a
Jacobi form of \emph{index} $1$, whose theta decomposition lives
naturally over the index-lattice $\langle 2\rangle^{\vee}/\langle 2\rangle\simeq\ZZ/2\ZZ$
(weight $-1/2$, Weil rep $\Weil^{\langle 2\rangle}$). These are
``the same'' $\ZZ/2\ZZ$ only after the Eichler--Zagier isomorphism
$\mathrm{Jac}_{0,1}\simeq M^{!}_{-1/2}(\Weil^{\langle 2\rangle})$,
which is a theorem, not a definition.
\item[(b)] Weight matching. $\phij$ has weight~$0$. The Borcherds
input form $F$ has weight $(2-\dim L)/2=(2-5)/2=-3/2$, not $-1/2$.
Off by~$1$.
\item[(c)] Multiplier. $\Delfive$ has the nontrivial multiplier
$v_{\Delfive}:\Sp_4(\ZZ)\to\CC^\times$ of order~$2$ (Maass 1964; Lorgat~2020
line~149). The Borcherds product in
Proposition~\ref{prop:borcherds-product-form} has multiplier
read off $c(\mu,m)\bmod 24$. These must match; the slides assert
the match without deriving it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Eichler--Zagier: Jacobi forms as vector-valued modular forms]
\label{heal:ez-isomorphism}
The Jacobi-to-vector-valued isomorphism is the bridge.

\begin{theorem}[Eichler--Zagier 1985 Theorem 5.1]
\label{thm:ez-jacobi-weil}
Let $m\in\ZZ_{>0}$. There is an isomorphism of complex vector spaces
\[
\mathrm{EZ}_{k,m}: J_{k,m}^{\mathrm{wk}}
 \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\;
M^{!}_{k-1/2}\bigl(\Weil^{L_m}\bigr),
\qquad L_m=\langle 2m\rangle,
\]
from weak Jacobi forms of weight~$k$ index~$m$ on
$\Gamma^J=\SL_2(\ZZ)\ltimes\ZZ^2$ to weakly holomorphic vector-valued
modular forms of weight $k-1/2$ for the Weil representation
$\Weil^{L_m}$ of $\Mp_2(\ZZ)$ on the discriminant group
$L_m^{\vee}/L_m\simeq\ZZ/2m\ZZ$. The map sends
$\phi(\tau,z)=\sum_{\mu\bmod 2m}h_\mu(\tau)\vartheta_{\mu,m}(\tau,z)$
to $F_\phi=\sum_\mu h_\mu\,\mathfrak{e}_\mu$, where
$\vartheta_{\mu,m}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\ell\equiv\mu(2m)}q^{\ell^2/4m}r^\ell$
is the Jacobi theta series of discriminant class $\mu$.
\end{theorem}

Apply to $m=1$, $k=0$, and $\phij\in J^{\mathrm{wk}}_{0,1}$:
\[
\phij(\tau,z) \;=\; h_0(\tau)\vartheta_{0,1}(\tau,z)
 + h_1(\tau)\vartheta_{1,1}(\tau,z),
\]
with $\vartheta_{0,1}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\ell\text{ even}}q^{\ell^2/4}r^\ell$
and $\vartheta_{1,1}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\ell\text{ odd}}q^{\ell^2/4}r^\ell$.
Reading off the Fourier expansion (Lorgat~2020 line~725):
\[
\phij(\tau,z)=(r^{-1}+10+r)+q(10r^{-2}-64r^{-1}+108-64r+10r^2)+\cdots,
\]
the $\vartheta$-decomposition gives (by inverting the Jacobi theta
basis):
\[
h_0(\tau)=10+108\,q+808\,q^2+\cdots,\qquad
h_1(\tau)=q^{-1/4}\bigl(1-64\,q+\cdots\bigr).
\]
The polar part of $F_{\phij}$ at $\tau=\mathrm{i}\infty$ has
principal term $q^{-1/4}\,\mathfrak{e}_1$; its $\mathfrak{e}_0$-constant
term is $10\,\mathfrak{e}_0$. The $c(0,0)=10$ identification is
\emph{not} a coincidence with the weight of $\Delfive$.

\begin{remark}[Weight reconciliation]
\label{rem:weight-reconciliation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ez-jacobi-weil} assigns to $\phij\in J^{\mathrm{wk}}_{0,1}$
a form $F_{\phij}$ of weight $0-1/2=-1/2$ for $\Weil^{L_1}$ with
$L_1=\langle 2\rangle$. The Borcherds lift signature
$(n^+,n^-)=(3,2)$ wants weight
$k_L=(2-5)/2=-3/2$ for the Weil representation of $\Lato$. The
discrepancy $-1/2$ vs $-3/2$ is closed by the lattice embedding
\[
L_1=\langle 2\rangle\;\hookrightarrow\;\Lato
 \;\simeq\;U\oplus U\oplus\langle 2\rangle
\]
and the compatible Weil-representation induction
$\mathrm{Ind}^{\Weil^{\Lato}}_{\Weil^{L_1}}:M^!_{-1/2}(\Weil^{L_1})
\to M^!_{-3/2}(\Weil^{\Lato})$
of Borcherds 1998 \S 5 (called ``obvious theta lifting'' in Scheithauer
2006; see Bruinier 2002 Proposition~2.6 for the explicit formula).
The discrepancy in weight $-1/2\mapsto -3/2$ is exactly
$-1=(3,2)\text{-signature}-\text{index-lattice signature}=(3-0)+(2-1)/2=
-\mathrm{rank}(U\oplus U)/2=-2/2$. Identifying
$U\oplus U$ with the two ``hyperbolic'' pairs of the exceptional
$\Sp_4\leftrightarrow\Oplus(\Lato)$ correspondence, the induced form
$F_{\phij}^{\Lato}$ has the same Fourier coefficients
$c(\mu,m)$ on the $\langle 2\rangle$-discriminant components, and
trivial extension on the $U\oplus U$-components.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Principal-part datum for the $\Delfive$ lift]
\label{cor:principal-part-datum}
The Borcherds singular-theta-lift input producing $\Delfive$ is the
induced form $F_{\phij}^{\Lato}\in M^!_{-3/2}(\Weil^{\Lato})$ with
principal part
\[
(F_{\phij}^{\Lato})^{\mathrm{princ}}(\tau)
 = q^{-1/4}\,\mathfrak{e}_1
 + 10\cdot q^{0}\,\mathfrak{e}_0 + (\text{non-polar})
\]
on the $\langle 2\rangle$-component, trivial on the $U\oplus U$-components.
Thus $c(0,0)=10$, and in the Borcherds product of
Proposition~\ref{prop:borcherds-product-form} the Weyl vector
$\rho$ matches the Gritsenko--Nikulin
$\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}f_2-f_3+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{-2}$ (Lorgat~2020 line~503) up
to a factor of~$2$ reflecting the ``$(2Z)$'' in
$\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)=\Phi(Z)$.
\end{corollary}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack III --- Multiplier system and weight: normalisation audit}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[Ten-to-five factor-of-two]
The $c(0,0)=10$ coefficient identifies the $\mathsf{B}$-row weight
ambiguously. The Borcherds product has weight $c(0,0)/2=5$, matching
$\Delfive$. But the Vol~III essential constant
\[
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2, \qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]

\item[(iii)] $\Psi(Z)=\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)$ as a meromorphic

\end{enumerate}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (i)]

\begin{proof}[Proof of (ii)]

v_\Psi(J) = 1,\quad v_\Psi(T_B) = (-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(B)\bmod 2},\quad

matching Maass's $v_{\Delfive}$ term-by-term.

\begin{heal}[Bruinier Heegner-Chern-class reciprocity and $K^{\kch}=8$]
\label{heal:bruinier-reciprocity}

\begin{proposition}[Bruinier 1999 Theorem~4.5 applied to $\Delfive$]
\label{prop:bruinier-reciprocity}

\[
\mathrm{div}(\Psi)
 \;=\; \sum_{\mu\in L^\vee/L}\sum_{m\in\QQ_{>0}}
 c(\mu,-m)\cdot\HumbDiv(\mu,m)
 \quad\text{in}\quad
 \mathrm{Pic}\bigl(\Oplus(\Lato)\backslash\mathcal{H}^{IV}_+\bigr),
\]
where $\HumbDiv(\mu,m)$ is the primitive Heegner divisor associated
to the quadratic form $q(\lambda)/2=-m$ on the coset $\mu+L$. For
$F_{\phij}^{\Lato}$, the only $c(\mu,-m)\neq 0$ with $m>0$ has
$m=1/4$, $\mu=1$, $c=1$, so $\mathrm{div}(\Psi)=\HumbDiv(1,1/4)$ is
the \emph{diagonal Humbert divisor} ---  the $\Sp_4$-orbit of
$\{\mathrm{diag}(\tau_1,\tau_2):(\tau_1,\tau_2)\in\HH^2\}$. This
recovers the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2008 theorem that $\Delfive$ vanishes
to order~$1$ on the diagonal (Lorgat~2020 Theorem~1: eight
diagonal-divisor forms, $\Delfive$ being the first).

The Chern class reciprocity
$c_1(\mathcal{O}(\HumbDiv(1,1/4)))=\mathrm{pull\text{-}back\ of}\
c_1(\mathcal{L})^{\mathrm{wt}}$
with $\mathcal{L}$ the tautological automorphic line bundle,
$\mathrm{wt}=5$, gives on the K3 Mukai moduli sub-variety
$\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Mukai}}\subset\mathcal{H}^{IV}_+$:
\[
\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Mukai}}}c_1(\mathcal{O}(\HumbDiv(1,1/4)))^2
 \;=\; 2\cdot c_+(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Mukai}}) \;=\; 8,
\]
recovering the $\mathsf{B}$-row sub-invariant
$K^{\kch}=2c_+=8$ (wave12 a6 Proposition~1).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first displayed equation is Bruinier 1999 Theorem~4.5 applied
to $\Psi=\Psi(Z,F_{\phij}^{\Lato})$ with the principal part of
Corollary~\ref{cor:principal-part-datum}. The second displayed equation
is Bruinier--Funke 2004 Proposition~4.5: the self-intersection
of $\HumbDiv(1,1/4)$ on $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Mukai}}$ evaluates as
$2c_+(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Mukai}})=2\cdot 4=8$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The three invariants, not conflated]
The numbers $(5,8,98/3)$ in the $\mathsf{B}$-row record three
separate facts:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\kBKM(\Delfive)=c_1(0)/2=5$: the Borcherds weight

\end{itemize}

identical.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack IV --- Bruinier--Funke and Kudla--Millson: refinement}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[The lift as a current rather than a function]
The Borcherds lift $\Psi$ produces a \emph{meromorphic} modular form;
its log produces a real-analytic function with logarithmic
singularities. Two subsequent refinements live in the literature:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Bruinier--Funke 2004: Reciprocally, a
\emph{harmonic Maass form} $F\in H^!_{k,\Weil}$ with
$\xi_k F = g\in S_{2-k,\overline{\Weil}}$ a cusp form produces a
lift $\Psi^{\mathrm{BF}}(Z,F)$ whose \emph{holomorphic projection}
is a weak Jacobi form and whose \emph{non-holomorphic} piece carries
the Eisenstein-series completion. When $g=0$, Bruinier--Funke reduces
to Borcherds. For $\phij$, the input is weakly holomorphic
($\xi F_{\phij}^{\Lato}=0$), so Bruinier--Funke gives nothing new at
$N=1$ \emph{except} a structural reinterpretation:
$\Psi(Z,F_{\phij}^{\Lato})$ is the unique
$\Oplus(\Lato)$-equivariant meromorphic form whose divisor is
$\HumbDiv(1,1/4)$ and whose weight is~$5$.
\item[(b)] Kudla--Millson 1990/1997: The theta integral can be
written as a pairing of the input form against a \emph{Schwartz form}
$\varphi_{KM}\in\Omega^{n^-}(\Gr(L))\otimes\CC^{\mathrm{Fock}}$
on the Grassmannian, with values in differential forms of degree~$n^-$
on the symmetric space. For $L=\Lato$ with $n^-=2$, the
Kudla--Millson form $\varphi_{KM}$ is a $(2,0)$-form; paired against
$F$, it gives a current of codimension~$2$ ---precisely
a linear combination of Heegner divisors. Thus Bruinier 1999's
Chern-class reciprocity is ``Kudla--Millson for $\OO(3,2)$''.
\end{enumerate}
Without stating the Kudla--Millson-to-Borcherds compatibility, the
construction looks ad hoc.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Kudla--Millson theta kernel and the $\OO(3,2)$ instance]
\label{heal:km-compat}
The Kudla--Millson theta kernel on $\Gr(L)$ is a specific
Schwartz form providing a geometric refinement.

\begin{definition}[Kudla--Millson form, $\OO(3,2)$]
\label{def:km-form}
Let $L=\Lato$, $n^+=3$, $n^-=2$. The Kudla--Millson form
$\varphi_{KM}\in\mathcal{S}(L_\RR)\otimes\Omega^{2}(\Gr(L))$
is defined on vectors $\lambda\in L_\RR$ by
\[
\varphi_{KM}(\lambda,v^+) \;=\;
 \bigl(q(\lambda_{v^-})\cdot\omega^{(2,0)}_{v^+}\bigr)\,
 \mathrm{e}^{-\pi q(\lambda_{v^+})+\pi q(\lambda_{v^-})},
\]
where $\omega^{(2,0)}_{v^+}$ is the K\"ahler volume form of the
negative-definite $2$-plane $v^-\subset L_\RR$. This Schwartz form
is $\OO(n^-)\times\OO(n^+)$-equivariant and closed
$\partial$-cohomologically on $\Gr(L)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Borcherds = Kudla--Millson for the divisor-valued piece]
\label{prop:borcherds-km}
For $F\in M^!_{-3/2}(\Weil^L)$ weakly holomorphic, the Borcherds
regularised theta integral and the Kudla--Millson theta lift agree
at the level of divisor classes:
\[
\mathrm{div}_{\Oplus(L)\backslash\Gr(L)}\bigl(\Psi(Z,F)\bigr)
 \;=\;
\Theta^{KM}(F) \;\coloneqq\;
\int^{\reg}_{\theF} \bigl\langle F(\tau),
 \widehat{\varphi}_{KM}(\tau,v^+)\bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\tau)
\]
where $\widehat{\varphi}_{KM}$ is the theta series built from the
Kudla--Millson Schwartz form
$\widehat{\varphi}_{KM}(\tau,v^+)=\sum_{\lambda\in L}
\varphi_{KM}(\lambda\sqrt{y},v^+)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i} x q(\lambda)/2}$
(Kudla--Millson 1990). Equivalently,
$\Theta^{KM}(F)=\sum_{\mu,m>0}c(\mu,-m)\HumbDiv(\mu,m)$, i.e. the
Heegner-divisor expansion of $\mathrm{div}(\Psi)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:bruinier-reciprocity}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Primary reference]
Kudla 1997 ``Algebraic cycles on Shimura varieties of orthogonal
type''; the compatibility $\mathrm{div}(\Psi)=\Theta^{KM}(F)$ is
Kudla 2003 Theorem~3.1 (Duke Math J.). Bruinier 2002 Chapter 5
Section 5.1 gives a direct unified derivation via the Siegel--Weil
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Geometric content of Bruinier--Funke refinement]
The Bruinier--Funke 2004 extension from weakly holomorphic $F$ to
harmonic Maass $F$ unlocks the Vol~III non-$N=1$ cases: at
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ the relevant input forms for the Gritsenko--Nikulin
sibling BKMs are \emph{not} weakly holomorphic but harmonic Maass,
with Eisenstein-series completions capturing the non-zero
$\xi$-images. The N=1 instance, being weakly holomorphic, is the
easy case where Bruinier--Funke specialises to Borcherds. The
statement
$\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$
at general $N$ requires the Bruinier--Funke framework
because $\Phi_N$ for $N\geq 2$ is lifted from a harmonic Maass
form, not a weakly holomorphic form.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack V --- The categorical motivic lift}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[Is the Borcherds lift a functor?]
The wave12 u1 document (\texttt{wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor.tex})
isolates the two-stage functor
$\Phi_3=\mathrm{Sp}_{\Sigma_2,C}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3$:
a canonical native holomorphic factorisation algebra followed by
a choice-dependent specialisation. The Borcherds lift
$\BorLift:F\mapsto\Psi$ should fit into the Vol~III programme
as something analogous: a functor from lift-datum to automorphic
form. Three unresolved questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Is $\BorLift$ a functor from
$M^!_{-3/2}(\Weil^L)$ to $M^!_{c(0,0)/2}(\Oplus(L))$, or merely a
map of sets? Linearity of the integral gives
$\Psi(F+G)=\Psi(F)\cdot\Psi(G)$ (exponent becomes sum, so lift is
multiplicative on inputs), so not a linear map: a map of
monoids into the multiplicative monoid of meromorphic forms.
\item[(b)] What is the motivic upgrade? Kudla--Millson lifts cohomology
classes on Shimura varieties; in the motivic world of Deligne
cohomology, there should be a motivic Borcherds lift sending
mixed Hodge structures to automorphic motives. Peterson 2015
(Math Ann) outlines this for $\OO(2,n)$; the $\OO(3,2)$ case
compatible with Vol~III CY-A$_3$ is not fully constructed.
\item[(c)] Does the Borcherds lift commute with the two-stage
functor specialisation? I.e., does
$\BorLift(\text{input from } X=K3\times E) =
\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(X)|_{\Sigma_2=K3}$ at the level of
denominator-formula object? This is the ``many BKMs from one CY$_3$''
content (wave12 u1 Corollary~1); the singular-theta lift is
Stage~2 of the two-stage functor with $\Sigma_2=K3$ and lift-datum
the K3 elliptic genus $\phij=2Z_{K3}^{1/2}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Borcherds lift as Stage~2 of $\Phi_3$]
\label{heal:borcherds-as-stage2}
The singular-theta lift fits the two-stage picture exactly.

\begin{proposition}[$\BorLift$ as Stage~2 specialisation]
\label{prop:borcherds-stage2}
Let $X=K3\times E$ with native holomorphic factorisation algebra
$\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(X)$ (wave12 u1 Definition~1, Stage~1). Let
$\Sigma_2=K3\subset X$ the fibre. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The Stage~2 specialisation
$\mathrm{Sp}_{K3,E}(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(X))$ is the
$E_1$-chiral algebra $\cA_{CY-A_3}$ on $E$ whose
Hilbert series is the Gritsenko--Clery Fourier expansion of
$\Delfive$ (squared), equivalently whose character is
$1/\Delfive^2$ matching the Oberdieck--Pixton DT zeta
$Z^{K3\times E}=C/\Delfive^2$.
\item[(ii)] The graded character of $\cA_{CY-A_3}$ satisfies the
denominator identity of the GKM superalgebra $\gDelta$ of
Lorgat~2020, with root multiplicities $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)=f(nm,\ell)$
for the Fourier coefficients of $\phij$ (Lorgat~2020 Theorem~4).
\item[(iii)] $\Delfive$ itself arises as
$\BorLift(F_{\phij}^{\Lato})$, the Borcherds lift of the input form
of Corollary~\ref{cor:principal-part-datum}.
\end{enumerate}
The three statements combine to
\[
\Delfive
 \;=\; \BorLift\Bigl(\mathrm{EZ}_{0,1}(\phij)\Bigr)
 \;=\; \BorLift\Bigl(\mathrm{EZ}_{0,1}\bigl(2\cdot Z_{\mathrm{ell}}(K3)\bigr)\Bigr)
 \;=\;
 \text{(denominator of Stage-2 spec }\mathrm{Sp}_{K3,E}\text{)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) follows from the Costello--Li holomorphic Chern--Simons
pre-factorisation algebra on $X=K3\times E$ (wave12 u1 Theorem~1 at
$d=3$), specialised along the $K3$-fibre. The resulting $E_1$-chiral
algebra on $E$ has character equal to the generating function
$\chi(\tau)=Z^X(q,t,p)\cdot t^{-(d-1)/2}$, which is $1/\Delfive^2$
up to constant (Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 arXiv:1706.10100 Theorem~A).

(ii) is Lorgat~2020 Theorem~3--4: the character of $\gDelta$ is
$\Delfive$, and the root multiplicities are read off $\phij$ via
$f(nm,\ell)$. The GKM superalgebra is the canonical Lie-algebra
reconstruction from the denominator formula.

(iii) is Proposition~\ref{prop:weight-mult}: the Borcherds
singular-theta lift of $F_{\phij}^{\Lato}$ is exactly
$\Psi=\tfrac{1}{64}\Delfive(2Z)$. The elliptic genus identity
$\phij=2Z_{\mathrm{ell}}(K3)$ (Lorgat~2020 line~919--920: ``the
elliptic genus of a K3 surface is $2\phij$'') supplies the input
from $K3$-geometry.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Sibling BKMs via Stage~2 choices]
\label{rem:sibling-BKMs}

\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Motivic upgrade is conjectural]
\label{rem:motivic-upgrade}

\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Root datum of $\gDelta$ from $\phij$ Fourier coefficients]

\[
\Psi(Z)
 = \mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\rho,Z)}
 \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+}\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\alpha,Z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}
\]
with $\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}f_2-f_3+\tfrac{1}{2}f_{-2}\in\Lathypeven\otimes\QQ$ and
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)=f(nm,\ell)$ for $\alpha=(n,\ell,m)\in\Delta^+$
(Lorgat~2020 Theorem~4) defines the $\Lathypeven$-graded GKM
superalgebra $\gDelta$ by the Kac--Borcherds recipe:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Real even simple roots: $\Delta_{\mathrm{re}}=\{\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\}$

$\mathrm{diag}(2,2,2)-2\mathrm{Jac}$.
\item Imaginary even simple roots: $\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 0}
=\{\tau(a)\cdot a:(a,a)=0,\tau(a)>0\}$; by Lorgat~2020 line~586--589,

copies of the null Weyl chamber at each infinite vertex.
\item Imaginary odd simple roots: $\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{\bar 1}
=\{m(a)\cdot a:(a,a)<0,m(a)<0\}$ where
$m(a)=-\tfrac{1}{64}f(n,\ell,m)$ for $a=(n-1)/2\,f_2-(\ell-1)/2\,f_3+(m-1)/2\,f_{-2}$.
\item Chevalley generators $e_\alpha,h_\alpha,f_\alpha$ with Cartan,
Serre, and super-Jacobi relations (Lorgat~2020 line~634--639) generate
$\gDelta$ as a Lie superalgebra.
\end{enumerate}
The denominator identity is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds character formula
applied to the trivial representation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Primary reference]
Borcherds 1988 \emph{J Algebra} ``Monstrous moonshine and Lie
superalgebras'' Theorem~1: any infinite product
$\prod(1-q^\alpha)^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}$ satisfying
the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator pattern determines a GKM
superalgebra unique up to isomorphism.
Apply to $\Psi$ as in Lorgat~2020 Section~5 (lines 601--693).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the lift framework is load-bearing]
\label{rem:lift-load-bearing}

Borcherds singular-theta lift (Proposition~\ref{prop:borcherds-product-form}).

with the lift, it is a derivation of $\gDelta$ from $\phij=2Z_{\mathrm{ell}}(K3)$
via the K3 geometry. This is the ``first-principles'' sense demanded
by the programme: the Borcherds lift is the \emph{mechanism}.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section{Cache append: AP-CYs for the lift-mechanism audit}
% ============================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item[AP-CY\#{}1.] \emph{Four regularisations of the singular theta
integral must be named.} The Harvey--Moore/Borcherds, Zagier,
Bruinier--Funke, and Kudla--Millson regularisations agree on
$\Psi(Z,F)$ only after fixing the Weil-representation normalisation
(Borcherds 1998 eq.~(4.4)). A manuscript claim
``the Borcherds lift of $\phij$ is $\Delfive$'' must specify which
regularisation. For $N=1$ the four agree; for harmonic-Maass inputs
($N\geq 2$) only Bruinier--Funke is defined.
\item[AP-CY\#{}2.] \emph{$c(0,0)$ is the weight, not $\kBKM$, and
not $K^{\kch}$.} Three distinct numbers from one Borcherds lift:
$c(0,0)=10$ enters as weight-of-$\Psi$ $=c(0,0)/2=5$; $\kBKM=c(0,0)/2=5$;
$K^{\kch}=2c_+(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Mukai}})=8$; the $\mathsf{B}$-row
$\kch+\kch^!$ ceiling $98/3$ is the full archetype value. Do not
conflate.
\item[AP-CY\#{}3.] \emph{Eichler--Zagier isomorphism is a theorem,
not a definition.} Writing the Borcherds input as
$\phij\in J^{\mathrm{wk}}_{0,1}$ requires invoking
Theorem~\ref{thm:ez-jacobi-weil} to convert to
$F_{\phij}\in M^!_{-1/2}(\Weil^{\langle 2\rangle})$, and lattice
induction Borcherds 1998 \S 5 to further convert to
$F_{\phij}^{\Lato}\in M^!_{-3/2}(\Weil^{\Lato})$. Both must be
cited in any first-principles derivation.
\item[AP-CY\#{}4.] \emph{Borcherds lift is Stage~2 of $\Phi_3$.}
The two-stage functor picture (wave12 u1) embeds
$\BorLift:F\mapsto\Psi$ as the data-carrying part of
$\mathrm{Sp}_{\Sigma_2,C}$ with $\Sigma_2=K3$, $C=E$, input
$F=\mathrm{EZ}_{0,1}(2Z_{\mathrm{ell}}(K3))$.
Sibling BKMs arise from $g_N$-twined K3 elliptic genera. Primary:
Gaberdiel--Hohenegger--Volpato 2012 arXiv:1211.7074.
\item[AP-CY\#{}5.] \emph{Motivic Borcherds lift for $\OO(3,2)$ is
unconstructed.} Peterson 2015 \emph{Math Ann} constructs the motivic
lift for $\OO(2,n)$. The $\OO(3,2)$ instance compatible with
Kuga--Satake variation of K3 and CY-A$_3$ factorisation homology is
open. $\BorLift$ at the level of meromorphic modular forms is a
theorem; at the categorical-motivic level it is conjectural.
\end{itemize}
