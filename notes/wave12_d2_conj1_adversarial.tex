\section{Wave-12 D2: five adversarial holes in Conjecture~1$'$}
\label{sec:wave12-d2-adversarial}

The Wave-11 W1 statement (post-U2 fabricated-BOP retraction) reads:
$(1,\mathrm{id})$ is a theorem, $(N\ge 2,\mathrm{id})$ partial/conjectural,
$N\in\{5,7,8\}$ is a distinct super-VOA theorem. This note attacks
each Wave-11 ``theorem'' label from first principles; five structural
holes are identified. Every hole is mathematics, not bookkeeping.

\subsection*{Hole 1. The $\dim S_5(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})=1$ claim is
cited from a \emph{twisted} dimension formula that Ibukiyama--Wakatsuki 2014
does \emph{not} cover.}
\label{subsec:d2-hole-dim-s5-twisted}

Wave-8 I3 Theorem~\ref{thm:dim-S5-one} cites Pitale--Schmidt 2014
(\emph{JNT} 134) as ``completing'' the twisted dimension
$\dim S_5(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})$ from Weissauer's untwisted
Hilbert series. Ibukiyama--Wakatsuki 2014 (arXiv:1305.4886),
\emph{``Siegel cusp forms of degree two and trace formula
for Hecke operators''}, is the standard trace-formula reference
for $\Sp_4(\ZZ)$-dimension computation and computes
$\dim S_k(\Sp_4(\ZZ))$ on \emph{trivial} character at weight
$k\ge 3$ via Hashimoto's explicit formula. Neither paper
tabulates $\dim S_5(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})$ directly; the
Wave-8 I3 proof outline interpolates
$J^{\mathrm{cusp}}_{5,1}\cong S_6(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ))=0$
(Eichler--Zagier), then asserts one-dimensionality by
``Saito--Kurokawa non-lift''.

\emph{Gap.} Eichler--Zagier maps $S_k(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})
\hookrightarrow \bigoplus_{m\ge 1} J^{\mathrm{cusp}}_{k,m}
(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ),\nu^{1/2}_{\Delta_5})$ only when the theta-multiplier
$\nu^{1/2}_{\Delta_5}$ extends to the Jacobi group; for the binary
sign character of $\Sp_4(\ZZ)^{\mathrm{ab}}=\ZZ/2\ZZ$ this extension
introduces a half-integer theta shift. The first Fourier-Jacobi
coefficient $\phi_1\in J^{\mathrm{cusp}}_{5,1}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ),\nu^{1/2})$
is \emph{not} $J^{\mathrm{cusp}}_{5,1}(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ))$; the latter
is $S_6$, but the twisted space can have dimension $0$ or $1$
independently. The Wave-8 cite ``Pitale--Schmidt 2014 Tables 1-2,
completed computation'' is not independently verified; the actual
Pitale--Schmidt 2014 paper treats $\mathrm{GSp}_4$ representation-theoretic
lifts, not $\Sp_4(\ZZ)$ twisted dimensions. \textbf{Status:
$\dim S_5(\Sp_4(\ZZ),\nu_{\Delta_5})=1$ is \ClaimStatusConjectured{}
until either a direct Hashimoto trace computation on $\nu_{\Delta_5}$
is exhibited or Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 is shown to prove it (they
prove \emph{existence} of $\Delta_5$ as the lift of $\phi_{0,1}$,
\emph{not} uniqueness in the twisted space).}

\subsection*{Hole 2. Four $c=-214$ derivations convergence: uniqueness
forces coincidence \emph{up to scalar}, not to the \emph{same} scalar.}
\label{subsec:d2-hole-four-derivations}

Wave-8 I3 Proposition~\ref{prop:four-c-minus-214} states that four
mechanically disjoint constructions (Arthur CAP, Kudla-Millson theta,
Harvey-Moore $c=-214$, Borcherds $c_1(0)/2$) land in $S_5$ and are
therefore scalar multiples of $\Delta_5$. The proposition then asserts
$C_1=C_2=C_3=C_4=1$ is a ``theorem (not a free match)'' provided
$a(T_0)=1$ is the shared normalisation.

\emph{Adversarial finding.} The shared-normalisation hypothesis is
precisely what needs proof. The four constructions operate in
different normalisation conventions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Arthur CAP-projection is normalised by the Arthur $SL_2$ packet
multiplicity, fixed only up to a sign $(\pm 1)$ by the
Arthur-endoscopic formula (Ta\"ibi 2019 gives $+1$ for $\Sp_4$ but
not for the $\nu_{\Delta_5}$-twisted case);
\item Kudla-Millson theta lift normalisation is fixed by the
Heegner-divisor class of $H_1\subset\mathcal{A}_2$; the Bruinier-Funke
regulator introduces a factor of $c_1(0)/2$ which is the very number
under test (circularity);
\item Harvey-Moore heterotic vacuum normalisation is convention-dependent
(affected by the charge-lattice choice and the GSO projection sign);
\item Borcherds' $c_1(0)/2$ normalisation is fixed by the Jacobi-form
constant-term coefficient.
\end{enumerate}
The statement $C_i=1$ for all $i$ is therefore four independent
normalisation-matching theorems, one per construction, not a
consequence of $\dim S_5=1$. \textbf{Status:
$C_1=C_2=C_3=C_4$ as numerical identity is \ClaimStatusOpen{};
up-to-scalar coincidence $\{C_i\Delta_5\}$ is theorem-forced, but this
is strictly weaker than what Wave-8 / Wave-11 claim.}

\subsection*{Hole 3. Oberdieck-Pixton 2017 is explicit only for
primitive K3-factor classes at $N=1$; no low-rank $(N,\mathrm{id})$
analog is explicitly computed.}
\label{subsec:d2-hole-oberdieck-explicit}

Wave-11 W1 labels $(2,\mathrm{id})$ and $(3,\mathrm{id})$ as
\ClaimStatusTheorem{} with primary input ``BOP 2019, Oberdieck 2018''.
Wave-11 U2 retracted BOP 2019 as fabricated. The residual citation
``Oberdieck 2018'' is Oberdieck-Pandharipande 2014 (arXiv:1411.1514,
\emph{conjectural at $N=1$}) plus Oberdieck-Pixton 2017
(\emph{Invent.\ Math.}\ 222 (2020); theorem at $N=1$ for
\emph{primitive-in-K3-factor} classes only).

\emph{Adversarial finding.} No paper in the verified literature
makes $Z^{\mathrm{DT}}_{[K3\times E/\ZZ_N]} = -1/\Phi_N$ a theorem
at any $N\ge 2$. The Bryan-Oberdieck 2018 (arXiv:1811.06102) paper
proves \emph{base cases at $N=2$, primitive, square $\in\{-2,0\}$}
via topological vertex; the general curve class at $N=2$ is
conjectural, and $N\in\{3,4,6\}$ has no base-case theorem at all.
\textbf{Status: Wave-11 W1 Table rows $(N,\mathrm{id})$ for
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ are \ClaimStatusConjectured{}, not
\ClaimStatusTheorem{}. The W1 table overstates by four rows.}

\subsection*{Hole 4. Cl\'ery-Gritsenko 2013 Theorem 5.1 proves paramodular
\emph{cusp forms exist}, not that they are \emph{BKM denominators}.}
\label{subsec:d2-hole-clery-gritsenko-bkm}

Wave-10 R5 and Wave-11 W1 invoke Cl\'ery-Gritsenko 2013
(\emph{Nagoya Math.\ J.}\ 199) Theorem~1.1 / Theorem~5.1 as
furnishing the exponential Borcherds lift of the twisted-twined
Jacobi form $\phi_{g,h}$ to a paramodular Siegel form
$\Phi^{(g,h)}_{10}$ whose reciprocal is the DT partition function.
Cl\'ery-Gritsenko's actual theorem proves the \emph{existence and
automorphy} of the lifted form and gives its \emph{infinite-product
expansion}; it does \emph{not} prove that the lifted form is a
\emph{BKM denominator identity} in the sense of Borcherds 1995.

\emph{Gap.} A BKM denominator identification requires that the
infinite product admit a \emph{real-simple-root} interpretation
(positive-norm roots of the superalgebra) together with
\emph{imaginary-simple-roots} whose multiplicities satisfy the
Borcherds superalgebra Serre relations. Cl\'ery-Gritsenko produces
a Siegel paramodular form; Borcherds 1995 Theorem 10.1 converts a
\emph{singular-weight} automorphic product into a BKM denominator,
which requires the lifted Jacobi form's polar part to encode a
Lorentzian lattice with hyperbolic Weyl chamber. The Wave-11 W1
identification
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha) = c_{g,h}(\alpha^2/2, \alpha\cdot\ell)$ holds
\emph{if} the Siegel form is of singular weight $(\mathrm{rk}\Lambda-2)/2$,
which at weight $k_N = 10/(N+1) < 5$ for $N\ge 2$ on a Lorentzian
lattice of rank $\le 26$ is \emph{not automatic}.
\textbf{Status: BKM-denominator interpretation of $\Phi^{(g,h)}_{k_N}$
at $N\ge 2$ is \ClaimStatusConjectured{}, not \ClaimStatusTheorem{};
Cl\'ery-Gritsenko provides automorphy, not BKM structure.}

\subsection*{Hole 5. $H^3(\Mtf,U(1))=\ZZ/12\oplus\ZZ/2$ is cited as
``theorem'' but the standard reference proves only cohomology with
integer coefficients; the $U(1)$-coefficient form requires an extra step.}
\label{subsec:d2-hole-h3-mason}

Wave-10 R5 and Wave-11 W1 cite Mason 1994 (\emph{Proc.\ London Math.\ Soc.})
for $H^3(\Mtf,U(1))\cong\ZZ/12\oplus\ZZ/2$. The actual Mason 1994 paper
(``The quantum double of a finite group and its role in conformal field
theory'') computes the Schur multiplier $H^2(\Mtf,\mathbb{C}^\times)=\ZZ/2$
and discusses the fusion-category third cohomology, but does not give
the full $H^3(\Mtf,U(1))$.

\emph{Adversarial finding.} The decomposition
$H^3(\Mtf,U(1))=\ZZ/12\oplus\ZZ/2$ appears in Gaberdiel-Persson-Volpato
2013 (arXiv:1211.7074) \S 4, \emph{cited} to Thomas 1986
(``$K$-theory of finite groups'') and to explicit cocycle computations
of the Mathieu group via the MAGMA/GAP cohomology package. The claim
is \emph{computational}, not a published theorem. The $\ZZ/12$-factor
generator $[\alpha_{K3}]$ of the K3 sigma-model cocycle is identified
via the transgression $H^4(BM_{24},\ZZ)\to H^3(\Mtf,U(1))$, and
vanishing on the $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$-slice is stated but the
transgression kernel is not independently verified at each order.

\emph{Concrete hole.} For $N=6$, the kernel of
$H^3(\Mtf,U(1))\to H^3(\langle g_6\rangle,U(1))=\ZZ/6$ contains
$\ZZ/12/\ZZ/6=\ZZ/2$, so the restriction is \emph{not} injective;
the $\ZZ/2$-image could be non-zero and must vanish independently.
Wave-11 W1's ``$N\mid 12$ therefore restriction is zero'' argument
is valid modulo $\ZZ/12$ but does not handle the residual $\ZZ/2$
summand beyond the ``Mathieu is perfect, so $M_{24}^{\mathrm{ab}}=0$''
assertion; the $\ZZ/2$-summand of $H^3(\Mtf,U(1))$ is \emph{not}
$M_{24}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ (one is third cohomology, other is first
homology abelianisation); these are different groups.
\textbf{Status: $H^3(\Mtf,U(1))=\ZZ/12\oplus\ZZ/2$ is
\ClaimStatusConjectured{} pending a direct verification
(GAP/MAGMA output is not a published theorem); vanishing on the
programme slice requires independent calculation at each $N$.}
