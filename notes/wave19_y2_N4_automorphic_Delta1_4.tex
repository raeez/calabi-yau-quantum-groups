\section*{Level-four CHL automorphic denominator}
\label{wn:sec:N4-automorphic-denominator}

Let $S$ be a K3 surface, let $g_4\in\mathrm{Aut}_s(S)$ be a symplectic
automorphism of order $4$, and let $t_4\in E[4]$ be a translation of
exact order $4$ on an elliptic curve $E$. The diagonal quotient
\[
  X_4=(S\times E)/\langle(g_4,t_4)\rangle
\]
is smooth and $K$-trivial. The holomorphic $3$-form descends because
$g_4$ fixes $H^{2,0}(S)$ and $t_4$ fixes $dz_E$. Mukai and Nikulin give
the Mathieu class $4B$, frame shape $1^4 2^2 4^4$,
\[
  \#S^{g_4}=4,\qquad
  \#S^{g_4^2}=8.
\]
The Lefschetz equations for the eigenvalue multiplicities
$(a,b,c,c)$ of $g_4$ on $H^2(S,\C)$ at eigenvalues
$(1,-1,i,-i)$ are
\[
  a+b+2c=22,\qquad a-b=2,\qquad a+b-2c=6.
\]
Thus
\[
  (a,b,c)=(8,6,4),\qquad
  \mathrm{rk}\,H^2(S,\Z)^{g_4}=8,\qquad
  \mathrm{rk}\,\widetilde H(S,\Z)^{g_4}=10,\qquad
  \mathrm{rk}\,(H^2(S,\Z)^{g_4})^\perp=14.
\]
The surface quotient $S/\langle g_4\rangle$ has four $A_3$ quotient
points from the $g_4$-fixed locus and two $A_1$ quotient points from
the other $g_4^2$-fixed orbits. These points record the fibre
monodromy; the diagonal translation removes stabilizers from the
threefold $X_4$.

\subsection*{The level-four Borcherds input}

Let $\phi^{(4)}_{0,1}=\phi^{(g_4)}_{0,1}$ be the $4B$-twined K3 weak
Jacobi form of weight $0$ and index $1$,
\[
  \phi^{(4)}_{0,1}(\tau,z)
  =
  \sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\ell\in\Z}
  c^{(4)}(4n-\ell^2,\ell)\,q^n\zeta^\ell .
\]
The primitive CHL Borcherds-input constant is
\[
  c_4(0)=4.
\]
Together with
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))=(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
this is the Gritsenko--Nikulin CHL normalisation. Borcherds' singular
theta lift then gives
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}2,\qquad
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
Hence the primitive level-four denominator has weight
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\PhiFour)=\frac{c_4(0)}2=2.
\]
The coefficient $c_4(0)$ belongs to the primitive CHL input. The
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery row $\DeltaOneFour$ below has its own coefficient
normalisation.

The notation used here is
\[
  \PhiFour=\DeltaFour
  \in M_2(\Gamma_0^+(4),\chi^{\mathrm{CHL}}_4).
\]
This is the primitive CHL denominator, on the CHL orthogonal model
\[
  L^{\mathrm{CHL}}_4=U\oplus U(4)\oplus\langle-2\rangle,
  \qquad \operatorname{sig}(L^{\mathrm{CHL}}_4)=(2,3),
\]
with Jacobi input $\phi^{(4)}_{0,1}$ and
$[q^0\zeta^0]\phi^{(4)}_{0,1}=4$. Its divisor is the
Gritsenko--Nikulin CHL reflective Humbert divisor in the product
chamber, with product exponents $c^{(4)}(4nm-\ell^2,\ell)$.

\subsection*{The symbol $\Delta_{1,4}$}

The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor row with coordinate
$(t,N_{\mathrm{lvl}};k)=(1,4;\tfrac32)$ is
\[
  \DeltaOneFour:=\NablaThreeHalf
  \in M_{3/2}(\Gamma_0^{(2)}(4),\chi_4).
\]
Its orthogonal lattice is
\[
  \mathcal O_{1,4}=U\oplus U(4)\oplus\langle-2\rangle,
\]
and its product-degree lattice is
\[
  M_1=U(4)\oplus\langle2\rangle.
\]
Its divisor is
\[
  \operatorname{div}(\DeltaOneFour)
  =
  \Gamma_0^{(2)}(4)\cdot\Hdiag,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{mult}_{\Hdiag}(\DeltaOneFour)=1.
\]
The Jacobi input in the Cl\'ery--Gritsenko product normalisation is
\[
  2\,\xi_{2,1}^{(4)}\xi_{2,3}^{(4)}
  \in J^w_{0,1}(\Gamma_0(4)).
\]
The product is integral on the $\mu_4$ multiplier cover and has
$\wt(\DeltaOneFour)=3/2$, equivalently
$c^{\mathrm{GC}}_{1,4}(0)=3$. The common orthogonal lattice is ambient
quadratic data. Transporting constants between the GC row and the
primitive CHL ladder demands a theorem identifying host, chamber,
multiplier, and coefficient convention.

\subsection*{Automorphic product}

For
\[
  Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&z\\ z&\sigma\end{pmatrix}\in\Hup,
  \qquad
  (q,\zeta,p)=(e^{2\pi i\tau},e^{2\pi iz},e^{2\pi i\sigma}),
\]
the Borcherds product is
\[
  \PhiFour(Z)
  =
  q^{a_0}\zeta^{b_0}p^{c_0}
  \prod_{\substack{(n,\ell,m)\in\Z^3\\(n,\ell,m)>0}}
  \bigl(1-q^n\zeta^\ell p^m\bigr)^{c^{(4)}(4nm-\ell^2,\ell)} .
\]
The Weyl vector $(a_0,b_0,c_0)$ is determined by the chosen cusp and
the level-four multiplier. Its precise representative is part of the
automorphic-product normalisation; the invariant needed for
$\kBKM$ is the weight $2$ above.

The product is the denominator of a Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra
$\gPhiFour$:
\[
  \PhiFour(Z)
  =
  e^{2\pi i(W_4,Z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}
  \bigl(1-e^{2\pi i(\alpha,Z)}\bigr)^{
    \smult_{\gPhiFour}(\alpha)}.
\]
For $\alpha=n\delta+\ell z+mw$ in the positive chamber,
\[
  \smult_{\gPhiFour}(\alpha)
  =
  c^{(4)}(4nm-\ell^2,\ell).
\]
The displayed identity is target automorphic BKM data. It determines
signed root supermultiplicities. A compact Hall--Drinfeld or
CY-to-chiral realisation is additional data: orientations, a completed
Hall product, parity, a radical quotient, PBW recognition, and a
protected-bracket comparison with these signed exponents. For a
threefold source the native CY-to-chiral output is $E_1$; $E_2$
structure lives after passage to the centre, Drinfeld double, or module
layer.

\subsection*{Primitive denominator and scalar square}

The primitive automorphic object is $\PhiFour$ of weight $2$. The
reduced Donaldson--Thomas scalar comparison fixes the scalar
denominator, after the chamber and variables are chosen, as the square
of the primitive level-four denominator:
\[
  \Phi^{\mathrm{scal}}_4(\tau,\sigma,z)=\PhiFour(\tau,\sigma,z)^2,
  \qquad
  \DTred(X_4)(\tau,\sigma,z)
  =
  -\,\mathcal C^{\mathrm{BO}}_4(\tau,z)\,
  \bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{scal}}_4(\tau,\sigma,z)\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
The prefactor $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{BO}}_4$ is fixed by the
Bryan--Oberdieck scalar normalisation and boundary terms. This identity
is the scalar content of a compatibility theorem placing Bryan--Steinberg
equivariant descent, Oberdieck's reduced $K3\times E$ theory, and the
primitive CHL denominator normalisation in one convention for
orientations, cosections, stability chambers, and completions. Thus the
scalar denominator has weight $4$.
The level-one normalisation split is
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{OP}}=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
These are scalar normalisations of the square. They leave the primitive
BKM characteristic $\kBKM(\PhiFour)=2$ unchanged.

Under the scalar comparison theorem, the scalar denominator contributes
the inverse-square factor
\[
  \PhiFour(Z)^{-2}
  =
  q^{-2a_0}\zeta^{-2b_0}p^{-2c_0}
  \prod_{\substack{(n,\ell,m)\in\Z^3\\(n,\ell,m)>0}}
  \bigl(1-q^n\zeta^\ell p^m\bigr)^{
    -2c^{(4)}(4nm-\ell^2,\ell)}.
\]
Ordinary even and odd BPS multiplicities require the parity and
Hall--Drinfeld recognition data named above.

\paragraph{Scalar compatibility datum.}
Borcherds' weight formula and the Gritsenko--Nikulin CHL tower prove
the automorphic product, its weight, and the BKM denominator identity.
The scalar comparison theorem must supply the common orientations,
cosections, stability chambers, and completions. The fixed automorphic
inputs are
\[
  c_4(0)=4,\qquad \mathrm{wt}(\PhiFour)=2.
\]

\subsection*{Four invariants at level four}

For the smooth CHL threefold $X_4$ and its primitive automorphic
denominator,
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiFour)=\frac{c_4(0)}2=2.
\]
The total-space categorical Euler characteristic is
\[
  \kcat(X_4)
  =
  \chi(\cO_{X_4})
  =
  1-1+1-1
  =
  0,
\]
because $g_4$ fixes $H^{0,0}(S)$ and $H^{0,2}(S)$, while translation
fixes $H^{0,0}(E)$ and $H^{0,1}(E)$.
The compact K-trivial threefold Hodge-supertrace value is
\[
  \kch(\mathcal A_{X_4})=0.
\]
The output attached to this threefold source is $E_1$-chiral. The
associated $E_2$ structures live on the centre, double, or module layer.
The fibre-lattice invariant at level four is the invariant Mukai-lattice
rank
\[
  \kfib^{(4)}
  =
  \mathrm{rk}\,\widetilde H(S,\Z)^{g_4}
  =
  10.
\]
The rank $14$ computed above is the coinvariant rank in $H^2(S,\Z)$.

These four numbers come from four constructions: automorphic weight,
total-space Euler characteristic, compact chiral Hodge supertrace, and
invariant fibre lattice. The parent $K3\times E$ values
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=24
\]
therefore remain separate from the level-four CHL tuple
$(0,0,2,10)$ attached to $(\kcat,\kch,\kBKM,\kfib)$.

\subsection*{Sources}

Borcherds' singular-theta product gives the weight formula
$\mathrm{wt}(\Psi(f))=c_0(0)/2$. Gritsenko--Nikulin's CHL tower gives
$\Delta^{(N)}_{k(N)}$ with
\[
  k(1),k(2),k(3),k(4),k(6)=(5,4,3,2,1),
\]
so $k(4)=2$ and $c_4(0)=4$. Eguchi--Hikami and
Cheng--Duncan--Harvey give the $4B$ twined Jacobi input. Mukai and
Nikulin give the fixed-locus and invariant-lattice data.
Bryan--Steinberg and Oberdieck supply the reduced-DT mechanisms. The
scalar comparison theorem supplies the common orientation, cosection,
chamber, and completion conventions needed to compare them with the
primitive CHL denominator.
