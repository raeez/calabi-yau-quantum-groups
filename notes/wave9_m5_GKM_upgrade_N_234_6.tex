\section{Twined Borcherds GKM algebras at CHL levels
$N \in \{2,3,4,6\}$}
\label{sec:gkm-borcherds-chl-N-2346}

\paragraph{Base algebra at $N=1$.}
At $N=1$, $\fgDel$ is the rank-$3$ Borcherds--Kac--Moody
superalgebra attached to the Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator
$D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)$. Its real simple roots
$\{\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\}$, Gram matrix
\[
  G^{(1)}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2&-2&-2\\
    -2&2&-2\\
    -2&-2&2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det G^{(1)}=-32,
\]
and Weyl vector $\rho=\tfrac12(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)$ satisfy
$(\rho,\delta_i)=-1$ and $(\rho,\rho)=-3/2$. The unnormalised
paramodular form is $\Delta_5=\Grit(\phi_{0,1}^{K3})$ in the
Gritsenko--Nikulin normalisation. The monic Borcherds product is
$D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5$, and
$\operatorname{den}(\fgDel)=D_5(2Z)$. Hence
$\kBKM(\Delta_5)=\operatorname{wt}(\Delta_5)=5=c_1(0)/2$.
This is the Hall--Drinfeld/Borcherds denominator branch; the K3
self-mirror Yangian branch has its own presentation data.

\paragraph{CHL constants and the diagonal-divisor catalogue.}
For the CHL denominator ladder at
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, the Jacobi constants are
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\operatorname{wt}(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
These are CHL constants. They are independent from the extended
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue, whose twice-weight tuple is
$(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)$ and which overlaps the CHL ladder only at
$N=1$ through $\Delta_5$.

For the compact host $K3\times E$, the invariant values are
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}=24.
\]
The five CHL weights above are Borcherds weights read from the chosen
automorphic input. Compact Hodge supertraces and fibre Euler
characteristics play no role in their construction.

\paragraph{Reflection divisor.}
Let $g_N$ be a symplectic K3 automorphism of order
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ and let
\[
  \Phi_N=\Bor(\phi^{(g_N)}_{0,1})
\]
be the Borcherds product of the $g_N$-twined weak Jacobi input on the
$g_N$-fixed signature-$(2,1)$ lattice. Gritsenko--Nikulin give the
Type-II square-$2$ reflection divisor at $N=1$. The
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery simplest-divisor theorem and the
Cl\'ery--Gritsenko--Hulek $6D_4$ construction give the corresponding
simple Humbert divisors for the CHL forms at $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$.
Therefore each real wall reflection acts through the denominator
character of $\Phi_N$.

\paragraph{Rank-three Gram data.}
Fix the following CHL rank-three symmetric chamber Gram matrices:
\[
  G^{(2)} =
  \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -2 & 4 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G^{(3)} =
  \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
  G^{(4)} =
  \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 4 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G^{(6)} =
  \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -2 \\ -1 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 6 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Together with $G^{(1)}$ above,
\[
  \det G^{(N)}=(-32,-24,-18,-12,-6)
  \quad\text{for}\quad N=(1,2,3,4,6).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \det G^{(N)}=
  \begin{cases}
    -3c_N(0)-2, & N=1,\\
    -3c_N(0), & N\in\{2,3,4,6\}.
  \end{cases}
\]
The associated rational Cartan matrix is
$A^{(N)}_{ij}=2G^{(N)}_{ij}/G^{(N)}_{jj}$. The matrices above establish
the symmetric chamber datum. An integral Borcherds generalised-Cartan
realisation at $N\geq2$ also requires the fixed-lattice divisibility and
parity data.

\paragraph{Weyl vectors.}
The chamber Weyl vector is determined in the Gram basis by
\[
  (\rho^{(N)},\delta_i^{(N)})=-\frac12G^{(N)}_{ii}.
\]
Solving $G^{(N)}c=-\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}G^{(N)}$ gives
\[
  \rho^{(2)} =
  \tfrac52\delta_1^{(2)}+\tfrac32\delta_2^{(2)}
  +\tfrac32\delta_3^{(2)},
  \qquad
  \rho^{(3)} =
  \tfrac23\delta_1^{(3)}+\tfrac23\delta_2^{(3)}
  +\tfrac56\delta_3^{(3)},
\]
\[
  \rho^{(4)} =
  2\delta_1^{(4)}+2\delta_2^{(4)}+\tfrac32\delta_3^{(4)},
  \qquad
  \rho^{(6)} =
  6\delta_1^{(6)}+6\delta_2^{(6)}+\tfrac72\delta_3^{(6)}.
\]
The squared norms at $N=(1,2,3,4,6)$ are
\[
  (\rho^{(N)},\rho^{(N)})
  =
  \left(-\tfrac32,-\tfrac{17}{2},-\tfrac{13}{6},-7,-\tfrac{45}{2}\right).
\]

\paragraph{Signed denominator exponents.}
Write the Borcherds product in chamber coordinates as
\[
  \Phi_N(Z)=e^{-2\pi i(\rho^{(N)},Z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+^{(N)}}
  \left(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,Z)}\right)^{e_N(\alpha)}.
\]
The exponent $e_N(\alpha)$ is the signed denominator exponent, equivalently
the root-space superdimension
$\dim \fg^{(N)}_{\alpha,\bar0}-\dim \fg^{(N)}_{\alpha,\bar1}$ after a
parity splitting is fixed. It becomes an ordinary root multiplicity
after the even and odd root spaces have been separated. Thus the
low-height table records signed exponents:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
N & e_N(0,1) & e_N(1,1) & e_N(1,0) & e_N(1,-1) \\
\hline
2 & 8 & 8 & -22 & 8 \\
3 & 6 & 6 & -18 & 6 \\
4 & 4 & 4 & -12 & 4 \\
6 & 2 & 2 & -6 & 2
\end{array}
\]
The $N=2$ value $e_2(1,0)=-22$ uses the Eguchi--Hikami
massive-sector normalisation. A Lie-superalgebra reconstruction from the
signed table additionally requires a parity splitting and bracket
relations.

\paragraph{Null-primitive denominator exponent.}
Let $a_0^{(N)}$ be a primitive isotropic ray in the CHL chamber. The
denominator-product exponent on this ray is
\[
  \tau_{\Phi_N}(a_0)=c_N(0)-1=(9,7,5,3,1)
  \quad\text{for}\quad N=(1,2,3,4,6).
\]
At $N=1$ this is the Gritsenko--Nikulin product identity
$1-\sum_{t\geq1}m(ta_0)q^t=\prod_{t\geq1}(1-q^t)^9$. The displayed
CHL tuple is the null-primitive exponent for the denominator product;
it is distinct from the total exponent of a Mason eta-product and from
the Jacobi constant $c_N(0)$.

\paragraph{Fixed-subalgebra tests.}
A fixed-subalgebra realisation of
\[
  \fgN\longrightarrow \fgDel
\]
requires two tests. On the Cartan side, the $g_N$-fixed sublattice must
identify with the chamber Gram data above after the displayed
rescaling. On the denominator side, the signed exponent $e_N(\alpha)$
must equal the $g_N$-trace on the corresponding $\fgDel$ root space.
At $N=2$ and $\alpha=(1,1,1)$ the first-order trace test gives
$8=\operatorname{tr}_{g_2}\vert_{\fgDel_\alpha}$ inside the
$64$-dimensional $M_{24}$ coefficient. These tests verify first-order
compatibility; higher Fourier orders require a separate comparison.

\paragraph{$N=6$ geometric anchor.}
At $N=6$ the CHL/umbral Niemeier anchor is the global rank-$24$
positive-definite even unimodular lattice with root system $6D_4$ and
umbral group $3.\mathrm{Sym}_6$. The cyclic quotient surface
$K3/\langle g_6\rangle$ has local singularity type
\[
  2A_5+2A_2+2A_1 .
\]
The Niemeier root system and the quotient singularity type are distinct
objects; the former is global lattice data, the latter local analytic
surface data.
