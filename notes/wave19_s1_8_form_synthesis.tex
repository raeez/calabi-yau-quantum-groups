\section*{Borcherds weights, CHL rows, and diagonal divisors}
\label{sec:borcherds-weight-catalogues}

The Borcherds weight is a rowwise automorphic invariant.  For a fixed
Borcherds product $\Phi_R$ with fixed cusp normalisation and input
coefficient $c_R(0)$, Borcherds' singular-theta theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_R)=\mathrm{wt}(\Phi_R)=\frac{c_R(0)}{2}.
\]
The row label $R$ is part of the datum.  The primitive CHL ladder, the
three remaining Nikulin symplectic orders, and the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor classification are three different row systems sharing
only the common entry $\Delta_5$.

\subsection*{Primitive CHL ladder}

For the primitive compact CHL orders the row label is the order
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ of a symplectic K3 automorphism compatible with an
elliptic-curve automorphism in the diagonal quotient.  Gritsenko--Nikulin
and Gritsenko's CHL paramodular construction give the following constants.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \text{Borcherds form} & c_N(0) & \kBKM & \text{proof scope}\\ \hline
1 & \Delta_5=\Phi_1 & 10 & 5 &
  \text{proved automorphic; primitive reduced scalar theorem}\\
2 & \Delfnm{4}{2} & 8 & 4 &
  \text{proved automorphic; compact BPS bracket theorem required}\\
3 & \Delfnm{3}{3} & 6 & 3 &
  \text{proved automorphic; compact BPS bracket theorem required}\\
4 & \Delfnm{2}{4} & 4 & 2 &
  \text{proved automorphic; compact BPS bracket theorem required}\\
6 & \Delfnm{1}{6} & 2 & 1 &
  \text{proved automorphic; compact BPS bracket theorem required}
\end{array}
\]
Thus the CHL weight sequence is
\[
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
  \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6)\bigr)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The $N=1$ row also fixes the Igusa normalisation:
\[
  [q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}]\Delta_5=64,\qquad
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2.
\]
In Oberdieck--Pandharipande's reduced-DT scalar convention
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2$ and
$Z_{\mathrm{OP}}^{K3\times E}=-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}$.  This is an inverse-square scalar convention.
It is separate from the primitive Borcherds denominator $\Delta_5$ of
weight $5$.

\subsection*{Nikulin symplectic orders beyond the CHL quotient}

The remaining Nikulin admissible symplectic orders have Borcherds weights
from the same rowwise formula and a distinct geometric scope.  They sit
outside the primitive compact CHL ladder unless a theorem supplies a
common compact host and a row map.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c|p{7.1cm}}
N & frame shape & $c_N(0)$ & $\kBKM$ & geometric scope\\ \hline
5 & $1^4 5^4$ & $4$ & $2$ &
  Borcea--Voisin CY$_3$ geometry; functor-level source package required\\
7 & $1^3 7^3$ & $2$ & $1$ &
  singular $(K3\times E)/\Z_7$ quotient with gerbe data; smooth source package required\\
8 & $1^2 2^1 4^1 8^2$ & $2$ & $1$ &
  Kummer-type hyperk\"ahler fourfold geometry; CY$_3$ interpretation requires a theorem
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
As a Nikulin automorphic order list, separate from the compact CHL ladder,
the eight orders give
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,4,2,2,2),\qquad
  \kBKM=(5,4,3,2,2,1,1,1)
\]
for $N=(1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8)$.

\subsection*{Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue}

Gritsenko--Cl\'ery classify dd-modular forms whose divisor is the diagonal
$\mathcal H_1$ with vanishing order $1$ on a paramodular quotient.  Their
classification first gives nine numerical candidates and eliminates the
candidate $(2,4;\tfrac12)$.  The eight surviving rows are indexed by
triples $(t,N;k)$, with $k$ the weight.
\[
\mathsf{GC}_8=
\{(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
(4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1)\}.
\]
For this catalogue the displayed constant $c_R(0)$ is the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery cusp-weighted zeroth term whose half is the weight of
the row $R$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c}
R & (t,N;k) & F_R & \text{modular space} & c_R(0) & \kBKM(F_R)\\ \hline
1 & (1,1;5) & \Delta_5 & M_5(\Gamma_1,\chi_2) & 10 & 5\\
2 & (2,1;2) & \Delta_2 & M_2(\Gamma_2,\chi_4) & 4 & 2\\
3 & (1,2;3) & \nabla_3 & M_3(\Gamma_0^{(2)}(2),\chi_2) & 6 & 3\\
4 & (3,1;1) & \Delta_1 & M_1(\Gamma_3,\chi_6) & 2 & 1\\
5 & (1,3;2) & \nabla_2 & M_2(\Gamma_0^{(2)}(3),\chi_2) & 4 & 2\\
6 & (4,1;\tfrac12) & \Delta_{1/2} & M_{1/2}(\Gamma_4,\chi_8) & 1 & \tfrac12\\
7 & (1,4;\tfrac32) & \nabla_{3/2} &
  M_{3/2}(\Gamma_0^{(2)}(4),\chi_4) & 3 & \tfrac32\\
8 & (2,2;1) & Q_1 & M_1(\Gamma_2(2),\chi_4) & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
The GC weight sequence is
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
and its twice-weight sequence is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The entries of weight $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac32$ use multiplier systems of
orders $8$ and $4$.  The displayed eight values are the complete GC
weight list.  The GC row squares in Corollary~2.3 are row-specific
paramodular identities; the primitive CHL constants $(10,8,6,4,2)$
retain their own row system.

\subsection*{Invariant separation}

The $K3\times E$ constants come from separate constructions:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=5,\qquad
  \kfib=24.
\]
The value $5$ is the Borcherds weight of the $\Delta_5$ denominator.
The value $3$ is a Heisenberg--Mukai chiral specialisation.  The value
$24$ is the Mukai-lattice rank of the K3 fibre.  The two displayed
zeros are the total-space categorical trace and the compact chiral
Hodge supertrace.  Every table above reads $\kBKM$ directly from the
relevant Borcherds product, independently of the other $K3\times E$
scalar invariants.  These constants belong to distinct constructions.

\subsection*{Native chiral and Hall boundaries}

For a Calabi--Yau input of dimension $d\geq3$, the native output of
$\Phi$ is an $\Eone$-chiral algebra.  An $\Etwo$ structure enters only
after applying a centre, Drinfeld double, or module-category layer.  Thus
the Hall positive half, the doubled Hall algebra, and the BKM denominator
belong to different levels of the construction.

For the local affine model, Schiffmann--Vasserot identify the critical
cohomological Hall algebra of $\mathbb C^3$ with the positive half
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)=\Yplus(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1).
\]
The full $\Woneinfty$ object appears only after double, centre,
completion, and evaluation data have been supplied.  This positive-half
statement supplies the local Hall input; a full vertex algebra requires
the later structures.

On the $K3\times E$ branch, the Hall--Drinfeld/BKM object is the
Drinfeld double or denominator-recognition branch attached to the Hall
positive half and the Borcherds product.  The Mukai Yangian branch is a
separate self-mirror K3 construction.  The two branches meet through
comparison theorems, with their names kept separate.

\subsection*{Recognition boundaries}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Automorphic weights.}  The CHL constants
$(10,8,6,4,2)$ and the Nikulin extensions $(4,2,2)$ are proved
automorphic data by Borcherds' 1998 singular-theta theorem together
with Gritsenko--Nikulin's denominator construction and Gritsenko's
paramodular CHL products.  The GC eight-row weights are proved by
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery's 2008/2011 classification and their Borcherds-product
construction.
\item \emph{CHL BPS realisation.}  At $N=1$ the
Oberdieck--Pandharipande scalar convention and the Oberdieck--Pixton
primitive theorem give the reduced scalar branch.  At
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$, a full BPS$\cong$\BKM statement requires a compact
reduced-DT theorem beyond the stated primitive scope, a Hall
bracket-preserving map to the Borcherds denominator algebra, and an
orientation/Pfaffian comparison.
\item \emph{Non-CHL source packages.}  At $N=5$ the Borcea--Voisin
geometry supplies the active CY$_3$ test object.  At $N=7$ the singular
quotient requires gerbe and McKay correction data.  At $N=8$ the
hyperk\"ahler fourfold supplies automorphic evidence.  Each row still
requires a CY-to-chiral source package before it can be read as a
compact CHL-type BPS theorem.
\item \emph{GC row-to-BKM attachments.}  The GC theorem proves
automorphy and the diagonal divisor.  A denominator algebra or compact
CY source attached to a GC row is an extra theorem.  The two
half-integer rows require source data carrying the relevant multiplier
system.
\item \emph{Igusa consistency.}  The normalisations
$D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5$, $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2$,
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2$, and
$Z_{\mathrm{OP}}=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}$ are scalar normalisations.  They
leave the rowwise Borcherds weights unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
