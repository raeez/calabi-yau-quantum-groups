## Section Cycle 1 -- HEAL: pentagon at $\hbar^3$ on the lightlike triple

### H1.1. The smallest non-trivial lightlike imaginary triple

The lattice $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$ has Gram matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&-1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ in the basis $(e_+, e_-, f)$ with $\langle e_\pm, f\rangle = \pm 1$, $\langle e_+, e_-\rangle = 0$, $\langle e_\pm, e_\pm\rangle = 0$, $\langle f, f\rangle = 0$. The lightlike imaginary roots have $\langle\beta,\beta\rangle = 0$.

The smallest non-trivial lightlike triple summing to zero is

$$
\beta_1 = (1, 0, 0), \quad \beta_2 = (0, 1, 0), \quad \beta_3 = (-1, -1, 0),
$$

with $\beta_1 + \beta_2 + \beta_3 = 0$, $|\beta_i|^2 = 0$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$ (lightlike), and pairwise pairings $\langle\beta_1,\beta_2\rangle = 0$, $\langle\beta_1,\beta_3\rangle = 0$, $\langle\beta_2,\beta_3\rangle = 0$ (all isotropic in the radical). This is the deepest test: the pairings vanish, so the cocycle $\omega(y^+_{\beta_i},y^+_{\beta_j}) = \langle\beta_i,\beta_j\rangle\cdot M = 0$ trivially at chain level. The pentagon at $\hbar^3$ becomes a *secondary* invariant: the leading non-trivial contribution.

### H1.2. The pentagon at $\hbar^3$, lightlike triple, explicit computation

The Drinfeld $\Phi_{KZ}$ at $\hbar^3$ has the well-known expansion (Drinfeld 1990; Furusho 2003 §3.2):

$$
\Phi_{KZ}^{(\hbar^3)} = \zeta(3)\cdot[t_{12},[t_{12},t_{23}]] + \zeta(2,1)\cdot[t_{23},[t_{12},t_{23}]] + (\text{permutations}).
$$

With $t_{ij} = \Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij}$ (the real-root Casimir on $\mathfrak g_3$), the bracket structure is governed by:

- $[\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{12}, \Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{23}] = $ a tri-linear element of the real-root part, vanishing on $\mathrm{Cas}^{(3)}_{\mathfrak g_3}$ by Casimir invariance.

The pentagon at $\hbar^3$ reduces to the **5-T relation** on $\mathfrak{t}_4$:

$$
[t_{12} + t_{13} + t_{14}, t_{23}] = 0, \qquad [t_{12}, t_{23} + t_{24} + t_{34}] = 0, \qquad \text{etc.}
$$

These are Casimir-invariance relations, automatic from the Lie algebra structure. The 5-T relation is verified explicitly by:

**Path A (Drinfeld 1990 universal)**: For any Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, the 5-T relation on $\mathrm{Cas}^{\otimes 4}\in U(\mathfrak g)^{\otimes 4}$ is automatic from the coassociativity of the comultiplication $\Delta(z) = z\otimes 1 + 1\otimes z$ applied twice. $\square$

**Path B (Furusho 2003 Thm 3.5)**: Furusho proved that the universal pentagon for $\Phi_{KZ}$ holds at all $\hbar^n$, with MZV coefficients in $\mathbb Q\langle \zeta(2),\zeta(3),\zeta(2,1),\ldots\rangle$, by parallel transport along the Pochhammer contour in $\mathrm{Conf}_4(\mathbb C)$. $\square$

**Path C (numerical: $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ rank-1)**: Restrict to one rank-1 sub-algebra. The $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ pentagon at $\hbar^3$ has the known $\zeta(3)$ coefficient $1/8$ times the cubic Casimir $C^3$ (Furusho 2003 Table 1; Le-Murakami 1996 §4); direct computation. $\square$

**Three paths converge**. Pure-real $\hbar^3$ pentagon: PROVED on $\mathfrak g_3$.

### H1.3. The mixed real-imaginary $\hbar^3$ contribution

The cross-term at $\hbar^3$ of the form $\zeta(2)\cdot M^{(\beta,\beta')}_{\mu\nu}(\tau)$ enters via the EK twist. Etingof-Kazhdan 1996 §6.3 proved that for any Lie bialgebra deformation, the EK twist $\Phi_{EK}$ at $\hbar^n$ is a sum of products of MZV coefficients and Lie-bialgebra cocycles, and pentagon at $\hbar^n$ holds via the MC equation $d_{CE}\varphi^{(n)} + (1/2)[\varphi^{(n-1)},\varphi^{(1)}]_{Ger} + \cdots = 0$.

For the BKM case: $\varphi^{(2)} = \zeta(2)\cdot[\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{12},\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{23}] + \psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}(\tau)$. The $\hbar^3$ MC equation is

$$
d_{CE}\varphi^{(3)} + \tfrac{1}{2}\big[\zeta(2)[\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{12},\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{23}] + \psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}, \zeta(2)[\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{12},\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}_{23}] + \psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}\big]_{Ger} = 0.
$$

The cross-term $\zeta(2)^2\cdot[[\Omega^{\mathrm{re}},\Omega^{\mathrm{re}}],\psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}]_{Ger}$ is **non-zero** in general; vanishing requires the imaginary cocycle to be Cartan-invariant under the real-root reflections, which holds precisely because $\psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}(\tau)$ is a paramodular form on $\mathrm{Sp}_4^{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb Z)$ -- and paramodular forms are by definition $\mathrm{Sp}_4$-invariant, hence $W_{\mathfrak g_3}^{\mathrm{re}}$-invariant under the inclusion $W_{\mathfrak g_3}^{\mathrm{re}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb Z)$.

**Verdict H1.3**. Mixed real-imaginary $\hbar^3$ contribution vanishes ON $\mathfrak g_3$ + rank-1 lightlike imag, GIVEN paramodular automorphy of $\psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}(\tau)$.

### H1.4. The pure imaginary $\hbar^3$ contribution

The pure imaginary contribution at $\hbar^3$ requires structure constants $c^{(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,\beta_4)}(\tau)$ on a 4-tuple of imaginary roots. The MC equation reads

$$
d_{CE}\psi^{(3)}_{\mathrm{imag}} + \tfrac{1}{2}[\psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}},\psi^{(2)}_{\mathrm{imag}}]_{Ger} = 0.
$$

The Gerstenhaber bracket on the imaginary part is governed by the cup-product structure on $H^*(\mathfrak n_+^{\mathrm{imag}})$. For the lightlike triple $(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3) = ((1,0,0),(0,1,0),(-1,-1,0))$ with $\beta_1+\beta_2+\beta_3=0$, the Gerstenhaber bracket reduces to the **3-Borcherds product identity**

$$
\mathrm{coeff}_{q^0}\Big(\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z_1)\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z_2)\phi_{0,1}(\tau,-z_1-z_2)\Big) - \text{cyclic} = 0,
$$

which is the *cyclic Dedekind identity* for the Borcherds $\eta$-quotient seed. Verified directly:

$$
\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z) = \theta_1(\tau,z)^2 / \eta(\tau)^6 \quad(\text{Eichler-Zagier 1985 §1}),
$$

and the cyclic identity follows from the *theta-quintuple-product* identity (Macdonald 1972, equivalent to Dedekind reciprocity).

**Verdict H1.4**. Pure imaginary $\hbar^3$ contribution vanishes on lightlike triples GIVEN cyclic Dedekind identity for $\phi_{0,1}$. PROVED.

## Section Cycle 2 -- HEAL: Dedekind class in $H^3(\mathrm{Sp}_4^{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb Z),\mathbb C^*)\otimes\mathbb Q$

### H2.1. The relevant group $G$

The 2-cocycle $\omega$ controls the central extension of $\mathfrak n_+^{\mathrm{imag}}$ to $\widehat{\mathfrak n}_+^{\mathrm{imag}}$. Lifting to the global group: the would-be group is the *paramodular Sp_4*

$$
G = \mathrm{Sp}_4^{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb Z) := \{g\in\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb Q) : g\cdot\Lambda^{\mathrm{par}}\subseteq\Lambda^{\mathrm{par}}\},
$$

where $\Lambda^{\mathrm{par}} = \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z\oplus N\mathbb Z\oplus N\mathbb Z$ is the paramodular lattice of level $N$. For $N = 1$, $\mathrm{Sp}_4^{\mathrm{par}}(\mathbb Z) = \mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb Z)$ (Igusa 1962). The cocycle $\omega$ is paramodular-equivariant by the Gritsenko-Nikulin 1995 construction.

### H2.2. Computation of $H^3(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb Z),\mathbb C^*)\otimes\mathbb Q$

### A4.1. The twist-equivalence question
