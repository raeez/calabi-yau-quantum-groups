The phrase ``the positive half of the affine Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$ passes to the
chiral Yangian'' hides at least six distinct mathematical objects and half a dozen
identification theorems of varying completeness.  This section disentangles them.

\subsection{Setup: equivariant parameters and the CY$_3$ constraint}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-setup}

Let $\mathbb{F} := \mathbb{C}(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$ and $\epsilon_3 := -\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_2$.
The relation $\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 = 0$ is the \emph{Calabi--Yau three-fold condition}:
it is the weight-space relation for the anti-canonical subtorus
$T^2 = \{t \in T^3 : t_1 t_2 t_3 = 1\}$ acting on $\mathbb{C}^3$.

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Shuffle kernel.} Set
\[
\omega(z, w) := \frac{(z - w - \epsilon_1)(z - w - \epsilon_2)(z - w - \epsilon_3)}{(z - w)^3}.
\]

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Shuffle algebra.}
Let $\Lambda_n := \mathbb{F}[z_1, \ldots, z_n]^{S_n}$ and $\Sh := \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \Lambda_n$.
Define the product $\star: \Lambda_m \otimes \Lambda_n \to \Lambda_{m+n}$ by
\[
(F \star G)(z_1, \ldots, z_{m+n}) := \frac{1}{m! n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_{m+n}}
\sigma \cdot \left( F(z_1, \ldots, z_m) G(z_{m+1}, \ldots, z_{m+n}) \prod_{1 \leq i \leq m < j \leq m+n} \omega(z_j, z_i) \right).
\]
Then $(\Sh, \star)$ is an associative unital $\mathbb{F}$-algebra.
Associativity is a direct calculation using the symmetry of $\omega$; it is verifiable
from the definition alone, no external input.

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Positive half.}
$\Yplus := \Sh$.

The name ``positive half of $Y(\gghone)$'' is retrospective: it reflects the later
identification with the upper triangular part of the Yangian in its Drinfeld-currents
presentation. That identification is a theorem, not a definition.

\emph{Remark on conventions.}
Multiple shuffle kernels appear in the literature, differing by sign conventions,
rescaling of $\epsilon_i$, and whether the CY$_3$ relation is imposed.
The form above matches Negut 2014 \texttt{arXiv:1405.1989}.
Feigin--Hashizume--Hoshino--Shiraishi--Yanagida 2009 use a different normalisation
equivalent after rescaling variables.

\subsection{Enlargement to $Y(\gghone)$ via Drinfeld double}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-drinfeld-double}

The Drinfeld double construction
(Drinfeld 1987 ICM; Kassel 1995 \emph{Quantum Groups} §IX.4)
takes a bialgebra $A_+$ together with a second bialgebra $A_-$ and a non-degenerate
Hopf pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle: A_+ \otimes A_- \to k$,
and produces a Hopf algebra $D(A_+, A_-)$ with underlying vector space $A_+ \otimes A_-$
and multiplication \ClaimStatusDerivation\ determined by Sweedler formula
\[
(a_+ \otimes a_-)(b_+ \otimes b_-) = \sum \langle a_-^{(1)}, b_+^{(1)} \rangle
\langle S(a_-^{(3)}), b_+^{(3)} \rangle \cdot a_+ b_+^{(2)} \otimes a_-^{(2)} b_-.
\]
This is derivable from the Hopf pairing axioms.

To apply this to $\Yplus$, one needs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A coproduct $\Delta_+$ on $\Sh$ making it a bialgebra.
\item A second bialgebra $Y^- \cong \Sh^{\mathrm{op}}$ with its own coproduct.
\item A non-degenerate Hopf pairing $\langle, \rangle: \Yplus \otimes Y^- \to \mathbb{F}$.
\end{enumerate}

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Negut 2014 Thm 3.4; Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013 \texttt{arXiv:1202.2756}).
The shuffle algebra $\Sh$ admits a topological coproduct, a non-degenerate Hopf pairing
with its opposite, and the resulting Drinfeld double has a canonical central extension
$Y^0 \cong \mathbb{F}[\![\psi_0, \psi_1, \ldots]\!]$ (a topological polynomial algebra in
Cartan-current modes).

The triangular decomposition
\[
Y(\gghone) = \Yplus \otimes Y^0 \otimes Y^-
\]
as left $\Yplus$-, right $Y^-$-, and middle $Y^0$-module.

\emph{Precision note.} The pairing is not defined on $\Sh$ with rational coefficients
without completion. Taking a specific $\widehat{\Sh}$ completion — analogous to formal
Laurent series — yields a non-degenerate pairing on the completion. Different completions
give different Hopf algebras: \emph{the} affine Yangian of $\mathfrak{gl}_1$ is an
isomorphism class of topological Hopf algebras, not a specific algebra over $\mathbb{F}$.

\subsection{Loop presentation via Drinfeld currents}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-loop-presentation}

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Generating currents.} For the Drinfeld-currents presentation
of $Y(\gghone)$, introduce $e(z), f(z), \psi(z)$ as formal power series in $z^{-1}$ with
coefficients in an abstract algebra on generators $\{e_k, f_k, h_k : k \geq 0\}$,
\[
e(z) = \sum_{k \geq 0} e_k z^{-k-1}, \qquad f(z) = \sum_{k \geq 0} f_k z^{-k-1},
\qquad \psi(z) = \exp\!\Big( \sum_{k \geq 0} h_k z^{-k-1} \Big).
\]

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Structure function.} Set
\[
\varphi(u) := \frac{(u + \epsilon_1)(u + \epsilon_2)(u + \epsilon_3)}{u^3}.
\]

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Relations.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item $[\psi(z), \psi(w)] = 0$ \quad (Cartan commutativity);
\item $\psi(z) e(w) \psi(z)^{-1} = \varphi(z - w) e(w)$
  \quad and \quad $\psi(z) f(w) \psi(z)^{-1} = \varphi(z - w)^{-1} f(w)$
  \quad (Cartan--root);
\item $(z - w - \epsilon_1)(z - w - \epsilon_2)(z - w - \epsilon_3)\, e(z) e(w)
= -(w - z - \epsilon_1)(w - z - \epsilon_2)(w - z - \epsilon_3)\, e(w) e(z)$
  \quad (and symmetrically for $f$);
\item $[e(z), f(w)] = \dfrac{\psi(z) - \psi(w)}{z - w}$ \quad (root--root / Cartan emergence);
\item Cubic Serre relations
  $\mathrm{Sym}_{z_1, z_2, z_3} (z_1 - 2 z_2 + z_3) [e(z_1), [e(z_2), e(z_3)]] = 0$,
  and similarly for $f$.
\end{enumerate}

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Tsymbaliuk 2017 \texttt{arXiv:1703.04551} Thm~1.1).
The $\mathbb{F}$-algebra presented by generators $\{e_k, f_k, h_k\}$ and
relations (R1)--(R5) is isomorphic (as a topological Hopf algebra after appropriate
completion) to the Drinfeld double $\Yplus \bowtie Y^0 \bowtie Y^-$ of §\ref{wn:subsec:yplus-drinfeld-double}.

\emph{Epistemic note.} The theorem asserts an isomorphism of topological Hopf algebras.
A reader verifying the theorem must track precisely which completions are used on each side;
see Tsymbaliuk 2017 §4 for the matching.

\subsection{Vacuum module versus vertex algebra: the central distinction}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-vacuum-vs-vertex}

\emph{The crucial conflation to avoid.}
A module of $Y(\gghone)$ is not the same as a vertex algebra.

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Vacuum module.}
Fix the trivial character $\chi_0: Y^0 \to \mathbb{C}$, $\psi_k \mapsto 0$.
The Verma module
$M(\chi_0) := Y(\gghone) \otimes_{Y^0 \otimes Y^-} \mathbb{C}_{\chi_0}$
is the vacuum Verma; its unique maximal submodule is generated by the Serre relations
and the quotient is the vacuum irreducible module $L(\chi_0)$.

\emph{This is a $Y(\gghone)$-module, not a vertex algebra.}

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Vertex algebra (Borcherds 1986).}
A vertex algebra is a vector space $V$ equipped with
(i) a vacuum vector $|0\rangle \in V$,
(ii) a state-to-field correspondence $Y(\cdot, z): V \to \mathrm{End}(V)[[z, z^{-1}]]$,
(iii) a translation operator $T: V \to V$,
subject to vacuum, translation, and locality axioms.

\emph{The Yangian $Y(\gghone)$ is not automatically a vertex algebra.}
To endow a representation $M$ of $Y(\gghone)$ with a vertex-algebra structure one must
specify the state-to-field map $Y(v, z)$ for each state $v$, verify that the products
$v_1 \cdot v_2(z)$ extend to local fields, and check the locality axiom.
None of this is a tautology.

\subsection{Geometric action on Hilbert schemes}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-MO-action}

Let $V := \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} H^*_T(\Hilb^n(\mathbb{C}^2), \mathbb{F})$ where $T$ acts
with weights $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$.

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Schiffmann--Vasserot 2012 Pub IHES 118 \S5;
Maulik--Okounkov 2012 \texttt{arXiv:1211.1287} §14).
There is an action $\rho: Y(\gghone) \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)$ with explicit
correspondences on $\Hilb^n \times \Hilb^{n+1}$.

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Nakajima 1997 \emph{Lectures on Hilbert schemes} §9;
Grojnowski 1996 Math Res Lett 3).
Independent of the Yangian action, $V$ carries a Heisenberg vertex algebra structure:
the generating fields are $\alpha_{-n}$ for $n \geq 1$ (raising operators) and
$\alpha_n$ for $n \geq 1$ (lowering), commuting as $[\alpha_m, \alpha_n] = m \delta_{m, -n}$.
This makes $V$ a free boson Fock space and a vertex algebra.

\ClaimStatusDerivation\ \emph{Logical consequence.}
$V$ is simultaneously a $Y(\gghone)$-module (via $\rho$) and a Heisenberg vertex algebra
(via Nakajima). The Yangian action is by \emph{vertex-algebra endomorphisms} of $V$;
this is the content of the compatibility between Yangian and Heisenberg actions.
$V$ itself is the Heisenberg VOA; the Yangian is an algebra of $V$-endomorphisms.

\subsection{The holomorphic-twist gauge-theory construction}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-costello}

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Costello 2013 \texttt{arXiv:1303.2632} for the 4D $\mathcal{N} = 1$
holomorphic-twist construction; Costello--Gwilliam 2017 Vol II §5 for the
factorisation-algebra packaging; the $\Omega$-deformed 4D $\mathcal{N} = 2$ upgrade is
made precise in Costello--Gaiotto 2018 \texttt{arXiv:1810.01970} /
Costello--Paquette 2020 \texttt{arXiv:2009.04834}.)
The twisted observable complex of 4D $\mathcal{N} = 2$ $U(1)$ pure gauge theory on
$\mathbb{C}^2_{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2}$ (i.e.~with $\Omega$-deformation by
$\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$), taken in the holomorphic-topological twist, produces a
factorisation algebra $\mathcal{F}^{U(1), \Omega}_{4D}$ on the ``spectral line''
$\mathbb{C}_z$.

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Costello 2013 §11).
A quantum deformation parameter $\hbar$ with $\epsilon_3 = -\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_2 + \hbar$
produces an action of $Y(\gghone)$ on the factorisation algebra, realising the Yangian as
a deformation of the classical observables.

\ClaimStatusDerivation\ \emph{Factorisation-algebra--vertex-algebra passage.}
A factorisation algebra on $\mathbb{R}^2 \cong \mathbb{C}$ satisfying holomorphic locality
(OPE singularities are poles in $z - w$) is equivalent to a vertex algebra
(Costello--Gwilliam 2017 Vol I Thm 5.3.3). Therefore
$\mathcal{F}^{U(1), \Omega}_{4D}$ is a vertex algebra.

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{Chiral Yangian, Costello sense.}
$\ChirY^{\mathrm{Cost}} := $ the vertex algebra arising from
$\mathcal{F}^{U(1), \Omega}_{4D}$ under the factorisation-algebra--vertex-algebra
equivalence.

\subsection{Factorisation homology: the Kapranov--Vasserot route}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-KV}

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ \emph{CoHA as factorisation algebra.}
Kapranov--Vasserot 2019 \texttt{arXiv:1901.07641} construct $\CoHA(\mathbb{C}^3)$
as an $E_2$-algebra (associative + compatible coalgebra) over $\mathbb{F}$ and promote
it to a factorisation algebra on $\mathbb{R}^2$.

\ClaimStatusConjectured\ (Kapranov--Vasserot).
$\int_{\mathbb{C}} \CoHA(\mathbb{C}^3) \cong \ChirY^{\mathrm{KV}}$ as factorisation
algebras on $\mathbb{C}_z$.

\ClaimStatusConjectured\ (Costello--Paquette 2020 \texttt{arXiv:2009.04834} framework).
$\ChirY^{\mathrm{Cost}} \cong \ChirY^{\mathrm{KV}}$.
Partial results exist; the general statement is a conjectural identification
of two factorisation algebras constructed by different means.

\subsection{Gaiotto--Rapčák $Y$-algebras}
\label{wn:subsec:yplus-GR}

\ClaimStatusDefinition\ (Gaiotto--Rapčák 2017 \texttt{arXiv:1703.00982}).
For each triple $(L, M, N)$ of non-negative integers, a vertex algebra
$Y_{L, M, N}^{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3}$ is defined as the algebra of
local observables of 4D $\mathcal{N} = 2$ gauge theory along a Y-shaped divisor in
$\mathbb{C}^3$ with prescribed ramification data $(L, M, N)$.

\ClaimStatusConjectured\ $Y_{0, 0, 0}^{\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3}
\cong \ChirY^{\mathrm{Cost}}$ at the central charge determined by
$(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$.

\emph{Status.} Widely believed, consistent with computations of OPE coefficients
at low order; a full identification theorem for general $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$
is not (to my knowledge) a single-citation result.

\subsection{Application to the Vol III programme}

The Vol III programme concerns the compact Calabi--Yau three-fold $K3 \times E$ and
its associated BKM Lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$. The analogies to the
$\mathbb{C}^3$ / chiral-Yangian story are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\CoHA(\mathbb{C}^3) \leftrightarrow \CoHA(K3 \times E)$ as $E_2$- (or in compact case
$E_1$-) algebras over equivariant parameters.
\item Positive half $\Yplus \leftrightarrow$ positive half of a ``BKM Yangian''
$Y^+(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})$; \ClaimStatusConjectured.
\item Drinfeld double $\leftrightarrow$ full ``BKM Yangian''; \ClaimStatusConjectured.
\item Vertex algebra image $\leftrightarrow$ chiral algebra at $\Delta_5$-locus;
\ClaimStatusConjectured. The \emph{Kontsevich--Soibelman BPS Lie algebra}
$\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BPS}}(K3 \times E) \cong \mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$
(numerical equality of multiplicities, with Lie structure requiring additional input)
is the compact analogue of the Lie-theoretic ``primitive'' sub-object at
$\mathbb{C}^3$, which there is $\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Key difference.} At $\mathbb{C}^3$ (non-compact, toric), the CoHA is well-defined
globally and the chiral-Yangian story is a web of proven and conjectural identifications
between concrete factorisation-algebraic and vertex-algebraic objects.
At $K3 \times E$ (compact, non-toric), even the CoHA construction requires careful input
(Davison 2017 \texttt{arXiv:1601.02479} for proper CY$_3$, conditional on the global
critical-chart hypothesis for the sheaf moduli); the passage to a chiral / vertex-algebra
structure is an \emph{additional} set of questions, not automatic.
The BKM $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ is \emph{not} the chiral algebra; it is the analogue
of $\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1 = $ the ``Lie primitive'' inside the positive half of the
Yangian, which in the $\mathbb{C}^3$ setting is itself a specific infinite-dimensional
Lie algebra (central extension of polynomial loop algebra).

\subsection{Open problems in the compact case}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct $\CoHA(K3 \times E)$ as an associative (or $E_1$) algebra intrinsically,
not via a local toric model.
\item Identify the BPS Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BPS}}(K3 \times E)$ from Davison 2017
with the Gritsenko--Nikulin BKM $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ at the Lie-algebra level
(numerical multiplicities established; Lie bracket compatibility requires checking
$[\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BPS}, \gamma_1}, \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BPS}, \gamma_2}]
\to \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{BPS}, \gamma_1 + \gamma_2}$ matches BKM bracket).
\item Formulate a ``chiral BKM'' as a factorisation or vertex algebra on a spectral curve,
with Costello-style gauge-theory derivation from the holomorphic twist of a relevant
5D / 6D theory on $K3 \times E \times \mathbb{C}$.
\item Identify the $\Delta_5$-invariant role: it is the generating function of
BKM root multiplicities, not a direct structure constant of a chiral algebra.
The ``binding'' of algebraic and geometric sides at $\Delta_5$ is via numerical
equality of multiplicities (OP 2017 theorem), not via direct isomorphism of algebras.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{End of treatise.}
