Pattern~273 and AP-CY10. The adversarial line is that the 24 Niemeier
lattices, the 8 Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor paramodular forms,
the 5 admissible Borcherds-lift cases $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, the 26

\emph{different} categories, glued by explicit lifts (singular theta,
multiplicative, BRST), not by a single functor; the working-notes

\subsection{The five atlases and the maps between them}

The subject matter of this note lives in five distinct categories with
five distinct atlases:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item \textbf{$\mathbf{Lat}^{\mathrm{II}}_{24,0}$:} the 24 Niemeier
  lattices, i.e. even self-dual positive-definite rank-24 lattices
  (Niemeier 1973). Each is determined by its root sublattice, ranging
  from the empty (Leech) to $D_{24}$ (Niemeier's item \#$24$). Their
  theta series all satisfy $\Theta_\Lambda = E_{12} + c_\Delta \Delta$
  with the Niemeier reduction coefficient
  $c_\Delta(\Lambda) = (691\cdot|R_\Lambda| - 65520)/691$ where $|R_\Lambda|$ is
  the number of root vectors.
\item \textbf{$\mathbf{ParMod}_\Gamma$:} Siegel paramodular forms on
  genus-2 upper half-space, with congruence levels
  $\Gamma_t(N) < \Sp_4(\ZZ)$. The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2008 theorem
  supplies exactly \emph{8} diagonal-divisor paramodular forms across
  all admissible $(\Gamma_t(N))_{N}$; the subset with integer weight
  $c_N(0)/2$ lies over $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ giving weights
  $(5,2,1,1,1)$.
\item \textbf{$\mathbf{BKM}$:} Borcherds--Kac--Moody Lie superalgebras
  (Borcherds 1988) with real and imaginary simple roots and a
  denominator identity. Each singular theta lift of a weak Jacobi form
  produces one BKM.
\item \textbf{$M_{24}\mathrm{-rep}$:} the 26 conjugacy classes of
  $M_{24}$ (ATLAS) labelling twined K3 elliptic genera $Z_g(\tau,z)$
  (Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa 2011; Gaberdiel--Hohenegger--Volpato
  2010, 2012); five classes $1A, 2A, 3A, 4B, 6A$ are the
  CHL-admissible Nikulin-automorphism classes.
\item \textbf{$\mathbf{MonsterRep}$:} the 194 conjugacy classes of the
  Monster $\mathbb{M}$ (ATLAS) labelling McKay--Thompson series
  (Conway--Norton 1979; Borcherds 1992).
\end{enumerate}

These atlases are \emph{not} indexed by the same set. The maps
between them are:

\begin{itemize}
\item The Borcherds 1992 lift $\mathbf{ParMod}_{\Sp_4(\ZZ)} \to
  \mathbf{BKM}$ sends $\Delta_5 \mapsto \fg_{\Delta_5}$ (rank-3 BKM;
  Lorgat 2020 \S5).
\item The Borcherds--Harvey--Moore 1995 \emph{Monster} lift
  $\mathbf{MonsterRep} \to \mathbf{BKM}$ sends $V^\natural$ to the
  Monster Lie algebra of rank 26 (denominator $j(\sigma)-j(\tau)$).
  This lift uses the $\mathrm{II}_{25,1}$ lattice, not an
  $\mathrm{II}_{3,2}$ paramodular lattice; it does \emph{not} factor
  through $\mathbf{ParMod}$.
\item The Mathieu lift (EOT 2011; GHV 2010, 2012) sends
  $M_{24}\mathrm{-rep} \to \mathbf{ParMod}$ (twined) for the five
  Nikulin-admissible classes, via the twined K3 elliptic genus.
\item The umbral-moonshine lift (Cheng--Duncan--Harvey 2014) sends
  $\mathbf{Lat}^{\mathrm{II}}_{24,0} \to$ (mock modular forms)
  by attaching $H^{(\Lambda)}$ to each Niemeier lattice $\Lambda$
  through the outer automorphism group modulo the Weyl group.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Segal's axiomatic frame}

A \emph{modular functor} (Segal 1988, Atiyah 1988, Moore--Seiberg 1989)
assigns to each closed oriented surface $\Sigma$ a finite-dimensional
vector space $\mathcal{Z}(\Sigma)$, covariantly under orientation-
preserving diffeomorphisms; to each cobordism a linear map
functorial under gluing. For a K3 SCFT at $c = 6$, the genus-1
partition function on $E_\tau$ with a $\ZZ/N\ZZ$ twist gives the
twined elliptic genus $Z_{g_N}(\tau, z) \in \mathcal{Z}(E_\tau, g_N)$.
The EOT observation that $Z_g(\tau,z)$ decomposes as a (mock) modular
object with $M_{24}$-representation-theoretic Fourier coefficients is
the first-principles content of Mathieu moonshine: Segal's axioms
produce a one-dimensional space at each conjugacy class, the Fourier
coefficient is the character value.

The Borcherds \emph{multiplicative lift} (singular theta correspondence,
Borcherds 1998 Inventiones 132) is a second-layer construction: given
a weak Jacobi form $\phi$ of weight $0$ index $m$, the Borcherds
product
\[
\Phi(Z) \ = \ e^{2\pi i (\rho, Z)} \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}
  \left(1 - e^{2\pi i (n\tau + \ell z + m\sigma)}\right)^{c_\phi(nm, \ell)}
\]
is a meromorphic Siegel modular form of weight $c_\phi(0,0)/2$ on
genus-2 upper half-space. The \emph{Borcherds weight theorem} is
$\mathrm{wt}(\Phi) = c_\phi(0,0)/2$; this is the formula
$\kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ of the CLAUDE.md Essential constants.

\subsection{Lorgat 2020 Conjecture~1 in its category}

Lorgat 2020 \S5 Conjecture~1 asserts: \emph{for
$X = (S\times E)/(\ZZ/N\ZZ)$ with $(S, L, g_N, h_M)$ K3 data and
$N, M \leq 8$ symplectic-automorphism orders, all 8 Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor paramodular forms arise as reciprocal-square roots
of the refined/motivic DT zeta function $Z^X_{L, h_M}$ AND as
denominator functions of BKM superalgebras with root multiplicities
equal to the $g_N$-$h_M$-twisted twined K3 elliptic genus coefficients.}

The status is stratified:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N=1$:} \ClaimStatusTheorem. Borcherds 1995, Gritsenko 1999;
  the reciprocal-square-root claim
  $Z^X_{L,1} = C/\Delta_5^2$ is Oberdieck--Pixton Igusa cusp conjecture
  (theorem of Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016 for K3$\times$E); the BKM
  denominator-function realisation is the GKM $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ in Lorgat 2020 \S5.
\item \textbf{$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$:} \ClaimStatusConjectured. Both sides
  of the Conjecture~1 equivalence hold at the \emph{weight} level
  (Gritsenko 1999; GHV 2010); the root-multiplicity $=$ twined genus
  identification is \emph{not} tabulated case-by-case in Lorgat 2020
  and requires the full Gritsenko--Cl\'ery 2008 data.
\item \textbf{$N\in\{5,7,8\}$:} Speculative / outside the admissible
  CHL set. For $N=5$ the form
  $\Phi_{0,1}(\tau, z; 5) / \text{something}$ has non-integer weight
  $c_5(0)/2$; the CY-to-chiral pipeline is not developed in Vol III
  for these cases.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Ghost theorem, precise error, correct relationship}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem.}
The lattice VOA $V_\Lambda$ for each Niemeier $\Lambda$ is a holomorphic
$c = 24$ VOA of central charge 24, produces the same Virasoro sector
$\mathrm{Vir}_{c=24}$ modulo its root lattice, and has the same
genus-1 shadow obstruction $\kch = 12 = c/2$ if one \emph{only}
tracks Virasoro data (cf.\ the Monster module treatment in line 693).
This is the genuine content: genus-1 shadow depth-2 data is a
\emph{Virasoro-central-charge} invariant, hence coincides for all
holomorphic $c=24$ VOAs.

\paragraph{Precise error.}
The passage asserts $\kch(V_\Lambda) = 24$ ``$ = d$ for $d = 24$ free
bosons at level $k=1$.'' This conflates the dimension of the
Heisenberg-free-boson coordinate space (the rank of $\Lambda$, which
is 24) with $\kch$. By the Vol III convention
\[
\kch \ = \ c/2 \qquad\text{on genus-1 shadow depth-2 chiral data,}
\]
the correct value is $\kch(V_\Lambda) = c/2 = 24/2 = 12$, matching
$V^\natural$ (line 693) exactly.

The pre-heal passage is then self-contradictory: it asserts
$\kch(V_\Lambda) = 24$ and $\kch(V^\natural) = 12$, then claims
``genuine shadow-tower separation'' between them; but the separation
is \emph{manufactured} by the $\kch(V_\Lambda) = 24$ mis-assignment.
The true separation between Niemeier lattice VOAs and the Monster
module is finer:

\begin{theorem}[\ClaimStatusTheorem\ Niemeier-vs-Monster shadow
  separation, correctly stated]
\label{thm:a15-niemeier-monster-separation}
All 24 Niemeier lattice VOAs $V_\Lambda$ and the Monster module
$V^\natural$ have the same genus-1 central charge $c = 24$ and
therefore the same Virasoro-shadow coefficient $\kch = 12$.
They are distinguished by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\dim V_1$: $V_\Lambda$ has $\dim V_1 = |R_\Lambda| + 24$ (roots
  plus Heisenberg Cartan) $\geq 48$ for every $\Lambda$ except the
  Leech lattice, for which $\dim V_1^\Lambda = 24$ (Cartan only);
  $V^\natural$ has $\dim V_1 = 0$ (this is the Griess construction
  input).
\item The Frame shape / McKay--Thompson series at depth $\geq 3$.
\item The genus-2 Siegel modular invariant of the partition function.
\end{enumerate}
The Virasoro shadow alone at depth 2 does not separate the 24 + 1
objects.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Correct relationship.}
The correct passage reads: all 24 Niemeier lattice VOAs + $V^\natural$
share $\kch = 12$ and are therefore indistinguishable by genus-1
depth-2 shadow; the Niemeier theta reduction coefficient
$c_\Delta(\Lambda) = (691 |R_\Lambda| - 65520)/691$ distinguishes them
at the theta-series level (which lives \emph{outside} the $\kch$
shadow); $V^\natural$ is distinguished from all 24 Niemeier VOAs by
$\dim V_1 = 0$, not by $\kch$.

The pre-heal passage's final clause --- ``This gives genuine
shadow-tower separation: $\kch(V^\natural) = 12 \neq 24 =
\kch(V_\Lambda)$ for all twenty-four Niemeier lattice VOAs'' ---
is false and should be deleted.

\section{Attack--heal cycle 3: the Fermat quintic Jacobian dimension identity and the Burnside-lemma claim}
% ============================================================

\subsection*{Scope}

Target: \texttt{working\_notes.tex} lines 2104--2106, entry (ii) of
the idiosyncrasies list: ``The Fermat quintic at the Gepner point has
orbifold-invariant Jacobian ring of dimension $204 = b_3(Q) = (4^5 - 4)/5$,
matching the quintic Hodge diamond $\{1, 101, 101, 1\}$ exactly. This is
a purely combinatorial identity verified by Burnside's lemma.''

\subsection*{Ghost theorem, precise error, correct relationship}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem.}
For the Fermat quintic $Q = \{x_0^5 + x_1^5 + \ldots + x_4^5 = 0\}
\subset \bP^4$, the Jacobian ring is
\[
\mathrm{Jac}(f) \ = \ \CC[x_0, \ldots, x_4] / (\partial_i f) \ = \
\CC[x_0, \ldots, x_4] / (x_i^4)
\]
of total dimension $4^5 = 1024$. The orbifold model is the Gepner
$(3)^5$ / $\ZZ_5$ diagonal quotient. The $\ZZ_5$-invariant part is
governed by the multi-degree $5$ graded piece of the ring $\CC[x_i]$
modulo $x_i^4$; the grading constraint
$\deg = \sum_i d_i \equiv 0 \pmod 5$
with $d_i \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ counts monomials with
$\sum_i d_i \in \{0, 5, 10, 15\}$.
The \emph{middle Hodge number} of the quintic is
$h^{2,1}(Q) = 101$ by Candelas--de la Ossa--Green--Parkes 1991 and
Batyrev 1994.

\paragraph{Precise error.}
The passage asserts $204 = b_3(Q) = (4^5 - 4)/5 = 1020/5 = 204$,
treating this as a Burnside-lemma identity matching the Hodge
diamond.

Three issues:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$b_3(Q)$ value.} The third Betti number of the quintic
  threefold is $b_3(Q) = h^{3,0} + h^{2,1} + h^{1,2} + h^{0,3} = 1 +
  101 + 101 + 1 = 204$. This is correct.
\item \textbf{$(4^5 - 4)/5$ computation.} $4^5 = 1024$, so
  $(4^5 - 4)/5 = 1020/5 = 204$. The numerical identity holds.
\item \textbf{Burnside-lemma interpretation.} The cyclic group
  $\ZZ_5$ acts on $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}^5$ (the monomial exponents in the
  Jacobian ring after quotienting by $x_i^4$) by cyclic permutation.
  Burnside's lemma gives
  \[
  |X/\ZZ_5| \ = \ \tfrac{1}{5} \sum_{k=0}^{4} |\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma^k)| \ = \
  \tfrac{1}{5}(4^5 + 4 \cdot 4) \ = \ \tfrac{1044}{5}
  \]

\section{Attack--heal cycle 5: non-CY local surfaces and the signed $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}}$}
% ============================================================

\subsection*{Scope}

\emph{$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{crv}}(\delta) = \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{crv}}(-\delta)$:
the curved modular characteristic is an \emph{even} function of the CY
defect. Genus-$1$ data cannot distinguish a geometry from its Koszul

\end{quote}

\subsection*{Ghost theorem, precise error, correct relationship}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem.}
For a rank-2 bundle $E \to C$ on a smooth projective curve $C$ of genus
$g$, the total space $\Tot(E)$ has CY defect $\delta = \deg(\det E) -
\deg(K_C) = \deg(\det E) - (2g - 2)$. When $\delta = 0$, $\Tot(E)$ is a
local CY$_3$ (Serre-duality condition). When $\delta \neq 0$, the
chiral algebra $A_E$ carries a curving $m_0 = \delta \cdot \omega_C$
and bar complexes live in the coderived category
$D^{\mathrm{co}}(A_E)$. A genuine theorem: the curving-squared
correction $\delta^2 \chi(C)/24$ (the BCOV-type defect) is even in
$\delta$.

\paragraph{Precise error.}
Three precision issues:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sign conventions on $\delta$.} The claim
  $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}}(\delta) = \kch^{\mathrm{crv}}(-\delta)$ depends
  on the precise definition of $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}}$. Under the
  formula $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}} = \kch + \delta^2 \chi(C)/24$, the
  $\delta^2$ dependence is indeed even. But a more refined invariant
  --- e.g. the curved stress-tensor level
  $k_{\mathrm{crv}}(\delta) = k + \delta \cdot (\text{linear}) + \delta^2 \cdot (\text{quadratic})$ ---
  has a linear-in-$\delta$ piece that is odd, not even. The
  working-notes passage identifies the even-in-$\delta$ piece with the
  full ``curved modular characteristic'' and declares the invariant
  even; but the ``curved modular characteristic'' is not a single
  number, it is a pair (level, central-charge shift), and the level
  part has an odd-in-$\delta$ component.

\item \textbf{The ``Koszul dual is $-\delta$'' claim.} Koszul duality
  on curved $A_\infty$-algebras (Positselski 2011, Booth--Lazarev
  2023) sends $(A, m_0) \mapsto (A^!, -m_0^!)$ under appropriate
  curvature sign conventions; but the sign of the dual curvature is
  \emph{convention-dependent}, not intrinsic. The $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}}$
  even-in-$\delta$ statement therefore amounts to a convention fix,
  not to a statement about Koszul duality classes.

\item \textbf{Genus-1 data cannot distinguish.} This is correct at
  depth 2 (even-in-$\delta$ piece controls genus-1 shadow), but
  genus-2 partition functions \emph{do} see the linear-in-$\delta$
  piece through the $\delta \cdot \omega_C$ coupling on the gluing
  annulus. The passage's ``genus-$1$ data cannot distinguish'' is
  correct; it should not be read as ``no data can distinguish.''
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Correct relationship.}

\begin{theorem}[\ClaimStatusTheorem\ Non-CY curved shadow discipline]
\label{thm:a15-noncy-curved}
For $E \to C$ a rank-2 bundle with CY defect $\delta \in \ZZ$, the
curved chiral algebra $A_E$ has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Genus-1 shadow obstruction $\kch^{\mathrm{crv}}(\delta) = \kch(\Tot(E)) + \delta^2 \chi(C)/24$,
  \emph{even} in $\delta$.
\item Classical level deformation $k_{\mathrm{crv}}(\delta) = k + \delta\cdot\ell_1(C) + \delta^2\cdot\ell_2(C)$
  with an odd-in-$\delta$ component $\ell_1(C) \neq 0$ when $2g - 2 \neq 0$.
\item Genus-2 partition function $Z_2(\tau; \delta) = Z_2(\tau; 0) \cdot (1 + \delta \cdot F_1(\tau) + O(\delta^2))$
  with $F_1(\tau) \neq 0$ generically, distinguishing $\delta$ from
  $-\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore ``genus-1 data is blind to $\delta$-sign'' is correct;
``all chiral data is blind to $\delta$-sign'' is false; the
distinction requires genus-2 or higher.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Heal for (iii) in lines 2169--2172.}
Replace ``Genus-$1$ data cannot distinguish a geometry from its
Koszul dual.'' with ``Genus-$1$ shadow data cannot distinguish
$(E, \delta)$ from $(E^\vee \otimes K_C, -\delta)$; genus-$2$

for the $t=1$ normalisation).''
