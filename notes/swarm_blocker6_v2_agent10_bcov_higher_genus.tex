\begin{abstract}
We heal the higher-genus BCOV overclaim for $X=K3\times E$. The full
BCOV holomorphic anomaly equation lives on the complete variation of
Hodge structure; it does not reduce to one elliptic variable without a
proved restricted VHS, Yukawa, and propagator calculation.
Oberdieck--Pixton supply the external DT boundary datum
$Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}}=C\cdot\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}$, not a reconstruction from
BCOV. The displayed $F_0$--$F_3$ formulas below are therefore replaced
by a coefficient-extraction register: what would have to be computed
from the Borcherds product and checked against the restricted HAE before
any theorem-grade genus tower is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: BCOV moduli and the $K3\times E$ collapse}
\label{sec:setup}

For a smooth CY$_3$ target $X$, BCOV
\cite{BCOV1993, BCOV1994} package the topological-string genus-$g$
amplitudes into sections
$F_g \in \Gamma(\cM_X^{\mathrm{cpx}}, \cL^{\otimes(2-2g)})$ over the
complex-structure moduli $\cM_X^{\mathrm{cpx}}$, where $\cL$ is the
Hodge line bundle. The HAE reads, on the holomorphic deformation
parameters $t^i$ dual to a basis of $H^{2,1}(X)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hae}
\partial_{\bar\imath} F_g
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\, \overline{C}_{\bar\imath}^{\,jk}
\Bigl( D_j D_k F_{g-1} \;+\; \sum_{g'+g''=g,\; 0<g',g''<g}
       D_j F_{g'}\, D_k F_{g''} \Bigr)
\qquad (g\ge 2),
\end{equation}
with $C_{ijk}$ the holomorphic Yukawa coupling, $\overline{C}_{\bar
\imath}^{\,jk} = \overline{C}_{\bar\imath\bar\jmath\bar k}\,
e^{2K} G^{j\bar\jmath}G^{k\bar k}$ its complex-conjugate raised by the
Weil--Petersson metric $G_{i\bar\jmath}$, $K$ the K\"ahler potential
on $\cM_X^{\mathrm{cpx}}$, and $D_j$ the connection on
$\cL^{\otimes(2-2g)}$ (Christoffel + $-(2g-2)\partial_j K$). The $g=1$
seed is BCOV's holomorphic anomaly for $F_1$:
$\partial_{\bar\imath}\partial_j F_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}
\overline{C}_{\bar\imath}{}^{kl}C_{jkl} - (\chi(X)/24 - 1)
G_{j\bar\imath}$.

\medskip

The proposed collapse on $X = K3\times E$ is the first obstruction.

\begin{lemma}[Restricted one-variable locus is an extra datum]
\label{lem:one-modulus}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}\\
K\"unneth gives
\[
\dim H^{2,1}(K3\times E) = h^{2,1}(K3)\cdot h^{0,0}(E) +
h^{1,1}(K3)\cdot h^{1,0}(E) + h^{2,0}(K3)\cdot h^{0,1}(E)
=0+20+1=21 .
\]
A one-variable equation in the elliptic modulus $\tau$ is available only
after choosing and proving a restricted VHS on the principal
K3-fibre locus, including the projection of the Yukawa tensor and
propagators to that locus. This note does not prove that restriction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The K\"unneth calculation is direct. The proposed one-variable
principal locus is a restriction problem, not a consequence of the
dimension count: the $20$ classes in
$H^{1,1}(K3)\otimes H^{1,0}(E)$ are present in $H^{2,1}(X)$. To reduce
the HAE one must supply a VHS splitting, prove that the discarded
directions do not contribute to the raised Yukawa tensor, and check the
BCOV propagators after restriction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional reduced HAE on $K3\times E$]
\label{cor:reduced-hae}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}\\
If a one-dimensional restricted VHS and its propagator calculus are
constructed on the principal locus, then \eqref{eq:hae} restricts to a
one-variable equation in $\bar\tau$ of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hae-1d}
\partial_{\bar\tau} F_g
\;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\overline{C}^{\,\tau\tau}_{\bar\tau}
\Bigl( D_\tau^2 F_{g-1} + \sum_{g'+g''=g}\!\!{}'\;
  D_\tau F_{g'}\, D_\tau F_{g''}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $C_{\tau\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{res}}$ is the restricted Yukawa
component. The existence and value of this restricted component are
proof obligations, not outputs of the present note.
\end{corollary}

\section{Yukawa coupling on $K3\times E$}
\label{sec:yukawa}

The classical statement is that on $X = K3\times E$ with holomorphic
volume form $\Omega_X = \omega_{K3}\wedge dz_E$ and elliptic
coordinate $\bar\alpha = d\bar z_E\in H^{0,1}(E)\subset
H^{0,1}(K3\times E)$, the cohomological cube vanishes:
$\bar\alpha^{\wedge 3} = 0$. The restricted Yukawa, if the principal
VHS is supplied, is not computed by this wedge cube; it is the
triple-pairing
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:yukawa-def}
C_{\tau\tau\tau}(\tau)
= \int_X \Omega_X \wedge \nabla_\tau\nabla_\tau\nabla_\tau \Omega_X,
\end{equation}
which on $K3\times E$ factors through the Gauss--Manin connection on
the elliptic factor.

\begin{proposition}[Restricted Yukawa is not computed here]
\label{prop:yukawa-k3xe}
\ClaimStatusOpen{}\\
On a supplied one-dimensional principal VHS, write the restricted
Yukawa as
\[
C_{\tau\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{res}}(\tau)
= \int_X \Omega_X \wedge
  \nabla_\tau^{\mathrm{res}}\nabla_\tau^{\mathrm{res}}
  \nabla_\tau^{\mathrm{res}}\Omega_X .
\]
No formula such as $C_{\tau\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{res}}\propto \Eis_4$,
nor any $\Eis_2$-corrected variant, is proved in this note.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The displayed expression is only the definition of the restricted
quantity once a restricted Gauss--Manin connection has been constructed.
Determining whether it is zero, modular, quasi-modular, or a polynomial
in Eisenstein generators requires the omitted VHS/Yukawa calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Healing the $\bar\alpha^3 = 0$ apparent paradox]
\label{rem:cubic-vanish-fix}
The cohomological vanishing $(\bar\alpha)^{\wedge 3}=0$ is a statement
about the wedge cube on $E$, which has $h^{0,1}(E)=1$. The Yukawa
\eqref{eq:yukawa-def} is not this wedge cube: the third covariant
derivative $\nabla_\tau$ involves the Gauss--Manin connection. This
observation blocks the naive $\bar\alpha^{\wedge 3}=0$ shortcut, but it
does not compute the restricted Yukawa.
\end{remark}

\section{Genus-tower closure: integration of \eqref{eq:hae-1d}}
\label{sec:integration}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional OP boundary datum for a restricted BCOV tower]
\label{thm:bcov-closure}
\ClaimStatusConditional{}\\
Assume a one-dimensional restricted VHS on the K3-fibre principal locus,
the restricted Yukawa and propagators of
Corollary~\ref{cor:reduced-hae}, and an MNOP/hCS--Hall comparison
identifying the relevant BCOV boundary sector with reduced
Donaldson--Thomas theory. Then the Oberdieck--Pixton identity
$Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}} = C\cdot\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}$ may be used as external
boundary data for the holomorphic ambiguities of the restricted tower.
In that conditional setting one seeks
\[
F_0 = -\log\Disc_{K3\times E}^{\mathrm{flat}} \pmod{\text{polynomial}},
\quad F_1 = -\tfrac{1}{2}\log\bigl(G^{1/2}_{\tau\bar\tau}\,
e^{(\chi/24 - 1)K}\bigr),
\]
\[
F_g(\tau)\bigr|_{\tau\to i\infty} \sim \log Z^X_{\mathrm{GW}}\bigr|_{2g-2},
\]
with OP/MNOP providing a candidate comparison
\[
\sum_{g\ge 0} \hbar^{2g-2}\, F_g(\tau)
\leadsto\; \log Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}}(\tau,\hbar) - \log C
\;=\; -2\log \Delta_5(Z(\tau,\hbar)),
\]
with $Z(\tau, \hbar) = \begin{psmallmatrix}\tau & z\\ z &
\sigma\end{psmallmatrix}$ the Siegel period and the
$\hbar$-specialisation $z = \hbar/(2\pi i)$, $\sigma$ the dual
elliptic period. This theorem records the conditional boundary-data
shape; it does not prove the restricted HAE, uniqueness, or coefficient
extraction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
\textbf{(i) Restricted HAE.} Corollary~\ref{cor:reduced-hae} is
conditional, so polynomiality in one variable is conditional as well.
Yamaguchi--Yau finite generation cannot simply be imported until the
restricted propagator ring has been identified.

\textbf{(ii) Boundary data.} Oberdieck--Pixton prove a reduced DT
identity with $\Delta_5^{-2}$. This is a theorem on the DT side and a
boundary datum for BCOV only after MNOP and the compact hCS--Hall
comparison are supplied.

\textbf{(iii) Coefficients.} The Borcherds product gives a formal
Fourier--Jacobi expansion. Extracting $\hbar^{2g-2}$ coefficients from
that product is a computation; placeholders or guessed Eisenstein
polynomials do not constitute it.
\end{proof}

\section{Three attack-heal cycles}
\label{sec:attack-heal}

\begin{attack}[Cycle 1: Moduli reduction]
The principal-locus-only reduction of \(\dim H^{2,1}(K3\times E)\) to a
single complex modulus appears to discard the $h^{1,1}(K3)$
$B$-field/K\"ahler directions. If those mix with the $\bar\tau$
HAE flow at higher genus, \eqref{eq:hae-1d} is incomplete.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Cycle 1]
The healing is not to assert decoupling, but to name the missing
restriction. The $20$ classes in
$H^{1,1}(K3)\otimes H^{1,0}(E)$ remain in $H^{2,1}(K3\times E)$.
A one-variable HAE is legitimate only after a restricted VHS is
constructed and the Yukawa/propagator components transverse to the
elliptic modulus are shown not to enter the chosen sector.
\end{heal}

\begin{attack}[Cycle 2: Yukawa normalisation]
$\Eis_4(\tau)$ is a holomorphic modular form, but the Yukawa
$C_{\tau\tau\tau}$ on a CY$_3$ is generically only quasi-modular
once the connection is included. The naive identification
$C\propto\Eis_4$ misses the $\Eis_2$-shift that sources the
$\partial_{\bar\tau} F_g$ residual.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Cycle 2]
The almost-holomorphic completion $\widehat{\Eis_2}(\tau, \bar\tau) =
\Eis_2(\tau) - \tfrac{3}{\pi\Im\tau}$ is what enters the propagators
$S^{\tau\tau}, S^\tau, S$ of BCOV on one-variable examples. For
$K3\times E$ this is evidence for the expected quasi-modular shape,
not a calculation of $C_{\tau\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{res}}$. The heal is to
replace the $\Eis_4$ formula with the proof obligation: compute the
restricted Yukawa and the restricted propagator ring before invoking
the Yamaguchi--Yau polynomial package.
\end{heal}

\begin{attack}[Cycle 3: Boundary at the Humbert divisor $H_1$]
Theorem~\ref{thm:bcov-closure} appeals to the Oberdieck--Pixton
identity $Z^X_{\mathrm{DT}}=C\cdot\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}$. But $\Delta_5$
vanishes on the Humbert divisor $H_1$, so $\Delta_5^{-2}$ has a
double pole there, and $\log\Delta_5^{-2}$ has an additive $-2\log
\mathrm{dist}(Z, H_1)$ singularity. The HAE wants finite holomorphic
ambiguity, not a logarithmic divergence.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Cycle 3]
The Humbert divisor $H_1$ on $\cM_2^{(2)}/\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$ is the
locus of decomposable abelian surfaces, which on the $K3\times E$
moduli is a natural boundary stratum for the OP formula. Its pole is
therefore legitimate DT boundary data. It does not, without the
restricted BCOV calculation, prove the BCOV gap coefficient or uniquely
pin the holomorphic ambiguity. Since $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3\times E)=0$
only cancels the universal constant-map term, any residual Hilbert-scheme
or fibre contribution must be computed separately.
\end{heal}

\section{Coefficient-extraction register for $F_0$ through $F_3$}
\label{sec:explicit}

The proposed genus coefficients would be extracted as coefficients of
$\hbar^{2g-2}$ in $-2\log\Delta_5(Z)$ only after three pieces of data
are fixed: the MNOP variable normalisation, the restricted VHS
coordinate, and the diagonal or non-diagonal Siegel specialisation.
With those choices still open, the Borcherds product is a source of
candidate boundary coefficients, not a completed BCOV computation. The
Gritsenko \cite{Gritsenko1995, GritsenkoNikulin1998} product has the
shape
\[
\Delta_5(Z) \;=\; q^{1/2}y^{1/2}s^{1/2}\!\!
\prod_{\substack{(n,\ell,m)>0\\ 4nm-\ell^2 \ge -1}}
(1 - q^n y^\ell s^m)^{c(4nm - \ell^2)},
\quad
\sum_{N\ge -1} c(N) q^{N/4} = \phi_{0,1}(\tau, z),
\]
with $\phi_{0,1}$ the unique weak Jacobi form of weight $0$ index $1$.

For a chosen specialisation, one may formally expand
\[
-2\log\Delta_5(Z)\bigr|_{s=q,\,y=e^\hbar}
\;=\; -3\log q - \hbar
+ 2\!\!\!\sum_{\substack{n,m\ge 1\\ \ell\in\Z}}\!
c(4nm-\ell^2)\sum_{k\ge 1}\frac{1}{k}\, q^{k(n+m)} e^{k\ell\hbar}.
\]
This formal expansion is not yet the promised $F_g$ computation. The
following obligations replace the former closed-form claims.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(C\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item Fix the specialisation \(Z(\tau,z,\sigma)\mapsto Z_{\mathrm{res}}\)
used by the restricted VHS. The diagonal choice \(s=q\) is a choice, not
a theorem.
\item Extract the coefficients of \(\hbar^{-2},\hbar^0,\hbar^2,\hbar^4\)
from the Borcherds product with the exact Fourier coefficients
\(c(4nm-\ell^2)\), including polar and regular terms.
\item Express the extracted functions in the correct modular ring for
the restricted monodromy group. It may be a quasi-Jacobi or
almost-holomorphic ring; it is not automatically
\(\C[\Eis_2,\Eis_4,\Eis_6]\).
\item Verify that the extracted coefficients satisfy the restricted
HAE with the computed \(C_{\tau\tau\tau}^{\mathrm{res}}\), metric, and
propagators.
\item Use the conifold/Humbert boundary only after the local coordinate
and gap normalisation are fixed. The vanishing
\(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3\times E)=0\) removes the universal constant-map
term but does not compute all residual fibre terms.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[No $F_0$--$F_3$ computation in this note]
\label{prop:no-F0-F3-computation}
\ClaimStatusOpen{}\\
The present note does not compute closed forms for
\(F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3\). In particular, the coefficients of
\(\Eis_2^a\Eis_4^b\Eis_6^c\), the constants
\(\alpha_4,\alpha_5\), and the holomorphic ambiguities \(f_g\) remain
undetermined until the five obligations above are discharged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed Borcherds product is a valid automorphic input for
\(\Delta_5\). A BCOV free energy is a solution of the HAE with a
specified VHS, propagators, boundary coordinate, and holomorphic
ambiguity. Since those data have not been computed in this note,
coefficient placeholders cannot be promoted to genus free energies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Four subscripted invariants]
The four values \(\{0,3,5,24\}\) remain separate:
\(\kcat(K3\times E)=0\) is the total-space Hodge/Euler reading,
\(\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3\) belongs to the Heisenberg specialisation,
\(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\) is the Borcherds weight, and
\(\kfib=24\) is the K3 fibre Mukai rank. They are evidence for the
same Vol III landscape, not coefficients of a proved BCOV genus tower.
\end{remark}

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The stable core is compatibility: the OP/DT partition function is
governed by $\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}$, and a restricted BCOV tower should use
that automorphic function as boundary data once the restricted VHS and
hCS-to-Hall comparison are built. Three open extensions remain:
(a) the CHL siblings $\Phi_N$ for $N=2,3,4,6$, requiring the
equivariant hCS-to-Hall comparison; (b) Borcherds-product expansions on
non-principal $\Sigma_2$ loci; (c) a genuine BCOV computation explaining
how the Heisenberg-rank witness $\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3$ and the
Euler-anomaly coefficient $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3\times E)/24=0$
coexist without conflation.
