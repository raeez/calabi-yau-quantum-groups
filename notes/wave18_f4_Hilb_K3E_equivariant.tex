\section*{$\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(K3\times E)$ and reduced elliptic descent}

\subsection*{Relative Hilbert scheme}

Let \(X=K3\times E\) and write \(\pi_E:X\to E\).  A \(T\)-point of the
relative Hilbert functor is a pair
\[
  e:T\to E,\qquad
  Z\subset K3\times T
\]
with \(Z\) finite flat of degree \(n\) over \(T\); it determines the
closed subscheme \(Z\subset K3\times E\times T\) supported over the graph
of \(e\).  Conversely every closed subscheme of \(X\times T\), finite
flat of degree \(n\) over \(T\) and supported over such a graph, is of
this form.  The representing scheme is
\[
  \Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X) \;=\;
  \Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}\bigl(K3\times E \xrightarrow{\,\mathrm{pr}_{E}\,} E\bigr),
\]
and the product structure identifies it canonically with
\[
  \Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X) \;\cong\; \Hilb^{n}(K3)\times E .
\]
The absolute point Hilbert scheme \(\Hilb^n(X)\) belongs to the
threefold punctual theory; the relative construction stays on K3
surface fibres.  Fogarty 1968 gives smoothness of \(\Hilb^{n}(K3)\)
of dimension \(2n\), hence
\[
  \Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X) \;\longrightarrow\; E
\]
is a smooth proper morphism with fibre \(\Hilb^{n}(K3)\), total space
smooth of complex dimension \(2n+1\).

\subsection*{Free elliptic translation}

The connected algebraic action for the reduction is translation by
the elliptic curve on the second factor.  It acts freely on
\(\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)\cong\Hilb^{n}(K3)\times E\).  The reduced
elliptic operation is descent along the free orbit:
\[
  \bigl[\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)/E\bigr]
  \;\cong\;
  \Hilb^{n}(K3).
\]
This quotient by a free elliptic translation is the geometric source of
the reduced fibre Euler form.  Finite origin-fixing automorphisms of
CM elliptic curves enter the CHL diagonal quotient independently of
this free translation descent.

\subsection*{Finite torsion descent}

For \(G=E[m]\), translation by \(G\) on the elliptic factor is free and
\[
  (K3\times E)/G \;\cong\; K3\times(E/G).
\]
A \(G\)-linearised coherent sheaf, ideal sheaf, or stable pair on
\(K3\times E\) descends to the quotient, and pullback from the quotient
recovers the \(G\)-linearised object.  The finite character algebra is
\[
  K_G(\mathrm{pt})=R(G)=\mathbb Z[G^\vee],
  \qquad
  R(G)_{\mathbb Q}\cong\mathbb Q[G^\vee].
\]
These idempotents are the torsion labels in finite CHL and rubber
sectors.  Fixed components for Atiyah--Bott localisation require a
continuous torus action; finite torsion contributes character idempotents
instead of a localisation parameter.
For a quotient charge whose pullback has total point degree \(n\), the
K3 contribution is still built from Nakajima summands
\[
  H^*\bigl(\Hilb^r(K3)\bigr),
\]
with \(r\) determined by the orbit degree and the imprimitive divisor
correction of the chosen theory.  Nakajima's operators
\(p_{-k}(\alpha)\), \(\alpha\in H^{*}(K3)\), furnish the Fock generators
on the K3 factor; finite elliptic characters record descent data.

\subsection*{K3 product after reduction}

Two Euler series are distinct.  For the absolute Hilbert scheme
of points on the smooth threefold, Cheah's power-structure formula gives
\[
  \sum_{n\geq 0} q^{n}\,\chi\bigl(\Hilb^{n}(K3\times E)\bigr)
   = F_3(q)^{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3\times E)}
   =1,
\]
because \(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3\times E)=24\cdot0=0\).  Equivalently,
\(\chi(\Hilb^{0}(K3\times E))=1\) and
\(\chi(\Hilb^{n}(K3\times E))=0\) for \(n\geq1\), with
\(F_3(q)=\sum_{n\geq0}\chi(\Hilb^{n}_{0}(\mathbb A^3))q^n\) the local
punctual threefold series.  For the relative product before descent,
\[
  \chi\bigl(\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)\bigr)
  =
  \chi\bigl(\Hilb^{n}(K3)\bigr)\chi(E)
  =
  0.
\]
The K3 product arises after quotienting the free elliptic orbit.
Define
\[
  \chi_{\mathrm{red},E}\bigl(\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)\bigr)
  :=
  \chi\bigl(\Hilb^{n}(K3)\bigr).
\]
Then Göttsche's theorem gives
\[
  \sum_{n\geq 0} q^{n}\,\chi_{\mathrm{red},E}\bigl(\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)\bigr)
   \;=\;
  \prod_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-q^{m})^{24}}
  \;=\; \frac{q}{\eta(q)^{24}},
\]
where \(\eta(q)=q^{1/24}\prod_{m\geq1}(1-q^m)\).  This is the
Göttsche product for the K3 fibre; the exponent \(24\) is supplied by
\(\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3)\) and by the rank of the Mukai cohomology
lattice.  Nakajima--Grojnowski theory upgrades the same vector space to
the rank-\(24\) Heisenberg Fock representation.

\subsection*{Surface boundary and threefold reduced DT}

There are two moduli problems.  The relative space
\(\Hilb^{n}_{K3/E}(X)\cong \Hilb^{n}(K3)\times E\) is a surface
Hilbert scheme varying over the elliptic base, and its reduced Euler
series is the K3 boundary product \(q/\eta^{24}\).  The threefold
reduced Donaldson--Thomas theory uses ideal sheaves
\(\Hilb^{n,\beta}(X)\) of one-dimensional subschemes and stable-pair
spaces \(P_{n,\beta}(X)\), equipped with the K3-sourced cosection
reduction.  Its numerical invariant is the reduced OP/PT/DT partition
function in variables \((q,\zeta,p)\).  The boundary product controls
the K3 fibre exponent.  A character identity between these series remains
a test of the proposed Hall target until a Hall--Borcherds recognition
theorem supplies generators and relations.  The construction of the
threefold Hall algebra and its comparison with
\(\Hilb^{n,\beta}(X)\) or \(P_{n,\beta}(X)\) requires the compact
critical CoHA data below.

\subsection*{Borcherds denominator and DT scalar}

Let \(Z=(\tau,z,\sigma)\), \(q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\),
\(\zeta=e^{2\pi iz}\), and \(p=e^{2\pi i\sigma}\).  The weight-\(5\)
Gritsenko--Nikulin form \(\Deltafive\) supplies the primitive Borcherds
denominator.  Its monic product is
\[
  \Dfive(Z)
  =
  q^{1/2}\zeta^{1/2}p^{1/2}
  \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}
  \bigl(1-q^n\zeta^\ell p^m\bigr)^{c(4nm-\ell^2)},
\]
where \(c(D)\) are the Fourier coefficients of the K3 weak Jacobi input
\(\phi_{0,1}=y^{-1}+10+y+O(q)\).  The theta-normalised and monic
sections are related by
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Deltafive.
\]
The Borcherds input has
\[
  c_1(0)=10,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Deltafive)=\frac{c_1(0)}{2}=5.
\]
The Oberdieck--Pandharipande reduced DT scalar uses the monic
normalisation
\[
  \PhiOP=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\Deltafive^2,
\]
and its denominator scalar is
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{red}}_{\DT,\mathrm{den}}(K3\times E)
  = -(\PhiOP)^{-1}
  = -\Dfive^{-2}
  = -4096\,\Deltafive^{-2}.
\]
The full numerical partition function carries the Oberdieck numerator;
the displayed scalar records the denominator line.  The K3 fibre
product \(q/\eta^{24}\) supplies the boundary exponent \(24\).  The
compact reduced DT denominator is the three-variable Igusa-square object
in OP normalisation, and the factor \(4096=64^2\) converts the monic
denominator to the unnormalised \(\Deltafive\)-coordinate.

\subsection*{Positive half and Hall--Drinfeld double}

The Borcherds side supplies the positive-half target
\[
  U\bigl(\mathfrak n_+(\mathfrak g_{\Deltafive})\bigr),
  \qquad
  \operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_\alpha=c(D(\alpha)).
\]
A compact Hall interpretation requires an oriented compact critical
positive half \(\mathcal H^+_{K3\times E}\), a Hall product, a parity
fixture, root grading, the imprimitive divisor corrections, and finite
height recognition maps
\[
  \Psi^+_{\mathrm{Hall}\to\BKM}\colon
  \mathcal H^+_{K3\times E}
  \longrightarrow
  \widehat{U(\mathfrak n_+(\mathfrak g_{\Deltafive}))}.
\]
The reduced DT scalar and the fibrewise Hilbert product are
character-level tests for this target.  A compact source construction
also needs the orientation and completion data above.

The local toric comparison fixes the half/double convention:
\[
  \CoHA(\mathbb C^3)\cong Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1).
\]
This is the Hall positive half.  The full \(\mathcal W_{1+\infty}\)
vertex algebra appears after adjoining the opposite half, passing through
the double or centre, completing, and evaluating on a Fock module.

The full BKM-side quantum object requires the negative half, the
nondegenerate Hopf pairing, coproduct, centre compatibility, completion,
and Borcherds bracket comparison.  These data produce a
Hall--Drinfeld double, a BKM-side object distinct from the Mukai
self-mirror K3 Yangian branch.  Since \(X\) is a CY threefold, the
\(\Phi_3\)-output is natively \(\Eone\)-chiral; \(\Etwo\) structure
belongs to
\(\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A))\), to the completed double, or to stable-envelope
module categories.

\subsection*{\(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}, \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}},
\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}\) on \(K3\times E\)}

\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)
  =
  \chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\cdot\chi(\mathcal O_{E})
  =
  2\cdot 0
  =
  0
\]
on the total space, Künneth-multiplicative.  The compact chiral
Hodge-supertrace value is
\[
  \kch(K3\times E)
  =
  \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)
  =
  1-1+1-1
  =
  0.
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation is an independent chiral reading:
\[
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3.
\]
The Borcherds weight is
\[
  \kBKM(\Deltafive)=c_{1}(0)/2=10/2=5,
\]
and the K3 fibre rank is
\[
  \kfib(K3\times E;K3)
  =
  \operatorname{rank}\widetilde H^{*}(K3,\mathbb Z)
  =
  24.
\]
The compact \(\kch(K3\times E)=0\) and \(\kcat(K3\times E)=0\)
have the same numerical value and different definitions: the first is
the chiral Hodge supertrace, the second is the holomorphic Euler
characteristic.  The decorated slot list is
\[
  \{\kcat,\kch,\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kBKM,\kfib\}(K3\times E)
  =
  \{0_{\cat},0_{\ch},3_{\ch,\mathrm{Heis}},5_{\BKM},24_{\mathrm{fiber}}\}.
\]
After identifying repeated numerical values, the value spectrum is
\[
  \{0,3,5,24\}.
\]
The K3 fibre value \(\chi(\cO_{K3})=2\) is a fibre witness. These
invariants belong to separate constructions, with their own definitions
and verification paths.

For the primitive CHL denominator ladder,
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2 \\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2 & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
The BKM invariant is the Borcherds weight:
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
The primitive CHL ladder has exactly the displayed orders
\(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\).  The \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) rows, when present, are
non-CHL or Nikulin/Gritsenko--Clery catalogue entries and belong to a
separate denominator table.
At \(N=1\), the three axes read
\[
  \kBKM(\Deltafive)=5,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \chi(\cO_E)=0,
\]
so the Borcherds weight is determined by the input constant \(c_1(0)\)
and is independent of the compact chiral, Heisenberg, fibre, and Hall
axes.
