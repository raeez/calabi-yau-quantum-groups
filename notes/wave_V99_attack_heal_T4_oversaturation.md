## 1. Hodge structure of $T^4$ and the source of over-saturation

The Hodge diamond of $T^4 = \mathbb{C}^2/\Lambda$ is

$$
h^{p,q}(T^4) \;=\; \binom{2}{p}\binom{2}{q},\qquad 0\le p,q\le 2,
$$

with total $\sum_{p,q} h^{p,q} = (1+1)^2(1+1)^2 = 16$ classes and

$$
\chi(\mathcal O_{T^4}) \;=\; \sum_{q=0}^2(-1)^q h^{0,q}
\;=\; 1 - 2 + 1 \;=\; 0.
$$

**Source of $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$.** The chiral algebraization
$\Phi(T^4) = \Phi(D^b\operatorname{Coh}(T^4))$ inherits the universal
$\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$-grading. Each $H^{1,0}$-class
$\omega\in H^{1,0}(T^4)$ generates an involution
$\varepsilon_\omega$ on $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$: contraction
with $\omega$ gives an odd derivation of square zero, which exponentiates
to a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading flip. Since $h^{1,0}(T^4) = 2$, we obtain
**two extra "Hodge-piece" involutions** $\varepsilon_{\omega_1},\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$
beyond the universal pair $(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}})$
that already exists for any CY chiral algebra.

This is the over-saturation: $T^4$ supports 4 commuting involutions, not 2.

For comparison: $E = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda_E$ has $h^{1,0}(E)=1$, giving 1
extra Hodge involution beyond the universal pair, hence $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$.
But on $E$ the universal $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ is conjugate to the
unique Hodge involution by $H^{1,0}$-Serre duality (the holomorphic
1-form pairs $H^{0,1}$ with $H^{1,0}$ non-degenerately and intertwines
the two parities), so the effective grading collapses to
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2 = V_4$, recovering the standard $M_E=(1,0,0,-1)$
presentation.

For $T^4$ no such collapse occurs at $h^{1,0}=2$ (two independent
Hodge classes cannot all be absorbed into $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$),
which is exactly why the over-saturation is genuine.

For $K3$: $h^{1,0}(K3) = 0$, so $r_{K3} = 0$ and there is no
over-saturation; the $V_4$-presentation $M_{K3} = (0,5,-16,13)$ is
already the final invariant.

## 2. The four involutions on $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(T^4),\cdot)$

Fix a basis $\omega_1,\omega_2$ of $H^{1,0}(T^4) \cong \mathbb{C}^2$
adapted to the product structure $T^4 = E_1\times E_2$ (so $\omega_i$
pulls back from the $i$-th factor).

### 2.1 Universal involutions (algebraization-independent of the Hodge data)

* $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$: weight-grading involution.
  Acts as $(-1)^{|x|_{\mathrm{wt}}}$ on a homogeneous element $x$ of
  $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, where $|\cdot|_{\mathrm{wt}}$ is
  the conformal-weight grading. Universal for any chiral algebra. Eigenvalue
  $+1$ on even-weight, $-1$ on odd-weight.

* $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$: cohomological parity.
  Acts as $(-1)^{|x|_{\mathrm{coh}}}$, the cohomological-degree parity
  on $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$. Universal.

These are the two factors generating the $V_4$ used in
`T4_bigraded_Lefschetz_kunneth.md` (with the identification
$\epsilon_1 \leftrightarrow \varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$,
$\epsilon_2\leftrightarrow\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$).

### 2.2 $T^4$-specific (Hodge-piece) involutions

* $\varepsilon_{\omega_1}$: contraction-then-exponentiation of $\omega_1$.
  Concretely: $\omega_1\in H^{1,0}(T^4)$ defines an odd derivation
  $\iota_{\omega_1}$ on $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$
  with $\iota_{\omega_1}^2=0$; the
  associated involution is $\varepsilon_{\omega_1}=(-1)^{N_{\omega_1}}$
  where $N_{\omega_1}$ is the eigenvalue of the bigrading shift induced
  by Lie-with-$\omega_1$. Equivalently: the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading by
  $\omega_1$-Hodge type $(p_1,q_1)\bmod 2$.

* $\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$: the same construction with $\omega_2$.

The four involutions $\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},
\varepsilon_{\omega_1},\varepsilon_{\omega_2}\}$ pairwise commute: each
is a $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading by an independent decomposition of
$\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$ inherited from the bigraded Hodge
structure on $T^4$. They generate an action of $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$.

**Independence check.** $\varepsilon_{\omega_1}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$ project nontrivially to distinct $1$-dimensional
$H^{1,0}$-subspaces and are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_2$ as
characters of $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$. Neither is a function
of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ because the
Hodge type $(p,q)$ is finer than the total cohomological degree $p+q$
(which is what $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ sees) and is unrelated to
the conformal weight (what $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$ sees).

**Geometric content.** Under the Künneth identification
$T^4 = E_1\times E_2$, $\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ acts only on the
$\Phi(E_i)$-factor of $\Phi(T^4) = \Phi(E_1)\otimes\Phi(E_2)$ (no
mixing between factors), so $\varepsilon_{\omega_1}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$ are the two factor-wise Hodge involutions.

## 3. The 16 Hodge-twisted traces $\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}$

Index each of the 16 characters of $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$ by
$\vec\epsilon = (\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},
\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)\in \{+,-\}^4$.

Define the Hodge-twisted Lefschetz trace
$$
\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}
\;=\;
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]}
\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}
\,\varepsilon_{\omega_1}^{[\epsilon_1=-]}
\,\varepsilon_{\omega_2}^{[\epsilon_2=-]}
\;\bigm|\;
\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(T^4),\Phi(T^4))\bigr).
$$

Because each of the four $\mathbb{Z}/2$-graded pieces factors via
$T^4 = E_1\times E_2$ ($\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ acts only on the $i$-th
factor; $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ are
multiplicative under tensor product), the trace factorises as:

$$
\Pi^{T^4}_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)}
\;=\;
\Pi^{E_1}_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1)}\;\cdot\;
\Pi^{E_2}_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_2)},
$$

where $\Pi^E_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon)}$
is the analogous 8-character trace on the elliptic curve.

### 3.1 The $E$-factor traces

For $E$, $h^{1,0}=1$ collapses $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ and the
unique Hodge involution to a single effective $\mathbb{Z}/2$-grading
via Serre-duality conjugacy: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\varepsilon_\omega$
acts trivially on $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(E),\Phi(E))$
(because $H^{1,0}\otimes H^{0,1} \cong H^{1,1} \to \mathbb{C}$ is the
Serre pairing, which equates the two $(-1)$-eigenspaces). So of the 8
characters $(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon)$,
the four with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\epsilon = +$ all give the
same trace, and the four with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\epsilon = -$
all give the same trace.

Concretely, putting $a = \epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$ and
$b = \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\epsilon\in\{+,-\}$:

$$
\Pi^E_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon)}
\;=\;
M_E^{(a,b)}
\;=\;
\begin{cases}
+1 & (a,b) = (+,+),\\
\phantom{+}0 & (a,b) = (+,-),\\
\phantom{+}0 & (a,b) = (-,+),\\
-1 & (a,b) = (-,-),
\end{cases}
$$

reproducing $M_E = (1,0,0,-1)$ in the $V_4$-presentation, with each
$V_4$-entry counted with $\mathbb{Z}/2$-multiplicity $2$ when lifted to
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$.

### 3.2 The 16 $T^4$-traces explicitly

Write $a = \epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$, $b_1 = \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\epsilon_1$,
$b_2 = \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\cdot\epsilon_2$. By §3 factorisation,

$$
\Pi^{T^4}_{(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)}
\;=\; M_E^{(a,b_1)}\,M_E^{(a,b_2)}.
$$

The product $M_E^{(a,b_1)}M_E^{(a,b_2)}$ is non-zero exactly when
$b_1 = b_2$ (since $M_E^{(a,+)}$ is non-zero only at $a=+$ and equals
$+1$, and $M_E^{(a,-)}$ is non-zero only at $a=-$ and equals $-1$,
forcing both $b_i$ to take the same sign for a non-zero product). When
$b_1 = b_2 = +$ and $a=+$, the product is $(+1)(+1)=+1$; when
$b_1 = b_2 = -$ and $a=-$, the product is $(-1)(-1)=+1$; all other
combinations are zero.

So the 16 traces are

$$
\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}
\;=\;
\begin{cases}
+1 & \text{if } b_1 = b_2 = a,\\
\phantom{+}0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

The condition $b_1 = b_2 = a$ unpacks to
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_2 = \epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}$,
equivalently $\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_2 = \epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$.
For each choice of $(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}})$
(four choices), there is exactly one $(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)$ satisfying
this. So **4 of the 16 characters give $\Pi=+1$, the other 12 give
$\Pi=0$**.

The four non-zero characters:

| $(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)$ | $\Pi^{T^4}$ |
|---|---|
| $(+,+,+,+)$ | $+1$ |
| $(+,-,-,-)$ | $+1$ |
| $(-,+,-,-)$ | $+1$ |
| $(-,-,+,+)$ | $+1$ |

### 3.3 The 16-term unsigned identity (the wrong identity)

$$
\sum_{\vec\epsilon\in(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4}\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}
\;=\; 4\cdot 1 + 12\cdot 0 \;=\; 4.
$$

**This is not $0$.** The naive unsigned sum over all 16 characters is
$4$, not $\chi(\mathcal O_{T^4}) = 0$. This is the key subtlety the
attack exposes: the Lefschetz identity $\sum \Pi = \chi(\mathcal O)$
holds for the **signed** sum (with the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$-weights), not for the unsigned
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$-sum. The unsigned $4$ is a **different invariant**:
it counts the dimension of the $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$-fixed subspace on the
diagonal of $\operatorname{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, which equals
$\dim H^{0,0}(T^4) + \dim H^{2,2}(T^4) + 2\dim H^{1,1,\mathrm{symm}}(T^4)$
or some related Hodge-fixed quantity.

### 3.4 The 16-term signed Lefschetz identity (the right identity)

Weight by $(-1)^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=-] + [\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}$:

$$
\sum_{\vec\epsilon}(-1)^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]+[\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}
\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}
\;=\; \Bigl(\sum_{\vec\eta_1}(-1)^{[\eta_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]+[\eta_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}
\Pi^E_{\vec\eta_1}\Bigr)
\Bigl(\sum_{\vec\eta_2}(-1)^{[\eta_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]+[\eta_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}
\Pi^E_{\vec\eta_2}\Bigr).
$$

Each $E$-factor signed sum equals $\chi(\mathcal O_E) = 0$ by the
elliptic curve Lefschetz identity (an instance of the standard
$E$-bigraded analysis). So the product is

$$
\boxed{\;
\sum_{\vec\epsilon}(-1)^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]+[\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}
\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}
\;=\; \chi(\mathcal O_E)^2
\;=\; 0^2
\;=\; 0
\;=\; \chi(\mathcal O_{T^4}).
\;}
$$

Direct check using §3.2: the four non-zero characters with their
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$-signs are

| $(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)$ | $\Pi$ | sign | contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(+,+,+,+)$ | $+1$ | $+$ | $+1$ |
| $(+,-,-,-)$ | $+1$ | $-$ | $-1$ |
| $(-,+,-,-)$ | $+1$ | $-$ | $-1$ |
| $(-,-,+,+)$ | $+1$ | $+$ | $+1$ |

Sum: $+1 - 1 - 1 + 1 = 0$. ✓ matches both
$\chi(\mathcal O_{T^4}) = 0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(M_{T^4}) = 2 + 0 + 0 - 2 = 0$.

**This is the corrected 16-term Lefschetz identity.**

## 4. The reduction map $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4 \twoheadrightarrow V_4$

The Künneth / $V_4$-presentation collapses the four involutions to two
by **gauging** the Hodge involutions $\varepsilon_{\omega_1},
\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$ into the universal pair via factor-wise Serre
conjugacy.

### 4.1 The projection

The natural projection $\pi : (\mathbb{Z}/2)^4 \twoheadrightarrow V_4$
that is compatible with Künneth is the **factor-wise** one: regard
$(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4 = (\mathbb{Z}/2)^3_{E_1} \times_{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2_{\mathrm{univ}}}
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3_{E_2}$ (factoring as two copies of the $E$-character
group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3$ glued along the universal $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$),
and project each $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3_{E_i}$ to the $V_4$ of $E_i$ by
collapsing $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\sim\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ via
the Serre conjugacy of §3.1. This corresponds to the change of
variables

$$
\pi : (\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)
\;\longmapsto\;
(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\;\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_1\epsilon_2)
$$

on character labels (not on the trace itself --- the trace transforms
under the dual map). The kernel
$K := \ker\pi = \{\vec\epsilon : \epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=+ \text{ and }
\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_1\epsilon_2=+\}$ has order $4$,
generated by $\varepsilon_{\omega_1}\varepsilon_{\omega_2}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\varepsilon_{\omega_1}$.

**Geometric interpretation.** The kernel $K$ encodes the two factor-wise
Serre conjugacies (one per $E_i$) that are **invisible** at the $V_4$
level but **visible** at $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$.

### 4.2 Recovering $M_{T^4}$ from the 16-term data via Künneth convolution

The push-forward of the over-saturated trace $\widetilde M_{T^4}\in
\mathbb{Z}[(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4]$ to $V_4$ is computed not by direct sum
along fibres of $\pi$, but by **Künneth convolution** of the
push-forwards of each $E$-factor:

$$
M_{T^4}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
\;=\;
\sum_{(\delta_1,\delta_2)\in V_4}\;
M_E^{(\delta_1,\delta_2)}\cdot M_E^{(\alpha+\delta_1,\beta+\delta_2)}
\;=\; (M_E * M_E)^{(\alpha,\beta)}.
$$

This reproduces exactly the Klein-four convolution formula of
`T4_bigraded_Lefschetz_kunneth.md` §1, giving $M_{T^4}=(2,0,0,-2)$.

### 4.3 Sanity check: trace consistency

* $\operatorname{tr}(M_{T^4}) = M^{(+,+)} + M^{(+,-)} + M^{(-,+)} + M^{(-,-)}
  = 2 + 0 + 0 + (-2) = 0 = \chi(\mathcal O_{T^4})$ ✓
* This matches the signed 16-term sum of §3.4: $\chi(\mathcal O_E)^2 = 0$ ✓

### 4.4 Why the unsigned and signed sums differ

The unsigned 16-term sum of §3.3 gives $4$, not $0$. The reason is that
the unsigned sum corresponds to the **unsigned push-forward**
$\sum_{\vec\epsilon}\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}$, which equals
$(\sum_{\vec\eta}\Pi^E_{\vec\eta})^2 = 2^2 = 4$ (each $E$-factor
unsigned sum is $2$, the Hodge-fixed dimension of $\Phi(E)$). This is
the *Hodge-fixed dimension* on the diagonal, **not** $\chi(\mathcal O)$.
The Künneth-multiplicative invariant $\chi(\mathcal O_{T^4})=0$ requires
the $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$-signed sum,
since each $E$-factor contributes $\chi(\mathcal O_E)=0$ under that
weighting.

In short: $\chi(\mathcal O)$ is **multiplicative** under $\times$
($0\cdot 0 = 0$), not **additive**, and the $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$
over-saturation respects this exactly because the signed sum factors as
a product over $E$-factors.

## 6. Ghost theorem (AP-CY61 extraction)

The clash between $V_4$ and $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4$ is not a contradiction;
it is the shadow of the following genuine theorem:

**Theorem (Hodge-piece refinement of bigraded Lefschetz).** For a CY
$d$-fold $X$ with $h^{1,0}(X) = r$, the bigraded Lefschetz matrix
$M_X$ admits a canonical refinement $\widetilde M_X$ valued in
$\mathbb{Z}[(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2+r}]$, where the $r$ extra
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-factors are the Hodge-piece involutions
$\varepsilon_{\omega_1},\dots,\varepsilon_{\omega_r}$ from $H^{1,0}(X)$.
The reduction
$\pi:(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2+r}\twoheadrightarrow V_4$ collapses the $r$
Hodge-piece factors into the universal $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ via
Serre conjugacy:
$\pi(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_r)
= (\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\prod_i\epsilon_i)$,
and the $V_4$-image $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$ recovers the standard
matrix via signed push-forward weighted by
$(-1)^{[\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}=-]+[\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}=-]}$.

Special cases:

* $X = K3$: $r = 0$. $\widetilde M_{K3} = M_{K3} = (0,5,-16,13)$ is
  already $V_4$-valued; no over-saturation.
* $X = E$: $r = 1$. $\widetilde M_E$ valued in $\mathbb{Z}[(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3]$;
  Serre kernel of size $2$ pushes forward to $M_E = (1,0,0,-1)$.
* $X = T^4$: $r = 2$. $\widetilde M_{T^4}$ valued in $\mathbb{Z}[(\mathbb{Z}/2)^4]$;
  4 non-zero characters listed in §3.2; Serre kernel of size $4$ pushes
  forward to $M_{T^4} = (2,0,0,-2)$ via Künneth convolution (§4.2).
* $X$ = abelian surface with $h^{1,0}=2$: same as $T^4$ via Künneth.
  *Independent verification target.*

**Implication for asymmetric Drinfeld coupling.** For asymmetric
products $X\times Y$ with $r_X\neq r_Y$, the Drinfeld coupling
correction $\Delta_{X,Y}$ of `T4_bigraded_Lefschetz_kunneth.md` §4
admits a Hodge-piece interpretation: it measures the failure of
factor-wise Serre conjugacies to commute with Künneth convolution,
which fails precisely when the over-saturation ranks $r_X,r_Y$ differ.

For $K3\times E$: $r_{K3}=0$, $r_E=1$; ranks differ; $\Delta\neq 0$
predicted (matches the computed $\Delta_{K3,E}=(13,-16,5,-2)$).

For $T^4\times E$: $r_{T^4}=2$, $r_E=1$; ranks differ but both $\geq 1$;
$\Delta$ predicted possibly non-zero, but `T4_bigraded_Lefschetz_kunneth.md`
§5 conjectured $\Delta_{T^4,E}=0$ on the basis of both being
$\sigma^*_{\mathrm{tot}}$-anti-symmetric. The Hodge-piece refinement
predicts a correction at the $\widetilde M$ level that vanishes after
push-forward to $V_4$ when $r_X+r_Y$ is odd or both are non-zero --- a
sharper criterion that subsumes the $\sigma^*_{\mathrm{tot}}$-anti-symmetry
condition. *This refinement is the ghost theorem, conjectural in
general.*

For $K3\times T^4$: $r_{K3}=0$, $r_{T^4}=2$; ranks differ; $\Delta\neq 0$
predicted, matching `T4_bigraded_Lefschetz_kunneth.md` §5's
$\Delta_{K3,T^4}=(13,-16,5,-2)$ (same as $K3\times E$, which is
explained by the over-saturation prediction that only the *parity* of
$r$ matters at the $V_4$-projection).
