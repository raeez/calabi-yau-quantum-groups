\subsection*{Inventory}

Slides 200--300 house the \emph{single} technical span that Waves
13--14 skipped: the passage from $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$ acting
on $\wedge^2(\mathbb{Z}^4)$ to the orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+$
acting on the Type-IV symmetric domain $\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_+$, and the
attendant reflection calculus that distinguishes $W^{(2)}_{\mathrm{I}}$
(under which $\Delta_5$ is invariant) from $W^{(2)}_{\mathrm{II}}$
(under which $\Delta_5$ is anti-invariant). No Fourier--Jacobi expansion
appears here; the FJ material sits earlier (lines 100--200, Wave~14~Q1).
What slides 200--300 carry is the \emph{lattice skeleton} that will, at
line~\textasciitilde 720, force the denominator formula to read as a
Weyl--Kac--Borcherds sum indexed by $W^{(2)}_{\mathrm{II}}$.

\subsection*{The bilinear-form construction}

Let $\Lambda^4 = \bigoplus_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{Z}e_i$. The Pfaffian pairing
\[
u \wedge v = (u,v)\, e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge e_4 \in \wedge^4\Lambda^4
\]
is $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{Z})$-invariant, integral, symmetric, even, unimodular,
of signature $(3,3)$ on $\wedge^2\Lambda^4$. Fixing the skew form
$q = e_1\wedge e_3 + e_2\wedge e_4$, the scalar
$B_q(x,y)e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\wedge e_4 = -x\wedge y\wedge q$
defines the integral symplectic form preserved by $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$.
The stabiliser of $q$ inside $\mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{Z})$ is therefore
\[
\{g \in \mathrm{SL}_4(\mathbb{Z})\,:\, (g\wedge g)(q) = q\}
\simeq \mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z}).
\]

\subsection*{The orthogonal complement $\Lambda^{3,2}$}

Inside $\wedge^2\Lambda^4$ one takes
\[
\Lambda^{3,2} \;=\; (e_1\wedge e_3 + e_2\wedge e_4)^{\perp}
\;\simeq\; \Lambda^{1,1}\oplus\Lambda^{1,1}\oplus[2],
\]
the even lattice whose Gram matrix in $(f_i)$-basis is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\oplus\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\oplus[2]$
with basis
\[
f_1 = e_1\wedge e_2,\quad f_2 = e_2\wedge e_3,\quad
f_3 = e_1\wedge e_3 - e_2\wedge e_4,\quad
f_{-2} = e_4\wedge e_1,\quad f_{-1} = e_4\wedge e_3.
\]
The square-$2$ coordinate is $f_3$; the two hyperbolic planes carry
$f_{\pm 1}, f_{\pm 2}$.

\subsection*{The Type-IV domain and its $\Imag$-positivity}

The real orthogonal group $\mathrm{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Lambda^{3,2})$
acts on
\[
\cH^{\mathrm{IV}} = \bigl\{Z\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{3,2}\otimes\mathbb{C})
\,:\, (Z,Z)=0,\ (Z,\overline{Z})<0\bigr\}
= \cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_{+}\cup\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_{-}.
\]
In the $(f_i)$-basis a point of $\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_{+}$ is
$Z = ((z_2^2 - z_1 z_3),\, z_3,\, z_2,\, z_1,\, 1)\cdot z_0$ with
$\Imag(z_1)>0$. The isotropy relation $(Z,Z)=0$ is automatic; the
negativity $(Z,\overline{Z})<0$ reduces to
\[
y_1 y_3 - y_2^{\,2} > 0, \qquad y_i = \Imag z_i,
\]
the classical Siegel upper-half-plane-of-genus-$2$ condition written as
a positive-definite imaginary part of the $2\times 2$ matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z_2&z_3\\z_1&z_2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The index-$2$ subgroup $\mathrm{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+$ is the
one fixing $\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_+$; its kernel on $\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_\pm$
is $\pm I_5$, and since $\Lambda^{3,2}$ is odd-dimensional,
$\mathrm{O}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+ = \pm I_5\cdot
\mathrm{SO}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+$.

\subsection*{The $\wedge^2$-isomorphism (Lemma~1)}

Lifting generators of $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})$ to $\wedge^2\Lambda^4$
and comparing kernels yields the isomorphism
\[
\wedge^2\,:\, \mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})/\{\pm I_4\}
\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \mathrm{SO}_+(\Lambda^{3,2})
\;\simeq\; \mathrm{O}(\Lambda^{3,2})_+/\{\pm I_5\}.
\]
This is the precise transfer that promotes the Igusa cusp form
$\Delta_5$ on $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}_2$ to a
reflective modular form on $\cH^{\mathrm{IV}}_+$ and makes the Borcherds
product expansion meaningful.

\subsection*{Reflections and the sign of $\Delta_5$ (Lemma~2)}

Fix the primitive hyperbolic sublattice
$\Lambda^{2,1} = \Lambda^{1,1}\oplus[2] \simeq \mathbb{Z}f_2\oplus\mathbb{Z}f_3
\oplus\mathbb{Z}f_{-2}$. For $\alpha\in\Lambda^{2,1}$ primitive with
$(\alpha,\alpha)>0$ and $(\alpha,\alpha)\mid 2(\Lambda^{2,1},\alpha)$
the reflection is
\[
s_{\alpha}(x) = x - \frac{2(x,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha, \qquad
\alpha^2 = 2 \;\Rightarrow\; s_{\alpha}(x) = x - (x,\alpha)\alpha.
\]
The Lemma splits the square-$2$ roots into two classes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\alpha\in\{\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3,\ \delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3,\
\delta_3 = f_3\}$: \quad $\Delta_5(s_\alpha z) = -\Delta_5(z)$,
\item $\alpha\in\{f_{-2}-f_2,\ f_2-f_3,\ f_2+f_3\}$: \quad
$\Delta_5(s_\alpha z) = +\Delta_5(z)$.
\end{itemize}
The three anti-invariant generators $\{\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\}$
are the real simple roots of the GKM superalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$;
their Gram matrix is $2$ on the diagonal and $-2$ off-diagonal, the
hyperbolic Cartan matrix that controls $W^{(2)}_{\mathrm{II}}$.
The proof sets $U\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, lifts via
$\wedge^2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&U\\{}^tU^{-1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and applies Maass' explicit multiplier formula. The three invariant
reflections generate $W^{(2)}_{\mathrm{I}}$, the discrete subgroup under
which $\Delta_5$ descends to a well-defined form.

\subsection*{Triadic $\kappaBKM = 5$ witness}

The three $\delta_i$ of Lemma~2 are the same three that, in
Lorgat~2020 (memory file, lattice-and-Weyl-data block), generate the
Weyl vector $\rho = \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
= f_2 - \tfrac{1}{2}f_3 + f_{-2}$. The Borcherds denominator identity
\[
\Delta_5(z) \;=\; e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}
\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}\bigl(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}
\]
reads the weight $5$ of $\Delta_5$ directly off the half-Weyl-vector
norm; independently, Borcherds' weight theorem gives
$\kappaBKM(\Phi_1) = c_1(0)/2 = 10/2 = 5$ from $\phi_{0,1}$; and the
$\wedge^2$-transfer of slides 200--300 certifies that these two
$5$'s live on the \emph{same} modular form, not on different lifts.
Three independent paths, one value: $\kappaBKM = 5$.
