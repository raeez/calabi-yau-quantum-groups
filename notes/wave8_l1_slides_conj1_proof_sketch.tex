\section*{Lorgat 2020 Conjecture~1: automorphic boundary}

\paragraph{Conjecture.}
Let $X=(S\times E)/\langle(g_N,t_N)\rangle$ be a diagonal quotient of
an elliptic K3 surface and an elliptic curve, let $L$ be a K3 lattice
polarisation, and let $h_M$ be a commuting finite-order twist. Lorgat
2020 Conjecture~1 asserts that the diagonal-divisor Siegel paramodular
forms in the Gritsenko-Cl\'ery range arise, up to scalar, as
reciprocal square-roots of the $L$-polarised, $h_M$-twisted
Donaldson-Thomas series $\ZLh$, and that these forms are denominator
functions of generalized Borcherds-Kac-Moody superalgebras whose root
multiplicities are supplied by the $(g_N,h_M)$-twisted K3 elliptic
genera.

The assertion has three layers. The automorphic layer is the
classification and Borcherds-lift construction of paramodular forms.
The curve-counting layer is the reduced, twisted Donaldson-Thomas
identity in the same normalisation. The Lie-superalgebra layer is an
explicit root datum, Weyl vector, parity split, multiplicity rule, and
Weyl-Kac-Borcherds denominator identity. The first layer gives a
modular form; the third layer gives a BKM superalgebra.

\paragraph{The primitive \texorpdfstring{$\Delta_5$}{Delta5} row.}
Let $\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$ be the Eichler-Zagier weak Jacobi form
of weight $0$ and index $1$, normalised by
\[
  \phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\big|_{q^0}=\zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta,
  \qquad c_1(0)=10.
\]
The geometric K3 elliptic genus is
\[
  \operatorname{Ell}(K3;\tau,z)=2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}(\tau,z),
\]
while the primitive Borcherds product producing $\Delta_5$ uses the
single Eichler-Zagier form. Borcherds' singular-theta weight theorem
(Borcherds 1998, Theorem~13.3) gives
\[
  \kBKM(\gDelta)=\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)=\frac{c_1(0)}{2}=5.
\]
Gritsenko-Nikulin 1995 Part~II, Theorem~2.1, and Borcherds' product
formalism construct the generalized BKM superalgebra $\gDelta$ with
primitive denominator
\[
  \operatorname{den}(\gDelta)=D_5(2Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z),
\]
with signed root multiplicities read from the Fourier coefficients of
$\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$.

Oberdieck-Pandharipande 2016, Theorem~1, in the Oberdieck-Pixton
normalisation, gives the reduced scalar branch
\[
  Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}
  =-\bigl(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}\bigr)^{-1}
  =-D_5^{-2}
  =-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2},
  \qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
The unnormalised Igusa square is $\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2$.
Thus the primitive row is proved at the automorphic denominator level
and at the reduced scalar level. Compact Hall recognition and
higher-level root data are additional structures.

\paragraph{The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery catalogue.}
Gritsenko-Cl\'ery 2008, Theorem~1.4, classifies the Siegel modular
forms of genus $2$ whose divisor is the diagonal
$\mathcal H_1\subset\mathbb H_2$ with vanishing order $1$. The complete
list is indexed by triples
\[
 (t,N;k)\in
 \bigl\{(1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
 (4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1)\bigr\}.
\]
The weights are
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
and the twice-weight tuple is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
Gritsenko-Cl\'ery 2008, Theorem~3.1, gives the corresponding
cusp-weighted Borcherds product formula row by row. The catalogue
overlaps the primitive CHL row at $F_{1,1}=\Delta_5$. Its remaining
rows live at their own paramodular levels, multiplier systems, Jacobi
inputs, and branches.

\paragraph{Borcherds weights on the CHL ladder.}
Let $\Phi_N^{\mathrm{CHL}}$ denote the primitive CHL denominator form at
order $N$. For
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
Gritsenko-Nikulin 1995 Part~II, Theorem~2.1, Gritsenko 1999,
Corollary~3.3, and Borcherds 1998, Theorem~13.3, give the
Borcherds-input constants and BKM weights
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))
  =(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
\[
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_1^{\mathrm{CHL}}),\kBKM(\Phi_2^{\mathrm{CHL}}),
  \kBKM(\Phi_3^{\mathrm{CHL}}),\kBKM(\Phi_4^{\mathrm{CHL}}),
  \kBKM(\Phi_6^{\mathrm{CHL}})\bigr)
  =(5,4,3,2,1),
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{CHL}})=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
This is the CHL denominator table. It is independent of the
Gritsenko-Cl\'ery twice-weight tuple
$(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)$ away from the shared $\Delta_5$ entry.

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3xE} invariants.}
At the compact $K3\times E$ boundary the subscripted values are
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\gDelta)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=24,
\]
with distinct constructions:
\[
  \chi(\mathcal O_{K3\times E})
  =\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0,
\]
\[
  \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)=1-1+1-1=0,
\]
\[
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\gDelta)=c_1(0)/2=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\mathbb Z)=24.
\]
The compact Hodge-supertrace value, the Heisenberg-Mukai
specialisation, the Borcherds denominator weight, and the K3 Mukai
lattice rank are separate invariants.

\paragraph{The order-six quotient.}
At $N=6$ the CHL denominator is anchored by the Niemeier root system
$6D_4$. Nikulin 1979, Theorem~4.5, in the fixed-point tabulation
reproduced by Xiao 1996, gives
\[
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6)=2,\qquad
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6^2)=6,\qquad
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6^3)=8.
\]
Thus $K3/\langle g_6\rangle$ has two stabiliser-$\mathbb Z_6$ orbits,
two stabiliser-$\mathbb Z_3$ orbits, and two
stabiliser-$\mathbb Z_2$ orbits. A cyclic stabiliser of order $m$
contributes a Du~Val singularity of type $A_{m-1}$, so the quotient
surface has local type
\[
  2A_5+2A_2+2A_1,
\]
and the Euler check is
\[
  e(K3/\mathbb Z_6)=\frac{24+2+6+8+6+2}{6}=8,\qquad
  8+(2\cdot5+2\cdot2+2\cdot1)=24.
\]
The label $6D_4$ is a global positive-definite even unimodular
rank-$24$ Niemeier datum. The ADE string above is the local analytic
stratification of the quotient surface.

\paragraph{Higher-level BKM data.}
For each $N\geq2$ row in Conjecture~1, the following data must
be supplied before the paramodular form determines a BKM superalgebra:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{root lattice} & \Lambda_N\ \text{with positive cone and real simple roots},\\
\text{Weyl vector} & \rho_N\ \text{compatible with the product prefactor},\\
\text{parity split} &
  \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{N,\bar0}\sqcup\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{N,\bar1},\\
\text{multiplicities} &
  \mathrm{sdim}\,\mathfrak g_{N,\alpha}
  =c_N\bigl((\alpha,\alpha)/2,\ell(\alpha)\bigr),\\
\text{denominator identity} &
  \Phi_N(Z)=e^{-(\rho_N,Z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_N^+}(1-e^{-(\alpha,Z)})^{
  \mathrm{sdim}\,\mathfrak g_{N,\alpha}}\\
&\hfill
  =\sum_{w\in W_N}\det(w)\,w\!\left(e^{-\rho_N}
  \sum_{a\in\mathcal I_N}\epsilon_N(a)e^{-a}\right).
\end{array}
\]
The automorphic product theorem supplies the product side after the
Jacobi input, multiplier system, chamber, and normalisation have been
fixed. The Weyl-sum side is the Lie-superalgebra construction.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery classification proves the eight
diagonal-divisor paramodular forms and their weights. Borcherds'
Theorem~13.3 proves the weight formula $c(0)/2$ for a Borcherds product
after the weak Jacobi input is fixed. The $\Delta_5$ row has a constructed
generalized BKM superalgebra with denominator $\Delta_5$.  The CHL
constant table
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{CHL}})\bigr)_{N=1,2,3,4,6}
  =(5,4,3,2,1),
\]
is an automorphic weight table.

For $N\geq2$, Conjecture~1 is a construction problem: build the root
datum, Weyl vector, parity rule, signed multiplicities, and denominator
identity for the asserted superalgebra, and identify the resulting
denominator with the appropriate Gritsenko-Cl\'ery or CHL paramodular
form in a fixed normalisation. The Donaldson-Thomas layer requires a
reduced, twisted $K3\times E$ theory whose scalar is $\ZLh^{-1/2}$ in
the same normalisation. These data are the exact proof obligations.
