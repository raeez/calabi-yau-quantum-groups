\section*{Eight Orders, CHL Denominators, and Scalar Squares}

\paragraph{Translation quotients.}
Let \(S\) be a K3 surface with a symplectic automorphism \(g_N\) of
order \(N\leq8\), let \(E\) be an elliptic curve, and choose a torsion
point \(e_0\in E\) of exact order \(N\). The diagonal action
\[
  (s,e)\longmapsto(g_Ns,e+e_0)
\]
of \(\ZZ/N\ZZ\) on \(S\times E\) is free, since translation by
\(e_0\) acts freely on \(E\). It preserves the holomorphic \(3\)-form,
so
\[
  X_N=(S\times E)/(\ZZ/N\ZZ)
\]
is a smooth \(K\)-trivial threefold for every Mukai-admissible cyclic
order \(N\leq8\). The quotient follows the holomorphic-volume
Calabi--Yau convention. Since \(g_N\) is symplectic on \(S\) and
translation is trivial on \(H^{0,\bullet}(E)\),
\[
  H^{0,\bullet}(X_N)
  \cong
  \bigl(H^{0,\bullet}(S)\otimes H^{0,\bullet}(E)\bigr)^{\ZZ/N\ZZ},
  \qquad
  (h^{0,0},h^{0,1},h^{0,2},h^{0,3})=(1,1,1,1).
\]
Thus \(\kcat(X_N)=\chi(\cO_{X_N})=0\). The strict convention
\(h^{0,1}=h^{0,2}=0\) belongs to quotients with additional geometry; this
translation quotient has the displayed nonzero \(h^{0,1}\) and
\(h^{0,2}\).

The translation quotient supplies a smooth \(K\)-trivial host. The
primitive \(K3\times E\) CHL denominator ladder below is a separate
automorphic input.

\paragraph{Reduced DT series.}
When \(S\to\PP^1\) is elliptically fibered and an \(N\)-torsion section
is fixed, let \(s_1,\ldots,s_N\) be its translates and let \(F\) be the
fibre class. Put
\[
  \beta_h=\frac{s_1+\cdots+s_N+hF}{N}.
\]
The reduced DT series has the convention
\[
  Z^{X_N}(q,t,p)
  =
  \sum_{h\geq0}\sum_{d\geq0}\sum_{n\in\ZZ}
  \mathrm{DT}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}
  q^{d-1}t^{\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle/2}(-p)^n ,
\]
where \(\mathrm{DT}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}\) is the Behrend-weighted Euler
characteristic of the Hilbert scheme modulo the elliptic translation
action. The monomial \(q^{d-1}t^{\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle/2}(-p)^n\)
is part of the definition.

\paragraph{The \(N=1\) scalar.}
For \(X_1=S\times E\), the Oberdieck--Pandharipande reduced-DT theorem
in the monic scalar normalisation gives
\[
  \PhiTenOP=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\DeltaFive^2,
  \qquad
  \ZOP=-(\PhiTenOP)^{-1}
  =-\Dfive^{-2}=-4096\,\DeltaFive^{-2},
  \qquad
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\DeltaFive .
\]
The primitive square coordinate is
\[
  \PhiTenUN=\DeltaFive^2 .
\]
The primitive Borcherds denominator uses the Eichler--Zagier input
\(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\), with \(c_1(0)=10\), and gives
\[
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=c_1(0)/2=5 .
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is \(2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\) and lands on the
square \(\PhiTenUN\). The OP scalar records the inverse square of the
monic coordinate \(\Dfive\). The primitive root datum of \(\gDelta\)
uses the Gritsenko--Nikulin/Kac denominator theorem, the Lorentzian
lattice, real simple roots, imaginary simple roots, parity, and Weyl
vector.

\paragraph{\(K3\times E\) constants.}
For the compact total space and its distinguished specialisations,
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kchHeis(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3\times E)=\kfib(K3)=24 .
\]
The first equality is K\"unneth multiplicativity:
\(\chi(\cO_{K3\times E})=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot0\).
The second is the compact Hodge supertrace on the total space. The
Heisenberg value, the Borcherds weight, and the fibre rank are separate
constructions, with independent definitions and independent verification
paths.

\paragraph{Primitive CHL denominator ladder.}
The primitive CHL denominator levels are
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
For \(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{CHL}}\) the Borcherds input constants and modular
characteristics are
\[
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{CHL}})=c_N(0)/2=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
Borcherds's multiplicative-lift theorem gives the weight as half the
constant term of the weak Jacobi input. The table is indexed by
primitive compact CHL denominator data, with Jacobi input, product
normalisation, and denominator algebra fixed at each row. Cyclic
translation quotients form a different index.

\paragraph{Nikulin order extension.}
For symplectic K3 automorphisms of the remaining Mukai-admissible
orders, let \(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}}\) denote the order-indexed
Borcherds product of the \(g_N\)-twined K3 Jacobi input. Its constants
and automorphic weights are
\[
  (c_5(0),c_7(0),c_8(0))=(4,2,2),
  \qquad
  \bigl(\wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}})\bigr)_{N=5,7,8}
  =c_N(0)/2=(2,1,1).
\]
These three rows give Borcherds-weight data. A statement with
\(\kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}})\) requires a specified BKM denominator
algebra; after denominator recognition the corresponding \(\kBKM\)-values
are \((2,1,1)\). A CY-to-chiral realisation requires a supplied
\(E_1\)-chiral host, comparison map, and denominator identity.

\paragraph{The Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue.}
The simplest-divisor diagonal-divisor catalogue is the separate
triple-indexed list
\[
 (t,N;k)=
 (1,1;5),(2,1;2),(1,2;3),(3,1;1),(1,3;2),
 (4,1;\tfrac12),(1,4;\tfrac32),(2,2;1).
\]
Its weights are
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
and its twice-weight constants are
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The common entry with the primitive CHL ladder is \((1,1;5)\), the
\(\DeltaFive\) row. Every other comparison requires a row-map theorem
preserving the Jacobi input, paramodular group, character, divisor
multiplicity, Weyl chamber, and product normalisation.

\paragraph{Hall and chiral layers.}
For \(d\geq3\), the native CY-to-chiral output is an \(E_1\)-chiral
algebra; \(E_2\) appears only on a centre, double, or module category
after that layer has been constructed. On the affine toric chart,
\[
  \CoHA(\bC^3)=Y^+(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1),
\]
the positive Hall half of Schiffmann--Vasserot. The full
\(\cW_{1+\infty}\) vertex algebra is reached only after the
Drinfeld double, centre, completion, and evaluation steps. The
Hall--Drinfeld/BKM branch attached to \(\DeltaFive\) is distinct from
the Mukai Yangian branch.

\paragraph{Twisted denominators.}
A formula of the form
\[
  -\bigl(Z^X_{L,h_M}(q,t,p)\bigr)^{-1}
  =
  \text{Siegel denominator}
\]
contains these data: the lattice polarisation \(L\), a commuting pair
\((g_N,h_M)\), a Jacobi input, a product normalisation, orientation
data, and a proof that the reduced-DT scalar equals the chosen
automorphic product. A generalized BKM algebra
\(\mathfrak g_{(g_N,h_M)}\) contains a Lorentzian root lattice, real
simple roots, Weyl vector, imaginary simple roots, parity split, and
denominator identity. A modular form supplies scalar automorphic data;
a physical interpretation supplies a heuristic until it is identified
with a BPS index or a reduced-DT theorem.

The translation-quotient row is geometric. The OP row is a scalar

extension. The Gritsenko--Clery row is the diagonal-divisor
classification.
Identifying two rows is a theorem with its own data.
