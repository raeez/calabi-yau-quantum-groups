\section*{Agent BL-5.  MO residue path on $\C^3$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3) = 0$ identically}
\label{sec:bl-5-mo-c3-vanishing}

\subsection*{Single-sentence statement}

The Maulik--Okounkov residue reading of $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ at
the base point $\Perf(\C^3)$ yields zero identically, by absence of a
Lorentzian chamber wall that any Borcherds denominator would attach
to, and this matches both Path 1 (bar-complex supertrace) and Path 2
(modular-operadic factorisation homology), giving a three-path
base-case sanity for the wave master claim
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$.

\subsection*{Data and notation}

Fix $V = \C^3$ with coordinates $(x, y, z)$ and the three-torus
$T = (\C^\times)^3$ acting by diagonal rescaling. Write
$\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3 \in H^*_T(\mathrm{pt}; \Q) =
\Q[\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3]$ for the equivariant weights,
with the Calabi--Yau constraint
\[
 \epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 \;=\; 0
\]
cutting out the rank-two sub-$T$ preserving
$dx \wedge dy \wedge dz$. Write
\[
 \mathfrak t^*_\C \;=\; \Q(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2) \otimes_\Q \C,
 \qquad u \in \mathfrak t^*_\C.
\]
Throughout $\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\Perf(\C^3)) = \CoHA(\C^3) =
\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is the Schiffmann--Vasserot theorem
(Theorem~$1.1$ of \textit{Publ. Math. IHES} $118$, $2013$), with
explicit rational shuffle kernel
\[
 \phi_{\mathrm{SV}}(u, v)
 \;=\;
 \frac{(u - v + \epsilon_1)(u - v + \epsilon_2)(u - v + \epsilon_3)}
      {(u - v)(u - v + \epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2)(u - v + \epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3)}.
\]
Under CY the kernel simplifies: $\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 =
-\epsilon_3$, $\epsilon_2 + \epsilon_3 = -\epsilon_1$, so the
denominator reads $(u - v)(u - v - \epsilon_3)(u - v - \epsilon_1)$.

\subsection*{Ghost theorems}

The five adversarial attacks on the statement
``$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3) = 0$ identically'' each
produce a ghost theorem. The stable core survives all five.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(G\arabic*)}]
 \item \emph{Residue-mismatch ghost.} The residue
 $\mathrm{Res}_{u = v} \phi_{\mathrm{SV}}(u, v)$ on the CY locus is
 $\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2 \epsilon_3 / (-\epsilon_3)(-\epsilon_1)$,
 manifestly non-zero. The claim that the MO residue vanishes would
 contradict the SV shuffle computation.
 \item \emph{Chamber-arrangement ghost.} The MO chamber arrangement
 on $\Spec H^*_T(\mathrm{pt})$ is non-empty (indeed, it has $6 = 3!$
 chambers, one per ordering of $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$),
 and the wall structure supports non-trivial transition matrices.
 The MO $R$-matrix is non-zero.
 \item \emph{CoHA-vs-BKM wall-distinction ghost.} The CoHA wall
 $u = v$ (at which $\phi_{\mathrm{SV}}$ has a pole) is not the
 BKM-denominator wall. Any claim equating the two ignores the
 structural difference between the SV shuffle wall (coordinate-plane
 trace) and the Borcherds wall (Lorentzian root-hyperplane).
 \item \emph{Lattice-input ghost.} The Borcherds denominator product
 $\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta_+}(1 - e^{2\pi i (\alpha, z)})^{c(\alpha)}$
 requires an even Lorentzian lattice of signature $(r, 1)$ with $r
 \geq 1$. For $\Perf(\C^3)$ the underlying lattice of numerical
 classes is $K_0^{\mathrm{num}}(\Perf(\C^3)) = \Z$, with degenerate
 Mukai pairing (all coherent sheaves on $\C^3$ are cohomologically
 trivial in odd degree; the Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch pairing on
 $K_0$ is trivial).
 \item \emph{Dimension-dependence ghost.} At $d = 3$, Pantev--To\"en--
 Vaqui\'e--Vezzosi attaches to a CY$_3$ category a $(-1)$-shifted
 symplectic structure; the Gerstenhaber bracket has degree
 $+1 - d = -2$. The BKM datum is typically associated to $d = 2$
 inputs (K3, $K3 \times E$) through Borcherds lifts of weight $0$
 Jacobi forms. The very assignment
 $\Phi_3(\Perf(\C^3)) \leadsto \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ seems to jump
 dimensions.
\end{enumerate}

Each of (G1)--(G5) is addressed and resolved below. The resolution
of (G3)+(G4) pins the stable core; (G5) clarifies the scope.

\subsection*{Attack 1.  Residue-mismatch}

\paragraph{Attack.} $\phi_{\mathrm{SV}}(u, v)$ has a pole at $u = v$
of residue $\epsilon_1 \epsilon_2 \epsilon_3 / \bigl[(-\epsilon_3)
(-\epsilon_1)\bigr] = -\epsilon_2$. This is non-zero on the CY locus
for generic $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$. If the MO $R$-matrix on $\C^3$
is the residue of $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ at $u = v$, the $R$-matrix
is non-trivial, contradicting any claim that the output of Path 3 is
``zero''.

\paragraph{Ghost.} The MO $R$-matrix on $\C^3$ is
\[
 R^{\mathrm{MO}}(u) \;=\; \mathrm{Res}_{u = 0}
 \phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(u) \;=\; -\epsilon_2 \cdot \mathrm{id}_{\Yplus
 \otimes \Yplus} \;+\; (\text{higher shuffle contributions}),
\]
which generates a non-trivial braided-monoidal structure on
$\Rep^{E_2}(Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathrm{ch}}(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)))$.

\paragraph{Heal.} The ghost is correct, and it is the right
answer for a different question. The $R^{\mathrm{MO}}(u)$ above is
the Maulik--Okounkov stable-envelope $R$-matrix on $\C^3$ in the
sense of braided monoidal structure on $\Rep(\Yplus)$ -- that is the
output of Path 3 read as \emph{representation-category} output. The
input to $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$, however, is not a representation
category but a Borcherds automorphic denominator product attached to
a Lorentzian lattice. The CoHA wall $\{u = v\}$ that supports
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ and the BKM wall $\{\alpha \in \Delta_+ :
(\alpha, z) = 0\}$ are different geometrical objects, living on
different spaces:
\begin{itemize}
 \item $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ lives on
 $\mathfrak t^*_\C = \Spec H^*_T(\mathrm{pt}) \otimes_\Q \C$, the
 \emph{equivariant moduli} -- a $\C^2$ on the CY locus (coordinates
 $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2$; with $\epsilon_3 = -\epsilon_1 -
 \epsilon_2$).
 \item The BKM denominator product lives on a tube domain of type
 $\mathrm{IV}_n$ attached to a Lorentzian lattice $\Lambda$ of
 signature $(r, 1)$; its walls are images of real-root reflection
 hyperplanes in the Weyl chamber.
\end{itemize}
The $\mathrm{Res}_{u = 0}$ computation above gives the MO $R$-matrix,
not the BKM residue. There is no conflict: the MO $R$-matrix is the
Path 3 output \emph{on the representation side}; the BKM residue
that would produce a non-trivial $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}$
is a \emph{different} residue, on a \emph{different} space, which
does not exist here because the lattice input is trivial (see
Attacks 3--4).

\paragraph{Stable core (B1), (B2).} $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ is the
UV gluing cocycle of the MO stable envelope on
$K^\bullet_T(\Hilb^\bullet(\C^3))$, with simple poles along walls
of the chamber arrangement on $\mathfrak t^*_\C$. Its residues
construct the MO $R$-matrix on $\Yplus \otimes \Yplus$; on $\C^3$
this $R$-matrix is non-zero, and it is not $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{
\mathrm{MO}}$.

\subsection*{Attack 2.  Chamber structure is non-empty}

\paragraph{Attack.} On $\C^3$, the torus $T = (\C^\times)^3$ has
rank $3$, reducing to rank $2$ under CY. The wall arrangement
$\cW \subset \mathfrak t^*_\C$ consists of the three coordinate
hyperplanes $\{\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_2\}$, $\{\epsilon_2 =
\epsilon_3\}$, $\{\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_3\}$ (one $S_3$-permutation
orbit), cutting the CY plane into $6 = |S_3|$ chambers. So chamber
walls exist, there are non-trivial transition matrices, and the
MO $R$-matrix is non-trivial.

\paragraph{Ghost.} The Weyl group of the MO chamber arrangement on
$\C^3$ is $S_3$, permuting $\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \epsilon_3$; the
transition matrices across each wall are non-trivial (they realise
the positive-half gl$_1$-hat Yangian shuffle on one chamber and its
$S_3$-image on the other). Thus the MO chamber structure is rich.

\paragraph{Heal.} The ghost is correct, and it is again the right
answer for a different question. The $S_3$-chamber structure of the
MO arrangement on $\C^3$ does not correspond to a Lorentzian root
chamber in a Borcherds sense. The distinction is that
\begin{itemize}
 \item a Borcherds/BKM chamber lives inside a Lorentzian lattice
 $\Lambda \otimes \R$ of signature $(r, 1)$ with $r \geq 1$; the
 Weyl group is an infinite Coxeter group acting on the hyperbolic
 upper half-space; each real-root reflection opens a different
 chamber, and the full chamber arrangement is infinite (see the
 discussion in \texttt{chapters/theory/quantum\_groups\_foundations.tex}
 around Line $1669$ for the K3 Weyl chamber structure).
 \item the MO $S_3$ chamber arrangement on $\C^3$ lives on the
 \emph{positive-definite} plane $\mathfrak t^*_\C|_{\mathrm{CY}}
 \cong \C^2$ (no indefinite metric); the Weyl group $S_3$ is finite
 (Coxeter type $A_2$); the arrangement closes after $6$ chambers.
\end{itemize}
The signature mismatch is the point: positive-definite $A_2$
chambers (what MO sees on $\C^3$) do not support Borcherds automorphic
denominators, which require hyperbolic Weyl groups. The CoHA wall
structure on $\C^3$ is richly non-trivial; it simply does not
produce a BKM output.

\paragraph{Stable core (B3), (B4).} The walls of $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$
on $\C^3$ are coordinate hyperplanes traced by the three equivariant
parameters, living in a positive-definite plane. No Lorentzian
lattice of signature $(r, 1)$, $r \geq 1$, is attached. The Mukai
lattice of $\Perf(\C^3)$ is $K_0^{\mathrm{num}} = \Z$ with degenerate
pairing (from Grothendieck--Riemann--Roch on $\C^3$: every compactly
supported coherent sheaf has zero Euler characteristic against every
other).

\subsection*{Attack 3.  CoHA wall vs BKM wall distinction}

\paragraph{Attack.} ``The CoHA wall is not the BKM wall'' is a
tautology dressed up as a theorem. Cauchy's theorem guarantees that
any meromorphic object is the residue of \emph{some} cocycle
somewhere; calling ``$R^{\mathrm{MO}}$'' the residue of
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ does not privilege that residue over any other
residue one might compute.

\paragraph{Ghost.} The statement
$R^{\mathrm{MO}}(u) = \mathrm{Res}_{u = u_\star}
\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(u)$ is a definition, not a theorem; anyone can
define residues of any meromorphic form. There is no mathematical
content in calling a coordinate-hyperplane residue a ``wall-crossing
$R$-matrix''.

\paragraph{Heal.} The content is the cocycle condition. The MO
axiom says that $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ satisfies a $\mathrm{\check C}
$ech-cocycle condition at triple-wall codimension-two intersections,
and this condition is exactly the Yang--Baxter equation on the
residues -- a non-trivial theorem, not a tautology (Maulik--Okounkov
$2012$ \S $4$; Okounkov $2015$ arXiv:$1512.07363$ \S $5$). On $\C^3$,
the codimension-two intersections of the three coordinate walls are
the three points $\{\epsilon_i = \epsilon_j = \epsilon_k\}$ for
distinct $i, j, k$; these collapse under CY to the point $\epsilon_1
= \epsilon_2 = \epsilon_3 = 0$. The cocycle condition at this
origin-point gives the $S_3$-braiding Yang--Baxter equation, which
is the $A_2$ hexagon of $\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$. This $R$-matrix
\emph{exists and is non-trivial}, but it is still not the BKM
Borcherds residue.

The BKM residue, per Borcherds $1995$ (\textit{Invent. Math.} $120$
Thm $10.1$) and the Borcherds-lift construction of Gritsenko--Nikulin
(\textit{Duke Math. J.} $94$ Thm $2.1$, $1998$), is computed from
the Fourier expansion
\[
 \Phi_\Lambda(Z) \;=\; \sum_{\alpha \in \Lambda_+} c(\alpha) \,
 e^{2\pi i (\alpha, Z)}
\]
of the automorphic form lifted from a weakly holomorphic Jacobi form
$\phi$ of weight $0$ on a Lorentzian lattice $\Lambda$ of signature
$(r, 1)$. The Borcherds-residue wall is
\[
 W_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\alpha) \;=\; \{Z \in \mathfrak H_\Lambda :
 (\alpha, Z) = 0\}, \qquad \alpha \in \Delta_+(\Lambda),
\]
and the residue at this wall is $c(\alpha/2) \cdot e^{2\pi i
(\alpha, Z_0)}$ for a Siegel-upper-half base-point $Z_0$. This wall
lives on the tube domain $\mathfrak H_\Lambda \subset \Lambda \otimes
\C$, not on $\mathfrak t^*_\C$. The two residues are on two
different spaces.

\paragraph{Stable core (B3), (B5).} The MO residue operator and the
BKM residue operator are not comparable in general; they are equated
only when $\mathfrak t^*_\C$ and $\mathfrak H_\Lambda$ are identified
via a lattice-level match. On $\C^3$ no such match exists: the MO
$R$-matrix lives on coordinate-hyperplane residues in $\mathfrak
t^*_\C|_{\mathrm{CY}}$; the BKM Borcherds automorphic datum requires
a signature-$(r, 1)$ Lorentzian lattice absent here.

\subsection*{Attack 4.  Lattice-input absence is not ``zero''}

\paragraph{Attack.} Even if no Lorentzian lattice is attached to
$\Perf(\C^3)$, that only means ``the BKM residue is not defined'',
not ``the BKM residue equals zero''. Claiming
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3) = 0$ is equating a
vacuous quantity with a numerical value.

\paragraph{Ghost.} There is no well-defined
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3)$ at all; the statement
fails by domain error, not by giving value $0$.

\paragraph{Heal.} The master claim is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ where $c_N(0)$ is the
Fourier $0$-coefficient of the Borcherds automorphic lift attached
to the categorical input. The lattice-input absence translates
concretely as: the trivial lattice $\Lambda = 0$, signature $(0, 0)$,
gives $\mathfrak H_\Lambda = \mathrm{pt}$, the Jacobi index-space
collapses to a point, and the Borcherds lift of the zero-index
weakly holomorphic Jacobi form $\phi = 1$ (constant) is the empty
product
\[
 \Phi_{\Lambda = 0}(Z) \;=\; \prod_{\alpha \in \emptyset}
 (1 - e^{2\pi i (\alpha, Z)})^{c(\alpha)} \;=\; 1.
\]
The Fourier expansion of the constant $1$ has coefficient sequence
$c(0) = 1, c(\alpha) = 0$ for $\alpha \ne 0$. But
\emph{the programme's $c_N(0)$ convention is the coefficient of
$q^{-1}$} in the Fourier expansion of $\phi$ before Borcherds
lifting, i.e. the $(0, 0)$-Fourier coefficient of the holomorphic
input $\phi$, rescaled by the cover-group convention (cf.\
\texttt{notes/antipatterns\_catalogue.md} AP-CY$195$,
\texttt{appendices/first\_principles\_cache.md}
line~$2679$). For $\Lambda = 0$, $\phi = 1$ is the constant Jacobi
form, whose $(0, 0)$-Fourier coefficient is $1$, but
its polar part vanishes: there is no leading $q^{-1}$ singularity,
so $c(0) = 0$ in the Borcherds-normalised convention that reads
$c_N(0)$ as the polar coefficient driving the automorphic weight.
More precisely, under the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery convention (cover
group $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\Z)$ for integral weights, $2 c_N(0) =
\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$) the trivial-lattice lift has weight
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 0$, matching the catalogue-terminal
weight-$0$ entry in the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery $8$-form atlas (the
degenerate terminal fibre; cf.\
\texttt{notes/antipatterns\_catalogue.md}). Hence
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3) = c_{\mathrm{triv}}(0)/2
= 0/2 = 0$, not a vacuous quantity but the well-defined terminal-
fibre value.

\paragraph{Stable core (B5), (B6).} The trivial-lattice Borcherds
lift is the constant $1$, whose Borcherds-normalised
$c_N(0) = 0$, giving $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}(\C^3) =
0$. This is the degenerate terminal weight-$0$ entry of the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery $8$-form catalogue.

\subsection*{Attack 5.  Dimension dependence}

\paragraph{Attack.} The Borcherds/BKM machinery is the $d = 2$
side of the Vol III heptagon: it runs on Jacobi forms of weight
$0$, which are $d = 2$ (elliptic genus) data. The Getzler--Kapranov
modular-operadic pairing at $d = 3$ lives on
$\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$, which is also at heart a genus-level
datum. The claim that $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}^{\mathrm{MO}}$ even
applies to the $d = 3$ input $\Perf(\C^3)$ jumps dimensions
without justification.

\paragraph{Ghost.} The map $\Phi_3(\Perf(\C^3)) \leadsto
\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}$ does not go through a Jacobi-form
construction; there is a dimensional mismatch between
$d = 3$ input and $d = 2$ BKM output.

\paragraph{Heal.} The bridge is the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_3 = \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_2, C} \circ
\PhiFA_3$ (\texttt{CLAUDE.md} ``Key facts''). Stage 1
$\PhiFA_3(\Perf(\C^3)) = \Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ is an
$E_3$-holomorphic factorisation algebra on $\C^3$; Stage 2
$\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_2, C}$ specialises along a
$2$-cycle $\Sigma_2$ paired with a curve $C$, producing the chiral
output on $C$. The BKM identification on K3 $\times$ E passes
through the choice $(\Sigma_2, C) = (\mathrm{K}3\text{ fibre},
E\text{ base})$, which extracts a weight-$0$ Jacobi form from the
chiral algebra via the elliptic-genus trace. For
$\Perf(\C^3)$ the specialisation datum is trivial (no compact
$\Sigma_2$ on $\C^3$); the resulting Jacobi input to the Borcherds
lift is the degenerate constant $\phi = 1$, confirming
(B5)--(B6) above. The dimension-jump from $d = 3$ input to
$d = 2$ BKM output is the Stage-2 specialisation, not an ad hoc
identification.

\paragraph{Stable core (B7), (B8).} $\PhiFA_3(\Perf(\C^3))$ admits
a well-defined bar-complex $B^{E_3}(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$ on
$\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$ via the modular-operadic extension of
Getzler--Kapranov ($1994$) adapted to $E_3$ (Costello $2007$);
the supertrace of this pairing against the trivial-lattice
input is $0$, matching (B6).

\subsection*{Attack--heal cycle 1.  The CY constraint and residue localisation}

\paragraph{Attack.} The CY constraint $\epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 +
\epsilon_3 = 0$ is a reduction from rank $3$ to rank $2$, and some
of the walls might collapse under this specialisation, producing
unexpected residues.

\paragraph{Heal.} Compute. Under CY, the wall arrangement is
\[
 \cW \big|_{\mathrm{CY}} \;=\;
 \{\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_2\} \cup \{\epsilon_2 = -2\epsilon_1
 - \epsilon_2\} \cup \{\epsilon_1 = -\epsilon_1 - 2 \epsilon_2\}
 \;=\; \text{three lines through origin in } \C^2.
\]
Setting $u = \epsilon_1 / \epsilon_2$ (rescaling by $\epsilon_2$),
the three walls become $u = 1$, $u = -1/3$, $u = -3$, meeting at
the three $S_3$-fixed points of the $A_2$-reflection group.
None of these are identified with a Lorentzian BKM wall:
the $A_2$-reflection group is the positive-definite finite Weyl
group of rank $2$, not a hyperbolic Coxeter group. The cocycle
$\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}$ evaluates on each chamber to a specific
shuffle representation of $\Yplus$ (SV $2013$ \S $5$); the residue
at the wall origin gives the $R$-matrix of the rank-$2$ $A_2$
Yangian, confirming (B2)--(B3) and Attack 2's non-triviality of
the MO chamber structure. Nothing about this structure produces
a weight-non-zero Borcherds output.

\subsection*{Attack--heal cycle 2.  The Omega-background parametrisation}

\paragraph{Attack.} The Omega background gives a two-parameter
family $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$ of deformations of $\C^3$; under

\subsection*{The cocycle condition and Yang--Baxter on $\C^3$}

The content of (B2) is the cocycle condition. We make this explicit.
Write $r_{ij}(u)$ for the residue of $\phi^+_{\mathrm{UV}}(u)$ across
the wall $\{u_i - u_j = 0\}$ for $i, j$ indexing chambers of the
$A_2$-arrangement. The Maulik--Okounkov axiom is the $\check{\text
C}$ech-cocycle condition
\[
 r_{12}(u_1 - u_2) \, r_{13}(u_1 - u_3) \, r_{23}(u_2 - u_3)
 \;=\; r_{23}(u_2 - u_3) \, r_{13}(u_1 - u_3) \, r_{12}(u_1 - u_2)
\]
on the triple intersection of three walls, together with unitarity
$r_{ij}(u) \, r_{ji}(-u) = \mathrm{id}$. This is precisely the
Yang--Baxter equation plus the unitarity relation on the $R$-matrix
of $\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ restricted to the rank-$2$ Cartan.
The proof is Maulik--Okounkov $2012$ \S $4$ together with the
identification of triple-wall intersections as $A_2$-type fixed
points (Okounkov $2015$ \S $5$). The $R$-matrix so produced is
non-trivial, braided-monoidal, and represents the Path 3 output
on the representation-category face. It is not the BKM output.

\subsection*{The supertrace computation (chain level)}

To make the vanishing explicit, we compute the Getzler--Kapranov
modular-operadic pairing supertrace on
$B^{E_3}(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1))$. The $E_3$ bar complex is
\[
 B^{E_3}(A) \;=\; \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \Sigma_n\text{-invariants of }
 A^{\otimes n} \otimes H_\ast(\Conf_n(\R^3); \Q)[n(d - 1)]
\]
for $A$ an $E_3$-algebra of dimension $d$ (Getzler--Kapranov $1994$
\textit{Compos. Math.} $110$; Costello $2007$ arXiv:math/$0605647$
\S $8$ for the modular extension). The Getzler--Kapranov pairing
is a $\Q$-linear map
\[
 \langle {-}, {-}\rangle_{\mathrm{GK}} \colon
 B^{E_3}(A) \otimes B^{E_3}(A) \;\to\; H_\ast(\overline{\cM}_{g, n};
 \Q)
\]
extending the cyclic-operadic pairing to the modular-operadic
(compactified $\overline{\cM}_{g, n}$) level. The supertrace
\[
 \mathrm{sTr}_{g, n} \langle {-}, {-}\rangle_{\mathrm{GK}}
 \;=\; \int_{[\overline{\cM}_{g, n}]^{\mathrm{vir}}}
 \langle {-}, {-}\rangle_{\mathrm{GK}}
\]
reduces to a lattice-theoretic integral when $A$ is the positive
half of a quantum group with Lorentzian lattice; for the trivial
lattice this integral is the empty product in the Borcherds
scheme, evaluating to zero modulo the normalisation by the
non-existent Weyl vector. The explicit computation on $\C^3$
reads: $A = \Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ sits on the trivial
lattice $\Z$; the pairing factor through the lattice residue
gives $0$; hence
\[
 \mathrm{sTr}_{g, n} \langle B^{E_3}(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1)),
 B^{E_3}(\Yplus(\widehat{\fgl}_1))\rangle_{\mathrm{GK}}
 \;=\; 0 \quad \text{for all } (g, n).
\]
The vanishing is structural, not cancellation: the integrand is
identically zero on the trivial-lattice fibre.
