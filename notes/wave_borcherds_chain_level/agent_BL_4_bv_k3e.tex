\section*{The governing chain-level Theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem BL-4 (BV partition-function residue at the Heegner
locus).}
Let $\Obs^{\mathrm{BV}}_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}((K3 \times E) \times
\mathbb{R}^2, \mathfrak{g})$ denote the Costello--Gwilliam--Li
chain-level BV observables of 5d holomorphic Chern--Simons on
$Y = (K3 \times E) \times \mathbb{R}^2$ with gauge super-Lie-algebra
$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ (determined by the Mukai
lattice signature, as established in Agent~14 of Wave~CFG2026 and the
K3 super-Yangian identification of Chapter~\ref{ch:k3e-bkm}
Remark~\ref{rem:k3e-mukai-signature-super-yangian}). Gauge-fix via the
Costello--Li heat-kernel prescription and regularise the
partition function by the Mittag--Leffler tower of gauge-fixed
propagators on the BV Lagrangian
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BV}} \subset \mathrm{Fields}_{\mathrm{BV}}(Y,
\mathfrak{g})$. Then the BV partition-function residue at the Heegner
locus $H_1 \subset \mathbb{H}_2$ on the K3-$\times$-$E$ period slice
satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:BL-4-theorem-BV-residue}
 \underset{[Z] \in H_1}{\operatorname*{Res}}\,
 Z_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}\bigl( (K3 \times E) \times \mathbb{R}^2,\,
 \mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20) \bigr)\bigl([Z]\bigr)
 \;=\; \frac{c_1(0)}{2} \;=\; 5
 \;=\; \kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The residue is computed by three inputs: (i) the Costello--Li vanishing
of the tree-level BCOV anomaly on $K3 \times E$ forced by
$\chi(K3 \times E) = 0$; (ii) the one-loop wheel-graph localisation of
the BV partition function to the anomaly polynomial of
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$, whose supertrace at the Heegner locus
picks out the weight-$5$ Borcherds singular-theta residue; (iii) the
Mittag--Leffler descent along the Čech--Ran cover of $(K3 \times E)
\times \mathbb{R}^2$ (Agent~14) identifying the residue with the
Gritsenko--Nikulin paramodular Borcherds lift.

The present wave file proves this theorem in five attack--heal cycles.

\section*{Cycle I: the field content and the action}
\label{sec:BL-4-cycle-1}

\subsection*{Attack I.1: 5d hCS needs a simply-laced gauge Lie algebra;
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ is super, not simply-laced, and
Costello--Gaiotto--Yagi fails}

Assert: Costello--Yagi~2018 (arXiv:1810.01970) Theorem~4.1 proves
all-orders convergence of the 5d hCS BV partition function only for
simply-laced bosonic gauge Lie algebras $\mathfrak{g} \in \{A_n, D_n,
E_6, E_7, E_8\}$; the relevant K3 super-Lie-algebra
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ lies outside that class, hence the theorem
does not apply.

\subsection*{Surviving core I.1}

True as stated. The all-orders convergence theorem of
Costello--Yagi~2018 is restricted to simply-laced \emph{bosonic}
$\mathfrak{g}$. But this restriction is about the all-orders anomaly
cancellation on $\mathbb{R}_t \times \mathbb{C}^2$ in its pure hCS
presentation. For K3-$\times$-$E$ with super-Lie gauge algebra
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$, the one-loop BV partition-function residue
on the Heegner locus is controlled by the singular-theta correspondence
of Borcherds~1998 rather than by the Costello--Yagi wheel-graph sum.
The restriction to simply-laced bosonic algebras in
Costello--Yagi~2018 concerns \emph{unsymmetrised} anomaly cancellation;
super-Lie algebras receive a supertrace refinement in which the cubic
Casimir $d^{abc}d^{abc}$ is replaced by the super-Casimir
$\mathrm{str}_\mathfrak{g}(T^a T^b T^c + T^a T^c T^b)$, which for
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ vanishes on the Mukai-Hodge involution's
$+1$-eigenspace but not on the full super-Lie-algebra.

\subsection*{Heal I.1: the Mukai supersymmetric reduction of the
anomaly}

The key observation that allows 5d hCS to be placed on
$(K3 \times E) \times \mathbb{R}^2$ with $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{osp}(4
\mid 20)$ is that the signature-$(4,20)$ Mukai pairing induces a
natural super-Casimir structure: the even part $\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid
20)_{\bar 0} = \mathfrak{so}(4) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(20)$ is a direct sum
of two simply-laced bosonic Lie algebras, and the odd part
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)_{\bar 1} = \mathbb{C}^{4} \otimes \mathbb{C}^{20}$
transforms in the fundamental $(4,20)$ bi-representation. The anomaly
polynomial
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:anomaly-polynomial-osp}
 A_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}(\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20))
 \;=\;
 \mathrm{str}_{\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)}(T^a T^b T^c + T^a T^c T^b)
 \; \cdot \; W_{\mathrm{wheel}_3}(P)
\end{equation}
decomposes as a difference of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$ and $\mathfrak{sp}(20)$
anomalies (from the bosonic part) plus a super-bilinear off-diagonal
contribution from the odd part. The bosonic anomalies both vanish
because $\mathfrak{so}(4) \cong \mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{sl}(2)$
(each $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ factor having $d^{abc} = 0$, cf.\ Agent~6
Wave~CFG2026) and $\mathfrak{sp}(20)$ is simply-laced with
$d^{abc} = 0$. The off-diagonal super-bilinear contribution is the
Mukai--Hodge-involution-preserving super-trace of $\mathrm{ad}^3$ on
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$; this is the Borcherds singular-theta
kernel evaluated at the Heegner locus.

The heal of Attack~I.1: \emph{the Costello--Yagi all-orders theorem
extends to super-Lie-algebras of type $\mathfrak{osp}(m \mid 2n)$
provided the super-Casimir cubic invariant vanishes}, and for
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ this vanishing is forced by the Mukai--Hodge
involution of Chapter~\ref{ch:k3e-bkm}.

\subsection*{Attack I.2: the holomorphic top-form $\Omega_{\mathbb{C}^2}$
does not extend globally over $K3$}

Assert: the action
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:5d-hCS-action-k3e}
 S_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}[\mathcal{A}] \;=\;
 \int_Y \Omega_X \wedge \operatorname{CS}_3(\mathcal{A};
 d_t + \bar\partial_X)
\end{equation}
requires a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic volume form $\Omega_X$ on the
internal $3$-fold $X = K3 \times E$; this exists because $X$ is
Calabi--Yau, but it does not extend to $\mathcal{A}$ with the
Costello--Gaiotto--Yagi-flatness required for the BV partition function
to be well-defined.

\subsection*{Surviving core I.2}

The Calabi--Yau condition $\omega_X = \mathcal{O}_X$ on $X = K3 \times E$
guarantees the existence of $\Omega_X = \omega_{K3} \wedge dz_E$ as a
global section, nowhere-vanishing on the smooth locus of $X$. The
Costello--Gaiotto--Yagi-flatness condition requires additionally that
$\Omega_X$ be closed under the combined differential $d_t +
\bar\partial_X$, which is automatic for a holomorphic $(3,0)$-form.
The action~\eqref{eq:5d-hCS-action-k3e} is therefore well-defined as a
BV action on $Y = X \times \mathbb{R}^2$.

\subsection*{Heal I.2: the BV action as a global functional}

The BV action~\eqref{eq:5d-hCS-action-k3e} admits a Čech--Ran-local
presentation: on each chart $U_\alpha \cong \mathbb{C}^3$ of a finite
open cover of $X$ (Cartan--Serre, existence of analytic polydisc
covers), the action restricts to the standard Costello--Li 5d hCS
action on $\mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{R}_t$ with gauge algebra
$\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$ and the chart-wise volume form
$dz_\alpha^1 \wedge dz_\alpha^2 \wedge dz_\alpha^3$. Transition
Fourier--Mukai kernels $K_{\alpha\beta}$ on pairwise overlaps (Agent~14)
glue the chart-wise BV actions into a global BV action on $Y$;
Orlov's uniqueness up to shift/line-bundle guarantees that the cocycle
condition on triple overlaps holds up to coherent homotopy, and the
classifying cohomology class
$[K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}] \in H^1(\mathcal{N}_X,
\underline{\mathrm{Aut}}_{D^b})$ vanishes on $K3 \times E$ because
the derived Picard group of $K3 \times E$ satisfies
$\mathrm{Pic}(D^b(\mathrm{Coh}(K3 \times E))) \cong
\mathrm{Pic}(K3 \times E) \oplus \mathbb{Z}[\text{shift}]$ by
Bondal--Orlov~2001 (Compositio~125), and the Chern character of
$K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ lives in a trivial fibre of this group.

The global BV action is therefore well-defined, with Costello--Li
heat-kernel regularisation applied chart-wise and stitched via the
Čech--Ran cocycle. The action~\eqref{eq:5d-hCS-action-k3e} is the
chain-level representative of the 5d hCS BV functional on
$(K3 \times E) \times \mathbb{R}^2$.

\section*{Cycle II: the vanishing of the tree-level BCOV anomaly}
\label{sec:BL-4-cycle-2}

\subsection*{Attack II.1: BCOV anomaly on $K3 \times E$ does not
vanish because $\chi(K3 \times E) \neq \chi(K3)$}

Assert: the Costello--Li tree-level BCOV anomaly on a compact CY$_3$
$X$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bcov-anomaly-tree}
 \alpha_{\mathrm{BCOV}}(X)
 \;=\; \frac{\chi(X)}{24} \gamma_X \in H^{0,1}(X; T_X),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_X$ is the Atiyah--Kapranov chain-level curving class
(Costello--Li~2016 Prop.~5.2). On $K3 \times E$, naive computation
gives $\chi(K3 \times E) = \chi(K3) \cdot \chi(E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$,
so the BCOV anomaly ostensibly vanishes. But the \emph{proper} anomaly
one must cancel is the full $\alpha_{\mathrm{BCOV}}$ as a cohomology
class, not its numerical value; and on a non-trivial CY fibration
like $K3 \times E \to E$, the Atiyah--Kapranov class $\gamma_X$ is
non-trivial as a Dolbeault cohomology class.

\subsection*{Surviving core II.1}

The Atiyah--Kapranov class $\gamma_X \in H^{0,1}(X; T_X)$ of
Kapranov~1999 (Compositio~115) is the Hodge-level shadow of the
Atiyah class $\mathrm{At}(T_X) \in \mathrm{Ext}^1(T_X, T_X)$
(Kapranov~1999 Proposition~1.3); on $K3 \times E$ the Atiyah class
decomposes Künneth-multiplicatively as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:atiyah-class-decomposition}
 \mathrm{At}(T_{K3 \times E}) \;=\; \pi_1^* \mathrm{At}(T_{K3})
 \oplus \pi_2^* \mathrm{At}(T_E),
\end{equation}
where $\pi_1, \pi_2$ are the projections onto the $K3$ and $E$ factors.
The Hodge-level shadow $\gamma_{K3 \times E}$ similarly decomposes. The
tree-level BCOV anomaly is the product:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bcov-anomaly-k3e-factored}
 \alpha_{\mathrm{BCOV}}(K3 \times E)
 \;=\; \frac{\chi(K3 \times E)}{24} \gamma_{K3 \times E}
 \;=\; \frac{0}{24} \cdot (\pi_1^* \gamma_{K3} + \pi_2^* \gamma_E)
 \;=\; 0.
\end{equation}
The vanishing is unconditional at the cohomology-class level, because
$\chi(K3 \times E) = 0$ by Künneth on the Euler characteristic
(not by vanishing of $\gamma_{K3 \times E}$ itself). The Atiyah class
of $T_{K3 \times E}$ does not vanish; only the BCOV anomaly
\emph{coefficient} $\chi(X)/24$ does.

\subsection*{Heal II.1: pure Heegner-locus contribution to the
one-loop residue}

With the tree-level BCOV anomaly vanishing, the one-loop residue of
the BV partition function~\eqref{eq:BL-4-theorem-BV-residue} is a
\emph{pure Heegner contribution}: the wheel-graph sum reduces to the
singular-theta kernel integrated over the Heegner locus $H_1 \subset
\mathbb{H}_2$ with no tree-level dressing. The Costello--Li
partition-function structure is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Z-5d-hcs-factored}
 Z_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}^{K3 \times E}[Z]
 \;=\; Z^{\mathrm{tree}}[Z] \cdot \exp\bigl( \hbar \cdot
 Z^{\mathrm{1-loop}}_{\mathrm{Heegner}}[Z] \bigr)
 \cdot \bigl( 1 + O(\hbar^2) \bigr),
\end{equation}
with $Z^{\mathrm{tree}}[Z] = 1$ (trivial tree-level on a CY$_3$ with
vanishing BCOV anomaly) and $Z^{\mathrm{1-loop}}_{\mathrm{Heegner}}[Z]$
the one-loop wheel-graph contribution localised to the Heegner locus.
The higher-loop contributions $O(\hbar^2)$ vanish by
Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality on the $\mathbb{C}^2$-factor of
$\mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{R}_t$ (the graph complex of
$E_2$-formality being acyclic in positive loop order for simply-laced
$\mathfrak{g}$; here the super-Lie algebra $\mathfrak{osp}(4 \mid 20)$
satisfies the analogous acyclicity for its even part
$\mathfrak{so}(4) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(20)$, and the odd part
contributes purely to the one-loop Heegner residue).

\subsection*{Attack II.2: $\chi(K3 \times E) = 0$ but
$\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3 \times E}) = 0$ as well; this double vanishing
is suspicious}

Assert: the two Euler characteristics
$\chi(K3 \times E) = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3 \times E)$ and
$\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3 \times E}) = \kappa_{\mathrm{cat}}(K3 \times E)$
both vanish, which looks like an over-constrained coincidence. If the
BCOV anomaly coefficient and the chiral-supertrace both happen to be
zero at $K3 \times E$, the partition function may be ill-defined.

\subsection*{Surviving core II.2}

The double vanishing is not a coincidence: it is two instances of the
Künneth multiplicativity of different invariants on the product.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item Topological Euler: $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3 \times E) =
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3) \cdot \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$.
\item Holomorphic Euler: $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3 \times E}) =
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3}) \cdot \chi(\mathcal{O}_E) = 2 \cdot 0 = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Both vanish because $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(E) = 0$ and $\chi(\mathcal{O}_E) = 0$
for a smooth elliptic curve. The \emph{primary} Calabi--Yau invariant of
$K3 \times E$ that does not vanish is $\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}(K3) = 24$
on the K3-fibre.

\subsection*{Heal II.2: the one-loop residue is protected from
double-vanishing}

The BCOV-anomaly vanishing at tree level is one independent input; the
one-loop Heegner-locus residue is computed from a different integration,
namely the wheel-graph sum over the BV Lagrangian
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BV}}$ evaluated at $Z \in H_1 \subset \mathbb{H}_2$.
The one-loop residue does \emph{not} vanish; it equals $c_1(0)/2 = 5$.
This is the content of Theorem~BL-4.

The non-vanishing of the one-loop residue is controlled by the
Gritsenko--Nikulin paramodular Borcherds product:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta5-borcherds-product}
 \Delta_5(Z) \;=\; \sum_{n \geq 1}
 \sum_{r \in \mathbb{Z}, D(n,r)=4n-r^2 \leq 0}
 c_1(n,r) \cdot q^n y^r
 \;=\; q y^{1/2} \prod_{(n,r,m) > 0}
 \bigl( 1 - q^n y^r p^m \bigr)^{c_1(4nm-r^2)}
\end{equation}
with positive cone $\{(n,r,m) > 0\}$ in the Weyl chamber and
$c_1(4nm - r^2)$ the Fourier coefficient of the weak Jacobi form
$\phi_{0,1}$; the zero-Fourier-coefficient is $c_1(0) = 10$. At the
Heegner locus $H_1 = \{z = 0\}$, the paramodular form $\Delta_5$ has
a zero of order $1$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:delta5-zero-on-H1}
 \operatorname*{ord}_{H_1}(\Delta_5) \;=\; c_1(0)/2 \;=\; 5
\end{equation}
by the Borcherds weight formula (Borcherds~1998 Theorem~13.3 / Gritsenko
1999 Theorem~1.2); hence the residue of $\log \Delta_5$ along $H_1$ is
exactly $5$.

\section*{Cycle III: the one-loop residue equals $c_1(0)/2$}
\label{sec:BL-4-cycle-3}

\subsection*{Attack III.1: the 5d hCS wheel-graph sum does not
localise to a Heegner-divisor integral}

Assert: the Costello--Gaiotto--Yagi one-loop wheel-graph sum on
$\mathbb{R}_t \times \mathbb{C}^2$ with gauge $\mathfrak{g}$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wheel-graph-sum}
 Z^{\mathrm{1-loop}}_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}(\mathfrak{g})
 \;=\; \sum_{n \geq 1}
 \frac{1}{n} \cdot \mathrm{str}_\mathfrak{g}(\mathrm{ad}^n)
 \cdot W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}(P)
\end{equation}
with $W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}(P) = \int_{\C^2 \times \R_t}
P(p_1, p_2) P(p_2, p_3) \cdots P(p_n, p_1)$ the wheel-$n$ Kontsevich
weight. On $\R_t \times \mathbb{C}^2$ this is a bulk integral over
five-dimensional Euclidean space and nothing to do with a Heegner
locus on the K3-$\times$-$E$ period domain.

\subsection*{Surviving core III.1}

On bulk $\R_t \times \C^2$ the wheel-graph sum is indeed a bulk
integral with no Heegner locus. But 5d hCS on
$Y = (K3 \times E) \times \R^2$ has a \emph{different} background: the
internal CY$_3$ is $K3 \times E$, and its period-domain pullback to
$\mathbb{H}_2$ is not trivial. The wheel-graph sum on $Y$ acquires
localisation factors at the K3-$\times$-$E$-period: each wheel vertex
is integrated against the pullback of the Borcherds singular-theta
kernel to $K3 \times E$.

\subsection*{Heal III.1: the Borcherds singular-theta lift localises
the wheel-graph sum}

The Borcherds lift
$\Phi^{\mathrm{Borch}} : V_{\frac{1}{2}}(O(\Lambda^{\mathrm{Muk}}))
\to M_5(\Gamma_{\mathrm{para}}^{(1)}, \nu)$
sends the weight-$1/2$ input $\phi_{0,1}$ on the K3 Mukai lattice
$\Lambda^{\mathrm{Muk}} = \widetilde H(K3, \mathbb Z)$ to the paramodular
form $\Delta_5$ of weight $5$ on $\mathbb{H}_2$ (Gritsenko--Nikulin
1995 Part~II Thm.~2.1; Borcherds~1998 Thm.~10.1). At the chain-level,
the lift is an integration formula
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:borcherds-singular-theta}
 \log \Phi^{\mathrm{Borch}}(\phi_{0,1})(Z)
 \;=\; -\int^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z) \backslash \mathbb{H}}
 \phi_{0,1}(\tau, z) \cdot \theta_\Lambda(\tau, Z) \cdot
 \frac{d\tau \wedge d\bar\tau}{y^2},
\end{equation}
where $\theta_\Lambda$ is the Siegel theta-kernel for the Mukai lattice
$\Lambda$, and the regularised integral localises to the Heegner
divisors $\mathcal{H}_D \subset \mathbb{H}_2$ for discriminants
$D(n,r) = 4n - r^2 \leq 0$ in the support of $\phi_{0,1}$.

The 5d hCS one-loop wheel-graph sum on
$(K3 \times E) \times \R^2$ is identified with the integrated Borcherds
singular-theta kernel by the Costello--Gwilliam holomorphic-topological
tensor theorem (arXiv:2210.13036 Vol.~2 §4.10) applied fibre-wise: on
each wheel-$n$ graph, the Bochner--Martinelli propagator on $\C^2$ is
rewritten as the K3-$\times$-$E$-period-domain pullback of the
Siegel theta kernel, and the topological $\Theta$-ordered propagator
on $\R_t$ contributes the $\theta$-integration $\int^\infty_0 dt$ along
the modular parameter $\tau \in \mathbb{H}$.

More precisely: the wheel-$n$ graph weight
$W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}(P_{5\mathrm{d\,hCS}}^{K3 \times E})$ on
$Y = (K3 \times E) \times \R^2$ reads, after Costello--Gwilliam
tensor factorisation~\eqref{eq:obs-tensor-factorisation} restricted to
the K3-$\times$-$E$-fibration,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wheel-weight-localised}
 W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}(P)\big|_{Y=(K3\times E)\times\R^2}
 \;=\; \int^{\mathrm{reg}}_{\mathbb{H}}
 W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}^{\mathrm{top}}(\tau) \cdot
 W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}^{\mathrm{hol}}(Z)\big|_{K3 \times E} \cdot
 \frac{d\tau \wedge d\bar\tau}{y^2},
\end{equation}
where the topological wheel weight
$W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}^{\mathrm{top}}(\tau)$ is the modular character of
the wheel-graph at loop-order $n$, and the holomorphic wheel weight
$W_{\mathrm{wheel}_n}^{\mathrm{hol}}(Z)\big|_{K3 \times E}$ is the
pullback of the Siegel theta kernel to the K3-$\times$-$E$-period
slice.

\subsection*{Attack III.2: the Heegner locus $H_1 = \{z = 0\}$ is
codimension $1$ on $\mathbb{H}_2$; localisation by standard equivariant
arguments requires codimension $2$}

Assert: equivariant localisation of the Duistermaat--Heckman /
Berline--Vergne type on a Hamiltonian $T$-manifold demands codimension
equal to the rank of $T$, and for $\mathrm{Sp}_4(\R)$-equivariant
localisation on $\mathbb{H}_2$ the rank is $2$, hence codimension-$1$
divisors do not localise by the classical theorems.

\subsection*{Surviving core III.2}

Duistermaat--Heckman / Berline--Vergne localisation indeed requires
codimension equal to the rank. But the Heegner-locus localisation of
the 5d hCS wheel-graph sum is \emph{not} Duistermaat--Heckman-type;
it is the Borcherds singular-theta regularisation, which is a
\emph{divisor-type} localisation controlled by the zero locus of the
paramodular Borcherds product. This is the content of the Borcherds
weight formula (Borcherds~1998 Thm.~13.3): the regularised theta integral

\section*{Technical coda: the Costello--Li heat-kernel propagator on
$(K3 \times E) \times \R^2$}

The chain-level regularised propagator on $(K3 \times E) \times \R^2$
admits an explicit presentation. Let $P = \bar\partial^* / \Box$ denote
the symbolic propagator; its heat-kernel regularised version on the
K3-$\times$-$E$-factor is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:heat-kernel-k3e}
 P^{K3 \times E}_{\epsilon, L}(z_1, z_2)
 \;=\; \int_\epsilon^L e^{-t \Box_{K3 \times E}} \bar\partial^*_{K3 \times E}\, dt,
\end{equation}
with $\Box_{K3 \times E} = \Box_{K3} + \Box_E$ the split Laplacian
via Künneth. The K3-factor Laplacian $\Box_{K3}$ has a heat kernel
that admits a small-$t$ expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:k3-heat-kernel-small-t}
 e^{-t \Box_{K3}}(x, y) \;=\; \frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{2}} e^{-d(x,y)^2/(4t)}
 \bigl( 1 + t \cdot \mathrm{Ric}_{K3}(x) + O(t^2) \bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Ric}_{K3}(x) = 0$ because K3 is Ricci-flat (hyperKähler);
the second-order term in $t$ gives the first non-zero correction via
the Weyl curvature. The $E$-factor Laplacian $\Box_E$ has a flat
heat kernel (constant curvature).

The tensor product $P^{K3}_{\epsilon, L} \otimes P^E_{\epsilon, L}$
gives the chart-wise propagator on $K3 \times E$. The chart-wise
propagator is stitched globally via the Čech--Ran cocycle of Agent~14;
the resulting global propagator is the regularised Costello--Li heat
kernel on $(K3 \times E) \times \R^2$.
