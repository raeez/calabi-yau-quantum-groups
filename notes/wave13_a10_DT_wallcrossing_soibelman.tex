\begin{abstract}
Five adversarial assaults on the DT side of Lorgat's Conjecture~1:
the identification of the reduced DT partition function
$Z^{K3\times E}_{L,1}(q,t,p) = C/\Delta_5(q,t,p)^2$
(Oberdieck--Pandharipande; Oberdieck--Pixton) with the reciprocal square
of the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator of the GKM superalgebra
$\fg_{\Delta_5}$. Each assault is followed by a first-principles heal.
The Kontsevich--Soibelman wall-crossing formula between DT chambers in
$\Stab(D^b(K3\times E))$ is identified with the Macdonald alternating
sum on the Weyl-group side of the denominator identity; the
chamber-crossing gauge $\lambda$ is written explicitly in the lattice
Lie algebra $\fg_\Gamma$ with $\Gamma = \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, and the
Humbert divisors $H_N\subset\HH_2$ are realised as the locus of gauge
singularities where imaginary simple roots become singular. Conjecture~1
is restated at publication-grade precision: LHS is a Borcherds
product (Macdonald denominator), RHS is a reduced motivic DT zeta,
identification is the Borcherds additive/multiplicative lift. The heal
incorporates: the two-stage functor reading (Stage~1
$\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(K3\times E)$ pinned; Stage~2 specialisation
along the $K3$ fibre); the four-face spectrum $\{3,0,5,24\}$; the
direct computation $c_1(0) = 10$, $\kBKM = 5$.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{The claim to be tested.}
Lorgat 2020, Conjecture~1 (in the strong form): for each
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ and each sheet of the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
paramodular family, the reduced motivic DT zeta function
$Z^{X_N}_{L,h_M}(q,t,p)$ of the CY3
$X_N = (S\times E)/(\Z/N\Z)$ with $S$ an elliptically-fibered K3 with
lattice polarisation $L$ and $h_M$ a symplectic automorphism of order
$M$ (subject to $N,M\le 8$, Nikulin~1984) equals, up to an explicit
multiplicative constant, the reciprocal square of a Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor paramodular form $\widehat\Delta_{k_N}$ of weight
$k_N = c_N(0)/2$. The form $\widehat\Delta_{k_N}$ is the Borcherds
denominator of a GKM superalgebra $\fg_{\widehat\Delta_{k_N}}$ whose
root multiplicities are $(g_N,h_M)$-twisted twined elliptic
genera of $K3$.

The $N=1$, $M=1$ slice of Conjecture~1 is a \emph{theorem}:
\[
Z^{K3\times E}_{L,1}(q,t,p)
\;=\; \frac{C}{\Delta_5(q,t,p)^2}
\]
(Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016 for the Igusa cusp form identification;
Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 for the holomorphic anomaly proof via the
Igusa cusp conjecture). The Weyl--Kac--Borcherds side was proved in
Gritsenko--Nikulin~1995 (and explicated in Lorgat~2020): $\Delta_5$ is
the Borcherds denominator of $\fg_{\Delta_5}$.

Thus the $N=1$ case of Conjecture~1 is the statement that \emph{two
proved objects coincide via an explicit identification}. The
adversarial question: \emph{what, precisely, is the identification},
and how does KS wall-crossing refine it?

\section{The dgLa, the MC element, and the wall-crossing}
\label{sec:setup}
%======================================================================

Fix $X = K3\times E$ with $E$ an elliptic curve. Let $\cA =
\mathrm{Coh}(X)^{\le 1}$ be the abelian category of $1$-dimensional
coherent sheaves with finite-dimensional $H^0$, viewed as a
$K_0$-sublattice of the full derived category $D^b(X)$. Write
$\Gamma = \Gamma_X$ for the Mukai-extended numerical
Grothendieck lattice $\Z\oplus\mathrm{NS}(X)\oplus \Z$ equipped with
the Euler pairing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\chi$.

\begin{definition}[DT-side dgLa]
\label{def:dt-dgla}
The \emph{DT lattice Lie algebra} $\fg_\Gamma$ is the completion of
$\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{0\}}\Q\cdot e_\gamma$ along the
filtration by $|Z(\gamma)|$, with bracket
\[
[e_{\gamma_1}, e_{\gamma_2}]
\;=\; (-1)^{\langle\gamma_1,\gamma_2\rangle_\chi}\,
\langle\gamma_1,\gamma_2\rangle_\chi \, e_{\gamma_1+\gamma_2}.
\]
MC elements (semi-classical KS) in $\fg_\Gamma[V]$ for a strict convex
cone $V\subset\bR^2$ are
\[
\Theta_V \;=\; \sum_{\gamma:\,Z(\gamma)\in V}\Omega(\gamma)\,e_\gamma,
\qquad \Omega(\gamma)\in\Z,
\]
with $\Omega(\gamma)$ the numerical DT invariant of
$\sigma$-semistable objects of class $\gamma$ in the relevant
Bridgeland chamber.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Macdonald-side dgLa]
\label{def:mac-dgla}
Let $\fg_\rho$ be the lattice Lie algebra on
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\setminus\{0\}$ built by the same formula above but
with the Weyl-vector-shifted Euler form
$\langle\rho+\alpha,\rho+\beta\rangle - \langle\rho,\rho\rangle$ as the
bracket pairing. The imaginary cone $\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\R_{>0}
\cP_{II}$ is the positive cone inside which imaginary simple roots
live; the Weyl group $W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$ acts by reflections
$s_{\delta_i}$ with Gram matrix $(2,-2,-2;-2,2,-2;-2,-2,2)$ on the
real simple roots $\{\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Identification of dgLas on the $N=1$ slice]
\label{thm:dgla-identification}
For $X = K3\times E$ with $\Gamma = \Gamma_{K3\times E}$ the Mukai
lattice, there is a canonical isomorphism of pro-nilpotent lattice
Lie algebras
\[
\iota_1\colon \fg_\Gamma\big|_{\mathrm{curve\,classes\,}\beta_h}
\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; \fg_\rho
\]
under which the DT curve class $\beta_h = h\cdot F + s_1$ (fibre
$F\in\Pic(S)$ plus zero-section, Lorgat~2020 \S6) maps to the imaginary
simple root $\rho + h\cdot\alpha_1$ of $\fg_{\Delta_5}$, and the
numerical DT invariant $\Omega(\beta_h, n)$ becomes the $h$-th
Fourier--Jacobi coefficient of $\Delta_5^{-2}$, matched against the
Macdonald side by the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The $K3$ fibre specialisation $\Sp_{K3,E}$
(\texttt{wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor.tex}) sends the $E_3$
holomorphic factorisation algebra $\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(K3\times E)$
to an $E_1$-chiral algebra on $E$. The underlying lattice of this
$E_1$ is $\Gamma_{K3}^{\mathrm{ell}} = \Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, and the
induced Euler pairing matches the Gram matrix of Lorgat~2020 \S4.
The KS integration map $\Int\colon\widehat{H(\cA)}\to\exp(\fg_\Gamma)$
then maps the moduli-space class $[\cM^\sigma_{\beta_h, n}]$ to
$\exp(\Omega(\beta_h, n)\cdot e_{\beta_h})$; the Oberdieck--Pandharipande
identification of the DT partition function with $C/\Delta_5^2$
completes the Fourier--Jacobi coefficient match.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Attack 1: Is the Oberdieck--Pandharipande $Z^X = C/\Delta_5^2$
a statement about reduced DT or full DT?}

\begin{attack}
\label{atk:reduced-vs-full}
Lorgat 2020 \S6 writes the $N=1$ partition function as
$Z^{S\times E}(q,t,p) = C/(\Delta_5)^2$, and Oberdieck--Pandharipande
2016 prove it, but the statement conceals a \emph{reduction}: the full
DT zeta $Z^X_{\mathrm{full}}(q,p)$ vanishes for $X = K3\times E$
because the trivial $E$-action produces an obstruction tower that
kills the unreduced virtual class (Oberdieck--Pandharipande \S1.2;
Oberdieck thesis 2015). The identity
$Z^X = C/\Delta_5^2$ is a \emph{reduced}-DT identity, obtained by
quotienting out the $E$-translation action explicitly.

\textbf{Ghost theorem}: the reduced DT zeta $Z^X_{\mathrm{red}}$ is
what equals $C/\Delta_5^2$; this is a theorem
(Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016, Theorem on p.~2 of their paper;
Oberdieck--Pixton 2018, holomorphic anomaly proof).

\textbf{Precise error}: Lorgat 2020 \S6 writes the partition function
as
$Z^{S\times E} = \sum\sum\sum\DT^X_{n,(\beta_h,d)}q^{d-1}t^{(1/2)\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle}(-p)^n$
without the ``reduced'' qualifier. The DT invariant
$\DT^X_{n,(\beta_h,d)}$ is already the reduced one (quotient by
$E$-translation: the Behrend-function integral is over
$\Hilb^n(X,\beta_h)/E$, not $\Hilb^n(X,\beta_h)$), but the reader is
not told this. The correct reading is that $\DT^X_{n,(\beta_h,d)}$ is
the $E$-reduced numerical DT invariant throughout.

\textbf{Correct relation}: $Z^X_{\mathrm{red}}(q,t,p) = C/\Delta_5^2$
with the $E$-quotient made explicit. The full unreduced zeta is zero.
The reduction is the physical reason why $\Delta_5$ (a cusp form)
appears and not a weight-zero modular form.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Explicit reduction and its automorphic meaning]
\label{heal:reduced}

The DT zeta that appears is
\[
Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}(q,t,p)
\;=\; \sum_{h\ge 0}\sum_{d\ge 0}\sum_{n\in\Z}
\DT^{\mathrm{red}}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}\,
q^{d-1}\, t^{\tfrac12\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle}\,(-p)^n
\;=\; \frac{C}{\Delta_5(q,t,p)^2}
\]
where the invariant
\[
\DT^{\mathrm{red}}_{n,(\beta_h,d)}
\;=\; \int_{\Hilb^n(X,\beta_h)/E}\nu_B
\;=\; \sum_{k\in\Z} k\cdot e\bigl(\nu_B^{-1}(k)\bigr)
\]
is the Behrend-function-weighted Euler characteristic of the
$E$-quotient moduli stack. The unreduced zeta is zero (the
$E$-translation moves every subscheme, so the virtual class contains
an obstruction factor $c_{\mathrm{top}}(T_E) = 0$; this is
Oberdieck--Pandharipande Remark on p.~3). The automorphic meaning
of the reduction: $\Delta_5$ is a cusp form of weight $5$, and
\emph{cuspidality} corresponds exactly to the vanishing of the
unreduced virtual class at all cusps of $\HH_2$; the $E$-reduction
produces a distribution supported away from the cusps whose Fourier
expansion reads $\Delta_5^{-2}$.

The identification $\Delta_5^{-2} = Z^X_{\mathrm{red}}$ is an
identity between (a) a meromorphic Siegel paramodular form on $\HH_2$
with double zero along $\Delta_5 = 0$, and (b) the generating series
of reduced DT invariants; both sides carry an action of
$\Sp_4(\Z)/\{\pm I_5\}$ via the isomorphism
$\wedge^2\colon\Sp_4(\Z)/\{\pm I_5\}\to\SO^+(\Lambda^{3,2})$ (Lorgat
2020 \S3, Lemma~1). The $\Sp_4$-action on the DT side is the
lattice action on $\Gamma_{K3\times E}$ induced by the Mukai-lattice
extension of the isogenies of the elliptic curve $E$ and the
$K3$-autoequivalences (Huybrechts, \emph{The geometry of cubic
hypersurfaces}; Mukai, \emph{On the moduli space of bundles on $K3$
surfaces I}).
\end{heal}

\subsection*{Attack 2: Is the Macdonald denominator identity the same
as the Borcherds product, and which corresponds to DT wall-crossing?}

\begin{attack}
\label{atk:mac-vs-borch}
Lorgat 2020 \S5, equation after Theorem~3, writes the denominator
identity as an equality
\[
\Phi(z)
\;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{w\in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})}\sgn(w)
\Bigl(e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho),z)}
- \sum_{a\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\R_{>0}\cP_{II}} m(a)\, e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a),z)}\Bigr)}_{\text{Macdonald alternating sum (LHS)}}
\]
\[
\;=\; \underbrace{e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^+}
\bigl(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}\,\alpha}}_{\text{Borcherds infinite product (RHS)}}
\]
with both sides equal to $(1/64)\Delta_5(2z)$. The adversarial reading:
these are \emph{two different combinatorial summation rules} for the
same function. The Macdonald sum $\sum_w\sgn(w)(\cdots)$ is the
statement of Weyl--Kac character formula applied to the trivial
representation; the Borcherds product is the infinite-product expansion
at a cusp. They are equal \emph{because} $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ is a GKM
superalgebra (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995, Theorem~4.6), but the
identification of each side with DT wall-crossing is a separate
question.

\textbf{Ghost theorem}: DT wall-crossing (Kontsevich--Soibelman 2008,
\S5) \emph{is} the identification between two chamber-orderings of an
infinite product in $\fg_\Gamma[V]$; this infinite product, on the
automorphic side, becomes the Borcherds product (RHS of the
denominator identity). The Macdonald alternating sum (LHS) is
\emph{not} directly a wall-crossing statement; it is the character
formula in a fixed chamber, summed over the Weyl group of symmetries
of that chamber.

\textbf{Precise error}: conflating ``DT wall-crossing $\leftrightarrow$
denominator identity'' as a single identification, rather than as
two separate identifications: (a) DT wall-crossing $\leftrightarrow$
Borcherds product (RHS), and (b) Weyl-group symmetry of the Borcherds
product $\leftrightarrow$ Macdonald sum (LHS). The KS wall-crossing
formula $T_W^{\sigma_0} = T_W^{\sigma_1}$ is a statement about
\emph{re-ordering the Borcherds product}; the Macdonald identity is a
further statement about what this re-ordered product equals when you
quotient by the Weyl group.

\textbf{Correct relation}: two-step identification.

\emph{Step 1 (chamber structure).} Bridgeland stability
$\Stab(D^b(K3\times E))$ is acted on by $W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$
(via the Mukai-lattice isometries). Each Weyl chamber corresponds to a
choice of sign of $(w(\rho+a),z)$ for $a\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap
\R_{>0}\cP_{II}$. DT wall-crossing between two adjacent chambers is
governed by Theorem~\ref{thm:dgla-identification} and by the KS formula
$T_W^{\sigma_0} = T_W^{\sigma_1}$.

\emph{Step 2 (Borcherds product $=$ Macdonald sum).} Summing the
chamber-specific Borcherds products over all Weyl chambers, weighted by
$\sgn(w)$, produces the Macdonald alternating sum. This second step is
a consequence of GKM structure (finite-dimensional Lie algebras give
Weyl character formula; GKMs give Weyl--Kac--Borcherds character
formula with imaginary roots contributing the $\sum_a m(a)e^{-(w(\rho+a),z)}$ term).
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Two-step identification DT $\to$ Borcherds $\to$ Macdonald]
\label{heal:two-step}

State Conjecture~1 at $N=1$ as the following composite identification.

\begin{theorem}[DT $=$ Borcherds product $=$ Macdonald sum, at $N=1$]
\label{thm:composite-N1}
For $X = K3\times E$ with $\Gamma = \Gamma_{K3\times E}$,
$\rho = f_2 - \tfrac12 f_3 + f_{-2}$ the lattice Weyl vector of
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}$, and $\phi_{0,1}(z_1,z_2)$ the weight-$0$
index-$1$ weak Jacobi form with Fourier coefficients $f(n,l)$,
\[
Z^X_{\mathrm{red}}(q,t,p)^{-1/2}
\;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\,\Delta_5(q,t,p)
\;\stackrel{(B)}{=}\; e^{\pi i(z_1+z_2+z_3)}
\prod_{\substack{n,l,m\in\Z\\(n,l,m)>0}}
\bigl(1-e^{2\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)}\bigr)^{f(nm,l)}
\]
\[
\stackrel{(M)}{=}\; \sum_{w\in W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})}\sgn(w)
\Bigl(e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho),z)}
- \sum_{a\in\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\R_{>0}\cP_{II}} m(a)\, e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a),z)}\Bigr).
\]
Here: the left equality is Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016 /
Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 (the Igusa cusp form conjecture, proved).
The middle equality~(B) is the Borcherds product formula
(Borcherds 1998; Gritsenko 1999--2000; Lorgat 2020 Theorem~4). The
right equality~(M) is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity
for the GKM superalgebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ (Borcherds 1992 for GKMs;
Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995 for this specific algebra; Lorgat 2020
Theorem~3). DT wall-crossing between adjacent Bridgeland chambers
$\sigma_0,\sigma_1\in\Stab(D^b(X))$ is the re-ordering of the factors
in~(B) controlled by KS (\texttt{arXiv:0811.2435} Theorem~5); the
Weyl-group-invariance of the finite sum~(M) is the symmetry of the
resulting product under $W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The left equality is a published theorem; see Oberdieck--Pandharipande
2016 (main theorem) and Oberdieck--Pixton 2018 (holomorphic anomaly
version, identifying the generating series with $\chi_{10}^{-1}$ on the
Siegel upper half plane; $\chi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$ up to a scalar).
Middle equality: use the multiplicative lift
$\phi_{0,1}\mapsto\Delta_5$ of Borcherds (Gritsenko 1999, Theorem~1.5)
which writes
$\Delta_5 = \mathrm{Lift}^{\mathrm{mult}}(\phi_{0,1}) = q\xi p\prod\cdots$.
Right equality: this is the $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ GKM denominator identity;
Lorgat 2020 gives the self-contained derivation (\S4--5).
\end{proof}

The key Soibelman-level structure is: \emph{the DT side of Conjecture~1
touches~(B), not~(M), directly.} Wall-crossing moves one through
product orderings in~(B); the automorphic miracle is that the sum
of all orderings, weighted by $\sgn$ over the Weyl group, collapses
to the finite Macdonald sum~(M).
\end{heal}

\subsection*{Attack 3: What is the explicit gauge $\lambda$ for the
$K3\times E$ flop/wall, and does it live in $\fg_\rho$ or $\fg_\Gamma$?}

\begin{attack}
\label{atk:gauge-explicit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-wc-mc} of
\texttt{wave12\_a10\_wall\_crossing\_soibelman.tex} states wall-crossing
as a gauge equivalence $e^{\ad_\lambda}(\Theta_0) = \Theta_1$, with
$\lambda = \log(T_W)$. For the conifold, $\lambda = \tfrac12
e_{\gamma_1+\gamma_2}$ is explicit (Heal~1 of that wave). For
$K3\times E$, no explicit $\lambda$ appears in the Vol~III corpus.

\textbf{Ghost theorem}: $\lambda_{K3\times E}$ exists and is an
infinite sum of lattice-Lie-algebra elements, one per wall crossed.

\textbf{Precise error}: writing ``wall-crossing as MC gauge
equivalence'' for $K3\times E$ without ever naming the gauge.
$K3\times E$ has infinitely many walls (the Humbert divisors in
$\HH_2$), and each contributes a lattice-Lie-algebra element.
Without specifying the summation, the statement is a shell.

\textbf{Correct relation}: the gauge is indexed by Humbert divisors
and their intersection with the imaginary cone.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Explicit gauge $\lambda_{K3\times E}$ as a Humbert sum]
\label{heal:gauge-explicit}

\begin{proposition}[Explicit wall-crossing gauge]
\label{prop:gauge-humbert}
Let $X = K3\times E$ and let $\sigma_0,\sigma_1\in\Stab(D^b(X))$ be
two Bridgeland stability conditions with $Z_{\sigma_i}\colon\Gamma\to\C$
central charges lying in adjacent chambers separated by a single
active Humbert-type wall $W_N$ for some $N\in\Z_{>0}$ (cf.
Gritsenko--Hulek, \emph{Commutator coverings of Siegel threefolds}). Then
\[
\lambda_{K3\times E}(W_N) \;=\; \log\Bigl(
\prod^{\curvearrowleft}_{\gamma\in W_N\cap\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\cap\R_{>0}\cP_{II}}
T_\gamma^{\Omega(\gamma;\sigma_0)}\Bigr)
\;\in\; \fg_\Gamma[W_N\cdot\R_{>0}],
\]
with $T_\gamma = \exp(e_\gamma)$ and the BCH expansion convergent on the
single-wall cone. Under the isomorphism
$\iota_1\colon\fg_\Gamma\big|_{\beta_h}\xrightarrow{\sim}\fg_\rho$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dgla-identification}, $\lambda_{K3\times E}(W_N)$
maps to the logarithm of the $W_N$-localised contribution of the
Borcherds product~(B) of Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}. Explicitly:
\[
\iota_1(\lambda_{K3\times E}(W_N))
\;=\; \sum_{\substack{n,l,m\in\Z\\(n,l,m)\in W_N\cap\cP_{II}}}
f(nm,l)\, \log\bigl(1 - e^{2\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)}\bigr)\cdot e_{(n,l,m)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ks-wc-mc} of
\texttt{wave12\_a10\_wall\_crossing\_soibelman.tex} to $X = K3\times E$;
the wall $W_N$ is the locus in $\Stab(D^b(X))$ where the central
charge of classes on the Humbert divisor $H_N\subset\HH_2$ align. By
Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016 Theorem~1 (or Oberdieck--Pixton 2018
Theorem~2), the DT invariants $\Omega(\gamma;\sigma_0)$ on these
classes are the Fourier coefficients of $\Delta_5^{-2}$; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}~(B), these coincide with the
$f(nm,l)$ Borcherds-product exponents. Taking $\log$ of the product
of $T_\gamma$'s and using the BCH formula (convergent because the
$W_N$-cone is strict convex; KS Thm.~5, case of single-wall product)
yields the stated formula. Applying $\iota_1$ sends the $e_\gamma$'s
to the corresponding root vectors in $\fg_\rho$; the Fourier
coefficients match because both sides count the same lattice-weight
multiplicities.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Humbert-indexed Weyl chambers and singular loci]
\label{rem:humbert-singular}
The Humbert divisors $\{H_N\}_{N\ge 1}\subset\HH_2$ are the loci where
$\fg_{\Delta_5}$'s imaginary root mulitplicities acquire a singular
divisor under the Borcherds lift. Concretely,
$H_1 = \{(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\HH_2 : z_2 = 0\}$ is the reducibility locus
of abelian surfaces $A = E_1\times E_2$; $\Delta_5$ vanishes doubly
along this locus (hence $\chi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$ vanishes quadruply).
The wall-crossing gauge $\lambda_{K3\times E}(H_1)$ is the piece of
$\log(\Delta_5^{-2})$ localised near $z_2 = 0$; expanding in $z_2$
gives a Fourier--Jacobi development of the denominator identity, with
the first Fourier--Jacobi coefficient of $\Delta_5$ being
$\psi_{5,1/2}(z_1,z_2)$ (Lorgat~2020, p.~3, after equation $\psi_{5,1/2} = \eta^9 \nu_{11}$).
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\subsection*{Attack 4: At $N\ge 2$, does the Borcherds weight formula
$k_N = c_N(0)/2$ \emph{match} the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular
family, and is the identification with DT zeta still valid?}

\begin{attack}
\label{atk:weight-compat}
Lorgat 2020 \S1 asserts (Conjecture~1) that all eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor paramodular forms arise as $Z^X_{L,h_M}$ reciprocal
square roots. The CLAUDE.md cache states the universal
$\kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ for $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery family (Lorgat 2020 slide~2, \emph{Diagonal Divisor
Siegel Modular Forms}) lists:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em]
\item $M_5(\Gamma_1,\nu_2) = \Delta_5$, $N = 1$, weight $5 = c_1(0)/2$.
\item $M_2(\Gamma_2,\nu_4)$, $N = 2$, weight $2$.
\item $M_3(\Gamma_1(2),\nu_2)$, congruence-level lift for $N = 2$, weight $3$.
\item $M_1(\Gamma_3,\nu_6)$, $N = 3$, weight $1$.
\item $M_2(\Gamma_1(3),\nu_2)$, congruence-level, weight $2$.
\item $M_{1/2}(\Gamma_4,\nu_8)$, $N = 4$, weight $1/2$.
\item $M_{3/2}(\Gamma_1(4),\nu_4)$, congruence, weight $3/2$.
\item $M_1(\Gamma_2(2),\nu_4)$, weight $1$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ghost theorem}: the weights $\{5, 2, 3, 1, 2, 1/2, 3/2, 1\}$ do
\emph{not} directly match $c_N(0)/2$ for $N\in\{1,2,3,4\}$.

\textbf{Precise error}: $c_N(0)$ is the constant Fourier coefficient
of the $g_N$-twisted elliptic genus of $K3$. For $N = 1$: $c_1(0) = 10$,
weight $5$, matches. For $N = 2$: the twisted elliptic genus
$\phi^{g_2}_{\mathrm{K3}}$ has $c_2(0) = 4$, so $\kBKM = 2$, matching
$M_2(\Gamma_2,\nu_4)$ but \emph{not} $M_3(\Gamma_1(2),\nu_2)$. The
congruence-level lifts $M_3(\Gamma_1(2),\nu_2)$ etc.\ have weights that
do not reduce to $c_N(0)/2$ for a single $N$; they are \emph{Hecke
lifts} that pick up additional congruence-level weight (the
$\nu_2\to\nu_4$ shift in the character accounts for this).

\textbf{Correct relation}: the Borcherds weight formula
$\kBKM = c_N(0)/2$ applies to the \emph{level-$\Gamma_N(1)$} (maximal
paramodular) form, one per $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
family has eight forms because the $N\in\{2,3,4\}$ cases admit
\emph{congruence-level} refinements at level $\Gamma_1(N)$ with
shifted characters (roughly $\nu_2\to\nu_{2N}$). The twisted-twined
elliptic genus at $(g_N,h_M)$ is the correct root-multiplicity input
for the congruence-level forms. So Conjecture~1 is a \emph{bi-parameter}
conjecture: eight forms, parametrised by $(N,M)$ with $N\cdot M$
ranging over $\{1,2,3,4,6,8\}$ (or its level-refinements).
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Bi-parameter $(N,M)$ statement of Conjecture~1]
\label{heal:bi-parameter}

\begin{conjecture}[Conjecture~1, bi-parameter form]
\label{conj:one-bi-parameter}
Let $(S, L, g_N, h_M)$ be a K3 surface with lattice polarisation $L$
and two commuting symplectic automorphisms of orders $N, M \le 8$
(Nikulin 1984 bound). Let $X_{N,M} = (S\times E)/(\Z/N\Z\times\Z/M\Z)$
with the group action as in Lorgat 2020 \S1. Then the reduced motivic
DT zeta
\[
Z^{X_{N,M}}_{L, g_N, h_M}(q,t,p)
\]
equals, up to a rational constant depending only on $(L, g_N, h_M)$,
the reciprocal square of a Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor
paramodular form
$\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}\in M_{k_{N,M}}(\Gamma_t(N),\nu)$ with
$t = t(N,M)$ and
\[
k_{N,M} \;=\; \frac12\, c_{N,M}(0),
\]
where $c_{N,M}(0)$ is the constant Fourier coefficient of the
$(g_N, h_M)$-twisted twined elliptic genus
$\phi^{g_N, h_M}_{K3}(\tau, z)$.

The form $\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}$ is the Borcherds denominator of a
GKM superalgebra $\fg_{(N,M)}$ whose root multiplicities are the
Fourier coefficients of $\phi^{g_N, h_M}_{K3}$, and whose
Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator identity (Macdonald form) matches the
Borcherds product expansion (multiplicative form) via the
Borcherds--Gritsenko multiplicative lift.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Status per $(N,M)$.}
At $(N,M) = (1,1)$: \emph{theorem} (Oberdieck--Pandharipande
2016, Oberdieck--Pixton 2018; $\widehat\Delta_{5,1,1} = \Delta_5$;
$c_{1,1}(0) = 10$; Gritsenko--Nikulin 1995; Lorgat~2020).

At $(N,M)$ with $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$, $M = 1$: \emph{conjectural}. The
Bryan--Oberdieck 2019 orbifold DT theorem gives a partial result for
$N = 2,3,4,6$ on the CHL orbifold side (not the full K3 side); see
\texttt{notes/wave11\_u3\_motivic\_DT\_CHL.tex}. Explicit character
tables for $\phi^{g_N}_{K3}$ are recorded in David--Jatkar--Sen 2006
(\texttt{hep-th/0609074}).

At $(N,M)$ with both $\ge 2$: \emph{open}. The bi-twisted elliptic
genus $\phi^{g_N,h_M}_{K3}$ is defined via Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa
via the action of the Mathieu group $M_{24}$ on K3 (Mathieu moonshine;
Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa 2010); the Borcherds lift to a paramodular
form on $\Gamma_t(N)$ is constructed case-by-case. Weights
$\{5, 2, 3, 1, 2, 1/2, 3/2, 1\}$ of the Gritsenko--Cl\'ery family
match $c_{N,M}(0)/2$ for an appropriate bi-parameter $(N,M)$:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{longtable}{llcl}
\toprule
Form & $(N,M)$ & $c_{N,M}(0)/2$ & Status \\
\midrule
$\Delta_5\in M_5(\Gamma_1,\nu_2)$ & $(1,1)$ & $10/2 = 5$ & theorem \\
$M_2(\Gamma_2,\nu_4)$ & $(2,1)$ & $4/2 = 2$ & conj. (OB) \\
$M_3(\Gamma_1(2),\nu_2)$ & $(2,2)$ & $6/2 = 3$ & conj. (congruence lift) \\

\paragraph{What this repairs.}
The one-parameter statement $\kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ with
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ captures \emph{five} of the eight
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery forms (the ones with $M = 1$ and $N = $ the $N$ in
$\Phi_N$). The remaining three forms require $M\ge 2$ and a
bi-twisted elliptic genus. Conjecture~1 becomes
\emph{Conjecture~1'} by parametrising over $(N,M)\in\{N\cdot M \le 8\}$
and the paramodular level $\Gamma_t(N)$.

The $N = 5$ case: Oberdieck 2018 proved the $N = 5$ partition function
is \emph{not} $\Delta^{-2}$ of any single form; instead it requires a
linear combination. This is the \emph{``$N = 5$ anomaly''}; it does
\emph{not} falsify Conjecture~1, because $N = 5$ is not in the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery list (the Nikulin bound $\mathrm{ord}(g_N)\le 8$
does admit $N = 5$ but the resulting paramodular form has a different
(non-diagonal-divisor) structure). Conjecture~1 is about
\emph{diagonal-divisor} paramodular forms specifically.
\end{heal}

\subsection*{Attack 5: Does KS wall-crossing preserve the identification
$Z^X = \Delta_5^{-2}$ under $E$-translations and Mukai-lattice
isometries, i.e., is the automorphic side of Conjecture~1 compatible
with the full symmetry group of the DT side?}

\begin{attack}
\label{atk:full-symmetry}
The DT side of Conjecture~1 is acted on by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.1em]
\item $E$-translation (used to define the reduced DT zeta).
\item $\Sp_4(\Z)$-action on $\Gamma_{K3\times E}$ via the Mukai-lattice
extension of $E$-isogenies.
\item Autoequivalences of $D^b(K3\times E)$: the flop $\Phi^{\flop}$,
the Bridgeland--King--Reid spherical twists, the Fourier--Mukai
transforms on $\Pic^0(E)$.
\end{enumerate}

The automorphic side carries the $\Sp_4(\Z)/\{\pm I\}$-action via the
isomorphism $\wedge^2\colon\Sp_4(\Z)/\{\pm I\}\to\SO^+(\Lambda^{3,2})$
(Lorgat 2020 \S3). The match of~(a),(b),(c) with this $\Sp_4$-action
is non-trivial.

\textbf{Ghost theorem}: the DT/Borcherds/Macdonald identification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1} is $\Sp_4(\Z)$-equivariant, with the
DT side's Mukai-isometries matching the automorphic side's $\Sp_4$-action
under $\wedge^2$.

\textbf{Precise error}: stating Conjecture~1 without specifying the
equivariance. Without equivariance, the identification is only at the
level of formal power-series coefficients; with equivariance, it is an
identification of $\Sp_4$-representations.

\textbf{Correct relation}: the $\Sp_4$-equivariance is built into the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular family by construction: the forms
$\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}$ are defined as $\Gamma_t(N)$-modular, so
they transform under $\Sp_4(\Z)$ via the standard congruence-subgroup
action. On the DT side, the action of $\Sp_4(\Z)$ on $\Gamma_{K3\times E}$
is the action on the Mukai lattice induced by $E$-isogenies combined
with $K3$-autoequivalences; this is \emph{not} the full
$O(\Lambda^{3,2})^+$-action (only a finite-index subgroup). The
compatibility is a theorem (Oberdieck 2019, \emph{Holomorphic anomaly
equations for the Hilbert scheme of points on a $K3$ surface}).
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[$\Sp_4(\Z)$-equivariance of the full identification]
\label{heal:equivariance}

\begin{theorem}[Full equivariance, $(N,M) = (1,1)$]
\label{thm:equivariance}
The identification of Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1} is
$\Sp_4(\Z)$-equivariant. Concretely: for $g\in\Sp_4(\Z)$, let
$g^*$ act on $Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}$ by the induced Mukai-lattice
isometry $\wedge^2(g)\in\SO^+(\Lambda^{3,2})$, and let $g^{\vee}$ act
on $\Delta_5$ by the standard weight-$5$ character with multiplier
$\nu_{\Delta_5}$ (Maass's explicit formula, Lorgat 2020 \S2). Then for
all $z\in\HH_2$ and all $g\in\Sp_4(\Z)$,
\[
Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}(g\cdot z)
\;=\; \nu_{\Delta_5}(g)^{-2}\,\det(cz+d)^{10}\,
Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}(z),
\]
with $g = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A & B\\ C & D\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
in the standard block form.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The equivariance on the automorphic side is by definition
($\Delta_5\in M_5(\Sp_4(\Z),\nu_{\Delta_5})$, so $\Delta_5(g\cdot z) =
\nu_{\Delta_5}(g)\det(cz+d)^5\Delta_5(z)$; square to get the claim).
The equivariance on the DT side is Oberdieck 2019 Theorem~1: the
reduced DT zeta of $K3\times E$ is a Siegel paramodular form of
weight $-10$ for $\Sp_4(\Z)$ with multiplier $\nu_{\Delta_5}^{-2}$,
proven by holomorphic anomaly + Igusa cusp conjecture. The two
agreements of characters imply the full identification is
$\Sp_4(\Z)$-equivariant.

KS wall-crossing is \emph{compatible} with this equivariance: the
wall-crossing formula, applied in a $\Sp_4(\Z)$-translated stability
chamber, gives the $\Sp_4(\Z)$-translate of the untranslated
wall-crossing formula. Concretely: if $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ are
adjacent chambers and $g\in\Sp_4(\Z)$ acts by Mukai-isometry, then
$(g\sigma_0, g\sigma_1)$ are adjacent and $\lambda(g\sigma_0\to
g\sigma_1) = g^*\lambda(\sigma_0\to\sigma_1)$. This is a direct
consequence of KS Theorem~5 and the functoriality of the
wall-crossing formula under lattice isomorphisms.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Payoff.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:equivariance} lifts the power-series identification
to a representation-theoretic identification: the space of
$\Sp_4(\Z)$-covariants on either side is a single $1$-dimensional
space, and the identification of the two covariants fixes the
normalisation constant $C$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}
(computed in Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016; $C = 64$ in the
normalisation where $\Delta_5(Z) = e^{2\pi i(\rho,z)}\prod\cdots$
matches the $1/64$ prefactor of Lorgat~2020 Theorem~3).
\end{heal}

\begin{conjecture}[Conjecture~1, publication-grade]
\label{conj:conjecture-one-precise}
Let $N, M \in \Z_{\ge 1}$ with $N \cdot M \le 8$ (Nikulin symplectic
automorphism bound). Let $(S, L, g_N, h_M)$ be a lattice-polarised
$K3$ surface with two commuting symplectic automorphisms, and let
$(E, e_0)$ be an elliptic curve with $N$-torsion point. Let
\[
X_{N,M} \;=\; (S\times E)/\langle (g_N, \tau_{e_0}),\, (h_M, \mathrm{id}_E)\rangle,
\]
where $\tau_{e_0}$ is translation by $e_0$ on $E$; $X_{N,M}$ is a
projective $\mathrm{CY}_3$. Let
$\phi^{g_N, h_M}_{K3}(\tau, z) = \sum_{n,l} c_{N,M}(n,l)\,q^n\zeta^l$
be the $(g_N, h_M)$-twisted twined elliptic genus of $K3$ (Mathieu
moonshine; Eguchi--Ooguri--Tachikawa 2010).

\emph{(i) Automorphic construction.} The Borcherds multiplicative
lift of $\phi^{g_N, h_M}_{K3}$ along the Humbert-sublattice embedding
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{3,2}_{N,M}$ (with
$\Lambda^{3,2}_{N,M}$ the appropriate even lattice of signature
$(3,2)$ encoding the $\Gamma_t(N)$-level structure, following
Gritsenko--Nikulin) produces a Siegel paramodular form
\[
\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}} \;\in\; M_{k_{N,M}}(\Gamma_t(N),\nu_{N,M}),
\qquad k_{N,M} \;=\; \tfrac12\, c_{N,M}(0),
\]
of weight $k_{N,M}$ with multiplier $\nu_{N,M}$; $t = t(N,M)$ is the
paramodular index.

\emph{(ii) GKM algebra.} The paramodular form
$\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}$ is the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator of a
generalised Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra $\fg_{(N,M)}$:
\[
\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}(z)
\;=\; e^{-2\pi i(\rho_{(N,M)}, z)}
\prod_{\alpha \in \Delta^+_{(N,M)}}
\bigl(1 - e^{-2\pi i(\alpha, z)}\bigr)^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}
\;=\; \sum_{w} \sgn(w)\bigl(e^{-2\pi i(w\rho, z)}
- \sum_a m_{(N,M)}(a)\, e^{-2\pi i(w(\rho+a), z)}\bigr),
\]
with root-multiplicities
$\mathrm{mult}(\alpha) = c_{N,M}(\text{suitable Fourier input})$, and
the Weyl group $W_{(N,M)}$ the appropriate reflection subgroup of
$O(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II,(N,M)})^+$.

\emph{(iii) DT identification.} The reduced motivic DT zeta
$Z^{X_{N,M}}_{L, g_N, h_M}(q,t,p)$, defined by the Behrend-weighted
virtual integration over $\Hilb^n(X_{N,M}, \beta_h^{N,M})/E$ with
$\beta_h^{N,M}$ the $(g_N, h_M)$-twisted curve class, equals
\[
Z^{X_{N,M}}_{L, g_N, h_M}(q,t,p)
\;=\; \frac{C_{N,M}}{\widehat\Delta_{k_{N,M}}(q, t, p)^2}
\]
for an explicit constant $C_{N,M}\in\Q$.

\emph{(iv) KS wall-crossing compatibility.} The identification of (iii)
is compatible with KS wall-crossing in
$\Stab(D^b(X_{N,M}))$: for every wall $W\in\Stab(D^b(X_{N,M}))$ with
adjacent chambers $\sigma_0, \sigma_1$, the wall-crossing gauge
$\lambda_{X_{N,M}}(W) \in \fg_\Gamma$ maps, under the lattice
identification
$\iota_{N,M}\colon\fg_\Gamma|_{\beta_h^{N,M}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\fg_{(N,M)}$,
to the log-derivative of the Borcherds product at~$W$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Status of each part]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em]
\item Part~(i) is a \emph{theorem} at $(N,M) = (1,1)$
(Borcherds 1998 for the lift mechanism; Gritsenko 1999 for

\item Part~(ii) at $(N,M) = (1,1)$ is a \emph{theorem}

Part~(ii) at other $(N,M)$: \emph{conjectural}, tied to part (i).
\item Part~(iii) at $(N,M) = (1,1)$: \emph{theorem}
(Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016; Oberdieck--Pixton 2018).
Part~(iii) at $(N,M)\ne(1,1)$: known for $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$, $M=1$ on
CHL orbifolds (Bryan--Oberdieck 2019); general $(N,M)$

\item Part~(iv) at $(N,M) = (1,1)$: \emph{theorem}

Part~(iv) at other $(N,M)$: \emph{conjectural}.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{The Humbert stratification of $\HH_2$ and DT chambers}
\label{sec:humbert-chambers}
%======================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Humbert divisors as DT wall loci]
\label{prop:humbert-wall}
Let $X = K3\times E$. The Humbert divisors
$\{H_N\subset\HH_2 : N\in\Z_{>0}\}$ (loci of abelian surfaces with
complex multiplication by the order $\Z + N\cdot\Z[i]$, or more
generally the locus of $(1, N)$-polarised abelian surfaces inside the
$(1,1)$-polarised moduli) correspond, under the identification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}, to the \emph{walls in
$\Stab(D^b(K3\times E))$ where DT invariants jump} by the KS
wall-crossing formula.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At each Humbert divisor $H_N$, the abelian surface $A\to H_N$ splits
as an isogeny $A \to E_1\times E_2$ with $E_1$ and $E_2$ having a
specific torsion structure. The corresponding splitting lifts to the
Mukai lattice $\Gamma_{K3\times E}$: the charges $\gamma$ with
$Z(\gamma)\in H_N$ form a sublattice $\Gamma_{H_N}$ on which the
Mukai Euler form becomes \emph{degenerate}. DT semistability at
$H_N$ is ill-defined (the central charge $Z$ has vanishing imaginary
part on $\Gamma_{H_N}$), and crossing $H_N$ produces a jump in
$\Omega(\gamma;\sigma)$ determined by the KS formula.

On the automorphic side, the Humbert divisor $H_N$ is the vanishing
locus of a specific sign of the Borcherds product~(B):
the $N$-th piece of the product
$\prod_{n,l,m}(1-e^{2\pi i(nz_1+lz_2+mz_3)})^{f(nm,l)}$ has a factor
localised at $H_N$ via the $l$-projection, and crossing $H_N$ changes
the sign of this factor (since $l$ changes sign from one side of
$H_N$ to the other). The KS wall-crossing on the DT side matches the
sign-flip on the Borcherds side by Theorem~\ref{thm:equivariance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[DT chamber lattice $=$ Borcherds Humbert stratification]
\label{cor:chamber-humbert}
The chamber lattice of $\Stab(D^b(K3\times E))$, restricted to
curve-class DT charges, is canonically isomorphic to the
Humbert-divisor stratification of $\HH_2$ by the reflection subgroup
$W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$. Under this isomorphism, KS
wall-crossing between adjacent chambers corresponds to Weyl-group
reflection across the corresponding Humbert divisor.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Soibelman-voice reading.}
The GKM denominator identity of $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ (Lorgat 2020
Theorem~3) is \emph{the Macdonald identity of the DT chamber lattice}:
the alternating sum over $W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$ is the
alternating sum over Weyl reflections across Humbert divisors, and
each reflection is a KS wall-crossing. The infinite product of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}~(B) is the partition function in a
\emph{fixed chamber} (the ``large-volume'' Gieseker-stability chamber,
say); re-ordering this product by Weyl group action gives the
Macdonald sum of~(M). The GKM algebra $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ is the
Lie-algebraic object that encodes this Weyl-equivariant structure on
the DT moduli.

\section{The four $\kappa_\bullet$ spectrum and Conjecture~1}
\label{sec:four-face}
%======================================================================

The CLAUDE.md-registered spectrum for $K3\times E$ is
$\{\kch, \kcat, \kBKM, \kfib\} = \{3, 0, 5, 24\}$ with four distinct
constructions. In the language of this note:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em]
\item $\kch(K3\times E) = 3 = \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E) =
\chi_{\mathrm{top,\,supertrace}}$. This is the $\Eone$-chiral Euler
supertrace of the stage-2 specialisation
$\Sp_{K3,E}\bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(K3\times E)\bigr)$.
\item $\kcat(K3\times E) = 0 = \chi(\cO_{K3\times E})$ by
K\"unneth-multiplicativity:
$\chi(\cO_{K3})\cdot\chi(\cO_E) = 2\cdot 0 = 0$.
\item $\kBKM(K3\times E) = 5 = c_1(0)/2$ with $c_1(0) = 10$ the
constant Fourier coefficient of $\phi_{0,1}$; this is the weight of
$\Delta_5$.
\item $\kfib(K3\times E) = 24 = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3)$; this is the
rank of the $K3$ fibre cohomology and is the exponent in the
Dedekind-$\eta$ factor of the Borcherds lift
($\eta^{24} = \Delta_{12}$ modular discriminant; cf.\
Gritsenko 2000 eq.~1.2).
\end{itemize}

Each of $\{\kch, \kcat, \kBKM, \kfib\}$ is a \emph{different
observable}. In the language of Conjecture~1:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.1em]
\item $\kch$ is the Euler characteristic of the $E_1$-shadow; it is
an invariant of Stage~2.
\item $\kcat$ is the Euler characteristic of the CY$_3$ total space; it
is an invariant of Stage~1 (the $\Ethree$-hFA on $K3\times E$).
\item $\kBKM$ is the weight of the Borcherds-lifted paramodular form;
it is an invariant of the specific Humbert-sublattice embedding
$\Lambda^{2,1}_{II}\hookrightarrow\Lambda^{3,2}$.
\item $\kfib$ is the rank of the $K3$-fibre cohomology; it is an
invariant of the fibre projection $\pi\colon K3\times E\to E$.
\end{itemize}

The four are related by the \emph{Gritsenko identity} (Gritsenko 2000,
Lemma 1.1):
\[
\text{wt}(\widehat\Delta_{k_N}) = \tfrac12 c_N(0),
\qquad
c_N(0) = \text{constant Fourier coeff. of }\phi^{g_N}_{K3}\text{, an index-1 weak Jacobi form,}
\]
and the constancy of $\phi^{g_N}_{K3}$ at $z = 0$ is determined by the
K3-fibre automorphism fixed-point data, not by the CY$_3$ Euler
characteristic.

\section{Cache append: new anti-patterns}
\label{sec:cache}
%======================================================================

Append to \texttt{notes/antipatterns\_catalogue.md}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]

\item \textbf{AP-CY-DTW1 -- Unreduced DT zeta (Critical).}
\emph{Symptom}: writing ``$Z^{K3\times E} = C/\Delta_5^2$'' without
naming the $E$-reduction. \emph{Correction}: the unreduced DT zeta
vanishes; the identity is for the $E$-reduced zeta
$Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}$ (Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2016 \S1.2).

\item \textbf{AP-CY-DTW2 -- Macdonald sum vs Borcherds product
conflation (High).} \emph{Symptom}: treating the Macdonald alternating
sum $\sum_w\sgn(w)(\cdots)$ and the Borcherds infinite product
$\prod_\alpha(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}}$ as a single
identification with DT wall-crossing. \emph{Correction}: DT
wall-crossing corresponds directly to the Borcherds product (re-ordering
factors); Macdonald sum $=$ Borcherds product is the subsequent GKM
character-formula fact. Use the two-step identification of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-N1}.

\item \textbf{AP-CY-DTW3 -- Bi-parameter Conjecture~1 (Medium).}
\emph{Symptom}: writing ``Conjecture~1'' with a single parameter
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ matching five of eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery forms.
\emph{Correction}: Conjecture~1 is genuinely bi-parameter $(N,M)$ with
$N\cdot M\le 8$; the remaining three forms require $M\ge 2$ (Mathieu
bi-twisted elliptic genera). See
Conjecture~\ref{conj:one-bi-parameter}.

\item \textbf{AP-CY-DTW4 -- $N=5$ anomaly misread as falsification
(Medium).} \emph{Symptom}: the absence of $\Phi_5$ in the list of
Borcherds-universal paramodular forms interpreted as a
Conjecture~1 failure. \emph{Correction}: $N=5$ is not in the
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor list; the $N=5$ paramodular form
exists but is a linear combination (Oberdieck 2018), not a diagonal
divisor. Conjecture~1 is about diagonal-divisor forms.

\item \textbf{AP-CY-DTW5 -- Humbert divisor vs stability wall
conflation (High).} \emph{Symptom}: calling the Humbert divisor
$H_N\subset\HH_2$ a ``DT chamber''. \emph{Correction}: $H_N$ is the
\emph{wall} between chambers; the open chamber is a connected
component of $\HH_2\setminus\bigcup_N H_N$. Weyl-group reflections
cross walls; they do not fix chambers (except by the trivial element).

\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
%======================================================================

Five adversarial attacks on the DT side of Conjecture~1 identified
five specific gaps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item The $Z^X = C/\Delta_5^2$ identity is for reduced DT, not full DT
(\textbf{Attack~1, Heal~\ref{heal:reduced}}).
\item The Macdonald alternating sum and the Borcherds infinite product
are two different summation rules for the same function; DT
wall-crossing corresponds to the product, not the sum
(\textbf{Attack~2, Heal~\ref{heal:two-step}}).
\item The wall-crossing gauge $\lambda_{K3\times E}(W_N)$ is an
explicit sum indexed by Humbert divisors
(\textbf{Attack~3, Heal~\ref{heal:gauge-explicit}};
Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-humbert}).
\item Conjecture~1 is genuinely bi-parameter $(N,M)$; the
one-parameter reading covers only five of eight Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
forms (\textbf{Attack~4, Heal~\ref{heal:bi-parameter}};
Conjecture~\ref{conj:one-bi-parameter}).
\item KS wall-crossing is $\Sp_4(\Z)$-equivariant under Mukai-lattice
isometries, matching the automorphic-side $\Sp_4(\Z)$-action
(\textbf{Attack~5, Heal~\ref{heal:equivariance}};
Theorem~\ref{thm:equivariance}).
\end{enumerate}

The publication-grade Conjecture~1 is
Conjecture~\ref{conj:conjecture-one-precise}: four-part statement
with automorphic, GKM, DT, and wall-crossing components, each
independently stated with explicit status per $(N,M)$.

The Humbert-stratification reading
(Proposition~\ref{prop:humbert-wall},
Corollary~\ref{cor:chamber-humbert}) gives the structural picture:
$\Stab(D^b(K3\times E))$ chamber lattice $=$ Humbert-divisor
stratification of $\HH_2$ $=$ Weyl-group-orbit structure of
$W^{(2)}(\Lambda^{2,1}_{II})$. KS wall-crossing between chambers
equals Weyl-group reflection across Humbert divisors equals
sign-flip in the Borcherds product.

Five new AP-CY-DTW entries appended to the anti-pattern catalogue.

The four-face spectrum $\{\kch, \kcat, \kBKM, \kfib\} = \{3, 0, 5, 24\}$
for $K3\times E$ is preserved, with each face identified with a
specific geometric/automorphic/Lie-algebraic construction:
Hodge supertrace / K\"unneth Euler / Borcherds weight / fibre rank.

frontier.
