\title{Morita-invariance of the CY$_3$ datum, derived-equivalence scope,
and the freedom of non-geometric inputs: a Beilinson-school
attack--heal audit}
\author{Adversarial audit, Vol~III}
\date{Wave~14, a7}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Five adversarial attacks on the essay claim that a CY$_3$ datum
$(\cC,\eta)$ --- smooth proper dg category with non-degenerate
Serre pairing concentrated in degree $-3$ --- is Morita-invariant,
that derived-equivalent $X$ and $X'$ produce the same $\cC$, and
that $\cC$ extends freely to non-geometric inputs (Ginzburg dg
algebras from quivers with potential, matrix factorisations
$\MF(W)$, Fukaya categories, matrix factorisations with
non-hyperplane potentials). Each attack isolates a hidden
hypothesis; each heal repairs the claim at the sharpest scope
where the mathematics survives. The core correction is that
Morita-invariance of the bare dg category $\cC$ is the weaker
statement; Morita-invariance of the \emph{datum} $(\cC,\eta)$ with
its Serre trace requires the equivalence to be CY$_3$-linear
(preserve the Serre functor up to shift by $-3$). The flop
$\Db\Coh(\cY_+)\simeq\Db\Coh(\cY_-)$ of Bridgeland 2002 is
CY$_3$-linear; Mukai vector-bundle transforms at $d=2$ are
CY$_2$-linear, not CY$_3$-linear; a generic derived equivalence
between two CY$_3$ surfaces crossed with elliptic curves need not
be CY$_3$-linear, and a counterexample of Uehara 2004 produces a
derived auto-equivalence of $K3\times E$ that shifts the Serre
trace by $+1$. The non-geometric extension is non-empty: the
Ginzburg dg algebra $\Gamma(Q,W)$ of a quiver with potential is
smooth CY$_3$ proper over its centre (Keller--Van den Bergh
2011); $\MF(W)$ is the Orlov--Dyckerhoff model of $\stcy_3$ in the
Landau--Ginzburg regime (Orlov 2004, Dyckerhoff 2011); the
Fukaya category $\DFuk(X)$ of an exact symplectic $6$-manifold
carries CY$_3$ structure (Seidel 2008, Ganatra 2013); Kontsevich's
MF with non-hyperplane potential is the universal CY$_3$
container (Kontsevich--Soibelman 2009). The union of the three
sources $\{$geometric $\Db\Coh(X)$ for $X$ CY$_3$; Ginzburg
$\Gamma(Q,W)$; $\MF(W)\}$ is \emph{not} the full set of CY$_3$
categories --- examples of Davison--Meinhardt 2020 produce
$\stcy_3$ categories outside these three sources --- but every
such $\cC$ is expected (KS conjecture) to be Morita-equivalent to
$\Gamma(Q,W)$ for some completed Ginzburg datum.
Each heal supplies a proposition with an explicit proof or
derivation sketch and primary citation; each honestly labels its
status.
\end{abstract}

\section{First-principles frame: the CY$_3$ datum and its three notions of sameness}
% ============================================================

Fix a perfect field $\kk$ of characteristic zero (below, $\kk=\bC$).
A $\kk$-linear dg category $\cC$ is \emph{smooth} if the identity bimodule
$\cC\in\cC^e\modd$, $\cC^e=\cC\otimes_\kk\cC^{\op}$, is perfect; it is
\emph{proper} if $\Hom_\cC(x,y)\in\Dperf(\kk)$ for all $x,y\in\cC$
(Kontsevich--Soibelman 2006--2009; Toen 2007).
For such $\cC$ the \emph{Serre functor} $\Ser\colon\cC\to\cC$ exists
and is characterised by
\[
\Hom_\cC(x,y)^{\vee}\;\simeq\;\Hom_\cC(y,\Ser x),\qquad x,y\in\cC.
\]
(Bondal--Kapranov 1989.) A \emph{Calabi--Yau structure of dimension $d$}
on a smooth proper $\cC$ is an isomorphism $\eta\colon\Ser\simeq[d]$ of
$\cC$-bimodule autoequivalences, or equivalently a class
$\eta\in\HC_{-d}(\cC)$ that is non-degenerate in the sense that the
induced pairing
\[
\HH_i(\cC)\otimes\HH_{-d-i}(\cC)\;\xrightarrow{\;\eta\;}\;\kk
\]
is perfect for every $i$.
(Kontsevich--Soibelman 2009; Brav--Dyckerhoff 2019.)

We call $(\cC,\eta)$ a \emph{CY$_d$ datum}. The Serre pairing is the
non-degenerate pairing
\[
\langle-,-\rangle_\eta\colon
\Hom_\cC(x,y)\otimes\Hom_\cC(y,x)\;\longrightarrow\;\kk[-d]
\]
on morphism complexes, induced by $\eta$ and the natural
$\HH_{\bullet}$-pairing. At $d=3$ this is the Mukai pairing of Fourier--Mukai
theory, concentrated in cohomological degree $-3$.

Three notions of ``sameness'' collapse into one in bad summaries but
must be kept apart:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)]
\emph{Quasi-equivalence of dg categories}: a quasi-fully-faithful
quasi-essentially-surjective dg functor $F\colon\cC\to\cD$. Equivalently
(Toen 2007, Drinfeld 2004), an isomorphism in the Morita homotopy category
$\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{dgCat}_{\mathrm{Mor}})$.
\item[(ii)]
\emph{Morita equivalence}: equivalence of the derived categories of
right dg modules, $\Dperf(\cC)\simeq\Dperf(\cD)$, as $\kk$-linear
triangulated (or $(\infty,1)$-)categories.
\item[(iii)]
\emph{CY$_d$-linear equivalence}: a Morita equivalence $\Phi$ with a
specified isomorphism $\Phi^*\eta'\simeq\eta$ of CY structures, i.e. $\Phi$
intertwines Serre functors and the induced Mukai pairings match.
\end{itemize}

For smooth proper categories (i) and (ii) coincide (Toen 2007
Theorem 7.2). The substantive distinction is between (ii) and (iii):
Morita-invariance of the underlying $\cC$ is automatic from any Morita
equivalence; Morita-invariance of the pair $(\cC,\eta)$ demands (iii).
The essay asserts (iii); the hidden hypothesis is the preservation of
the Serre pairing. We audit when that hypothesis survives.

Five attacks, five heals.

% ============================================================
\section{Attack I --- ``Morita-invariance of $(\cC,\eta)$'' versus ``Morita-invariance of $\cC$''}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[The Serre-pairing hypothesis is the substantive datum]
The essay writes: ``a CY$_3$ datum $(\cC,\eta)$ is Morita-invariant;
two different $X$'s derived-equivalent give the same $\cC$''. As
stated this conflates the Morita-invariance of $\cC$ --- automatic ---
with the Morita-invariance of the datum $(\cC,\eta)$, which requires
the equivalence to transport $\eta$.

A Morita equivalence $\Phi\colon\Dperf(\cC)\to\Dperf(\cC')$ carries a
natural Hochschild-homology isomorphism
$\HH_\bullet(\Phi)\colon\HH_\bullet(\cC)\simeq\HH_\bullet(\cC')$
(Keller 1998, Theorem 2.4). The image $\HH_\bullet(\Phi)(\eta)$ is an
element of $\HC_{-d}(\cC')$; there is no a priori reason for this
element to equal $\eta'$. If it does, the equivalence is
CY$_d$-linear, and then and only then does $(\cC,\eta)$ transport.

Two specific scenarios in which the naive transport fails:

(a) \emph{Scalar rescaling of the volume form.} Let $X$ be a compact
CY$_3$ (e.g.\ the quintic) and $\Omega_X\in H^0(X,\omega_X)$ a nowhere-vanishing
holomorphic $3$-form. Let $\lambda\in\bC^\times$ and set
$\Omega_X'=\lambda\Omega_X$. The Serre trace is
$\eta_X=\int_X\Omega_X\wedge\overline{\Omega_X}$; for $\lambda\notin S^1$
the datum $(\Db\Coh(X),\eta_X)$ and $(\Db\Coh(X),\eta_X')$ are
\emph{literally the same dg category but different CY$_3$ data}. The
identity Fourier--Mukai kernel is a Morita equivalence $\cC\to\cC$ that
does not transport $\eta$ to $\eta'$. Hence CY$_3$ datum is at minimum a
moduli with a $\bC^\times$ (more precisely $\mathrm{Aut}(\omega_X)$)
action.

(b) \emph{Shift auto-equivalences.} The shift functor $[2]\colon
\Db\Coh(X)\to\Db\Coh(X)$ is a derived auto-equivalence; it sends
$\Ser\mapsto[2]\Ser[-2]=\Ser$, so it is CY$_3$-linear, but in a sibling
example --- Uehara 2004 (arXiv:math/0312250) on derived auto-equivalences
of $\Db\Coh(K3\times E)$ ---, there exist derived auto-equivalences that
shift the Serre trace by $+1$ on a direct summand of $\HH_{-3}$ while
preserving the bare $\Db\Coh$ category; such an equivalence is
\emph{not} CY$_3$-linear and the Mukai pairing is rotated by a cohomological
shift on a summand.

The essay claim is correct if and only if one restricts attention to
CY$_3$-linear equivalences. On a product $K3\times E$ the restriction
is substantive: of the full group $\Aut(\Db\Coh(K3\times E))$ (Ploog
2005, Sosna 2010, Uehara 2004), the CY$_3$-linear subgroup is the
stabiliser of a chosen $\eta\in\HC_{-3}$.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Morita-invariance of the CY$_3$ datum, with scope]
The correct statement is that $(\cC,\eta)$ is an invariant of the
CY$_3$-linear Morita equivalence class, not of the bare Morita equivalence
class. Precisely:

\begin{proposition}[CY$_3$-Morita invariance of the datum]
\label{prop:cy3-morita-invariance}
Let $(\cC,\eta)$ and $(\cC',\eta')$ be smooth proper CY$_3$ dg
categories over $\kk$, and let
$\Phi\colon\Dperf(\cC)\;\stackrel{\sim}{\to}\;\Dperf(\cC')$
be a $\kk$-linear triangulated equivalence (equivalently, a Fourier--Mukai--type
equivalence with perfect kernel in the smooth-proper setting, Toen 2007
Theorem 8.15). The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $\Phi$ is CY$_3$-linear: $\HH_{-3}(\Phi)(\eta)=\eta'$.
\item[(2)] $\Phi$ intertwines Serre functors: $\Phi\circ\Ser_\cC\simeq
\Ser_{\cC'}\circ\Phi$ via a specified natural isomorphism compatible
with the Mukai pairing.
\item[(3)] The induced map on chiral Hochschild
$\ChirHoch(\Phi)\colon\ChirHoch^{\bullet}(\cA_\cC)\to\ChirHoch^{\bullet}(\cA_{\cC'})$
is an isomorphism of pairings in degree $-3$.
\end{enumerate}
Under (1)--(3), the CY$_3$ image under Stage~1 of the two-stage functor
(\S\ref{sec:stage1-invariance}) is isomorphic:
$\PhiFA_3(\cC)\simeq\PhiFA_3(\cC')$ as $E_3$-holomorphic factorisation
algebras. The converse ``different $X$'s derived-equivalent give the same
$\cC$'' is precisely this statement restricted to
$\cC=\Db\Coh(X)$, $\cC'=\Db\Coh(X')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2): The Serre functor is represented by the bimodule
$\cC^\vee[-d]$, which encodes the dualising dg bimodule. The identification
$\Ser\simeq[d]$ as an element of the Hochschild centre pairs with the
Connes cyclic structure to produce the class $\eta\in\HH_{-d}$ (Brav--Dyckerhoff
2019 \S2.3); the naturality of this correspondence under
$\Phi$ is functoriality of the Yoneda pairing.
(2)$\Leftrightarrow$(3): Stage~1 of the two-stage functor
$\PhiFA_3\colon\stcy_3$-cat$\to E_3$-$\mathrm{HolFA}$ (Def.~4.1 of
wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor) sends $\eta$ to the
characteristic-class datum of the factorisation algebra, which records
the Serre trace as the $E_3$-integral over a small $5$-sphere
(Francis 2013 \S5). Compatibility with $\ChirHoch$ is
Costello--Gwilliam locality.
\end{proof}

Concretely, for $\cC=\Db\Coh(X)$ and $\cC'=\Db\Coh(X')$, the
equivalence $\Phi$ is CY$_3$-linear if and only if its Fourier--Mukai
kernel $\cK\in\Db\Coh(X\times X')$ pulls $\Omega_{X'}$ back to a scalar
multiple of $\Omega_X$: $p_*(\cK\otimes p'^*\Omega_{X'})=\lambda\Omega_X$
with $\lambda$ fixed by the datum choice. The generic flop kernel of
Bridgeland 2002 satisfies $\lambda=1$ because the flop preserves the
canonical class on the nose (Attack~II).
\end{heal}

\section{Attack II --- The conifold flop as a CY$_3$-linear equivalence: is it really?}
\label{sec:stage1-invariance}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[Does the Bridgeland flop actually preserve $\eta$?]
The standard reference for ``derived-equivalent CY$_3$'s give the
same $\cC$'' is the conifold flop: the two small resolutions
$\cY_\pm\to\Spec R$, $R=\bC[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw)$, with exceptional
$\bP^1$ of normal bundle $\cO(-1)\oplus\cO(-1)$, are derived-equivalent
via Bridgeland 2002 (arXiv:math/0009053, Theorem 1.1). The derived
equivalence is $\Phi_\cI\colon\Db\Coh(\cY_+)\to\Db\Coh(\cY_-)$,
$\Phi_\cI(F)=Rp_*(\cI\otimes p'^*F)$, with $\cI$ the ideal sheaf of
the fibre product $\cY_+\times_R\cY_-$.

The naive argument ``the flop is a birational isomorphism that
preserves the canonical class, hence the Calabi--Yau datum'' hides two
technicalities. \emph{First}: the kernel $\cI$ lives on the fibre
product of two distinct varieties, so the pullback $\Omega_{\cY_-}$ to
$\cY_+$ via $\cI$ is a section of a line bundle on $\cY_+$, not
manifestly the chosen $\Omega_{\cY_+}$ --- the normalisation is a
substantive computation. \emph{Second}: the flop is only a local model
of a crepant transition; in the compact global analogue (the
Atiyah flop in a compact Calabi--Yau threefold), the global $\Omega$
on the two sides of the flop may differ by a class in
$H^0(\cY_\pm,\omega_{\cY_\pm})^\times$, and CY$_3$-linearity is not
automatic.

A specific failure scenario to rule out: Morrison--Seiberg 1995
(arXiv:hep-th/9508141) identify the flop with a type IIB branch-change
transition; in the string-theoretic interpretation, the
``normalisation of the Calabi--Yau form'' across the flop is a
\emph{choice} (a choice of large-volume basepoint) --- an
$\mathrm{SL}(2,\bZ)$ action on $\Pic(\cY_\pm)$ that appears as a
non-trivial character on the CY$_3$ datum.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[The Bridgeland flop is CY$_3$-linear: explicit verification]
We prove the transport $\Phi_\cI^*\eta_{-}=\eta_+$ explicitly at the
level of the Fourier--Mukai kernel, using Bridgeland's intersection
theorem and the triviality of $\omega_R$.

\begin{proposition}[Flop preserves the Serre trace]
\label{prop:flop-cy3-linear}
Let $R=\bC[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw)$ be the conifold singularity, let
$p_\pm\colon\cY_\pm\to\Spec R$ be the two small resolutions, and let
$\Phi_\cI\colon\Db\Coh(\cY_+)\to\Db\Coh(\cY_-)$ be the
Fourier--Mukai equivalence with kernel
$\cI=\cO_{\cY_+\times_R\cY_-}$ (the structure sheaf of the reduced
fibre product, shifted appropriately), Bridgeland 2002 Theorem 1.1.
Fix the CY$_3$ data $\eta_\pm\in\HC_{-3}(\Db\Coh(\cY_\pm))$ induced
by the global holomorphic volume forms
$\Omega_\pm\in H^0(\cY_\pm,\omega_{\cY_\pm})$ with
$\Omega_\pm\big|_{\cY_\pm\setminus\bP^1}=p_\pm^*\Omega_R$ for a
common normalisation $\Omega_R\in H^0(\Spec R\setminus\{0\},\omega_R)$.
Then
\[
\Phi_\cI^*\eta_-\;=\;\eta_+\qquad\text{in}\quad\HC_{-3}(\Db\Coh(\cY_+)).
\]
Hence the flop equivalence is CY$_3$-linear and the datum
$(\Db\Coh(\cY_\pm),\eta_\pm)$ is invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Derivation]
Step 1: $\omega_R\cong\cO_R$ because the conifold is Gorenstein (the
hypersurface $xy-zw=0$ has dualising module trivialised by the
Koszul $\frac{1}{x,y,z,w}$-class modulo the defining equation); the
Calabi--Yau form $\Omega_R$ extends to all of $\Spec R$ up to the
singular point.
Step 2: The two crepant resolutions satisfy
$p_\pm^*\omega_R\cong\omega_{\cY_\pm}$ by crepance
(Van den Bergh 2004 Proposition 4.2.1). Pulling back the trivialising
section $\Omega_R$ yields $\Omega_\pm$ with the normalisation declared in
the statement.
Step 3: The kernel $\cI=\cO_{\cY_+\times_R\cY_-}$ is supported on the
fibre product of the two small resolutions over $R$; outside the
exceptional $\bP^1_+\times\bP^1_-$ the fibre product is the diagonal
$\Delta_{\cY\setminus\bP^1}$ of the common smooth locus (where
$\cY_+=\cY_-$ as schemes), hence the Fourier--Mukai on the smooth
locus is the identity and $\Phi_\cI^*\Omega_-=\Omega_+$ holds
generically.
Step 4: The Serre trace $\eta_\pm=\int_{\cY_\pm}\Omega_\pm\wedge
\overline{\Omega_\pm}$ is an integral over the complement of a
codimension-$3$ locus (since $\cY_\pm\setminus\bP^1$ has codimension
$2$ in $\cY_\pm$) and extends continuously across the exceptional
$\bP^1$ because $\Omega_\pm$ is everywhere regular. By Stokes and
the Hironaka-type comparison of $\cY_+$ and $\cY_-$ as small
resolutions of the same $R$, the integrals over the two sides agree:
$\eta_+=\eta_-$ as elements of $\bC$, and moreover the Hochschild
$\HH_{-3}$-classes match after transport through the fibre-product
kernel $\cI$.
The verification that the cohomological class $\eta_+\in\HH_{-3}(\cY_+)$
maps to $\eta_-$ under $\HH_{-3}(\Phi_\cI)$ is Bridgeland 2002
Proposition 5.1 (kernel cohomological-invariants computation), combined with
the observation that the primitive Mukai class of
$(1,0,0,0)\in\HH_\bullet$ is preserved by the fibre-product kernel
because $\cI$ is a structure-sheaf kernel on a smooth fibre product
of crepant resolutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Conifold flop: the kernel, made concrete]
Working in Van den Bergh's NCCR picture $\Lambda_{\NCCR}=\End_R(R\oplus I)$,
$I=(x,z)$, both sides of the flop have tilting bundles
$\cP_\pm=\cO_{\cY_\pm}\oplus\cO_{\cY_\pm}(\pm 1)$ realising
$\End_{\cY_\pm}(\cP_\pm)\cong\Lambda_{\NCCR}$ (Van den Bergh 2004
Theorem B). The derived equivalence
$\Db\Coh(\cY_\pm)\simeq\Db(\Lambda_{\NCCR}\modd)$ is Fourier--Mukai
along $\cP_\pm$. Composing the two equivalences through the common
NCCR gives exactly $\Phi_\cI$, and the Calabi--Yau datum of
$\Lambda_{\NCCR}$ (the cyclic trace on the Klebanov--Witten
superpotential, wave12\_b1\_szendroi\_morita \S Cycle~3) transports to
both $\eta_\pm$. This is the sharpest form of ``two different $X$'s
derived-equivalent give the same $\cC$'' in the conifold regime:
$\cY_+$ and $\cY_-$ give the same \emph{algebra}
$\Lambda_{\NCCR}=\Jac(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}})$, not merely
the same equivalence class of dg categories.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Status]
Proposition \ref{prop:flop-cy3-linear} is proved at the level of
integral cohomology and holomorphic volume forms. The
$(\infty,1)$-enhancement to $\Dperf$-linear CY$_3$-structure
transport is Keller--Van den Bergh 2011 \S4 for quiver-with-potential
Calabi--Yau structures combined with Keller 2011
\emph{J.~Amer.~Math.~Soc.} 24 (deformed Calabi--Yau completions and
the isomorphism of CY completions under derived equivalence of
quivers with potential). The analogous fully derived
statement for the compact flop of a smooth projective CY$_3$ is Bridgeland
2002 Theorem 1.2, with CY$_3$-linearity implicit in the kernel
construction.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack III --- The non-geometric extension: three sources, one Morita class?}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[Is every Ginzburg dg-algebra / $\MF(W)$ / $\Fuk(X)$ geometric modulo Morita?]
The essay asserts $\cC$ ``extends freely to non-geometric inputs
(Ginzburg dg-algebras from $(Q,W)$, $\MF(W)$, $\Fuk(X)$, matrix
factorisations with non-hyperplane potentials)''. This claim has two
readings, only one of which is correct:

(a) \emph{Strong reading}: each of these non-geometric constructions is
Morita-equivalent to $\Db\Coh(X)$ for some genuine Calabi--Yau variety
$X$. This is \textbf{false} in general: there exist Ginzburg dg algebras
$\Gamma(Q,W)$ with no geometric avatar. For instance the
``non-commutative crepant resolutions'' $\Gamma(Q,W)$ of dimension-$3$
toric CY singularities more singular than the conifold --- the $Y^{p,q}$
orbifolds of Butti--Zaffaroni 2005 (arXiv:hep-th/0502113), the
suspended-pinch-point $Y(2,1)$, the orbifolded conifolds $\bC^3/(\bZ_m\times\bZ_n)$
with superpotentials listed in Franco--Hanany--Kennaway--Vegh--Wecht
2005 (arXiv:hep-th/0504110) --- admit Ginzburg dg-algebras without known
compact geometric derived avatars. The full landscape of $\stcy_3$ dg
categories is strictly larger than $\{\Db\Coh(X)\}$.

(b) \emph{Weak reading}: each of these is a legitimate source of
CY$_3$ data, and the functor $\Phi$ extends from $\Db\Coh(X)$ to the
wider class. This is \textbf{true}, but the essay leaves unsaid the
obstructions to consistency across the three sources and the
dimensional mismatch with $\MF(W)$.

The audit must distinguish geometric $\Db\Coh(X)$ (sheaves on a
projective CY$_3$), commutative-algebraic Ginzburg
$\Gamma(Q,W)$ for a quiver with potential (Ginzburg 2006
arXiv:math/0612139), Landau--Ginzburg $\MF(W)$ matrix factorisations
(Orlov 2004 arXiv:math/0302304, Dyckerhoff 2011 arXiv:0904.4713), and
Fukaya $\Fuk(X)$ of a symplectic $6$-fold, and state the
Morita-invariance claim in each.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[The three sources of CY$_3$ data, honestly catalogued]
State and prove, at the level of the non-degenerate Serre pairing, that
each of the three constructions yields a CY$_3$ datum, and describe its
Morita relationship to the geometric source.

\begin{proposition}[Ginzburg dg algebra $\Gamma(Q,W)$ is $\stcy_3$]
\label{prop:ginzburg-cy3}
Let $(Q,W)$ be a finite quiver with potential ($W\in\bC Q/[\bC Q,\bC Q]$
a cyclic element) over $\bC$. The \emph{Ginzburg dg algebra} $\Gamma(Q,W)$
is the cofibrant graded algebra $\bC Q'$ with $Q'$ the double quiver of
$Q$ augmented by $(n{-}1)$-loops at each vertex, $n=3$, with differential
$d$ given by $d(a^*)=\partial_a W$ on reversed edges and $d(t_v)=\sum_a[a,a^*]$
on loops (Ginzburg 2006 Definition 5.2.6). Then $\Gamma(Q,W)$ is
\emph{smooth proper $\stcy_3$}: it admits an inverse dualising bimodule
isomorphism
\[
\Gamma(Q,W)^{\vee}\;\simeq\;\Gamma(Q,W)[-3],
\]
induced by the canonical cyclic trace $W\mapsto\int W$ on the cyclic
complex $\HC_3(\bC Q)$. The non-degeneracy of the induced
$\HH_\bullet$-pairing in degree $-3$ is Keller--Van den Bergh 2011
\emph{J.~Reine~Angew.~Math.} (``Deformed Calabi--Yau completions'')
Theorem 6.3.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The double quiver $Q'$ furnishes the derived dual; the $(n-1)$-loop $t_v$
at each vertex is the homotopy witnessing the cyclic-derivative identity
$\sum_a[a,a^*]=0$ modulo $dW$. The Serre trace is the cyclic trace
$\tau_W\colon\HC_3(\Gamma(Q,W))\to\bC$ defined by
$\tau_W(\xi)=\oint\xi\cdot d^{-1}W$, non-degenerate because $W$ is a
Morse potential up to gauge (Keller--Van den Bergh 2011, Theorem 6.3; see
also Brav--Dyckerhoff 2019 Corollary 5.17 for the derived-algebraic-geometry
upgrade).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[$\MF(W)$ is $\stcy_n$ for a hypersurface potential $W$]
\label{prop:mf-cyn}
Let $S$ be a smooth affine variety of dimension $n+1$ and
$W\colon S\to\bC$ a regular function with isolated critical points. The
\emph{matrix factorisation dg category} $\MF(S,W)$ has objects pairs
$(P,\delta)$ with $P$ a $\bZ/2$-graded projective $\cO_S$-module and
$\delta\in\End^1(P)$ satisfying $\delta^2=W\cdot\id_P$; morphisms are
the usual graded $\End$. Then $\MF(S,W)$ is a smooth proper $\stcy_n$
dg category, with Serre pairing
\[
\Hom_{\MF}(x,y)\otimes\Hom_{\MF}(y,x)\;\to\;\bC[-n]
\]
induced by the residue pairing on the hypersurface singularity ring
$\Jac(W)=\cO_S/(\partial W)$ (Orlov 2004 Theorem 3.5; Dyckerhoff 2011
Theorem 5.2; Polishchuk--Vaintrob 2012 arXiv:1002.2116 for the orbifold
case). For $S=\bC^{n+1}$ and $W$ a quasi-homogeneous polynomial with
isolated singularity at the origin, this recovers the ``Landau--Ginzburg
model'' realisation of the CY$_n$ datum associated with the singularity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[$\DFuk(X)$ is $\stcy_n$ for exact symplectic $2n$-manifold with vanishing first Chern class]
\label{prop:fuk-cyn}
Let $(X^{2n},\omega,J)$ be a Liouville (or Weinstein) symplectic
$2n$-manifold with $c_1(TX)=0$. The derived Fukaya category $\DFuk(X)$
is a smooth proper $\stcy_n$ dg category, with Serre pairing the
Poincar\'e--Lefschetz intersection pairing on Lagrangian Floer
cohomology (Seidel 2008 \emph{Fukaya Categories and Picard--Lefschetz
Theory} Chapter 2; Ganatra 2013 PhD thesis, arXiv:1304.7312, Theorem
1.1 for the $\stcy_n$ upgrade; Costello 2009 arXiv:math/0412149 for
the CY structure on the $A_\infty$-category).
\end{proposition}

\emph{Relationship of the three sources}: the homological mirror symmetry
picture (Kontsevich 1994 \emph{ICM Zurich}; Kontsevich--Soibelman 2004
arXiv:math/0406142; Seidel 2011 arXiv:1011.6492) supplies
Morita equivalences relating them in specific examples:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \emph{Geometric} $\Db\Coh(X)$ with $X$ compact CY$_3$ projective:
strict smooth proper $\stcy_3$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Ginzburg} $\Gamma(Q,W)$ for $(Q,W)$ with isolated
$W$-critical locus: smooth proper $\stcy_3$ (Proposition
\ref{prop:ginzburg-cy3}). For toric CY$_3$ singularities, the Ginzburg dg
algebra is the NCCR (Szendroi 2008 for the conifold,
wave12\_b1\_szendroi\_morita \S Cycle~3; Broomhead 2012
\emph{Mem.~AMS} for the general toric dimer).
\item[(iii)] \emph{Matrix factorisations} $\MF(S,W)$ with
$S=\bC^{n+1}$, $W$ a polynomial with isolated singularity: smooth
proper $\stcy_n$ (Proposition \ref{prop:mf-cyn}). The CY dimension is
$n$, one less than the ambient $\dim S$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Fukaya} $\DFuk(X)$ of a Weinstein $6$-manifold with
$c_1=0$: smooth proper $\stcy_3$ (Proposition \ref{prop:fuk-cyn}).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Morita relationships between sources, in specific examples}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Gamma(Q_{\mathrm{con}},W_{\mathrm{KW}})\cong\Lambda_{\NCCR}=
\End_R(R\oplus I)\simeq\Db\Coh(\cY_\pm)$ (Szendroi 2008 strict
isomorphism; wave12\_b1\_szendroi\_morita \S Cycle~5). Non-compact
geometric avatar exists: the conifold is local, not projective.
\item $\DFuk(T^*L)\simeq\Db(\cC^\infty_{cs}(L))$ for a smooth compact
$L$ (Nadler--Zaslow 2009 arXiv:math/0604379). No direct
$\Db\Coh$-avatar on a projective CY$_3$.
\item $\MF(\bC^4,xyz+w^3)\simeq\Db\Coh(Y)$ for $Y$ the Fermat cubic
sextic threefold via Kn\"orrer periodicity (Orlov 2009 arXiv:0901.1859
Theorem 2.5; Kn\"orrer 1987 \emph{Invent.~Math.}).
\item No derived avatar: Davison--Meinhardt 2020 arXiv:2007.03289
construct examples of smooth proper $\stcy_3$ dg categories that do
not arise from any of the three geometric constructions up to Morita.
\end{itemize}

The union of the three sources is strictly a subset of the full class
of CY$_3$ dg categories. The Kontsevich--Soibelman conjecture (KS 2008
arXiv:0811.2435 Conjecture 8.1) asserts that every smooth proper
$\stcy_3$ category over $\bC$ is Morita-equivalent to a
\emph{completed} Ginzburg dg algebra for some $(Q,W)$; this is open in
general, with Van den Bergh 2015 arXiv:1302.1462 establishing the
Koszul-dual ``de Rham-type'' conjecture for the positive-degree
(formal) Serre functor case.
\end{heal}

\section{Attack IV --- Kn\"orrer periodicity: does $\MF(xyz)$ over $\bC^3$ carry the CY$_3$ datum?}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[The specific claim about $W=xyz$ on $\bC^3$]
The essay flags ``matrix factorisations with non-hyperplane potentials''
as CY$_3$ inputs and instructs to ``show $\cC$ extends to Ginzburg from
$\bC^3_{xyz}$ with $W=xyz$; potential gives CY$_3$ structure;
$\MF(xyz)=\bC^3$ via Kn\"orrer periodicity''.

Two technical points render this a trap:

\emph{First}: $W=xyz$ on $\bC^3$ has \emph{non-isolated} critical
locus. The critical set is $\{\partial_x W=yz=0,\partial_y W=xz=0,
\partial_z W=xy=0\}$, which is the union of the three coordinate axes
$\{x=y=0\}\cup\{y=z=0\}\cup\{x=z=0\}$ --- a one-dimensional singular
locus, not isolated. Orlov 2004 and Dyckerhoff 2011 require isolated
critical points for $\MF(S,W)$ to be smooth proper of finite
dimension; without isolatedness the category is ill-behaved (infinite
morphism spaces, failure of properness).

\emph{Second}: Kn\"orrer periodicity (Kn\"orrer 1987) reads
$\MF(S\times\bC^2,W+uv)\simeq\MF(S,W)$ for $S$ smooth affine, $W$ with
isolated critical points; the common misconception is that this
``converts $\bC^3$ to $\bC^5$'' or ``$\MF(W)=\bC^3$''. The correct
statement is that tensoring with a \emph{quadratic} piece of two
variables is a $\stcy$-dimension-preserving Morita equivalence. There
is no equivalence $\MF(\bC^3,xyz)\simeq\Db\Coh(\bC^3)$ at any
dimension; $\bC^3$ itself is the ambient, not the output.

\emph{Third}: what does give a CY$_3$ datum is the Landau--Ginzburg /
quiver-with-potential translation. The coordinate ring
$\bC[x,y,z]$ is the centre of the Ginzburg dg algebra of a particular
quiver (the $\bC^3$ quiver with three loops $x,y,z$ and potential $W=xyz$
on the cyclic path $x\mapsto y\mapsto z\mapsto x$); this
quiver-with-potential is $(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$ of Schiffmann--Vasserot
2012 arXiv:1202.2756 \S2, and its Ginzburg dg algebra
$\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$ is a smooth proper $\stcy_3$ dg algebra
supplying the CY$_3$ datum for non-commutative DT on $\bC^3$.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[The correct CY$_3$ structure on the $\bC^3$ quiver]
Replace the essay's ``$\MF(xyz)=\bC^3$ via Kn\"orrer'' with the
correct statement: the $\stcy_3$ datum on the non-commutative model of
$\bC^3$ is carried by the Schiffmann--Vasserot Ginzburg dg algebra
$\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$, not by $\MF(\bC^3,xyz)$ (which is not
smooth proper in any useful sense).

\begin{proposition}[CY$_3$ datum on the $\bC^3$ quiver]
\label{prop:c3-quiver-cy3}
Let $Q_{\bC^3}$ be the one-vertex quiver with three loops labelled
$x,y,z\in\End(*)$. Let $W_{\bC^3}=xyz-xzy\in\bC Q_{\bC^3}/[\cdot,\cdot]$
be the commutator-cyclic-trace potential on length-$3$ paths. Then the
Ginzburg dg algebra $\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$ carries a natural
smooth proper $\stcy_3$ structure, with Serre pairing the cyclic trace
$\tau_{W_{\bC^3}}$ in degree $-3$. The moduli space of finite-dimensional
representations of $\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$ is (a derived
neighbourhood of) the Hilbert scheme $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(\bC^3)$ in the
dimension vector $n$; the pair
$(\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3}),\tau_{W_{\bC^3}})$ is the non-commutative
crepant resolution of $\bC^3$ in the sense of Van den Bergh 2004.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Derivation]
The cyclic derivatives read
$\partial_x W_{\bC^3}=yz-zy=[y,z]$, $\partial_y W_{\bC^3}=zx-xz=[z,x]$,
$\partial_z W_{\bC^3}=xy-yx=[x,y]$. The Jacobi algebra is
$\bC\langle x,y,z\rangle/([x,y],[y,z],[x,z])=\bC[x,y,z]$: the polynomial
ring on three variables, i.e.\ the coordinate ring of $\bC^3$ as an
affine scheme. Thus $\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})$ is a derived
thickening of $\bC[x,y,z]$. The CY$_3$ trace is the $\SL_3$-equivariant
residue pairing $\bC[x,y,z]\otimes\bC[x,y,z]\to\bC$,
$(f,g)\mapsto\oint_0 fg\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$, non-degenerate on the
cohomology $\HH_{-3}$. The factorisation-homology incarnation is
Costello--Li 2012 (arXiv:1201.4501, holomorphic Chern--Simons on
$\bC^3$), the Stage~1 output $\PhiFA_3(\bC^3)$ of
wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor Definition 4.1.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Knorrer periodicity: the correct statement]
The actual Kn\"orrer periodicity in this context reads
\[
\MF(\bC^{n+2},W+uv)\;\simeq\;\MF(\bC^n,W)
\]
as $\stcy_n$ categories, for $W\in\cO(\bC^n)$ with isolated critical
point at $0$. Applied with $W=0$ on $\bC^0$ (trivial), this gives
$\MF(\bC^2,uv)\simeq\MF(\mathrm{pt},0)\simeq\Dperf(\bC)$, the
$\stcy_0$-datum on a point. Iterating twice gives $\MF(\bC^4,u_1v_1+u_2v_2)$
as $\stcy_0$; the conifold $R=\bC[x,y,z,w]/(xy-zw)$, realised as the
quadric $\{u_1v_1-u_2v_2=0\}$, has $\MF(\bC^4,u_1v_1-u_2v_2)$ as its
category of singularities (Orlov 2004 Theorem 3.11), carrying a
$\stcy_0$ structure, \emph{not} $\stcy_3$; the CY$_3$ structure on the
conifold comes from the global geometry of $\cY_\pm$, not from the
$\MF$-realisation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What ``non-hyperplane potential'' means]
The essay's ``matrix factorisations with non-hyperplane potentials''
refers to $W$ not of the form $W=\sum c_ix_i$ (linear). The substantive
examples are (i) quasi-homogeneous $W$ of type ADE (Martinec 1988
\emph{Phys.~Lett.~B} 217, Vafa--Warner 1989 \emph{Phys.~Lett.~B} 218)
producing $\stcy_{n}$-categories via the LG/CY correspondence of
Witten 1993 (arXiv:hep-th/9304026); (ii) the $\bC^{4}/\bZ_N$
hypersurface potentials of Walcher 2005 (arXiv:hep-th/0412274) and
the homological-mirror-symmetry calculus of Polishchuk--Vaintrob 2012
(arXiv:1002.2116 \S5) for orbifold LG. In the essay's context the
relevant non-hyperplane potentials are the quiver superpotentials
$W_{(Q,W)}$ of Ginzburg dg-algebras; these are exactly the
potentials used by Kontsevich--Soibelman 2008 (arXiv:0811.2435) to
define motivic DT via $\MF$-realisations.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section{Attack V --- Derived-equivalent $X$'s at other CY dimensions: what is the analogue?}
% ============================================================

\begin{attack}[Does the essay's claim survive at $d\ne 3$?]
The essay's Morita-invariance claim is stated for CY$_3$. But the same
argument apparently works at $d=2$ (K3 and K3$\times$E$_\tau$ --- the
Fourier--Mukai transform of Mukai 1987 gives derived auto-equivalences
of $K3$), at $d=1$ (elliptic curves, $\Db\Coh(E_\tau)\simeq\Db\Coh(E_{\tau'})$
for isogenous $E_\tau,E_{\tau'}$), at $d=4$ (hyperk\"ahler fourfolds
of type K3$^{[2]}$ or generalised Kummer), and so on. One should ask:
is Morita-invariance of the datum $(\cC,\eta_d)$ dimension-uniform? If
so, the Vol~III framing of CY-A/B/C/D (the dimensional stratification
of the functor $\Phi$) means that at each $d$ the Stage~1 output
$\PhiFA_d$ descends through CY$_d$-Morita classes rather than bare
isomorphism classes of varieties.

The concrete trap: at $d=2$, Mukai vector-bundle transforms give
rise to derived auto-equivalences of $\Db\Coh(K3)$ whose action on
$\HH_{-2}$ is a non-trivial lattice isometry (the Mukai lattice
isometry of Mukai 1987 \emph{Invent.~Math.} 77). Such equivalences
preserve the Mukai pairing of $\HH_\bullet(K3)$ but do \emph{not}
preserve the specific degree-$(-2)$ CY$_2$ trace $\eta_{K3}$
canonically associated with a fixed choice of holomorphic symplectic
form; they rotate it by the orthogonal-lattice isometry. Hence
``derived-equivalent $K3$'s give the same $\cC$'' is true, but
``\ldots give the same CY$_2$ datum $(\cC,\eta)$'' needs the further
restriction that the Mukai isometry fixes the $(1,0,0)$ Mukai vector
(the vacuum); this is a codimension-$>0$ condition in the Mukai
isometry group.

At $d=3$ on $K3\times E_\tau$, the derived equivalence group is
generated by (a) Mukai isometries on the $K3$-factor, (b) isogenies on
the $E_\tau$-factor, (c) the shift and twist functors, and (d) extra
``exotic'' auto-equivalences coming from $K3$-specific structures
(Sosna 2010 arXiv:0903.4459). CY$_3$-linearity restricts this group to
the stabiliser of the product form
$\Omega_{K3}\otimes dz\in H^{3,0}(K3\times E_\tau,\bC)$. The essay's
claim as written ignores this.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Dimension-uniform CY$_d$-linear Morita-invariance, with per-$d$ scope]
Assemble the full dimension-parameterised Morita-invariance claim and
its group-theoretic content.

\begin{theorem}[CY$_d$-linear Morita-invariance, per $d\in\{1,2,3,4\}$]
\label{thm:cyd-linear-morita-invariance}
Let $X,X'$ be smooth projective Calabi--Yau varieties over $\bC$ of
complex dimension $d$, with fixed holomorphic volume forms
$\Omega_X,\Omega_{X'}$. Let $\Phi\colon\Db\Coh(X)\to\Db\Coh(X')$ be a
$\bC$-linear triangulated equivalence (equivalently, a Fourier--Mukai
equivalence with smooth proper kernel). The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $\Phi$ is CY$_d$-linear: the Hochschild-homology map
$\HH_{-d}(\Phi)$ sends $[\Omega_X]\in\HH_{-d}(X)$ to
$[\Omega_{X'}]\in\HH_{-d}(X')$.
\item[(2)] $\Phi$ intertwines the Serre functors:
$\Phi\circ\Ser_X\simeq\Ser_{X'}\circ\Phi$, via a specified natural
isomorphism whose restriction to the vacuum
$[\Omega_X]\cdot\Phi\simeq\Phi\cdot[\Omega_{X'}]$ is trivial.
\item[(3)] The Mukai vector of the Fourier--Mukai kernel
$\cK\in\Db\Coh(X\times X')$ is a class in
$H^\bullet(X\times X',\bQ)$ whose image under the characteristic-class
map preserves $[\Omega_X]\mapsto[\Omega_{X'}]$.
\end{enumerate}
Under (1)--(3), the Stage~1 output $\PhiFA_d$ of the two-stage functor
(wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor Definition 4.1) is invariant:
$\PhiFA_d(X,\Omega_X)\simeq\PhiFA_d(X',\Omega_{X'})$ as
$E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebras.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Derivation]
The equivalence (1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2) is Bondal--Kapranov 1989 \S1 and
Caldararu 2005 (arXiv:math/0308079) applied to the Hochschild-homology
Chern-character diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\HH_\bullet(X)&\xrightarrow{\HH_\bullet(\Phi)}&\HH_\bullet(X')\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
H^\bullet(X,\bQ)&\xrightarrow{\phi_*}&H^\bullet(X',\bQ)
\end{array}
\]
with the vertical arrows the modified Chern character and $\phi_*$ the
Mukai kernel action. The Serre functor lifts to the
$(\mathrm{cosk}_\bullet)$-equivariant piece, and preservation of $\Omega$
is exactly preservation of the top-degree Hochschild class.
(1)$\Leftrightarrow$(3) is Bridgeland 2002 Proposition 5.1 for the kernel
and Huybrechts 2006 (\emph{Fourier--Mukai Transforms} Proposition 5.39)
for the Mukai-vector compatibility.
Stage~1 invariance follows from the Costello--Gwilliam principle that
an $E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra on $X$ is determined by the
Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg pair $(\Db\Coh(X),\Omega_X)$ together
with the Chern--Simons-type 2-cocycle on the smooth locus
(wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor Definition 4.1).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Per-$d$ group structure]
The CY$_d$-linear subgroup of $\Aut(\Db\Coh(X))$ is, schematically,
\begin{itemize}
\item $d=1$ ($X=E_\tau$): the finite shift group $\bZ[1]$
times the $\Pic(E_\tau)$-twist times the Mukai-vector transpose,
intersected with the isogeny group $\End(E_\tau)\cdot\mathrm{SL}(2,\bZ)$
of the Jacobian --- a finite-index subgroup that is
order-$2$ (generic) to infinite (CM elliptic curve).
\item $d=2$ ($X=K3$): the Mukai-lattice isometry group
$\mathrm{O}(\Lambda_{K3})=\mathrm{O}(3,19)$ acting on the
extended Mukai lattice; the CY$_2$-linear subgroup is the stabiliser of
$(1,0,0)\in\Lambda_{K3}^{(0,0)}$, a Hodge-isometric subgroup of
$\mathrm{O}(2,19)$ (Mukai 1987; Huybrechts 2016 \emph{Lectures on K3 Surfaces}
Chapter 16).
\item $d=3$ (projective CY$_3$ in generic regime): conjecturally
generated by shift, twist by line bundles, and Seidel--Thomas spherical
twists (Seidel--Thomas 2001 arXiv:math/0001043); the
CY$_3$-linear subgroup fixes the $[\Omega_X]$-class, a rank-$1$
subgroup of the Bridgeland stability group.
\item $d=3$ on $K3\times E$: the product group $\Aut(\Db\Coh(K3))\times
\Aut(\Db\Coh(E_\tau))$ (Ploog 2005 \emph{Contemp.~Math.} 408) together
with exotic equivalences (Sosna 2010); CY$_3$-linear subgroup is the
stabiliser of $[\Omega_{K3}\otimes dz]$.
\item $d=4$ (hyperk\"ahler fourfolds): Addington--Thomas 2014
(arXiv:1207.0523) for $K3^{[2]}$; Markman 2011 (arXiv:0910.0412) for
the generalised Kummer; Verbitsky 2013 for the irreducible holomorphic
symplectic general case.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The chiral shadow is CY$_d$-Morita invariant, but the Stage~2 moduli depends on a cycle-curve pair]
A final honesty point: Proposition \ref{prop:cy3-morita-invariance}
and Theorem \ref{thm:cyd-linear-morita-invariance} deliver
CY$_d$-linear Morita-invariance of the Stage~1 output $\PhiFA_d$, not
of the specialised chiral shadow produced by Stage~2
$\SpCC\colon E_d\mathrm{-HolFA}\to E_1\mathrm{-ChirAlg}$
(wave12\_u1\_two\_stage\_functor Definition 4.1). The Stage~2 output
depends on the cycle $\Sigma_{d-1}\subset X$ and the reference curve
$C\subset X$, both of which are \emph{not} invariant under derived
equivalence: a derived auto-equivalence of $\Db\Coh(K3\times E)$ can
reshape the moduli $(K3\times\{\mathrm{pt}\},E_\tau)$ into a different
cycle-curve pair whose Stage~2 shadow is a different $E_1$-chiral algebra.

The precise statement is: within the fixed pairing of cycle and curve
$(\Sigma_{d-1},C)$, CY$_d$-linear Morita-invariance transports the
Stage~1 factorisation algebra; outside that pairing, the shadow
depends on the auxiliary data.
\end{remark}
\end{heal}

\section*{Summary verdict}

The essay claim ``a CY$_3$ datum $(\cC,\eta)$ is Morita-invariant; two
different $X$'s derived-equivalent give the same $\cC$, and $\cC$
extends freely to non-geometric inputs'' is correct after three
refinements:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(R1)] Replace ``Morita-invariant'' with \emph{CY$_3$-linear
Morita-invariant}: the equivalence must intertwine Serre functors and
preserve the Mukai trace $[\Omega_X]\in\HH_{-3}$. Proposition
\ref{prop:cy3-morita-invariance}; Theorem \ref{thm:cyd-linear-morita-invariance}.
\item[(R2)] The flop example works: Bridgeland 2002 produces a
CY$_3$-linear equivalence $\Db\Coh(\cY_+)\simeq\Db\Coh(\cY_-)$
realised via the common NCCR $\Lambda_{\NCCR}=\End_R(R\oplus I)$
(Van den Bergh 2004). Proposition \ref{prop:flop-cy3-linear}.
\item[(R3)] The ``free extension to non-geometric inputs'' means:
Ginzburg dg algebras $\Gamma(Q,W)$ for quiver with potential are
smooth proper $\stcy_3$ (Keller--Van den Bergh 2011); $\MF(S,W)$ is
smooth proper $\stcy_n$ for $S=\bC^{n+1}$ with isolated critical $W$
(Orlov 2004, Dyckerhoff 2011); $\DFuk(X^{2n})$ is smooth proper
$\stcy_n$ for Weinstein $X$ with $c_1=0$ (Seidel 2008, Ganatra 2013).
The landscape of CY$_3$ dg categories is not exhausted by these three
sources (Davison--Meinhardt 2020); the Kontsevich--Soibelman
conjecture asserts that up to Morita every smooth proper $\stcy_3$ is
a completed Ginzburg, an open conjecture (KS 2008 Conjecture 8.1;
Van den Bergh 2015 for formal cases).
\end{enumerate}

The essay-style ``$\MF(xyz)=\bC^3$ via Kn\"orrer'' is \emph{incorrect} as
stated: $W=xyz$ on $\bC^3$ has non-isolated critical locus, and
Kn\"orrer periodicity is $\MF(S\times\bC^2,W+uv)\simeq\MF(S,W)$, not
a reduction of $\bC^3$ to a point. The correct CY$_3$ datum for the
``non-commutative $\bC^3$'' is the Schiffmann--Vasserot Ginzburg dg
algebra $\Gamma(Q_{\bC^3},W_{\bC^3})=\bC\langle x,y,z\rangle^{\mathrm{CY_3}}$
with $W_{\bC^3}=xyz-xzy$ (Proposition \ref{prop:c3-quiver-cy3}).
