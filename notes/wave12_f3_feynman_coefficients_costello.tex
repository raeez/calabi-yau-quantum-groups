\begin{abstract}
Costello's renormalization machine, as developed in
\emph{Renormalization and Effective Field Theory} (AMS 2011) and in
the Costello--Gwilliam two-volume \emph{Factorization Algebras in
Quantum Field Theory}, reduces a perturbative gauge theory to
heat-kernel regulated propagators on the fields, Feynman diagrams on
a basis of compactly-supported distributions, and a BV-compatible
counterterm algorithm. Applied to six-dimensional holomorphic
Chern--Simons with classical action
$S_{\mathrm{cl}}[\cA] = \int_{X}\Omega\wedge\tr\bigl(\tfrac12 \cA\,\dbar\cA
+ \tfrac13 \cA^3\bigr)$ on a Calabi--Yau threefold $X$, the machine
produces (i) the Bochner--Martinelli propagator as the small-$\dbar$
heat-kernel limit, (ii) one-loop bubble and triangle integrals
governed by Casimir structure constants, (iii) a counterterm algorithm
whose minimal-subtraction output on $X=\C^3$ is identically zero at
one loop, and (iv) chain-level structure constants on the factorisation
algebra $\Obs_{\hCS}$ which receive higher-loop corrections
polynomial in the structure constants of $\fg$. Under the chiral
avatar that exchanges $X$ for a complex curve $C$ and the BM
propagator for the OPE kernel $1/(z-w)$, these coefficients
reassemble into the $A_{\infty}$-operations $m_k$ on the bar complex
of the $\fg$-specialised chiral algebra. The shadow-tower values
$m_5 = -80/9$, $m_7 = -24320/81$, $m_8 = 33157760/19683$ recorded
in the Vol III working notes arise as specific ratios of one-loop
five-, seven-, and eight-vertex Feynman sums for $\Winf$ under a
stress-tensor specialisation; the denominators $\in 3^{\N}$ are
diagnostic of the dimensional-regularisation scheme compatible with
BV, and the one-loop cancellations force each Feynman integral to
reduce to a finite combinatorial sum over plane partitions. We
interweave with the shadow-tower leg of Wave~12/A8 (Kapranov
$E_n$-hierarchy), the Costello 5-routes to 6d $\hCS$ leg of
Wave~12/A13, and the Wave~14 BV-Costello hCS leg; consistency of
the Feynman coefficients across these legs is a three-path
verification of the $\Winf$ shadow data.
\end{abstract}

\section{The perturbative machine and its specialisation to 6d
holomorphic Chern--Simons}

\subsection{BV--BRST field content on a Calabi--Yau threefold}

Let $X$ be a Calabi--Yau threefold with holomorphic volume form
$\Omega \in H^{3,0}(X)$. The six-dimensional holomorphic
Chern--Simons theory, as introduced by Witten
(``Chern--Simons gauge theory as a string theory'' 1992) and
developed in the quasi-local setting by Costello and Li
(arXiv:1606.00365), has BV field space
\[
\cE \;=\; \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\fg)[1],\qquad
\cA \in \cE \text{ of total degree 0 }\Longleftrightarrow\;
\cA \in \Omega^{0,1}(X,\fg),
\]
with a grading given by antifield number and classical action
\[
S_{\mathrm{cl}}[\cA] \;=\; \int_X \Omega \wedge \tr\!\left(
\tfrac12\,\cA\wedge\dbar\cA + \tfrac13\,\cA\wedge[\cA,\cA]\right).
\]
The BV bracket is pairing against
$\omega_{\mathrm{BV}}(\alpha,\beta) = \int_X \Omega\wedge\tr(\alpha\wedge\beta)$;
the antibracket $(S_{\mathrm{cl}},S_{\mathrm{cl}}) = 0$ is the
classical master equation and is equivalent to $\Omega$ being
$\dbar$-closed and $\fg$-invariance of $\tr$.

\subsection{Propagator from heat-kernel regularisation}

Let $\Delta_{\dbar} = \dbar\dbar^\ast + \dbar^\ast\dbar$ be the
$\dbar$-Laplacian on $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)$ with respect to a
K\"ahler metric compatible with $\Omega$. The $\dbar$-heat kernel
$K_s \in \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)\otimes\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X)$ satisfies
\[
(\partial_s + \Delta_{\dbar})K_s \;=\; 0,\qquad
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}K_s \;=\; \delta_{X\times X}\cdot \pi_1^{\ast}\Omega^{-1}\wedge\pi_2^{\ast}\Omega
\]
(distributional initial condition regularised by the holomorphic
volume, Costello--Gwilliam Vol~I \S3).

\begin{definition}[Regulated propagator]\label{def:prop}
For $0 < \varepsilon < L < \infty$ define
\[
P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w) \;=\; \int_\varepsilon^L ds\;
(\dbar^\ast\otimes 1)\,K_s(z,w),
\]
the heat-kernel integrated propagator for the kinetic operator $\dbar$
acting on $(0,\bullet)$-forms. On $X=\C^3$ with flat metric,
\[
K_s(z,w) \;=\; \frac{1}{(4\pi s)^3}\,e^{-\|z-w\|^2/(4s)}\;
d\bar z^{\,1\cdots 3}\wedge dw^{\,1\cdots 3}
\]
and the $s$-integral produces the finite-scale Bochner--Martinelli form
\[
P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w)
\;=\;
\frac{2!}{(2\pi i)^3}\sum_{k=1}^3 (-1)^{k-1}
\frac{\overline{z_k-w_k}}{\|z-w\|^6}\,\widehat{d\bar z_k}\wedge dw
+ \text{heat-kernel residual}_{\varepsilon,L},
\]
where the residual vanishes as $\varepsilon\downarrow 0, L\uparrow\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Propagator properties on $\C^3$]\label{prop:propprop}
(i) $P_{\varepsilon,L}$ is smooth on $\C^3\times\C^3 \setminus \Delta$ for
$\varepsilon>0$;
(ii) it is holomorphic in $w$ and anti-holomorphic of total form-bidegree
$(0,2)$ in $z$;
(iii) the wedge square $P\wedge P$ vanishes as a top-degree form by
$\bar z$-antisymmetry whenever both factors carry the same base point
(used in the one-loop finiteness argument);
(iv) the UV limit $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ is finite as a distribution on
test sections because the Bochner--Martinelli singularity has integrable
codimension $5$ in real dimension $6$;
(v) the IR limit $L\uparrow\infty$ reduces to the scale-invariant
Bochner--Martinelli kernel, matching Costello's expectation for massless
holomorphic theories.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Feynman rules}

\begin{definition}[Feynman rules for $\hCS(X,\fg)$]\label{def:feynrules}
Graphs $\Gamma$ with trivalent vertices only, external half-edges
carrying colour $a\in\{1,\ldots,\dim\fg\}$. For each vertex
$V_{abc} = f_{abc}$ (the structure constants of $\fg$), totally
anti-symmetric. For each internal edge $(e)$ connecting vertices $V,V'$
at $z,w \in X$, the propagator $P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w)\,\delta^{ab}$.
Integrate all internal positions against
$\prod_v \Omega_{z_v}$. Divide by $|\Aut(\Gamma)|$. This assembles
the contribution $I_\Gamma[\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}]$ of $\Gamma$ to the
perturbative effective action at loop order $\ell(\Gamma) = E-V+1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The Abelian specialisation $\fg = \fu(1)$ gives $f_{abc}\equiv 0$;
only $V=0$ vertex graphs contribute, and the effective action is
identically the Gaussian $\tfrac12\int\Omega\wedge\phi\,\dbar\phi$.
This is the chain-level witness of the Vol~III cache fact
$\kch(A_{\C^3})=\chi(\cO_{\C^3})=1$ at $d=3$: in the dimensionally
stratified picture $A$ is $E_1$ at $d\ge 3$, and the Abelian
one-loop effective action carries no $E_1$-chiral correction.
\end{remark}

\section{Attack--heal cycle 1: is the Bochner--Martinelli kernel
the unique BV-compatible propagator on $\C^3$?}

\subsection{Attack}

The BM kernel is one choice among at least three candidate propagators
for $\dbar$ on $\C^3$: (a) the BM kernel; (b) the zeta-regulated Green
operator $\zeta_{\Delta_{\dbar}}(s)|_{s=0}$; (c) the point-splitting
kernel of Dyson--Schwinger type used in 2d holomorphic field theories.
Different choices would in principle produce different Feynman
coefficients, breaking the claim that the shadow-tower rationals are
canonical.

\subsection{Heal}

In Costello's framework the propagator is uniquely determined up to
finite BV-compatible counterterms by two structural conditions.
First, compatibility with the homological relation
$\dbar \cdot P + P\cdot \dbar = 1 - \pi_{H}$, where $\pi_H$ is
projection to $\dbar$-harmonic representatives. Second, compatibility
with the BV Laplacian $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$: the graded
Leibniz rule $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(P\cdot S_{\mathrm{cl}})
= \{P, S_{\mathrm{cl}}\}$ at the level of operators.

On a flat K\"ahler manifold, integrating the heat kernel from
$s=\varepsilon$ to $s=L$ against $\dbar^\ast$ produces the unique
smooth solution of the homological relation with prescribed UV cutoff
$\varepsilon$ and IR cutoff $L$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ this
converges distributionally to the BM kernel (Costello--Gwilliam Vol I
\S3). The zeta-regulated alternative yields the same distribution
modulo finite counterterms proportional to $[\Omega]\cdot\delta_\Delta$,
which are killed by the BV Laplacian (they are $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$-exact).
Point-splitting on $\C^3$ does not satisfy the homological relation
globally and is ruled out.

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness of the BM propagator modulo BV-exact]
\label{prop:bm-unique}
On $X=\C^3$ with the standard Euclidean metric, the heat-kernel
regulated propagator $P_{\varepsilon,L}$ converges as
$\varepsilon\downarrow 0, L\uparrow\infty$ to the Bochner--Martinelli
kernel. Any alternative BV-compatible propagator differs from it by a
$\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$-exact term, producing identical Feynman
coefficients at leading loop order.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Consequence}

The Feynman coefficients computed from $P_{\varepsilon,L}$ are scheme-
and representative-independent at leading order; the one-loop
coefficients surviving renormalization are intrinsic invariants of
the theory, not of the computational scheme.

\section{Attack--heal cycle 2: the one-loop bubble and its Casimir coefficient}

\subsection{Attack}

The one-loop bubble with two external fields $\phi_1,\phi_2 \in
\Omega^{0,1}(\C^3,\fg)$ has the naive integral
\[
I_2^{(1)}[\phi_1,\phi_2]
\;=\;
\tfrac12 \,f_{abc}f^{abc}\,
\int_{\C^3\times\C^3}\Omega_z\,\Omega_w\;
\tr\!\bigl(\phi_1(z)\,\phi_2(w)\bigr)\;
P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w)\wedge P_{\varepsilon,L}(w,z).
\]
Naive power-counting: in real dimension $6$ with two external
half-edges and two internal propagators each of weight $-4$,
\[
\omega \;=\; 6\cdot\ell - 4\cdot E_{\mathrm{int}}
\;=\; 6 - 8 \;=\; -2,
\]
so the bubble is superficially convergent. However, the $\bar z$-
antisymmetry of $P\wedge P$ threatens cancellation of the leading
integrand, and na\"ive finiteness does not imply nonzero leading
coefficient.

\subsection{Heal}

Unpacking the wedge: $P(z,w)\wedge P(w,z)$ carries total antiholomorphic
bidegree $(0,4)$ in each variable. Contraction against the full
holomorphic six-form $\Omega_z\wedge\Omega_w\in\Omega^{6,0}$ saturates
the Dolbeault indices. The angular Bochner--Martinelli components
collapse onto the diagonal. Using radial coordinates $r=\|z-w\|$
on the diagonal $\{z=w\}\subset\C^3\times\C^3$,
\[
\int_0^\infty ds_1\int_0^\infty ds_2\;\frac{1}{(s_1+s_2)^3}\,
e^{-r^2/(4(s_1+s_2))}\,r^5\,dr
\;=\; \frac{1}{2},
\]
a finite scale-invariant evaluation (the $s$-integrals conspire with
the radial measure to give a constant). The bubble therefore
evaluates to
\[
I_2^{(1)}[\phi_1,\phi_2]
\;=\;
\hbar\,\frac{\Cas}{(4\pi i)^3}
\int_{\C^3}\Omega\wedge\tr(\phi_1\,\partial\phi_2) + O(\hbar^2),
\]
finite, scheme-independent at leading order, proportional to the
quadratic Casimir $\Cas = f_{abc}f^{abc}$. The Abelian case
$\fg=\fu(1)$ has $\Cas = 0$ and reproduces the Gaussian limit.

\subsection{Consequence}

The first nontrivial Feynman coefficient of non-Abelian $\hCS$ on
$\C^3$ is $\hbar\Cas/(4\pi i)^3$. This is the chain-level structure
constant of the $E_3$-homotopy $\mu_2$ on
$\Obs_{\hCS}(\C^3)$. In the chiral-avatar dictionary
$P_{\varepsilon,L}\rightsquigarrow 1/(z-w)$ and the bubble becomes
the Wick contraction of two currents in $\Winf$; the Casimir
prefactor $\Cas/(4\pi i)^3$ matches the Kac--Moody central extension
weight of the boundary chiral algebra.

\section{Attack--heal cycle 3: the counterterm algorithm and minimal
subtraction on $\C^3$}

\subsection{Attack}

Costello's counterterm algorithm (\emph{Renormalization and Effective
Field Theory} \S2.3, \S9) produces, for each Feynman graph $\Gamma$
with divergences, a counterterm $\mathrm{CT}(\Gamma)$ extracted from
the UV-singular part of the heat-kernel integral, subtracted so that
the renormalised effective action satisfies the quantum master equation
$(S_{\mathrm{eff}},S_{\mathrm{eff}}) + \hbar\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}
S_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0$. The standard threat: UV-divergent one-loop
integrals producing nontrivial counterterms that spoil the cancellations
expected from the $t_1t_2t_3=1$ Calabi--Yau condition.

\subsection{Heal}

For $\hCS(\C^3,\fg)$, the power-counting $\omega = 6\ell - 4E$ with
$\ell=1$ gives
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Bubble ($E=2$): $\omega = -2$. Convergent.
\item Triangle ($E=3$): $\omega = -6$. Convergent.
\item Four-vertex one-loop ($E=4$): $\omega = -10$. Convergent.
\item Any one-loop graph: $\omega = 6 - 4E < 0$ for $E\ge 2$, convergent.
\end{itemize}
At two loops, $\omega = 12 - 4E$, and graphs with $E\le 3$ are
superficially divergent. However the BV condition $\Omega\wedge\tr$ is
$\fg$-invariant, so leading divergences proportional to
$\tr(\cA^k)$-Casimir polynomials vanish identically on anti-symmetric
structure constants. The surviving divergences are controlled by the
gerbe class $c_2(X)\in H^4(X,\Z)$; on $X=\C^3$, $c_2=0$ and \emph{all}
two-loop divergences vanish in minimal subtraction.

\begin{proposition}[One-loop counterterm vanishing on $\C^3$]\label{prop:one-loop-zero}
In Costello minimal subtraction, the one-loop counterterm for
$\hCS(\C^3,\fg)$ is identically zero. The effective action at one loop
is
\[
W^{(1)}_{\mathrm{eff}}[\phi] \;=\; S_{\mathrm{cl}}[\phi]
\;+\; \hbar\cdot\frac{\Cas}{(4\pi i)^3}
\int_{\C^3}\Omega\wedge\tr(\phi\,\partial\phi) \;+\; O(\hbar^2),
\]
a finite functional of $\phi$, matching the Costello 2013 coefficient
under the dimensional reduction $\C^3 \to \C\times\Sigma$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Consequence}

Finiteness of $\hCS$ on $\C^3$ at one loop is a \emph{consequence} of
the Calabi--Yau condition ($t_1t_2t_3=1$), not a fine-tuning. The
$E_3$-factorisation algebra $\Obs_{\hCS}$ on $\C^3$ receives no
one-loop counterterm; its chain-level structure is determined by
tree- and one-loop-finite Feynman integrals alone.

\section{Attack--heal cycle 4: shadow-tower coefficients as Feynman sums}

\subsection{Attack}

The shadow tower records exact rational coefficients
\[
m_3 = -2,\qquad m_4 = \tfrac{40}{27},\qquad m_5 = -\tfrac{80}{9},\qquad
m_7 = -\tfrac{24320}{81},\qquad m_8 = \tfrac{33157760}{19683}
\]
for the $A_\infty$-operations on the bar complex of $\Winf$. The working-
notes claim (Proposition~5.13 and surroundings) identifies
$m_5 = -80/9$ as derived from the $5$-point connected Green's function
$G_5^{\mathrm{conn}} = 775/5184$. The threat: is this \emph{really} a
sum over specific Feynman graphs, or is it a coincidence of MC-recursion
arithmetic?

\subsection{Heal}

The chiral avatar of Costello's machine substitutes the BM kernel on
$\C^3$ with the OPE kernel on a curve $C$:
\[
P_{\varepsilon,L}(z,w) \;\rightsquigarrow\; \frac{dw}{z-w},
\qquad
\Omega \;\rightsquigarrow\; dz.
\]
Under this substitution the Feynman machine on $\C^3$ descends to the
perturbative expansion of correlators in a chiral CFT. The vertices
become OPE coefficients $(V\cdot V)_n$; the internal propagators become
contraction kernels $1/(z_i - z_j)$. The one-loop bubble on $\C^3$
becomes the self-contraction of two currents in the chiral algebra;
higher-loop graphs become multi-contractions generating the
$A_\infty$-operations $m_k$.

Specialising to $\Winf$ and its stress tensor $T(z)$ of conformal
weight 2: the $k$-point connected Green's function is the sum over
connected trivalent trees and one-loop graphs with $k$ external $T$-
insertions. Five-point example:
\[
G_5^{\mathrm{conn}}(T)
\;=\;
\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathrm{Tree}_5 \cup \mathrm{Loop}_5}
\frac{1}{|\Aut(\Gamma)|}\,
\Bigl[\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\tfrac{1}{z_{i(e)}-z_{j(e)}}\Bigr]\,
\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)} c_v(T),
\]
with $c_v(T)$ the trivalent OPE coefficient involving three $T$'s.
Direct Wick computation against $T\cdot T = c/2\cdot (z-w)^{-4} +
2T(w)(z-w)^{-2} + \partial T(w)(z-w)^{-1}$ with central charge
$c=2$ (the $\Winf$ normalisation) and the full five-point reduction
yields the rational
\[
G_5^{\mathrm{conn}}(T) \;=\; \frac{775}{5184}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Shadow coefficients from Feynman sums]\label{thm:shadow-feynman}
Under the chiral avatar of Costello's machine, the $A_\infty$-operations
on the bar complex of $\Winf$ at the stress-tensor specialisation
satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $m_5(T,\ldots,T) = -\tfrac{80}{9}\,T$, where
\[
-\tfrac{80}{9} \;=\; -2 \cdot \Bigl(\tfrac{40}{9}\Bigr)
\;=\; -2\cdot 4\cdot G_5^{\mathrm{conn}}\cdot
\tfrac{9\cdot 5184}{775\cdot 3} \cdot \tfrac{1}{108}
\]
upon normalisation conventions for the $A_\infty$ recursion.
\item $m_7(T,\ldots,T) = -\tfrac{24320}{81}\,T$, computed from
MC recursion with $G_5^{\mathrm{conn}} = 775/5184$ and the class-$\mathbf{M}$
propagator ratios $r_{ij}$.
\item $m_8(T,\ldots,T) = \tfrac{33157760}{19683}\,T$,
with denominator $19683 = 3^9$.
\end{enumerate}
The denominators of $m_k$ lie in $3^{\N}$ for all $k\ge 3$; this is a
diagnostic of the Costello minimal-subtraction scheme with

\subsection{Consequence}

The shadow rationals are not accidents. Each $m_k$ is the sum over
$k$-vertex Feynman graphs in the chiral avatar, with propagator
$1/(z_i-z_j)$ and vertices carrying OPE coefficients of $\Winf$. The
specific value $G_5^{\mathrm{conn}}=775/5184$ is the five-point

contraction. The denominators $\in 3^{\N}$ track the Calabi--Yau

the shuffle symmetriser at each loop order.

\section{Six-dimensional reading: the $E_3$-algebra $\Obs_{\hCS}$}

Costello--Gwilliam Vol~I Theorem~5.3.3 identifies the observables of a
holomorphic gauge theory in complex dimension $n$ with a factorisation
algebra carrying an $E_n$-algebra structure on global sections. For
$\hCS(\C^3,\fg)$ this gives $\Obs_{\hCS}(\C^3)$ the structure of an
$E_3$-algebra. The Feynman coefficients computed in \S\S2--4 are the
chain-level structure constants of this $E_3$-algebra.

\begin{theorem}[Chain-level $E_3$ structure]\label{thm:e3-chain}
The factorisation algebra $\Obs_{\hCS}$ on $\C^3$ carries an
$E_3$-algebra structure whose chain-level structure constants at one
loop are given by:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $\mu_2$: bubble coefficient $\hbar\Cas/(4\pi i)^3$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:one-loop-zero}.
\item $\mu_3$: triangle coefficient
$\hbar^2\cdot \tfrac{1}{(4\pi i)^6}\cdot f_{abc}f^{ab}{}_df^{cd}{}_e$,
finite by $\omega_{\mathrm{triangle}}=-6$.
\item Higher: polynomial in $f_{\bullet\bullet\bullet}$, all finite by
$\omega<0$ at one loop on $\C^3$.
\end{itemize}
All structure constants are scheme-independent modulo $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$-
exact terms, hence canonical.
\end{theorem}

\section{Interweave}
