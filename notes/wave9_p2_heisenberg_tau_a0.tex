\section*{The null-root multiplicity \texorpdfstring{\(\tau(a_0)=9\)}{tau(a0)=9}}

\paragraph{Eichler-Zagier input.}
Let
\[
 \phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}(\tau,z)
 = \sum_{n\geq 0,\,\ell\in\mathbb Z} c(4n-\ell^2)q^n\zeta^\ell
\]
be the weight \(0\), index \(1\) Eichler-Zagier weak Jacobi form.  Its
degree-zero row is
\[
 \phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\big|_{q^0}
 = \zeta^{-1}+10+\zeta ,
 \qquad c(0)=10,\qquad c(-1)=1 .
\]
In the Gritsenko-Nikulin denominator normalisation,
\[
 D_5(Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z)
 = q^{1/2}r^{1/2}s^{1/2}
   \prod_{(n,\ell,m)>0}(1-q^n r^\ell s^m)^{c(4nm-\ell^2)} .
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is \(Z_{K3}=2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\), so the
second-quantised scalar normalisation squares the primitive denominator:
\[
 D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2 .
\]
The root exponents of \(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}\) are therefore read from
\(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\) itself.

\paragraph{The zero-dimensional cusp.}
Let \(a_0\) be a primitive isotropic generator of a zero-dimensional cusp
of the Gritsenko-Nikulin chamber.  Put
\[
 Q=e^{2\pi i z_1},\qquad R=e^{2\pi i z_2}.
\]
The boundary part of the product obtained from the \(q^0\)-row of
\(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}\) is, after the Weyl monomial,
\[
 \prod_{n\geq 1}
 (1-Q^n)^{10}
 (1-Q^n R)
 (1-Q^n R^{-1}) .
\]
The Jacobi triple product packages the two charged factors and one
uncharged factor into the theta factor \(\nu_{11}\).  The remaining
uncharged product is
\[
 \prod_{n\geq 1}(1-Q^n)^{10-1}
 = \prod_{n\geq 1}(1-Q^n)^9 .
\]
Equivalently, the first Fourier-Jacobi boundary coefficient is
\[
 \psi_{5,1/2}(z_1,z_2)e^{2\pi iz_3}
 = \eta(z_1)^9\nu_{11}(z_1,z_2),
\]
and the exponent is the coefficient identity
\[
 9=c(0)-c(-1)=10-1 .
\]

\paragraph{Isotropic simple roots.}
Gritsenko-Nikulin's denominator identity writes the isotropic additive
factor as
\[
 1-\sum_{t\geq1}m(ta_0)Q^t
 =\prod_{t\geq1}(1-Q^t)^{\tau(ta_0)} .
\]
The formal product expansion is unique: taking logarithms and applying
M\"obius inversion recovers the exponent attached to each \(1-Q^t\).  The
cusp product above gives
\[
 \tau(ta_0)=9\qquad(t\geq1),
\]
and in particular
\[
 \boxed{\tau(a_0)=9=c(0)-c(-1).}
\]
Thus each positive multiple of the primitive isotropic ray carries nine
even imaginary simple-root directions in the Gritsenko-Nikulin simple-root
package.  The integer \(9\) belongs to the \(\tau\)-data of isotropic
simple roots; the product exponents \(c(4nm-\ell^2)\) and the K3\(\times E\)
subscripted invariants are separate data.

\paragraph{Subscripted invariant separation.}
For \(Y=K3\times E\), the relevant invariants are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \kcat(Y)
 &=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)
   =2\cdot0=0,\\
 \kch(Y)
 &=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(Y)
   =1-1+1-1=0,\\
 \kHeis(Y)
 &=2+1=3,\\
 \kBKM(\Delta_5)
 &=\frac{c_1(0)}2=\frac{10}{2}=5,\\
 \kfib(Y)
 &=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\mathbb Z)=1+22+1=24 .
\end{aligned}
\]
The null-root multiplicity applies the operation \(c(0)-c(-1)\).  The
Borcherds weight applies the operation \(c_1(0)/2\).  These two operations
share the same degree-zero Jacobi row at \(N=1\) and compute different
objects.

\paragraph{CHL denominator ladder.}
For the primitive CHL denominator ladder, the constants are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
N & c_N(0) & \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2 \\ \hline
1 & 10 & 5\\
2 & 8 & 4\\
3 & 6 & 3\\
4 & 4 & 2\\
6 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
These constants determine the automorphic weights of the primitive CHL
denominators.  The level-one null computation remains the separate
coefficient identity \(10-1=9\).

\paragraph{Gritsenko-Clery catalogue.}
The Gritsenko-Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue is a separate eight-row
system:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\mathrm{row} & \mathrm{wt}(F) & c_F(0) \\ \hline
1 & 5 & 10\\
2 & 2 & 4\\
3 & 3 & 6\\
4 & 1 & 2\\
5 & 2 & 4\\
6 & \tfrac12 & 1\\
7 & \tfrac32 & 3\\
8 & 1 & 2
\end{array}
\]
Its row constants satisfy \(\mathrm{wt}(F)=c_F(0)/2\) row by row.  This
catalogue and the five-entry CHL ladder have their own indexing and
normalisation data; overlap requires an explicitly named row map.
