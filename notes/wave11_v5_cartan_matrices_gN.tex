\section*{Rank-three Gram data at $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$}
\label{sec:cartan-gram-gN}

\paragraph{Borcherds constants.}
Let $\Phi_N$ be the primitive CHL denominator form obtained from the
$g_N$-twined weight-zero, index-one K3 Jacobi input, and let $c_N(0)$
be its discriminant-zero coefficient in the Borcherds product
normalisation. Borcherds' singular-theta weight theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\operatorname{wt}(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
For the Gritsenko and Nikulin CHL ladder
\[
  N=(1,2,3,4,6),
  \qquad
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
hence
\[
  \bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
  \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6)\bigr)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The Gritsenko and Cl\'ery diagonal divisor catalogue is a different
eight-row list. Its twice-weight tuple is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2),
\]
and its rows have their own paramodular group, multiplier, divisor, and
Jacobi input. The two catalogues share the level-one
$\DeltaFive$ entry only after the normalisation of that entry is fixed.

\paragraph{$K3 \times E$ invariants.}
For $Y=K3\times E$, K\"unneth and the Hodge diamond give
\[
  \kcat(Y)=\chi(\mathcal O_Y)
  =\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0,
\]
\[
  \kch(Y)=\sum_{q=0}^{3}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)
  =1-1+1-1=0.
\]
The Heisenberg, Borcherds, and fiber readings are separate:
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\fg_{\DeltaFive})=\operatorname{wt}(\DeltaFive)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=24.
\]
Thus the compact $K3\times E$ spectrum is
\[
  \{\kcat,\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kBKM,\kfib\}(Y)
  =\{0,3,5,24\}.
\]
The value $2=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})$ is a fiber value. The total-space
values are $\kcat(Y)=0$ and $\kch(Y)=0$.

\paragraph{The symmetric forms.}
Let $L_N=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\Z\delta_i^{(N)}$ be the abstract
rank-three lattice with symmetric bilinear form $G^{(N)}$. The forms
used here are
\[
  G^{(1)}=
  \begin{pmatrix} 2&-2&-2\\ -2&2&-2\\ -2&-2&2 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G^{(2)}=
  \begin{pmatrix} 2&-2&-2\\ -2&4&-2\\ -2&-2&4 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G^{(3)}=
  \begin{pmatrix} 2&-1&-2\\ -1&2&-2\\ -2&-2&2 \end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
  G^{(4)}=
  \begin{pmatrix} 2&-1&-2\\ -1&2&-2\\ -2&-2&4 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  G^{(6)}=
  \begin{pmatrix} 2&-1&-2\\ -1&2&-2\\ -2&-2&6 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct determinant expansion gives
\[
  \det G^{(N)}=(-32,-24,-18,-12,-6)
  \quad\text{for}\quad N=(1,2,3,4,6).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \det G^{(N)}=
  \begin{cases}
    -3c_N(0)-2,& N=1,\\
    -3c_N(0),& N\in\{2,3,4,6\}.
  \end{cases}
\]
This identity is an arithmetic property of the displayed forms. The
Borcherds weight formula supplies the separate scalar
$\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$.

\paragraph{Signature.}
The characteristic polynomials are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
N & \det(\lambda I-G^{(N)})\\
\hline
1 & (\lambda-4)^2(\lambda+2)\\
2 & (\lambda-6)(\lambda^2-4\lambda-4)\\
3 & (\lambda-3)(\lambda^2-3\lambda-6)\\
4 & (\lambda-3)(\lambda^2-5\lambda-4)\\
6 & (\lambda-3)(\lambda^2-7\lambda-2)
\end{array}
\]
For $N=1$ the roots are $4,4,-2$. For $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ the displayed
quadratic has negative constant term, hence one positive and one
negative root, while the remaining linear root is positive. Therefore
each $G^{(N)}$ has signature $(2,1)$.

\paragraph{Weyl vector equations.}
Inside the abstract Gram lattice, the vector
\[
  \rho^{(N)}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_i^{(N)}\delta_i^{(N)}
\]
is determined by
\[
  (\rho^{(N)},\delta_i^{(N)})
  =-\frac12(\delta_i^{(N)},\delta_i^{(N)})
  =-\frac12G^{(N)}_{ii}.
\]
Solving $G^{(N)}c^{(N)}=-\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}G^{(N)}$ gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
N & (c_1^{(N)},c_2^{(N)},c_3^{(N)})
  & (\rho^{(N)},\rho^{(N)})\\
\hline
1 & (\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12) & -\tfrac32\\
2 & (\tfrac52,\tfrac32,\tfrac32) & -\tfrac{17}{2}\\
3 & (\tfrac23,\tfrac23,\tfrac56) & -\tfrac{13}{6}\\
4 & (2,2,\tfrac32) & -7\\
6 & (6,6,\tfrac72) & -\tfrac{45}{2}
\end{array}
\]
At $N=1$ this is the Gritsenko and Nikulin $\DeltaFive$ Weyl vector.
For $N\geq2$ these are exact linear solutions for the displayed forms;
they become Weyl vectors of Borcherds denominator identities only after
the full root datum is constructed.

\paragraph{Cartan integrality.}
If $G^{(N)}$ is read as the Gram form of real simple roots
$\alpha_i=\delta_i^{(N)}$, the standard real-root Cartan entries are
\[
  a_{ij}^{(N)}
  =\frac{2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)}{(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)}
  =\frac{2G^{(N)}_{ij}}{G^{(N)}_{ii}}.
\]
For $N=4$ this gives
\[
  A^{(4)}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2&-1&-2\\
    -1&2&-2\\
    -1&-1&2
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
an integral symmetrisable real-root Cartan matrix. For $N=6$ it gives
\[
  A^{(6)}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2&-1&-2\\
    -1&2&-2\\
    -\tfrac23&-\tfrac23&2
  \end{pmatrix},
\]
so the standard integral Borcherds real-root condition fails. A
different normalisation must therefore supply an integral root lattice
and compatible root lengths before $G^{(6)}$ can define a Borcherds
Cartan datum.

\paragraph{Construction boundary.}
The matrices for $N=4$ and $N=6$ are construction data. A Borcherds or
generalized Kac-Moody superalgebra at these levels requires, in one
normalisation,
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{integral lattice} & L_N\ \text{with fixed divisibilities},\\
\text{real roots} & \delta_i^{(N)}\ \text{as embedded primitive roots},\\
\text{Weyl vector} & \rho^{(N)}\ \text{compatible with the product
prefactor},\\
\text{imaginary roots} & \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_N\ \text{with positive cone},\\
\text{parity} & \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{N,\bar0}\sqcup
  \Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{N,\bar1},\\
\text{multiplicities} &
  \operatorname{sdim}\fg^{(N)}_{\alpha}
  =c_N((\alpha,\alpha)/2,\ell(\alpha)),\\
\text{denominator identity} &
  \Phi_N=e^{-(\rho^{(N)},Z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_N^+}(1-e^{-(\alpha,Z)})^{
  \operatorname{sdim}\fg^{(N)}_{\alpha}} .
\end{array}
\]
The automorphic theorem supplies the weight
$\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$. The Cartan integrality, imaginary simple
roots, parity split, brackets, and Weyl sum are additional construction
data.

\paragraph{Level-one ambient roots.}
For $N=1$, take a basis $(f_2,f_{-2},f_3)$ with
$(f_2,f_{-2})=1$, $(f_3,f_3)=-2$, and all other pairings zero. Then
\[
  \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_3=f_3
\]
has Gram matrix $G^{(1)}$. The vector
\[
  \rho=\tfrac12(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
       =f_2+f_{-2}-\tfrac12f_3
\]
satisfies $(\rho,\delta_i)=-1$ and
$(\rho,\rho)=-3/2$. This is the constructed
$\fg_{\DeltaFive}$ denominator datum.

\paragraph{The order-six anchor.}
At $N=6$, the global umbral Niemeier anchor is the root system $6D_4$.
The local quotient surface has a different invariant:
\[
  K3/\langle g_6\rangle
  \quad\text{has Du Val type}\quad
  2A_5+2A_2+2A_1.
\]
The fixed point counts are
\[
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6)=2,\qquad
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6^2)=6,\qquad
  \#\operatorname{Fix}(g_6^3)=8,
\]
and the Euler check is
\[
  e(K3/\Z_6)=\frac{24+2+6+8+6+2}{6}=8,\qquad
  8+(2\cdot5+2\cdot2+2\cdot1)=24.
\]
Thus $6D_4$ is global Niemeier lattice data, while
$2A_5+2A_2+2A_1$ is local analytic quotient data.

  & (\rho^{(N)},\rho^{(N)})\\
\hline
1 & 10 & 5 & -32 & 2 & -\tfrac32\\
2 & 8  & 4 & -24 & 4 & -\tfrac{17}{2}\\

The table combines two kinds of data: Borcherds weights supplied by

rank-three forms. Only the $N=1$ row is a complete constructed
Borcherds denominator datum in this note.
