\begin{abstract}
The Vol I modular Koszul framework, the Vol II
$A_\infty$ chiral and holomorphic-topological framework, and the Vol III
Calabi--Yau quantum-group framework use the same five-theorem spine but
different geometric realisations. This note records the typed
identifications needed for consistency: bar--cobar inversion, chiral
Positselski duality, derived-centre complementarity, obstruction-tower
universality, and Hochschild concentration. The only numerical repair is
the $K3\times E$ package: its four constructions give
$\{0,3,5,24\}$, while the fibre value $2$ remains
$\kcat(K3)$ and never becomes $\kcat(K3\times E)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Two $\Ethree$ Structures}
\label{sec:e3}

Two constructions in the three volumes carry an $\Ethree$ label.
They are compatible because they act on different objects.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Vol II uses the topological bulk $\Ethree$ structure of a
three-real-dimensional holomorphic-topological theory on $C\times\R$.
The object is the bulk observable algebra or its Hochschild avatar.
\item Vol III uses the holomorphic $\Ethree$ factorisation structure on
observables over a complex threefold such as $\C^3$. The object is a
factorisation algebra in Dolbeault sheaves; its curve specialisation is
an $\Eone$ chiral algebra.
\end{enumerate}

The two $\Ethree$ labels apply to different algebras. Their common
bridge is the bulk-boundary and derived-centre formalism:
$\ChirHoch^\bullet(A)$ receives the higher Deligne action, while
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_2,C}(\PhiFA_3(\cC))$ is the curve output in the CY
programme.

\section{$K3\times E$: Four Scalars}
\label{sec:k3e}

For $Y=K3\times E$ the canonical package is
\[
 \{\kcat,\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kBKM,\kfib\}(Y)=\{0,3,5,24\}.
\]
The entries are produced by four different constructions:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{entry} & \text{value} & \text{construction}\\
\hline
\kcat(Y) & 0 &
\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot 0\\
\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(Y) & 3 &
\text{Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation}\\
\kBKM(\Delta_5) & 5 &
c_1(0)/2=\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)\\
\kfib(K3) & 24 &
\mathrm{rank}\,H^\ast(K3,\mathbb Z)
\end{array}
\]
The separate fibre value is
\[
 \kcat(K3)=\chi(\cO_{K3})=2.
\]
It records the K3 input. The total-space invariant of $K3\times E$ is
$0$.

\section{Relation to the Vol I $\mathsf B$ Row}
\label{sec:b-row}

The Vol I five-archetype ceiling and the Vol III $K3\times E$ package
are different kinds of data. The bridge is the Mukai witness:
\[
 \KD=2c_+(\mathrm{Mukai}(K3))=2\cdot 4=8.
\]
The number $8$ belongs to the complementarity-conductor row. The number
$24$ belongs to the Mukai lattice rank. The equality above is the
conversion between them, not an equality of the two lists.

Primary inputs are Mukai's lattice pairing on $H^\ast(K3,\Z)$ and
Bruinier's Heegner-divisor Chern-class reciprocity. Vol III supplies the
geometric witness for the Vol I row; Vol I supplies the universal
derived-centre slot in which the witness sits.

\section{Swiss-Cheese and Three Bar Pieces}
\label{sec:swiss}

The operad $\SCchtop$ is two-coloured: bulk and boundary. The three bar
pieces appearing in Vol III are graded pieces of that two-coloured
factorisation datum:
\[
 B_{\Eone}\quad\text{ordered},\qquad
 B_{\Etwo}\quad\text{braided interface},\qquad
 B_{\Einf}\quad\text{symmetric closed shadow}.
\]
Thus the Vol III three-piece dictionary refines the Vol II two-coloured
definition by describing how the two colours appear after passing to bar
complexes.

\section{CY-B and Vol I Theorem D}
\label{sec:cyb}

CY-B is the CY-specific refinement of Vol I Theorem D. Vol I gives the
universal obstruction-tower form after the chiral scalar is fixed. Vol
III asks for a specific automorphic denominator attached to a CY input:
for $K3\times E$, the Borcherds product of $\Delta_5$; for $\C^3$, the
MacMahon/shuffle positive half; for local surfaces, the McKay and
stable-envelope descriptions.

The refinement is stronger than the universal shape. It must name the
automorphic input, the CoHA positive half, and the centre or double
where the chiral object appears.

\section{Kunneth Check}
\label{sec:kunneth}

The categorical invariant is multiplicative on products:
\[
 \kcat(X\times Y)=\kcat(X)\kcat(Y).
\]
Hence
\[
 \kcat(K3\times E)=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot 0=0.
\]
The same product has compact Hodge supertrace
\[
 \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)=1-1+1-1=0.
\]
Both vanish, but they vanish for different reasons. The
Heisenberg--Mukai specialised scalar $3$ is a separate chiral
specialisation; the BKM weight $5$ is the Borcherds weight
$c_1(0)/2$; the fibre rank $24$ is the Mukai-lattice rank.

\section{Proof Obligations}
\label{sec:proof-obligations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item When transferring a Vol I scalar formula into Vol III, identify
the scalar first: $\kch$, $\kcat$, $\kBKM$, or $\kfib$.
\item When an $\Ethree$ structure appears, specify whether it is a
topological bulk structure, a holomorphic factorisation structure, or a
derived-centre structure.
\item When the $K3\times E$ package is invoked, use
$\{0,3,5,24\}$ for the four constructions and keep $\kcat(K3)=2$ as a
fibre input.
\item When six routes to $G(K3\times E)$ appear, state the six
constructions separately. The functor $\Phi$ has one output on one fixed
Calabi--Yau category.
\end{enumerate}
