\section*{Oberdieck-Pandharipande reduced pairs and CHL quotients}

Let $S$ be a projective K3 surface and let $E$ be an elliptic curve.
Set $X_1=S\times E$.  The Oberdieck-Pandharipande reduced-pairs
normalisation is fixed by the monic Gritsenko-Nikulin product
\cite{OberdieckPandharipande2018,GritsenkoNikulin1998}
\[
  D_5(Z)=64^{-1}\Delta_5(Z).
\]
Thus the product denoted by the OP scalar formula is
\[
  \PhiOP(Z)=D_5(Z)^2
  =4096^{-1}\Delta_5(Z)^2,
\]
whereas the unnormalised Igusa square is
\[
  \Phiun(Z)=\Delta_5(Z)^2=4096\,\PhiOP(Z).
\]
Consequently the reduced scalar branch is
\[
  \ZredPT(X_1)=\ZredDT(X_1)
  =-\PhiOP(Z)^{-1}
  =-4096\,\Delta_5(Z)^{-2}.
\]
Writing $-\Delta_5^{-2}$ for the OP scalar drops the factor
$4096=64^2$.  The factor $4096$ and the minus sign are scalar
normalisation data.  Pfaffian orientation, Hall source, and the
square-root character of $\Delta_5$ are additional structures.

\paragraph{Reduced theory on $S\times E$.}
The elliptic curve acts on $X_1$ by translation on the second factor.
Ordinary Behrend-weighted Euler characteristics vanish in positive
translation orbits.  The reduced invariant is the Behrend-weighted
Euler characteristic of the quotient stable-pairs space by $E$,
equivalently the degree of the reduced virtual class after the
semiregularity direction
\[
  H^{0,2}(X_1)=H^{0,2}(S)\otimes H^0(E)
\]
has been removed.  Oberdieck's reduced-stable-pair theorem identifies
the quotient definition with the reduced virtual-class definition and
supplies the reduced DT/PT comparison in this geometry
\cite{OberdieckPandharipande2018,Oberdieck2017}.  The Igusa formula
above is the OP scalar input in that reduced convention.

\paragraph{Diagonal CHL quotient.}
Let $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.  Let $g_N$ be a symplectic automorphism of
$S$ of order $N$ in Nikulin's symplectic K3 list
\cite{Nikulin1980}, and let $t_N$ be translation by an $N$-torsion
point of $E$.  Put
\[
  X_N=(S\times E)/G_N,\qquad
  G_N=\langle g_N\times t_N\rangle .
\]
For every nontrivial $a\in\Z/N\Z$, the translation $t_N^a$ has no fixed
point on $E$.  Hence $g_N^a\times t_N^a$ has no fixed point on
$S\times E$, even when $g_N^a$ has fixed points on $S$.  The quotient
$X_N$ is a smooth projective threefold, and the quotient map
$S\times E\to X_N$ is finite \'etale of degree $N$.  No ADE orbifold
correction enters this diagonal translation quotient.

The holomorphic three-form $\Omega_S\wedge dz_E$ is $G_N$-invariant:
$g_N$ fixes $\Omega_S$ by the symplectic assumption, and $t_N$ fixes
$dz_E$.  It descends to a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic three-form on
$X_N$.  Thus $X_N$ is Calabi-Yau in the trivial-canonical sense used
for $K3\times E$.  The residual elliptic curve
$E_N=E/\langle t_N\rangle$ acts on $X_N$, and the reduced stable-pairs
theory on $X_N$ is the $E_N$-quotient theory with the same
semiregularity reduction.

\paragraph{Holomorphic Euler characteristic.}
Since the quotient is finite \'etale,
\[
  H^q(X_N,\cO_{X_N})
  =H^q(S\times E,\cO_{S\times E})^{G_N}.
\]
The K3 symplectic automorphism fixes $H^{0,2}(S)$ and acts trivially on
$H^0(S,\cO_S)$; the elliptic translation acts trivially on
$H^0(E,\cO_E)$ and $H^1(E,\cO_E)$.  Therefore
\[
  h^{0,q}(X_N)=(1,1,1,1)_q,\qquad 0\le q\le 3,
\]
and
\[
  \kcat(X_N)=\chi(\cO_{X_N})=1-1+1-1=0.
\]
In particular
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot 0=0.
\]
The value $\chi(\cO_{K3})=2$ belongs to the K3 fibre.  It supplies no
total-space value and no correction term for $\kBKM$.

\paragraph{Borcherds constants.}
Let $\PhiN$ be the Borcherds product attached to the standard
twined-K3 CHL input at order $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.  Let $c_N(0)$ be the
constant term of the zero component of the input form at the cusp used
in the singular theta lift.  Borcherds' weight theorem
\cite{Borcherds1998} gives
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiN)=\mathrm{wt}(\PhiN)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
For the standard twined-K3 normalisation of Gritsenko's CHL ladder
\cite{Gritsenko1999},
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
N & \text{frame shape} & c_N(0) & \kBKM(\PhiN)\\
\hline
1 & 1^{24} & 10 & 5\\
2 & 1^8 2^8 & 8 & 4\\
3 & 1^6 3^6 & 6 & 3\\
4 & 1^4 2^2 4^4 & 4 & 2\\
6 & 1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Thus
\[
  (\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
    \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6))=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The proof is the singular-theta-product formula: the weight of a
Borcherds product is one half of the zero-component constant term.
The $N=1$ entry is the weight-$5$ Gritsenko lift $\Delta_5$; squaring
it gives the OP weight-$10$ scalar product.  The primitive BKM
invariant remains $\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5$.

\paragraph{Index separation.}
The heterotic Jatkar-Sen dyon forms use the physical level set
$N\in\{1,2,3,5,7,11\}$ and weights
\[
  24/(N+1)-2=(10,6,4,2,1,0).
\]
This is a physical genus-two dyon sequence, separate from the
twined-K3 Borcherds constants
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiN)=(5,4,3,2,1)
  \quad\text{for}\quad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
At the shared $N=1$ symbol, the physical Igusa form is the square
$\Delta_5^2$ and the primitive BKM denominator is $\Delta_5$.

\paragraph{Gritsenko-Cl\'ery catalogue.}
The Gritsenko-Cl\'ery simplest-divisor catalogue is a third list
\cite{GritsenkoClery2013}.  Its eight diagonal-divisor rows have
twice-weight tuple
\[
  (2\,\mathrm{wt})=(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
This tuple is attached to the triple-indexed diagonal-divisor
catalogue.  The primitive CHL constant tuple remains
\[
  (c_N(0))_{N=(1,2,3,4,6)}=(10,8,6,4,2).
\]
Both families obey the same Borcherds weight theorem
$\kBKM=c(0)/2$ in their own input conventions.

\paragraph{The CHL reduced-pairs identity.}
The CHL scalar assertion extending the OP formula is
\[
  \ZredPT(X_N)(Z)
  =-\Phi_N^{\mathrm{OP}}(Z)^{-1},
\]
where $\Phi_N^{\mathrm{OP}}$ denotes the square-normalised reduced-pairs
product attached to the $g_N$-twined K3 input.  At $N=1$ this is the
Oberdieck-Pandharipande scalar formula in the $D_5$ normalisation.
For $N>1$ the assertion is conditional on three inputs: a
$G_N$-equivariant KKV theorem for stable pairs on $S$, the
$E_N$-quotient reduced obstruction theory on $X_N$, and a degeneration
calculus whose rubber contribution is computed by the same twined
Jacobi coefficients $c_N(n,\ell)$ that enter the Borcherds product.
The smooth quotient, the semiregularity direction, and the Borcherds
weight constants supply the geometric and automorphic boundary data for
this conditional identity.

\paragraph{Invariant separation.}
The identities in this note use four different invariants:
\[
  \kcat(X_N)=0,\qquad
  \kchcomp(X_N):=\sum_q(-1)^qh^{0,q}(X_N)=0
\]
on the compact Hodge-supertrace branch,
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiN)=c_N(0)/2,
\]
and the fibre/lattice invariant $\kfib$, whose value depends on the
chosen K3 lattice sector.  At the untwisted $K3\times E$ anchor,
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kchcomp(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kchHeis(K3\times E)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3)=24.
\]
The universal Borcherds formula is $\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2$.  The
additive defect
\[
  \delta_N :=
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)-\kchcomp(X_N)-\chi(\cO_{\mathrm{fiber}})
\]
is already nonzero at $N=1$: the BKM side is $5$, while the compact
total-space Hodge-supertrace side gives $0+\chi(\cO_E)=0$.  The six
constructions leading to $G(K3\times E)$ are distinct constructions of
a common target.  They are separate from the single two-stage
CY-to-chiral specialisation $\Phi_3^{(K3,E)}$, whose native output at
$d=3$ is $E_1$-chiral.
