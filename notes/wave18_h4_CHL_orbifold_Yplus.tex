\section*{CHL orbifold positive half and the primitive Borcherds row}

\paragraph{The CHL datum.}
Let $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.  Choose a symplectic automorphism
$g_N\in\Auts(K3)$ of exact order $N$ and, for $N>1$, a point
$t_N\in E[N]$ of exact order $N$; for $N=1$ set $g_1=\mathrm{id}$ and
$t_1=0$.  The diagonal generator
\[
  \sigma_N=(g_N,t_N)\in \Auts(K3)\times E[N],
  \qquad
  G_N=\langle\sigma_N\rangle\simeq \bZ/N\bZ
\]
acts freely on $K3\times E$: if $0<k<N$, then $k t_N\neq 0$, so
$\sigma_N^k$ is fixed-point-free on the elliptic factor.  The quotient
\[
  X_N=(K3\times E)/G_N
\]
is smooth, and the form $\omega_{K3}\wedge dz$ descends because $g_N$
is symplectic and translations preserve $dz$.  The finite \'etale cover
$K3\times E\to X_N$ gives
\[
  \chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_N})
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3\times E})
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{K3})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{E})
  =
  0 .
\]

\paragraph{The positive half.}
Let $\Yp(K3\times E)$ be the reduced Hall positive half attached to an
effective chamber of the compact $K3\times E$ DT/Hall moduli problem.
The CHL positive-half datum is the finite-idempotent summand
\[
  \Yp_{G_N}
  :=
  e_{G_N}\Yp(K3\times E),
  \qquad
  e_{G_N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sigma_N^k .
\]
Over characteristic zero this is the same vector-space summand as
$\Yp(K3\times E)^{G_N}$.  It records the CHL quotient/orbifold positive
half.  The BKM denominator and the scalar OP square are separate
automorphic and enumerative constructions.

\paragraph{Equivariant convolution.}
Let $\mathfrak{M}^{+}_{K3\times E}$ denote the effective chamber in the
reduced Hall moduli stack.  Pull-push along $\sigma_N$ acts on objects
and on the Hall correspondence
\[
  \mathfrak{M}^{+}\times_{\mathfrak{M}}\mathfrak{M}^{+}
  \longleftarrow
  \mathfrak{E}^{+}
  \longrightarrow
  \mathfrak{M}^{+}.
\]
If the reduced orientation line is $G_N$-linearised, the convolution
product commutes with $e_{G_N}$, hence $\Yp_{G_N}$ is an $E_1$ algebra.
After the $d=3$ Stage-$2$ specialisation it is an $E_1$-chiral algebra.
The braided $E_2$ structure appears after the centre, Drinfeld double,
or module-category layer is constructed.

The surface chart is concrete.  On Nakajima varieties attached to the
K3 Mukai lattice, choose a $G_N$-invariant K\"ahler chamber.  Maulik--
Okounkov stable envelopes are then equivariant for the symplectic
action of $g_N$; translation on $E$ contributes the charge-sector
character.  The compact threefold statement requires the reduced
orientation and the Hall correspondences beyond the Nakajima surface
model.

\paragraph{Invariant character.}
For finite-dimensional graded pieces the character of the idempotent
summand is the cyclic average
\[
  \chi(\Yp_{G_N};q,\tilde q,y)
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
  \chi^{(g_N^k,t_N^k)}
  (\Yp(K3\times E);q,\tilde q,y).
\]
On strata admitting Lefschetz localisation, the K3 factor contributes
the $g_N^k$-twined K3 elliptic genus
$\phi^{(g_N^k)}_{0,1}(\tau,z)$, while the elliptic translation supplies
the charge-sector factor in the Jatkar--Sen normalisation
(Jatkar--Sen 2005, arXiv:hep-th/0510147, \S3, eq.~(3.1)).

Two constants occur, with different definitions:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&6\\ \hline
\tau_{\mathrm{fix}}(g_N)=\chi(\Fix(g_N))&24&8&6&4&2\\
c_N(0)&10&8&6&4&2
\end{array}
\]
The first row is Mukai fixed-point data for a symplectic K3
automorphism.  The second row is the discriminant-zero coefficient of
the CHL Borcherds input.  The numerical agreement in the four
nontrivial rows is a table coincidence of this primitive family; the
Borcherds weight is read from $c_N(0)$.

\paragraph{Borcherds denominator.}
Let
\[
  \varphi_N(\tau,z)=
  \sum_{n,\ell}c_N(4n-\ell^2,\ell)\,q^n y^\ell
\]
be the CHL weak Jacobi input in the Borcherds normalisation, with
\[
  c_N(0):=c_N(0,0)=(10,8,6,4,2)
  \quad\text{for }N=(1,2,3,4,6).
\]
Borcherds 1998 (arXiv:alg-geom/9609022, Theorem 13.3) and
Gritsenko 1999 (arXiv:math/9906075, Theorem 1.2) give a primitive
paramodular denominator $\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}}$ of weight
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =
  \frac{c_N(0)}{2}
  =
  (5,4,3,2,1) .
\]
Thus
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})
  =
  \frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
At $N=1$ this is the Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator $\Delta_5$:
$c_1(0)=10$ and $\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5$.  The denominator-side root
supermultiplicities are
\[
  \operatorname{sdim}
  (\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}}})_{\alpha(n,\ell,m)}
  =
  c_N(4nm-\ell^2,\ell),
\]
with the $N=1$ normalisation matching Lorgat 2020,
\emph{A Borcherds lift of $\phi_{0,1}$}, Proposition 2.1.

\paragraph{Hall recognition.}
The Hall recognition theorem is the construction of a completed map
\[
  \mathcal{R}_N:\Yp_{G_N}
  \longrightarrow
  U(\mathfrak{n}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}}}))
\]
and the proof that it is an isomorphism in the chosen completion.  This
requires the $G_N$-linearised reduced orientation, a Hopf pairing, PBW
control, relation-exhaustion control, and compatibility of the Hall
degree with the Borcherds positive cone.  The automorphic denominator
alone supplies the target root multiplicities; compact Hall source
recognition is an additional theorem.

\paragraph{Primitive CHL rows and arithmetic extensions.}
The primitive CHL order set is exactly
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
  \qquad
  c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),
  \qquad
  \kBKM=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The Mukai-admissible arithmetic extension has
\[
  N\in\{5,7,8\},
  \qquad
  c_N(0)=(4,2,2),
  \qquad
  \kBKM=(2,1,1).
\]
Those rows enter here as Borcherds-weight rows.  The primitive CHL
quotient $X_N$, the positive half $\Yp_{G_N}$, and the OP scalar
statement use the five-order set $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$.

\paragraph{OP scalar square.}
At $N=1$, Oberdieck--Pandharipande 2018 (arXiv:1603.05238, Theorem 1)
prove
\[
  Z^{\red}_{\DT}(K3\times E;q,\tilde q,y)
  =
  -(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
  =
  -D_5^{-2}
  =
  -4096\,\Delta_5^{-2},
\]
where $D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5$ and
$\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2$.  The OP form is the scalar square:
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=(D_5)^2,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})=10=2\kBKM(\Delta_5).
\]
For $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ the scalar CHL analogue has the form
\[
  \Phi_N^{\mathrm{OP}}=(D_N)^2,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{wt}(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{OP}})=c_N(0),
\]
with $D_N$ the monic primitive Borcherds product in the chosen
normalisation.  The scalar assertion requiring the orbifold DT
orientation comparison is
\[
  Z^{\red}_{\DT}(X_N;q,\tilde q,y)
  =
  -(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{OP}}(\tau,\sigma,z))^{-1},
  \qquad
  (q,\tilde q,y)=(e^{2\pi i\tau},e^{2\pi i\sigma},e^{2\pi iz}).
\]
Bryan--Oberdieck orbifold DT theory supplies the geometric framework;
Chattopadhyaya--David 2019 (arXiv:1704.00434) and
Cheng--Duncan--Harvey 2014 (arXiv:1307.5793, Table 2) supply coefficient
checks.  Passing from $\Yp_{G_N}$ to this scalar square requires the
Serre-dual negative half, the Hopf pairing, completion, and the Behrend
orientation comparison.

\paragraph{Invariant separation.}
For every primitive CHL quotient in this scope,
\[
  \kcat(X_N)
  =
  \chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_N})
  =
  0,
\]
and
\[
  \kch(X_N)
  =
  \sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X_N)
  =
  0 .
\]
Indeed $H^{0,\bullet}(X_N)=H^{0,\bullet}(K3\times E)^{G_N}$, and
$g_N$ fixes $\omega_{K3}$ while translations fix $dz$, so the invariant
Hodge column has one generator in degrees $0,1,2,3$.

The unorbifolded $K3\times E$ frame has the scalar readings
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=3,
\]
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3\times E;K3)=24.
\]
The CHL fixed-locus trace
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{fix}}(g_N)
  =
  (24,8,6,4,2)
\]
is a Lefschetz input to twined characters.  A CHL fibre value must name
its lattice, for example an invariant Mukai lattice or a quotient fibre
datum.  Additive laws are absent among these readings.  The universal
formula in the Borcherds lane is
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}})=c_N(0)/2.
\]

\paragraph{Recognition hypotheses.}
The quotient geometry, the invariant-character average, and the
Borcherds weights above are fixed by the cited constructions and the
local computation of $c_N(0)$.  A complete Hall--Borcherds comparison
requires construction of the $G_N$-equivariant reduced DT theory;
identification of its orientation line with the Hall orientation line;
proof that the completed Hall recognition map is an isomorphism onto
$U(\mathfrak{n}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Phi_N^{\mathrm{BKM}}}))$; construction
of the Hall--Drinfeld double and Hopf pairing; and a match of the first
Fourier coefficients for $N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ with
$c_N(0)=(8,6,4,2)$.
