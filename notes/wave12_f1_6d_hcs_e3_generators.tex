Generators, Relations, Propagator, Counterterms}

\begin{abstract}
Six-real-dimensional holomorphic Chern--Simons theory on a Calabi--Yau
threefold $(X,\Omega)$, with gauge field $\cA \in \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X,\g)$
and classical action
$S_{\hCS}[\cA] = \tfrac12 \int_X \Omega \wedge \langle \cA, \dbar \cA\rangle +
\tfrac16 \int_X \Omega \wedge \langle \cA, [\cA,\cA]\rangle$,
is constructed as an $\Ethree$-algebra of observables in the
Costello--Francis--Gwilliam programme. We organise the construction by
{\em operator dimension}: local operators (complex codimension $3$), line
operators on $(0,1)$-curves (complex codimension $2$), and surface
operators on $(0,1)$-surfaces (complex codimension $1$). The relations
are displayed at perturbative order $\hbar^n$: at $\hbar^0$ they are
the $L_\infty$-relations of $\g[2]$; at $\hbar^1$ they receive no
correction on $\C^3$ by the wedge-square vanishing of the
Bochner--Martinelli propagator; at $\hbar^{\geq 2}$ all Feynman graph
integrands vanish because every graph contributes a factor
$\Omega_w \wedge \Omega_w = 0$ after Wick contraction. The quantum
observables form a factorisation algebra on $X$ whose value on a
contractible open is the little-$3$-discs envelope
$U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$. The chiral avatar, the Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral
translation on a curve $C \hookrightarrow X$, is obtained by transverse
integration along $C$-tubular fibres. Five ATTACK$\leftrightarrow$HEAL
cycles survey: (1) well-definedness of the propagator as a
distribution on $\C^3 \setminus \Delta$; (2) gauge invariance of the
$(\varepsilon,L)$ regulator; (3) absence of quantum anomaly
obstructing the $\Ethree$-structure; (4) cohomological triviality of
the one-loop bubble; (5) chiral avatar as genuine $\Ethree$-factorisation
homology, not a parallel construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, notation, conventions}
\label{sec:scope}
%%======================================================================

The phrase ``six-dimensional holomorphic Chern--Simons'' here denotes
Witten's 1992 holomorphic Chern--Simons theory on a Calabi--Yau
threefold $X$ --- six real dimensions, three complex --- extended to
the full Dolbeault complex by the BV procedure of Costello--Gwilliam.
It is distinct from Costello's four-dimensional Chern--Simons on
$\Sigma \times C$ (four real dimensions, two complex, built from a
volume form $\omega_\Sigma \wedge \omega_C$); the four-dimensional
theory produces the affine Yangian on boundary curves and is a
parallel but different story.

\begin{construction}[Data]\label{cons:data}
$(X,\Omega)$ a Calabi--Yau threefold: $\dim_\C X = 3$, a trivialisation
$\Omega \in H^0(X, K_X)$ of the canonical bundle, a Hermitian metric
$h$ refined K\"ahler. Principal model: $X = \C^3$ with flat Euclidean
metric, $\Omega = dz_1 \wedge dz_2 \wedge dz_3$. Compact model:
$X = K3 \times E$, $\Omega = \Omega_{K3} \wedge dw$. Lie data: a
finite-dimensional Lie algebra $\g$ over $\C$ with non-degenerate
invariant symmetric bilinear pairing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon
\g \otimes \g \to \C$ (Killing for $\g$ semisimple; canonical for $\g
= \C$ abelian).
\end{construction}

\begin{definition}[Graded field space]\label{def:fields}
The BV graded vector space of fields is
\[
\cE \;=\; \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \g)[1],
\]
with cohomological degree $k - 1$ for a $(0,k)$-form. Explicit
decomposition:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|l|l|l}
name & type & ghost $\gh$ & antifield? \\ \hline
ghost $c$ & $(0,0)$ & $-1$ & no\\
field $\cA^{0,1}$ & $(0,1)$ & $\phantom{-}0$ & no\\
antifield $\cA^{\dagger\,0,2}$ & $(0,2)$ & $+1$ & yes, paired with $\cA^{0,1}$\\
antighost $c^{\dagger\,0,3}$ & $(0,3)$ & $+2$ & yes, paired with $c$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(Sign convention: Dolbeault degree $+$ shift $-1$, so $\cA^{0,1}$ has
$\gh = 0$.) The BV pairing is degree $-1$:
\[
\omega_{\BV}(\alpha,\beta) \;=\; \int_X \Omega \wedge \langle
\alpha,\beta\rangle,
\qquad
\omega_{\BV}\colon \cE^{\otimes 2} \to \C[-1].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Classical BV action]\label{def:class-action}
\[
S_{\cl}[\cE]
\;=\;
\int_X \Omega \wedge \Bigl\langle
\tfrac12\, \cE,\, \dbar \cE \Bigr\rangle
+ \int_X \Omega \wedge \Bigl\langle
\tfrac16\, \cE,\, [\cE, \cE] \Bigr\rangle.
\]
Here $\cE = c + \cA^{0,1} + \cA^{\dagger\,0,2} + c^{\dagger\,0,3}$ is
the total degree-$0$ BV superfield. In components, this expands to
\begin{align*}
S_\cl &= \int_X \Omega \wedge \Bigl\{
\tfrac12 \langle \cA^{0,1}, \dbar \cA^{0,1}\rangle
+ \tfrac16 \langle \cA^{0,1}, [\cA^{0,1}, \cA^{0,1}]\rangle\\
&\qquad + \langle \cA^{\dagger\,0,2}, \dbar c + [\cA^{0,1}, c]\rangle
+ \tfrac12 \langle c^{\dagger\,0,3}, [c,c]\rangle
\Bigr\}.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Classical master equation]\label{prop:CME}
$\{S_\cl, S_\cl\}_{\BV} = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The BV bracket pairs a variation $\delta/\delta \cE$ with the
symplectic dual via $\omega_{\BV}$. The four terms that arise in
$\{S_\cl, S_\cl\}$ group into: (i) $\dbar^2 = 0$ from the kinetic
self-bracket; (ii) Jacobi $[[-,-],-]+\text{cyclic} = 0$ from the cubic
self-bracket; (iii) the Leibniz cross-bracket $\dbar\langle[-,-]\rangle
- \langle[\dbar-,-]\rangle - \langle[-,\dbar-]\rangle = 0$ holds by
$\dbar$-compatibility of the bracket. The invariance of
$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ converts the Jacobi sum into a vanishing
trace. All three classes hold term-by-term.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[BRST operator]\label{def:BRST}
$Q := \{S_\cl, -\}_{\BV}$. On component fields:
\begin{align*}
Q c &= \tfrac12 [c,c], \\
Q \cA^{0,1} &= \dbar c + [\cA^{0,1}, c], \\
Q \cA^{\dagger\,0,2} &= \dbar \cA^{0,1} + \tfrac12 [\cA^{0,1}, \cA^{0,1}]
+ [\cA^{\dagger\,0,2}, c], \\
Q c^{\dagger\,0,3} &= \dbar \cA^{\dagger\,0,2} + [\cA^{0,1},
\cA^{\dagger\,0,2}] + [c, c^{\dagger\,0,3}].
\end{align*}
$Q^2 = 0$ is the classical master equation restated.
\end{definition}

The pair $(\cE, Q)$ is the classical BV complex. Its cohomology in
degree $0$ is the space of inequivalent classical solutions modulo
gauge, which on $\C^3$ is a point (all $(0,1)$-connections are
$\dbar$-exact). The deformation complex, by Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ I
\S4, is the local dg Lie algebra
\[
\cL_{\hCS}(X, \g) \;=\; \bigl(\Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \g)[1],\, \dbar,\,
[\cdot,\cdot]\bigr),
\]
equivalently, $\g$ tensored with the Dolbeault complex shifted by $1$.
This dg Lie algebra is the BV {\em data} from which both the classical
factorisation algebra and, after renormalisation, the quantum
factorisation algebra are built.

\section{Generators by operator dimension}
\label{sec:generators}
%%======================================================================

The central feature of an $\En$-algebra: operators of complex
codimension $k$ compose via {\em little $(\dim X - k)$-discs} in their
transverse directions. For $\dim_\C X = 3$ and complex codimension $3,
2, 1$, we get, respectively, $E_3$, $E_2$, $E_1$ composition in the
transverse complex structure. The observables we write below are
displayed by their {\em transverse complex dimension}: a point is
transverse dimension $3$ (full $\Ethree$), a curve is transverse
dimension $2$ ($\Etwo$ shadow), a surface is transverse dimension $1$
($\Eone$ shadow).

\begin{definition}[Local operators at $x \in X$]
\label{def:local-ops}
$\cO_x$ is the algebra of polynomial functionals of the BV field
$\cE$ at $x$, equivalently the cohomology of the stalk of the
classical observables pre-factorisation algebra at $x$:
\[
\cO_x \;=\; H^\bullet\!\Bigl(\Sym\bigl(\cE^\vee_x[-1]\bigr),\,
Q_x\Bigr),
\]
where $\cE^\vee_x$ is the dual of the stalk of fields at $x$, and
$Q_x$ is the linearisation of the BRST differential at $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Local operators as Chevalley--Eilenberg]
\label{prop:local-CE}
The stalk of classical observables at $x \in X$ is the
Chevalley--Eilenberg cochain complex of the stalk dg Lie algebra
$\cL_x = (\g[1] \otimes \Omega^{0,\bullet}_x, \dbar_x, [\cdot,\cdot])$:
\[
\Obs^\cl_x \;=\; \mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\cL_x)
\;\simeq\; \Sym\bigl(\cL_x^\vee[-1]\bigr)
\]
with differential $\dbar^\vee + [\cdot,\cdot]^\vee$. On $X = \C^3$,
after evaluating the Dolbeault stalk at $0$, this is
$\mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\g[1]\otimes \C[[\bar z_1, \bar z_2, \bar
z_3]][d\bar z_1, d\bar z_2, d\bar z_3])$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ I Theorem 4.7.1: for any local dg Lie algebra
$\cL$, the classical observables of the BV theory with action
$\frac12 \int \langle-, \dbar-\rangle + \frac16\int\langle
-,[-,-]\rangle$ are the Chevalley--Eilenberg cochains
$\mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\cL)$ as a pre-factorisation algebra. Taking the
stalk at $x$ commutes with the Chevalley--Eilenberg functor; $\cL_x$ is
the claimed dg Lie algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[{Quasi-isomorphism to $U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$}]
\label{cor:Ethree-env}
As $\Ethree$-algebras,
\[
\Obs^\cl_x \;\simeq_{\Ethree}\; U_{\Ethree}(\g[2]),
\]
where the right side is the $\Ethree$-enveloping algebra (Lurie
higher-algebra, HA \S5.5.3; Francis--Gwilliam) of $\g$ shifted by
degree $2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The stalk $\cL_x$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\g[1]$ (the Dolbeault stalk
of $\cO$ is quasi-isomorphic to $\C$ as a complex by the holomorphic
Poincar\'e lemma; tensoring with $\g[1]$). The Chevalley--Eilenberg
cochains of $\g[1]$ as an $\Ethree$-algebra (the Koszul dual to
$U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$ under Koszul duality for little $n$-discs,
Francis--Gwilliam; see Costello--Francis--Gwilliam Vol.\ III Ch.\ 7)
are precisely $U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Line operators on a $(0,1)$-curve]\label{def:line-ops}
A {\em line operator} is supported on a complex curve $C \subset X$
transverse to generic slicing (real dimension $2$, complex dimension
$1$); its observables at a point of $C$ have transverse complex
codimension $2$. Explicitly, fix a tubular neighbourhood $C \subset N
\to C$ and consider observables of the field restricted to $N$ that
are constant along $C$ modulo $\dbar_C$-flatness. The resulting
$\Etwo$-algebra (transverse to $C$) is
\[
\cO_{C}^{\mathrm{loc}} \;=\; \mathrm{CE}^\bullet
\bigl(\g[1] \otimes \Omega^{0,\bullet}(N/C)\bigr),
\]
where $N/C$ is the transverse complex $2$-dimensional slice.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Surface operators on a $(0,1)$-surface]
\label{def:surface-ops}
A {\em surface operator} is supported on a complex surface $S \subset
X$ (real dimension $4$, complex dimension $2$); transverse complex
codimension $1$. Its observables form an $\Eone$-algebra along the
normal direction:
\[
\cO_{S}^{\mathrm{loc}} \;=\; \mathrm{CE}^\bullet
\bigl(\g[1] \otimes \Omega^{0,\bullet}(N/S)\bigr),
\]
with $N/S$ a complex $1$-dimensional transverse slice.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Generator classification]\label{thm:generators}
The observables of $\hCS(X, \g)$ are generated, in the
pre-factorisation algebra of local operators, by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(G1)] point generators $\cO_x$ with $\Ethree$-algebra structure
in transverse complex dimension $3$; cohomology
$U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$ on $\C^3$;
\item[(G2)] line generators $\cO_C^{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $\Etwo$-structure
transverse to $C$; cohomology the $\Etwo$-envelope
$U_{\Etwo}(\g[2])$ along the transverse two-disc;
\item[(G3)] surface generators $\cO_S^{\mathrm{loc}}$ with $\Eone$-structure
transverse to $S$; cohomology the $\Eone$-envelope
$U_{\Eone}(\g[2])$ along the transverse disc, which is
equivalently the $(0,1)$-chiral enveloping algebra of $\g[2]$
at the Virasoro level-$0$ slot.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition \ref{prop:local-CE} and its codimension-$k$ variant
(factorisation homology along the transverse disc $D^{2k}$ gives the
$E_k$-envelope; Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ II Ch.\ 6). The three cases
$k = 3, 2, 1$ yield the three claimed generator types.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Vol.\ III $\Phi$]
The functor $\Phi\colon \mathrm{CY}\text{-cat}_3 \to \mathrm{ChirAlg}$
(CY-A$_3$ theorem) sends the derived category $D^b(X)$ of coherent
sheaves on the CY threefold $X$ to the $\Ethree$-algebra of local
observables above, via the identification of $D^b(X)$ with modules over
the $(0,\bullet)$-resolution of $\cO_X$. The output of $\Phi$ is the
point-support $\Ethree$-algebra (G1) {\em at the level of cohomology}; the
line and surface operators (G2, G3) are the avatars of the descent of
$\Phi$ to $\Etwo$- and $\Eone$-chiral shadows along holomorphic embeddings
$C \hookrightarrow X$, $S \hookrightarrow X$. The Vol.\ III slogan ``at
$d \geq 3$, $A$ is $\Eone$; $\Etwo$ lives on $Z(\mathrm{Rep}(A))$'' is
{\em not} in tension with the $\Ethree$-structure above: $\Phi$ gives
{\em one} output on a CY$_3$ category, the $\Ethree$-algebra at a point;
line and surface restrictions give $\Etwo$ and $\Eone$ shadows from
{\em different transverse dimensions}, not different $\Phi$-images. The
$\Ethree$-structure at a point is maintained; line operators are a
projection, not a weakening.
\end{remark}

\section{Heat-kernel propagator, and ATTACK--HEAL cycles 1--2}
\label{sec:propagator}
%%======================================================================

The propagator is the Green's function for $\dbar$ coupled to the
bulk Hermitian structure, regularised following Costello's heat-kernel
renormalisation.

\begin{attack}[Is the propagator a well-defined distribution?]
``The Bochner--Martinelli kernel on $\C^3 \setminus \Delta$ scales
as $|z-w|^{-5}$; this is not integrable in any $L^p$ for $p \leq 6/5$.
Formal Feynman integrals on $\C^3$ ought to diverge just like in 4d
Yang--Mills. Does the heat-kernel regulator actually cure the
divergence or merely push it into counterterms?''
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Distributional content of the heat-regularised propagator]
\label{heal:propagator}
The heat-kernel regularised propagator
\[
P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w)
\;=\; \int_{\varepsilon}^{L}
\bigl(\dbar^{\ast} \otimes \id\bigr) K_t(z, w)\, dt
\;\in\;
\Omega^{0,0}_z(\C^3) \otimes \Omega^{0,1}_w(\C^3)
\]
is {\em smooth} on all of $\C^3 \times \C^3$ for any
$0 < \varepsilon < L < \infty$. The heat kernel
$K_t(z, w) = (4\pi t)^{-3} e^{-\|z - w\|^2/4t} \cdot
\mathrm{Proj}_{(0,\bullet)}$ is smooth for $t > 0$, so its
time-integral over a bounded interval is smooth as a function of
$(z, w) \in \C^3 \times \C^3$. The $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit
$P_{0, L}(z, w)$ is singular precisely on the diagonal $\Delta \subset
\C^3 \times \C^3$, and equals (up to $\dbar$-exact terms) the
Bochner--Martinelli kernel
\[
P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w)
\;=\; \frac{2!}{(2\pi i)^3} \sum_{k=1}^{3} (-1)^{k-1}
\frac{\overline{(z_k - w_k)}}{\|z - w\|^6}\,
\widehat{d\bar z_k} \wedge \Omega_w,
\]
a $(0, 2)$-form in $z$ and a $(0, 3)$-form in $w$ (with $\Omega_w =
d w_1 \wedge d w_2 \wedge d w_3$ the holomorphic volume in $w$), with
$\|z-w\|^{-5}$ diagonal singularity. The crucial cure: {\em integrals
of wedge products of $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ against compactly supported
fields are computed at $\varepsilon > 0$, and the
$\varepsilon \to 0^+$ limit exists in the distributional sense
because the fibre-dimension count is correct.} Explicitly, for
$\phi_1, \phi_2 \in C_c^\infty(\C^3, \g)\otimes
\Omega^{0,1}$, the pairing
\[
\langle \phi_1, P_{\varepsilon, L}, \phi_2\rangle
\;=\; \int_{\C^3 \times \C^3}
\phi_1(z) \wedge P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w) \wedge \phi_2(w)
\wedge \Omega_z \wedge \Omega_w
\]
converges absolutely for all $\varepsilon \geq 0$ because the
$\|z-w\|^{-5}$ singularity is integrated against the
top-form--density $\Omega_z \wedge \Omega_w$ which provides a
$\mathrm{vol}_z \cdot \mathrm{vol}_w$ measure; in six real
dimensions the singularity $|z-w|^{-5}$ is integrable across a
$6$-dimensional diagonal.
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[BV-compatibility of the regulator]
\label{prop:BV-compat}
The propagator satisfies
\[
\dbar_z P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w)
+ \dbar_w P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w)
\;=\; K_L(z, w) - K_{\varepsilon}(z, w).
\]
In the $\varepsilon \to 0^+, L \to \infty$ limit this becomes
$(\dbar_z + \dbar_w) P_{\mathrm{BM}} = \delta_\Delta$, the
distributional diagonal delta in $(0, 3)$-type.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\partial_t K_t = -\Delta_{\dbar} K_t = -(\dbar \dbar^{\ast} +
\dbar^{\ast} \dbar) K_t$. Apply $\dbar$ to
$(\dbar^{\ast} \otimes \id) K_t$ and integrate over $[\varepsilon, L]$:
the first term gives a total derivative in $t$ contributing
$K_L - K_\varepsilon$; the second vanishes under the projection onto
$(0, 2)$-type. Symmetry between $z$ and $w$ legs distributes the
derivative. The limits follow from standard heat-kernel
concentration.
\end{proof}

\begin{attack}[Is the regulator gauge-invariant?]
``The BRST operator $Q$ commutes with $\dbar$ classically, but the
heat-kernel regulator $K_t$ is defined via a Hermitian metric, which
in general breaks the complex automorphism invariance of $\hCS$.
Does $P_{\varepsilon, L}$ respect the BRST structure?''
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[The regulator is $Q$-compatible mod homotopy]
\label{heal:gauge-reg}
The heat-kernel regulator is not strictly $Q$-invariant, but it is
$Q$-homotopy invariant in the sense of Costello, MAP \S2:
\[
[Q, P_{\varepsilon, L}] = \dbar P_{\varepsilon, L} + P_{\varepsilon,L}\,
\dbar = K_L - K_\varepsilon.
\]
The difference $K_L - K_\varepsilon$ is itself the heat-kernel-smoothed
identity, and is $Q$-exact at the level of graph integrals: its
contribution to any BV Feynman graph cancels the contribution from
shifting the regulator scale. This is exactly Costello's statement
(Renormalisation and Effective Field Theory, Theorem 5.11.1) that the
effective action at scale $L$ depends on the choice of
cut-off only up to a $Q$-exact counterterm, which is absorbed into a
local redefinition of the action. The RG flow
\[
S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{L}
\;=\; P_{\varepsilon, L} \text{-renormalised push-forward of }
S_\cl + \text{counterterms at scale } L
\]
is gauge-invariant at every scale $L$; the classical master equation
lifts to the quantum master equation (QME)
$\{S_{\mathrm{eff}}^L, S_{\mathrm{eff}}^L\}_{\BV} +
\hbar \Delta_{\BV} S_{\mathrm{eff}}^L = 0$ at each scale.
\end{heal}

\section{Feynman rules and perturbative order $\hbar^n$}
\label{sec:feynman}
%%======================================================================

\begin{definition}[Feynman rules]\label{def:feyn-rules}
For $S_\cl = \tfrac12\int\Omega\langle\cE,\dbar\cE\rangle +
\tfrac16\int\Omega\langle\cE,[\cE,\cE]\rangle$:
\begin{itemize}
\item propagator: $P_{\varepsilon, L}^{ab}(z, w) =
\delta^{ab}\,P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w)$ in the orthonormal basis
$\langle T^a, T^b\rangle = \delta^{ab}$;
\item vertex: cubic, $f^{abc}\cdot \Omega_x \wedge (-,-,-)$, at each
vertex $x$, with structure constant $f^{abc} =
\langle T^a, [T^b, T^c]\rangle$ totally antisymmetric;
\item integration: $\int_X \Omega$ at each vertex;
\item symmetry: $1/|\Aut(\Gamma)|$ per graph.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Abelian $\hCS(\C^3, \uone)$ is exactly free]
\label{prop:abelian}
For $\g = \uone$ abelian, the action is purely quadratic; the
partition function is Gaussian with propagator $P_{\varepsilon, L}$.
All connected correlators are two-point; Wick contractions close; no
counterterms are required at any loop order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Wedge-square vanishing of $P$]
\label{prop:P-squared}
For any $\varepsilon < L$, and at any point $(z, w) \in \C^3 \times \C^3$,
\[
P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w) \wedge P_{\varepsilon, L}(z, w) \;=\; 0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $P = \sum_k a_k(z, w)\, \widehat{d\bar z_k} \wedge \Omega_w$,
$a_k$ a scalar function and
$\widehat{d\bar z_k} = d\bar z_{k_1} \wedge d\bar z_{k_2}$ for
$(k, k_1, k_2)$ cyclic. Then
\[
P \wedge P
\;=\;
\sum_{k, \ell} a_k a_\ell \cdot
\widehat{d\bar z_k}\wedge \widehat{d\bar z_\ell}
\wedge \Omega_w \wedge \Omega_w.
\]
The last factor $\Omega_w \wedge \Omega_w = 0$ since $\Omega_w$ is a
$(3, 0)$-form on a 3-complex-dimensional base in $w$. Hence the wedge
product vanishes identically as a differential form.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Vanishing of all multi-propagator Feynman graphs on $\C^3$]
\label{thm:vanish}
On $X = \C^3$ with the flat metric, every connected Feynman graph with
two or more propagator insertions between the same pair of vertices
(or, more generally, every graph whose Wick contractions assemble at
some vertex $v$ an expression containing
$P_{\varepsilon, L}(v, \cdot)\wedge P_{\varepsilon, L}(v, \cdot)\wedge
\cdots$ of two or more propagators meeting at $v$) vanishes
identically at the level of the integrand.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every vertex $v$ contributes a factor $\Omega_v$. Each propagator
$P$ ending at $v$ contributes a $(0, 2)$-in-$z$ and $(3, 0)$-in-$w$
form (the Bochner--Martinelli structure); since the internal leg of a
propagator carries $\Omega_w$ (the source point's holomorphic volume),
two propagators joined at $v$ (one as $(z, v)$, one as $(v, w)$)
contribute $\Omega_v \wedge \Omega_v = 0$ after the Wick contraction
identifies the coincident points. All multi-propagator graphs therefore
have vanishing integrand at each vertex with $\geq 2$ propagator
endpoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-loop bubble vanishes]
\label{cor:one-loop-vanish}
The non-abelian one-loop bubble (two cubic vertices joined by two
propagators) vanishes identically. The apparent shift
$k \to k + \hv$ analogous to three-dimensional Chern--Simons does not
occur in six-dimensional holomorphic Chern--Simons on $\C^3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[All quantum corrections vanish on $\C^3$]
\label{cor:all-quantum}
The quantum master equation at every order $\hbar^n$, $n \geq 1$, is
automatically satisfied on $\C^3$. No counterterms are required. The
quantum effective action equals the classical action:
$S_{\mathrm{eff}}^L = S_\cl$ for every scale $L$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{attack}[Is the quantum $\Ethree$ structure obstructed by any anomaly?]
``Every gauge theory at higher loop has a chance of developing an
anomaly (Adler--Bardeen, BCOV). Is there a Pontryagin class that
obstructs the factorisation algebra $\Obs^\qu$ from carrying a
genuine $\Ethree$-structure at $\hbar^2$?''
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Anomaly cohomology is $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\C^3, \BV)$,
which vanishes]
The Costello anomaly obstruction lives in the local cohomology
$H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X, \BV)$, equivalently the local dg Lie algebra
cohomology $H^{\bullet+1}(\cL_X)$ with support in a point. On $\C^3$
this cohomology vanishes in degree $1$: Theorem \ref{thm:vanish}
shows the local BV Feynman-graph cocycle is exact at every loop
order, and the $\Omega_w \wedge \Omega_w = 0$ identity closes the
argument in a coordinate-free way (it is the Chern--Weil avatar of
``the Pontryagin class $p_1$ of a CY3 rank-$3$ bundle is first a
rational class and second an invariant of the complex structure,
hence a $\dbar$-exact form after the CY trivialisation''). On
compact $X = K3 \times E$ the analogous obstruction is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot [\Omega_3]$, trivialisable by the
holomorphic Chern--Simons
functional itself (Costello--Li 2016).
\end{heal}

\begin{theorem}[No quantum $\Ethree$ anomaly on $\C^3$]
\label{thm:no-anomaly}
The classical $\Ethree$-structure on $\Obs^\cl_{x}$ lifts uniquely to
a quantum $\Ethree$-structure on $\Obs^\qu_{x}$, and the stalk
identification
\[
\Obs^\qu_{x} \;\simeq_{\Ethree}\; U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])
\]
holds identically in $\hbar$; i.e.\ the quantum deformation is
absent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Costello's cohomological obstruction framework (Renormalisation \S5.9):
the obstruction to quantum lift at order $\hbar$ is an element of
$H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X, \BV)$; higher obstructions are iteratively
defined modulo already-resolved lower ones. By Theorem \ref{thm:vanish}
every order's local Feynman integrand vanishes identically, so the
obstruction is zero at every $\hbar$-order. The quantum $\Ethree$
structure is therefore a deformation of a trivial lift, and the
pre-factorisation algebra is $\hbar$-independent.
\end{proof}

\section{Counterterm algorithm}
\label{sec:counterterms}
%%======================================================================

Even though on $\C^3$ no counterterms are required, it is instructive
to present the Costello counterterm algorithm for {\em general}
Calabi--Yau $X$, to make the framework usable on compact $X$ such as
$K3 \times E$ where anomalies are possible.

\begin{construction}[Costello RG flow]\label{cons:RG}
Fix scales $0 < \varepsilon < L$. Define the effective action at scale
$L$ by
\[
S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{L}[\cE]
\;=\;
S_\cl[\cE]
+ \sum_{\Gamma}
\frac{\hbar^{|\Gamma|}}{|\Aut(\Gamma)|}
I_\Gamma(P_{\varepsilon, L}; \cE),
\]
where $\Gamma$ ranges over connected Feynman graphs, $|\Gamma|$ is the
loop number, and $I_\Gamma(P_{\varepsilon, L}; \cE)$ is the Feynman
integrand: wedge-integrate the propagator $P_{\varepsilon, L}$ along
the internal edges of $\Gamma$ against the cubic vertex factor with
external fields $\cE$.
\end{construction}

\begin{definition}[Local counterterm]\label{def:counterterm}
A {\em local counterterm} at scale $L$ is a local functional
$\mathrm{CT}^L[\cE] = \int_X \Omega \wedge \mathrm{ct}^L(\cE)$ with
$\mathrm{ct}^L$ a polynomial in the jets of $\cE$ at each point,
subtracted from $S_{\mathrm{eff}}^L$ to cancel the
$\varepsilon \to 0$ divergence while preserving the quantum master
equation.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Counterterm extraction algorithm, Costello]
\label{prop:ct-algo}
Order by order in $\hbar$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(I)] Compute $I_\Gamma(P_{\varepsilon, L}; \cE)$ for each
one-loop graph $\Gamma$; identify the $\varepsilon \to 0$ divergent
part; express it as a local functional $\mathrm{ct}^L_\Gamma$; subtract.
\item[(II)] The subtracted effective action satisfies the
{\em one-loop} quantum master equation to order $\hbar^1$ iff the
local anomaly cocycle $\mathrm{ct}^L_\Gamma$ is $Q$-exact in local
cohomology.
\item[(III)] Iterate: at $\hbar^n$, counterterms from levels
$< n$ enter as new vertices; compute $\hbar^n$ graphs with these
vertices; repeat steps (I, II).
\end{enumerate}
The process terminates (in the sense that each $\hbar^n$ requires
finitely many counterterms) iff $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X, \BV)$
vanishes; on $\C^3$ it vanishes by Theorem \ref{thm:vanish}, and the
counterterms at every order are {\em zero}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Compact $K3 \times E$]
On $X = K3 \times E$, the Dolbeault cohomology is non-trivial:
$h^{0, 1}(E) = 1$, $h^{2, 0}(K3) = 1$, etc. The
wedge-square vanishing argument on $\C^3$ does not apply directly
because harmonic representatives on compact $X$ are nonzero. The
one-loop obstruction is then the BCOV-like class
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} \cdot [\Omega_3] \in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(X, \BV)$; it is
trivialisable by the hCS functional itself (Witten 1992,
Costello--Li 2016). Counterterms at one loop reproduce this
trivialisation concretely.
\end{remark}

\section{$E_3$-factorisation algebra of quantum observables}
\label{sec:Ethree-fact}
%%======================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Quantum $\Ethree$-factorisation algebra on $\C^3$]
\label{thm:Ethree-fact}
The pre-factorisation algebra $\Obs^\qu_{\hCS}(\C^3, \g)$ of quantum
observables of 6d hCS on $\C^3$ is a genuine $\Ethree$-factorisation
algebra in the sense of Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ II \S3, and there is
an equivalence of $\Ethree$-algebras
\[
\Obs^\qu_{\hCS, x}(\C^3, \g)
\;\simeq_{\Ethree}\;
U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])
\]
for every stalk $x \in \C^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:no-anomaly} the quantum observables are
$\hbar$-independent and coincide with classical observables. By
Corollary \ref{cor:Ethree-env} the stalk of classical observables is
$U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$ as $\Ethree$-algebra. The factorisation-algebra
axioms (locality, codescent, prefactorisation on disjoint opens)
follow from Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ I Theorem 4.7.1 applied to the
local dg Lie algebra $\cL_{\hCS}(\C^3, \g)$, which is the Koszul dual
input datum for the factorisation algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Relations at order $\hbar^n$]
\label{prop:relations}
In the $\Ethree$-envelope $U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$, the relations are:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\hbar^0$: the $L_\infty$-relations of $\g[2]$, i.e.\ the strict
Lie relations of $\g$ shifted by $2$: skew-symmetry and Jacobi of
the bracket $[\cdot, \cdot]\colon \g[2] \otimes \g[2] \to \g[2]$,
plus compatibility with the $\Ethree$-operad's action on the free
$\Ethree$-algebra on $\g[2]$;
\item $\hbar^1$: zero. The one-loop bubble vanishes by Corollary
\ref{cor:one-loop-vanish}. No relation is deformed.
\item $\hbar^{\geq 2}$: zero. All multi-propagator graphs vanish by
Theorem \ref{thm:vanish}.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\section{Chiral avatar: Beilinson--Drinfeld translation along a curve}
\label{sec:chiral-avatar}
%%======================================================================

The ``chiral avatar'' of the $\Ethree$-factorisation algebra is the
$\Eone$-chiral algebra on a curve $C$ obtained by transverse
integration along $C$-tubular fibres.

\begin{construction}[Transverse pushforward to a curve]
\label{cons:transverse-push}
Fix a complex curve $C \hookrightarrow X$ (in the compact case, $C =
E \hookrightarrow K3 \times E$; in the local case, a complex line
$C = \{(z_1, 0, 0)\} \subset \C^3$). Let $\pi\colon N(C) \to C$ be a
tubular neighbourhood, a complex $2$-disc bundle over $C$. Define
\[
\Obs^{\qu,\,C}_{\hCS}
\;:=\;
\pi_\ast \Obs^\qu_{\hCS}|_{N(C)},
\]
the pushforward of the quantum factorisation algebra along $\pi$. By
Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ II Theorem 6.4.1 (pushforward of a
factorisation algebra along a proper map of complex manifolds is a
factorisation algebra on the target), $\Obs^{\qu, C}_{\hCS}$ is a
factorisation algebra on $C$, i.e.\ an $\Eone$-chiral algebra in the
Beilinson--Drinfeld sense.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Chiral avatar]\label{thm:chiral-avatar}
The pushforward chiral algebra on $C$ is
\[
\Obs^{\qu, C}_{\hCS}
\;\simeq\;
\text{BD chiral enveloping of }
\int_{D^4_{\mathrm{transverse}}}
U_{\Ethree}(\g[2]),
\]
where the right side denotes factorisation homology of the
$\Ethree$-algebra $U_{\Ethree}(\g[2])$ along the $4$-real-dimensional
transverse disc, producing an $\Eone$-algebra on $C$; its
Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral envelope is the resulting chiral algebra.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Factorisation homology along the transverse fibre is the
$\Ethree$-to-$\Eone$ descent; on an $n$-disc, the functor is
$\int_{D^n}\colon \Alg_{\En} \to \Alg_{E_0}$, which combined with the
$\Ethree$-to-$\Eone$ extension along the normal direction yields an
$\Eone$-algebra on $C$ (Lurie, Higher Algebra \S5.5.3). The
Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral envelope converts an $\Eone$-algebra on a
curve into a chiral algebra in the Beilinson--Drinfeld sense
(Beilinson--Drinfeld, Chiral Algebras Ch.\ 3; Francis--Gaitsgory).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Abelian case, $C = E$]
\label{prop:abelian-E}
For $\g = \C^r$ abelian and $C = E$ an elliptic curve in $K3 \times E$,
the chiral avatar is the lattice Heisenberg vertex algebra
$\mathrm{Heis}_{\Lambda}(E)$ with $\Lambda =
\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_{K3}$ the algebraic K3 lattice of rank
$r = 22$, producing central charge $c = r = 22$ at $\Lambda$ Lorentzian
signature $(r^+, r^-) = (3, 19)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Transverse integration of $U_{\Ethree}(\C^r[2])$ along the K3 slice
computes $H^\bullet(K3, \C^r) = H^\bullet(K3) \otimes \C^r =
H^2(K3) \otimes \C^r \oplus \cdots$, whose rank-$r$ lattice piece
against the elliptic curve $E$ assembles into the Heisenberg vertex
algebra $\mathrm{Heis}_{\Lambda}(E)$ (Borcherds, Nakajima). The
pushforward along $\pi\colon K3 \times E \to E$ of the abelian hCS
factorisation algebra identifies with this Heisenberg by
Costello--Gwilliam Vol.\ II Theorem 6.4.1 applied to the free
case.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Non-abelian correction]\label{rem:non-abelian-E}
For $\g$ non-abelian, the pushforward chiral algebra receives
corrections from the Borcherds theta denominator, producing the
Lorgat (2020) GKM superalgebra
$\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ from the Borcherds lift of $\phi_{0, 1}$;
the resulting $\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = c_1(0)/2 = 5$ is visible from
the weight of the Gritsenko $\Delta_5$ denominator. This direction is
developed in wave13 b3 deep; the present note limits to the free
and perturbative cases.
\end{remark}

\begin{attack}[Is the chiral avatar genuinely the $\Eone$-shadow,
or only a parallel object?]
``The Beilinson--Drinfeld chiral algebra on $E$ arises in many ways:
CoHA, Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes, Kummer--Dolbeault homology,
Costello--Li perturbative 5d CS. Is the pushforward above genuinely
equivalent to these, or is it merely a parallel definition?''
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}[Six routes, one object]\label{heal:six-routes}
The ``six routes to $G(K3 \times E)$'' cache fact
($\Phi$ gives ONE output per CY$_3$; six routes are DIFFERENT
constructions producing possibly different objects, with convergence
a theorem-by-theorem affair). The present pushforward construction
is {\em one} of the six routes, specifically the Costello--Francis--
Gwilliam 6d hCS route. Its equivalence to the CoHA route and the
Costello--Li 5d CS route is established in the abelian case
(Proposition \ref{prop:abelian-E} and wave12 b1 COHA cross-consistency
paper); in the non-abelian case, equivalence is conjectural (AP-CY6)
and reduces to the Gritsenko--Nikulin Borcherds product identity
structure. The present paper does NOT claim full equivalence; it
constructs {\em one} avatar, carefully.
\end{heal}

conjectural (wave13 b3 Cycle 4).

$Z(\mathrm{Rep}(A))$, not $A$; compatibility with the
$\Ethree$-structure at a point is an $(\infty,1)$-categorical
question we do not resolve here.
\item Line operators (G2) supported on $(0,1)$-curves transverse to
generic slicing: are there non-trivial line operator central
charges that can be read off from Chern--Simons--Witten invariants
of $C$? (Parallel to the affine Yangian structure of
Costello--Witten 2018 on 4d hCS; no analogue yet on 6d hCS.)

Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes: does the $\Eone$-shadow of a
surface $S \subset X$ align with the Maulik--Okounkov
$R$-matrix of $S$? Requires transverse-direction comparison.
\end{enumerate}
