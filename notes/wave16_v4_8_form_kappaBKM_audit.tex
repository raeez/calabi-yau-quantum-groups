\section*{Eight Gritsenko--Clery Forms and BKM Scope}
\label{sec:eight-form-BKM-scope}

Three index systems meet at the level-one form $\Delta_5$ and then
separate. The primitive CHL denominator branch is indexed by
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue is
indexed by paramodular triples $(t,N_0;k)$. The order-indexed Nikulin
branch is indexed by symplectic K3 automorphism orders
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}$.

\subsection*{Primitive CHL Denominators}

The CHL denominator branch on the $K3\times E$ slice has
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2 \\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2 & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
Here $c_N(0)$ is the $\zeta^0q^0$ coefficient of the Borcherds input in
the CHL normalisation. Borcherds' singular theta lift gives
\[
  \wt(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2,
\]
and the Vol~III BKM characteristic on this branch is
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\wt(\Phi_N),\qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
At $N=1$,
\[
  \Phi_1=\Delta_5,\qquad
  c_1(0)=10,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5.
\]
The monic denominator for the Gritsenko--Nikulin BKM algebra is
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=\Dfive(2Z).
\]

\subsection*{Gritsenko--Clery Catalogue}

The Gritsenko--Clery diagonal-divisor catalogue consists of the eight
rows
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\mathrm{row} & (t,N_0;k) & \mathrm{form} & \wt & c^{\mathrm{GC}}(0) \\
\hline
1 & (1,1;5) & \Delta_5 & 5 & 10 \\
2 & (2,1;2) & D_2^{(2)} & 2 & 4 \\
3 & (1,2;3) & D_3^{(1,2)} & 3 & 6 \\
4 & (3,1;1) & D_1^{(3)} & 1 & 2 \\
5 & (1,3;2) & D_2^{(1,3)} & 2 & 4 \\
6 & (4,1;\tfrac12) & D_{1/2}^{(4)} & 1/2 & 1 \\
7 & (1,4;\tfrac32) & D_{3/2}^{(1,4)} & 3/2 & 3 \\
8 & (2,2;1) & D_1^{(2,2)} & 1 & 2
\end{array}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \wt=(5,2,3,1,2,1/2,3/2,1),
  \qquad
  c^{\mathrm{GC}}(0)=(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
For every catalogue row the modular-weight identity is
\[
  \wt(D_j)=c_j^{\mathrm{GC}}(0)/2.
\]
The catalogue weights are exactly the displayed set; rows of weights
$0$ and $1/4$ require a separate index system. A compact
Hall--Borcherds reading of a
catalogue row requires a root lattice, Weyl vector, positive chamber,
reflection character, simple-root multiplicities, and a comparison with
a compact Hall source.

\subsection*{Nikulin Orders}

For a symplectic K3 automorphism $g_N$ and its twined weak Jacobi input,
the order-indexed Nikulin branch has
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 4 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\
\wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}})=c_N(0)/2
& 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 2 & 1 & 1 & 1
\end{array}
\]
The CHL subsequence is $N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. The remaining Nikulin rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
N & \mathrm{frame\ shape} & T_{H^*}(g_N) & c_N(0) &
\wt(\Phi_N^{\mathrm{Nik}}) \\ \hline
5 & 1^4 5^4 & 8 & 4 & 2 \\
7 & 1^3 7^3 & 6 & 2 & 1 \\
8 & 1^2 2^1 4^1 8^2 & 6 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
These constants come from the specified twined Jacobi input:
\[
  c_N(0)=T_{H^*}(g_N)-2A_N,\qquad A_5=A_7=A_8=2.
\]
Thus
\[
  (c_5(0),c_7(0),c_8(0))=(4,2,2),
  \qquad
  \wt=(2,1,1).
\]
A smooth compact $K3\times E$ CHL CY$_3$ host for $N=5,7,8$ is an
additional construction, since the elliptic factor in the primitive CHL
quotient supplies an origin-fixing cyclotomic automorphism precisely at
\[
  \varphi(N)\mid2,\qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]

\subsection*{Level-One Normalisation}

Let $\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$ be the Eichler--Zagier weak Jacobi form
with
\[
  \phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}|_{q=0}=r^{-1}+10+r.
\]
The primitive Borcherds denominator uses $\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$ and
therefore
\[
  \operatorname{Borch}(\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}})=\Dfive,
  \qquad
  \wt(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=10/2=5.
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is
\[
  Z_{K3}=2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}.
\]
The doubled input lies on the scalar square line:
\[
  \operatorname{Borch}(2\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}})=\Dfive^2,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\theta}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2.
\]
Thus $\Delta_5$ has primitive denominator weight $5$, while
$\Phi_{10}$ has scalar-square weight $10$.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{$K3\times E$}{K3 x E} Invariants}

For $Y=K3\times E$,
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(Y)=(1,1,1,1),
  \qquad
  \kch(Y)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)=0.
\]
K\"unneth multiplicativity gives the compact categorical invariant
\[
  \kcat(Y)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)=2\cdot0=0.
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, the Borcherds denominator, and the
K3 fibre rank are distinct constructions:
\[
  \kHeis(Y)=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5,\qquad
  \kfib(Y)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=24.
\]
The compact chiral supertrace and compact categorical Euler
characteristic vanish on the product. The Borcherds value is the weight
of the denominator form. The fibre value is the Mukai-lattice rank.

\subsection*{Branch Boundaries}

The Hall--Borcherds branch is controlled by Borcherds products, Weyl
chambers, Hall--Drinfeld doubles, PBW comparison, and compact Hall
recognition. The Mukai self-mirror Yangian branch is controlled by the
Mukai lattice, stable envelopes, and Yangian or quantum-toroidal
specialisations.

For a GC row or a Nikulin row to enter the compact $K3\times E$ BKM
branch, the following data are required:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}, leftmargin=1.8em]
\item a CY$_3$ host theorem in the relevant geometric class;
\item the Borcherds product input, automorphic group, character, and
  constant $c(0)$ in the chosen normalisation;
\item the Lorentzian root lattice, Weyl vector, positive chamber,
  reflection character, and signed simple-root multiplicities;
\item a Hall--Drinfeld or compact-source comparison identifying the
  denominator algebra with the source-side primitive Hall algebra;
\item fresh computations of $\kcat$, $\kch$, and any specialised
  $\kch$-branch for the host.
\end{enumerate}
