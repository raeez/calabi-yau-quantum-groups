\begin{abstract}
For \(g\) independent copies of the Virasoro VOA at central charge
\(c\), with \(c \ne 0\) and \(5c+22 \ne 0\), the \(E_3\) bar spectral
sequence has
\[
  E_4 \cong [0,3,3,0]^{\otimes g},
  \qquad
  P_{E_4}(t) = (3t+3t^2)^g.
\]
Thus \(\dim E_4=6^g\). Degree reasons force \(E_4=E_\infty\) for
\(g\leq 3\). For \(g\geq4\) the identity is not a theorem without the
vanishing of the induced higher differentials; the first unresolved
map is \(d_5:E_4^8\to E_4^4\) at \(g=4\). The computation concerns
bar cohomology of an auxiliary \(E_3\) centre-type construction. It is
not a chain-level dimension, not a character-variety count, not a
native \(E_2\)-structure on a \(d\geq3\) CY-to-chiral output, and not a
construction of a global quantum vertex group \(G(X)\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}

Work over a field \(\kk\) of characteristic \(0\). Let
\[
  A_g=\bigoplus_{i=1}^g \Vir_c^{(i)},
  \qquad c\in\kk,\quad c\ne0,\quad 5c+22\ne0,
\]
where the summands are independent Virasoro copies: mixed OPEs between
distinct summands vanish. The symbol \(g\) is the number of independent
copies in this algebraic model. It is not the assertion that a single
chiral algebra on a genus-\(g\) curve factorises into \(g\) unrelated
Virasoro algebras.

The class \(M\) label used here is the shadow-depth class of the
\(A_\infty\) bar spectral sequence. In the Virasoro case the shadow
tower is non-terminating and the first class-distinguishing differential
on the \(E_3\) page is the quartic differential \(d_4\). No assertion
about moduli of curves, mock-modular K3 classes, BKM superalgebras, or
Maulik-Okounkov stable envelopes is part of this definition.

\section{The centre boundary}

The \(E_3\) bar complex in this note is a bar-cohomological object
attached to \(A_g\). It is not the native CY-to-chiral output in
complex dimension \(d\geq3\).

\begin{proposition}[Centre hypotheses and native level]
\label{prop:centre-scope}
Let \(A\) be an \(E_1\)-chiral algebra in a presentable stable
monoidal setting satisfying the dualisability and completeness
hypotheses required for the Ben-Zvi-Francis-Nadler comparison. Then
\[
  Z_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{der}}(A)=\HH^\bullet(A,A)
\]
is an \(E_2\)-algebra by the higher Deligne theorem, and its
representation category agrees with the Drinfeld centre of
\(\Rep^{E_1}(A)\) under those hypotheses. This produces a centre
object. It does not promote \(A\) itself from \(E_1\) to \(E_2\).

Consequently, if \(A=\Phi_d(\mathcal C)\) comes from the CY-to-chiral
construction with \(d\geq3\), the native output remains \(E_1\). Any
non-symmetric \(E_2\) braiding belongs to the derived or Drinfeld
centre, to a double, or to a module category where the half-braiding
has been constructed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The higher Deligne theorem gives an \(E_2\)-structure on Hochschild
cochains of an \(E_1\)-algebra. The BZFN comparison identifies this
algebraic centre with the categorical Drinfeld centre only after the
usual presentability and dualisability hypotheses. Both constructions
take \(A\) as input and produce a new centre object. Neither changes
the operadic level of \(A\). The CY-to-chiral convention used in
\texttt{chapters/theory/cy\_to\_chiral.tex} and
\texttt{chapters/theory/en\_factorization.tex} is exactly this:
\(d\leq2\) gives native \(E_2\)-chiral structure, while \(d\geq3\)
gives native \(E_1\)-chiral structure and recovers \(E_2\)-braiding
only through a centre or double construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No global \(G(X)\)]
Nothing below constructs \(G(X)\) for a general CY threefold. The
notation \(G(X)\) requires a positive half, a non-degenerate Hopf
pairing, a compatible completion, a Drinfeld double, and centre
compatibility. Those inputs are available only on constructed loci.
\end{remark}

\section{The one-copy computation}

\begin{proposition}[Virasoro \(d_4\) differential]
\label{prop:virasoro-one-copy}
Assume the tricomplex model for \(B_{E_3}(\Vir_c)\) used in
\texttt{chapters/theory/en\_factorization.tex}
Proposition \texttt{prop:virasoro-d4}. The \(E_3\) page is
\[
  E_3\cong \Lam^\bullet(\kk^3)=[1,3,3,1].
\]
The Virasoro OPE
\[
  T(z)T(w)\sim
  \frac{c/2}{(z-w)^4}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}
  +\frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}
\]
gives
\[
  \kch=\frac c2,\qquad
  S_4(c)=\frac{10}{c(5c+22)}.
\]
The induced quartic differential is the scalar map
\[
  d_4:\Lam^3(\kk^3)\longrightarrow \Lam^0(\kk^3),
  \qquad
  d_4(\omega_3)=8\kch S_4(c)=\frac{40}{5c+22}.
\]
It is an isomorphism between one-dimensional spaces. Hence
\[
  E_4=[0,3,3,0],
  \qquad
  P_{E_4}^{(1)}(t)=3t+3t^2,
  \qquad
  \dim E_4=6.
\]
For one copy, all \(d_r\) with \(r\geq5\) vanish by degree reasons, so
\(E_4=E_\infty\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first three pages reduce to the exterior algebra on the three
\(E_3\) directions. The quartic shadow \(S_4(c)\) is the connected
four-point Virasoro contribution after subtracting Gaussian pairings.
In the tricomplex normalization the volume class
\(\omega_3=e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3\) contracts with the unit by the
factor \(8\kch S_4(c)\). Substitution gives \(40/(5c+22)\), nonzero
under the stated hypotheses. Therefore \(\Lam^0\) is the image of
\(d_4\), \(\Lam^3\) has zero kernel on \(E_4\), and the middle
exterior degrees survive unchanged. The \(E_4\) support is contained
in degrees \(1\) and \(2\); a later differential has degree shift
\(r-1\geq4\), so it has no source-target pair inside the support.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent copies}

\begin{theorem}[Higher-genus class \(M\) bar cohomology]
\label{thm:class-m-higher-genus}
For \(A_g=\bigoplus_{i=1}^g\Vir_c^{(i)}\) under the hypotheses above,
the \(E_4\) page of the \(E_3\) bar spectral sequence is
\[
  E_4(A_g)
  \cong
  \bigotimes_{i=1}^g [0,3,3,0]_i.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  P_{E_4(A_g)}(t)=(3t+3t^2)^g=3^g t^g(1+t)^g,
\]
and
\[
  \dim E_4^n(A_g)=
  \begin{cases}
    3^g\binom{g}{n-g}, & g\leq n\leq2g,\\
    0, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
The total dimension of \(E_4\) is \(6^g\). For \(g\leq3\),
\[
  E_4=E_\infty,\qquad
  \dim H^\bullet\bigl(B_{E_3}(A_g),d_{\mathrm{tot}}\bigr)=6^g.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the \(E_3\) page,
\[
  \Lam^\bullet(\kk^{3g})
  \cong
  \bigotimes_{i=1}^g \Lam^\bullet(\kk_i^3).
\]
Because the summands of \(A_g\) are independent, all mixed quartic
terms \(m_4(T_i,T_j,T_k,T_l)\) with indices from more than one
summand vanish. Thus
\[
  d_4=\sum_{i=1}^g
  1\otimes\cdots\otimes d_4^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes1,
\]
where each \(d_4^{(i)}:\Lam^3(\kk_i^3)\to\Lam^0(\kk_i^3)\) is
multiplication by \(40/(5c+22)\). The Kunneth theorem over \(\kk\)
therefore gives
\[
  H^\bullet(\Lam^\bullet(\kk^{3g}),d_4)
  \cong
  \bigotimes_{i=1}^g
  H^\bullet(\Lam^\bullet(\kk_i^3),d_4^{(i)})
  =
  [0,3,3,0]^{\otimes g}.
\]
Multiplying the one-copy polynomial \(3t+3t^2\) gives the stated
degree formula and total dimension.

The support of this \(E_4\) page is the interval \([g,2g]\). A
differential \(d_r\) lowers total degree by \(r-1\). A nonzero
\(d_r\) on \(E_4\) requires both \(n\) and \(n-(r-1)\) to lie in
\([g,2g]\), hence \(r\leq g+1\). For \(g\leq3\) no differential with
\(r\geq5\) can act. The \(d_4\) differential has already been used in
forming \(E_4\), so \(E_4=E_\infty\) in this range.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit low-genus pages]
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccccc|c}
g & E_\infty^0 & E_\infty^1 & E_\infty^2 & E_\infty^3
  & E_\infty^4 & E_\infty^5 & E_\infty^6
  & E_\infty^7 & E_\infty^8 & E_\infty^9 & \dim\\
\hline
1 & 0 & 3 & 3 & 0 & & & & & & & 6\\
2 & 0 & 0 & 9 & 18 & 9 & 0 & 0 & & & & 36\\
3 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 27 & 81 & 81 & 27 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 216
\end{array}
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Chain level}

\begin{proposition}[Cohomology is finite, chains are not]
\label{prop:chain-level}
For one Virasoro copy, the chain-level \(E_3\) bar complex has
infinitely many nonzero conformal-weight pieces. Its vacuum-character
growth is represented by
\[
  P_{E_3}^{\mathrm{chain}}(q)
  =
  \prod_{n\geq2}(1-q^n)^{-3}
  =
  1+3q^2+3q^3+9q^4+9q^5+\cdots .
\]
Thus the total chain dimension is infinite. The number \(6^g\) is the
total dimension of \(E_\infty\) for \(g\leq3\), equivalently the
total dimension of \(E_4\) for all \(g\), not a chain-level dimension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The generic Virasoro vacuum module has independent descendants
generated by \(L_{-n}\) for \(n\geq2\). The \(E_3\) tricomplex has
three directions, giving the third power of the vacuum partition
product. The coefficient of each fixed \(q\)-degree is finite, but
there are infinitely many nonzero degrees, so the total dimension
diverges at \(q=1\). The cohomological computation above first passes
to the finite \(E_3\) page \(\Lam^\bullet(\kk^{3g})\) and then kills
the bottom and top exterior degrees in each independent copy by
\(d_4\).
\end{proof}

\section{The first open high-genus differential}

\begin{proposition}[High-genus status]
\label{prop:high-genus-status}
For \(g\geq4\), the equality
\[
  \dim H^\bullet(B_{E_3}(A_g),d_{\mathrm{tot}})=6^g
\]
is not proved by the \(d_4\) calculation. The unconditional statement is
\[
  \dim H^\bullet(B_{E_3}(A_g),d_{\mathrm{tot}})\leq6^g.
\]
At \(g=4\) the first unresolved map is
\[
  d_5:E_5^8\longrightarrow E_5^4,
\]
represented on the \(E_4\) cohomology by the \(m_5\)-induced map
between two \(81\)-dimensional vector spaces. Hence
\[
  \dim E_\infty(A_4)=1296-2\rk(d_5).
\]
The value of \(\rk(d_5)\) is an open computation. At \(c=1\) the local
shadow table records \(S_5=-16/9\), so no vanishing follows from the
shadow coefficient alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(g=4\), \(E_4\) is supported in degrees \([4,8]\). Since \(d_5\)
lowers degree by \(4\), the map \(E_5^8\to E_5^4\) is allowed by
degree. No \(d_r\) with \(r\geq6\) can act, because it would lower
degree by at least \(5\), outside the interval \([4,8]\). The total
dimension of a page drops by twice the rank of a differential, giving
the displayed formula. For larger \(g\), degree reasons allow further
higher differentials up to \(d_{g+1}\). The \(E_4\) dimension remains
an upper bound because spectral sequence pages over a field cannot
increase in total dimension.
\end{proof}

\section{No character-variety count}

The formula \(6^g\) is the Kunneth tensor power of the one-copy
cohomology \([0,3,3,0]\). It is not a Hausel-Rodriguez-Villegas
mixed-Hodge polynomial, not a point count of a \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\)
character variety, and not a Hitchin-system Betti number. Such a
comparison would require an explicit functor from the \(E_3\) bar
tricomplex of \(A_g\) to sheaves on \(\mathrm{Bun}_G(\Sigma_g)\) or to
Higgs moduli. No such functor is used or constructed here.
