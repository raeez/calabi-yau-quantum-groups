\section*{The Order-5 Borcea--Voisin Host Problem}
\label{sec:n5-borcea-voisin-candidate}

The integer \(5\) belongs to Nikulin's list of symplectic K3
automorphism orders.  The primitive CHL \(K3\times E\) ladder has
levels \(1,2,3,4,6\), since an elliptic curve has origin-fixing
automorphisms of orders \(1,2,3,4,6\).  Hence \(N=5\) supplies a
non-CHL host problem.

Let \(\PhiNik\) denote the order-indexed Nikulin Borcherds product
attached to the \(5A\)-twined K3 Jacobi input.  Its automorphic weight is
\[
  c_5(0)=4,\qquad
  \wt(\PhiNik)=\frac{c_5(0)}{2}=2 .
\]
The local arithmetic witness is the frame shape \(1^4 5^4\), the
frame-shape trace parameter \(T_{H^*}(g_5)=8\), and the
singular-theta correction \(A_5=2\), hence
\(c_5(0)=T_{H^*}(g_5)-2A_5=4\).  This is the
order-indexed Nikulin normalisation.  The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery
diagonal-divisor catalogue uses a different row system: \(\Dhalf\) is
the triple
\((t,N_{\mathrm{lvl}};k)=(4,1;\tfrac12)\), a half-integral dd-modular
form on the paramodular group \(\Gamma_4\).  The numerical equality
\(\#\Fix(g_5)=c_5(0)=4\) relates two distinct inputs: the first is a
fixed-locus count, while the second is a Jacobi constant term.  After a
BKM denominator algebra with denominator \(\PhiNik\) is fixed, the
corresponding automorphic characteristic is
\[
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\PhiNik})=2 .
\]

\subsection*{Borcea--Voisin Geometry}

Let \(S_5\) be a projective K3 surface with a symplectic automorphism
\(g_5\in\Auts(S_5)\) of order \(5\).  Nikulin's classification permits
this order, and Mukai's Mathieu classification gives
\(\#\Fix(g_5)=4\).  A Borcea--Voisin datum is the stronger triple
\((S_5,g_5,\iota_S)\), where \(\iota_S\) is an anti-symplectic
involution commuting with \(g_5\).  The commutation is an additional
hypothesis on the chosen surface and must be included in the
Borcea--Voisin input datum.

For a non-exceptional \(2\)-elementary K3 pair \((S_5,\iota_S)\), write
\((r,a,\delta)\) for Nikulin's invariant-lattice triple.  The fixed
locus has the form
\[
  \Fix(\iota_S)=C_g\sqcup R_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_k,
  \qquad
  g=\frac{22-r-a}{2},\qquad
  k=\frac{r-a}{2},
\]
where the \(R_i\) are rational curves.  The usual exceptional triples
are \((10,10,0)\), whose fixed locus is empty, and \((10,8,0)\), whose
fixed locus is the disjoint union of two elliptic curves.

Let \(E\) be any elliptic curve and let \(\iota_E(z)=-z\).  The quotient
\[
  X_5^{\BV}:=(S_5\times E)/(\iota_S\times\iota_E)
\]
has singular locus \(\Fix(\iota_S)\times E[2]\).  Borcea and Voisin
construct a crepant resolution
\[
  \pi:\widehat X_5^{\BV}\longrightarrow X_5^{\BV}
\]
which is a smooth Calabi--Yau threefold.  Its Hodge numbers are
\[
  h^{1,1}(\widehat X_5^{\BV})=5+3r-2a,\qquad
  h^{2,1}(\widehat X_5^{\BV})=65-3r-2a,
\]
and therefore
\[
  \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\widehat X_5^{\BV})
  =2\bigl(h^{1,1}-h^{2,1}\bigr)=12(r-10).
\]
The automorphism \(g_5\times\mathrm{id}_E\) descends to
\(\widehat X_5^{\BV}\).  Its fixed locus depends on
\(\Fix(g_5)\cap\Fix(\iota_S)\) and on the induced action over the
exceptional divisors.  These fixed-locus data belong to the compact
geometry; the automorphic constant \(c_5(0)=4\) belongs to the
order-5 Jacobi input.

\subsection*{Automorphic Separation}

Borcherds' singular-theta formula gives the weight of a Borcherds
product from the constant term of its Jacobi input:
\[
  \wt(\mathrm{Borch}(\psi))=\frac{c_\psi(0)}{2}.
\]
For the order-5 Nikulin input the constant is \(c_5(0)=4\), hence the
target line has weight \(2\).  The denominator product has the formal
shape
\[
  \PhiNik(Z)=
  e^{2\pi i(\rho,Z)}
  \prod_{(m,n,\ell)>0}
  \bigl(1-e(m\tau+n\sigma+\ell z)\bigr)^{c_5(mn,\ell)},
\]
where \(e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)\), after fixing the order-5 Jacobi input,
Weyl chamber, reflection character, and Weyl vector.  These data are
part of the automorphic construction.  The Borcea--Voisin fixed locus
supplies compact geometric data.

The \(N=1\) Igusa normalisation remains a different object:
\[
  \Dmonic=64^{-1}\DeltaFive,\qquad
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\DeltaFive^2 .
\]
Here the subscript in \(\DeltaFive\) is the weight \(5\) of the
primitive \(K3\times E\) denominator.  It is a level-one Igusa label.
Those identities govern the primitive \(K3\times E\) denominator and
its scalar square.  The order-5 Nikulin product \(\PhiNik\) is governed
by the \(c_5(0)=4\) Jacobi input.

\subsection*{Reduced Donaldson--Thomas Comparison}

A reduced virtual class for \(K3\times E\) uses the holomorphic
two-form on the K3 factor and the translation geometry of the elliptic
factor.  A Borcea--Voisin comparison at order \(5\) requires, at
minimum, a \(g_5\)-equivariant obstruction theory, a
cosection or filtration producing a reduced zero-dimensional class, an
orientation character compatible with the quotient and the crepant
resolution, and a proof that the resulting Jacobi input is the one
whose constant term is \(c_5(0)=4\).

Once those data are supplied, the reduced series is
\[
  Z^{\BV}_{5,\beta}(p,q,y)=
  \sum_n p^n q^{\beta^2/2}y^{(\beta,f)}
  \int_{[\mathcal M_{\beta,n}(\widehat X_5^{\BV})]^{\red}}\!1 .
\]
Here \(\mathcal M_{\beta,n}\) denotes the chosen moduli stack of stable
sheaves or stable pairs with curve class \(\beta\) and Euler parameter
\(n\); changing this moduli problem changes the orientation and the
series.
A comparison theorem has the form
\[
  Z^{\BV}_{5,\beta=L}=C_5\,\PhiNik^{-m_5},
  \qquad C_5\in\QQ^\times,\quad m_5\in\QQ,
\]
on a specified chamber and cover.  The comparison theorem must supply
the exponent \(m_5\); the automorphic weight \(\wt(\PhiNik)=2\)
supplies the target line, while \(m_5\) is enumerative data.

\subsection*{BPS Algebra Recognition}

A compact cohomological Hall algebra for \(\widehat X_5^{\BV}\),
constructed with the reduced orientation above, produces an
\(E_1\)-chiral object after applying the Calabi--Yau threefold functor.
At \(d=3\) the output algebra \(A\) is \(E_1\); any \(E_2\)-structure
belongs to a derived centre or a representation category.

The Borcherds target is a BKM denominator algebra attached to
\(\PhiNik\).  This is the Hall--Drinfeld/Borcherds branch.  The Mukai
self-mirror Yangian of a K3 surface is a separate branch with a
different presentation.  A theorem identifying the positive half of a
compact Hall algebra with the positive half of this BKM algebra must
provide:
\[
\begin{gathered}
  \text{orientation data},\quad
  \text{Hall product},\quad
  \text{Hall--Borcherds bracket compatibility},\\
  \text{parity convention for signed root multiplicities},\quad
  \text{finite-height PBW and relation-preservation checks}.
\end{gathered}
\]
These data are precisely the proof obligations for the equality
\(\mathfrak g_{\BPS}(\widehat X_5^{\BV})\cong\mathfrak g_{\PhiNik}\)
as a theorem-level recognition statement.

\subsection*{Invariants}

For every smooth compact Calabi--Yau threefold \(X\),
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(X)=(1,0,0,1),\qquad
  \chi(\mathcal O_X)=1-1=0 .
\]
Therefore
\[
  \kcat(\widehat X_5^{\BV})=0,\qquad
  \kch(\widehat X_5^{\BV})=0
\]
in the compact \(d=3\) Hodge-supertrace normalisation.  This invariant
calculation is separate from the order-5 reduced DT package and the
Hall--Borcherds comparison.  The automorphic invariant is
\[
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\PhiNik})=2 .
\]
The value is read from \(c_5(0)/2\) after the denominator algebra
\(\mathfrak g_{\PhiNik}\) is fixed.  The primitive \(K3\times E\)
reference constants are
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kch(K3\times E)=0,\qquad
  \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,\qquad
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\DeltaFive})=5,\qquad
  \kfib=24 .
\]
They are distinct invariants from distinct constructions.  The exceptional
\(\mathbb P^1\)-bundles in the Borcea--Voisin resolution contribute to
ordinary topology and Hodge numbers.  A Borcea--Voisin value of
\(\kfib\) requires its own construction.

\subsection*{Comparison Data}

Borcea--Voisin gives a smooth compact CY$_3$ once the commuting pair
\((g_5,\iota_S)\) is supplied.  The order-5 automorphic row has
\[
  (c_5(0),\wt,\kBKM)=(4,2,2),
\]
where the last entry refers to the recognised BKM denominator algebra.
The compact CY$_3$ realisation of that automorphic row requires four
inputs: the commuting Borcea--Voisin host, the reduced DT package, the
weight-\(2\) Borcherds product \(\PhiNik\), and the finite
Hall--Borcherds recognition datum.  Until these inputs are constructed
in one normalisation, \(\PhiNik\) remains an automorphic target.
