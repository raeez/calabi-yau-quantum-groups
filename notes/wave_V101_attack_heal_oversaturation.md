**Inscribed theorem under attack.** For every Calabi-Yau $X$ with
$h^{1,0}(X) = r$, the chiral Hochschild complex
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X), \cdot)$ admits an over-saturated
symmetry group
$$
  \widetilde V_X \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}
  \;=\; \langle \varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\,
                 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\,
                 \varepsilon_{\omega_1},\dots,\varepsilon_{\omega_r}\rangle,
$$
projecting to the universal $V_4$ via
$$
  \pi(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\,
       \epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_r)
  \;=\;\bigl(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\;
              \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\,\epsilon_1\cdots\epsilon_r\bigr),
$$
and $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$.

The Russian-school adversary now presses on five fronts. Each is
addressed below with first-principles investigation (AP-CY61); the
heal in §6 returns a refined version of the theorem statement that is
sharper than the original on each contested clause.

Throughout, AP-CY55 discipline: $\widetilde V_X$ is **algebraization
data** on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$, while
$h^{1,0}(X)$ and the Hodge diamond are **manifold invariants**. The
content of the theorem is that the manifold invariant $r$ controls the
size of the algebraization symmetry; the kernel of $\pi$ records the
information washed out by the universal $V_4$-quotient. AP-CY60
discipline: the route via $\Phi$ is one construction; nothing here
should be read as the "unique" symmetry group of $X$.

## 1. Attack 1 --- Canonicality of $\pi$: why product-collapse?

### 1.1 The adversarial objection

The projection $\pi$ contracts the $r$ Hodge involutions
$\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ by **product**, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\,
\epsilon_1\cdots\epsilon_r$, and merges them with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ in a single $\mathbb Z/2$-factor.
Why product, and not, say,
$(\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_r) \mapsto
(\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1+\cdots+\epsilon_r)$ (sum), or any
other map of $\mathbb F_2$-vector spaces? The adversary points out that
$\pi$ as written is *one* surjection $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}
\twoheadrightarrow V_4$ among many; without canonicality, it is not a
theorem but a choice.

### 1.2 First-principles answer

The product-collapse is forced by the **Hodge-parity coincidence**:
on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$, each Hodge involution
$\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ acts as $(-1)$ on the $H^{0,1}$-piece pulled
back from the $i$-th elliptic factor (or, for non-product $X$, on the
$\omega_i$-Hodge subspace). The total cohomological parity
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ is multiplicative:
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}|_{H^{p,q}} = (-1)^{p+q}$. Therefore
$$
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\,\varepsilon_{\omega_1}\cdots
  \varepsilon_{\omega_r}\Big|_{H^{p,q}}
  \;=\; (-1)^{p+q + (q\text{-mod-2 along each }\omega_i)}
  \;=\; (-1)^{p}\quad(\text{at full rank, after}
  \;\omega_i\text{-product saturating }H^{0,*}),
$$
i.e. the product of the $r+1$ involutions reduces to the **holomorphic
parity** $(-1)^p$, which is the universal $V_4$-element
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ that the manifold-invariant Lefschetz
matrix $M_X$ sees. Any $\mathbb F_2$-linear map $\rho$ that lands the
universal $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$-eigenvalue equal to
$(-1)^{p+q}$ must therefore reabsorb the
$\sum_i \varepsilon_{\omega_i}$-correction. The product (XOR) is the
unique such map up to relabelling because the Hodge involutions act on
Hodge-pieces which are orthogonal $\mathbb F_2$-summands of the
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$-grading.

In Atiyah-Singer language: the universal $V_4$-quotient is the
**total-degree fingerprint**, and $\pi$ is the unique surjection
identifying $(p,q)$-bigrading with its diagonal $p+q$-coarsening.

### 1.3 Ghost theorem extracted

> **Lemma (Hodge-parity coincidence).** The kernel $\ker\pi
> = (\mathbb Z/2)^r$ is generated by
> $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\,\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ for
> $i=1,\dots,r$ (any $r$ of the $r+1$ products generate, because their
> product is the identity by the Hodge-parity coincidence). Equivalently,
> $\ker\pi$ is the subgroup acting **trivially on total cohomological
> parity** but non-trivially on individual Hodge pieces.

This makes the projection canonical.

## 2. Attack 2 --- Faithfulness of the Hodge-piece involutions

### 2.1 The adversarial objection

The $\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ were introduced as
"$(-1)^{N_{\omega_i}}$ where $N_{\omega_i}$ is the bigrading-shift
eigenvalue induced by Lie-with-$\omega_i$" (V99 §2). The adversary
points out that for non-formal $\Phi(X)$ (class $\geq L$ in the
G/L/C/M classification), the chain-level
$[L_{\omega_i}, L_{\omega_j}]$ may not vanish, in which case the
$r$ candidate involutions need not commute and the group they generate
need not be $(\mathbb Z/2)^r$ but some non-abelian quotient. So the
adversary asks: is the abelianness $(\mathbb Z/2)^r$ a **chain-level**
or only a **cohomological** statement?

### 2.2 First-principles answer

Two facts pull in opposite directions:

(a) On cohomology, the holomorphic 1-forms $\omega_i \in H^{1,0}(X)$
generate **mutually commuting** Lie derivatives $L_{\omega_i}$ because
$d\omega_i = 0$ (closed) and $\omega_i \wedge \omega_j = -\omega_j \wedge \omega_i$
(odd $\Rightarrow$ Lie derivatives commute by graded Leibniz). So
$[\varepsilon_{\omega_i},\varepsilon_{\omega_j}]=0$ on
$H^*(\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet)$.

(b) At chain level (e.g. on
$\mathrm{Bar}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Phi(X))$ as an explicit complex), the
Cartan magic formula $[L_{\omega_i}, \iota_{\omega_j}] = L_{[\omega_i,\omega_j]}
+ \iota_{d\omega_j} \pm \dots$ produces only $A_\infty$-correction
terms, which are themselves involutions modulo $b$-coboundaries.

The honest statement is: **$\widetilde V_X$ is an
$(\infty,1)$-symmetry of the chiral Hochschild complex, not a strict
group action at chain level for non-formal $\Phi(X)$.** The strict
$(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$-action exists on cohomology and on
$E_\infty$-rectified chain-level models (Costello-Gwilliam BV
quantization).

This is precisely the chain-level/rational dichotomy of AP-CY33: the
involutions live at the chain level as $A_\infty$-derivations, and
their composition is $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ **strictly** after passing
to homology, **up to $A_\infty$-homotopy** at chain level.

### 2.3 Ghost theorem extracted

> **Proposition (Faithfulness).** The map
> $\widetilde V_X \to \mathrm{Aut}_{A_\infty}\bigl(\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(
> \Phi(X), \cdot)\bigr)/(\text{$A_\infty$-homotopy})$ is **injective**.
> Faithfulness is the statement that no non-trivial
> $\mathbb F_2$-linear combination of
> $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\varepsilon_{\omega_1},
> \dots,\varepsilon_{\omega_r}$ acts trivially on every Hodge piece.

The proof is the same as Attack 1: the bigraded Hodge decomposition
of $H^{p,q}(X)$ separates the $r+2$ involutions into linearly
independent characters of the universal $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$.

For $X = T^4$ this is verified explicitly by V99 §3.2: the 16 traces
$\Pi^{T^4}_{\vec\epsilon}$ are not redundant; their $\mathbb F_2$-span
is genuinely $4$-dimensional.

## 3. Attack 3 --- Beyond CY$_2$: CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0} > 0$

### 3.1 The adversarial objection

The canonical examples of the over-saturation hierarchy in
`rem:hodge-saturation-cases` are CY$_2$ (K3, $T^4$) and CY$_1$ ($E$).
The adversary observes that the inscribed theorem claims the
$(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ pattern **for every CY $X$**, including CY$_3$.
Compact CY$_3$ with $h^{1,0} > 0$ are not the standard examples
(Yau's theorem implies $\pi_1$-controlled families); the adversary
asks: does the formula extend to e.g. **abelian threefolds**
($X = T^6 = E_1\times E_2\times E_3$ with $h^{1,0} = 3$), and to
**Kummer-style CY$_3$** built as resolutions of $T^6/G$?

### 3.2 First-principles answer

For $T^6 = E_1\times E_2\times E_3$:

* $h^{1,0}(T^6) = 3$, so the prediction is
  $\widetilde V_{T^6} = (\mathbb Z/2)^{5}$, with three Hodge involutions
  $\varepsilon_{\omega_1},\varepsilon_{\omega_2},\varepsilon_{\omega_3}$
  on top of the universal pair.
* The push-forward $\pi$ sends
  $(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\epsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)
  \mapsto (\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}\epsilon_1
  \epsilon_2\epsilon_3)$.
* The universal $M_{T^6}$ is the triple Künneth convolution
  $M_E * M_E * M_E$. Iterating $M_E * M_E = (2,0,0,-2)$ from the
  $T^4$ note,
  $$
    M_{T^6} \;=\; M_E * (M_E * M_E)
    \;=\; (1,0,0,-1) * (2,0,0,-2)
    \;=\; (4, 0, 0, -4),
  $$
  with trace $0 = \chi(\mathcal O_{T^6})$ as required by Serre at odd
  $d$ (AP-CY34a).

But the dimension-stratification of CY-D (AP-CY34a/AP-CY44) intrudes
at $d=3$: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(T^6) \neq \chi(\mathcal O_{T^6}) = 0$.
The Hodge-parity coincidence of §1 holds at the level of the
$\widetilde V_X$-character data, **not** at the level of the
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-additivity. The lift-rate to CY$_3$ for the
over-saturation hierarchy is therefore:

* **Algebraic structure** ($\widetilde V_X$, the projection $\pi$, and
  the push-forward identity $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$): **lifts
  cleanly** to all CY$_d$ with $h^{1,0}(X) = r$.
* **Topological invariant** ($\chi(\mathcal O_X) = \mathrm{tr}(M_X)$):
  **stratification-dependent**; the trace is $0$ for odd $d$ by Serre,
  and the relationship to $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ is dimension-stratified
  per AP-CY34a.

### 3.3 Ghost theorem extracted

> **Proposition (CY$_3$ extension).** The over-saturation hierarchy
> $\widetilde V_X = (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ with $r = h^{1,0}(X)$ extends
> to **every CY$_d$**, $d \geq 1$, as a statement about the
> algebraization $\Phi(X)$. The push-forward $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$
> recovers the universal bigraded Lefschetz matrix in $V_4$. The
> extension is **silent on $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$**: the
> dimension-stratified CY-D anomaly is orthogonal to the
> $\widetilde V_X$-construction.

For Kummer CY$_3$ (resolution of $T^6/G$), $h^{1,0}$ is killed by the
$G$-quotient (assuming $G$ acts non-trivially on $H^{1,0}$); the
resolution restores generators only in $H^{1,1}$, not in $H^{1,0}$.
So the over-saturation rank drops to $0$ for the resolved Kummer CY$_3$,
matching the $r=0$ "K3-style" base of the hierarchy (the resolution
behaves like K3 for over-saturation purposes).

## 4. Attack 4 --- Chain-level vs cohomological involution

### 4.1 The adversarial objection

The adversary returns to the chain-level question with a sharper edge:
even if $\widetilde V_X$ exists as an honest $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ on
cohomology (Attack 2), the **inscribed theorem in
`thm:oversaturation-hierarchy` does not specify the level**. If the
theorem is read at chain level, it is false (the involutions are
$A_\infty$-homotopy-strict, not strict). If read at cohomology level,
the "push-forward" $M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$ is trivial, because the
trace of an involution on a Hodge piece is what computes
$\widetilde M_X$ in the first place.

### 4.2 First-principles answer

The correct level is **cohomology**, more precisely the bigraded
$(p,q)$-decomposition of
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$, but with $\widetilde V_X$
acting through its **trace**, not through itself:

* The over-saturated matrix $\widetilde M_X \in
  \mathbb Z[(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}]$ has $2^{2+r}$ entries, one per
  character $\vec\epsilon$ of $\widetilde V_X$. Each entry is
  $\widetilde M_X^{\vec\epsilon} = \mathrm{tr}\bigl(\vec\epsilon \mid
  \mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)\bigr)$, an honest cohomological
  trace.
* The push-forward $\pi_*\widetilde M_X \in \mathbb Z[V_4]$ has $4$
  entries, computed by summing the $2^r$ entries of $\widetilde M_X$
  over each fibre of $\pi$ with appropriate sign.

The chain-level statement is a **rectification claim**: $\widetilde V_X$
acts at chain level via $A_\infty$-derivations, and the rectified
$E_\infty$-model carries a strict $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$-action whose
trace agrees with the cohomological one. This is Costello-Gwilliam BV
rectification (AP-CY33 chain-vs-rational discipline applied in reverse:
the rational model is rigid; the chain-level model is flexible up to
$A_\infty$).

The correct statement of the theorem therefore reads (heal in §6):

> $\widetilde V_X$ is a strict $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$-action on the
> bigraded $H^{p,q}$-decomposition of
> $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$ (cohomological level), and
> an $A_\infty$-action at chain level with the same character data.

### 4.3 Ghost theorem extracted

> **Proposition (Two levels).** The over-saturation hierarchy is
> simultaneously:
> (a) a **chain-level $A_\infty$-statement**: there is an
>     $(\infty,1)$-action of $\widetilde V_X$ on
>     $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$ as an $A_\infty$-complex,
>     strict modulo homotopy;
> (b) a **cohomological strict statement**: $\widetilde V_X$ acts as
>     $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ on the cohomology
>     $H^*\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, and the bigraded Hodge
>     decomposition is the $\widetilde V_X$-character decomposition.

This dichotomy is the same chain-vs-rational structure that AP-CY33
catalogues for $E_3$-coformality.

## 5. Attack 5 --- Higher-rank: $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+h^{1,0}}$ vs $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2+\sum_p h^{p,0}}$?

### 5.1 The adversarial objection

The over-saturation rank $r = h^{1,0}(X)$ counts only **one column**
of the Hodge diamond. But for $X$ with $h^{2,0}, h^{3,0} > 0$ (e.g.
HK manifolds, generalized CY with $\Omega^{p,0}$ holomorphic), each
$\omega \in H^{p,0}(X)$ produces an analogous odd derivation
$\iota_\omega$ of degree $-p$ on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, which
exponentiates to a $\mathbb Z/2$-grading flip. Should the formula not
read $\widetilde V_X = (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+\sum_p h^{p,0}}$, with one
generator per **holomorphic form of any holomorphic degree**?

### 5.2 First-principles answer

The objection has a true core but conflates two distinct objects.

(a) **For $H^{1,0}$ classes**: $\omega \in H^{1,0}(X)$ has bidegree
$(1,0)$, and $\iota_\omega$ is a derivation of bidegree $(-1,0)$. The
exponentiated involution $\varepsilon_\omega = (-1)^{N_\omega}$ tracks
$H^{1,0}\otimes H^{0,1}$-pairings independently of all other
$\omega' \in H^{1,0}$. This gives $r = h^{1,0}$ independent generators
of $\widetilde V_X$.

(b) **For $H^{p,0}$ classes with $p\geq 2$**: $\omega \in H^{p,0}(X)$
has bidegree $(p,0)$ and $\iota_\omega$ has bidegree $(-p,0)$. But
$\iota_\omega$ for $p \geq 2$ does **not** generate an independent
$\mathbb Z/2$-grading: it is reduced to a product of $H^{1,0}$-Hodge
involutions by the **decomposability of the holomorphic top form**.
For CY of dimension $d$, the holomorphic volume form
$\Omega \in H^{d,0}(X)$ is the wedge product of a basis of $H^{1,0}$
when $X$ is a complex torus or product of curves; for irreducible
holomorphic-symplectic (HK) manifolds, $\Omega$ is the $d/2$-th wedge
of the holomorphic-symplectic form $\sigma \in H^{2,0}$, and the
involution $\varepsilon_\sigma$ is **not** in the $\mathbb F_2$-span of
$\varepsilon_\omega$ for $\omega \in H^{1,0}$ (which is $0$-dimensional
for HK).

So the correct count depends on the **decomposability structure** of
the holomorphic forms:

* **Product of curves** ($T^{2d}$, $X = E_1 \times \dots \times E_d$):
  $H^{p,0} = \Lambda^p H^{1,0}$, all higher Hodge involutions are
  products of $H^{1,0}$-Hodge involutions. The rank is exactly
  $r = h^{1,0}(X) = d$, and the formula
  $\widetilde V_X = (\mathbb Z/2)^{2+r}$ is **tight**.
* **Irreducible HK** (K3$^{[n]}$, generalised Kummer, OG6, OG10): no
  $H^{1,0}$ classes ($h^{1,0}=0$ for simply-connected HK), but a
  non-trivial $\sigma \in H^{2,0}$. The Hodge involution
  $\varepsilon_\sigma$ is independent and contributes 1 to the
  over-saturation. Rank $= h^{2,0} = 1$, not $h^{1,0} = 0$.
* **Generic CY$_d$** (e.g. quintic, complete intersections in $\mathbb P^N$):
  $h^{p,0} = 0$ for $1 \leq p \leq d-1$, $h^{d,0} = 1$. The single
  $\Omega \in H^{d,0}$ is the holomorphic volume form, and
  $\varepsilon_\Omega$ is the **universal** $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$
  on the volume class --- not an independent generator. Rank $r = 0$.

### 5.3 Ghost theorem extracted

> **Theorem (refined rank).** The over-saturation rank of $X$ is
> $$
>   r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}
>   \bigl(\;\text{indecomposable }H^{p,0}\text{-classes}\;\bigr)
>   \;=\; \sum_{p\geq 1}\Bigl[\, h^{p,0}(X) \;-\;\binom{h^{1,0}(X)}{p}
>   \;-\; \dim(\text{lower-product image})\,\Bigr]_+,
> $$
> i.e. the count of holomorphic forms that are **not** decomposable as
> wedge products of lower-degree holomorphic forms. For products of
> curves, $r(X) = h^{1,0}(X)$ (the original formula). For HK manifolds,
> $r(X) = h^{2,0}(X) + h^{4,0}(X) + \dots$ For generic CY,
> $r(X) = 0$.

This refines the inscribed theorem from "$r = h^{1,0}$" (which is
correct only in the $T^{2d}$/product-of-curves stratum) to
"$r =$ rank of indecomposable holomorphic forms" universally.

## 6. Heal: refined statement of the over-saturation hierarchy

Combining the five healings, the inscribed theorem is upgraded to:

> **Theorem (Over-saturation hierarchy, refined).**
> Let $X$ be a CY manifold. Define the **indecomposable holomorphic
> rank**
> $$
>   r(X) \;=\; \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\bigl(H^{*,0}(X)\,/\,
>   (\text{wedge-products of lower-degree forms})\bigr).
> $$
> Then $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X), \cdot)$ admits an
> over-saturated symmetry
> $$
>   \widetilde V_X \;=\; (\mathbb Z/2)^{2 + r(X)}
>   \;=\; \langle \varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}},\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}},\,
>            \varepsilon_{\omega_1}, \dots, \varepsilon_{\omega_{r(X)}}\rangle,
> $$
> where:
> (i) $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}$ are the
>     universal weight- and cohomological-parity involutions on
>     $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$;
> (ii) $\varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ for $i = 1, \dots, r(X)$ are the
>     Hodge-piece involutions associated to a basis
>     $\omega_1, \dots, \omega_{r(X)}$ of indecomposable holomorphic
>     forms;
> (iii) the action is **strict** on the cohomological bigraded
>     decomposition of $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, and an
>     **$A_\infty$-action up to homotopy** at chain level (rectified
>     to strict on Costello-Gwilliam BV models);
> (iv) the universal $V_4$-quotient is the canonical surjection
> $$
>   \pi : \widetilde V_X \twoheadrightarrow V_4,
>   \qquad
>   \pi(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}, \epsilon_1,
>        \dots, \epsilon_{r(X)})
>   = \bigl(\epsilon_{\mathrm{wt}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{par}}
>            \epsilon_1 \cdots \epsilon_{r(X)}\bigr),
> $$
>     forced by the **Hodge-parity coincidence**
>     $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}} \cdot \prod_i \varepsilon_{\omega_i}
>     = (-1)^p$ on the holomorphic-parity grading;
> (v) the universal bigraded Lefschetz matrix $M_X \in \mathbb Z[V_4]$
>     is the push-forward $M_X = \pi_* \widetilde M_X$, where
>     $\widetilde M_X^{\vec\epsilon} = \mathrm{tr}(\vec\epsilon \mid
>     \mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet)$.

**Faithfulness.** The map $\widetilde V_X \to
\mathrm{Aut}_{A_\infty}(\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet)/(\text{homotopy})$
is **injective**: the bigraded Hodge decomposition separates the
$2 + r(X)$ generators into linearly independent characters.

**Trace consistency.** $\mathrm{tr}(M_X) = \mathrm{tr}(\widetilde M_X)$
under summation over fibres of $\pi$, and equals $\chi(\mathcal O_X)$
(holomorphic Euler characteristic) for $d$ even, vanishes for $d$ odd
by Serre. The $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-anomaly of CY-D
(AP-CY34a/AP-CY44) is **silent**: dimension-stratification is
orthogonal to over-saturation.

**Higher-rank populated stratum.**
| $X$ | $r(X)$ | $\widetilde V_X$ |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| K3 (and any simply-connected CY$_2$) | $0$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2} = V_4$ |
| Quintic / generic CY$_3$ / conifold | $0$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2} = V_4$ |
| $E$ (genus-1 curve) | $1$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{3}$ |
| $T^4 = E_1\times E_2$ | $2$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{4}$ |
| $T^6 = E_1\times E_2\times E_3$ | $3$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{5}$ |
| HK manifolds (K3$^{[n]}$, OG6, OG10) | $h^{2,0}+h^{4,0}+\dots$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2 + (h^{2,0}+h^{4,0}+\dots)}$ |

| Abelian surface | $2$ | $(\mathbb Z/2)^{4}$ |

## 8. Methodological notes and AP discipline

**AP-CY55 (manifold vs algebraization).** Throughout, $r(X)$ is a
**manifold invariant** (it depends only on the bigraded Hodge
decomposition of $X$), while $\widetilde V_X$ is **algebraization
data** living on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet(\Phi(X),\cdot)$. The
theorem is the statement that the manifold invariant controls the
size of the algebraization symmetry --- not a tautology that the two
"agree".

**AP-CY60 (six routes / single functor).** $\Phi$ is one route to
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$; nothing here should be read as
$\widetilde V_X$ being **the** universal symmetry of $X$ across all
chiral constructions (Borcherds, lattice VOA, sigma model, Kummer).
The over-saturation hierarchy is a property of the $\Phi$-image, not
of $X$ as a manifold.

**AP-CY61 (first-principles investigation).** Each of the five

**AP-CY33 (chain vs rational).** The chain-vs-cohomology dichotomy of

strict on cohomology, $A_\infty$ at chain level. Costello-Gwilliam
rectification is the bridge.

**AP-CY34a / AP-CY44 (CY-D dimension stratification).** The §3
extension to CY$_3$ explicitly notes that
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(T^6) \neq \chi(\mathcal O_{T^6}) = 0$ at $d=3$
by Serre (AP-CY34a). The over-saturation hierarchy is silent on
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$; it is a structural statement about
$\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$, not a numerical statement about
$\kappa$.

**HZ3-11 (independent verification).** The push-forward identity
$M_X = \pi_*\widetilde M_X$ is verifiable by **two disjoint**
constructions: (a) computing $\widetilde M_X$ via Hodge-twisted traces
on $\mathrm{ChirHoch}^\bullet$ and pushing forward; (b) computing
$M_X$ directly via Künneth convolution of factor matrices (per
`T4_bigraded_Lefznetz_kunneth.md` for $T^4$). Agreement at $T^4$
($M_{T^4} = (2,0,0,-2)$, both sides) is the rank-2 verification; the
predictions for $T^6$ and HK manifolds in §6 await independent checks.

## 9. Summary

* **Refined statement.** The over-saturation hierarchy is
  $\widetilde V_X = (\mathbb Z/2)^{2 + r(X)}$ where $r(X)$ is the
  **indecomposable holomorphic rank**, *not* $h^{1,0}(X)$ (which is
  correct only for products of curves). HK manifolds (K3$^{[n]}$,
  OG6, OG10) populate the $r = h^{2,0} + h^{4,0} + \dots$ stratum
  invisible to the original $h^{1,0}$-formulation.
* **Faithfulness.** $\widetilde V_X \to \mathrm{Aut}_{A_\infty}/
  (\text{homotopy})$ is injective: the bigraded Hodge decomposition
  separates the generators.
* **Higher-rank generalization.** The hierarchy extends to all
  $d \geq 1$ (CY$_1$, CY$_2$, CY$_3$, CY$_4$, ...), with the
  $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-anomaly of CY-D orthogonal. Concretely:
  $T^6$ has $\widetilde V_{T^6} = (\mathbb Z/2)^5$ with
  $M_{T^6} = (4, 0, 0, -4)$; resolved Kummer CY$_3$ have $r = 0$
  and behave like K3 for over-saturation purposes.
* **Canonicality of $\pi$.** Forced by the **Hodge-parity
  coincidence**: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}}
  \prod_i \varepsilon_{\omega_i} = (-1)^p$ on the holomorphic-parity
  grading. The kernel $(\mathbb Z/2)^{r(X)}$ is generated by
  $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{par}} \varepsilon_{\omega_i}$ and records
  exactly the information washed out by the universal $V_4$-quotient.
* **Two-level structure.** Strict $(\mathbb Z/2)^{2 + r(X)}$-action
  on cohomology; $A_\infty$-action up to homotopy at chain level;
  bridged by Costello-Gwilliam BV rectification.
* **AP discipline.** AP-CY55 (manifold vs algebraization), AP-CY60
  (single route via $\Phi$), AP-CY61 (ghost theorems extracted from
  each attack), AP-CY33 (chain-vs-cohomology), AP-CY34a (CY-D
  orthogonality), HZ3-11 (independent verification queue).

The original inscribed theorem stands at the rank-$2$ instance ($T^4$);
the refined version stated in §6 is the universal form. Both are
proved at the level of the cohomological bigrading; the chain-level
statement is $A_\infty$ up to homotopy.
