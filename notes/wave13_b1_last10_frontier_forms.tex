\section*{Frontier Automorphic Forms Around the K3$\times E$ Denominator}
\label{sec:frontier-forms-standalone}

The genus-two diagonal-divisor problem asks for modular forms on
Hecke subgroups of paramodular groups whose divisor is exactly the
diagonal Humbert divisor, with vanishing order one.  The
Gritsenko--Cl\'ery theorem gives exactly eight such forms.  Lorgat's
2020 conjecture proposes an enumerative realisation of those eight
forms: after choosing a K3 surface \(S\), a lattice polarisation \(L\),
commuting finite-order symplectic automorphisms \((g_N,h_M)\), and an
elliptic curve \(E\) with an \(N\)-torsion point, the reduced
Donaldson--Thomas series of
\[
        X=(S\times E)/(\Z/N\Z)
\]
should have reciprocal square root equal, up to scalar, to the
corresponding diagonal-divisor form.  The associated denominator
algebra is a generalized Borcherds--Kac--Moody superalgebra whose
root multiplicities are read from the \((g_N,h_M)\)-twisted and twined
K3 elliptic genus.

The associated arithmetic boundary is larger than the eight
diagonal-divisor rows.  Nikulin's theorem permits
symplectic K3 automorphism orders \(N\leq 8\).  The primitive CHL
denominator branch has compact \(K3\times E\) BKM scope at
\[
        N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
The orders \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\) remain boundary automorphic and Nikulin
arithmetic data until a compatible Calabi--Yau threefold host and
a full BKM root datum are supplied.

\subsection*{Normalisations}
\label{sec:frontier-normalisations}

Let
\[
        q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\qquad r=e^{2\pi iz},\qquad
        s=e^{2\pi i\sigma},\qquad
        Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&z\\ z&\sigma\end{pmatrix}\in\HH_2 .
\]
For an index-one weak Jacobi form
\[
        \phi(\tau,z)=\sum_{n,\ell}c(n,\ell)q^nr^\ell ,
\]
write \(c(0)\) for the discriminant-zero coefficient in the
Eichler--Zagier variable \(D=4n-\ell^2\).  For the standard generator,
\[
        \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=r^{-1}+10+r+O(q),
\]
so \(c(0)=10\), while \(\phi_{0,1}(\tau,0)\) has constant term \(12\).
The Borcherds weight theorem gives
\[
        \wt(\mathrm{Borch}(\phi))=\frac{c(0)}{2}.
\]

At level one the primitive Gritsenko--Nikulin denominator is
\[
        \Delta_5=\mathrm{Borch}(\phi_{0,1}),\qquad
        c_1(0)=10,\qquad
        \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5 .
\]
The monic denominator product is
\[
        D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
        \operatorname{den}(\fg_{\Delta_5})=D_5(2Z).
\]
The square-normalised Igusa forms are separate scalar conventions:
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2 .
\]
Oberdieck--Pandharipande's reduced \(K3\times E\) scalar uses
\[
        Z_{\mathrm{OP/DT}}=-(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}})^{-1}
        =-D_5^{-2}=-4096\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
The factor \(64\) is a theta-leading coefficient in the monic product.
The BKM weight remains \(\kBKM(\Delta_5)=5\).

\subsection*{The Primitive CHL Denominator Row}
\label{sec:frontier-chl-row}

The primitive CHL branch is indexed by
\[
        N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
With the CHL Borcherds input normalised at the relevant cusp, the
constant terms and BKM weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6\\
\hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2\\
\kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2 & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1
\end{array}
\]
This is the denominator-algebra row.  Lower twining or row-normalised
constant lists carry no primitive CHL meaning without an explicit
conversion map.

\subsection*{The Eight Diagonal-Divisor Rows}
\label{sec:frontier-gc-table}

The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery catalogue is indexed by triples \((t,N;k)\).
The primitive CHL branch is indexed by the order \(N\).  In the
standard catalogue order its weights and twice-weight product constants are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{form} & (t,N) & \wt & c^{\mathrm{GC}}(0)=2\wt\\
\hline
\Delta_5 & (1,1) & 5 & 10\\
\Delta_2 & (2,1) & 2 & 4\\
\nabla_3 & (1,2) & 3 & 6\\
\Delta_1 & (3,1) & 1 & 2\\
\nabla_2 & (1,3) & 2 & 4\\
\Delta_{1/2} & (4,1) & \tfrac12 & 1\\
\nabla_{3/2} & (1,4) & \tfrac32 & 3\\
Q_1 & (2,2) & 1 & 2
\end{array}
\]
Only the first row is simultaneously the primitive level-one CHL
denominator.  The other seven rows may be compared with twisted K3
elliptic genera through Lorgat's conjectural DT construction, while
their product constants remain catalogue-row constants
\[
        (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2),
\]
separate from the primitive CHL row \((10,8,6,4,2)\).

\subsection*{The Reduced Donaldson--Thomas Series}
\label{sec:frontier-dt-series}

Let \(S\) be an elliptically fibred K3 surface
\(\pi:S\to\bP^1\) with sections \(s_1,s_2\), with \(s_2\) of order
\(N\) relative to \(s_1\).  Let \(F\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) be the
fibre class and set
\[
        \beta_h=\frac{1}{N}(s_1+\cdots+s_N+hF),\qquad h\geq 0 .
\]
For \(X=(S\times E)/(\Z/N\Z)\), the reduced numerical DT series has
the form
\[
Z^X_{L,h_M}(q,t,p)=
\sum_{h,d\geq0}\sum_{n\in\Z}
\DT^X_{n,(\beta_h,d)}\,
q^{d-1+\frac12\langle\beta_h,\beta_h\rangle}
t^d(-p)^n .
\]
At \(N=1\), the Oberdieck--Pandharipande/Oberdieck--Pixton scalar
identifies the reduced theory with the square convention above.  In
Lorgat's conjectural extension, the eight diagonal-divisor forms arise
as
\[
        \Phi_{t,N;k}(Z)=C\cdot\bigl(-Z^X_{L,h_M}(q,t,p)\bigr)^{-1/2},
\]
with the corresponding generalized BKM root multiplicities extracted
from the \((g_N,h_M)\)-twisted K3 elliptic genus
\[
        F^{(g_N,h_M)}_L(\tau,z)\in J^{\mathrm{weak}}_{0,1}.
\]
This statement is a conjectural DT-to-automorphic comparison for the
seven non-\(\Delta_5\) catalogue rows.

\subsection*{Borcherds Input and Root Data}
\label{sec:frontier-borcherds-input}

A twisted K3 elliptic genus \(F^{(g_N,h_M)}_L\) supplies the exponents
of a Borcherds product after four pieces of data have been fixed:
\[
        (L_N,\ \rho_N,\ \Delta^+_N,\ \operatorname{mult}_N).
\]
Here \(L_N\) is the Lorentzian lattice or its orthogonal realisation,
\(\rho_N\) is a Weyl vector, \(\Delta^+_N\) is a positive-root chamber,
and \(\operatorname{mult}_N(\alpha)\) is the signed supermultiplicity
read from the Jacobi coefficient attached to \(\alpha\).  Once these
data are supplied, Borcherds' theorem gives
\[
        \kBKM(\Phi_N)=\wt(\Phi_N)=\frac{c_N(0)}{2}.
\]
At \(N=1\) this recovers
\[
        \operatorname{den}(\fg_{\Delta_5})=D_5(2Z),\qquad
        \kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5})=5 .
\]
At \(N\in\{2,3,4,6\}\) the primitive CHL row gives the denominator
weights \(4,3,2,1\).  At \(N\in\{5,7,8\}\), a theorem requires the
same four data together with a compatible Calabi--Yau threefold host;
the Nikulin order alone supplies only the arithmetic boundary.

\subsection*{The \(K3\times E\) Invariant Family}
\label{sec:frontier-k3xe-invariants}

For \(Y=K3\times E\), the compact total-space Hodge column is
\[
        h^{0,\bullet}(Y)=(1,1,1,1),
\]
and hence
\[
        \kch(Y)=\sum_q(-1)^q h^{0,q}(Y)=0 .
\]
The categorical Euler invariant is also zero:
\[
        \kcat(Y)=\chi(\cO_{K3})\chi(\cO_E)=2\cdot0=0 .
\]
The Heisenberg--Mukai specialisation, the BKM denominator, and the K3
fibre rank are separate constructions:
\[
 \{\kcat(Y),\ \kch(Y),\ \kHeis(Y),\
   \kBKM(\fg_{\Delta_5}),\ \kfib(Y)\}
 =
 \{0,\ 0,\ 3,\ 5,\ 24\}.
\]
The value \(3\) is the rank-sum Heisenberg specialisation \(2+1\).
The value \(5\) is the Borcherds weight of \(\Delta_5\).
The value \(24\) is the Mukai-lattice rank
of the K3 fibre.  These five numbers belong to different
constructions; no sum identity relates them.

\subsection*{Placement in the CY-to-Chiral Construction}
\label{sec:frontier-cy-to-chiral-placement}

The dimension-three construction factors as
\[
        \Phi_3=\mathrm{Sp}_{\Sigma_2,C}\circ\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3 .
\]
The first stage is the native holomorphic factorisation algebra
\[
        \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\mathrm{Perf}(Y)).
\]
For \(d\geq3\), every specialised chiral output
\[
        \mathrm{Sp}_{\Sigma_2,C}
        \bigl(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3(\mathrm{Perf}(Y))\bigr)
\]
is \(E_1\)-chiral.  The \(E_2\)-structure lives on the centre, double,
or module category associated to this \(E_1\)-object.  Thus the
automorphic denominators in the table record Stage-2 specialisation
data.  The native \(\Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_3\) object is single-valued.

\subsection*{Boundary Orders \(N=5,7,8\)}
\label{sec:frontier-boundary-orders}

The orders \(N=5,7,8\) are meaningful as K3 twining and
Nikulin-order arithmetic.  They lie outside the primitive compact
\(K3\times E\) CHL denominator table above.  A denominator theorem at
one of these orders must specify
\[
        X_N,\quad L_N,\quad \rho_N,\quad
        \Delta_N^+,\quad \operatorname{mult}_N,\quad
        F^{(g_N,h_M)}_{L_N}.
\]
Without those data, the correct mathematical statement is conditional:
if the supplied root datum gives a Borcherds product with input
constant \(c_N(0)\), then its BKM weight is \(c_N(0)/2\).  The
existence of the automorphic product and its identification with a
reduced DT reciprocal square root are additional assertions.

\subsection*{Mathematical Conclusions}
\label{sec:frontier-conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The primitive CHL constants are
\[
        c_N(0)=(10,8,6,4,2),\qquad
        \kBKM(\Phi_N)=(5,4,3,2,1),
        \quad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
\item The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor catalogue has weights
\[
        (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1)
\]
and row constants
\[
        (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
\item The \(\Delta_5\) denominator normalisation is
\[
        D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad c_1(0)=10,\qquad
        \kBKM(\Delta_5)=5.
\]
The Igusa square convention is
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,
\]
and the OP scalar convention is
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2.
\]
\item For \(K3\times E\), the compact invariants and specialisation
invariants are
\[
        \kcat=0,\qquad \kch=0,\qquad
        \kHeis=3,\qquad \kBKM=5,\qquad \kfib=24.
\]
\item For \(d\geq3\), the specialised \(\Phi\)-output is \(E_1\)-chiral.
The \(E_2\)-structure belongs to the centre, double, or module layer.
\item The orders \(N=5,7,8\) are boundary Nikulin orders for the
denominator statement above.  A compact BKM-denominator theorem at
those orders requires a Calabi--Yau threefold host and explicit root
datum.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Sources}
\label{sec:frontier-sources}

The diagonal-divisor catalogue is Cl\'ery--Gritsenko's classification of
genus-two modular forms with simplest divisor.  The level-one
denominator identity is the Gritsenko--Nikulin construction of
\(\fg_{\Delta_5}\), with Borcherds' singular theta lift supplying the
weight formula \(c(0)/2\).  The K3 elliptic genus normalisation is the
Eichler--Zagier convention
\(\phi_{0,1}=r^{-1}+10+r+O(q)\).  The square normalisations are the
Igusa and Oberdieck--Pandharipande/Oberdieck--Pixton conventions
summarised by
\[
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_5^2,\qquad
        \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}=D_5^2,\qquad
        D_5=64^{-1}\Delta_5 .
\]
