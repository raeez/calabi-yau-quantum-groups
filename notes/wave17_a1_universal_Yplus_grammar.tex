\section*{Universal positive-Hall grammar
         $\Yplus_{G_X}(X)=H^{\mathrm{BM},*}_{G_X}(\Mbps(X),\varphi_{W})$}
\label{sec:universal-Yplus-grammar}

Let $X$ denote an oriented Calabi-Yau datum whose Kontsevich and
Soibelman critical Hall algebra is defined.  Its charge lattice is
$\Gamma(X)$, the chosen stability condition cuts a strict cone
$\Gamma^{+}(X)$, and
\[
  \Mbps(X)
  :=
  \coprod_{\gamma \in \Gamma^{+}(X)} \mathcal{M}_{\gamma}(X)
  \subset
  \Meff(X)
\]
is the positive effective moduli stack.  The local potential $W$ gives
the vanishing-cycle sheaf $\varphi_{W}$.  A coefficient datum
$G_X$ records the symmetry, quotient, monodromy, or reduced obstruction
theory that survives on the stratum containing $X$.  The positive Hall
half is
\[
  \Yplus_{G_X}(X)
  :=
  H^{\mathrm{BM}, *}_{G_X}
  \bigl(\Mbps(X), \varphi_{W}\bigr)
  =
  \bigoplus_{\gamma \in \Gamma^{+}(X)}
  H^{\mathrm{BM}, *}_{G_X}
  \bigl(\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}(X), \varphi_{W}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \Yplus_{G_X}(X)
  =
  H^{\mathrm{BM}, *}_{G_X}
  \bigl(\Meff(X), \varphi_{W}\bigr)\big|_{\Gamma^{+}(X)}.
\]
The product is the Hall correspondence
\[
  \mathcal{M}_{\gamma_{1}} \times \mathcal{M}_{\gamma_{2}}
  \xleftarrow{\;p\;}
  \mathcal{M}_{\gamma_{1} \subset \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}}
  \xrightarrow{\;q\;}
  \mathcal{M}_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}},
  \qquad
  a \star b = q_{*}p^{!}(a \boxtimes b).
\]
This is an associative $E_{1}$ Hall algebra.  At dimension $d \geq 3$,
the two-stage $\Phi_d$ output is also $E_{1}$; the $E_{2}$ object lives
on the Drinfeld centre of its representation category.

\subsection*{Coefficient strata}

The coefficient datum $G_X$ is part of the construction.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Smooth toric CY$_3$.}
For $X$ toric, the Calabi-Yau subtorus
\[
  T_{\mathrm{CY}}
  =
  \{(t_1,t_2,t_3)\in(\C^\times)^3: t_1t_2t_3=1\}
\]
acts on the critical Hall stack, and
$\Heq = H^{*}_{T_{\mathrm{CY}}}$.  Localisation reduces the Hall product
to fixed quiver data.  For $X=\C^3$, the torus-fixed points of
$\Hilb^{n}(\C^3)$ are plane partitions and the character is MacMahon.
Schiffmann and Vasserot 2013 identify
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)
  \cong
  Y^{+}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1})
\]
as the positive half of the affine Yangian.

\item \emph{Compact non-toric CY$_3$.}
For a compact non-toric threefold, $G_X$ is supplied by reduced
obstruction theory, quotient data, and actual automorphisms of the
geometry.  A $K3 \times E$ target uses the reduced $K3$ obstruction
theory together with the quotient by translations of the elliptic curve
$E$.  A $\C^\times$ parameter requires a genuine $\C^\times$-action.

\item \emph{Finite symplectic quotient.}
For $X=[Y/G]$ with $G$ finite symplectic, the coefficient datum is the
finite-equivariant one, and the Hall stack decomposes over the fixed
strata of $Y$.  In the K3 case, Nikulin and Mukai place the finite
symplectic groups inside the Mathieu-lattice picture; the
Davison and Meinhardt 2020 BPS Lie algebra descends through the orbifold
cotangent complex after orientation data are fixed.

\item \emph{Lattice-polarised boundary.}
For a lattice-polarised K3 or $K3 \times E$ boundary, $G_X$ is the
monodromy group preserving the polarising lattice.  The natural period
domain is orthogonal for the K3 lattice; its Siegel or paramodular
realisation appears after the genus-two comparison.  When the
Borcherds and Gritsenko denominator theorem supplies an automorphic
product, the Hall character is read as a Borcherds character and
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi)=\frac{c(0)}{2}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The correspondence product is unchanged across the four regimes; the
coefficient ring $H^{*}_{G_X}(\mathrm{pt})$ and the quotient stack are
the moving pieces.

\subsection*{\texorpdfstring{$K3 \times E$}{K3 x E} reduced boundary}

Let $S$ be a K3 surface and let $E$ be an elliptic curve.  In a
primitive fibre class, Oberdieck and Pandharipande 2016 prove the
reduced DT/GW identity
\[
  Z^{S\times E,\,\red,\,\mathrm{prim}}_{\mathrm{DT}}(\Omega)
  =
  -\,\frac{C}{\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{OP}}(\Omega)},
  \qquad C=pqt,
\]
with the Weyl-vector prefactor absorbed in $C$.  The positive Hall
object on this boundary is the reduced, elliptic-quotient critical
cohomology
\[
  H^{+}_{S\times E}
  :=
  H^{\mathrm{BM}, *}_{\red/E}
  \bigl(\mathcal{M}^{\red,\bps}(S\times E)_{\mathrm{prim}},
        \varphi_{W}\bigr).
\]
Its numerical character is the reciprocal Igusa form above.  The
scalar square-root relation
\[
  \Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}}=\Delta_{5}^{2}
\]
separates the two Borcherds weights:
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_{5})=5,
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Phi_{10}^{\mathrm{un}})=10.
\]
The four $K3 \times E$ invariants are construction-distinct:
\[
  \kcat(S\times E)
  =
  \chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{E})
  =
  2\cdot 0
  =
  0,
  \qquad
  \kchir^{\mathrm{Heis}}=3,
  \qquad
  \kBKM(\Delta_{5})=5,
  \qquad
  \kfib=24.
\]
The total-space Hodge supertrace is
\[
  \sum_{q}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(S\times E)=0.
\]
The value $2$ belongs to the K3 fibre invariant
$\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})$; the categorical invariant of the total space
is $0$.  The value $3$ belongs to the Heisenberg-Mukai specialisation.
The value $5$ belongs to the Borcherds product $\Delta_5$.

\subsection*{Double, centre, and evaluation}

A double is available after the positive half carries a non-degenerate
Hopf pairing compatible with the chosen coefficient datum:
\[
  \langle -, - \rangle:
  \Yplus_{G_X}(X)\otimes \Yminus_{G_X}(X)
  \longrightarrow
  \Yzero_{G_X}(X).
\]
Then
\[
  D\bigl(\Yplus_{G_X}(X)\bigr)
  =
  \Yplus_{G_X}(X)
  \bowtie
  \Yzero_{G_X}(X)
  \bowtie
  \Yminus_{G_X}(X).
\]
For $\C^3$, Schiffmann and Vasserot 2013 give the positive half, and the
Drinfeld double gives the full affine Yangian:
\[
  \CoHA(\C^3)
  \cong
  Y^{+}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1}),
  \qquad
  D\bigl(Y^{+}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1})\bigr)
  \cong
  Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1}).
\]
By Prochazka and Rapcak 2018, the vertex algebra
$\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}[\lambda]$ is reached through
the centred or evaluated full Yangian,
\[
  Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{1})
  \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}\;}
  \operatorname{End}
  \bigl(\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}[\lambda]\text{-}\mathrm{vac}\bigr).
\]
The positive Hall algebra supplies the positive modes.  Cartan,
negative modes, completion, and the pairing supply the full object.

For $K3 \times E$, the compact double theorem has the form
\[
  H^{+}_{K3\times E}
  \longrightarrow
  U\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})\bigr).
\]
If the Hall pairing, Lorentzian Cartan, automorphic completion, and
radical quotient satisfy the Hall-Borcherds recognition gates, the
reduced double is the completed Borcherds algebra:
\[
  D^{\red}\bigl(H^{+}_{K3\times E}\bigr)
  \simeq
  \widehat{U}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}\bigr).
\]
The denominator character is fixed by
\[
  \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})
  =
  64^{-1}\Delta_5(2Z)
  =
  e^{-2\pi i(\rho,z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}
  \bigl(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,z)}\bigr)^{\operatorname{smult}(\alpha)}.
\]
The bracket-level identification is the remaining mathematical content
of the compact Hall-Borcherds theorem; the character identity alone
leaves it as an extra theorem.

\subsection*{Compatibility with \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_d$}{Phi-d}}

For a specialisation datum $(\Sigma_{d-1},C)$, the
Calabi-Yau-to-chiral construction is
\[
  \Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}
  =
  \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ \Phi^{\mathrm{FA}}_d
  :
  \mathrm{CY}_{d}\text{-}\mathrm{Cat}
  \longrightarrow
  E_{d}\text{-}\mathrm{HolFA}(X)
  \longrightarrow
  E_{n(d)}\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(C),
\]
where
\[
  n(d)=
  \begin{cases}
    \infty, & d=1,\\
    2, & d=2,\\
    1, & d\geq 3.
  \end{cases}
\]
The Hall grammar is a separate positive-geometry construction attached
to the same CY input.  A comparison map
\[
  \rho_X^{+}:
  \Yplus_{G_X}(X)
  \dashrightarrow
  \Gamma_{\mathrm{Hall}}\!
  \bigl(\Phi_d^{(\Sigma_{d-1},C)}(X)\bigr)
\]
requires the relevant hCS-Hall, reduced-DT, or localisation witnesses.
At $d=3$, the target
$\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(X)$ is $E_{1}$-chiral.  The braided
$E_{2}$ structure belongs to
\[
  \mathcal{Z}
  \bigl(\mathrm{Rep}^{E_1}
  (\Phi_3^{(\Sigma_2,C)}(X))\bigr),
\]
or to an equivalent doubled or centred construction.

On compact even-dimensional constructed loci, the scalar
\[
  \kchir(A_X)=\sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)
\]
is the Hodge-supertrace invariant of the chiral output.  At odd
dimension the compact Hodge supertrace cancels by Serre duality; the
nonzero $d=3$ values in the CY landscape come from chain-level
noncompact or specialised computations, such as local $\mathbb{P}^{2}$,
the conifold, and the Heisenberg-Mukai specialisation of $K3\times E$.
