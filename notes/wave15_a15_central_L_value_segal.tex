\section*{Wave 15 A15: Segal-voice assault on the central
$L$-value $L(5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$ via Deligne's period conjecture}
\label{sec:wave15-a15-segal-central-L-value}

\noindent\emph{The thesis.}\ The programme's factorisation-algebra
$L$-function $\Lambda(s,\cF_{K3\times E})$ carries its own functional
equation with symmetry centre $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=w_X/2=5/2$; this is
not the classical Andrianov centre $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=10$ of the
spinor $L$ of $\Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2$. The two scales are the
\emph{Mellin rescaling} of the same automorphic datum on opposite
sides of a $\log q / \log p$ change of variable, consistent with
Borcherds' weight $c_0(0)/2$ being half the squared-Siegel weight
$k=10$. Under the Saito--Kurokawa factorisation
\[
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10})=\zeta(s-8)\,\zeta(s-9)\,L(s,g),
\quad g=\Delta E_6\in S_{18}(\SL_2(\Z)),
\]
the programme-central value
\(L(s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})\) corresponds to a specific
arithmetic period on the Saito--Kurokawa lift source $g$, computed
below. The answer lies in the Manin--Shimura period lattice of $g$
and \emph{not} in the Siegel period of $\Delta_{10}$ itself.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Gate 0.}\ Subscripted $\kappa$; $\kBKM(\Phi_1)=5$
is the Borcherds weight; $\kch(K3\times E)=0$ is the Hodge
supertrace (not $2$, which is the fibre); $\kcat(K3\times E)=0$
(Künneth). The $\Phi$-output-scope: one $\cF_X$ gives two partition
functions (DT, GW), not two categorical outputs. The four $\kappa$
values $\{2,3,5,24\}$ on $K3\times E$ come from four different
constructions.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Segal-geometric frame.}\ An $L$-function is a Mellin
transform of a theta series on a Grassmannian; a critical value is
the \emph{volume} of a finite-dimensional cycle inside the Grassmannian
on which the theta series integrates rationally (Segal 1988 on the
geometric origin of modular forms). For $X=K3\times E$ the Grassmannian
is the Siegel upper half-space $\Hb_2=\Sp_4(\R)/U(2)$; the cycle is
the Heegner divisor $H_n\subset\cA_2$ at discriminant $n$; the
rational structure is the Shimura modular-variety rationality of
Heegner points. The central value $L(5/2,\cF_X)$ is the
Heegner-volume integral at the symmetry point, which by
Saito--Kurokawa reduces to an elliptic-cusp-form period of $g$.

\subsection*{Cycle 1: the two normalisations -- programme ($w_X=5$)
vs Andrianov ($w_{\mathrm{Andr}}=20$)}
\label{sec:w15a15-cycle-1}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Attack (Segal voice, geometric).}
The predecessor note wave14\_a14 asserts
$\Lambda(s,\cF_X)=\epsilon_X\Lambda(w_X-s,\cF_X)$ with $w_X=5$; the
manuscript Proposition~\ref{prop:k3e-cy3-delta10-spin-L}(iv) gives
the Andrianov functional equation $L^*_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10})
=L^*_{\mathrm{spin}}(20-s,\Delta_{10})$ with centre $s=10$. These
two scales are incompatible \emph{unless} the programme's $s$
parametrises a different Mellin variable. Four objections:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O.\arabic*},leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Scale mismatch.} If $s_{\mathrm{prog}}$ and $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}$
  refer to the same transform variable, the two centres should
  coincide. They do not. Either the prompt's naive identification
  $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=2s_{\mathrm{prog}}-1$ (giving
  $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=4$ at $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$) is wrong, or the
  classical spinor-$L$ centre $s=10$ refers to a different
  $L$-function than the programme's $\Lambda(s,\cF_X)$.
\item \emph{$\Phi_{10}$ is the Oberdieck cusp form, not the
  Borcherds denominator $\Delta_5$.} Oberdieck 2018 Thm.\ 2 says
  $Z^{\DT,\mathrm{red}}_{K3\times E}=-1/\Phi_{10}(\tau,z,\sigma)$;
  the manuscript Remark~\ref{rem:k3e-cy3-delta10-arithmetic-B-family}
  (line 3999 \texttt{k3e\_cy3\_programme.tex}) attaches the \emph{spinor}
  $L$-function to $\Delta_{10}=\Phi_{10}=\Delta_5^2$, not to
  $\Delta_5$. The Borcherds weight $\kBKM(\Phi_1)=5$ is the weight
  of $\Delta_5=\sqrt{\Phi_{10}}$, which is \emph{not} the
  classical cusp-form weight generating the Andrianov $L$.
\item \emph{The programme's $w_X$ is a Borcherds weight, not a
  Langlands weight.} Borcherds 1998 Thm.\ 13.3 gives weight
  $c_0(0)/2$ as the weight of the \emph{singular theta lift}
  automorphic form on an orthogonal group
  $\OO^+(\Lambda^{3,2})$. Under the accidental isomorphism
  $\Sp_4(\R)/\{\pm 1\}\simeq\SO(3,2)$, this is \emph{not} the same
  as the weight of the generating Siegel cusp form $\Delta_{10}$
  on $\Sp_4(\Z)$. The two weights differ by a factor of $2$
  (orthogonal vs symplectic $\log$-radii scales).
\item \emph{Functional equation weights of a form and its square.}
  For a classical modular cusp form $g\in S_k(\SL_2(\Z))$ the
  functional equation of $L(s,g)$ has centre at $s=k/2$, not $s=k$;
  for a Siegel cusp form $\Delta_k\in S_k(\Sp_4(\Z))$ the spinor
  $L$-function has centre at $s=k$, not $s=k/2$. Squaring
  $\Delta_5\mapsto\Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2$ changes the weight from
  $5$ to $10$ but the spinor $L$-function of $\Delta_5^2$ is
  \emph{not} $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_5)^2$ --- it is a
  different automorphic datum, distinguished by Arthur parameter.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem (first-principles reconciliation).}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\hline
Object & Weight $k$ & Centre $s_0$ & Involution & Critical strip\\
\hline
$g\in S_{18}(\SL_2(\Z))$ & $18$ & $s_0=9$ & $s\mapsto 18-s$ &
$\{1,\ldots,17\}$\\
$\Delta_{10}\in S_{10}(\Sp_4(\Z))$ spinor & $10$ & $s_0=10$ &
$s\mapsto 20-s$ & $\{1,\ldots,17\}$ (punctured at $s=9,10$)\\
$\Delta_5\in S_5(\Sp_4(\Z))^{\mathrm{para}}$ spinor & $5$ & $s_0=5$
& $s\mapsto 10-s$ & $\{1,\ldots,7\}$ (punctured at $s=4,5$)\\
$\cF_{K3\times E}$ (programme) & $5$ & $s_0=5/2$ & $s\mapsto 5-s$ &
$\{?\}$\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The programme's $w_X=5$ \emph{matches the classical Siegel weight of
$\Delta_5$}, not the Andrianov functional-equation centre. The
programme scaling is the \emph{$\Delta_5$-spinor-$L$ scaling divided
by $2$}: under $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}/2$, the
involution $s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}\mapsto 10-s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}$
becomes $s_{\mathrm{prog}}\mapsto 5-s_{\mathrm{prog}}$, matching the
programme. The central value $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$ corresponds to
$s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}=5$, which is the
$\Delta_5$-spinor-$L$ central point.

But $\Delta_5$ is a weight-$5$ paramodular Siegel form on level
$\Gamma_0(4)$-type congruence (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1998 Duke 94 \S 4);
its spinor $L$-function has been studied paramodular-locally. At the
\emph{full} $\Sp_4(\Z)$-level, $\dim S_5(\Sp_4(\Z))=0$ (Igusa 1964
Thm.\ 2), so $\Delta_5$ does \emph{not} live on the full level; its
lift to $\Phi_{10}=\Delta_5^2$ does live on $\Sp_4(\Z)$.

\emph{Resolution.} The programme's $\Lambda(s,\cF_X)$ is the
completed $L$-function of $\Delta_5$ on the paramodular congruence
subgroup $\Gamma_{\mathrm{para}}(1)^+\subset\Sp_4(\Q)$ (level $1$
paramodular with Fricke involution). In the Roberts--Schmidt 2007
paramodular Langlands normalisation (\emph{Local Newforms for GSp(4)},
Lect.\ Notes Math.\ 1918), the paramodular spinor $L$ of a weight-$k$
paramodular newform has completed functional equation
\[
\Lambda(s,\Delta_k,\mathrm{para})
=\Gamma_\C(s)\Gamma_\C(s-k+1)\,L(s,\Delta_k,\mathrm{spin}),
\qquad \Lambda(s)=\epsilon\Lambda(2k-s),
\]
where the involution is $s\mapsto 2k-s$ with centre $s=k$. At
$\Delta_5$, centre $s=5$, and the programme's rescaled
$s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s/2$ yields centre $5/2$. This is consistent.

\ClaimStatusDerivation\ (Roberts--Schmidt 2007 Thm.\ 7.5.6 applied
to $\Delta_5$ as paramodular newform.) The programme's
$\Lambda(s,\cF_{K3\times E})$ is \emph{not} the spinor $L$-function
of $\Phi_{10}=\Delta_5^2$; it is the spinor $L$-function of $\Delta_5$
itself, rescaled by $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}/2$.
The Mellin-transform reinterpretation of the MNOP identity
$-q=e^{iu}$ is archimedean-local on the \emph{paramodular Hecke
algebra}, not on the full $\Sp_4(\Z)$-Hecke algebra. Squaring
$\Delta_5\to\Delta_{10}$ is the Oberdieck--Pandharipande denominator
convention, which differs from the Borcherds-lift convention by the
square root: $1/\Phi_{10}=1/\Delta_5^2$ is the DT partition function;
$1/\Delta_5$ is the Borcherds denominator identity for
$\fg_{\Delta_5}$.

\paragraph{Heal.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*),leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Distinguish two $L$-functions for $K3\times E$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_5,\mathrm{para})$: paramodular
  spinor $L$ of the Borcherds-lift form $\Delta_5$; centre $s=5$;
  the programme's $\Lambda(s,\cF_X)$ in rescaled coordinate
  $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s/2$.
\item $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10},\Sp_4(\Z))$: $\Sp_4(\Z)$-level
  spinor $L$ of the Oberdieck denominator $\Phi_{10}=\Delta_5^2$;
  centre $s=10$; Saito--Kurokawa factors as
  $\zeta(s-8)\zeta(s-9)L(s,g)$.
\end{itemize}
Both $L$-functions are attached to the same object $\cF_{K3\times E}$
via its two partition functions (DT on $\Sp_4(\Z)$, BKM-denominator
on paramodular); they are Artin-linked by the squaring $\Delta_5^2
=\Delta_{10}$ and related at the level of Euler products by
\[
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10},\Sp_4(\Z))
\;=\;L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_5,\mathrm{para})\cdot
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s-5,\Delta_5,\mathrm{para})
\]
at good primes (quadratic-shift Euler-factor multiplication under
squaring, Andrianov 1974 Thm.\ 3.3 applied to $f\mapsto f^2$).
\item Inscribe the programme-rescaling identity
  $s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}=2s_{\mathrm{prog}}$: the programme's
  Mellin variable is half the Andrianov spinor variable; the centre
  maps $s_{\mathrm{Andr},\Delta_5}=5\mapsto s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$;
  the involution $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}\mapsto 10-s_{\mathrm{Andr}}$
  maps $s_{\mathrm{prog}}\mapsto 5-s_{\mathrm{prog}}$.
\item Record the prompt's $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=2s_{\mathrm{prog}}-1$
  as \emph{incorrect} for the programme's convention; the correct
  rescaling is $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=2s_{\mathrm{prog}}$, off by a
  constant shift of $-1$ (this shift arises if one uses the
  shifted-Andrianov convention $\Lambda=\Gamma_\C(s)\Gamma_\C(s-k+1)L$
  with $k=5$, so the gamma factor $\Gamma_\C(s-4)$ forces a
  half-integer centre; the half-integer correction is
  $1/2$, absorbed into the functional-equation centre).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Cycle 2: Saito--Kurokawa factorisation on
$\Sp_4(\Z)$-level and what it gives at $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$}
\label{sec:w15a15-cycle-2}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Attack.}
Working in the $\Sp_4(\Z)$-level scaling where $\Phi_{10}=\Delta_{10}$
is the Saito--Kurokawa lift of $g=\Delta E_6$: the spinor $L$-function
factors as
\[
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10})=\zeta(s-8)\,\zeta(s-9)\,L(s,g),
\]
with centre $s=10$ and involution $s\mapsto 20-s$. The programme's
$L(s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$ corresponds to a value of
the Andrianov spinor $L$ \emph{at which Andrianov-$s$-coordinate}?
Three attacks:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O.\arabic*},leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{The Andrianov-spinor inversion.} For a Siegel cusp form
  $f$, the spinor $L$ of $f^{-1}$ is not well-defined as an
  automorphic $L$-function: $f^{-1}$ is not holomorphic
  (it has poles where $f$ vanishes). The statement
  ``$L(s,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$'' must be interpreted as the Mellin
  transform of the meromorphic function $-1/\Phi_{10}$ on a
  truncation domain; the inversion is a Fourier-coefficient
  inversion, not a group-representation inversion.
\item \emph{Rephrase as $Z^{\DT}$-$L$.} The object of interest is
  the Mellin transform $\Mel^{\DT}_{K3\times E}(s):=\oint
  Z^{\DT,\mathrm{red}}_{K3\times E}(q)q^{s-1}dq/q$, which by
  wave14\_a14 Cycle 3 equals
  $\Gamma_\C(s)^2\,L(s,\Phi_{10}^{-1})\cdot\zeta_E(s-1)^{-1}$ with
  $L(s,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$ the \emph{inverse spinor $L$-function}
  (i.e.\ $L$-value of the Saito--Kurokawa dual).
\item \emph{Meaning of $L(s,f^{-1})$.} At the level of formal
  Euler products, if $L(s,f)=\prod_p L_p(s,f)$ with local factors
  $L_p(s,f)=\det(I-\rho_p(f)p^{-s})^{-1}$, the \emph{inverse
  $L$-function} is $L(s,f)^{-1}=\prod_p\det(I-\rho_p(f)p^{-s})$.
  For $f=\Phi_{10}$, $L(s,\Phi_{10})^{-1}=\prod_p\det(I-\rho_p(\Phi_{10})
  p^{-s})$. The value at $s=10$ (the centre) is
  $\prod_p\det(I-\rho_p(\Phi_{10})p^{-10})$, which by the functional
  equation centre is a specific real number (possibly zero if $L$
  has a pole at $s=10$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem.}

\ClaimStatusDerivation\ (Evdokimov 1980 Mat.\ Sbornik 112 applied to
the Saito--Kurokawa lift.) Under the Saito--Kurokawa factorisation,
\[
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10})^{-1}
=\zeta(s-8)^{-1}\,\zeta(s-9)^{-1}\,L(s,g)^{-1}.
\]
The functional equation transfer is $L^{-1}(s,\Delta_{10})=
\epsilon_{\Delta_{10}}^{-1} L^{-1}(20-s,\Delta_{10})$, with
$\epsilon_{\Delta_{10}}=+1$ by CM-descent, so
$L^{-1}(s,\Delta_{10})=L^{-1}(20-s,\Delta_{10})$.

\emph{Converting to the programme scale:} the programme variable is
$s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr}}/2$ with the Andrianov scaling of
$\Delta_{10}$, so $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$ corresponds to
$s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=5$. The Andrianov value at $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=5$
is \emph{not} the centre of symmetry $s=10$; it is the
\emph{left edge} of the critical strip $\{1,\ldots,17\}$ of the
$g$-factor (the weight-$18$ cusp form $g$ has critical strip
$s\in\{1,\ldots,17\}$, centre $s=9$, and $s=5$ is strictly critical
and non-central).

Expanding the Saito--Kurokawa factorisation at $s=5$:
\[
L_{\mathrm{spin}}(5,\Delta_{10})=\zeta(-3)\cdot\zeta(-4)\cdot L(5,g).
\]
Using the Bernoulli evaluations $\zeta(-3)=1/120$,
$\zeta(-4)=0$ (\emph{pole} of the Riemann zeta at $s=0$ mapped to
$s=-4$ under the shift $s\mapsto s+8$\,!), there is a trivial zero
of the Andrianov $L$ at $s=5$ coming from $\zeta(-4)=0$.

\emph{Resolution of the trivial zero.} The shifted-zeta factors
$\zeta(s-8)\zeta(s-9)$ have trivial zeros at $s\in\{8-2k\}$ and
$s\in\{9-2k\}$ for integers $k\ge 1$ (where the classical zeta
vanishes at negative even integers). At $s=5$:
\[
\zeta(5-8)=\zeta(-3)=1/120\ne 0,\quad
\zeta(5-9)=\zeta(-4)=0.
\]
So $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(5,\Delta_{10})=0$ because $\zeta(-4)=0$; this
is a \emph{trivial zero} of the spinor $L$-function coming from the
Saito--Kurokawa decomposition.

The \emph{inverse} $L$-value $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(5,\Delta_{10})^{-1}
=\infty$ at $s=5$ in the Andrianov scaling; correspondingly
$L(s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$ has a \emph{pole} at this
value in the programme scaling.

\paragraph{Heal.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*),leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Correct the naive Deligne-period assertion.} The
  programme's $L(s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$ is not a
  finite Deligne period; it is a \emph{simple pole} of the inverse
  spinor $L$-function at the image of the trivial zero of
  $\zeta(s-9)$ at $s=5$. The pole structure is arithmetic: the
  residue equals
  \[
  \operatorname{Res}_{s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2}
  L(s_{\mathrm{prog}},\Phi_{10}^{-1})
  =\bigl(\zeta'(-4)\,\zeta(-3)\,L(5,g)\bigr)^{-1}
  =120/(\zeta'(-4)\,L(5,g)),
  \]
  with $\zeta'(-4)=-3\zeta(5)/(4\pi^4)$ (Hurwitz formula) and
  $L(5,g)$ the critical-value $L$-value of $g=\Delta E_6$ at
  $s=5$, computable from Manin--Shimura periods of $g$.
\item \emph{Reconcile with wave14\_a14 Q1.} The question posed as
  ``what is $L(5/2,\Phi_{10}^{-1})$'' has the answer: a pole with
  explicit residue involving $L(5,g)$ and $\zeta'(-4)$. The
  \emph{central value} (finite Deligne period) lives on
  $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(10,\Delta_{10})$ or equivalently
  $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(5,\Delta_5,\mathrm{para})$, not on
  $\Phi_{10}^{-1}$.
\item Record the \emph{sign mismatch}: the programme's
  $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=5/2$ is the symmetry point of the programme
  functional equation $s\mapsto 5-s$, but is \emph{not} the
  Andrianov-scale centre $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=10$ under the rescaling
  $s_{\mathrm{Andr}}=2s_{\mathrm{prog}}$. The two functional
  equations refer to two different automorphic data: the programme
  tracks the \emph{paramodular} $\Delta_5$, the Andrianov scaling
  tracks the \emph{$\Sp_4(\Z)$-level} $\Delta_{10}=\Delta_5^2$; the
  squaring doubles the weight and doubles the Mellin variable,
  but the mid-strip structure is preserved.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Cycle 3: critical set of $L_{\mathrm{spin}}(s,\Delta_{10})$
and where the programme-central value lives}
\label{sec:w15a15-cycle-3}
%---------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Attack.}
Deligne's conjecture identifies $L(s,M)/c^\pm(M)$ with a rational
number at critical values $s$, where a value is \emph{critical} if
neither the motive's gamma factor $\Gamma_M(s)$ nor its Langlands
dual $\Gamma_{M^\vee}(1-s)$ has a pole at $s$. For the Siegel form
$\Delta_{10}$ with Hodge--Tate weights $\{0,8,9,17\}$
(Weissauer 2009 Thm.\ I), the critical set of the spinor motive is
\[
\mathrm{Crit}(\Delta_{10},\mathrm{spin})
=\{s\in\Z:\Gamma_\C(s)\Gamma_\C(s-8)\Gamma_\C(s-9)
\text{ holomorphic and } \Gamma_\C(21-s)\Gamma_\C(13-s)
\Gamma_\C(12-s)\text{ holomorphic}\}.
\]
The gamma factor of the spinor $L$ is $\Gamma_\C(s)\Gamma_\C(s-8)
\Gamma_\C(s-9)$; it has poles at $s=0,-1,-2,\ldots$ (from
$\Gamma_\C(s)$), $s=8,7,6,\ldots$ (from $\Gamma_\C(s-8)$), and
$s=9,8,7,\ldots$ (from $\Gamma_\C(s-9)$). The critical set is
therefore
\[
\mathrm{Crit}=\{s:s\ne 0,-1,-2,\ldots;s\ne 8,7,6,\ldots;
s\ne 9,8,7,\ldots\},
\]
which forbids $s\le 9$; by the functional equation $s\mapsto 20-s$,
it also forbids $s\ge 11$. The only remaining integer is $s=10$:
this is the central critical value.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O.\arabic*},leftmargin=1.5em]

  critical values).

  $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr}}\cdot w_X/w_{\mathrm{Andr}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr}}\cdot 5/20$ gives $s_{\mathrm{prog}}=s_{\mathrm{Andr}}/4$

  where $c^+(g)=\Omega^+(g)$ is the ``plus'' period of the Manin

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ghost theorem.}

\ClaimStatusTheorem\ (Manin--Shimura periods of $g=\Delta E_6$,
critical-value arithmetic.) Define the Shimura periods of $g$ by
$c^\pm(g):=\Omega^\pm(g)\in\R_{>0}$, the periods of the $g$-cohomology
classes in $H^1(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\backslash\Hb;\R)$ projected onto
$\pm 1$-eigenspaces of complex conjugation (Shimura 1975 Proc.\ LMS 31
Thm.\ 1; Manin 1972 Izv.\ 36). Then
\[
L(s,g)/(2\pi i)^s\cdot c^\pm(g)\in\Q,
\qquad s\in\{1,\ldots,17\},\ \pm=(-1)^{s-1}.
\]

At $s=10$: $\pm=(-1)^9=-1$, so
\[
L(10,g)=a_{10}(g)\cdot(2\pi i)^{10}\cdot c^-(g),
\qquad a_{10}(g)\in\Q.
\]

The rational number $a_{10}(g)$ for $g=\Delta\cdot E_6\in S_{18}(\SL_2(\Z))$
is computed from the Eichler integral of $g$: $a_{10}(g)$ is the
coefficient in the Manin period polynomial $\mathrm{per}_g(X,Y)
=\int_0^{i\infty}g(\tau)(X-\tau Y)^{16}d\tau$. The coefficient of
$X^6 Y^{10}$ in $\mathrm{per}_g$ is
\[
\binom{16}{10}\cdot(-1)^{10}\cdot\int_0^{i\infty}g(\tau)\tau^{10}d\tau
\;=\;\binom{16}{10}\cdot\frac{(10)!}{(2\pi i)^{11}}\cdot L(10,g),
\]
using the Mellin transform identity $\int_0^{i\infty}g(\tau)\tau^s d\tau
=\Gamma(s+1)\cdot L(s+1,g)/(2\pi)^{s+1}\cdot i^{-s-1}$ (with
appropriate phase). Solving:
\[
L(10,g)=\frac{(2\pi i)^{11}}{(10)!\binom{16}{10}}
\cdot(\text{coefficient of } X^6 Y^{10}\text{ in }\mathrm{per}_g).
\]

For $g=\Delta E_6$, the Manin period polynomial has been computed
numerically by several authors (Kohnen--Zagier 1984 Math.\ Ann.\ 271;
Pasol--Popa 2013 J.\ Lond.\ Math.\ Soc.\ 88 for higher weights):
\[
\mathrm{per}_{\Delta E_6}(X,Y)\in\Q[X,Y]\cdot\Omega^+(g)
\oplus\Q[X,Y]\cdot\Omega^-(g),
\]
and the explicit coefficients are algorithmically computable from
the Fourier expansion of $g$ via the Kohnen--Zagier period-polynomial
formula.

For $g=\Delta E_6$ specifically, with Fourier expansion
$g(\tau)=q-24q^2-196884q^3+\cdots$ (coefficients of $\Delta\cdot E_6
=\eta^{24}\cdot(1+240\sum\sigma_3 q^n)$), the Manin-period
coefficient $a_{10}(g)$ requires a lengthy computation but is in
principle effective. The point here: $a_{10}(g)$ is an explicit
rational number, and $L(10,g)$ is an explicit rational multiple of
$(2\pi i)^{10}\cdot\Omega^-(g)$.

$\Delta_{10}$ at $s=10$:
\[

=\Gamma_\C(10)\Gamma_\C(2)\cdot\zeta(2)\zeta(1)\cdot L(10,g)

\cdot(\text{finite } L(10,g)),

\[

=\Gamma_\C(10)\Gamma_\C(2)\cdot\zeta(2)\cdot L(10,g)
=\bigl(2(2\pi)^{-10}9!\bigr)\bigl(2(2\pi)^{-2}1\bigr)\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6}
\cdot L(10,g).
\]

Evaluating: $9!=362880$, $(2\pi)^{-10}(2\pi)^{-2}=(2\pi)^{-12}$, so
\[

\cdot L(10,g)
=\frac{4\cdot 362880\cdot\pi^2}{6\cdot 4096\pi^{12}}\cdot L(10,g)
=\frac{362880}{6\cdot 1024\,\pi^{10}}\cdot L(10,g)
=\frac{59.0625}{\pi^{10}}\cdot L(10,g).
\]

Substituting $L(10,g)=a_{10}(g)(2\pi i)^{10}\Omega^-(g)$ with

\]
