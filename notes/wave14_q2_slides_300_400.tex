\section*{Slides 300--400: the two reflection groups $W_I^{(2)}, W_{II}^{(2)}$}

The segment is the Gritsenko--Nikulin reflection-group calculation, transcribed in
presentation form. The mathematical objects are the hyperbolic lattice
$\Lat^{2,1} = \Lat^{1,1} \oplus [2] = \Z f_2 \oplus \Z f_3 \oplus \Z f_{-2}$,
its cone $\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+$, and the two normal reflection subgroups
$W^{(2)}(\Lat_I^{2,1}) \lhd W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1}) \lhd W^{(2)}(\Lat^{2,1})$
generated by square-two primitive roots of Type I and Type II.

\subsection*{1. Hyperbolic lattice and cone}
The embedding $O(\Lat^{2,1}) \hookrightarrow O(\Lat^{3,2})$ extends $g$ by the
identity on $(\Lat^{2,1})^\perp$; automorphy of $\Dfive$ with respect to
$O(\Lat^{2,1})$ is the question the lemmas answer.
The cone $\cC(\Lat^{2,1}) = \{x \in \Lat^{2,1}\otimes\R \mid (x,x) < 0\}$
has two half-cones; $\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+$ is singled out by compatibility with the
Type IV tube domain $\Omega(\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+) = \Lat^{2,1}\otimes\R +
i\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+ \subset \fhp$. The fixing subgroup $O(\Lat^{2,1})_+ \cong
\mathrm{PGL}_2(\Z)$ acts discretely; a fundamental domain of
$\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+/\R_{>0}$ has finite volume.

\subsection*{2. Reflections and the two types}
For $\alpha \in \Lat^{2,1}$ with $(\alpha,\alpha) > 0$ and
$(\alpha,\alpha) \mid 2(\Lat^{2,1}, \alpha)$, the Weyl reflection
$s_\alpha : x \mapsto x - \tfrac{2(x,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha$
fixes $\alpha^\perp$ and negates $\alpha$; for square-two $\alpha$ the formula
collapses to $s_\alpha(x) = x - (x,\alpha)\alpha$.

Write $\Delta^{(k)}(\Lat^{2,1}) = \{\delta \in \Lat^{2,1} \mid
(\delta,\delta) = k,\ \delta\ \text{primitive}\}$ and $W^{(k)}$ for the group
generated by $\{s_\delta\}_{\delta\in\Delta^{(k)}}$. \emph{Lemma.}
$W(\Lat^{2,1}) = W^{(2)}$, and each square-two primitive reflection is
exactly one of two types:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Type I: $(\delta, \Lat^{2,1}) = \Z$,
  \item Type II: $(\delta, \Lat^{2,1}) = 2\Z$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3. Sublattices, normal subgroups, indices}
The Type-I and Type-II sublattices
\[
  \Lat_I^{2,1} = \{m f_2 + \ell f_3 + n f_{-2} \mid m+\ell+n \equiv 0\,(2)\},
  \quad
  \Lat_{II}^{2,1} = \{m f_2 + \ell f_3 + n f_{-2} \mid m \equiv n \equiv 0\,(2)\}
\]
define normal subgroups $W^{(2)}(\Lat_I^{2,1}), W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1})$
of index $2$ and $6$ in $W^{(2)}(\Lat^{2,1})$. The three fundamental
polyhedra $\cP, \cP_I, \cP_{II}$ are cut out by square-two orthogonal roots:
\[
  \cP'_{\mathrm{prim}} = \text{square-2 primitive roots orthogonal to }\cP',
  \qquad
  \cP' = \bigcap_{\delta \in \cP'_{\mathrm{prim}}} \cH^+_\delta.
\]
The explicit sets are
\begin{align*}
  \cP_{\mathrm{prim}} &= \{f_2 - f_3,\ f_{-2} - f_2,\ f_3\},
  & A(\cP) &= \langle 1\rangle, \\
  \cP_{I,\mathrm{prim}} &= \{f_2 - f_3,\ f_{-2} - f_2,\ f_2 + f_3\},
  & A(\cP_I) &= \langle s_{f_3}\rangle \cong S_2, \\
  \cP_{II,\mathrm{prim}} &= \{\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3,\ \delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3,\ \delta_3 = f_3\},
  & A(\cP_{II}) &= \langle s_{f_2 - f_3}, s_{f_{-2} - f_2}\rangle \cong S_3.
\end{align*}
The master decomposition
\[
  O(\Lat^{2,1})_+ \cong W^{(2)}(\Lat^{2,1})
  \cong W^{(2)}(\Lat_I^{2,1}) \rtimes A(\cP_I)
  \cong W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1}) \rtimes A(\cP_{II})
\]
matches $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\Z) \cong W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1}) \rtimes S_3$.

\subsection*{4. Maass sign and the $\Dfive$ dichotomy}
The concrete sign computation on slides 298--320 is the heart of the
segment. For the three $\cP_{II}$ roots $\{\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3\}$
the Borcherds lift reads $\Dfive(s_\alpha z) = -\Dfive(z)$ (Type II,
anti-invariant). For $\alpha \in \{f_{-2}-f_2,\ f_2-f_3,\ f_2+f_3\}$
(Type I) one finds $\Dfive(s_\alpha z) = \Dfive(z)$. The proof uses the
block structure $\wedge^2 U = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & U \\ {}^tU^{-1} & 0\end{pmatrix}$
and the explicit matrix forms
\[
  s_{f_{-2}-f_2} = -\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix},\quad
  s_{f_3} = -\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},\quad
  s_{f_2-f_3} = -\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & -1\end{pmatrix},
\]
followed by Maass' explicit formula for the multiplier. The dichotomy
Type I $\mapsto +1$, Type II $\mapsto -1$ is exactly the Gritsenko--Nikulin
1998 Lemma~3 statement.

\subsection*{5. Frontier: what lives here but not yet in the manuscript}
The explicit $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\Z) \cong W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1}) \rtimes S_3$
semidirect decomposition and the $A(\cP_{II}) = \langle s_{f_2-f_3},
s_{f_{-2}-f_2}\rangle$ generators are not yet transcribed into
\texttt{chapters/examples/cy\_d\_kappa\_stratification.tex} or
\texttt{chapters/theory/cy\_to\_chiral.tex}. Inscribing them upgrades the
N=1 row of the Borcherds weight table from ``$c_1(0)/2 = 5$ per
Gritsenko--Nikulin'' (citation-only) to a first-principles witness:
$W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1})$ is the Weyl group of $\g_{\Dfive}$, and the
three simple roots $\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3$ carry the Cartan matrix
with $2$ on the diagonal and $-2$ off-diagonal.

\subsection*{6. Triangulating $\kBKM(\Dfive) = 5$}
This segment does \emph{not} by itself witness the weight; the Borcherds
weight formula $\kBKM(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ is read off from the weight-0
constant Fourier coefficient of $\phi_{0,1}$, which is $10$, giving
$\kBKM(\Dfive) = 5$. Slides 300--400 instead witness the
\emph{compatibility} of that weight with the reflection-group action:
$\Dfive$ is anti-invariant under the index-6 subgroup
$W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1})$, invariant under its Type~I complement, and
the full automorphy on $\Omega(\cC(\Lat^{2,1})_+)$ factors as
$\mathrm{PGL}_2(\Z) = W^{(2)}(\Lat_{II}^{2,1}) \rtimes S_3$ acting with
the Type~I/II dichotomy as $\mathrm{sgn}$ on $S_3$.

\subsection*{7. Weyl vector check}
The memory file records $\rho = (1/2)(\delta_1 + \delta_2 + \delta_3) =
f_2 - (1/2) f_3 + f_{-2}$. Direct substitution with
$\delta_1 = 2f_2 - f_3$, $\delta_2 = 2f_{-2} - f_3$, $\delta_3 = f_3$:
\[
  \tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_1 + \delta_2 + \delta_3)
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\big((2f_2 - f_3) + (2f_{-2} - f_3) + f_3\big)
  = f_2 - \tfrac{1}{2} f_3 + f_{-2}. \quad\checkmark
\]
The formula is consistent. Slides 300--400 establish the $\delta_i$
themselves as the $\cP_{II,\mathrm{prim}}$ generators; the Weyl vector
is then the half-sum, in agreement with the Gritsenko--Nikulin and
Lorgat-2020 presentations.
