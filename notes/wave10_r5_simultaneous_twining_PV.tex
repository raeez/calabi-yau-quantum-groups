\section*{Simultaneous Twining, Anomaly, and Borcherds Input Data}

\paragraph{The three functions.}
Three objects must be kept separate.

The ordinary Mathieu twining genus is the one-variable function
\[
  \phi_{e,h}(\tau,z)
  =\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H_{RR}}
    \bigl(h(-1)^{F+\widetilde F}q^{L_0-c/24}
    \bar q^{\widetilde L_0-\widetilde c/24}y^{J_0}\bigr),
\]
with no twisted sector.  The Mathieu moonshine construction gives
weak Jacobi forms of weight \(0\) and index \(1\), with multiplier,
for the \(M_{24}\)-classes of \(h\).

The simultaneous, or twisted twining, genus is
\[
  \Zgh(\tau,z)
  =\operatorname{Tr}_{\Hg}
    \bigl(\rho_g(h)(-1)^{F+\widetilde F}q^{L_0-c/24}
    \bar q^{\widetilde L_0-\widetilde c/24}y^{J_0}\bigr),
  \qquad gh=hg,
\]
where \(\rho_g\) is generally a projective representation of
\(\CMg(g)\) on the \(g\)-twisted sector.  This is a two-variable
moonshine datum requiring the twisted sector, the centralizer action,
and the transgressed cocycle in addition to the ordinary twining genus
\(\phi_{e,h}\).

The second-quantized Persson--Volpato product is a genus-two function
\(\PhiGH(Z)\), obtained from \(\Zgh\) by the twisted equivariant Hecke
construction and the Hodge-anomaly correction.  Its Siegel modularity
supplies a corrected multiplicative lift.  A Borcherds--Kac--Moody
denominator, a Calabi--Yau host, or a Hall/chiral algebra requires
additional construction data.

\paragraph{The anomaly class.}
Gaberdiel--Persson--Ronellenfitsch--Volpato construct the twisted
twining genera for the elliptic genus of K3 and prove that their
modular behaviour is controlled by a primitive class
\[
  [\alpha]\in H^3(M_{24},U(1))\cong\mathbb Z/12 .
\]
For \(g\in M_{24}\), the transgressed cocycle on the centralizer is
\[
  c_g(x,y)
  =\frac{\alpha(g,x,y)\alpha(x,y,(xy)^{-1}gxy)}
    {\alpha(x,x^{-1}gx,y)} ,
\]
and for \(x,y\in\CMg(g)\) this becomes
\[
  c_g(x,y)
  =\frac{\alpha(g,x,y)\alpha(x,y,g)}{\alpha(x,g,y)} .
\]
Thus the coefficients of \(\Zgh\) are characters of projective
\(c_g\)-representations of \(\CMg(g)\).

The class labels \(7A,7B,11A,23A,23B\) are anomalous in the
Persson--Volpato sense.  This is class-level information.  For a
twisted-twined genus one still has to choose an actual commuting pair
\((g,h)\), compute the transgressed cocycle \(c_g\), and evaluate the
regularity and inverse-pair phases.  The centralizer order of \(g\)
records group size; the cocycle calculation supplies the cohomological datum.

The first obstruction is regularity.  If \(g,h,k\) commute, then
\[
  \Zgh=\xi_{g,h}(k)\,\Zgh .
\]
When \(\xi_{g,h}(k)\ne 1\), equivalently when
\[
  c_g(h,k)\ne c_g(k,h),
\]
the twisted twining genus vanishes.  A second obstruction occurs when
\((g,h)\) is conjugate to \((g^{-1},h^{-1})\) but the multiplier produced by
\([\alpha]\) is incompatible with the \(z\mapsto -z\) symmetry of a
weight-zero, index-one Jacobi form.  These are cohomological
obstructions, not failures of notation.

\paragraph{Classification of the twisted twining genera.}
The \(SL_2(\mathbb Z)\times M_{24}\)-action on commuting pairs reduces
the calculation to \(55\) independent functions.  The first \(21\) are
ordinary twining genera of the form \(\phi_{e,h}\).  The remaining
\(34\) are genuinely twisted.  For every representative the
Gaberdiel--Persson--Ronellenfitsch--Volpato construction gives either
the zero function forced by the obstruction above, or a weak Jacobi
form of weight \(0\), index \(1\), and the multiplier prescribed by
\([\alpha]\).

Consequently no theorem is obtained by filling a symmetric matrix of
orders \(N,M\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\) with the same label.  A statement about
a class pair \(([g],[h])\) must specify an actual commuting pair, the
\(M_{24}\)-orbit of that pair, the centralizer conjugacy class of
\(h\) inside \(\CMg(g)\), and the value of the anomaly phases.

\paragraph{Cyclic and geometric pairs.}
If \(g=x^a\) and \(h=x^b\) for a single element \(x\), the pair belongs
to a cyclic subgroup.  The \(SL_2(\mathbb Z)\)-orbit may then relate
\(\phi_{g,h}\) to an ordinary twining genus for a power of \(x\), but
only after the cocycle restriction to \(\langle x\rangle\) and the
chosen orbit representative are fixed.  Thus the assertion
\((2A,3A)\subset\langle6A\rangle\) is meaningful only after choosing
one element \(x\) of class \(6A\) and setting \(g=x^3\), \(h=x^2\).
The assertion is a statement about those chosen representatives.

For a geometric pair, \(g\) and \(h\) are commuting symplectic
automorphisms of one K3 surface or one K3 sigma model.  Mukai's theorem
embeds finite groups of symplectic automorphisms of a K3 surface into
\(M_{23}\).  The single-automorphism fixed-point counts are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
N&1&2&3&4&5&6&7&8\\ \hline
\#\operatorname{Fix}(g_N)&24&8&6&4&4&2&3&2 .
\end{array}
\]
For a simultaneous genus the fixed-locus datum is finer: one needs the
action of \(h\) on the \(g^r\)-fixed strata and the induced phases on
the exceptional divisors of the orbifold resolution.  The
Gaberdiel--Persson--Ronellenfitsch--Volpato fixed-locus computations
verify selected geometric pairs by this method.  A common K3 model for
all low-order pairs requires a separate construction.

\paragraph{Second quantization.}
Persson--Volpato define
\[
  \Psi_{g,h}(\sigma,\tau,z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}p^n\,
  \mathcal T_n^\alpha\Zgh(\tau,z)\right)
\]
with \(\mathcal T_n^\alpha\) the twisted equivariant Hecke operator.
After multiplication by the explicit anomaly factor \(A_{g,h}\), the
function
\[
  \PhiGH=A_{g,h}\,\Psi_{g,h}^{-1}
\]
is a meromorphic Siegel modular form for a discrete subgroup
\(\Gamma^{(2)}_{g,h}\subset\Spfour(\mathbb R)\).  Its degeneration at
the separating divisor recovers Mason's eta products.  This theorem is
a modularity theorem for a corrected multiplicative lift.  A
Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator interpretation requires additional
lattice, chamber, Weyl-vector, character, cover, and product-exponent
data.

\paragraph{Borcherds constants and the CHL ladder.}
For the five CHL denominator inputs
\[
  N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\},
\]
the Borcherds input constants are
\[
  (c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0))=(10,8,6,4,2),
\]
and Borcherds' singular theta-lift theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\PhiN)=\wt(\PhiN)=\frac{c_N(0)}2 .
\]
Hence
\[
  (\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
  \kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6))=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
The primitive denominator ladder has exactly the five levels displayed
above.
In the level-one normalization
\[
  \phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)=y^{-1}+10+y+O(q),
\]
so \(c_1(0)=10\) and the Borcherds lift has weight \(5\).  This form is
the Gritsenko--Nikulin square root \(\DeltaFive\), with character, of
the weight-ten Igusa cusp form:
\[
  \Phi_{10}=\DeltaFive^2 .
\]
The DMVV reciprocal \(1/\Phi_{10}\) is therefore the square of the
\(\DeltaFive\)-denominator reciprocal.  The \(\kBKM\) normalization
stays on the primitive \(\DeltaFive\) denominator.

\paragraph{Gritsenko--Cl\'ery diagonal-divisor rows.}
The Gritsenko--Cl\'ery classification gives exactly eight genus-two
Siegel modular forms with simplest diagonal divisor.  The rows and
weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{form}&\text{weight}\\ \hline
\Delta_5&5\\
\Delta_2&2\\
\Delta_1&1\\
\Delta_{1/2}&\tfrac12\\
\nabla_3&3\\
\nabla_2&2\\
\nabla_{3/2}&\tfrac32\\
Q_1&1 .
\end{array}
\]
This diagonal-divisor catalogue has a different index set from the CHL
ladder \((10,8,6,4,2)\) and from the ordinary one-variable twining
table.  The common visible point is the level-one row \(\DeltaFive\).
Every other comparison requires an explicit row map preserving the
paramodular group or its cover, the character, the divisor, the product
variables, and the Fourier normalization.

\paragraph{What can be inferred.}
From \(\Zgh\) alone one may infer a twisted twining Jacobi form, its
multiplier, its anomaly phases, and its projective centralizer
characters.  A Borcherds denominator assertion requires a lattice
\(L\), a Weyl chamber, a Weyl vector, a real-root system, a character
or multiplier cover, an automorphic product, and a source theorem
identifying the product with the Weyl--Kac--Borcherds denominator.

From \(\PhiGH\) alone one may infer the Persson--Volpato corrected
Siegel modular form and Mason degeneration.  A compact Calabi--Yau host
theorem must construct the charge lattice, the protected one-particle
index, the orientation character, the reduced integration theorem
producing the scalar partition function, and the primitive Hall or
chiral comparison.

The unresolved higher-order and projective-twining obligation is
therefore precise: compute the restriction of \([\alpha]\) to the
normalizer of the chosen commuting pair, verify the regularity phases
and inverse-pair phases, and then, if a denominator or Calabi--Yau
interpretation is claimed, supply the missing lattice, cover, chamber,
orientation, and primitive-comparison data.  With only the twining
input, the mathematical object is the twisted twining genus.
