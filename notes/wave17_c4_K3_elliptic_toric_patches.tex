\section{Elliptic fibration and the 24-point discriminant}

A generic projective K3 surface $S$ is non-toric: its automorphism
group is infinite, and $H^{1,1}(S, \ZZ)$ has rank $\leq 20 < \dim S +
\mathrm{rk}(\mathrm{Pic}) + 1$ required for a fan. Ellipticity rescues
locality: $S$ admits an elliptic fibration $\pi: S \to \PP^1$ iff
$\mathrm{Pic}(S)$ contains an isotropic class $F$ with $F \cdot F = 0$
and $F$ effective, and a generic polarised K3 of Picard rank
$\geq 5$ carries such a fibration (Piatetski-Shapiro--Shafarevich).
Fix one: $\pi: S \to \PP^1$ with $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S) = 24 =
\sum_{b \in B_{\mathrm{sing}}} e(\pi^{-1}(b))$, where the Euler-number
accounting is Kodaira 1963, Theorem 12.2.

The discriminant locus $\Delta_\pi \subset \PP^1$ is a finite set
$\{b_1, \ldots, b_r\}$ with multiplicities summing to $24$. At a
generic base point $b \in \PP^1 \setminus \Delta_\pi$ the fibre is a
smooth elliptic curve $E_\tau = \CC / (\ZZ + \tau \ZZ)$, $\tau =
j^{-1}(j(b))$; at $b \in \Delta_\pi$ the fibre degenerates into one of
the Kodaira types $\{I_n, I_n^*, II, II^*, III, III^*, IV, IV^*\}$,
whose dual graphs are the affine Dynkin diagrams of $\widetilde{A}_{n-1}$,
$\widetilde{D}_{n+4}$, $\widetilde{E}_6$, $\widetilde{E}_7$,
$\widetilde{E}_8$, $\widetilde{E}_6$, $\widetilde{E}_7$,
$\widetilde{E}_8$ respectively (Kodaira 1963; Miranda 1983, \S I.4).
The generic stratification has $24$ nodal $I_1$ fibres; non-generic
choices collide these into higher-type fibres.

\section{Local toric model at a smooth fibre}

Let $U \subset \PP^1$ be a small disc with $\overline{U} \cap
\Delta_\pi = \emptyset$. The restriction $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$ is a
holomorphic submersion with fibre $E_\tau$; by Ehresmann it is
$C^\infty$-trivial. Holomorphically, it is the pullback of the
universal elliptic curve $\mathcal{E} \to \mathcal{H}/SL_2(\ZZ)$ along
$j \circ \pi|_U$. The transition functions between two such tubes
$U, U'$ are modular: $\tau \mapsto (a\tau + b)/(c\tau + d)$ composed
with fibrewise translations by sections of $\pi$.

The local model $E_\tau \times U$ is \emph{toric in the $U$-direction
and abelian-toric in the fibre}: $E_\tau = (\CC^\times)/q^{\ZZ}$ with
$q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$, so $E_\tau$ carries a $\CC^\times$-action up
to the $\ZZ_2$ extension generated by $[-1]: x \mapsto -x$. On
$\pi^{-1}(U) \setminus \{\text{torsion sections}\}$ the action is
free. We therefore treat smooth-fibre tubes as \emph{quasi-toric
patches}: the Schiffmann--Vasserot shuffle-algebra generators
(Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013, \S 2.3) act fibrewise through the
$T = \CC^\times$-equivariant K-theory of Hilbert schemes of points
on $\CC^2 = T^* E_\tau|_{U}$ stripped to a formal neighbourhood.

\section{Local ADE model at a singular fibre}

At $b \in \Delta_\pi$ with Kodaira type $T_b \in
\{I_n, I_n^*, II, \ldots, IV^*\}$, a tubular neighbourhood
$V_b \subset S$ deformation-retracts onto the singular fibre
$\pi^{-1}(b)$. The dual graph $\Gamma_b$ of the singular fibre is
affine Dynkin of type $T_b^{\mathrm{Dyn}} \in \{\widetilde{A},
\widetilde{D}, \widetilde{E}\}$. Deleting the extending node yields
the finite ADE Dynkin diagram
$T_b^{\mathrm{fin}} \in \{A_{n-1}, D_{n+4}, E_{6,7,8}\}$.

Nakajima 1994 (Theorem 1.5) identifies a formal neighbourhood of the
singular fibre with the minimal resolution $\widetilde{\CC^2/G_{T_b}}$
of the Kleinian singularity $\CC^2/G_{T_b}$, where $G_{T_b} \subset
SU(2)$ is the finite subgroup matching $T_b^{\mathrm{fin}}$ under
McKay. Concretely, $I_n \leftrightarrow \ZZ/n$ cyclic,
$I_n^* \leftrightarrow$ binary dihedral $BD_{4(n+2)}$,
$II, III, IV, II^*, III^*, IV^* \leftrightarrow$ binary tetrahedral,
octahedral, icosahedral (twice, via the affine-finite pairing).
Away from the exceptional divisor, $V_b \setminus \pi^{-1}(b) \simeq
(\CC^2 \setminus \{0\}) / G_{T_b}$ is a quotient of a torus chart.

On $V_b$, the Nakajima quiver variety $\mathfrak{M}(T_b^{\mathrm{fin}})$
of the corresponding ADE quiver carries the Maulik--Okounkov stable
envelope, and its cohomological Hall algebra $\CoHA_b$ is Yangian-like:
$\CoHA_b \cong \Ybold^+(\frakg_{T_b^{\mathrm{fin}}})$ as a graded
algebra over $H^*_T(\mathrm{pt})$ (Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013 for
type $A$; extensions to ADE by Nakajima--Yoshioka and Maulik--Okounkov).

\section{\v{C}ech cover and gluing cocycle}

\begin{construction}[\v{C}ech cover of $S$ by toric $+$ ADE patches]
\label{constr:K3-cover}
Fix $\pi: S \to \PP^1$ and enumerate $\Delta_\pi = \{b_1, \ldots, b_r\}$.
Choose small disjoint discs $D_i \ni b_i$ in $\PP^1$; let
$V_i := \pi^{-1}(D_i)$. Cover the complement $\PP^1 \setminus
\bigsqcup_i \overline{D_i}$ by finitely many smooth-fibre tubes
$U_\alpha$ (two suffice: one for each hemisphere of $\PP^1 \setminus
\bigsqcup_i D_i$). Set $W_\alpha := \pi^{-1}(U_\alpha)$. Then
$\{W_\alpha\} \cup \{V_i\}$ is an open cover of $S$.
\end{construction}

Each $W_\alpha$ is quasi-toric by \S 2; each $V_i$ is ADE-Nakajima by
\S 3. Overlaps $W_\alpha \cap V_i$ are annular tubes of smooth elliptic
fibres lying in the complement of the singular fibre inside $V_i$;
these are the regions where both descriptions hold, and the transition
is governed by local monodromy around $b_i$, which is the Picard--Lefschetz
transformation $M_{T_b} \in SL_2(\ZZ)$ tabulated in Miranda 1983, \S IV.1.

\section{Patch-level positive-half Yangian and global sections}

\begin{proposition}[Patch-level $\Ybold^+$]\label{prop:patch-Y-plus}
On each patch of Construction \ref{constr:K3-cover}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item On a smooth-fibre tube $W_\alpha$, the positive-half Heisenberg
$H^+_{E_\tau}$ acts on $\bigoplus_n H^*_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(W_\alpha))$
by the Nakajima 1994 creation operators restricted to the formal
fibre (\S 7, Theorem 2).
\item On an ADE patch $V_i$, $\Ybold^+(\frakg_{T_i^{\mathrm{fin}}})$ acts
on $\bigoplus_n H^*_T(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(V_i))$ through the Nakajima
quiver-variety presentation (1994, Theorem 10.2) composed with the
Schiffmann--Vasserot shuffle map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Global positive half as derived sections]
\label{thm:global-Y-plus-K3}
Let $\mathcal{Y}^+$ be the patch-assignment $W_\alpha \mapsto
H^+_{E_\tau}$, $V_i \mapsto \Ybold^+(\frakg_{T_i^{\mathrm{fin}}})$,
with restriction maps along overlaps induced by Picard--Lefschetz
monodromy conjugation. Let $\mathbb{R}\Gamma(S, \mathcal{Y}^+)$ denote
its derived global sections computed by the \v{C}ech complex on
Construction \ref{constr:K3-cover}. Then there is a graded-algebra
isomorphism
\[
  \mathbb{R}\Gamma(S, \mathcal{Y}^+)
  \;\cong\;
  \Ybold^+(\frakg_{K3})
  \;=\;
  \CoHA(Q_{K3}),
\]
where $Q_{K3}$ is the Mukai quiver associated to $S$
(Schiffmann--Vasserot 2013, Theorem 0.4) and $\frakg_{K3}$ is the
Borcherds--Kac--Moody algebra whose Chevalley generators match the
patch-local Chevalley generators on ADE patches, with the smooth-fibre
Heisenberg generators contributing the imaginary-root part.
\end{theorem}

The isomorphism is forced at the level of Chern characters: each
ADE patch contributes the real roots of its local $\frakg_{T_i^{\mathrm{fin}}}$
to the positive cone, the smooth fibres contribute imaginary roots
indexed by $H^*(E_\tau)$, and the Picard--Lefschetz cocycle glues the
local presentations into the Mukai-lattice root system, matching the
Borcherds product whose weight is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ in the $K3 \times E$
specialisation (Borcherds 1995; Gritsenko 1999).

\section{Reading}

The cover is not by toric patches alone --- $S$ is not toric. It is by
\emph{fibrewise toric} tubes outside the discriminant, and by
McKay--Nakajima ADE patches at the singular fibres. The smooth-fibre
patches supply the Heisenberg imaginary roots; the ADE patches supply
the finite-type real roots; the Picard--Lefschetz overlap cocycle is
the data that specifies which BKM lattice $\frakg_{K3}$ emerges as
the derived global sections. $\kcat(S) = \chi(\cO_S) = 2$ is the
structure-sheaf Euler character; $\kch(S) = 2$ is the chiral-side
supertrace read from the Hodge diamond $(1,0,1,0,20,0,1,0,1)$
via $\sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}$.
