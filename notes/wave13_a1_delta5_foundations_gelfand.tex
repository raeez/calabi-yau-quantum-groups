\section*{$\Delta_5$, $\Phi_{10}$, and the Primitive CHL Denominator}
\label{sec:delta5-foundations-gelfand}

The form $\Delta_5$ is the weight-$5$ square root of the Igusa cusp form
on the level-$1$ Siegel modular group:
\[
  \Delta_5\in M_5(\Sp_4(\mathbb Z),\nu_{\Delta_5}),\qquad
  \Phiten^{\theta}=\Delta_5^2\in M_{10}(\Sp_4(\mathbb Z)).
\]
The monic Borcherds denominator used by the BKM algebra is
\[
  \Dfive=64^{-1}\Delta_5,\qquad
  \operatorname{den}(\fg_{\Delta_5})=\Dfive(2Z).
\]
Multiplying a denominator by a non-zero scalar does not change its
modular weight, divisor, character, or BKM root multiplicities.

\subsection*{Eichler--Zagier and K3 Normalisations}

The Eichler--Zagier generator satisfies
\[
  \phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}(\tau,z)
  =
  r^{-1}+10+r+O(q).
\]
The K3 elliptic genus is twice this form:
\[
  Z_{K3}(\tau,z)=2\,\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}(\tau,z).
\]
The primitive $N=1$ Borcherds input records the normalised coefficient
\[
  c_1(0)=10,
\]
so Borcherds' weight theorem gives
\[
  \kBKM(\Delta_5)=c_1(0)/2=5.
\]
The reduced DT scalar on $K3\times E$ uses the square normalisation:
\[
  Z^{K3\times E}_{\mathrm{red}}=C\,(\Phiten^\theta)^{-1}
  =C\,\Delta_5^{-2}.
\]
In the Oberdieck--Pandharipande monic convention
$\Phiten^{\mathrm{OP}}=\Dfive^2=4096^{-1}\Delta_5^2$, so the reciprocal
scalar differs by the corresponding constant.

\subsection*{Primitive CHL Constants}

For the primitive CHL/BKM denominator branch,
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6 \\ \hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2 \\
\kBKM(\Phi_N) & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\
2\,\kBKM(\Phi_N) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2
\end{array}
\]
The last row is the scalar-square weight. At $N=1$ the primitive
denominator is $\Delta_5$ and the scalar square is
$\Phiten^\theta=\Delta_5^2$. For $N>1$, identifying a primitive CHL
denominator with a specific Gritsenko--Clery row is an additional
normalisation theorem. The tuple $(10,4,2,2,2)$ is a fixed-locus or
twining-row convention and is not the primitive CHL table.

\subsection*{Gritsenko--Clery Diagonal-Divisor Catalogue}

Gritsenko--Clery classify eight diagonal-divisor paramodular forms. The
catalogue is triple-indexed by paramodular level, congruence level, and
weight:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{row} & (t,N_0) & \text{form} & \mathrm{wt} \\
\hline
1 & (1,1) & \Delta_5 & 5 \\
2 & (2,1) & D_2^{(2)} & 2 \\
3 & (1,2) & D_3^{(1,2)} & 3 \\
4 & (3,1) & D_1^{(3)} & 1 \\
5 & (1,3) & D_2^{(1,3)} & 2 \\
6 & (4,1) & D_{1/2}^{(4)} & 1/2 \\
7 & (1,4) & D_{3/2}^{(1,4)} & 3/2 \\
8 & (2,2) & D_1^{(2,2)} & 1
\end{array}
\]
The twice-weight row is
\[
  c^{\mathrm{GC}}(0)=(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
This row belongs to the Gritsenko--Clery catalogue. It is separate from
the primitive CHL tuple $(10,8,6,4,2)$. The catalogue contains no
quarter-weight row and no weight-$0$ row.

\subsection*{Borcherds Weight and Denominator Interpretation}

Borcherds 1998, Theorem~13.3, states that the singular theta lift of a
weakly holomorphic input with constant coefficient $c_0(0)$ has weight
$c_0(0)/2$. On the primitive CHL branch this yields
\[
  \kBKM(\Phi_N)=c_N(0)/2,\qquad N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}.
\]
On a Gritsenko--Clery row it yields the modular weight of that row after
the row and input normalisation have been fixed:
\[
  \mathrm{wt}(D_j)=c^{\mathrm{GC}}_j(0)/2.
\]
The second formula is a weight statement for the catalogue. It becomes
a Vol~III BKM denominator statement only when the corresponding Hall or
Borcherds superalgebra has been constructed with its multiplier,
Weyl-vector, and root-multiplicity conventions.

\subsection*{$K3\times E$ Branch Separation}

The $K3\times E$ BKM branch is the Hall--Drinfeld double or compact Hall
branch under the compactness, orientation, PBW, and finite comparison
hypotheses:
\[
  \mathcal D_{\hbar}\bigl(\CoHA(K3\times E)\bigr)
  \leadsto
  \fg_{\Delta_5}.
\]
It is not a Drinfeld Yangian. The K3 Yangian is the separate Mukai
self-mirror branch. The two-stage factorisation
\[
  \Phi_d=\mathrm{Sp}_{\Sigma_{d-1},C}\circ\Phih_d
\]
produces a chiral specialisation; it does not identify the BKM branch
with a Yangian branch.

\subsection*{Reduced DT and Reciprocal Squares}

For $X=K3\times E$, Oberdieck--Pandharipande prove the reduced
Donaldson--Thomas identity in the $N=1$ sector:
\[
  Z^X_{\mathrm{red}}=C\,\Phiten^{-1}=C\,\Delta_5^{-2}
\]
in the unnormalised theta convention. The CHL sectors
$N\in\{2,3,4,6\}$ carry primitive denominator forms of weights
$(5,4,3,2,1)$ and scalar-square weights $(10,8,6,4,2)$. Matching those
primitive denominators with individual Gritsenko--Clery rows requires a
named overlap theorem and a scalar normalisation.
