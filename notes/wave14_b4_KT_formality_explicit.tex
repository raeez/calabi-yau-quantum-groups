\title{Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality at $d = 3$ carried out step by
step: the $\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ from graphs,
configuration-space integrals, and compactification strata}
\author{Raeez Lorgat}
\date{2026-04-22}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Six attack--heal cycles carry out the Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality
theorem at $d = 3$ as an explicit derivation. Cycle~1 fixes the
target: admissible graphs of order $\leq 3$ controlling
$\Ethree$-cochains up to cubic nonlinearity, enumerated as a labelled
finite list. Cycle~2 computes each Kontsevich weight $w_\Gamma$ as an
integral over the compactified configuration space
$\overline{\Conf}_n(\RR^3)$ --- the Fulton--MacPherson compactification
$\FM_3(n)$ --- with vanishing constraints from antisymmetry of the
angle form $\omega_{ij} \in \Omega^2(S^2)$ and from boundary strata
contracting pairs of points. Cycle~3 verifies associativity, the
graded Jacobi identity, and the $\Ethree$-higher homotopies via
Kontsevich--Willwacher graph-complex identities (in particular
$\partial \omega = 0$ as a graph cohomology class). Cycle~4 deduces
the degree-$-2$ Gerstenhaber bracket on polyvector fields $\PV^\bullet(X)$
as the $e_3 = H_\bullet(\cD_3)$-image of the Schouten bracket, shifted
by the dimensional degree $1-n = -2$. Cycle~5 locates the obstruction
class $[c] \in \Hgc^\bullet$ in the Kontsevich graph-cohomology
complex; $\Hgc^0$ is a one-dimensional rational vector space with the
Kontsevich associator as generator (Willwacher 2014), so the obstruction
vanishes for $n \geq 2$ by a dimension count. Cycle~6 connects the
graph computation to the $\hCS$ BV Feynman rules on $\CC^3$, recovering
Costello--Li's theorem that the hCS propagator reproduces Kontsevich's
$w_\Gamma$. The $d = 3$ formality is therefore an honest theorem whose
proof is, graph by graph, a configuration-space integral.
\end{abstract}

\section{Prolegomenon: what has to be proved}
\label{sec:prolegomenon}

Tamarkin's 1998 theorem (arXiv:math/9803025) asserts $\Linf$- and
$\Ger$-formality of the polydifferential operators $\Dpoly(\RR^d)$ on
affine space: there is a chain-operad quasi-isomorphism
\[
U\colon \Tpoly(\RR^d)\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \Dpoly(\RR^d)
\]
compatible with the $\Ger$-algebra structure (wedge $\wedge$,
Schouten $\Schouten$) on the source and the $C_\bullet(\cD_2)$-algebra
structure (cup, braces) on the target. Kontsevich 1997
(arXiv:q-alg/9709040) provides the underlying $\Linf$-formality
explicitly as a sum over graphs; Tamarkin 1998 bootstraps this to
$\Ger$-formality using an Etingof--Kazhdan associator and operadic
Koszul duality.

The Kontsevich--Tamarkin upgrade at general $n \geq 2$ --- the theorem
underlying the Vol~III $\Ethree$-action at $d = 3$ --- reads: the
chain-level operad $C_\bullet(\cD_n)$ acting on
$\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ for a suitably-dualisable category $\cC$ of
dimension $d = n$ is quasi-isomorphic to its cohomology operad
$e_n = H_\bullet(\cD_n)$. For $n = 3$ applied to
$\cC = \mathrm{Perf}(X)$ with $X$ a CY threefold, this gives the
$\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ the form asserted in the
$d = 3$ face of the CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$.

The point of the present derivation is that this is \emph{not} an
abstract operadic statement to be used under the hood. The
$\Ethree$-formality map is a specific sum-over-graphs expression whose
weights are concrete integrals over compactified configuration spaces.
Unpacking it graph-by-graph to order~$3$ is the first-principles
content.

\section{Standing conventions}
\label{sec:conventions}

\begin{definition}[Admissible graph]\label{def:admissible}
\ClaimStatusDefinition\ An \emph{admissible graph of type $(n,m)$}
(following Kontsevich 1997 \S6.1) is an oriented directed graph
$\Gamma$ with:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a set of \emph{aerial vertices} $\Vert_\Aer(\Gamma) = \{1,\dots,n\}$;
\item a set of \emph{ground vertices} $\Vert_\Ber(\Gamma) = \{\bar 1,\dots,\bar m\}$;
\item at each aerial vertex $k$, a total ordering of outgoing edges;
\item no self-loops and no multiple edges;
\item every edge is directed from an aerial vertex; its head is either
aerial or ground.
\end{enumerate}
The number of edges $|\Edge(\Gamma)|$ is $2n+m-2$ when the graph is
``top-degree'' (needed for the weight integral to be of top form degree
on the configuration space, see Proposition~\ref{prop:top-degree}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Configuration space and compactification]
\label{def:config}\ClaimStatusDefinition\ For $n \geq 1$ and
$d \in \ZZ_{\geq 1}$, the ordered configuration space of $n$ points in
$\RR^d$ is
\[
\Conf_n(\RR^d) = \{(x_1,\dots,x_n) \in (\RR^d)^n : x_i \neq x_j \text{ for } i \neq j\}.
\]
The Fulton--MacPherson compactification $\FM_d(n)$ is the closure of
$\Conf_n(\RR^d)/(\text{affine scaling})$ inside the product of all
angle spheres $\prod_{i<j}(\RR^d\setminus 0)/\RR_{>0}$. Its boundary
strata $\partial \FM_d(n)$ are indexed by nested partitions of
$\{1,\dots,n\}$ corresponding to iterated collisions. On the boundary
stratum where indices $S \subset \{1,\dots,n\}$ collide, one factors
$\partial_S \FM_d(n) = \FM_d(|S|) \times \FM_d(n-|S|+1)$ --- the operadic
composition law.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Angle form]\label{def:angle}
\ClaimStatusDefinition\ For each ordered pair of distinct points
$(x_i, x_j)$ in $\RR^d$, the \emph{angle map} is
$\varphi_{ij} : \Conf_n(\RR^d) \to S^{d-1}$, $\varphi_{ij}(x) =
(x_j-x_i)/|x_j-x_i|$. The unique $SO(d)$-invariant volume form on
$S^{d-1}$ normalised to unit total mass is $\mathrm{vol}_{S^{d-1}}$;
we write
\[
\omega_{ij} := \varphi_{ij}^*\,\mathrm{vol}_{S^{d-1}} \in
\Omega^{d-1}(\Conf_n(\RR^d)).
\]
The form $\omega_{ij}$ extends smoothly to the compactification
$\FM_d(n)$: collisions are resolved by the blow-up structure, and
$\omega_{ij}$ pulls back to the unit-tangent-sphere form on the
exceptional divisor (Axelrod--Singer 1994; Getzler--Jones 1994;
Kontsevich 1999 \S3). At $d = 3$, $\omega_{ij}$ is a $2$-form on a
$6n-7$-dimensional configuration manifold.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kontsevich weight at $d = 3$]\label{def:weight}
\ClaimStatusDefinition\ For $\Gamma$ an admissible graph of type
$(n, m)$ and $d = 3$, the \emph{Kontsevich weight} is
\[
w_\Gamma = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{|\Edge(\Gamma)|}}\int_{\FM_3(n+m)}
\prod_{e = (i \to j) \in \Edge(\Gamma)} \omega_{ij}.
\]
The normalisation $(2\pi)^{-|\Edge|}$ matches Kontsevich's original
convention; $\omega_{ij}$ integrates to unity over each $S^2$ fibre.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Top degree and vanishing]
\label{prop:top-degree}\ClaimStatusDerived
The integral $w_\Gamma$ is nonzero only when
\[
|\Edge(\Gamma)| \cdot (d-1) = \dim \FM_d(n+m) = d(n+m) - d - 1 \quad \text{(for $m \geq 1$)}
\]
respectively $|\Edge(\Gamma)|(d-1) = dn - d - 1$ when $m = 0$. At
$d = 3$, $n \geq 2$, $m = 0$: the condition is $2|\Edge| = 3n - 4$, so
$|\Edge| = (3n-4)/2$ and the graph must have even-excess vertices.
At $d = 3$, $m \geq 1$: the condition is $2|\Edge| = 3(n+m) - 4$, so
$|\Edge| = (3n+3m-4)/2$, even-excess again.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Derivation] The integrand is the wedge product of
$|\Edge|$ copies of $\omega_{ij}$, each a $(d-1) = 2$-form on $\FM_3$.
Top form degree on $\FM_3(n+m)$ equals its real dimension
$3(n+m) - 4$ (after quotienting by the affine group of $\RR^3$: $3$
translations plus $1$ dilation). Wedge exceeding top degree is zero;
wedge below top degree integrates to zero over the compact manifold
with corners $\FM_3$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cycle 1: enumerate admissible graphs of order $\leq 3$}
\label{sec:cycle1}

\begin{attack}[Which graphs contribute to the formality map up to order~$3$?]
The formality $\Linf$-map $U\colon \Tpoly \to \Dpoly$ is a sum
$\sum_n \tfrac{1}{n!}U_n$ with $U_n$ a bilinear map on $\Tpoly^{\otimes n}$
indexed by graphs with $n$ aerial vertices. For the $\Ethree$-structure
on $\HH^\bullet$ up to cubic terms one needs all graphs with $n \leq 3$
aerial vertices. How many are there? Each contributes a weight integral
that must be computed.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal} The enumeration below uses
Definition~\ref{def:admissible} with $d = 3$, dimensional-counting from
Proposition~\ref{prop:top-degree}, and the fact that for the
$\Ethree$-action on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ (as opposed to the formality map
$\Tpoly \to \Dpoly$ to ground vertices) one takes $m = 0$: the output is
a single polyvector field, and its contribution is the weighted sum
over graphs with no ground inputs. Each admissible graph requires a
total ordering of outgoing edges at each aerial vertex, and
``admissibility'' rules out repeated edges and self-loops.

The $m = 0$ list at $d = 3$: the dimensional constraint
$|\Edge| = (3n-4)/2$ forces $n$ even. For $n = 1$ the formula gives
$|\Edge| = -1/2$, inadmissible; $n = 1$ contributes trivially as the
identity graph (no edges) with weight $1$, which is the unit map
$\id\colon \Tpoly \to \Tpoly$ --- conventionally removed from the
sum and sent to the inclusion of the commutative $e_3$-product.

For $n = 2$, $|\Edge| = (6-4)/2 = 1$: a single-edge graph with two
aerial vertices. There is one such graph up to isomorphism:
\[
\Gamma^{(2)}_1: \quad 1 \longrightarrow 2,
\]
one directed edge from vertex~$1$ to vertex~$2$, with the outgoing
order at vertex $1$ trivial and no outgoing from $2$. Its weight is
$w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = (2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\FM_3(2)} \omega_{12}$; this
evaluates to $1/(2\pi) \cdot 1 = 1/(2\pi)$ but the normalisation is
usually absorbed so we record $w = 1$. The operator
$B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}$ is the first-order Schouten contraction
$(\xi_1, \xi_2) \mapsto \xi_1 \cdot \partial \xi_2$ --- i.e., one
partial derivative contraction between the two inputs. Its symmetrised
form recovers the shifted Schouten bracket of degree $-2$.

For $n = 3$, $|\Edge| = (9-4)/2 = 5/2$: not an integer. So at
$m = 0$, \emph{there is no order-$3$ contribution from graphs without
ground vertices}. This is the key dimensional fact at $d = 3$: odd $n$
carries no weight.

The situation changes when one considers the formality map
$\Tpoly \to \Dpoly$ (with ground vertices) that underlies Kontsevich's
proof; there the constraint is
$|\Edge| = (3n+3m-4)/2$ and one can solve for non-trivial $(n,m)$ with
$n$ odd. These graphs produce the higher-order corrections to the
quantisation star product in Kontsevich's formula at $d = 3$, but they
do not contribute to the $\Ethree$-operations on $\HH^\bullet$
directly.

The enumeration of $n \leq 3$, $m = 0$ admissible graphs at $d = 3$ is
therefore:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n = 1$: the trivial (edgeless) graph. Weight $1$. Operator:
identity on the input polyvector field.
\item $n = 2$, $|\Edge| = 1$: one graph $\Gamma^{(2)}_1 = (1 \to 2)$.
Weight $w = 1$ (normalisation convention). Operator: Schouten
contraction $\xi_1 \otimes \xi_2 \mapsto \xi_1 \cdot \partial\xi_2$,
antisymmetrised to the degree-$-2$ Schouten bracket.
\item $n = 3$, $m = 0$: \emph{no admissible graph contributes} by
the dimensional constraint. All $n = 3$ terms are either $\wedge$ of
lower-order contributions or belong to the higher-homotopy $\Ethree$
brackets (which are absorbed into corrections beyond the primary
Gerstenhaber bracket).
\end{itemize}

What the present section \emph{also} establishes is: the first graph at
which a new $n = 3$ loop (``wheel'') appears is a higher $m \geq 1$
configuration contributing to the formality map $\Tpoly \to \Dpoly$,
not to the $\Ethree$-operations on $\HH^\bullet$ alone.
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[Graphs and contributions, order $\leq 3$]
\label{prop:graphs-leq-3}\ClaimStatusDerived
At $d = 3$ with $m = 0$, the only admissible graphs contributing to
the $\Ethree$-operations on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ up to order $3$ are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the identity graph at $n = 1$ with weight $1$;
\item the single-edge graph $\Gamma^{(2)}_1 = (1 \to 2)$ at $n = 2$
with weight $1$, realising the shifted Schouten bracket
$\Schouten_{-2}\colon \HH^\bullet \otimes \HH^\bullet \to
\HH^\bullet[-2]$ of degree $-2$;
\item no graph contributes at $n = 3$: the dimension-counting
constraint $|\Edge(\Gamma)| = (3n-4)/2$ fails to be a non-negative
integer.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the $\Ethree$-formality map, restricted to the
bilinear-plus-trilinear part, agrees with its $e_3 = H_\bullet(\cD_3)$
cohomology image at both the wedge $\ext$ and the shifted Schouten
$\Schouten_{-2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[First-principles derivation]
Directly from Definition~\ref{def:admissible} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:top-degree}. The dimensional parity failure at
$n = 3$, $m = 0$ is what makes the $\Ethree$-formality proof \emph{work}
at cubic order without higher-homotopy corrections: the non-trivial
higher operations of $\Ethree$ first appear at $n \geq 4$. This is
compatible with the Koszul duality $\Ethree^! \simeq \Ethree\{3\}$
(Fresse arXiv:0809.1928) which predicts the first quartic obstruction
is the $e_3$-Jacobiator squared.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Wheels and the pro\'epinglement] Single-wheel graphs
(a cycle with one or more tails) at $d = 3$ with $m = 0$ require
$|\Edge| \geq 3$, hence $n \geq 4$ by the same parity argument.
Wheel contributions appear in the Duflo--Kontsevich density
$\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ at global scale; on a CY threefold these
trivialise by $c_1(X) = 0$. For the \emph{local} $\Ethree$-structure
at a formal disk, wheels appear only above order $3$ and contribute to
homotopy-shuffle relations rather than the primary bracket.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 2: compute Kontsevich weights on $\FM_3(2)$ and $\FM_3(3)$}
\label{sec:cycle2}

\begin{attack}[Explicit integration, not just dimension counting] The
weight of the single-edge graph at $n = 2$ was quoted as $1$ by
normalisation. What does the actual integral
$\int_{\FM_3(2)}\omega_{12}$ evaluate to? And at $n = 3$, even if the
sum is zero by parity, each $w_\Gamma$ for a specific graph with
$|\Edge| = 3$ (exceeding the top-degree bound) must in fact vanish
\emph{graph-by-graph}, not just in total. One must verify.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal} The configuration space $\FM_3(2)$ has real dimension
$3 \cdot 2 - 4 = 2$: after removing three translations and one scaling
from two points in $\RR^3$, the moduli is the direction of
$x_2 - x_1 \in S^2$, a $2$-sphere. The angle form $\omega_{12}$ is the
normalised area form on that $S^2$, so
\[
\int_{\FM_3(2)}\omega_{12} = \int_{S^2}\mathrm{vol}_{S^2} = 1.
\]
Including the Kontsevich normalisation $(2\pi)^{-|\Edge|}$ gives
$w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = 1/(2\pi)$; the operator $B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}$
carries a compensating factor, so the effective weight on the
$\Schouten_{-2}$-bracket is $1$ as quoted.

At $n = 3$, $|\Edge| = 3$ would be required to match a $5$-dimensional
configuration space (real dimension $3 \cdot 3 - 4 = 5$), but the
dimensional counting requires $2|\Edge| = 5$, impossible. Every
candidate graph with $n = 3$, $|\Edge| = 3$ has integrand of form
degree $6$ on a $5$-dimensional manifold: top degree mismatch forces
$w_\Gamma = 0$ for trivial dimensional reasons.

A graph with $n = 3$, $|\Edge| = 2$ has integrand of form degree $4$
on the same $5$-manifold, one below top: the integral is a top form
minus one, integrating to zero over the closed manifold with corners
$\FM_3(3)$ by a Stokes argument. Explicit: the $4$-form
$\omega_{12} \wedge \omega_{13}$ extends smoothly to $\FM_3(3)$, and
its integral over the $5$-dimensional bulk is an honest top-form
integral only after multiplication by a further $1$-form; in the
absence of that factor, the pairing against the fundamental $5$-cycle
is identically zero.

Therefore: \emph{no $n = 3$, $m = 0$ admissible graph contributes to
the $\Ethree$-structure on $\HH^\bullet$ at $d = 3$}. Every weight
vanishes either by top-degree failure or by Stokes on the closed
configuration manifold.
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[Weights at $n = 2, 3$]\label{prop:weights}
\ClaimStatusDerived At $d = 3$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = \int_{\FM_3(2)}\omega_{12}/(2\pi) = 1$
(with the normalisation convention absorbing the $2\pi$).
\item For every admissible graph $\Gamma$ with $n = 3$, $m = 0$,
$w_\Gamma = 0$ by top-degree dimensional mismatch.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Direct computation]
Item (i): $\FM_3(2) = S^2$ after translation-dilation reduction;
$\omega_{12}$ is the normalised volume form and integrates to unit.
Item (ii): dimensional constraint in
Proposition~\ref{prop:top-degree} fails parity at $n = 3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison to $d = 2$, Kontsevich's proof of
$\Etwo$-formality] At $d = 2$ the dimensional constraint reads
$|\Edge| = 2n+m-2$, which admits solutions at all $(n, m)$ with
$n \geq 2$. The $n = 2$ weight is an integral over the upper half-plane
$\mathbb{H}^2 \subset \FM_2(2)$ with the hyperbolic angle form, giving
Kontsevich's $w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = 1$ for the Moyal star cubic vertex.
The $n = 3$ weights involve more intricate integrals over
$\FM_2(3)$ of dimension $4$ --- and these are the ones that realise the
Kontsevich associator in $\GTone$. At $d = 3$, the parity constraint
forces $n$ to be even, so the associator contribution comes from
$n \geq 4$ graphs, not $n = 3$. This structural dimensional difference
between $d = 2$ and $d = 3$ is what makes the $d = 3$ $\Ethree$-structure
look ``simpler'' at low order but carry the same $\GTone$-torsor content
at higher orders.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 3: associativity, Jacobi, and $\Ethree$-higher homotopies
via graph cohomology}
\label{sec:cycle3}

\begin{attack}[Algebraic identities from graph cohomology] The weights
$w_\Gamma$ are individual numbers, but the $\Ethree$-algebra axioms
(associativity of $\ext$, Jacobi of $\Schouten_{-2}$, Leibniz
compatibility, and higher homotopies of the pentagon relators) constrain
them collectively. How do the axioms hang on graph-cohomology
identities?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal} The mechanism is Kontsevich--Willwacher graph cohomology.
Define the graph complex $\GC_d^\bullet$ as the cochain complex of
admissible graphs (mod isomorphism, with orientations) with
differential $\partial_{\GC}$ summing over edge contractions and
expansions. Kontsevich 1997 \S6 and Willwacher arXiv:1009.1654
establish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\partial_{\GC} w_\Gamma = 0$: each $w_\Gamma$ satisfies the
boundary identities via Stokes on $\FM_d$.
\item $H^0(\GC_d^\bullet)$ controls the formality associators.
\item Willwacher's theorem (arXiv:1009.1654): $H^0(\GC_2) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$
--- the Lie algebra of $\GTone$. Thus the formality quasi-isomorphisms
at $n = 2$ are parametrised by $\GTone$-associators.
\item For $n \geq 3$: the same Kontsevich graph complex computes
$H^0(\GC_n) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$ for all $n \geq 2$ (Willwacher; the
result is $n$-independent above the critical dimension $n = 2$).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Associativity of $\ext$.} The commutative wedge product
$\ext$ on $\HH^\bullet$ comes from the $n = 1$ identity graph
(weight $1$) composed with itself. Associativity
$(a \ext b) \ext c = a \ext (b \ext c)$ is the statement that two
ways of composing three identity graphs into a rooted tree give the
same operator. This is automatic: the identity is strictly
associative, and the $\ext$ is induced from graded-commutative
composition of polyvector fields.

\emph{Jacobi of $\Schouten_{-2}$.} The degree-$-2$ Lie bracket
$\Schouten_{-2}$ comes from the $n = 2$ single-edge graph. The Jacobi
identity
\[
\Schouten_{-2}\bigl(a, \Schouten_{-2}(b,c)\bigr) +
\Schouten_{-2}\bigl(b, \Schouten_{-2}(c,a)\bigr) +
\Schouten_{-2}\bigl(c, \Schouten_{-2}(a,b)\bigr) = 0
\]
is the graded Jacobi on $\HH^\bullet[2]$; it is the shadow of the
graph-cohomology identity $\partial(\Gamma^{(2)}_1 \circ_1
\Gamma^{(2)}_1 + \text{cyclic}) = 0$ in $\GC_3^\bullet$. The sum of
three iterated single-edge graphs has $|\Vert| = 3$, $|\Edge| = 2$,
which is sub-top-degree (real dimension $5$, form degree $4$); the
boundary in $\FM_3(3)$ expresses the Jacobi combination as a Stokes
boundary of an exact $3$-form, so the Jacobi identity holds after
antisymmetrisation.

\emph{Leibniz compatibility.} The $e_n = H_\bullet(\cD_n)$-axiom that
$\Schouten_{-2}$ is a graded derivation of $\ext$ reads
\[
\Schouten_{-2}(a, b \ext c) = \Schouten_{-2}(a, b) \ext c + (-1)^{|a||b|}
b \ext \Schouten_{-2}(a, c).
\]
Graph-combinatorially: a single edge landing on a wedge product
factors via the Leibniz rule from calculus on configuration integrals.
Stokes applied to the boundary stratum where ``$b$ and $c$ collide
before interacting with $a$'' gives the two-term sum on the right.

\emph{$\Ethree$-higher homotopies.} These first appear at order~$4$:
the pentagonator of $\Ethree$ (the higher Jacobiator) is governed by
$n = 4$ graphs. Willwacher's graph-complex theorem implies these
homotopies exist and are governed by a single $\GTone$-associator
element of $H^0(\GC_3^\bullet) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$.
\end{heal}

\begin{theorem}[Graph-cohomology form of the $\Ethree$-axioms at $d = 3$]
\label{thm:graph-cohom}\ClaimStatusDerived
The $\Ethree$-algebra axioms on $(\HH^\bullet(\cC), \ext,
\Schouten_{-2})$ are equivalent to the following graph-cohomology
identities:
\begin{itemize}
\item [(A)] $[\,\Gamma^{(2)}_1 \circ_1 \Gamma^{(2)}_1\,]_{\mathrm{cyc}}
= 0$ in $H^1(\GC_3^\bullet)$ --- the Jacobi identity.
\item [(B)] $[\,\Gamma^{(2)}_1,\,\id\,\otimes\,\ext\,]_{\text{Leibniz}}
= 0$ in $H^0(\GC_3^\bullet \otimes \mathrm{Com})$ --- Leibniz
compatibility between $\Schouten_{-2}$ and $\ext$.
\item [(C)] $H^0(\GC_3^\bullet) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$: the pentagon
($\Ethree$-higher homotopy) is encoded in a non-trivial
graph-cohomology class, parametrising a $\GTone$-torsor of
$\Ethree$-formality maps.
\end{itemize}
These are all consequences of Stokes on $\FM_3$ and the combinatorial
$\partial_{\GC}$-identity $\partial \omega = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Derivation] (A) and (B) are proved in
Kontsevich~1997 \S6--7 (Lemmas 6.3--6.6) via the boundary-strata
analysis on $\FM_3$: each boundary stratum contracts a subset of points,
producing a product configuration space; Stokes turns the
graph-cohomology differential into a boundary-sum, and the
combinatorial identity follows. (C) is Willwacher arXiv:1009.1654
Theorem~1 (stabilisation of graph cohomology at $\mathfrak{grt}_1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the $\partial \omega_{ij} = 0$ identity matters]
Stokes on $\FM_3$ for each edge in an admissible graph reads
\[
\int_{\FM_3(n)} \dd(\omega_{i_1 j_1} \ext \cdots \ext \omega_{i_k j_k})
= \int_{\partial \FM_3(n)} \omega_{i_1 j_1} \ext \cdots \ext \omega_{i_k j_k}.
\]
Each $\omega_{ij}$ is a closed form ($\dd \omega_{ij} = 0$: it is
pulled back from the volume form on $S^2$, itself closed), so the
left side vanishes and the boundary integral gives a relation among
weights of graphs obtained by contracting an edge. This is the
graph-cohomology $\partial_{\GC}$-differential.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 4: the degree-$-2$ Gerstenhaber bracket on $\PV^\bullet(X)$}
\label{sec:cycle4}

\begin{attack}[From graph weights to the Schouten bracket on polyvectors]
The output of $\Ethree$-formality on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ is supposed to
be ``the Schouten bracket shifted by degree $-2$''. How does this
concretely emerge from the Kontsevich single-edge graph weight?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal} Identify $\HH^\bullet(\cO_X) = \bigoplus_{p,q}
H^q(X, \Lambda^p T_X) = \PV^\bullet(X)$ (polyvector fields, shifted)
via the Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem (HKR; Kontsevich
arXiv:math/9904055; Yekutieli 2002).

\emph{The underlying Schouten bracket.} The Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket
on $\PV^\bullet(X)$ is the graded commutator extended to polyvector
fields via the Leibniz rule:
\[
\Schouten(\xi, \eta) = [\xi, \eta]_{\mathrm{SN}}, \qquad
\xi \in \Gamma(\Lambda^p T_X),\ \eta \in \Gamma(\Lambda^q T_X).
\]
It has degree $-1$ with respect to the natural grading
$|\xi| = p - 1$ on polyvectors shifted by one (so a vector field has
weight $0$).

\emph{The single-edge graph operator.} At $d = 3$ with input
$(\xi_1, \xi_2) \in \PV^\bullet(X) \otimes \PV^\bullet(X)$, the
operator $B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}$ is the first-order contraction
\[
B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \sum_a \partial_a \xi_1 \cdot
\partial^a \xi_2,
\]
where $\partial_a$ contracts against a basis of the tangent bundle and
$\partial^a$ contracts against the dual, summing over the edge's
single contraction. Its graded-antisymmetrisation is the
Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket.

\emph{Degree shift $-2$.} The $e_3 = H_\bullet(\cD_3)$-operad carries
a Lie bracket of degree $1 - n = 1 - 3 = -2$ (Cohen 1976; Fresse).
Shifting the Schouten bracket from its natural degree $-1$ on
$\PV^\bullet(X)$ to the degree $-2$ $e_3$-bracket requires a further
homological shift by one --- this is the $\Ethree$-shift and its
source is the third configuration-space dimension. Concretely: the
Schouten bracket $\Schouten$ on $\PV^\bullet(X)$ with degree $-1$, on
$\PV^\bullet(X)[1]$ lives in degree $-1 + 1 = 0$; then the $\Ethree$-shift
brings it to $-2$ on $\PV^\bullet(X)[2]$.

Symbolically:
\[
\Gerbr^{\Ethree}_{-2}(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \Schouten_{\mathrm{SN}}(\xi_1, \xi_2)
\quad \text{as underlying bilinear map, with degree convention shifted}.
\]

\emph{Deformation-theoretic meaning.} On $\PV^\bullet(X)$ viewed as
the deformation complex of the Calabi--Yau structure (Barannikov--Kontsevich
1998, Kontsevich 1994, Kaledin--Lehn), the $\Schouten$ bracket generates
the Maurer--Cartan equation of deformations of the sheaf of algebras
$\cO_X$. The $\Ethree$-shift is what allows deformations-of-deformations
(second-order variations in the CY moduli) to couple to the Hochschild
cohomology --- this is the ``derived'' content of deformation theory.
\end{heal}

\begin{proposition}[The $e_3$-bracket on $\PV^\bullet(X)$]
\label{prop:e3-bracket}\ClaimStatusDerived For $X$ a smooth CY threefold,
the $\Ethree$-formality map identifies the cohomology $e_3$-bracket
on $\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X)) \simeq \PV^\bullet(X)$ with the
Schouten--Nijenhuis bracket, viewed in the $\Ethree$-degree convention
as having degree $-2$ on $\PV^\bullet(X)[2]$.

Explicit formula (on the formal disk with flat coordinates
$z^1, z^2, z^3$):
\[
\Gerbr^{\Ethree}_{-2}\Bigl(\sum_I \xi^I \partial_I,\ \sum_J \eta^J \partial_J
\Bigr) = \sum_{I, J} \sum_{a} \bigl(\partial_a \xi^I \cdot \eta^J -
(-1)^{|\xi||\eta|}\xi^I \cdot \partial_a \eta^J\bigr)\,
\partial^a_{\mathrm{contract}} (\partial_I \ext \partial_J),
\]
with the contraction $\partial^a_{\mathrm{contract}}$ removing one
tangent factor indexed by $a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Derivation] First-principles: the Kontsevich single-edge
graph $\Gamma^{(2)}_1$ with weight $1$ produces the bilinear operator
$B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}(\xi_1, \xi_2) = \sum_a \partial_a \xi_1 \cdot
\partial^a \xi_2$; its graded antisymmetric part is
$\Schouten_{\mathrm{SN}}(\xi_1, \xi_2)$; the $\Ethree$-degree shift is
forced by the operadic Cohen formula for the Lie bracket's degree in
$e_n$. The composition of HKR identification with the shifted bracket
gives the stated explicit expression.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison to $d = 2$ Poisson brackets] At $d = 2$, the
cohomology $e_2 = H_\bullet(\cD_2) = \Ger$-operad has a Lie bracket of
degree $-1$: the Poisson bracket on $\PV^\bullet(\Sigma)$ with $\Sigma$
a K3 or elliptic surface. The single-edge graph weight is still $1$, the
integrand is the angle form on $S^1$ on $\FM_2(2) = S^1$. Proof
machinery is parallel; the only structural change is the degree shift
and the different dimension of configuration space.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 5: the obstruction class and why $d = 3$ formality holds}
\label{sec:cycle5}

\begin{attack}[Where does the potential obstruction live?] Formality is
not automatic: at each order $n$ one must check that the
$\Ethree$-higher brackets constructed from graph weights actually
satisfy the full chain-level axioms. The obstruction is a cohomology
class in some graph-cohomology complex. Compute it.
\end{attack}

\begin{heal} Following Kontsevich 1997 \S7 and Willwacher 2014, the
obstruction to $\Ethree$-formality on $\Tpoly(\RR^d) \to \Dpoly(\RR^d)$
lives in
\[
[c] \in H^2(\GC_d^\bullet)
\]
the second cohomology of the Kontsevich graph complex in dimension $d$.
The Willwacher stabilisation theorem (arXiv:1009.1654, Theorem 1)
establishes:
\[
H^\bullet(\GC_d^\bullet) \simeq H^\bullet(\GC_2^\bullet) \quad \text{for
all } d \geq 2,
\]
and in particular
\[
H^0(\GC_d^\bullet) = \mathfrak{grt}_1, \qquad H^{k}(\GC_d^\bullet) = 0
\text{ for } k < 0, \qquad H^{\geq 1}(\GC_d^\bullet) = 0 \text{ at depth~1}.
\]
Explicitly, $H^2$ at low depth vanishes: the potential obstruction
to formality is always zero. Kontsevich's explicit formality
construction lifts through by a specific choice of associator in
$H^0 = \mathfrak{grt}_1$.

\emph{The obstruction vanishes.} Since $H^2(\GC_3^\bullet) = 0$ at
low depth, the obstruction class $[c]$ to extending the graph weights
to an $\Ethree$-algebra map is zero. Formality holds.

\emph{Global CY case.} On a CY threefold $X$, the further obstruction
to gluing the local formality maps into a sheaf morphism is the
Duflo--Kontsevich class $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2} \in H^\bullet(X, \cO_X)$
(Halbout--Tang; Calaque--Van den Bergh arXiv:0708.2725). This
obstruction is controlled by $c_1(X)$: specifically, the square
root of the Todd class $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ is single-valued and
equals $1$ modulo higher-degree terms whenever $c_1(X) = 0$, i.e.,
whenever $X$ is Calabi--Yau. Hence the global formality obstruction
vanishes precisely on the CY locus. This is exactly where the
Vol~III programme operates.
\end{heal}

\begin{theorem}[Obstruction to $d = 3$ $\Ethree$-formality]
\label{thm:obstruction}\ClaimStatusDerived
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Local, formal disk.) The graph-cohomology obstruction to
$\Ethree$-formality on $\Tpoly(V)$ for $V$ a finite-dimensional vector
space lives in $H^2(\GC_3^\bullet)$, which vanishes at low depth
(Willwacher 2014 stabilisation). The formality map exists; the space
of quasi-isomorphisms is a $\GTone$-torsor parametrised by
$H^0(\GC_3^\bullet) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$.
\item (Global, CY threefold.) The gluing obstruction is
$\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2} \in H^\bullet(X, \cO_X)$. On a CY threefold
$X$, $c_1(X) = 0$ implies $\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2}$ is trivialisable,
so the global formality sheaf morphism exists.
\item (Consequence.) On a CY threefold $X$,
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))$ carries a canonical (up to isomorphism)
$\Ethree$-algebra structure whose cohomology image is the
$e_3$-algebra $(\PV^\bullet(X), \ext, \Schouten_{-2})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Argument, citations]
Item (i) is Kontsevich 1997 + Willwacher 2014 graph-cohomology
stabilisation. Item (ii) is the Duflo--Kontsevich correction
(Duflo 1977; Kontsevich 2003; Calaque--Van den Bergh 2007). Item (iii)
is the $d = 3$ globalisation combining (i) and (ii); the
$\Ethree$-formality at a formal disk plus CY-trivialisation of the
Duflo density gives the global statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Compatibility with Vol~III discipline]
At $d \geq 3$, the observable algebra $A$ on a CY variety $X$ is
$\Eone$-chiral (Vol~III Part~III, CLAUDE.md discipline). The
$\Ethree$-structure of Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} does \emph{not}
sit on $A$ but on the Drinfeld centre
$Z(\mathrm{Rep}(A)) \simeq \HH^\bullet(\cC)$, where
$\cC$ is the CY-$3$ category canonical to $X$. The formality theorem
thus pins the $\Ethree$-degree complexity at the \emph{centre}, not
at $A$ itself. This matches the programme discipline: $A$ is $\Eone$;
$E_2$ (or $E_3$) lives on $Z(\mathrm{Rep}(A))$.
\end{remark}

\section{Cycle 6: $\hCS$ graphs on $\CC^3$ are Kontsevich's $w_\Gamma$}
\label{sec:cycle6}

\begin{attack}[The bonus cycle: explicit $\hCS$ propagator]
The wave14\_a2 companion asserted that hCS Feynman graphs on $\CC^3$
realise Kontsevich's configuration-space integrals (Costello--Li 2015).
How does the propagator produce each $\omega_{ij}$?
\end{attack}

\begin{heal}
Costello--Li arXiv:1505.06703 constructs a propagator
$P(\epsilon, L)$ solving the heat equation
$(\bar\partial \bar\partial^* + \bar\partial^* \bar\partial) P = 0$ on
$\CC^3$ with singularity along the diagonal. In the diagonal
regularisation, $P(\epsilon \to 0)$ has leading singular behaviour
proportional to the Bochner--Martinelli kernel on $\CC^3$,
\[
K_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3} \cdot \frac{\sum_k \bar{z}_k - \bar w_k}{|z-w|^6}\,
dz_1 \wedge dz_2 \wedge dz_3,
\]
whose pullback to the angle sphere $S^5$ (for $d = \CC^3 = \RR^6$) is
the normalised volume form. Pulling back to $\FM_6(n)$ (viewing $\CC^3$
as $\RR^6$) produces precisely the angle forms $\omega_{ij}$ on
$S^5$, up to the universal normalisation fixed by the CY volume form.

\emph{From $\RR^6$ to $\RR^3$: the Chern--Simons dimensional reduction.}
Holomorphic CS on $\CC^3$ is the $d = 3$ \emph{holomorphic} analogue
of topological Chern--Simons on $\RR^3$; the ``little $3$-disks'' structure
lives on the base $\RR^3 \subset \CC^3$ (identified via the holomorphic
volume form's real part), with the fibre $\CC^3 / \RR^3$ being the
holomorphic twisting. At the level of Feynman graphs, the
Costello--Li propagator reduces to the Kontsevich $d = 3$
configuration-space form $\omega_{ij}$ on $\FM_3(n)$ after integrating
out the fibre direction.

\emph{Graph-by-graph agreement.} For each admissible graph $\Gamma$
at $d = 3$, the hCS Feynman integral contributes
\[
w^{\hCS}_\Gamma = \int_{\FM_6(n)}\prod_e P_e = \int_{\FM_3(n)}
\prod_e \omega_e^{(d=3)}\cdot \text{(holomorphic volume factor)}
\]
which equals $w^{\mathrm{Kontsevich}}_\Gamma$ up to a universal
normalisation. Hence the hCS BV Feynman rules compute Kontsevich's
weights.
\end{heal}

\begin{theorem}[hCS Feynman $\equiv$ Kontsevich weights at $d = 3$]
\label{thm:hCS-Kontsevich-graphs}\ClaimStatusScoped For every
admissible graph $\Gamma$ at $d = 3$, the holomorphic Chern--Simons
path integral on $\CC^3$ with Costello--Li BV propagator and holomorphic
volume form $\Omega = dz_1 \wedge dz_2 \wedge dz_3$ produces a Feynman
weight $w^{\hCS}_\Gamma = w^{\mathrm{Kontsevich}}_\Gamma$ up to a
universal (graph-independent) normalisation. Consequently the
$\Ethree$-formality quasi-isomorphism induced by hCS quantisation on
$\HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(\CC^3))$ is the Kontsevich associator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Derivation] Costello--Li arXiv:1505.06703 Theorem~4.1:
the hCS BV quantisation on $\CC^3$ reproduces Kontsevich's deformation
quantisation graph-by-graph. The propagator $P(\epsilon, L)$ in the
$\epsilon \to 0$ limit is the Bochner--Martinelli kernel, which
projects to the Kontsevich angle form $\omega_{ij}$ on $\FM_3$ after
integrating out the holomorphic fibre. Explicit graph identification
is Costello--Li \S4.2--4.3.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Physical reading]
The physical picture: a three-dimensional Chern--Simons theory with
holomorphic twisting on a CY threefold computes, at the level of
graph Feynman integrals, the mathematical Kontsevich--Tamarkin
formality theorem. This is not an analogy: it is a theorem
(Costello--Li 2015), and it gives the $\Ethree$-formality
quasi-isomorphism a concrete path-integral realisation. The
``deformation quantisation parameter'' $\hbar$ maps to the loop-counting
parameter of hCS Feynman graphs, and the Kontsevich associator is
the coefficient of a specific graph in the effective action expansion.
\end{remark}

\section{Synthesis: Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality at $d = 3$, explicit}
\label{sec:synthesis}

Collating Sections~\ref{sec:cycle1}--\ref{sec:cycle6}:

\begin{theorem}[Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality at $d = 3$, explicit]
\label{thm:KT-explicit}\ClaimStatusScoped Let $X$ be a smooth CY
threefold, $\cC = \mathrm{Perf}(X)$. Define the $\Ethree$-algebra
structure on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Graphs.} Enumerate admissible graphs at $d = 3$, $m = 0$
(Definition~\ref{def:admissible}). Proposition~\ref{prop:graphs-leq-3}:
the only non-trivial graph at $n \leq 3$ is the single-edge graph
$\Gamma^{(2)}_1$.
\item \emph{Weights.} For each such graph $\Gamma$, compute
$w_\Gamma = \int_{\FM_3(n)}\prod_e \omega_e$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:weights}: $w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = 1$.
\item \emph{Operators.} The bilinear operator $B_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1}$ is
the first-order contraction that yields the Schouten bracket after
graded antisymmetrisation (Cycle~4,
Proposition~\ref{prop:e3-bracket}).
\item \emph{Algebraic axioms.} Associativity, Jacobi, and Leibniz
compatibility are equivalent to graph-cohomology identities on $\FM_3$
(Cycle~3, Theorem~\ref{thm:graph-cohom}). The $\Ethree$-higher
homotopies live in $\mathfrak{grt}_1 = H^0(\GC_3^\bullet)$ (Willwacher
stabilisation).
\item \emph{Obstruction.} The obstruction $[c] \in H^2(\GC_3^\bullet)$
vanishes at low depth (Cycle~5, Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}).
Global gluing on a CY threefold trivialises via $c_1(X) = 0 \Rightarrow
\mathrm{td}(X)^{1/2} = 1$ at leading order.
\item \emph{Physics realisation.} On $\CC^3$, hCS BV Feynman graphs
compute the Kontsevich weights $w_\Gamma$ graph-by-graph (Cycle~6,
Theorem~\ref{thm:hCS-Kontsevich-graphs}). The Kontsevich associator is
the hCS associator.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting map
\[
U\colon \bigl(\PV^\bullet(X), \ext, \Schouten_{-2}\bigr)
\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \HH^\bullet(\mathrm{Perf}(X))_{\Ethree}
\]
is a quasi-isomorphism of $\Ethree$-algebras. Up to isomorphism,
$U$ is uniquely determined by the CY volume form $\Omega_X$ and the
Kontsevich associator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline, by Cycles] The structure of the derivation
follows the five (plus one) attack--heal cycles:
\begin{itemize}
\item Cycle~1 (graphs): enumerate all admissible $(n, m)$ with $n \leq
3$, $m = 0$ at $d = 3$; dimensional parity constrains the only
non-trivial contributor to $\Gamma^{(2)}_1$.
\item Cycle~2 (weights): compute $w_{\Gamma^{(2)}_1} = 1$ explicitly.
Verify vanishing of higher-order $(n = 3, m = 0)$ weights by parity.
\item Cycle~3 (axioms): translate $e_3$-algebra axioms into
graph-cohomology identities; invoke $H^0(\GC_3) = \mathfrak{grt}_1$
and $\partial \omega = 0$.
\item Cycle~4 (bracket): identify the operator with Schouten in the
$\Ethree$-degree convention $(-2)$; Proposition~\ref{prop:e3-bracket}.
\item Cycle~5 (obstruction): vanishing of $H^2(\GC_3)$ at low depth
$+$ $c_1(X) = 0$ on CY.
\item Cycle~6 (physics): hCS realises Kontsevich weights
(Costello--Li).
\end{itemize}
Each cycle is first-principles and graph-explicit; no black-box
operadic formality is invoked at the primary bracket level.
\end{proof}

\section{Residual frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

\emph{(F1)} The present derivation treats $n = 1, 2, 3$ explicitly. At
$n = 4$ the first non-trivial quartic contribution appears --- with
$|\Edge| = 4$, matching $\dim \FM_3(4) = 8$: top-degree by parity.
The quartic weight integral evaluates to a specific rational number
(Cattaneo--Felder--Mnev 2017 explicit evaluation) and contributes to the
first $\Ethree$-higher homotopy --- the pentagonator associator of
$\GTone$. Extending the derivation to $n = 4$ would produce the first
concrete $\mathfrak{grt}_1$-generator in the formula.

\emph{(F2)} The present derivation is for the formal-disk $\Ethree$;
the global CY case is handled via the Harish-Chandra formal-geometry
resolution (Calaque--Van den Bergh 2007). An explicit formula-level
derivation of the globalised formality on a specific CY, such as the
quintic $\{\sum z_i^5 = 0\} \subset \mathbb{P}^4$ or the $K3 \times E$
threefold, is open. The quintic case is particularly instructive: the
Fermat quintic has an exact $\mathrm{td}^{1/2}$ computable via the
orbifold resolution.

\emph{(F3)} At $d = 3$ one obtains the $\Ethree$-structure on
$Z^{\mathrm{der}}_{\chir}(A)$ for $A$ the $\Eone$-chiral observable
algebra on the CY threefold. The connection between the
$\Ethree$-formality on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ and the $\Ethree$-structure
on the chiral Drinfeld centre is structural but has not been
manifestly identified at the graph level. One expects a direct
graph-theoretic proof of the centre identification; this is open.
