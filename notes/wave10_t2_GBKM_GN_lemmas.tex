\section*{Reflection Data for $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delfive}$}

\paragraph{The rank three lattice.}
Work in
\[
  \Lat^{2,1}
  =
  \Z f_2\oplus \Z f_3\oplus \Z f_{-2},
  \qquad
  (f_2,f_{-2})=-1,\quad (f_3,f_3)=2,
\]
with $f_2$ and $f_{-2}$ isotropic and all other pairings zero. For
$x=mf_2+\ell f_3+nf_{-2}$,
\[
  (x,x)=2\ell^2-2mn.
\]
The three Type II simple roots are
\[
  \delta_1=2f_2-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_2=2f_{-2}-f_3,\qquad
  \delta_3=f_3 .
\]
They have Gram matrix
\[
  ((\delta_i,\delta_j))_{i,j=1}^3
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2&-2&-2\\
    -2&2&-2\\
    -2&-2&2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det=-32,
\]
with signature $(2,1)$.

\paragraph{Type I and Type II reflection lattices.}
A primitive square-$2$ vector $\alpha$ is Type I if
$(\alpha,\Lat^{2,1})=\Z$ and Type II if
$(\alpha,\Lat^{2,1})=2\Z$. Direct pairing gives
\[
  \LII
  =
  \{mf_2+\ell f_3+nf_{-2}:m\equiv n\equiv0\pmod2\},
\]
and
\[
  \LI
  =
  \{mf_2+\ell f_3+nf_{-2}:m+\ell+n\equiv0\pmod2\}.
\]
The $f_3$ coordinate is unconstrained in $\LII$ because
$(\ell f_3,f_3)=2\ell$ is already even. Hence $\delta_3=f_3$ lies in
$\LII$.

\paragraph{The $S_3$ chamber symmetry.}
The reflections in $f_2-f_3$ and $f_{-2}-f_2$ act on the ordered triple
$(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)$ by
\[
  s_{f_2-f_3}:(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)
  \mapsto(\delta_3,\delta_2,\delta_1),
\]
\[
  s_{f_{-2}-f_2}:(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)
  \mapsto(\delta_2,\delta_1,\delta_3).
\]
They generate $\operatorname{Aut}(\cP_{\mathrm{II,prim}})\cong S_3$.
The three null vertices
\[
  2f_2,\qquad 2f_{-2},\qquad 2(f_2-f_3+f_{-2})
\]
form one $S_3$ orbit.

\paragraph{Maass character and Igusa sign.}
Lemma 3 of Gritsenko and Nikulin gives two equivalent readings of the
same Maass character. For the Type I decomposition,
\[
  \Delfive(waz)=\det(a)\Delfive(z),
  \qquad
  w\in W^{(2)}(\LI),\quad
  a\in\operatorname{Aut}(\cP_{\mathrm{I,prim}}).
\]
For the Type II decomposition,
\[
  \Delfive(waz)=\det(w)\Delfive(z),
  \qquad
  w\in W^{(2)}(\LII),\quad
  a\in\operatorname{Aut}(\cP_{\mathrm{II,prim}}).
\]
Thus $\Delfive$ is anti-invariant under the three Type II simple
reflections and invariant under the Type I reflection subgroup. The
sign change records the Levi factor in the reflection decomposition.
The automorphic form is unchanged.

\paragraph{Weyl vector.}
The Weyl vector is determined by
$(\rho,\delta_i)=-1$ for $i=1,2,3$. Solving the linear system gives
\[
  \rho
  =
  {1\over2}(\delta_1+\delta_2+\delta_3)
  =
  f_2-\frac12 f_3+f_{-2}.
\]
Indeed $(\rho,\delta_i)=-1$ for each $i$, and
$(\rho,\rho)=-3/2$.

\paragraph{Denominator.}
Borcherds' product theorem and the Gritsenko and Nikulin construction give
the monic denominator section
\[
  \operatorname{den}(\mathfrak g_{\Delfive})(Z)
  =
  64^{-1}\Delfive(2Z).
\]
Equivalently, on the positive cone of $\LII$,
\[
  64^{-1}\Delfive(2Z)
  =
  e^{-2\pi i(\rho,Z)}
  \prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}
  \left(1-e^{-2\pi i(\alpha,Z)}\right)^{
    \operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\alpha}}.
\]
The exponent is the signed denominator exponent
$\operatorname{sdim}\mathfrak g_{\alpha}=
  \dim\mathfrak g_{\alpha,\bar0}-\dim\mathfrak g_{\alpha,\bar1}$.
A fixed parity decomposition converts it to an ordinary root
multiplicity. The signed exponents are read from the
Eichler and Zagier coefficients of $\phioo$ by the discriminant
$4nm-\ell^2$. Since the input constant is $c(0)=10$,
\[
  \kBKM(\Delfive)=\operatorname{wt}(\Delfive)=c(0)/2=5 .
\]

\paragraph{Primitive CHL constants.}
For the primitive CHL denominators $F_N^{\mathrm{CHL}}$ attached to the
$g_N$-averaged K3 elliptic genus, with
$N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, the input constants and Borcherds weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
N & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 6\\
\hline
c_N(0) & 10 & 8 & 6 & 4 & 2\\
\kBKM(F_N^{\mathrm{CHL}})=c_N(0)/2 & 5 & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 .
\end{array}
\]
The equality is Borcherds' singular theta weight formula applied to the
weight zero index one Jacobi input. At $N=1$,
$F_1^{\mathrm{CHL}}=\Delfive$, and the scalar Igusa form is the square
\[
  \PhiTen=\Delfive^{\,2},\qquad
  \operatorname{wt}(\PhiTen)=10=2\kBKM(\Delfive).
\]
Thus the primitive BKM denominator has weight $5$, while the OP and DT
scalar partition form has weight $10$.

\paragraph{Gritsenko and Cl\'ery diagonal divisor catalogue.}
The diagonal divisor catalogue is indexed by triples $(t,N;k)$, not by
the CHL order alone. The complete eight row list is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(t,N;k) & F_{t,N} & \kBKM(F_{t,N})=\operatorname{wt}(F_{t,N}) & 2k\\
\hline
(1,1;5) & \Delfive & 5 & 10\\
(2,1;2) & \Delta_2 & 2 & 4\\
(1,2;3) & \nabla_3 & 3 & 6\\
(3,1;1) & \Delta_1 & 1 & 2\\
(1,3;2) & \nabla_2 & 2 & 4\\
(4,1;\tfrac12) & \Delta_{1/2} & \tfrac12 & 1\\
(1,4;\tfrac32) & \nabla_{3/2} & \tfrac32 & 3\\
(2,2;1) & Q_1 & 1 & 2 .
\end{array}
\]
The tuple of weights is
\[
  (5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1),
\]
and the twice weight tuple is
\[
  (10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
At single cusp rows this twice weight is the zeroth Borcherds input
coefficient. At multi cusp rows it is the corresponding cusp weighted
sum in Gritsenko and Cl\'ery Theorem 3.1. The six integer weight rows
live on paramodular subgroups and their normal extensions. The rows
$\Delta_{1/2}$ and $\nabla_{3/2}$ are half integral weight Siegel
modular forms with multiplier systems of orders $8$ and $4$. The
weight set is exactly
\[
  \{5,3,2,1,\tfrac12,\tfrac32\}.
\]

\paragraph{Separation of the two automorphic scopes.}
The CHL tuple
\[
  (c_N(0))_{N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}}=(10,8,6,4,2)
\]
and the Gritsenko and Cl\'ery tuple
\[
  (2\operatorname{wt} F_{t,N})=(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2)
\]
have intersection the single row $\Delfive$. Any comparison between the
CHL entries $N=2,3,4$ and the Gritsenko and Cl\'ery rows $(1,N)$ needs a
named row map, a common group, a common multiplier, and the same
Borcherds input.

\paragraph{The $K3\times E$ invariant package.}
Let $X=K3\times E$ as a compact CY threefold. Kunneth gives
\[
  h^{0,\bullet}(X)=(1,1,1,1),
\]
so the compact Hodge supertrace is
\[
  \kch(X)=\sum_{q=0}^{3}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)=1-1+1-1=0.
\]
The categorical Euler invariant is also a total space invariant:
\[
  \kcat(K3\times E)
  =
  \chi(\mathcal O_{K3})\chi(\mathcal O_E)
  =
  2\cdot 0
  =
  0.
\]
The Heisenberg specialization is additive instead:
\[
  \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)
  =
  \kch(K3)+\kch(E)
  =
  2+1
  =
  3.
\]
The Borcherds denominator and the K3 fibre lattice give the remaining
two entries
\[
  \kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delfive})=5,\qquad
  \kfib(K3\times E)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=1+22+1=24.
\]
The four construction values for $K3\times E$ are therefore
\[
  \{\kcat,\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}},\kBKM,\kfib\}
  =
  \{0,3,5,24\}.
\]
The value $2$ is $\kcat(K3)=\chi(\mathcal O_{K3})$, a K3 fibre
categorical witness. The total space and fibre rank entries are
$\kcat(K3\times E)=0$ and $\kfib(K3\times E)=24$.

\paragraph{Normalization check.}
The denominator normalization
\[
  D_5=64^{-1}\Delfive,\qquad
  D_5^2=4096^{-1}\Delfive^2
\]
is compatible with the OP and DT scalar branch
$Z^{X}_{\mathrm{DT}}=-4096\,\Delfive^{-2}$. This scalar square fixes the
$N=1$ normalization. Comparisons between the primitive CHL ladder and the
Gritsenko and Cl\'ery catalogue beyond the common $\Delfive$ row require
row maps. Parity fixtures convert signed denominator exponents into
ordinary root multiplicities.
