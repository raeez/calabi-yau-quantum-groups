\section*{Level Six Data: Niemeier, Mathieu, and Borcherds Constants}

The level-six row contains four independent objects. The Niemeier entry
is a rank-$24$ positive-definite even unimodular lattice. The Mathieu
entry is a frame shape on the $24$-point permutation representation.
The cyclic quotient entry is a local Du Val stratification of
$K3/\langle g_6\rangle$. The Borcherds entry is the constant term of
the primitive CHL input Jacobi form.

The sources used here are Niemeier 1973 \emph{J. Number Theory}
\textbf{5}, 142-178; Mukai 1988 \emph{Invent. Math.} \textbf{94},
183-221, Theorem 0.1 and Table 2; Kondo 1998 \emph{Duke Math. J.}
\textbf{92}, 593-603, Theorem 1; Cheng, Duncan, and Harvey 2014
\texttt{arXiv:1307.5793}; Gritsenko and Nikulin 1995 Part II,
Theorem 2.1; Gritsenko 1999, Corollary 3.3; Gritsenko and Clery 2008
\texttt{arXiv:0812.3962}, Theorems 1.4 and 3.1; and Govindarajan and
Krishna 2010 \emph{JHEP} 05, 014, Table 1.

\subsection*{Niemeier Lattice Data}

Niemeier's list has two Coxeter-number-six entries relevant to the
level-six comparison:
\[
N_{6D_4}\colon R(N_{6D_4}) = D_4^{\oplus 6}, \qquad
N_{A_5^4D_4}\colon R(N_{A_5^4D_4}) = A_5^{\oplus 4}\oplus D_4 .
\]
The umbral level-six anchor is \(N_{6D_4}\). Its Coxeter number is
\(h = 6\), its glue code is an \(\mathbb{F}_3\)-linear code on the six
\(D_4\)-blocks, and its umbral group is
\[
G^{(6D_4)}=3.\mathrm{Sym}_6,\qquad |G^{(6D_4)}|=2160 .
\]
The lattice \(N_{A_5^4D_4}\) has the same Coxeter number and belongs to
a different Niemeier entry, with outer automorphism group of order
\(144\). The level-six umbral character attached to \(H^{(6)}\) pairs
with the \(6D_4\) glue code and the character
\(\chi_{6D_4}\colon 3.\mathrm{Sym}_6\to\C^\times\).

\subsection*{Mathieu Frame-Shape Data}

The Mathieu datum for the symplectic order-six class is the class \(6A\)
with frame shape
\[
\pi_{6A}=1^2\,2^2\,3^2\,6^2 .
\]
Its permutation dimension is
\[
1\cdot 2+2\cdot 2+3\cdot 2+6\cdot 2=24.
\]
The adjacent order-six Mathieu class \(6B\) has frame shape \(6^4\).
The primitive level-six CHL and umbral row uses the \(6A\) frame shape.
This is character-theoretic data on the \(24\)-point Mathieu
representation, separate from the Niemeier root system.

\subsection*{Cyclic Quotient Surface Data}

Let \(g_6\) be a symplectic automorphism of a K3 surface of order \(6\).
Nikulin's fixed-point counts are
\[
\#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6)=2,\qquad
\#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6^2)=6,\qquad
\#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6^3)=8 .
\]
The two points fixed by \(\langle g_6\rangle\cong\Z_6\) give two
\(A_5\) singularities. The four further points fixed by
\(\langle g_6^2\rangle\cong\Z_3\) form two \(\Z_6\)-orbits and give two
\(A_2\) singularities. The six further points fixed by
\(\langle g_6^3\rangle\cong\Z_2\) form two \(\Z_6\)-orbits and give two
\(A_1\) singularities. Hence
\[
K3/\langle g_6\rangle
\quad\hbox{has singularity type}\quad
2A_5+2A_2+2A_1 .
\]
The Euler check after crepant resolution is
\[
e(K3/\Z_6)=\frac{24+2+6+8+6+2}{6}=8,\qquad
8+(2\cdot5+2\cdot2+2\cdot1)=24 .
\]

Mukai's Table 2 gives the same cyclic order-six stratum in lattice
language: the coinvariant lattice has rank \(8\), the invariant Mukai
rank is
\[
r_6=22-8=14,
\]
and Kondo's refinement embeds the cyclic order-six datum into the
Niemeier lattice \(N_{6D_4}\). The rank \(r_6=14\) is fixed-lattice
data for the automorphism; it is separate from the compact \(K3\)-fibre
rank \(\kfib=24\).

\subsection*{Primitive CHL Borcherds Constants}

For the primitive CHL ladder \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\), the Borcherds input
constant terms are
\[
\bigl(c_1(0),c_2(0),c_3(0),c_4(0),c_6(0)\bigr)
=(10,8,6,4,2).
\]
Borcherds' weight theorem gives
\[
\bigl(\kBKM(\Phi_1),\kBKM(\Phi_2),\kBKM(\Phi_3),
\kBKM(\Phi_4),\kBKM(\Phi_6)\bigr)
=\frac12(10,8,6,4,2)=(5,4,3,2,1).
\]
At level six,
\[
c_6(0)=2,\qquad \kBKM(\Phi_6)=\frac{c_6(0)}2=1 .
\]
The \(N=6\) frame shape \(1^2 2^2 3^2 6^2\), the fixed-point count
\(\#\mathrm{Fix}(g_6)=2\), and the paramodular form
\(\Delta_1^{(6)}\) of weight \(1\) give the same value through three
independent normalisations.

\subsection*{Gritsenko and Clery dd Catalogue}

The Gritsenko and Clery dd-modular catalogue is a separate genus-two
classification. Its eight triples are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(1,1;5),\ (2,1;2),\ (1,2;3),\ (3,1;1),\\
(1,3;2),\ (4,1;\tfrac12),\ (1,4;\tfrac32),\ (2,2;1),
\end{gathered}
\]
with weights
\[
(5,2,3,1,2,\tfrac12,\tfrac32,1)
\]
and twice-weight tuple
\[
(10,4,6,2,4,1,3,2).
\]
The catalogue is indexed by diagonal-divisor paramodular data
\((t,N;k)\) and multiplier systems. The primitive CHL table is indexed
by orbifold levels \(N\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\). The two classifications share
the common entry \((1,1;5)=\Delta_5\); their level-six entries belong to
different indexing principles.

\subsection*{Compact \(K3\times E\) Invariants}

For the compact product \(K3\times E\), K\"unneth gives
\[
h^{0,\bullet}(K3\times E)=(1,1,1,1).
\]
Thus the compact Hodge supertrace and the categorical Euler are
\[
\kch(A_{K3\times E})
=\sum_{q=0}^{3}(-1)^q h^{0,q}(K3\times E)
=1-1+1-1=0,
\qquad
\kcat(K3\times E)=\chi(\cO_{K3\times E})=0 .
\]
The Heisenberg branch records the additive free-field rank
\[
\kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E)=2+1=3.
\]
The Borcherds denominator algebra attached to the untwined K3 elliptic
genus has
\[
\kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5})=\mathrm{wt}(\Delta_5)=5.
\]
The fibre rank is the full Mukai-lattice rank
\[
\kfib(K3)=\operatorname{rk}\widetilde H(K3,\Z)=24.
\]
Consequently
\[
\{\kch(A_{K3\times E}),\ \kch^{\mathrm{Heis}}(K3\times E),\
\kBKM(\mathfrak g_{\Delta_5}),\ \kfib(K3),\ \kcat(K3\times E)\}
=\{0,3,5,24,0\},
\]
with each value arising from its own construction.
