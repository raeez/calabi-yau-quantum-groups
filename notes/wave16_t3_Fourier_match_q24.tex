\section{Fourier match through $q^{24}$: the Heisenberg-on-Mukai identification}
\label{wn:sec:wave16-t3-Fourier-match-q24}

\paragraph{The identity to prove.}
At the Humbert surface $H_1 = \{\sigma = \tau\} \subset \mathcal{H}_2$,
Gritsenko--Nikulin's double zero of $\Phi_{10}$ along $\{z = 0\}$
produces the residue identity (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1996 Cor.~5.2; Borcherds
1995 Thm.~10.1; van der Geer \emph{Siegel modular forms} Lect.~6,
Eq.~(6.12))
\[
  \lim_{z \to 0}\,
  \frac{(2\pi i z)^{2}}{\Phi_{10}(\tau, z, \tau)}
  \;=\;
  -\,\frac{1}{\eta(\tau)^{48}}.
\]
Since $\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^{2}$ (Gritsenko--Nikulin 1996 Thm.~1.1),
this is the specialisation
\[
  \bigl[(2\pi i z)^{2}\,\Delta_5^{-2}\bigr]_{H_1,\,z=0}
  \;=\;
  -\,\eta(\tau)^{-48}
  \;=\;
  -\,q^{-2}\,\prod_{n \geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{-48}.
\]
Writing $g(\tau) := \sum_{n \geq 0} g_{n} q^{n}$ with
$[(2\pi i z)^{2}\,\Delta_5^{-2}]_{H_1,z=0} = -q^{-2}\,g(\tau)$, direct
computation of $\prod_{n \geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{-48}$ through $q^{24}$

\paragraph{The chiral side.} \ClaimStatusTheorem{}
The Mukai lattice $\Muk(K3) = H^{0} \oplus H^{2} \oplus H^{4}$ has
rank $24$, and the Heisenberg VOA $\Heis(\Muk(K3) \oplus \Muk(K3))$
on the K\"unneth sum has rank $48$ (cf.\ \texttt{chapters/theory/cy\_to\_chiral.tex}
line~421). Its vacuum character is the free-boson product
\[
  \chi_{\Heis(\Muk(K3)^{\oplus 2})}(q)
  \;=\;
  \prod_{n \geq 1} (1 - q^{n})^{-48}.
\]
No Kac--Shapovalov null vectors arise: Heisenberg Verma modules at
generic highest weight are irreducible (Frenkel--Ben-Zvi \emph{Vertex
algebras on algebraic curves} Prop.~3.5.1), and the vacuum lies in the
generic locus. Thus
\[
  \chi_{\Heis(\Muk(K3)^{\oplus 2})}(q) \;=\; g(\tau)
  \qquad \text{coefficient-by-coefficient, through all orders.}
\]
In particular the match holds through $q^{24}$ and onward, verified
explicitly at $g_{0}, \ldots, g_{24}$ above.

\paragraph{Retraction of the Wave-15 N3 iterated-DS identification.}
\ClaimStatusRetracted{} The ancestor note
\texttt{notes/wave15\_n3\_V24\_Delta5\_Fourier\_match.tex} asserted
\[
  \mathcal{V}_{24} \;:=\; \DS\!\bigl(L_{-2 + 1/22}(\sltwo)^{\otimes 22}\bigr),
  \qquad
  c_{\mathcal{V}_{24}} \;=\; -214,
  \qquad
  \chi_{\mathcal{V}_{24}} \stackrel{?}{=} \Delta_5^{-2}|_{H_1, z=0}.
\]
Three independent failures mark the iterated-DS identification:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Per-copy central charge.} At $k + 2 = 1/22$ the
$\mathcal{W}$-Virasoro quotient has
$c = 1 - 6(k+1)^{2}/(k+2) = 1 - 6(21/22)^{2}\cdot 22
     = 1 - 1323/11 = -1312/11$, not $-14432/121$ (the Wave-15 N3
arithmetic $6 \cdot 441 \cdot 22 / 484 = 58212/484$ simplifies to
$1323/11$, and $1 - 1323/11 = -1312/11$). Hence
$22 \cdot c_{\mathrm{per}} = -28{,}864/11$, which is not an integer and
is not $-214$.
\item \emph{Fourier coefficients.} Wave-15 N3 recorded
$(g_{0}, \ldots, g_{10}) = (1, 48, 1176, 19456, 247416, 2611200,$
$23914776, 195747392, 1455978072, 9985147200, 63699554496)$, but
direct computation of $\prod(1-q^{n})^{-48}$ gives
$(1, 48, 1224, 21952, 309876, 3657312, 37468928, 341773440,$
$2826752418, 21491641808, 151810235136)$. The sequences disagree
starting at $g_{2}$ (1176 vs.\ 1224; difference 48) and diverge
thereafter.
\item \emph{Kac--Shapovalov at $(2,45)$.} The Virasoro minimal model
$\mathrm{Vir}(2, 45)$ has central charge
$c_{2,45} = 1 - 6 \cdot 43^{2}/(2\cdot 45) = 1 - 1849/15 = -1834/15$,
not $-214 = -3210/15$. The $(2,45)$-minimal null-vector enumeration
invoked in Wave-15 N3 heals~(4) therefore does not apply to the
claimed chiral algebra.
\end{enumerate}
The iterated-DS identification fails; the $(2, 45)$-minimal apparatus
is a red herring; and the Wave-15 N3 coefficient table is not the
Fourier expansion of $\Delta_{5}^{-2}|_{H_1, z = 0}$.

\paragraph{What survives, sharpened.}
The true Humbert identity is the Heisenberg-on-Mukai match above, and
it holds to all orders in $q$ (not merely through $q^{10}$ or $q^{24}$):
\[
  \chi_{\Heis(\Muk(K3)^{\oplus 2})}(q)
  \;=\;
  \bigl[(2\pi i z)^{2}\,\Delta_{5}^{-2}\bigr]_{H_{1},\,z=0}
  \cdot (-q^{2}).
\]
The Wave-14/15 advertised ``Kac--Shapovalov null-vector truncation''
is unnecessary because the Heisenberg VOA is free-field: its character
is the pure partition generating function raised to the power $= $
rank $= 48 = 2 \cdot \mathrm{rk}(\Muk(K3))$, which is automatically
the Gritsenko--Nikulin residue.

\paragraph{Attack--heal cycles.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[(1)] \emph{Attack.} Is the Gritsenko--Nikulin residue formula
$[(2\pi i z)^{2}/\Phi_{10}]_{H_{1},z=0} = -\eta^{-48}$ correct with
the stated sign? \emph{Heal.} Gritsenko--Nikulin 1996 Cor.~5.2 and
van der Geer Lect.~6, Eq.~(6.12) both give this sign; an independent
check via Borcherds's $\Phi_{10}$ product
$\Phi_{10} = q\,\zeta\,p\prod(1 - q^{n}\zeta^{l}p^{m})^{c(4nm - l^{2})}$
with $c(D)$ from $2\phi_{0,1}$ (so $c(-1) = 2$, $c(0) = 20$) and
$\zeta$-Taylor expansion around $z = 0$ produces the same
$-\eta^{-48}$ leading term at $p = q$ after the $z^{2}$-stripping.
\item[(2)] \emph{Attack.} Do higher-order ($q^{11}$ through $q^{24}$)
coefficients of $\prod(1-q^{n})^{-48}$ admit an independent
partition-theoretic formula? \emph{Heal.} Yes: $p_{48}(n) =
\sum_{\lambda \vdash n}\prod_{i \geq 1}\binom{48 + m_{i}(\lambda) - 1}
{m_{i}(\lambda)}$ with $m_{i}(\lambda)$ the multiplicity of part $i$
in $\lambda$. Checked at $n = 24$: $g_{24} = 993{,}392{,}557{,}953{,}$
$227{,}803{,}294$, independent route.
\item[(3)] \emph{Attack.} Kac determinant at weight $h_{2, 45}$
through $q^{24}$: does it introduce null vectors that reduce the
effective rank from $48$? \emph{Heal.} No, because the chiral algebra
is Heisenberg, not Virasoro. The Kac determinant formula $\det M_{n} =
\prod_{rs \leq n}(h - h_{r,s}(c))^{p(n - rs)}$ is a Virasoro object;
the Heisenberg Shapovalov form is block-diagonal with each block the
Gram matrix of $\mathfrak{h}^{\otimes k}$, whose determinant is $1$
up to $\Z$-scalars (Kac 1990 Ch.~2 Prop.~2.6). No truncation at any $n$.
\item[(4)] \emph{Attack.} Does the Beem--Rastelli map
$c_{2d} = -12\,c_{4d}$ with $c_{4d} = 107/6$ giving $c_{2d} = -214$
survive, or does the Heisenberg identification force $c = 48$
(one per generator)? \emph{Heal.} Both hold in different lanes.
Heisenberg on an indefinite lattice has central charge equal to the
lattice rank $= 48$ in the standard normalisation
(Frenkel--Ben-Zvi Prop.~3.5.1). The Beem--Rastelli $-214$ is the
$\mathcal{T}_{24}$ superconformal-index central charge, a
small-$\mathcal{N} = 4$ quotient of a larger chiral algebra; the
vacuum characters of the two algebras differ by a specific
cohomological projection, and only the Heisenberg-on-Mukai lane
matches the $\Delta_{5}^{-2}|_{H_{1}}$ Fourier expansion.
\item[(5)] \emph{Attack.} Does the Humbert match identify with a
full $\mathcal{H}_{2}$ identity? \emph{Heal.} No; the Heisenberg
character $\chi_{\Heis}(q)$ has no $\sigma$-dependence, while
$\Phi_{10}^{-1}(\tau, z, \sigma)$ does. The match lives strictly on
$H_{1} \cap \{z = 0\}$ and in the
$(2\pi i z)^{2}$-residue-stripped sector. Extensions to Igusa $p$-adic
strata or to Siegel's $\mathcal{H}_{2}$ interior require the
full chiral index, not merely the vacuum character (cf.\ Beem--Rastelli
2014 Sec.~4).
\end{itemize}
