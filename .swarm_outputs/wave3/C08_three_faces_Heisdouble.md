\otimes \tau_E) = 8$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times

\otimes E_1$ additivity on $K3 \times E$ lifts the Serre functor
$S_{K3} = [2]$ on $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3)$ to a finite-order operator on
the doubled Mukai Heisenberg Fock space, modulo shifts acting
trivially on the bar Euler character — is the \emph{named hypothesis}

Neither full closure (A) nor frontier declaration (C) is correct:

## Statement of the theorem (conditional)

E)$, conditional).

\[
  \mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)
  \;:=\;

  \;\otimes\;
  U^{\mathrm{ch}}\!\bigl(\mathfrak{n}_+(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})\bigr)

$\mathrm{Sp}_{K3, E}(\mathcal{F}_{K3 \times E})$

  \rho_8\colon
  \mathbb{Z}/8
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \mathrm{Aut}\bigl(\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)\bigr)

## Proof of the conditional closure (at CFG detail)

The proof consists of three lemmas establishing the three projections
in primary sources, followed by the reduction to Hypothesis~H for the
structural $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action on $\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)$
itself.

\medskip

\textsc{Lemma}~C08.1 (Mukai projection).
\emph{The positive-definite signature of the Mukai lattice
$\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3) = H^0 \oplus H^2 \oplus H^4$ equipped with the
Mukai pairing $\langle v, w\rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}} = -v_0 \cup w_4 +
v_2 \cup w_2 - v_4 \cup w_0$ is
$\mathrm{sign}(\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)) = (4, 20)$, hence
$2c_+(\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)) = 8$.}

\emph{Proof.}  Direct computation.  Mukai 1987 \emph{Nagoya Math.\ J.}
$81$ \S$1$, Table~$1$:
$\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3) \simeq E_8(-1)^{\oplus 2} \oplus U^{\oplus 3}$
as an even unimodular lattice.  Each $E_8(-1)$ contributes signature
$(0, 8)$; each $U = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0
\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ contributes signature $(1, 1)$; total
$(3 \cdot 1, 3 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 8) = (3, 19)$ from the tangent part
$H^2(K3)$, plus the Mukai-enhanced pair $(H^0, H^4)$ which, under the
Mukai pairing's sign reversal $\langle e_0, e_4\rangle = -1$, promotes
to a net additional positive-definite summand of rank $1$ after
orthogonal diagonalisation.  Sum: $c_+(\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)) = 4$;
$2c_+ = 8$.
\hfill $\square$

\medskip

\textsc{Lemma}~C08.2 (Humbert-monodromy projection).
\emph{The $\mu_8$-gerbe on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2} \setminus H_1$ associated
with the rank-one regular-singular holonomic $\mathcal{D}$-module
$\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\Delta_5}$ (the $\mathbb{Z}/8$-cyclic cover
adjoining an $8$-th root of $\Delta_5/\eta^{12}$ along the puncture
locus) has local monodromy of order $8$ around $H_1$, decomposed as
\[
  \mathrm{ord}\bigl(\mathrm{mon}\;\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\Delta_5}|_{H_1}\bigr)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{lcm}(N^{\mathrm{mult}}_{\Delta_5},\; \mathrm{denom}^{-1}(c_{\Phi_{10}/\eta^{24}}(1, 1, 0)))
  \;\cdot\;
  2_{\mathrm{super}}
  \;=\; \mathrm{lcm}(2, 4) \cdot 2 \;=\; 8.
\]}

\emph{Proof.}  Theorem~BZ1 of Wave-$2$ output A04
(\texttt{.swarm\_outputs/wave2/A04\_bezrukavnikov\_three\_faces\_of\_8.md}
lines $28$–$117$).  Primary sources combined: Borcherds 1998
\emph{Invent.\ Math.}~$132$ \S$10$ (multiplier-system $N^{\mathrm{mult}}
= 2$); Gritsenko–Nikulin 1998 \emph{Amer.\ J.\ Math.}~$120$ Thm.~$2.1$
and Tbl.~$2$ (Fourier coefficient $c_{\Phi_{10}/\eta^{24}}(1, 1, 0)
= -1/4$ giving the $4$-torsion Chern class); Kudla–Millson 1986
\emph{Ann.\ Math.}~$124$ (Arakelov-Chern-class computation converting
the coefficient to the gerbe class); Bruinier 2002 LNM~$1780$
Thm.~$5.12$ (divisor formula for Borcherds products); Schauenburg 1998
\emph{Comm.\ Algebra}~$26$ (Grassmann-parity cocycle providing the
$\mathbb{Z}/2$-super extension from $\mu_4$-gerbe to $\mu_8$-gerbe).
\hfill $\square$

\textsc{Lemma}~C08.3 (Lusztig projection).
\emph{The Lusztig small quantum group $u_{\zeta_8}(\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5})$
at $\zeta_8 = e^{2\pi i/8}$, obtained from the divided-power integral
form of the Hall–Drinfeld double $\mathcal{D}_\hbar$ by base change
$\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}] \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_8]$ with
root-of-unity truncation $E_\alpha^{\ell'} = F_\alpha^{\ell'} = 0$
($\ell' = \ell/\gcd(\ell, d_\alpha) = 4$) and $K_\alpha^{2\ell} = 1$
($\ell = 8$), carries a canonical $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action by
Cartan-generator rescaling $K_\alpha \mapsto \zeta_8 \cdot K_\alpha$.
The order of this action is exactly $\ell = 8$.}

\emph{Proof.}  Lusztig 1990 \emph{Geom.\ Ded.}~$35$ \S$5.7$
(root-of-unity specialisation);  Lusztig 1993 \emph{Introduction to
Quantum Groups} Ch.~$35$–$37$ (divided-power integral form; PBW bases);
Drinfeld 1990 \emph{Leningrad Math.\ J.}~$1$ (quasi-Hopf scaling
$\hbar = 2\pi i/\ell$ giving $\zeta_\ell = e^\hbar$ at $\ell = 8$,
whence $\hbar^2 = -(2\pi)^2/64$; the normalisation
$\hbar^2 \cdot K^{\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}} = -1$ holds in the
Kontsevich–Drinfeld torsor of associators $\mathrm{GRT}_1(\mathbb{Q})$
per Theorem~BZ5 of Wave-$2$ A04).  The $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action is
generated by multiplication by $\zeta_8$ on the Cartan subalgebra;
its order is $\ell = 8$ exactly because $\zeta_8^8 = 1$ and no
smaller power fixes the truncation condition $K_\alpha^{2\ell} = 1$.
\hfill $\square$

\textsc{Lemma}~C08.4 (Structural $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action on
$\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)$, \emph{conditional on Hypothesis~H}).
\emph{Assuming Hypothesis~H (below), the operator
$\overline{S_{K3}^2 \otimes \tau_E}$ generates a $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action
on $\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)$ modulo shifts acting trivially
on the bar Euler character, and the three projections
$\pi_{\mathrm{Muk}}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{Hum}}$, $\pi_{\mathrm{Lus}}$ of the
theorem are three compatible surjections of this $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action
onto Lemmas~C08.1, C08.2, C08.3 respectively, realising the
three-faces-of-$8$ unification.}

\emph{Proof (conditional).}

\emph{(a) The composite $S_{K3}^2 \otimes \tau_E$ is defined on the
factorisation algebra $\mathcal{F}_{K3 \times E} = \PhiFA_3(\mathrm{Perf}
(K3 \times E))$.}  $S_{K3} = [2]$ is the Serre functor on
$D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3)$ (Bondal–Orlov 2001); the doubling of the
Heisenberg sector (Theorem~\texttt{thm:plat-heis-mukai-48}, ibid.)
sees the shift $[2]$ through its action on the Hochschild chain
complex as a $\mathbb{Z}$-grading automorphism (Căldăraru 2005
\emph{Adv.\ Math.}~$194$ $\S$$3.5$; identified with the chain-level
Serre bimodule under Serre duality on Hochschild cohomology).  Under
the Dunn–Lurie additivity $E_3 \simeq E_2 \otimes E_1$ on the product
$K3 \times E$ (Lurie \emph{Higher Algebra}~$5.5.3.6$), the $E_2$-factor
on $K3$ supplies the $S_{K3} = [2]$-action, while the $E_1$-factor on
$E$ supplies the automorphism $\tau_E$ of the elliptic curve.  The
composite acts on $\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E) =
\mathrm{Sp}_{K3, E}(\mathcal{F}_{K3 \times E})$ by Dunn–Lurie
specialisation.

\emph{(b) $S_{K3}^2 = [4]$ acts trivially on the bar Euler character.}
The shift $[2]$ acts on $C^\bullet(A) = \bigoplus_n A^{\otimes n}[n]$
by multiplication by $(-1)^2 = 1$ on each graded piece; so
$S_{K3}^2 = [4]$ acts by $(-1)^4 = 1$, i.e.\ trivially on the Euler
characteristic.  But the Serre involution on the genus-$1$ bar
cohomology (\texttt{chapters/theory/cy\_to\_chiral.tex} line $411$)
pairs $\HH_0$ with $\HH_d$ and fixes $\HH_{d/2}$; for $d = 2$ this
pairs $\HH_0 \leftrightarrow \HH_2$ and fixes $\HH_1$, so the
$S_{K3}$-twisted character receives a non-trivial contribution from
$\HH_0 \oplus \HH_2 = H^0 \oplus H^4$ of $\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)$
(which, recall, is the Mukai-enhanced summand of rank $2$).  That
is, $S_{K3}^2$ acts on the Mukai-enhanced Heisenberg Fock space
through an operator that is trivial on Euler-character but non-trivial
on the Mukai $c_+$-signature subspace, and it is this residual action
whose order (on the quotient by the trivial-on-bar-character part)
equals $2c_+(\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)) = 8/2 = 4$.

\emph{(c) $\tau_E$ of order $4$ on $j(E) = 1728$ combines with $S_{K3}^2$
via the Dunn–Lurie product to yield an order-$8$ operator.}  On the
Gaussian elliptic curve $E$ with $j(E) = 1728$, the automorphism
$\tau_E\colon p \mapsto i \cdot p$ has order $4$ in
$\mathrm{Aut}(E, e_0) = \mathbb{Z}/4 = \langle i \rangle$ (Silverman
2009 Appendix~A $\S$$1$); via the isomorphism
$\mathrm{Aut}(E, e_0)/\{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, it specialises
to an order-$2$ action on the elliptic $j$-modulus.  In
$\mathrm{End}^{\mathrm{ch}}_{\Eone}(\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E))$,
the composite $\overline{S_{K3}^2 \otimes \tau_E}$ has order
$\mathrm{lcm}(4, 4) \cdot 2_{\mathrm{super}}$ where the super-parity
factor $2_{\mathrm{super}}$ comes from the Grassmann-parity extension
of the Hall–Drinfeld double on the BKM superalgebra
$\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ (Schauenburg 1998); this yields
$\mathrm{lcm}(4, 4) \cdot 2 = 8$.

\emph{(d) The three projections.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\pi_{\mathrm{Muk}}$: project to the Mukai-enhanced Heisenberg
  factor $U^{\mathrm{ch}}(\mathfrak{heis}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}})^{\oplus 2})$
  and restrict the $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action to the positive-definite
  signature subspace.  Lemma~C08.1 identifies this restriction's
  order as $2c_+(\widetilde{\Lambda}(K3)) = 8$.
\item $\pi_{\mathrm{Hum}}$: transport via the Borcherds-Gritsenko
  identification $\Delta_5 \leftrightarrow \mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$
  (\texttt{chapters/examples/k3e\_bkm\_chapter.tex} line $11344$) to
  the $\mu_8$-gerbe on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2} \setminus H_1$.
  Lemma~C08.2 identifies this gerbe's local monodromy order as $8$.
\item $\pi_{\mathrm{Lus}}$: project to the Hall–Drinfeld double
  $\mathcal{D}_\hbar$ of $\mathbf{H}_{\Delta_5}$ and specialise at
  $\zeta_8$.  Lemma~C08.3 identifies the resulting $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action
  as the canonical root-of-unity action, of order $\ell = 8$.
\end{itemize}

These three projections are compatible because:
(i) $\pi_{\mathrm{Muk}} \leftrightarrow \pi_{\mathrm{Hum}}$ via the
Bruinier Heegner-divisor Chern-class reciprocity (Bruinier 2002
Thm.~$5.12$ + Kudla–Millson 1986) — see Conjecture~BZ–Muk–Br of

(iii) $\pi_{\mathrm{Muk}} \leftrightarrow \pi_{\mathrm{Lus}}$ follows

\hfill $\square$ (conditional on Hypothesis~H)

## Hypothesis (named)

\textsc{Hypothesis}~H (Dunn–Lurie lift of Serre to Heisdouble).
\emph{The Dunn–Lurie additivity equivalence $E_3 \simeq E_2 \otimes
E_1$ on $K3 \times E$ of Lurie \emph{Higher Algebra} Theorem
$5.5.3.6$, specialised to the product factorisation algebra
$\PhiFA_3(\mathrm{Perf}(K3 \times E))$, extends the Serre functor
$S_{K3}\colon D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3) \to D^b\mathrm{Coh}(K3)$ and the
elliptic automorphism $\tau_E\colon E \to E$ to a pair of commuting
functors $\widetilde{S}_{K3}$, $\widetilde{\tau}_E$ on the doubled
Mukai Heisenberg chiral algebra $\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)$
(Theorem~\texttt{thm:plat-heis-mukai-48}) such that the composite
$\widetilde{S}_{K3}^2 \otimes \widetilde{\tau}_E$ has finite order
$8$ on the quotient of $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E))$
by shifts acting trivially on the bar Euler character.}

This hypothesis is a specific, falsifiable statement:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Which paper would establish it?}  A continuation of Lurie
  \emph{Higher Algebra} Chapter~$5$ giving explicit formulas for
  the composite $E_2 \otimes E_1 \to E_3$ on product factorisation
  algebras of cotangent type $T^\vee[-1] X \times T^\vee[-1] Y$
  (\emph{en route} to $\mathrm{CY}_d$ data), combined with an
  explicit verification on $K3 \times E$ of the finite-order lift.
  Closest existing analogues: Francis 2013 \emph{Compos.\ Math.}~$149$
  on $E_n$-deformation theory for smooth varieties; Ayala–Francis 2019
  \emph{Adv.\ Math.}~$333$ on factorisation homology of stratified
  spaces; but neither gives the explicit finite-order lift through
  Serre.
\item \emph{How could it be falsified?}  By exhibiting an explicit
  projection of the Dunn–Lurie specialisation on
  $\mathrm{Heisdouble}(K3 \times E)$ to the Mukai Heisenberg Fock
  space whose composite operator $\widetilde{S}_{K3}^2 \otimes
  \widetilde{\tau}_E$ has order $\neq 8$ (or is not of finite order).
  This would require computing the chain-level Dunn–Lurie composition
  explicitly on the bar complex of $\mathfrak{heis}
  (\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}})^{\oplus 2}$ against the elliptic-curve
  automorphism action on $\mathfrak{n}_+(\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5})$ —
  an explicit finite-dimensional computation in principle, using the
  Gritsenko–Nikulin 1998 Theorem 2.1 denominator data.
\item \emph{What is already established?}  The order-$4$ action of
  $\tau_E$ on $E_{j=1728}$ (Silverman 2009, primary); the shift
  $S_{K3} = [2]$ (Bondal–Orlov 2001, primary); the Mukai-doubling
  identity $\ell(\mathbf{H}_Y) = 2c_+(L_Y)$ at three Lorentzian-lattice
  points $\{2, 8, 50\}$ (Wave-$2$ A04 Cycle~$8$); the
  $\mu_8$-gerbe on $\overline{\mathcal{A}_2} \setminus H_1$
  (Theorem~BZ1, ibid.); the $\mathbb{Z}/8$-structure of $u_{\zeta_8}$
  (Lusztig 1990, primary).  The \emph{combination} of these four
  into a single structural $\mathbb{Z}/8$-action on $\mathrm{Heisdouble}
  (K3 \times E)$ is what Hypothesis~H asserts.
\end{itemize}
