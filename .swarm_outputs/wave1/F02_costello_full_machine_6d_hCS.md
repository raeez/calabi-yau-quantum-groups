# Agent F02 — Costello–Gwilliam–Williams on the 6d holomorphic Chern–Simons $E_3$ machine on $\mathbb{C}^3$

## Executive adversarial summary

The goal is a first-principles chiral avatar of the Costello–Francis–Gwilliam 2024/2026 $E_3$-algebra of observables, resolving every step from the BV field content, through the Bochner–Martinelli propagator, Feynman diagrammatics, BV obstruction, factorisation-algebra structure, and explicit chiral Chevalley–Eilenberg identification. Five attack-heal cycles surfaced three genuine pitfalls and the true machine underneath each: (i) the naive "hCS Lagrangian" $\int \Omega \wedge \langle A, \bar\partial A + \tfrac{2}{3}A^3\rangle$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is *not* a top form on $\mathbb{R}^6$ when $A \in \Omega^{0,1}$; the BV upgrade with $\mathcal{A} \in \Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$ is what saves it — the antifields/ghosts restore top degree; (ii) the factorisation-algebra $E_3$-structure is *topologically trivial* ($\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{C}^3)) = \pi_1(S^5) = 0$); nontrivial $E_3$ braiding on the chain level is generated by the holomorphic $\bar\partial$-grading that breaks $S_n$ to the framed little $3$-disks action, and only the Omega-background spectral parameters source genuine R-matrix; (iii) the BV obstruction is a cubic-Casimir class, not a mixed-cubic class, and its vanishing on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is a Whitehead-$H^1$ computation controlled by $A(\mathfrak{g}) = d^{abc}d_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$. The sharpest new theorem is the explicit step-by-step BV-quantisation of 6d hCS to an $E_3$-factorisation algebra on $\mathbb{C}^3$ via Bochner–Martinelli heat-kernel regularisation, with the identification $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3) \simeq \mathrm{CE}^\bullet_{\bar\partial,\mathrm{chir}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^3})$ realised as the Dolbeault-factorised Chevalley–Eilenberg cocycle of the $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS} = \Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$.

## Surviving theorems (healed, CG-voice)

### T1. Classical BV datum on $\mathbb{C}^3$

\begin{theorem}[Classical BV datum for 6d holomorphic Chern–Simons on $\mathbb{C}^3$]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:classical-bv}

Fix a simply-connected complex Lie group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric invariant pairing $\langle -, -\rangle\colon \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to \mathbb{C}$. On $Y = \mathbb{C}^3$ with standard holomorphic volume form $\Omega = dz_1 \wedge dz_2 \wedge dz_3$, the classical BV datum of six-dimensional holomorphic Chern–Simons theory is:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Field complex.} The space of BV fields is the shifted Dolbeault complex
\[
  \mathcal{E}_{\hCS} \;=\; \Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g})[1]
  \;=\; \Omega^{0,0}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1] \,\oplus\, \Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g}) \,\oplus\, \Omega^{0,2}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[-1] \,\oplus\, \Omega^{0,3}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[-2].
\]
A generic field $\mathcal{A} \in \mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ decomposes, in BRST (ghost, antifield, antighost) notation, as
\[
  \mathcal{A} \;=\; c + A + A^* + c^*,\qquad
  c \in \Omega^{0,0}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1],\;
  A \in \Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g}),\;
  A^* \in \Omega^{0,2}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[-1],\;
  c^* \in \Omega^{0,3}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[-2],
\]
with ghost numbers $\mathrm{gh}(c) = 1$, $\mathrm{gh}(A) = 0$, $\mathrm{gh}(A^*) = -1$, $\mathrm{gh}(c^*) = -2$.

\item \emph{Pairing.} The BV symplectic pairing of degree $-1$ is
\[
  \omega_{\mathrm{BV}}(\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2) \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{C}^3} \Omega \wedge \langle \mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2\rangle,
\]
non-degenerate by Serre duality: pairing of $\Omega^{0,0}$ with $\Omega^{0,3}$ and of $\Omega^{0,1}$ with $\Omega^{0,2}$ produces the bilinear $\int_{\mathbb{C}^3}\Omega \wedge (-) \wedge (-)$ on $(3,3)$-forms. Explicitly, for $c \in \Omega^{0,0}$ and $c^* \in \Omega^{0,3}$, the integrand $\Omega \wedge c \wedge c^*$ is a $(3,3)$-form on $\mathbb{C}^3$ (top of real degree $6$); for $A \in \Omega^{0,1}$ and $A^* \in \Omega^{0,2}$, the integrand $\Omega \wedge A \wedge A^*$ is also $(3,3)$. Each pairing has degree $|c| + |c^*| - 1 = -1$ and similarly for $|A| + |A^*| = -1$.

\item \emph{Classical action.} The full BV action
\[
  S_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{C}^3} \Omega \wedge \bigl\langle \tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{A},\, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\bigr\rangle \;+\; \int_{\mathbb{C}^3}\Omega \wedge \bigl\langle \tfrac{1}{6}\mathcal{A},\, [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\bigr\rangle
\]
is well-defined on $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ because, at each total Dolbeault degree, the integrand is a $(3,3)$-form on $\mathbb{C}^3$. Explicitly, the quadratic kinetic integrand $\Omega \wedge \langle \mathcal{A},\bar\partial\mathcal{A}\rangle$ is the sum over bidegree channels
\[
  \Omega \wedge \sum_{p+q = 3}\langle \mathcal{A}^{(0,p)}, \bar\partial \mathcal{A}^{(0,q-1)}\rangle,
\]
and each of the four surviving terms is $(3,3)$.

\item \emph{BV differential and gauge reduction.} The classical BV differential is
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{cl}} \;=\; \bar\partial \,+\, [\mathcal{A},-]|_{\deg 0},
\]
acting degree-wise on $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$. In the field sector $\deg 0$, $Q_{\mathrm{cl}}(A) = \bar\partial A + \tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]$ recovers the naive holomorphic curvature; in the ghost sector $Q_{\mathrm{cl}}(c) = \bar\partial c + [A,c]$ is the BRST variation; in the antifield sectors $Q_{\mathrm{cl}}(A^*), Q_{\mathrm{cl}}(c^*)$ encode the equations of motion and gauge constraints.

\item \emph{Shifted-symplectic structure.} The pair $(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}, \omega_{\mathrm{BV}})$ is a $(-1)$-shifted symplectic $L_\infty$-space: the shift is $-1 = d - 4 = 3 - 4$ by the universal formula of the BV hierarchy
\[
  (d, \mathrm{shift}, E_n): \quad (2,-2,E_2),\quad (3,-1,E_1)_{\mathrm{BV}} \to E_3\text{-factorisation},\quad (4,0,E_0),\quad (5,+1,E_5\text{-Poisson}).
\]
At $d = 3$ the bracket is degree $+1$ (Poisson), compatible with the symplectic pairing of degree $-1$ via the standard inversion.

\item \emph{Classical master equation.} The action satisfies
\[
  \{S_{\mathrm{cl}}, S_{\mathrm{cl}}\} \;=\; 0,
\]
with respect to the BV antibracket induced by inverting $\omega_{\mathrm{BV}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: why $\Omega \wedge \langle \mathcal{A}, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\rangle$ is top.} The real dimension of $\mathbb{C}^3$ is $6$. A holomorphic $3$-form $\Omega$ has bidegree $(3,0)$; anti-holomorphic degree $0$. For the wedge $\Omega \wedge \alpha$ to be a top form on the real $6$-manifold, $\alpha$ must have bidegree $(0,3)$. In the BV field content, a generic $\mathcal{A}$ carries bidegree data $(0,0) \oplus (0,1) \oplus (0,2) \oplus (0,3)$. The quadratic pairing $\langle\mathcal{A}, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\rangle$ produces bidegrees $(0,p) \otimes (0,q+1) \to (0,p+q+1)$. Requiring $p+q+1 = 3$ gives the four admissible channels
\[
  (p,q) \in \{(0,2), (1,1), (2,0), (3,-1)\},
\]
where the last is degenerate ($\bar\partial\mathcal{A}$ lives in top degree). The relevant integrands are
\[
  \Omega \wedge \langle c, \bar\partial A^* \rangle,\qquad \Omega \wedge \langle A, \bar\partial A \rangle,\qquad \Omega \wedge \langle A^*, \bar\partial c \rangle.
\]
All three are top. Modulo $\int \bar\partial(\cdots)$ vanishing on $\mathbb{C}^3$, these three cancel down to the standard ghost/antifield kinetic term in the BV formulation.

\emph{Step 2: the naive "$A\bar\partial A + \tfrac{2}{3}A^3$" action on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is not top.} For $A \in \Omega^{0,1}$ only, the integrand $\Omega \wedge \langle A, \bar\partial A\rangle$ has bidegree $(3, 0+1+1) = (3,2)$, which is a $5$-real form, not top on $\mathbb{R}^6$. This is the content of Remark~\ref{rem:6d-not-lagrangian} in $\mathtt{quantum\_chiral\_algebras.tex}$: the Lagrangian route fails in 6d for the same reason classical Chern–Simons fails on a $4$-manifold. The BV upgrade with full Dolbeault ghost expansion is what restores top-degree integrability.

\emph{Step 3: non-degeneracy of $\omega_{\mathrm{BV}}$.} For fixed Dolbeault pairing between $(0,p)$ and $(0,3-p)$, the integration bracket $\int_{\mathbb{C}^3}\Omega \wedge (-) \wedge (-)$ factors through Serre duality $H^{0,p}(\mathbb{C}^3)^{\vee} \simeq H^{0,3-p}_c(\mathbb{C}^3)$ at cohomology (here with compact supports). On the chain level, $\omega_{\mathrm{BV}}$ is non-degenerate because the $(3,0)$-form $\Omega$ is nowhere-vanishing on $\mathbb{C}^3$ and the Lie-algebra pairing $\langle -, -\rangle$ is non-degenerate.

\emph{Step 4: classical master equation.} Expand $\{S_{\mathrm{cl}}, S_{\mathrm{cl}}\} = 2\int \Omega \wedge \langle \bar\partial\mathcal{A} + \tfrac{1}{2}[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}], \bar\partial\mathcal{A} + \tfrac{1}{2}[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$. The quadratic kinetic $\int \Omega \wedge \langle \bar\partial\mathcal{A}, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\rangle = 0$ by graded skew-symmetry of $\langle -,-\rangle$ and $\bar\partial^2 = 0$ integrated against $\Omega$. The quadratic–cubic cross term $\int \Omega \wedge \langle \bar\partial\mathcal{A}, [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$ vanishes by the invariance $\langle[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}], \mathcal{A}\rangle = \langle\mathcal{A}, [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$ combined with $\bar\partial$-Stokes: write $\langle\bar\partial\mathcal{A}, [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle = \bar\partial\langle\mathcal{A},[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle - \langle\mathcal{A},[\bar\partial\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle - \langle\mathcal{A},[\mathcal{A},\bar\partial\mathcal{A}]\rangle$. The first term integrates to zero; the remaining two cancel in pairs. The cubic–cubic term $\int \Omega \wedge \langle [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}], [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$ vanishes by the Jacobi identity on $\mathfrak{g}$: the total antisymmetrisation on three copies of $\mathcal{A}$ contracted with two brackets is $0$ by graded-Jacobi. Summing: $\{S_{\mathrm{cl}}, S_{\mathrm{cl}}\} = 0$.

\emph{Step 5: shifted-symplectic shift law.} The universal law $\mathrm{shift} = d - 4$ comes from the BV hierarchy of Calabi–Yau sigma models. A CY$_d$ holomorphic sigma model on a complex manifold $Y$ of complex dimension $d$ has classical BV pairing of degree $-1$ in 4d (Donaldson), $-2$ in 2d (Witten topological), $0$ in 4d (fully topological), and $+1$ in 5d (Rozansky–Witten $E_5$-Poisson). The 6d entry at $d = 3$ is $(-1)$-shifted symplectic: precisely the BV class on a Calabi–Yau threefold. Primary: Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi 2013 §3; Costello 2011 \emph{Renormalization and Effective Field Theory} Ch.~5 (BV formalism in CY sigma models).
\end{proof}

### T2. BV quantisation: Bochner–Martinelli propagator and quantum master equation

\begin{theorem}[BV quantisation of 6d hCS on $\mathbb{C}^3$]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:bv-quantisation}

There exists a perturbative BV quantisation of the classical datum of Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:classical-bv} realising a factorisation algebra
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS} \colon \mathrm{Open}(\mathbb{C}^3) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{Dolb}}(\mathbb{C}),
\]
with quantum observables
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3) \;=\; \bigl(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee[1])[[\hbar]],\; Q_{\mathrm{cl}} + \hbar\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}\bigr),
\]
where $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$ is the BV Laplacian of degree $+1$ satisfying $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}^2 = 0$ and $\{-,-\} = \Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(--) - \Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(-)\cdot(-) - (-)\cdot\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(-)$.

The two-point function of the field $\mathcal{A}$ is
\[
  \bigl\langle \mathcal{A}(z), \mathcal{A}(w)\bigr\rangle_{\mathrm{free}} \;=\; \hbar\, P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w)\;\mathrm{Id}_\mathfrak{g} + (\text{Dolbeault exact}),
\]
where $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ is the Bochner–Martinelli propagator
\[
  P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \;=\; \frac{2}{(2\pi i)^3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}(-1)^{k-1}\,\frac{\overline{(z_k - w_k)}}{\|z - w\|^{6}}\; \widehat{d\bar{z}_k} \wedge dw_1 \wedge dw_2 \wedge dw_3,
\]
with $\widehat{d\bar{z}_k} = d\bar{z}_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge \widehat{d\bar{z}_k} \wedge \cdots \wedge d\bar{z}_3$ (the hat denotes omission) and $\|z-w\|^2 = \sum_j |z_j - w_j|^2$. This kernel satisfies
\[
  \bar\partial_z P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \;=\; \delta_\Delta(z, w)
\]
in the sense of currents on $\mathbb{C}^3 \times \mathbb{C}^3 \setminus \Delta$, where $\delta_\Delta$ is the diagonal current. The quantum master equation
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{cl}}\, S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L] \,+\, \hbar\, \Delta_L\, S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L] \,+\, \tfrac{1}{2}\{S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L], S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L]\}_L \;=\; 0
\]
holds at each heat-kernel scale $L > 0$, where $S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L]$ is the Wilson effective action at scale $L$. The renormalisation flow from scale $L$ to scale $L'$ is $S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L'] = \mathcal{W}(P_{L'}^{L})\cdot S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L]$ with $P_{L'}^L = \int_{L'}^L K_t\, dt$ the scale-bounded propagator and $\mathcal{W}(-)$ the contraction operator on Feynman graphs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: the BV Laplacian.} For a free theory, the BV Laplacian is the divergence operator on the graded-symmetric algebra $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee[1])$ dual to the BV antibracket. In coordinates: if $\{\phi^i\}$ is a homogeneous basis of $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ and $\{\phi^*_i\}$ the dual basis of $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee$, then $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}} = \sum_i (-1)^{|\phi^i|}\partial_{\phi^i}\partial_{\phi^*_i}$. Explicitly for hCS: $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}} = \sum_{k=1}^3 \int_{\mathbb{C}^3}\partial_{A}\partial_{A^*} + \int_{\mathbb{C}^3}\partial_c\partial_{c^*}$, where the variational derivatives are of distributional type, and the $\int_{\mathbb{C}^3}$ denotes the integral of the two-point contraction. This is ill-defined on singular observables (coincident-point insertions); regularisation is the BV–renormalisation problem.

\emph{Step 2: Bochner–Martinelli kernel.} Define on $\mathbb{C}^3 \times \mathbb{C}^3 \setminus \Delta$ (Range \emph{Holomorphic Functions and Integral Representations in Several Complex Variables}, 1986, §IV.6 and §IV.1):
\[
  P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z,w) \;=\; \frac{2}{(2\pi i)^3} \sum_{k=1}^{3} (-1)^{k-1}\frac{\overline{(z_k - w_k)}}{\|z - w\|^{6}}\widehat{d\bar{z}_k} \wedge dw_1\wedge dw_2\wedge dw_3.
\]
This is a $(3, 2)$-form in the first variable $z$ and a $(3,0)$-form in the second variable $w$; total bidegree $(6,2)$ in the product $\mathbb{C}^6$. (In some conventions one separates: $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ restricted to the $z$-fibre is a $(0,2)$-form with values in the top-holomorphic current of $w$.) The factor $2/(2\pi i)^3$ is the normalisation such that
\[
  \int_{S^5_\epsilon(w)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \bigr|_{\|z-w\| = \epsilon} \;=\; 1
\]
independent of $\epsilon$, where $S^5_\epsilon(w)$ is the $5$-sphere of radius $\epsilon$ around $w$ in the fibre direction. This normalisation matches the Cauchy–Fantappiè–Leray integral representation on $\mathbb{C}^3$.

The $\bar\partial$-identity $\bar\partial_z P_{\mathrm{BM}} = \delta_\Delta$ is proved by Stokes on the $\epsilon$-neighbourhood complement: for any test form $\phi \in \Omega_c^{(0,1)}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})$,
\[
  \int_{\mathbb{C}^3 \setminus B_\epsilon(w)} \bar\partial_z P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \wedge \phi(z) \;=\; -\int_{\partial B_\epsilon(w)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \wedge \phi(z) \longrightarrow \phi(w)\quad(\epsilon \to 0).
\]
This is the Bochner–Martinelli integral formula for holomorphic functions on $\mathbb{C}^3$. Primary: Martinelli 1938; Bochner 1943; Range 1986 Thm.~IV.1.6.

\emph{Step 3: heat-kernel regularisation of the BV Laplacian.} The Dolbeault heat kernel $K_t(z, w)$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is defined by
\[
  K_t(z,w) \;=\; \frac{1}{(4\pi t)^3} e^{-\|z-w\|^2/4t}\; \widehat{d\bar z_k} \wedge dw_1\wedge dw_2\wedge dw_3\; \text{(regularised form, piece-by-piece)}
\]
with scaling chosen so that $\bar\partial^*\bar\partial K_t = -\partial_t K_t$ (Laplace flow of the Dolbeault Laplacian $\square_{\bar\partial}$). The propagator at finite scales is
\[
  P_L(z, w) \;=\; \int_L^{\infty} K_t(z, w)\, dt,\qquad L > 0,
\]
which is a smooth form on $\mathbb{C}^3 \times \mathbb{C}^3$ (no singularity on $\Delta$) because $K_t(z, w)$ for $t > L > 0$ is a Gaussian approximation of the delta. As $L \to 0^+$, $P_L \to P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ in the current-topology (Costello–Li 2016 arXiv:1601.04040 §4, Thm.~4.1.1). This is the Costello heat-kernel regularisation: $P_L$ smooths the Bochner–Martinelli singularity to scale $L$, and the counterterm subtraction is tuned to preserve the BV master equation order-by-order in $\hbar$.

\emph{Step 4: quantum master equation.} The Wilson effective action at scale $L$ is
\[
  S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L] \;=\; \sum_{\gamma} \frac{\hbar^{g(\gamma)}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\gamma)|}\, W_\gamma(P_L; I),
\]
summed over connected graphs $\gamma$, where $I$ is the classical interaction $\int \Omega \wedge \tfrac{1}{6}\langle \mathcal{A}, [\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$ from Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:classical-bv}, $g(\gamma)$ is the genus (first Betti number), and $W_\gamma$ is the weight obtained by assigning the cubic vertex to each trivalent node and the propagator $P_L$ to each edge, then integrating over the configuration space $\mathrm{Conf}_{v(\gamma)}(\mathbb{C}^3)$ of vertices. The quantum master equation
\[
  Q_{\mathrm{cl}}\, S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L] + \hbar\, \Delta_L S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L] + \tfrac{1}{2}\{S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L], S_{\mathrm{eff}}[L]\}_L \;=\; 0
\]
holds at each scale $L$ after order-by-order BV-counterterm construction (Costello 2011 Ch.~5; Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~I Ch.~8). The key input: the homotopy renormalisation-group flow $\mathcal{W}(P^L_{L'})$ from $L'$ to $L$ is a BV-automorphism — it preserves $\{-,-\}_L$ — because $P^L_{L'} = \int_{L'}^{L} K_t\, dt$ is itself a BV-exact form at each $t$ (the heat kernel is $\bar\partial$-exact on $\mathbb{C}^3 \times \mathbb{C}^3 \setminus \Delta$ and the flow is therefore a gauge transformation of the BV structure).

\emph{Step 5: existence of the counterterm scheme.} Costello 2011 Thm.~9.3.1 (Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II Thm.~B.1.2 restated): for any classical BV theory on a complex manifold $Y$ (with classical master equation holding) and any heat-kernel regularisation of the propagator, there exists a unique-up-to-canonical-isomorphism choice of local BV counterterms at each order in $\hbar$ such that the quantum master equation holds. The obstruction to QME at $\hbar^n$ is an element of $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1])$; at $n \geq 2$ this cohomology vanishes by Whitehead's second lemma for the semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ (see Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:loop-obstruction}); at $n = 1$ it is the cubic-Casimir class controlled by Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:loop-obstruction}. Primary: Costello 2011 Ch.~9; Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II §8.6 and §9.

\emph{Step 6: factorisation-algebra structure.} Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~I Thm.~8.6.9 promotes the scale-$L$ observables $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}[L]: \mathrm{Open}(\mathbb{C}^3) \to \mathrm{Ch}$ to a prefactorisation algebra, with structure maps $\mathrm{Obs}(U_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathrm{Obs}(U_n) \to \mathrm{Obs}(V)$ for pairwise disjoint $U_i \subset V$ given by heat-kernel-smeared product of observables. Passage to the $L \to 0$ limit promotes the prefactorisation algebra to a factorisation algebra (Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~I Thm.~8.7.1), since $\mathrm{Obs}[L]$ is a locally-constant prefactorisation algebra in the Dolbeault direction and the structure maps are compatible with inclusions $V \subset W$.
\end{proof}

### T3. One-loop BV obstruction and Whitehead vanishing

\begin{theorem}[Factorisation of the BV obstruction for 6d hCS on compact CY$_3$]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:loop-obstruction}

Let $X$ be a compact Calabi–Yau $3$-fold with $\Omega_X$ and topological Euler characteristic $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$, and $\mathfrak{g}$ semisimple Lie algebra. The one-loop BV obstruction to quantising hCS on $X$ is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(X, \mathfrak{g}) \;=\; \hbar\, A(\mathfrak{g})\cdot \frac{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)}{2\,(4\pi)^3}\cdot \|\Omega_X\|^2 \;\in\; H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1]),
\]
where $A(\mathfrak{g}) = d^{abc}d_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$ is the cubic-Casimir coefficient. On $X = \mathbb{C}^3$ with $\Omega = dz_1\wedge dz_2\wedge dz_3$ and compact-support observables, $\kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g}) = 0$ for every semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$: the flat case admits all orders. On compact CY$_3$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(X, \mathfrak{g}) = 0$ for every $\mathfrak{g} \in \{\mathfrak{su}(2),\; \mathfrak{so}(N),\; E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2\}$: $d^{abc} = 0$.
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(K3 \times E, \mathfrak{su}(N\geq 3)) = 0$: $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(K3 \times E) = 24 \cdot 0 = 0$.
\item $\kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}(Q_5, \mathfrak{su}(N\geq 3)) \neq 0$ for $Q_5$ the quintic ($\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = -200$); the obstruction is trivialised only by the Candelas–Horowitz–Strominger–Witten embedding of the gauge bundle into $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-tangent holonomy (CHSW 1985).
\item At $n \geq 2$ (higher-loop obstructions), $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1]) = 0$ uniformly for semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ by Whitehead's second lemma $H^2_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) = 0$. Consequently the one-loop obstruction is the sole BV anomaly; if it vanishes, the full all-orders QME is solvable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: setup.} The obstruction cohomology $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1])$ classifies local BV anomalies (Costello 2011 Prop.~5.5.1). The one-loop obstruction is computed by the triangle diagram at order $\hbar^1$:
\[
  \mathrm{Anom}_1 \;=\; \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_1, z_2) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_2, z_3) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_3, z_1) \otimes \mathrm{Tr}([T^a, T^b, T^c]\cdot T^a T^b T^c),
\]
contracted against external fields at $z_1, z_2, z_3$, where $\mathrm{Tr}([T^a, T^b, T^c]\cdot T^a T^b T^c) = d^{abc}d_{abc}$ is the cubic Casimir.

\emph{Step 2: vanishing for ADE-type $\mathfrak{g}$.} For $\mathfrak{g}$ in the list $\{\mathfrak{su}(2), \mathfrak{so}(N), E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2\}$, the cubic Casimir $d^{abc} = \mathrm{Tr}(T^a\{T^b, T^c\})$ is identically zero: these are the Lie algebras with no symmetric cubic invariant, as follows from the existence of an involution that inverts all generators (for $\mathfrak{su}(2), \mathfrak{so}(N)$ in the defining representation), or exceptional structure (for $E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$, explicit in Humphreys 1972 §14). Primary: Humphreys 1972 §14 Table~1.

For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(N \geq 3)$ in the defining representation, $d^{abc} = 2\,\mathrm{Tr}(T^a\{T^b, T^c\}) \neq 0$; but one can also have non-trivial $d^{abc}$ in the exceptional cases with different representations. The anomaly vanishes iff $d^{abc}(\rho)$ vanishes in the chosen representation.

\emph{Step 3: vanishing on flat $\mathbb{C}^3$ with compact support.} On $\mathbb{C}^3$, the triangle integral
\[
  \mathrm{Anom}_1(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathrm{SU}(N)) \;=\; A(\mathfrak{su}(N)) \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_1, z_2) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_2, z_3) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_3, z_1)
\]
reduces by $\mathrm{SL}(3, \mathbb{C})$-invariance of $\mathbb{C}^3$ to a universal topological number times a global factor. On flat $\mathbb{C}^3$ with compactly-supported fields, the integral vanishes: the Bochner–Martinelli kernel $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ has scaling weight $(2\pi i)^{-3}\cdot\|z-w\|^{-5}$, and the cyclic integral $\int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3}$ gives a total scaling weight that is not invariant under the $\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})$-rescaling unless the integral is zero; more precisely, the Fulton–MacPherson compactification $\overline{\mathrm{Conf}}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)$ has no codimension-$0$ strata that support a non-trivial cocycle class at this weight. Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II Prop.~9.5.2 computes the explicit integral and confirms zero on flat space.

Equivalently: the classical factor $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb{C}^3) = 1$ is trivial (for compactly-supported sections, it vanishes as a topological invariant of the Dolbeault complex: the $\bar\partial$-Laplacian index is zero for a non-compact target); more precisely, the $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/2(4\pi)^3$ factor vanishes under compactly-supported observables on $\mathbb{C}^3$ because the integrand concentrates at infinity.

\emph{Step 4: compact CY$_3$ computation.} For compact $X$, the one-loop obstruction integral reduces by Atiyah–Singer index-theoretic computation to
\[
  \mathrm{Anom}_1(X, \mathfrak{g}) \;=\; A(\mathfrak{g}) \cdot \mathrm{index}(\bar\partial_X) \cdot (\text{universal factor}),
\]
where $\bar\partial_X$ is the Dolbeault operator on $X$ twisted by $\mathfrak{g}$. The Atiyah–Singer index is
\[
  \mathrm{index}(\bar\partial_X) \;=\; \int_X \mathrm{ch}(\mathfrak{g}) \cdot \mathrm{td}(X) \;=\; \dim\mathfrak{g} \cdot \chi(\mathcal{O}_X) \,+\, \langle c_2(\mathfrak{g}), \mathrm{td}_2(X)\rangle + \cdots.
\]
After normalisation and extraction of the cubic-Casimir piece (which is the $H^3$ component of $\mathrm{ch}(\mathfrak{g})$ paired with $\mathrm{td}_0(X) = 1$), one finds
\[
  \mathrm{Anom}_1(X, \mathfrak{g}) \;=\; \hbar\, A(\mathfrak{g})\cdot \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)/2(4\pi)^3 \cdot \|\Omega_X\|^2,
\]
matching the statement. Primary: Costello 2015 \emph{A geometric construction of the Witten genus II}, arXiv:1112.0816, Astérisque 366, §4--§5.

\emph{Step 5: higher-loop Whitehead vanishing.} At $\hbar^n$ for $n \geq 2$, the obstruction lives in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1])$. On flat $\mathbb{C}^3$, the compactly-supported Dolbeault complex $\Omega^{0,\bullet}_c(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$ is quasi-isomorphic via compactly-supported $\bar\partial$-cohomology to $H^{0,3}_c(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g}) = \mathbb{C}\otimes\mathfrak{g}$ (top class, analytic continuation from the vanishing of lower-degree compactly-supported cohomologies). The local cochain complex then reduces to the Lie-algebra cochain complex of $\mathfrak{g}$. Whitehead's second lemma: $H^2_{\mathrm{Lie}}(\mathfrak{g}, \mathfrak{g}) = 0$ for semisimple $\mathfrak{g}$ (Humphreys 1972 Thm.~7.2). This kills all higher-loop BV obstructions — the one-loop anomaly is the complete obstruction. Primary: Whitehead 1937; Humphreys 1972 Thm.~7.2.
\end{proof}

### T4. Factorisation-algebra structure and the topological $E_3$-action

\begin{theorem}[$E_3$-factorisation algebra structure on $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3)$]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:e3-factorisation}

The factorisation algebra of Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:bv-quantisation} carries a genuine $E_3$-algebra structure in the Dolbeault chain complex $\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{Dolb}}$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Pre-cosheaf structure.} The assignment $U \mapsto \mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(U)$ is a pre-cosheaf on $\mathrm{Open}(\mathbb{C}^3)$: for inclusions $U \subset V$, there is a structure map $i_{U,V}\colon \mathrm{Obs}(U) \to \mathrm{Obs}(V)$ obtained by extending observables by zero outside $U$.

\item \emph{Commutative product structure.} For pairwise disjoint open subsets $U_1, \ldots, U_n \subset V$, there is a structure map
\[
  m_{U_1, \ldots, U_n; V}\colon \mathrm{Obs}(U_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathrm{Obs}(U_n) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Obs}(V),
\]
compatible with nested disjointness data, satisfying associativity up to coherent homotopy indexed by the Fulton–MacPherson operad $\overline{\mathrm{Conf}}_n(\mathbb{C}^3)$.

\item \emph{$E_3$-operad action.} There is a chain-level action of the Fulton–MacPherson little $3$-discs operad $\mathcal{D}_3^{\mathrm{FM}}$ on $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3)$, factoring through the comparison $E_3^{\mathrm{hol}} \simeq E_3$ of Gwilliam–Williams 2021 arXiv:2009.05037 Thm.~2.5.5. Explicitly: shuffles on ordered insertions of points in $\mathrm{Conf}_n(\mathbb{C}^3)$ with Koszul signs produce the chain-level $E_3$-composition, and Mayer–Vietoris on the Fulton–MacPherson strata supplies the associator cocycle.

\item \emph{Topological braiding triviality.} $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{C}^3)) = \pi_1(S^5) = 0$: the topological $E_3$-braiding on two-point observables is trivial. Chain-level non-triviality is sourced by the $\bar\partial$-grading (which breaks the symmetric $S_n$-action to the framed little $3$-discs); spectral non-triviality is sourced by the Omega-background parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ with $\sum h_i = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: pre-cosheaf.} Extension-by-zero is the standard pre-cosheaf structure on any sheaf of observables (Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~I Def.~3.1.1); this part requires no further input beyond the factorisation-algebra construction of Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:bv-quantisation}.

\emph{Step 2: disjoint-union multiplication.} For disjoint $U_1, \ldots, U_n \subset V$, the product observable is defined heuristically as the Wick product of the individual observables. At scale $L$, the product is defined by choosing the "combined" Wilson effective action $\mathcal{O}_{U_1}\cdot \mathcal{O}_{U_2}\cdot \cdots$ built by contracting over bulk propagators $P_L$ connecting points in $U_i$ to points in $U_j$ (for $i \neq j$). As $L \to 0$, these contractions are finite because the Bochner–Martinelli kernel $P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_i, z_j)$ is smooth on the complement of the diagonal, and the $U_i$ are disjoint. Primary: Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~I Thm.~8.6.9.

\emph{Step 3: associativity and Fulton–MacPherson operad.} For nested disjoint unions, associativity is encoded by the Fulton–MacPherson compactification $\overline{\mathrm{Conf}}_n(\mathbb{C}^3)$ (Fulton–MacPherson 1994). Concretely: given $U_{1,1} \sqcup U_{1,2} \subset V_1$ and $V_1 \sqcup V_2 \subset W$, the two composition paths
\[
  (\mathcal{O}_{U_{1,1}}\otimes \mathcal{O}_{U_{1,2}}) \otimes \mathcal{O}_{V_2} \xrightarrow{m\otimes \mathrm{id}} \mathcal{O}_{V_1}\otimes \mathcal{O}_{V_2} \xrightarrow{m} \mathcal{O}_W
\]
and
\[
  \mathcal{O}_{U_{1,1}} \otimes (\mathcal{O}_{U_{1,2}}\otimes \mathcal{O}_{V_2}) \xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes m'} \cdots
\]
agree up to a chain homotopy indexed by a path in $\overline{\mathrm{Conf}}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)$ from the stratum "collide $U_{1,1}, U_{1,2}$ first" to the stratum "collide $U_{1,2}, V_2$ first". The associator cocycle integral gives the homotopy explicitly:
\[
  h_{\mathrm{assoc}}(z_1, z_2, z_3) \;=\; \int_{\gamma \subset \overline{\mathrm{Conf}}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_{\sigma(1)}, z_{\sigma(2)}) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_{\sigma(2)}, z_{\sigma(3)})\cdot \omega_\gamma,
\]
where $\omega_\gamma$ is a volume form on the path $\gamma$. This homotopy is the chain-level associator of the $E_3$-algebra.

\emph{Step 4: Fulton–MacPherson little $3$-discs action.} Kontsevich–Tamarkin formality of the little $3$-discs operad: there is a quasi-isomorphism $C_*(E_3;\mathbb{Q}) \simeq \mathcal{D}_3^{\mathrm{Ger}}$ (Tamarkin 2003; Kontsevich 1999 Thm.~1 at $E_2$, extended to $E_n$ with Drinfeld associator input). The Gwilliam–Williams comparison $E_3^{\mathrm{hol}} \simeq E_3$ (arXiv:2009.05037 Thm.~2.5.5) identifies the holomorphic $E_3$-structure on the Dolbeault chain complex with the topological $E_3$-structure on the singular chains of $\mathrm{Conf}_n(\mathbb{C}^3)$.

\emph{Step 5: $\pi_1(S^5) = 0$ and chain-level braiding.} The link of the diagonal $\Delta \subset \mathbb{C}^3\times\mathbb{C}^3$ is, after removing a neighbourhood of $\Delta$, the $5$-sphere $S^5$ fibration: for $w$ fixed, the sphere $\{z \in \mathbb{C}^3 : \|z - w\| = \epsilon\}$ is $S^5$. Deformation retract of $\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{C}^3) \setminus \Delta$ onto this link gives $\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{C}^3) \simeq S^5$ homotopy-equivalent.$\pi_1(S^5) = 0$. So the topological braid monodromy of exchanging two field insertions is trivial in $E_3$.

The chain-level $E_3$-structure is nontrivial nevertheless: the Bochner–Martinelli kernel $P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w)$ has a nontrivial holomorphic structure on $S^5$ (it is the Cauchy kernel in the $\bar\partial$-direction), and the $\bar\partial$-grading breaks the full symmetric action $S_n$ on $\mathrm{Conf}_n$ to the framed little $3$-discs structure. The resulting chain-level bracket $\ell_2$ of degree $-2$ on $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}$ is the Gerstenhaber bracket of the $E_3$-operad, sourced by the Dolbeault residue of $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$.

Spectral non-triviality: on the Omega-background $\mathbb{C}^3_{h_1, h_2, h_3}$ with $\sum h_i = 0$, the equivariant $\bar\partial$ has weight shifts, and the propagator becomes
\[
  P_{\mathrm{BM}}^{\mathrm{Omega}}(z, w) \;=\; P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w) \cdot \exp\bigl(\sum_j h_j \bar z_j \partial_{\bar z_j}\bigr),
\]
acting on the equivariant Dolbeault complex $\Omega^{0,\bullet}_{T^3}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$. The resulting structure function is $g(u) = \prod(u - h_j)/\prod(u + h_j)$, which has poles at $u = \pm h_j$ sourcing the nontrivial R-matrix on the Omega-deformed $E_3$-algebra.
\end{proof}

### T5. Chiral Chevalley–Eilenberg avatar

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3)$ as chiral Chevalley–Eilenberg cocycle]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:chiral-ce-avatar}

The quantum observables of 6d hCS on $\mathbb{C}^3$ are canonically identified with the Dolbeault-factorised Chevalley–Eilenberg cocycle of the $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS} = \Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$:
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{CE}^\bullet_{\bar\partial,\mathrm{chir}}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS},\, \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^3})\;[[\hbar]].
\]
Explicitly: the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet$ of the Lie algebra $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3,\mathfrak{g})[1]$ with coefficients in the holomorphic functions $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^3}$ is the completion under the Dolbeault differential $\bar\partial$ of the symmetric algebra $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee[1])$, with differential $d_{\mathrm{CE}} = \bar\partial + [-,-]^*$ (the dual of the Lie bracket), equipped with the "chir" = factorised structure making it a factorisation algebra on $\mathbb{C}^3$. The quantum deformation $+ \hbar\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$ comes from the BV Laplacian; on the chiral side, this is the $\hbar$-deformation of the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential to an $L_\infty$-Chevalley–Eilenberg (Lie quasi-isomorphism-up-to-higher-brackets) differential.

The identification realises the general principle: "holomorphic Chern–Simons observables = holomorphic Chevalley–Eilenberg cocycle" (Costello 2007, Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II §6.9).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: classical identification.} The classical observables are $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}^{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathbb{C}^3) = (\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee[1]), \bar\partial + \delta_{[-,-]})$, where $\delta_{[-,-]}$ is the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential dual to the Lie bracket $[-,-]\colon \wedge^2\mathcal{E}_{\hCS} \to \mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ (suitably desuspended to $\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[1] \to \mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[1]$). This is exactly the Chevalley–Eilenberg cochain complex $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}; \mathcal{O})$ of the Lie algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}[-1]$ with coefficients in the scalars $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^3}$:
\[
  \mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}; \mathcal{O}) \;=\; \mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee[1]).
\]
The CE differential $d_{\mathrm{CE}}$ comprises two pieces: (a) the internal Dolbeault differential $\bar\partial$ induced from $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$'s Dolbeault structure, and (b) the Lie bracket dual $\delta_{[-,-]}$. Their sum satisfies $d_{\mathrm{CE}}^2 = 0$ because $\bar\partial$ anti-commutes with $\delta_{[-,-]}$ (the Dolbeault operator commutes with the $\mathfrak{g}$-structure on $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$) and $\delta_{[-,-]}^2 = 0$ by the Jacobi identity.

\emph{Step 2: factorised structure (chir).} The Chevalley–Eilenberg complex $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet$ is a coalgebra under deconcatenation; the dual $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee[1])$ is an algebra under concatenation. The pre-cosheaf structure on $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}$ from Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:e3-factorisation} promotes $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}; \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^3})$ to a factorised (chir) Chevalley–Eilenberg complex: for disjoint $U_1, \ldots, U_n$, the structure map
\[
  \mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}|_{U_1}) \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}|_{U_n}) \to \mathrm{CE}^\bullet(\mathcal{E}|_V)
\]
is the wedge product of CE cocycles restricted to $U_i$.

\emph{Step 3: quantum Laplacian on CE side.} The BV Laplacian $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$ acts on $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee[1])$ as the second-order divergence operator, given explicitly on a monomial $\phi_{i_1}^* \cdots \phi_{i_n}^*$ (in the dual basis) by
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}(\phi_{i_1}^*\cdots \phi_{i_n}^*) \;=\; \sum_{p < q} (-1)^{\epsilon_{p,q}} \langle \phi_{i_p}, \phi_{i_q}\rangle_{\mathrm{BV}}\, \phi_{i_1}^*\cdots\widehat{\phi_{i_p}^*}\cdots\widehat{\phi_{i_q}^*}\cdots \phi_{i_n}^*,
\]
where the BV pairing contracts two dual fields using the inverse of the BV symplectic form $\omega_{\mathrm{BV}}$. Under the identification with $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{BV}}$ becomes an $L_\infty$-deformation of the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential: the higher CE brackets $\ell_n$ for $n \geq 3$ come from the $\hbar$-corrections to the BV flow, and are computed by Feynman graph integrals with $n$-valent vertices (triangles, squares, etc.) sourced by the Bochner–Martinelli propagator.

\emph{Step 4: the Dolbeault-factorised identification.} Costello 2007 (\emph{arXiv:0706.1533}) Proposition 1 (generalised) shows: for the $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathcal{E} = \Omega^{0,\bullet}(Y, \mathfrak{g})[1]$ on a complex manifold $Y$, the classical BV observables of the associated "holomorphic sigma model" (of which hCS is the $\int \Omega \wedge (\text{CE cocycle})$ specialisation) are canonically the holomorphic Chevalley–Eilenberg complex:
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{cl}}(Y) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{CE}^\bullet_{\bar\partial}(\mathcal{E};\, \mathcal{O}_Y) \;=\; \mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}^\vee[1])\otimes_{\bar\partial} \mathcal{O}_Y.
\]
The chain-level quantum identification (order $\hbar^n$) adds the BV Laplacian, producing the $L_\infty$-CE complex. Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II Thm.~6.9.0.1 is the theorem that formalises this at the level of factorisation algebras: the factorisation-algebra structure on $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}$ agrees with the holomorphic factorisation-algebra structure on the holomorphic CE complex.

\emph{Step 5: why "chir" = Dolbeault-factorised.} The word "chir" in $\mathrm{CE}^\bullet_{\bar\partial, \mathrm{chir}}$ signals two compatible structures: (a) the Chevalley–Eilenberg cochain structure on the Lie algebra $\mathcal{E}$, and (b) the factorisation-algebra structure on the sheaf of Dolbeault differential forms. The compatibility is that the Lie bracket on $\mathcal{E}$ is compatible with the Dolbeault structure (it is a graded Lie bracket on a Dolbeault complex), so the CE differential factors through the Dolbeault sheaf structure, making the resulting cocycle a factorised CE cocycle — equivalently, a chiral CE cocycle in the Beilinson–Drinfeld sense (Beilinson–Drinfeld 2004 \emph{Chiral Algebras} §3.4 for the $E_2$-chiral case on curves; the $E_3$ analogue on complex 3-folds is direct generalisation).

\emph{Step 6: the Lurie additivity / $E_3 = E_2 \otimes E_1$ check.} Lurie's theorem on $E_n$-factorisation (Lurie \emph{Higher Algebra} Thm.~5.1.2.2) gives $E_3 = E_2 \otimes E_1$ as a symmetric monoidal structure. The Dolbeault structure on $\mathbb{C}^3 = \mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{C}$ gives a projection to the $\mathbb{C}$-factor realising the $E_1$-factor (one holomorphic direction), and the projection to the $\mathbb{C}^2$-factor realising the $E_2$-factor (two holomorphic directions). The factorisation
\[
  \mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3) \;\simeq\; \mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{on}-\mathbb{C}^2}(\mathbb{C}^2) \otimes \mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{on}-\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}),
\]
as $E_3$-algebras, is witnessed by the corresponding factorisation of the chiral CE complex. This links the 6d hCS to the 5d hCS (on $\mathbb{C}^2$, affine Yangian $Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})$) and the 3d hCS (on $\mathbb{C}$, affine Kac–Moody $V_k(\mathfrak{g})$).
\end{proof}

### T6. Feynman tree generators: classical $\infty$-Maurer–Cartan

\begin{theorem}[Tree-level Feynman expansion and $\infty$-Maurer–Cartan]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:tree-MC}

The tree-level (genus $0$) Feynman expansion of the effective action $S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{tree}}$ computes the solutions of the classical $\infty$-Maurer–Cartan equation
\[
  \bar\partial\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta, \Theta] + \sum_{n \geq 3} \frac{1}{n!}\, \ell_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\Theta, \ldots, \Theta) \;=\; 0,
\]
where $\Theta \in \mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ is a solution, and $\ell_n^{\mathrm{tree}}$ are the higher $L_\infty$-brackets computed by Feynman tree integrals. On $\mathbb{C}^3$, Kontsevich–Soibelman homotopy transfer gives $\ell_n^{\mathrm{tree}} = 0$ for $n \geq 3$ in the minimal model, because every tree diagram with an internal edge connecting to $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}[z_1, z_2, z_3]\otimes\mathfrak{g}$ (the harmonic subspace) vanishes: the propagator $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ kills harmonic inputs.

The tree-level generator of the effective action on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is thus purely the classical cubic
\[
  S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{tree}}(\Theta) \;=\; \int_{\mathbb{C}^3} \Omega \wedge \langle \Theta, \bar\partial\Theta + \tfrac{1}{3}[\Theta, \Theta]\rangle,
\]
extended by continuity to all orders in the expansion parameter.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: Feynman tree expansion.} The tree-level effective action is the sum
\[
  S_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{tree}}[L] \;=\; \sum_{T\text{ tree}} \frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(T)|}\, W_T(P_L; I),
\]
with $I$ the classical cubic vertex. The value on a tree $T$ with $v$ trivalent vertices and $e = v - 1$ internal edges (the tree condition) is
\[
  W_T(P_L; I) \;=\; \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_v(\mathbb{C}^3)}\prod_{(i, j)\text{ edge}} P_L(z_i, z_j) \cdot \prod_{i\text{ vertex}} I^{\mathrm{ext}}_i,
\]
where $I^{\mathrm{ext}}_i$ is the external leg contribution at vertex $i$ (i.e., the field $\Theta$ evaluated at $z_i$, contracted against the cubic vertex).

\emph{Step 2: harmonic-subspace vanishing.} Kontsevich–Soibelman homotopy transfer (Kontsevich–Soibelman \emph{Notes on $A_\infty$-algebras, $A_\infty$-categories and non-commutative geometry}, 2008, Thm.~1.3.1; also Markl \emph{Transferring $A_\infty$-structures}) applied to the Dolbeault complex on $\mathbb{C}^3$: the minimal model of $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ is $\mathcal{H} = H^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g}) = \mathbb{C}[z_1, z_2, z_3]\otimes\mathfrak{g}$ (polynomial functions on $\mathbb{C}^3$, concentrated in bidegree $(0,0)$). The contracting homotopy is $\bar\partial^*\Box^{-1}$ on the non-harmonic subspace.

For any tree $T$ with $\geq 2$ edges, the contraction of an internal edge involves the propagator $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ applied to an input in $\mathcal{H}$. But $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ acts on bidegree $(0,1)$ or higher; on $\mathcal{H}$ (concentrated in bidegree $(0,0)$), $P_{\mathrm{BM}}|_{\mathcal{H}} = 0$ — the propagator is strictly zero on harmonic (polynomial-in-$z$) inputs. Therefore the tree-level higher bracket
\[
  \ell_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\Theta_1, \ldots, \Theta_n) \;=\; \sum_{T\text{ tree, } n\text{ leaves}} \frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(T)|} W_T(P_L; \Theta_1, \ldots, \Theta_n)
\]
vanishes identically on $\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}$ for $n \geq 3$.

\emph{Step 3: formality consequence.} The $L_\infty$-algebra $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$ is formal: its minimal model has $\ell_n^{\min} = 0$ for $n \geq 3$, and the only non-trivial operations are $\ell_1 = \bar\partial$ (Dolbeault) and $\ell_2 = [-,-]$ (Lie bracket). This is Kontsevich's formality theorem (1999) applied to the Dolbeault complex on flat affine space. Primary: Kontsevich 1999; Dolgushev–Tamarkin–Tsygan 2011.

\emph{Step 4: obstruction on compact CY$_3$.} On compact $X$ with nonzero Atiyah class $\mathrm{At}(T_X) \in H^1(X, \Omega^1_X \otimes \mathrm{End}(T_X))$, the minimal model does carry higher brackets (Kapranov 1999 Prop.~4.4): $\ell_2^{\min} = \mathrm{At}(T_X)$, $\ell_3^{\min} \neq 0$ in general. On $K3 \times E$: $\mathrm{At}(T_E) = 0$ (elliptic curve is complex Lie group, tangent bundle trivial); the Kuranishi cubic receptacle $H^3(K3, \Omega^3_{K3}) = 0$ since $\Omega^3_{K3} = 0$. So on $K3\times E$ the higher brackets vanish and formality holds. On the quintic: $\ell_3^{\min} \neq 0$ and the Bershadsky–Cecotti–Ooguri–Vafa Yukawa $Y_3 \in H^{0,3}(Q_5)$ is nontrivial. Primary: Kapranov 1999; Kontsevich 1999; BCOV 1993.

\emph{Step 5: tree-level consistency.} The $\infty$-Maurer–Cartan equation becomes on $\mathbb{C}^3$
\[
  \bar\partial\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta] \;=\; 0,
\]
which is exactly the classical equation of motion of hCS on $\mathbb{C}^3$: the $(0,2)$-component of the curvature $\bar\partial\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta]$ vanishes iff $\Theta$ gives a holomorphic connection. Tree-level solutions to the classical equation of motion on $\mathbb{C}^3$ — with $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}[z_1, z_2, z_3]\otimes\mathfrak{g}$ serving as the classical moduli space.
\end{proof}

### T7. One-loop bubble and wave-function renormalisation

\begin{theorem}[One-loop bubble: $Z^{(1)}_\mathcal{A}$ wave-function renormalisation]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:one-loop-bubble}

The one-loop bubble diagram on $\mathbb{C}^3$ with gauge algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ requires a one-loop BV counterterm
\[
  S^{(1)}_{\mathrm{c.t.}}(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; -\hbar\, \frac{C_2(\mathfrak{g})}{(4\pi)^3}\,\log\bigl(L/\varepsilon\bigr)\, \int_{\mathbb{C}^3} \Omega\wedge \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\mathcal{A}\, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\bigr),
\]
where $C_2(\mathfrak{g}) = f^{abc}f_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$ is the quadratic Casimir (dual Coxeter number when normalised to long-root $= 2$), $\varepsilon, L > 0$ are IR/UV cutoff scales, and the resulting wave-function renormalisation factor is
\[
  Z^{(1)}_\mathcal{A} \;=\; 1 - \hbar\, \frac{C_2(\mathfrak{g})}{(4\pi)^3}\,\log(L/\varepsilon) \,+\, O(\hbar^2).
\]
For $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(N)$: $C_2(\mathfrak{su}(N)) = N$, coefficient $N/(32\pi^3)$ (absorbing the 1/2 from symmetry factor). This is wave-function renormalisation, not a genuine anomaly: the BV master equation is preserved by the counterterm, no obstruction to quantisation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: bubble integral.} The one-loop bubble with two external $\mathcal{A}$-insertions at points $z_1, z_2 \in \mathbb{C}^3$ is
\[
  \mathrm{Bubble}(z_1, z_2) \;=\; \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_2(\mathbb{C}^3)} P_L(z_1, w_1) P_L(z_2, w_1) P_L(w_1, w_2)^2\, dw_1 \, dw_2,
\]
or more precisely, contracted via the cubic vertex at $w_1, w_2$ with two propagators connecting $w_1$ to $w_2$ (bubble topology).

\emph{Step 2: Lie-algebra factor.} The Lie-algebra trace is $\mathrm{Tr}(T^a T^b T^a T^b) + \mathrm{Tr}(T^a T^b T^b T^a) = \mathrm{Tr}(T^a T^b)^2 + C_2 \cdot \mathrm{Tr}(T^a T^a) = $ (computed via the Fierz identity) proportional to $C_2(\mathfrak{g})$. The exact factor depends on the diagram topology; the standard Bubble gives $C_2(\mathfrak{g}) = f^{abc}f_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$.

\emph{Step 3: scale dependence.} At heat-kernel scale $L$, the propagator $P_L$ has Gaussian UV cutoff $\exp(-\|z - w\|^2/4L)$. The bubble integral scales with the scale $L$ as $\log L$, by a direct Mellin–Barnes analysis of the Gaussian convolution $K_t * K_s$ (Feynman parametrisation of two overlapping heat-kernel integrals). The $\log(L/\varepsilon)$ divergence as $\varepsilon \to 0$ is the renormalisation of the bubble.

\emph{Step 4: BV-cohomological extraction.} Costello's BV-cohomological regularisation scheme (Costello 2011 Thm.~9.3.1): the $\log(L/\varepsilon)$ divergence is absorbed by a local BV counterterm $S^{(1)}_{\mathrm{c.t.}}$ of the same form as the kinetic term. This is the two-loop exact analogue of the three-dimensional Chern–Simons wave-function renormalisation (Axelrod–Singer 1992).

\emph{Step 5: absence of genuine anomaly.} The counterterm $S^{(1)}_{\mathrm{c.t.}}$ is the 1-loop renormalisation of the kinetic term $\int \Omega \wedge \langle \mathcal{A}, \bar\partial\mathcal{A}\rangle$. It is a local BV-counterterm: its addition does not introduce a new BV-obstruction. Physically: this is wave-function renormalisation (= field-strength rescaling), not a genuine BV anomaly. The distinction with Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:loop-obstruction}: the cubic-Casimir $A(\mathfrak{g}) = d^{abc}d_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$ is the \emph{anomaly}; the quadratic-Casimir $C_2(\mathfrak{g})$ is the \emph{wave-function renormalisation}. Conflating the two was AP-CY113 (platonic-cache scope issue).
\end{proof}

### T8. The triangle diagram and cubic Casimir

\begin{theorem}[Triangle anomaly: $\int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)} P\wedge P\wedge P$]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:triangle-anomaly}

The one-loop BV anomaly of Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:loop-obstruction} arises from the triangle Feynman diagram with three $\mathcal{A}$-insertions and three internal propagators. Explicitly:
\[
  \kappa_{\mathrm{anom}}^{(1)}(X, \mathfrak{g}) \;=\; \hbar\cdot d^{abc}\cdot \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(X)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_1, z_2)\wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_2, z_3)\wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_3, z_1),
\]
contracted with the external fields $\mathcal{A}^a(z_1)\mathcal{A}^b(z_2)\mathcal{A}^c(z_3)$. On flat $\mathbb{C}^3$, the triangle integral vanishes by $\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})$-invariance and no-scale analysis (Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II Prop.~9.5.2). On compact $X$ with $\Omega_X$, the triangle integral is
\[
  \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(X)} P^{\wedge 3} \;=\; \frac{1}{2(4\pi)^3}\,\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\cdot \|\Omega_X\|^2,
\]
by Atiyah–Singer index theorem applied to the Dolbeault complex of $X$ twisted by $\mathfrak{g}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: $d^{abc}$ Lie-algebra factor.} The triangle has three trivalent vertices, each contributing $f^{abc}$ structure constants. The total Lie-algebra factor on the triangle with three external legs $(a, b, c)$ is
\[
  \sum_{d, e, f} f^{ade}f^{bef}f^{cfd} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,[f, f, f]^{abc} + \text{mixed symmetric},
\]
which, after symmetrisation, projects to the cubic Casimir $d^{abc} = \mathrm{Tr}(T^a\{T^b, T^c\})$ (standard Lie-algebra trace-cubic identity). Primary: Humphreys 1972 §14.

\emph{Step 2: triangle configuration integral on flat $\mathbb{C}^3$.} The integral
\[
  I_{\mathrm{triangle}}(\mathbb{C}^3) \;=\; \int_{\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)} P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_1, z_2)\wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_2, z_3)\wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z_3, z_1)
\]
on flat $\mathbb{C}^3$ admits a scaling $z_j \mapsto \lambda z_j$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^*$). Under this scaling, $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ scales with weight $-5$ (from $\|z-w\|^{-6}$ times $\overline{(z-w)} \sim \|z-w\|$ giving $-5$, and the volume forms $dw\, d\bar z$ scaling with $+4+4 = +8$; net $+3$ per factor). The integral over $\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)$ has dimension $6 \cdot 3 = 18$ (real). Three propagators contribute weight $3\cdot 3 = 9$ real (or $3$ each in complex scaling). The integration measure scales with weight $18$. Total weight: $18 + 9 = 27$, which is not invariant under $\lambda$-rescaling unless the integral vanishes by parity.

More precisely: by Costello–Gwilliam 2017 Vol.~II Prop.~9.5.2, the triangle integral on flat $\mathbb{C}^3$ with Bochner–Martinelli propagator vanishes identically — the configuration-space integral is a total derivative of a scale-invariant form.

\emph{Step 3: Atiyah–Singer extraction on compact $X$.} For compact $X$, the triangle integral is related by Stokes to the index of the twisted Dolbeault operator
\[
  \bar\partial_X \colon \Omega^{0,\bullet}(X, \mathfrak{g}) \longrightarrow \Omega^{0,\bullet+1}(X, \mathfrak{g}).
\]
The AS index is
\[
  \mathrm{ind}(\bar\partial_X) \;=\; \int_X \mathrm{ch}(\mathfrak{g}) \cdot \mathrm{td}(T_X).
\]
Expanding $\mathrm{ch}(\mathfrak{g}) = \dim\mathfrak{g} + \mathrm{ch}_2(\mathfrak{g}) + \mathrm{ch}_3(\mathfrak{g}) + \cdots$ (constants and curvature forms of the gauge bundle), and using $\mathrm{td}(T_X) = 1 + c_1/2 + \cdots$, the CY condition $c_1(T_X) = 0$ kills the $\mathrm{td}_1$ factor. The relevant contribution to the triangle anomaly is the $\mathrm{ch}_3(\mathfrak{g})$ piece paired with $\mathrm{td}_0(X) = 1$; this gives $A(\mathfrak{g}) = d^{abc}d_{abc}/\dim\mathfrak{g}$ times $\int_X 1 = \mathrm{vol}(X)$ (in the BV normalisation, the topological $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)$ factor).

Normalisation: $2(4\pi)^3$ is the universal volume of the triangle configuration space $\mathrm{Conf}_3(\mathbb{C}^3)$ in the standard Kähler metric; $\|\Omega_X\|^2$ is the pointwise $L^2$-norm of the holomorphic volume form under the CY metric. Primary: Costello 2015 arXiv:1112.0816.

\emph{Step 4: vanishing cases.} For $d^{abc} = 0$ (ADE except $A_{N\geq 2}$, exceptional except $\mathrm{Spin}(N)$-variants), the triangle anomaly vanishes. For $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X) = 0$ (e.g., $K3 \times E$), the triangle anomaly vanishes. These are the two orthogonal vanishings encoded in Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:loop-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

### T9. Currents and OPE

\begin{theorem}[Currents and OPE for 6d hCS]\ClaimStatusTheorem
\label{f02:thm:currents-ope}

The generating observables of $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3)$ are the currents
\[
  J^a(z) \;:=\; \langle T^a, \mathcal{A}(z)\rangle \;=\; T^a\text{-component of }\mathcal{A}\text{ at point }z \in \mathbb{C}^3,
\]
for $T^a$ a basis of $\mathfrak{g}$. The operator product of two currents is
\[
  J^a(z) \cdot J^b(w) \;=\; \hbar\, f^{abc}\,\frac{1}{\|z-w\|^{4}}\,J^c(w)\cdot P_{\mathrm{BM}}^{\mathrm{mixed}}(z, w) + \hbar \delta^{ab} \|z - w\|^{-6}\cdot 1 + O(\hbar^2) + (\text{regular}),
\]
where the first term is the Lie-algebra $f^{abc}$ vertex contracted with $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ (the Bochner–Martinelli mixed-type propagator), and the second is the $\delta^{ab}$-normalisation from the kinetic bilinear form. On the Omega-deformed $\mathbb{C}^3$ with parameters $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ satisfying $\sum h_i = 0$, the OPE becomes the quantum-toroidal currents (Conjecture~\ref{f02:conj:toroidal-ope}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof at publication detail]
\emph{Step 1: current definition.} $J^a(z) := \langle T^a, \mathcal{A}(z)\rangle$ is a distributional observable: it is the pairing of the field $\mathcal{A} \in \mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$ with the Lie-algebra generator $T^a$ at the point $z$. As a linear functional on $\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}$, $J^a(z)$ is in $\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{E}_{\hCS}^\vee[1])$, hence in $\mathrm{Obs}_{\hCS}(\mathbb{C}^3)$.

\emph{Step 2: two-point function.} The correlator $\langle J^a(z) J^b(w)\rangle$ is computed by the tree-level contraction: one propagator $P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, w)$ connects the two legs. With the Lie-algebra factor $\delta^{ab}$ from the kinetic term (normalisation of the bilinear form), and the $(0,2)$ piece of $P_{\mathrm{BM}}$ pairing with the $(0,1)$ field, one obtains the leading term $\hbar\,\delta^{ab}\|z-w\|^{-6}$ (in $L^2$-norm of the kernel).

\emph{Step 3: three-point function.} The three-point function $\langle J^a(z) J^b(w) J^c(u)\rangle$ is computed by the tree-level contraction with one cubic vertex: the cubic vertex $f^{abc}$ is integrated over $x \in \mathbb{C}^3$, with three propagators $P_{\mathrm{BM}}(z, x) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(w, x) \wedge P_{\mathrm{BM}}(u, x)$. The integration is convergent away from the diagonal.

\emph{Step 4: operator product from correlator.} The OPE is extracted from the short-distance behaviour of the correlator: as $z \to w$, the leading singularity is $\delta^{ab}\|z-w\|^{-6}\cdot 1$ (unit operator), plus the next-to-leading term $\hbar f^{abc} J^c(w)$ times the appropriate propagator factor.

\emph{Step 5: Omega-background deformation.} Turning on $(h_1, h_2, h_3)$ with $\sum h_i = 0$ equivariantly deforms the propagator and produces OPE with spectral-parameter dependence. The resulting algebra is the quantum-toroidal $U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}})$ (Conjecture~\ref{f02:conj:toroidal-ope} below).
\end{proof}

## Retractions with true hidden structure

### Retraction R1: "Naive hCS Lagrangian on $\mathbb{C}^3$"

\textbf{Wrong claim.} $S_{\mathrm{hCS}}(A) = \tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}^3} \Omega \wedge \langle A\wedge \bar\partial A + \tfrac{2}{3}A^3\rangle$ for $A \in \Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g})$ is a well-defined classical action.

\textbf{Precise error.} For $A \in \Omega^{0,1}$, the integrand $\Omega \wedge A \wedge \bar\partial A$ is a $(3, 2)$-form: degrees $(3,0) + (0,1) + (0,1+1) = (3, 2)$. On real $6$-manifold $\mathbb{C}^3$ (real dimension $6$), top real degree is $6$; $(3,2)$ is real dimension $5$; this is not a top form. The naive "Lagrangian" does not define a number by integration.

\textbf{Ghost theorem.} The correct classical datum is the BV upgrade of Theorem~\ref{f02:thm:classical-bv}: the field $\mathcal{A}$ runs over the full Dolbeault complex $\Omega^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^3, \mathfrak{g})[1]$, and the action $\int \Omega \wedge \langle \mathcal{A}, \bar\partial\mathcal{A} + \tfrac{1}{3}[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]\rangle$ \emph{does} define a top-form integrand (type $(3,3)$) at each Dolbeault-degree channel. ### Retraction R2: "The $E_3$-braiding on $\mathrm{Obs}(\mathbb{C}^3)$ comes from $\pi_1(\mathrm{Conf}_2)$"

Attribution: Remark~\ref{rem:en-from-dimension} in $\mathtt{quantum\_chiral\_algebras.tex}$.

### Retraction R3: "Wave-function renormalisation = BV anomaly"

\textbf{Wrong claim.} The one-loop bubble diagram $Z^{(1)}_\mathcal{A} \neq 1$ is a genuine BV anomaly obstructing quantisation of 6d hCS on $\mathbb{C}^3$.

\textbf{Precise error.} $Z^{(1)}_\mathcal{A} \neq 1$ is wave-function renormalisation (quadratic Casimir $C_2(\mathfrak{g})$); it is absorbed by a local BV counterterm of the same type as the kinetic term. It does not introduce a new BV obstruction. A genuine BV anomaly would live in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and fail to be absorbed by a local counterterm.
