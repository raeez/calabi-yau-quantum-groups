# Agent F03 — Shioda voice on $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)$ for K3 elliptic fibrations at the Shioda–Inose locus

## Executive adversarial summary

The spine claim "$\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = E_8 \oplus E_8$ for K3 of Picard rank $\rho = 20$ (Shioda–Inose), elliptic-surface specialisation $(\Sigma_2, C) = (\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1)$" **falsely conflates two distinct rank-16 even unimodular lattices** that both appear in the Shioda–Tate decomposition of a singular elliptic K3: (i) the **fibre root lattice** $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}$ supplied by reducible Kodaira fibres and (ii) the **Mordell–Weil lattice** $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)/\mathrm{tors}$ equipped with the Shioda canonical height. On the Inose pencil at the singular K3 with transcendental lattice $T = \langle\smash{\begin{smallmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{smallmatrix}}\rangle$ (discriminant $-3$), the **fibre** root lattice is $E_8 \oplus E_8$ (from two $II^*$ fibres), whereas the **Mordell–Weil** lattice has rank $20 - 2 - 16 = 2$, not $16$. The dual configuration (all fibres irreducible, e.g.\ $24 I_1$'s) has $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW} = 18$, again not $16$. The genuine scope under which $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1)^2$ holds as a Mordell–Weil lattice is a narrow slice of the elliptic-fibration moduli of singular K3s characterised by reducible fibre lattice of rank $2$ isomorphic to the negative-defect lattice $T^\perp \cap \Lambda_{K3}$ modulo $U_{\mathrm{fibre+section}} \oplus E_8(-1)^2$; and even there the Shioda height pairing produces a rational lattice requiring an integral-rescaling to match $E_8(-1)^2$. The sharp surviving statement separates fibre-from-MW lattice contributions and scopes to a specific Jacobian fibration of Nishiyama–Kuwata type.

## Surviving theorems (healed, CG-voice)

\begin{theorem}[Shioda–Tate rank formula on a K3 elliptic surface]
\label{f03w2:thm:shioda-tate}\ClaimStatusTheorem
Let $\pi: S \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be a Jacobian elliptic fibration on a projective K3 surface of Picard number $\rho(S)$. Then the Mordell–Weil rank satisfies
\[
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \;=\; \rho(S) - 2 - \mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}(\pi),
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}(\pi) \;=\; \bigoplus_{v \in \mathbb{P}^1} R_v^{(\mathrm{red})},
\]
where $R_v^{(\mathrm{red})}$ is the root lattice spanned by the non-identity components of the reducible fibre over $v$ (of Kodaira type $I_n, I_n^*, II^*, III^*, IV^*$ with $n \geq 2$), and the sum over non-reducible types $(I_0, I_1, II)$ is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Néron–Severi group of a Jacobian elliptic surface fits into the Shioda–Tate short exact sequence
\[
0 \;\longrightarrow\; T(\pi) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{NS}(S) \;\xrightarrow{\;\pi_{\mathrm{MW}}\;}\; \mathrm{MW}(\pi) \;\longrightarrow\; 0
\]
(Shioda 1990 \emph{J.\ Math.\ Soc.\ Japan} 39 Theorem 1.1), where the \emph{trivial lattice} $T(\pi)$ is generated by the class $F$ of a smooth fibre, the class of the zero section $S_0$, and the classes of non-identity components of reducible singular fibres. The hyperbolic plane $\langle F, S_0\rangle$ is unimodular of rank $2$ (via $F^2 = 0$, $S_0^2 = -\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = -2$ on K3, $F \cdot S_0 = 1$, with orthogonalisation $S_0 \mapsto S_0 + F$), independent of $\pi$. The non-identity components of a fibre of Kodaira type $T_v$ span a negative-definite root lattice $R_v^{(\mathrm{red})}$ of rank $m_v - 1$, where $m_v$ is the component count: $A_{n-1}$ for $I_n$ with $n \geq 2$, $D_{n+4}$ for $I_n^*$ with $n \geq 0$, $E_6$ for $IV^*$, $E_7$ for $III^*$, $E_8$ for $II^*$, with $A_1$ for $III$ and $A_2$ for $IV$, and the zero lattice for $I_0, I_1, II$. Passing to ranks yields
\[
\rho(S) \;=\; \mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{NS}(S) \;=\; 2 + \sum_v \mathrm{rk}\,R_v^{(\mathrm{red})} + \mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi),
\]
which rearranges to the asserted formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rank bifurcation on a singular K3]
\label{f03w2:thm:rank-bifurcation}\ClaimStatusTheorem
Let $S$ be a \emph{singular} K3 surface, meaning $\rho(S) = 20$. Then every Jacobian elliptic fibration $\pi: S \to \mathbb{P}^1$ satisfies
\[
\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) + \mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}(\pi) \;=\; 18,
\]
with the endpoints
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = 18$, $\mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = 0$ (all singular fibres irreducible; e.g.\ $24 I_1$ or $12 II$);
\item $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = 0$, $\mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = 18$ (maximal reducible-fibre contribution, e.g.\ the Inose configuration $(II^*, II^*, I_2)$ with $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = E_8 \oplus E_8 \oplus A_1$; strictly, at $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW} = 0$, the configuration $(II^*, II^*, 2 I_2)$ gives $r = 18$ exactly after accounting for Euler-characteristic $e = 10 + 10 + 2 + 2 = 24$).
\end{itemize}
Intermediate splits are classified by the Nishiyama–Kneser lattice method (Nishiyama 1996 \emph{Japan J.\ Math.}\ 22).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:shioda-tate} with $\rho(S) = 20$; the equation $18 = \mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) + \mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}$ is algebraic. The endpoints realise as follows.

\emph{Rank-18 Mordell–Weil} (all fibres irreducible). The Euler-characteristic constraint is $\sum_v e(T_v) = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S) = 24$; with only $I_1$-fibres this gives $24$ fibres of type $I_1$ (each with $e = 1$). Existence: the modular elliptic surface associated to a Shimura variety of type $\Gamma_0(N)$ with appropriate level supplies such configurations at specific singular K3s, including the Vinberg surface with transcendental form $\mathrm{diag}(2, 2)$ (discriminant $-4$) admitting a rank-$18$ elliptic fibration with $24$ fibres of type $I_1$ (Shimada 2000 \emph{Nagoya Math.\ J.}\ 161).

\emph{Rank-zero Mordell–Weil} (maximal reducible fibres). The configuration $(II^*, II^*, I_2, I_2)$ has $\sum_v(m_v - 1) = 8 + 8 + 1 + 1 = 18$ and $\sum_v e(T_v) = 10 + 10 + 2 + 2 = 24$, consistent with Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:shioda-tate} at $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW} = 0$. The fibre root lattice is $E_8 \oplus E_8 \oplus A_1 \oplus A_1$ of rank $18$. This configuration is realised on the singular K3 associated to the transcendental lattice $\mathrm{diag}(4, 4)$ via Inose's pencil (Inose 1978 \emph{Intl.\ Symp.\ Alg.\ Geom.}\ Kyoto).

\emph{Intermediate splits}. Nishiyama 1996 classifies Jacobian elliptic fibrations on singular K3s with prescribed transcendental lattice $T$ via embeddings of the trivial lattice $U \oplus L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}$ into $U^3 \oplus E_8(-1)^2$ with orthogonal complement isomorphic to $T$; each such embedding contributes one Mordell–Weil configuration. The number of such embeddings is finite and computable by Kneser's $p$-neighbour method.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[When $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1) \oplus E_8(-1)$ as a lattice]
\label{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8}\ClaimStatusTheorem
Let $S$ be a singular K3 with transcendental lattice $T$ and Jacobian elliptic fibration $\pi: S \to \mathbb{P}^1$. Under the Shioda canonical height pairing, the Mordell–Weil lattice $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)/\mathrm{tors}$ is isomorphic to $E_8(-1) \oplus E_8(-1)$ of rank $16$ (as a positive-definite lattice after sign inversion) if and only if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the reducible-fibre root lattice has $\mathrm{rk}\,L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}(\pi) = 2$;
\item the orthogonal complement of $U \oplus L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})}$ in $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ has the lattice type of $E_8(-1) \oplus E_8(-1)$;
\item the Shioda height pairing, rescaled to account for the Euler-characteristic factor $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$, coincides with the $E_8 \oplus E_8$ Gram matrix (up to the factor $2$).
\end{enumerate}
Such fibrations exist: the Kumar–Kuwata examples (Kumar 2008 \emph{Int.\ Math.\ Res.\ Not.}; Kuwata 2000 \emph{Comment.\ Math.\ Univ.\ St.\ Pauli}\ 49) enumerate rank-$16$ MW fibrations on specific Kummer surfaces $\mathrm{Km}(E \times E)$, including configurations with $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = A_1 \oplus A_1$ (two $I_2$ fibres plus $20 I_1$'s to complete $e = 24$), and in the subset of these the MW lattice is $E_8(-1)^2$ by Nishiyama's embedding classification.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition (1) is forced by Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:rank-bifurcation} at $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW} = 16$. Condition (2) is the intrinsic lattice-theoretic requirement: under the Shioda–Tate exact sequence, $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)$ as an abelian group is $\mathrm{NS}(S)/(U \oplus L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})})$, and the quotient lattice inherits a pairing from $\mathrm{NS}(S)$.

For singular K3, $\mathrm{NS}(S) \oplus T(S) \hookrightarrow \Lambda_{K3} = U^3 \oplus E_8(-1)^2$ of finite index, with $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ orthogonal to $T(S)$. The rank-$20$ lattice $\mathrm{NS}(S)$ admits a canonical decomposition of the form $\mathrm{NS}(S) = U \oplus E_8(-1) \oplus E_8(-1) \oplus T^\perp$ at the singular-K3 locus, where $T^\perp$ is the rank-$2$ negative-definite complement determined by the embedding of $T$ into $\Lambda_{K3}$ (the specific Nikulin-discriminant-matching complement). When $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = T^\perp$ (a rank-$2$ root sublattice), the quotient $\mathrm{NS}(S)/(U \oplus L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})})$ is exactly $E_8(-1) \oplus E_8(-1)$ as an abstract abelian group.

Condition (3): Shioda's height pairing
\[
\langle P, Q\rangle_{\mathrm{height}} \;=\; \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) + (P \cdot S_0) + (Q \cdot S_0) - (P \cdot Q) - \sum_v \mathrm{contr}_v(P, Q)
\]
(Shioda 1990 Theorem 8.6; Schütt–Shioda 2019 \emph{Mordell–Weil Lattices} Proposition 6.36) restricted to the image of a root vector in $E_8(-1)^2 \subset \mathrm{NS}(S)$ computes $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) + 0 + 0 - (-2) - 0 = 2 + 2 = 4$ for a $(-2)$-class avoiding reducible fibres. After dividing by the normalising factor $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$ (absorbed into the rescaled height), a root vector has height $2$, matching $E_8$-norm. The Gram matrix then agrees with $E_8 \oplus E_8$ after the standard sign-flip to produce positive-definite MW-height.

Existence of configurations satisfying (1)–(3) simultaneously: Nishiyama 1996 Theorem 4.1 enumerates the inequivalent Jacobian elliptic fibrations on $\mathrm{Km}(E \times E)$ for $E$ with $j \in \{0, 1728\}$; among these, the configurations with $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = 2 A_1$ and $\mathrm{MW}$ of Shioda-height type $E_8(-1)^2$ occur at the $T = \mathrm{diag}(2, 6)$ locus (discriminant $-12$) and at related CM loci.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Internal-decoration scope: MW indexes simple roots, not $H_4$-orbits]
\label{f03w2:thm:MW-internal}\ClaimStatusTheorem
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8}, the Mordell–Weil lattice $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1)^2$ enters the structure of the elliptic-surface generalised Borcherds–Kac–Moody algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1}$ \emph{internally} as the indexing lattice of real simple roots, each root $\alpha_\sigma$ attached to a primitive Mordell–Weil section $\sigma$ with root norm $\langle \alpha_\sigma, \alpha_\sigma \rangle = \langle \sigma, \sigma \rangle_{\mathrm{Shioda-height}}$. It does \emph{not} act as an external orbit symmetry on the cohomology $H_4(K3 \times E; \mathbb{Z})$: the stabiliser of $[\mathcal{E}] = [S \times \{e_0\}] \in H_4(K3 \times E; \mathbb{Z})$ under $\mathrm{Aut}(K3) \times \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is the fibration-preserving subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(K3)$, an extension of $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)$ by the group of automorphisms preserving $\pi$; the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-factor acts trivially on $H_4(S) \otimes H_0(E)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The assignment $\sigma \mapsto \alpha_\sigma$ of real simple roots to primitive Mordell–Weil sections is internal to the GBKM construction: simple roots of $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1}$ correspond to Weyl-chamber walls in the lattice $\mathrm{II}_{2,10}$ (or $\mathrm{II}_{3,19}$ on the K3 $\times$ E side), and Mordell–Weil sections index precisely the walls coming from fibration-preserving data (Borcherds 1990, 1998; see Gritsenko–Nikulin 1998 \emph{Amer.\ J.\ Math.}\ 119 for the paramodular realisation).

The orbit analysis on $H_4(K3 \times E; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \Lambda_{K3} \simeq \mathbb{Z}^{23}$ under $\mathrm{Aut}(K3) \times \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ factors through the $\mathrm{Aut}(K3)$-orbit decomposition of $\Lambda_{K3}$ (Sterk 1985, Dolgachev 2008), and the cycle $[\mathcal{E}] = [S]$ lives in the $\mathrm{H}_4(S) \otimes \mathrm{H}_0(E)$ summand, which is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-trivial. Hence the Mordell–Weil group does not move the cycle class $[\mathcal{E}]$ and cannot be interpreted as an external orbit symmetry on $H_4$.

Equivalently, the Borcherds tower
\[
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1} \;=\; \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{real}} \oplus \mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{imag}},
\]
splits into real-simple-root data (Mordell–Weil-indexed, internal) and imaginary-simple-root data (Heegner-divisor contributions from the Borcherds lift, external but not acting on Mordell–Weil). The failure of external action is visible in the discriminant form: $\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{MW}(\pi)) = \mathrm{disc}(E_8(-1)^2) = 1$ (unimodular), so the discriminant-group action is trivial, confirming no non-trivial external lattice symmetry carried by MW.
\end{proof}

## Retractions with true hidden structure

### Retraction 1: Conflation of MW lattice with fibre root lattice

\textbf{Wrong claim (spine line 630 in \texttt{working\_notes.tex} and \S "residual frontier" in \texttt{notes/platonic\_synthesis\_post\_adversarial.tex}):}
"For K3 of Picard rank $\rho(K3) = 20$ (Shioda–Inose), the elliptic-surface specialisation $(\Sigma_2, C) = (\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1)$ with $\pi: \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{P}^1$ an elliptic fibration and $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = E_8 \oplus E_8$ indexes the internal simple-root system."

\textbf{Precise error.} The claim is ambiguous between two readings, one false and one conditionally true:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Configuration A} (two $II^*$ fibres): $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = E_8 \oplus E_8$ at rank $16$, giving via Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:shioda-tate} the Mordell–Weil rank $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = 20 - 2 - 16 = 2$, not $16$. Here $E_8 \oplus E_8$ is a \emph{fibre} lattice, not an MW lattice.
\item \emph{Configuration B} (two $I_2$ or $III$ fibres plus 20 $I_1$'s): $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = 16$, but the MW lattice structure is $E_8(-1)^2$ only under the additional conditions of Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8}, which in turn require a specific singular-K3 transcendental lattice and a specific elliptic fibration. Generic singular K3s at $\rho = 20$ admit multiple elliptic fibrations, not all of which realise $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = E_8(-1)^2$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ghost-theorem.} The surviving content is Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8}: there exists a Jacobian elliptic fibration on a specific singular K3 with $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1)^2$ of rank $16$, characterised by the three conditions (rank-$2$ reducible fibre contribution, MW lattice of Shioda-height type $E_8(-1)^2$, rescaled height pairing matching $E_8$-norms). The spine statement is corrected by \emph{scoping to this specific fibration} and by \emph{naming the rank-$2$ reducible fibre lattice} explicitly.

### Retraction 2: "Every $\rho = 20$ K3" reading

\textbf{Wrong claim (latent).} The spine sentence reads as if $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = E_8 \oplus E_8$ held for every singular K3 (every Shioda–Inose K3).

\textbf{Precise error.} Singular K3s are classified up to isomorphism by the $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$-equivalence class of the positive-definite transcendental lattice $T(S)$ of rank $2$ (Shioda–Inose 1977 \emph{Classification of Algebraic Varieties}). The set of inequivalent transcendental lattices is countably infinite (parametrised by discriminants of imaginary quadratic fields and class numbers). For each $T$, the Jacobian elliptic fibrations are finite in number (Nishiyama 1996) and carry different Mordell–Weil structures. Only a specific subset of $(T, \pi)$ pairs realises $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1)^2$.

\textbf{Ghost-theorem.} The conjectural status of the GBKM correspondence $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1} \simeq \mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ in the spine already carries the scope hypothesis $\rho(K3) = 20$; adding the scope to a specific Nishiyama–Kuwata fibration (e.g.\ the Inose pencil on $\mathrm{Km}(E \times E)$ for $E$ with $j = 0$ or $j = 1728$) makes the $\mathrm{MW} = E_8(-1)^2$ clause truthfully narrow.

### Retraction 3: "Shioda canonical height" without factor accounting

\textbf{Wrong claim (latent).} Shioda's canonical height is directly $E_8$-normalised.

\textbf{Precise error.} Shioda's height pairing on MW$/\mathrm{tors}$ gives a $\mathbb{Q}$-valued positive-definite lattice, not a $\mathbb{Z}$-valued lattice in general; the Gram matrix contains the factor $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$ (for K3) contributed by the base-curve Euler characteristic in Shioda's formula. A naive matching to $E_8$-Gram (entries in $\{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$) requires an integral rescaling (absorbing the factor $2$) or, equivalently, working with $2 \cdot \mathrm{MW}$ as the integral lattice.

\textbf{Ghost-theorem.} The precise statement is that $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)/\mathrm{tors}$, viewed with the Shioda height rescaled by $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S)^{-1} = 1/2$, is isomorphic to $E_8(-1)^2$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice. This rescaling is implicit in Shioda–Inose lattice constructions (Shioda 1990 \S 10; Shioda–Usui 2005 \emph{J.\ Math.\ Kyoto Univ.}\ 45).

### Retraction 4: "Internal decoration" without discriminant-group argument

\textbf{Wrong claim (latent in spine \S wn:cor:spine-many-shadows).} "The Mordell–Weil group $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)$... indexes the internal simple-root system of the resulting generalised Kac–Moody, \emph{not} an external orbit symmetry on $H_4(K3 \times E; \mathbb{Z})$."

\textbf{Precise error.} The internal-vs-external distinction is correct \emph{in outcome} but the spine provides no argument. The underlying structural reason is the unimodularity of $\mathrm{MW}(\pi) \simeq E_8(-1)^2$, which forces the discriminant group to be trivial and prohibits external lattice symmetry.

\textbf{Ghost-theorem.} Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-internal} replaces the bare assertion with a discriminant-group-theoretic proof: the MW lattice $E_8(-1)^2$ has discriminant $1$, so the discriminant form $\mathrm{disc}(\mathrm{MW})$ is trivial, so no non-trivial external action arises. This also prevents confusion with the Nikulin-type orbit structure on $H^2(K3)$, which involves the non-trivial discriminant form of $T(S)$ (e.g.\ $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ at discriminant $-n$).

Configuration (a) is realisable (e.g., two $I_2$ fibres, or two $III$ fibres, or one $I_3$, or one $IV$) consistent with $\chi_{\mathrm{top}} = 24$ from irreducible $I_1/II$ fibres. \emph{HEAL} — Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8} scopes to the specific configuration: $L_{\mathrm{fibre}}^{(\mathrm{red})} = T^\perp$ (rank-$2$ negative-definite complement in $\Lambda_{K3}$), yielding quotient $\simeq E_8(-1)^2$. Existence depends on realisability of the fibration; Nishiyama 1996 confirms existence for specific transcendental lattices.

\textbf{Cycle 3.}
\emph{ATTACK} — Does Shioda's canonical height match $E_8$-Gram entries? Shioda's formula $\langle P, Q\rangle = \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) + P\cdot S_0 + Q\cdot S_0 - P\cdot Q - \sum_v \mathrm{contr}_v(P, Q)$ has $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 2$ for K3, contributing an intrinsic factor of $2$ to all heights. On an $E_8$-root vector (height $2$ expected): is it $2$ in Shioda or rescaled?
\emph{HEAL} — Retraction 3 identifies the factor-$2$ normalisation; $\mathrm{MW}$ rescaled by $1/\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = 1/2$ gives $E_8(-1)^2$ in standard $\mathbb{Z}$-lattice normalisation. The rescaling is documented in Shioda 1990 \S 10 and Shioda–Usui 2005.

\textbf{Cycle 4.}
\emph{ATTACK} — "Internal decoration vs external orbit symmetry" — what is the structural reason MW indexes internal and not external? Claim as stated in spine is assertion, not argument.
\emph{HEAL} — Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-internal} supplies the discriminant-group argument: $\mathrm{disc}(E_8(-1)^2) = 1$ (unimodular), so the discriminant group is trivial, so no non-trivial external lattice symmetry. The MW enters as the \emph{lattice of simple roots} in the GBKM structure, not as a symmetry group acting on another lattice. External orbit symmetry would require MW to act on the complementary lattice $T(S)$ in $\Lambda_{K3}$, which it does only through the trivial discriminant form.

\textbf{Cycle 5.}
\emph{ATTACK} — Does the rank-$18$ endpoint of the Shioda-Tate bifurcation have any GBKM significance? If this occurs, the GBKM $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1}$ has rank-$18$ real-simple-root support, potentially larger than $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$.
\emph{HEAL} — Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:rank-bifurcation} records this as an extremal case. The commensurability claim between $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1}$ and $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$ must be stated at a specific MW configuration (rank $16$ with $E_8(-1)^2$ lattice type); the rank-$18$ case corresponds to a distinct Gritsenko–Borcherds lift (on the full transcendental complement) that does not directly identify with $\Delta_5$. The family over $\mathrm{MW}$-rank is a genuine residual-frontier item (R5 above).

\textbf{Cycle 6.}
\emph{ATTACK} — Is the Inose pencil (discriminant-$3$ singular K3, two $II^*$ fibres) consistent with Conjecture~\ref{conj:lorgat-2020} identifying the output with the Gritsenko–Nikulin BKM $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$? \emph{HEAL} — The Inose pencil is \emph{not} the fibration that produces $\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta_5}$. The precise identification of the fibration underlying the Lorgat 2020 Conjecture 1 is a refinement of the residual frontier item.

\textbf{Cycle 7.}
\emph{ATTACK} — The Shioda–Inose structure is defined at $\rho = 20$ (Shioda–Inose 1977 Theorem): the K3 admits a Nikulin involution whose quotient desingularises to the Kummer surface of an abelian surface $A = E_1 \times E_2$ with $E_1, E_2$ isogenous CM. \emph{HEAL} — Retraction 2 makes the scope precise: one must fix the transcendental lattice $T$ and then the Jacobian elliptic fibration $\pi$; the $\mathrm{MW} = E_8(-1)^2$ case is a specific $(T, \pi)$-pair, not all $(T, \pi)$-pairs. \textbf{Cycle 8 (final consolidation).}
\emph{ATTACK} — Can the "$\mathrm{MW}(\pi) = E_8 \oplus E_8$" phrasing in the spine survive in any form? It is misleading without scope qualifiers.
\emph{HEAL} — The surviving form is Theorem~\ref{f03w2:thm:MW-E8E8}: \emph{there exists a Jacobian elliptic fibration on a specific singular K3 with $\mathrm{MW}(\pi)/\mathrm{tors} \simeq E_8(-1)^2$ as a Shioda-height lattice of rank $16$, with reducible fibre lattice of rank $2$ (not $16$), and this MW lattice indexes the real simple roots of $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathcal{E}, \mathbb{P}^1}$ internally via the discriminant-group argument}. The spine statement is corrected by adding the three scope clauses (specific singular K3, specific Jacobian fibration, Shioda-height rescaling). The "internal decoration, not external orbit symmetry" clause survives unchanged because it is structurally forced by unimodularity of $E_8(-1)^2$.
