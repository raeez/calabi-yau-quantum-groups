\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

A Calabi--Yau category, its local field observables, its Hall algebra, and a quantum double carry different operations. The comparison problem is to construct maps that preserve those operations in specified coefficient categories and completions. Hochschild operations and shifted symplectic transgression give categorical constructions under their respective hypotheses. A specified elliptic BV theory supplies local observables in its completed coefficient category. Geometric pushforward to a curve retains their factorization maps. A comparison with a separately specified field theory or chiral algebra requires the corresponding action and collision maps. Oriented extension correspondences, under the stated base-change and completion hypotheses, supply Hall multiplication. A paired triangular Hopf datum supplies a completed double and exchange operators on the declared continuous modules.

On smooth projective K3 moduli spaces, selected correspondences define algebras of actual cohomological operators. A specified tensor category and fibre functor instead give a matrix-coefficient bialgebra. Comparing these constructions requires their action maps. For $K3\times E$, the Borcherds root algebra and its Fock character are independently defined. A geometric primitive comparison requires the Hall coefficients, orientations, brackets, and parity-refined state spaces. The finite Hamiltonian results include the relative Rees comparison and oscillator quotient; passage to the full jet and mixed open--closed structures uses the specified compatible systems and operadic refinements. The BV constructions describe centres, deformation algebras, and master equations for a supplied complete mixed boundary datum. A physical or gravitational interpretation additionally requires a comparison of fields, boundary actions, propagators, and quantizations.

\chapter*{The comparison problem}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The comparison problem}
Local collision laws and automorphic characters belong to different mathematical constructions. The diagram records the comparison problems between them:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{collision}&\longrightarrow\text{ordered fusion}\longrightarrow\text{centre}\longrightarrow\text{Hall extension}\\
&\longrightarrow\text{quantum double}\longrightarrow\text{character}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each arrow requires a map in a specified category, together with its grading, completion, and preserved operations.

For a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra, the relative Rees comparison gives a quasi-isomorphism from relative Hochschild cochains to the Chevalley complex whose differential is scaled by the Rees parameter. Quadratic Hamiltonians generate the even Weyl algebra through the oscillator Casimir quotient. Scalar reduction and an augmentation give continuous stable trace maps of commutative algebras. Monoidal module fusion uses a separately specified coassociative, counital bialgebra in the chosen completed coefficient category.

The Hamiltonian jet limit requires compatible cochain transition maps and limit identifications. Higher mixed open--closed operations require the stated operadic refinements. A Hochschild centre and the Drinfeld centre of a monoidal module category are separate comparison targets. Hall, quantum-double, and physical bulk identifications require their actual comparison morphisms and the relevant orientation, descent, pairing, and analytic hypotheses. A proper filtration specifies an inverse-limit carrier; compatibility of the operations with that limit remains part of each comparison theorem.

\chapter*{Notation and normalization}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Notation and normalization}
The ground field has characteristic zero.  Stable categories are presentable and idempotent-complete.  Tensor products preserve colimits separately.  Completions are separated inverse limits of finite weight or proper-height quotients.  The Igusa normalization is
\[
 \Dtheta=64\Dmon,\qquad \ChiTen=\Dmon^2,
\]
with $\Dmon$ monic.  Physical charge is additive; Gram degree is carried through its polarization cocycle.  Bar, factorization, and Hopf coproducts retain distinct notation and distinct geometric meanings.
