\begin{center}
{\scshape Abstract}
\end{center}

\medskip

\noindent\itshape
A Calabi--Yau category~$\cC$ of dimension~$d$ carries a cyclic
$\Ainf$-structure with $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing on Hochschild homology.
A chiral algebra~$A$ on a curve carries a bar complex~$\barB(A)$,
a universal Maurer--Cartan element~$\Theta_A$, and a modular
characteristic~$\kappa(A)$ (Volumes~I--II).  This volume constructs
the bridge between them.

For $d = 2$, a functor
$\Phi \colon \CY_2\text{-}\Cat \to E_2\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}$
is constructed: cyclic $\Ainf$ to Lie conformal algebra, factorization
envelope, $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing enhancement (Theorem~CY-A$_2$).
The denominator identity of the associated Borcherds--Kac--Moody
superalgebra equals the bar-complex Euler product (Theorem~CY-B,
conditional on CY-A for $d = 3$).

For $d = 3$, the chiral algebra is natively $E_1$: the
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing obstruction vanishes topologically
($\pi_3(B\mathrm{Sp}) = 0$), but the holomorphic Chern--Simons
trivialization breaks $E_2$ to~$E_1$.  The $E_2$ braiding is
recovered through the Drinfeld center of the $E_1$-monoidal
representation category.  For toric CY$_3$ with $\Omega$-deformation,
$E_1$ universality is proved; for compact CY$_3$, it is conjectural.
The complete five-step functor chain is verified end-to-end for~$\C^3$:
polyvector fields, $\Omega$-deformation ($\sigma_3 = h_1 h_2 h_3$,
unique by $\HH^2 = 1$), factorization envelope producing
$\mathcal{W}_{1+\infty}$, Drinfeld center identifying
$\Rep^{E_2}(Y(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_1))$, quantum vertex chiral group.
The classical Lie bracket is abelian; all noncommutativity arises from
quantization.

A general CY$_3$ is assembled from local quiver charts: chambers
of the Bridgeland stability manifold index quiver-with-potential
presentations, wall-crossing mutations provide $E_1$-equivalences
between charts, and the global chiral algebra is the homotopy colimit.
$E_1$ descent is unobstructed (the space of $E_1$ gluings is contractible),
the bar construction commutes with homotopy colimits, and the modular
characteristic is atlas-independent.  The Kontsevich--Soibelman
wall-crossing formula is the gauge equivalence of $E_1$ Maurer--Cartan
elements.  DT invariants, the topological vertex, and crystal melting
admit bar-complex interpretations.

The prototype $K3 \times E$ has $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3 = \dim_\C$
and $\kappa_{\mathrm{BPS}} = 5$ (the weight of the Igusa cusp
form~$\Delta_5$); four structural obstructions prevent the shadow
tower from reconstructing~$\Delta_5$ directly
(Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-siegel-gap}).  Ten research programmes
ground the CY$_3$ frontier in formal conjectures.
For $d \geq 4$, the $E_1$ stabilization theorem identifies a Bott-periodic
$8$-fold pattern of shifted structures classified by~$\pi_d(B\mathrm{Sp})$.
Beyond the CY setting, rank-$2$ bundles with nonzero CY defect $\delta$
produce curved chiral algebras satisfying the inhomogeneous Maurer--Cartan
equation $D\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta] = m_0$, with
$m_0 = \delta \cdot \omega_C$.  The Hitchin system on a genus-$g$ curve
gives $\kappa = g$, independent of the gauge group rank.

For the quintic threefold, $E_1$ universality is proved: this is
the first compact CY$_3$ characterization.  The chain-level
$\mathbb{S}^3$-framing is constructed for the conifold but obstructed
for local~$\mathbb{P}^2$, identifying the analytic gap as the
remaining frontier.  The bar Euler product matches the Borcherds
denominator identity through arity~$6$ for $K3 \times E$.

Over $160$ compute engines with $16{,}900{+}$ tests back the numerical claims.

\section*{The bridge}

Volume~I builds the categorical logarithm: the bar construction
$\barB(A)$ for chiral algebras on curves, with five theorems
proving its existence, invertibility, Lagrangian complementarity,
leading coefficient, and polynomial Hochschild ring.  Volume~II
reads the output in three dimensions: the bar differential is the
holomorphic colour, the coproduct is the topological colour, and
the Swiss-cheese algebra on $\FM_k(\C) \times \Conf_k^{<}(\R)$
is the Steinberg presentation of the holomorphic-topological
theory.

This volume constructs the third leg of the triangle.  A
Calabi--Yau category carries cyclic $\Ainf$-structure; a chiral
algebra carries a bar complex.  The bridge functor $\Phi$ converts
the former into the latter: cyclic homology becomes shadow
obstruction tower, the CY trace becomes the modular
characteristic~$\kappa$, and the BPS spectrum becomes the
generalized root datum of a quantum vertex chiral group.  The bar
complex converts the enumerative geometry of the CY threefold ---
Donaldson--Thomas invariants, Gromov--Witten amplitudes,
automorphic forms --- into the homotopy algebra of a single
Maurer--Cartan element.

\section*{The elliptic curve as universal bridge}

The same elliptic curve $E$ appears in at least six distinct roles
across the three volumes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Base curve for chiral algebras} (Vol~I): the
  factorization algebra lives on $\Ran(E)$; genus-$1$ bar
  amplitudes are governed by the propagator $d\log E(z,w)$.
\item \emph{Sewing parameter space} (Vol~I, MC5): the modular
  parameter $\tau$ of $E$ is the sewing parameter for the
  Hilbert--Schmidt kernel.
\item \emph{Fiber in $K3 \times E$}: the DT partition function
  decomposes as a sewing of K3 fiber contributions along~$E$.
\item \emph{Jacobi form parameter space}: the Fourier coefficients
  of $\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)$ serve as root multiplicities, shadow
  tower inputs, and BPS degeneracies.
\item \emph{Siegel modular form period}: the Igusa cusp
  form $\Delta_5$ lives on $\cH_2 = \Sp_4(\Z) \backslash \bH_2$,
  parametrizing pairs of elliptic curves with a coupling.
\item \emph{Elliptic Hall algebra carrier}: the CY$_2$ quantum
  vertex chiral group is carried on $\Coh(E)$, with Felder's
  dynamical elliptic $R$-matrix.
\end{enumerate}
Every time the elliptic curve appears, it brings the same
structural package: a modular parameter, an $\SL_2(\Z)$ symmetry,
a $q$-expansion, and a sewing functor converting genus-$0$ data
into genus-$1$ data.  The elliptic curve is the modular clock of
the theory.

\section*{The soul of an algebra}

The modular characteristic $\kappa(A)$ is the single most condensed
invariant of a chirally Koszul algebra.  It is a rational number.
It controls: the genus-$1$ obstruction class ($F_1 = \kappa/24$);
the complementarity sum (Theorem~C of Vol~I); the
Faber--Pandharipande genus expansion (Theorem~D, on the
uniform-weight lane); the shadow connection residue (Koszul
monodromy $-1$); and, for $K3 \times E$, the Borcherds product
weight.

A single CY$_3$ manifold admits multiple chiral algebraizations,
each producing a different~$\kappa$.  For $K3 \times E$:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ (chiral de Rham complex),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = 2$ (derived category),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24$ (boundary lattice VOA),
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BPS}} = 5$ (Igusa weight).
These are four genuinely different algebras seeing four different
aspects of the same geometry: differential forms, coherent sheaves,
lattice vectors, and automorphic BPS content.  The
$\kappa$-spectrum $\operatorname{Spec}_\kappa(X) = \{2, 3, 5, 24, \ldots\}$
is a richer invariant than any single~$\kappa$.

\section*{Convergence and divergence}

The shadow obstruction tower and the topological string free energy
both produce genus-$g$ amplitudes, but they have opposite analytic
behaviour.  The shadow tower has Bernoulli decay
$|F_g^{\mathrm{sh}}| \sim (2\pi)^{-2g}/g$: convergent, with
radius~$2\pi$, Borel entire.  The topological string has factorial
growth $|F_g^{\mathrm{top}}| \sim (2g)!$: divergent, Gevrey-$1$,
requiring non-perturbative completion.  The shadow tower is the
algebraic soul of the topological string --- the part computable
from OPE structure constants alone, without worldsheet instantons.
For class~$\mathbf{G}$ algebras (Heisenberg, lattice VOAs), the
shadow \emph{is} the full answer.  For class~$\mathbf{M}$
(Virasoro, $\cW_{1+\infty}$), the shadow is a strict subtower,
and the instanton sum is infinite.

\section*{The Schottky prison}

At genus $g \leq 3$, the Torelli map $\cM_g \to \cA_g$ is
injective ($g = 1$), birational ($g = 2$), dominant ($g = 3$).
The shadow obstruction tower, which lives on
$\overline{\cM}_g$, sees everything at these genera.  At
$g \geq 4$, the Jacobian locus becomes a proper subvariety of
growing codimension, and the shadow tower is imprisoned in
tautological classes on $\overline{\cM}_g$.  This imprisonment is
also a strength: the shadow computes topological invariants
insulated from analytic subtleties.  The Schottky problem ---
characterising which abelian varieties are Jacobians --- is the
frontier between what the shadow tower can see and what it cannot.

\section*{The moonshine multiplier}

The K3 elliptic genus decomposes as
$\phi_{0,1} = 2\,\phi_{0,1}^{\mathrm{EZ}}$, where the factor~$2$
is $\kappa(K3) = \chi(\cO_{K3})$.  In the umbral moonshine
decomposition, $A_n = 2 \cdot \dim(\rho_n)$ where $\rho_n$ are
$M_{24}$ representations.  The bar complex provides the universal
coefficient $\kappa$; the sporadic group provides the
representation content~$\rho_n$; the physical multiplicity is their
product.  The homological side ($\kappa = 2$) is computable from
the OPE alone; the group-theoretic side ($\rho_n$) requires no
knowledge of the OPE.  The bar complex and the sporadic group see
orthogonal aspects of the same physics.

\section*{The bar complex as moral geometry}

The bar complex $\barB(A)$ converts OPE data into cohomological
data, recovering pieces of $H^*(X)$, $\chi(X)$, and the
deformation theory of~$X$.  For K3, the bar complex has
$\kappa = 2$, class~$\mathbf{M}$, and these are geometric
invariants of an algebraic object.  For a mirror pair $(X, X^\vee)$
under homological mirror symmetry:
$\mathrm{shadow}(A_{D^b(X)}) = \mathrm{shadow}(A_{\Fuk(X^\vee)})$.
The bar complex reflects the algebra into its mirror.

\section*{Organisation}

Part~I establishes the categorical foundations and develops
the $E_1$ and $E_2$ chiral theories.  Part~II constructs the bridge
functor from CY categories to chiral algebras.  Part~III studies
quantum groups and braided monoidal structure.  Part~IV works through
the standard landscape, centred on the $K3 \times E$ programme with
its thirteen structural results and ten research programmes.  Part~V
connects to the geometric Langlands programme and to Volumes~I--II.

The working notes ($\S$\ref{sec:working-notes}) record the
research frontier: the constructive gluing dream, the Leech
whisper, the $\eta^{18}$ factorisation, the Schottky--Igusa weight
question, mock modularity as incomplete knowledge, and thirty
additional thematic observations from the compute layer.
