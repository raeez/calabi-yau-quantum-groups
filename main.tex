\noindent
How does Calabi--Yau geometry produce a chiral algebra on a curve?

A Calabi--Yau category $\cA$ of complex dimension $d$ does not produce
a chiral algebra directly: it produces a canonical holomorphic
$E_d$-factorisation algebra $\cF_\cA$ on the Calabi--Yau variety $X$
carrying it. A choice of closed $(d{-}1)$-cycle
$\Sigma_{d-1} \subset X$ and reference curve $C \subset X$ then
specialises $\cF_\cA$ to an $E_1$-chiral algebra on $C$. Within $\cF_\cA$ lives a
positive effective moduli $\Meff(X)$ whose equivariant vanishing-cycle
cohomology is the positive half $Y^+(X)$; the full quantum group is the
Drinfeld double $G(X) = D(Y^+(X))$. Different $(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$ give
different $E_1$-chiral shadows of the same canonical object.
Six-dimensional holomorphic Chern--Simons realises the $d=3$ case:
$\cF_\cA \simeq \mathrm{CE}^\bullet_{\bar\partial,
\mathrm{ch}}(\cE_{\hCS}, \cO_X)$ as $E_3$-algebra in
$\mathrm{Ch}(\mathrm{Dolb})$; its specialisation on $K3 \times E$ at
$(\Sigma_2, C) = (K3, E)$ carries the chiral-bialgebra $\bH_{\Delta_5}$
whose character is $\Delta_5^{-2}$ at the Humbert divisor. Across all
eight diagonal-divisor Gritsenko--Cl\'ery paramodular forms
$\{\Delta_5, \Delta_2^{(2)}, \Delta_1^{(3)}, \Delta_1^{(4)},
\Delta_{1/2}^{(5)}, \Delta_1^{(6)}, \Delta_{1/4}^{(7)},
\Delta_0^{(8)}\}$ the universal Borcherds weight identity
\[
\kBKM(\Phi_N) \;=\; c_N(0)/2
\]
holds, with cover assignment $\{\Sp_4, \Mp_4, \widetilde{\Mp}_4\}$
selected by weight integrality. The CHL sub-slice $N \in \{1,2,3,4,6\}$
realises each form as a BKM denominator on the twisted-reduced
Donaldson--Thomas moduli of $(K3 \times E)/(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})$;
the non-CHL forms $N \in \{5, 7, 8\}$ lift through Borcea--Voisin, an
order-$4$ Cheeger--Simons gerbe, and the Mongardi--Tari--Wandel
Kummer-$3$ fourfold respectively. The positive-geometry grammar is
uniform: equivariance stratifies by geometric class (toric $T^d$,
reduced $\mathbb{C}^\times$ + symplectic automorphism, orbifold
inertia, lattice-polarised period domain); the Drinfeld double is
glued from local Van~den Bergh NCCR charts, or its compact
Serre-equivariant quasi-NCCR substitute on $K3 \times E$. Through this
grammar, Borcherds Monster and Igusa $\fg_{\Delta_5}$ are sibling
$d{=}3$ specialisations, while the Fake Monster is their $d{=}5$
cousin on $K3 \times K3 \times E$, obstructed from $d=3$ by a rank
count.

\medskip

\noindent\itshape
The $d$-indexed correspondence $\{\Phi_d\}_{d \geq 1}$ is a
per-$d$ family of symmetric-monoidal assignments rather than
a single category-theoretic functor
(Remark~\ref{rem:phi-not-unified-functor}),
\[
 \Phi_d \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(\cM_d),
 \qquad
 n = \begin{cases}\infty, & d = 1, \\ 2, & d = 2, \\
 1, & d \ge 3,\end{cases}
\]
characterised by four universal properties:
(U1)~\emph{Hochschild pullback},
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Phi_d(\cC)) \simeq \mathrm{CC}_\bullet(\cC)$
as factorisation coalgebras;
(U2)~\emph{CY-morphism functoriality}, wall-crossing in
$\cC$ pulls to $R$-matrix gauge transformations on $\Phi_d(\cC)$;
(U3)~\emph{Drinfeld-centre compatibility},
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi_d(\cC))) \simeq
\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\Phi_d(\cC)))$ at $d \ge 3$;
(U4)~\emph{standard-input recovery},
$\Phi_1(\Coh(E)) = $ lattice VOA at $d = 1$,
$\Phi_2(D^b(K3)) = $ Mukai Heisenberg at $d = 2$,
$\Phi_3(\CoHA(\C^3)) = Y^+(\widehat{\fgl}_1)$ at $d = 3$.
The four-step recipe (cyclic $\Ainf \to$ Lie conformal
$\to$ factorisation envelope $\to$ $\mathbb{S}^d$-enhanced
quantisation) proves existence and uniqueness.
\textbf{At $d \ge 3$, $\Phi_d(\cC)$ is $\Eone$-chiral; the
$\Etwo$-braiding lives on $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\Phi_d(\cC)))$
only}, never on the chiral algebra itself
($\pi_1(\Conf_2(\R^d)) = 0$ for $d \ge 3$).

\textbf{CY-A} ($d \in \{1,2\}$ unconditional; $d = 3$
unconditional on toric/formal CY$_3$; $d \ge 4$ via
stabilisation). $\Phi_d$ exists at every $d$. At $d = 2$ via
Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos $\mathbb{S}^2$-framing (CY-A$_2$,
unconditional). At $d = 3$ via the $(\infty,1)$-categorical
vanishing $\HH^{-2}_{\Eone}(\cC) = 0$ and Goodwillie
contractibility of the $E_3$-lifting space (CY-A$_3$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:derived-framing-obstruction}); unconditional
for toric and formal CY$_3$ ($\C^3$, conifold, local $\bP^2$,
local $\bP^1 \times \bP^1$); conditional for compact non-formal
CY$_3$ (quintic, $K3 \times E$, generic complete-intersection)

\part{\texorpdfstring{Foundations: CY Categories and Cyclic $\Ainf$}{Foundations: CY Categories and Cyclic A-inf}}
\label{part:cy-categories}
\label{part:I}

\noindent
The point of departure is the categorical datum: a Calabi--Yau category
$\cC$ of dimension~$d$, equipped with a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure encoding
the non-degenerate trace $\Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$.
This part establishes the language in which the rest of the volume is
written.  Everything here is $d$-independent and unconditional: the
definitions and constructions apply equally to CY surfaces, threefolds,
and higher-dimensional categories.  The reader who has worked through
Volumes~I and~II will recognize the bar-cobar machine and the modular
characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ as the output of a chiral algebra;
the present volume constructs the \emph{input} to that machine, and
this first part prepares the categorical raw material from which the
input is fashioned.

The CY datum feeds into the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation},
Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}) whose stage~$1$ is the canonical
$E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on the CY$_d$
target, and whose stage~$2$ is the specialisation
$\int_{\Sigma_{d-1}}(-)|_C$ along a codimension-one cycle and a
reference curve.  Everything assembled in this part --- the CY pairing,
the cyclic $\Ainf$ refinement, the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree
$1 - d$ on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$, the Hochschild calculus of $\cC$ ---
is the categorical input to $\PhiFA_d$: the stage-one functor consumes
precisely this data through Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality and
Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality on $X$.

Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} introduces the central question (how
does CY geometry produce chiral algebras?), states the four main
theorems CY-A through CY-D with their current status, and provides a
guide for the reader with three recommended paths through the volume.
Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories} develops CY categories: smooth proper
dg categories with Serre functors, the CY pairing, and the four
standard families (derived categories, Fukaya categories, matrix
factorizations, and quantum group representations).
Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf} constructs cyclic $\Ainf$-structures and
the Connes $B$-operator: the CY trace lives in negative cyclic
homology $\HC^-_d(\cC)$, not merely as a map $\HH_d \to k$, and the
$\mathbb{S}^d$-framing that governs the operadic level of the output
is encoded in this cyclic refinement.  Chapter~\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}
builds the Hochschild calculus (homology, cohomology, the
Gerstenhaber bracket, and the Lie conformal algebra
$\mathfrak{L}_\cC$) that mediates between the categorical CY
condition and the factorization envelope of
Part~\ref{part:bridge}.

The key theorem of this part is structural rather than dramatic: it
is the identification of the Hochschild complex $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$
as the correct receptacle for the CY data, carrying both the
$\mathbb{S}^d$-action from the trace and the Gerstenhaber bracket
from the $\Ainf$-structure.  These two pieces of data (the framing
and the bracket) are precisely what the functor family~$\{\Phi_d\}_{d \geq 1}$ of
Part~\ref{part:bridge} consumes, and they are equivalently the data
consumed by stage~$1$: the Gerstenhaber bracket of degree $1 - d$
feeds $\PhiFA_d$ via formality, the $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing fixes the
$E_d$-structure on the resulting factorisation algebra.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Into Part~\ref{part:bridge}.}
$\mathfrak{L}_\cC = (\HH_{\bullet+1}(\cC),\ \text{Gerstenhaber bracket},\ \Sd\text{-framing})
 \;\xrightarrow{\;U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X\;}\;
 A_\cC \in \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(X)$,
with $n = 2$ at $d = 2$, $n = 1$ at $d \ge 3$.  Equivalently, the
chain-level four-step passage of Part~\ref{part:bridge} is the
explicit realisation of the specialisation
$\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}(\PhiFA_d(\cC))$ on the reference curve $C$:

\part{The CY-to-Chiral Functor}
\label{part:bridge}
\label{part:II}

\noindent
The CY-to-chiral correspondence is a family $\{\Phi_d\}_{d \geq 1}$:
\[
 \Phi_d \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat
 \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(X),
 \qquad
 n = \begin{cases} 2 & d = 2, \\ 1 & d \ge 3. \end{cases}
\]
Input: a smooth proper CY-category $\cC$ with cyclic $\Ainf$-structure
and CY trace $\Tr \colon \HC^-_d(\cC) \to k$. Output: a chiral algebra
$A_\cC$ on a smooth curve $X$. Four steps.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Two-stage factorisation (organising principle).}
Each $\Phi_d$ factors canonically through an $E_d$-holomorphic
factorisation algebra on the CY$_d$ target
(Theorem~\ref{thm:phi-two-stage-factorisation},
Definition~\ref{def:phi-fa-and-sp}):
\[
 \Phi_d \;=\; \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \,\circ\, \PhiFA_d,
 \qquad
 \PhiFA_d \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow \EdHolFA(X),
 \qquad
 \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \colon \EdHolFA(X) \longrightarrow \EnHolFA(C),
\]
with stage~$1$ the canonical $E_d$-holomorphic factorisation algebra
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ on $X$, assembled from the Gerstenhaber bracket of
degree $1 - d$ on $\HH^\bullet(\cC)$ via Kontsevich--Tamarkin formality
and Costello--Gwilliam--Li locality, and stage~$2$ the chiral
specialisation $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}(\cF) = (\int_{\Sigma_{d-1}} \cF)|_C$
along a codimension-one cycle $\Sigma_{d-1} \subset X$ and a reference
curve $C \subset X$.  Stage~$1$ is pinned up to contractible choice by
the CY datum; stage~$2$ is determined by the geometric datum
$(\Sigma_{d-1}, C)$.  The native operadic level $n_{\mathrm{native}}(d)$
on $C$ is $\infty, 2, 1$ at $d = 1, 2, d \ge 3$ respectively
(Proposition~\ref{prop:native-en-level}); the $\Etwo$-braided refinement
at $d \ge 3$ lives on the derived chiral centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC))$,
not on $A_\cC$ itself (Remark~\ref{rem:e2-on-drinfeld-centre}).  A
compact CY$_d$ admitting several $\Sigma_{d-1}$-fibrations generates a
family of $\Eone$-chiral shadows of the single canonical $\PhiFA_d(\cC)$
(Remark~\ref{rem:multiple-e1-specialisations}): six routes to
$G(K3 \times E)$ are six distinct specialisations of this kind, not
six applications of $\Phi_3$ to one object.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Four steps of the explicit chain-level realisation.}
The four-step cyclic-to-chiral passage below is the computational
incarnation of $\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}$ composed with the explicit
realisation of $\PhiFA_d$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $\mathfrak{L}_\cC := \bigl(\HH_{\bullet+1}(\cC),\ \{\cdot_\lambda \cdot\},\ \partial\bigr)$:
Lie conformal algebra with $\lambda$-bracket from the Gerstenhaber
bracket on $\HH^\bullet$, translation $\partial$ from the $S^1$-action.
\item $A_\cC^{(0)} := U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X\bigl(\mathfrak{L}_\cC\bigr)$:
factorization envelope (Beilinson--Drinfeld).
\item $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing:
Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos enhancement of the factorization structure.
At $d = 2$: $\Etwo$-chiral. At $d \ge 3$: $\Eone$-stabilized.
\item Quantization: Maurer--Cartan closure.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{CY-A (proved at every $d$).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item[($d = 2$)]

\item[($d = 3$)]

\part{The CY Landscape}
\label{part:examples}
\label{part:V}

\noindent\textbf{Stage-factorised landscape.}
Each CY input feeds the two-stage factorisation
$\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$
at its own dimension.  The landscape is therefore a family of
canonical stage-one outputs $\{\PhiFA_d(\cC)\}$, one per CY$_d$
input, together with a family of stage-two specialisations
$\{\SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C}\}$ determined by the geometric data of the
target: toric CY$_3$ inputs ($\C^3$, conifold, local $\bP^2$) produce
stage-one $E_3$-factorisation algebras on their toric varieties,
specialised to the reference curve through toric codimension-one
cycles; compact CY$_3$ inputs ($K3 \times E$, quintic, abelian) do
the same through their defining fibrations; Fukaya-category and
matrix-factorisation inputs supply the symplectic and LG lanes of
the same construction.  The $\kappa_\bullet$-invariants record
stage-discriminated data:
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = \chi(\cO_X)$ is a stage-one invariant of
$\PhiFA_d(\cC)$ (Künneth-multiplicative on products);
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A_\cC) = \sum_q (-1)^q h^{0,q}(X)$ is the
stage-two invariant on the specialised chiral algebra;
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}}(\Phi_N) = c_N(0)/2$ is the Fourier-coefficient
invariant of the Borcherds lift attached to the stage-two output;
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}}$ is the lattice-rank invariant of the
codimension-one cycle.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{CY-D tri-stratum
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cy-d-tri-stratum}, Beauville--Bogomolov).}
Compact CY manifolds split into three mutually exclusive strata
governed by the holonomy decomposition:
\[
 \begin{array}{l|l|l}
 \text{stratum} & \text{condition} & \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}\ \text{behaviour} \\ \hline
 \text{(I) odd-}d & d\ \text{odd},\ \omega_X = \cO_X & \chi(\cO_X) = 0\ \text{by Serre} \\
 \text{(II) strict-CY even-}d & h^{2,0} = 0 & \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24\ \text{(BCOV)} \\
 \text{(III) holomorphic-symplectic} & \omega_2\ \text{closed nondeg.} & \Xi = n + 1\ \text{on}\ K3^{[n]}

\part{Frontiers}
\label{part:frontier}
\label{part:VII}

\noindent\textbf{The structural residue.}
CY-A is the existence direction: it closes in every dimension,
including CY-A$_3$ at the $\infty$-categorical level, with
chain-level $\Ainf$-compatible $S^3$-framing for non-formal CY$_3$
(quintic, generic hypersurface) retained in the pro-object and
J-adic ambients of the raw bar complex but open at the direct-sum
chain level. What CY-A does not touch is structure: the Langlands
kernel attached to $\Phi$, the closure of the Zamolodchikov
tetrahedron at $O(\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2)$, the $\Sp_4(\Z)$
covariance of the $E_3$ $S$-matrix, and the non-abelian K3 Yangian
extending the abelian case. These four problems are the minimal
set of structural obstructions whose resolution would promote
CY-A from an existence theorem to a classification; the present
Part is their catalogue.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage-distinguished frontier.}
The open problems below distribute across the two stages of the
factorisation $\Phi_d = \SpCh_{\Sigma_{d-1}, C} \circ \PhiFA_d$:
the non-abelian $Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$ lives on the stage-two output
$A_\cC = \SpCh_{\Sigma_1^{\mathrm{Nakajima}}, C}(\PhiFA_2(D^b\Coh(K3)))$
as a matrix refinement of the abelian Mukai Heisenberg; the ZTE
tetrahedron correction $S^{\mathrm{corr}} = S^{\mathrm{fact}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}^2 T$
closes at the derived chiral centre of $\Rep^{\Eone}(A_\cC)$, a
stage-two $\Etwo$-braided locus; the $\Sp_4(\Z)$-covariance of
$\Phi_{10}$ controls the Fourier--Jacobi decomposition of the
stage-two specialisation $\SpCh_{K3, E}(\PhiFA_3(D^b\Coh(K3 \times E)))$;
geometric Langlands reformulated as a CY correspondence attaches the
bulk leg to the derived centre of $A_\cC$ and the boundary leg to
$A_\cC$ itself, a stage-two/centre duality rather than a single
functor identity.  Root-of-unity categorification at the chiral
level is a stage-two refinement of $\Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(A_\cC))$ at
$q = \exp(2\pi i / N)$, where the stage-one factorisation algebra
retains its $E_d$-structure and the specialisation acquires a
modular tensor-category refinement.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Front~1: nonabelian $Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$.}

$V_k(\widehat{\fsl}_2)$; $\fsl_2$ Serre constraint
\[

 \;=\;

 \;=\;

 \qquad
 D = 3,
\]
($P_2 = 0$ at leading order proved; exact-vanishing conjectural).
The Yangian candidate sitting over the Mukai lattice
$H^{\mathrm{ev}}(K3, \Z)$ of signature $(4,20)$ is
$Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4, 20))$, not $Y(\mathfrak{gl}(4|20))$ and not
Kac's $\osp(4|20)$: the Mukai form is symmetric indefinite
(orthogonal), symmetric on both even and odd parts, hence the
structure-preserving Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{so}(4, 20)$. A
Hodge-parity $\Z/2$-super-extension $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{so}(4|20))$

Hodge even/odd split on $H^{\mathrm{ev}}(K3, \Z)$ is imposed;

symplectic form on the odd part. The rank-$(4,20)$ reflection

reflection Berezinian are the open directions.
