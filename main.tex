\begin{center}
{\scshape Abstract}
\end{center}

\medskip

\noindent\itshape
The bar--cobar machine of Volumes~I and~II extracts modular
invariants from a chiral algebra~$\cA$ on a curve~$X$: the ordered
bar complex $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\cA) = T^c(s^{-1}\bar\cA)$ with its
deconcatenation coproduct, the universal Maurer--Cartan element
$\Theta_\cA$, the modular characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(\cA)$,
and the $\SC^{\mathrm{ch,top}}$ structure on the derived centre
pair. The chiral algebra is the \emph{input}; Volumes~I and~II
assume it. This volume constructs it---from Calabi--Yau geometry.
The construction is a functor
\[
 \Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat \longrightarrow
 E_n\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}
 \qquad (n = \infty \text{ at } d = 1;\; n = 2 \text{ at } d = 2;\;
  n = 1 \text{ at } d \geq 3)\,,
\]
proved for all~$d$ (Theorem~CY-A). The $\En$-chiral Koszul
duality (Theorem~CY-B) is proved at $d = 3$ via the Verdier
spectral functor: $\Eone$-Koszul on~$\cA$ induces $\Etwo$-braided
equivalence on the Drinfeld centre $\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA))$,
identified at every spectral sequence page. For $d = 2$, the
output is natively $\Etwo$-chiral (Theorem~CY-A$_2$). For
$d = 3$, the chain-level $\mathbb{S}^3$-framing
obstruction---the central open problem of the first edition
of this volume---is resolved by an $\infty$-categorical argument:
$\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ for every smooth proper CY$_3$ category,
and the space of $E_3$-liftings is contractible
(Theorem~CY-A$_3$), producing an $\Eone$-chiral algebra whose
braided structure is recovered via the Drinfeld centre. The
modular CY characteristic (Theorem~CY-D) is proved at $d = 2$
in the form $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi^{\CY}$ (Serre duality
$S_\cC = [2]$ kills the one-loop correction) and is a
dimension-stratified programme at $d \geq 3$. The quantum group
realization (Conjecture~CY-C) remains conjectural; its abelian
level, $C(\mathfrak{g}, q) = D(Y^+(\mathfrak{g}_{K3}))$, is
explicitly constructed.

The \emph{Mukai Lagrangian}
$\Lambda_X \subset H^\bullet(X, \Q)$ with pairing
$\langle v, w \rangle_{\mathrm{Muk}}
= -\!\int_X v^\vee \cdot w \cdot \mathrm{td}(X)$
carries two structures simultaneously: the Borcherds singular
theta lift targets it
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}, weight
$c(0)/2$), and the critical cohomological Hall algebra
$\CoHA(Q_X, W_X) \cong Y^+(\widehat{\frakg}_X)$
(Schiffmann--Vasserot, Rap\v{c}\'ak--Soibelman--Yang--Zhao) has
$\Lambda_X$ as its weight lattice, with universal $R$-matrix
from Maulik--Okounkov stable envelopes on $\mathrm{Hilb}^n(X)$.
Both encode the generating series of BPS invariants on
$\Lambda_X$: once as an automorphic form, once as the universal
$R$-matrix of a quantum group. The CY-to-chiral functor $\Phi$
(Theorem~CY-A$_3$) is the arithmetic-to-Lie bridge; the
Borcherds singular theta lift
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borcherds-lift-universal}) is the
Lie-to-arithmetic bridge; their conjectural composition is the
identity on $\Lambda_X$ up to the
Grothendieck--Teichm\"uller action on the chiral
algebra---the same structural redundancy encoded in Volume~II's
seven faces of~$r(z)$. The principal example, the K3 Yangian
$Y(\mathfrak{g}_{K3})$, has $24$ generators, Mukai-signature
Serre relations, and degree-$(24, 24)$ structure function; it
is the first nontrivial quantum group constructed from
Calabi--Yau geometry via~$\Phi$. For the threefold
$X = K3 \times E$, the naive six-way convergence of CY-C is
\emph{falsified} at the generator level: the
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$-stratification reads $\{3, 12, 24\}$
across the six routes. The correct universal object is the
\emph{pentagon colimit} over five intertwiners $\beta_{13},
\beta_{34}, \beta_{45}, \beta_{56}, \beta_{61}$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:cy-c-pentagon-colimit}), with the
Borcherds branch $R_2$ as source and the denominator identity
$\Phi_{10} = \Delta_5^2$. The cascade extends to $d = 5$:
$\Phi_5$ is proved at $X = K3 \times K3 \times E$ via
K\"unneth on the Hodge diamond
$h^{0,q}(X) = (1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1)$, with $\Z/2$-gerbe
obstruction vanishing by Whitney + Wu. On the compact
quintic, strict formality of the Bondal--Van den Bergh DG
algebra $A_{\mathrm{BVDB}} = \End^\bullet\!\bigl(
\bigoplus_{i=0}^4 \cO_{X_5}(i)\bigr)$ is \emph{refuted}: the
Yukawa coupling $Y_3 = H^3 = 5$ is the non-zero
Kodaira--Spencer $m_3$, and the correct statement is curved
formality with Yukawa as BCOV datum.

Thirteen structural results beyond the functor.
(1)~\emph{Stokes $=$ wall-crossing}: Borel singularities of the
shadow tower are walls of marginal stability; trans-series sectors
are BPS sectors.
(2)~\emph{Non-abelian MTC from 6d geometry}: at level $N = 3$,
the K3 quantum group produces a non-abelian modular tensor
category with $3200$ modules, quantum dimension $d_1 = \sqrt{2}$,
and Ising fusion---the first such category from
six-dimensional geometry.
(3)~\emph{Chiral volume conjecture}:
$\lim_{N \to \infty} (1/N) \log |Z_N| = (1/2\pi)\, |AJ(C)|$,
the Abel--Jacobi period.
(4)~\emph{BCOV $=$ shadow tower}: genus-$g$ holomorphic anomaly
amplitudes equal the integrated degree-$(g + 1)$ shadow invariants.
(5)~\emph{Mock modular K3} proved at $d = 2$ in four steps: shadow
$24\,\eta^3$, mock theta transform, Zwegers completion,
Borcherds lift.
(6)~\emph{Universal $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ stratified by Hodge
type}: $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24 +
[h^{1,0} > 0] \cdot (h^{3,0} + 2)$.
(7)~\emph{Chiral Khovanov complex} $\mathrm{CKh}_{2,n}$ has
dimension~$2^g$ with Mukai bigrading.
(8)~\emph{Niemeier rigidity}:
$24 = \mathrm{rank}(\widetilde\Lambda) =
\#\{\text{Niemeier lattices}\}$, a structural identity, not a
coincidence.
(9)~\emph{Categorical DT}:
$D^b(B_n(H_{\mathrm{Muk}})) \simeq D^b(\mathrm{Hilb}^n(K3))$.
(10)~\emph{ZTE correction matrix $T$} computed explicitly
(exact rational, persymmetric).
(11)~Shadow tower computed through $m_{10}$.
(12)~\emph{No native CY$_4$ Yangian}: the first Pontryagin class
$p_1 \in \pi_4(BU) = \Z$ blocks the $E_4$ structure.
(13)~\emph{$E_n$-hierarchy}: the effective framing obstruction
$\Obs_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \in \pi_d(BU)$ is trivial at
$d \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 7\}$ and $\Z$-valued at every even~$d$;
$E_3$-bar cohomology is $(1 + t)^{3g} = 2^{3g}$ for shadow
classes~$\mathbf{L}$, $\mathbf{C}$ and $6^g$ for class~$\mathbf{M}$.

\part{Foundations: CY Categories and Cyclic $\Ainf$}
\label{part:cy-categories}

\noindent
The point of departure is the categorical datum: a Calabi--Yau category
$\cC$ of dimension~$d$, equipped with a cyclic $\Ainf$-structure encoding
the non-degenerate trace $\Tr \colon \HH_\bullet(\cC) \to k[-d]$.
This part establishes the language in which the rest of the volume is
written.  Everything here is $d$-independent and unconditional: the
definitions and constructions apply equally to CY surfaces, threefolds,
and higher-dimensional categories.  The reader who has worked through
Volumes~I and~II will recognize the bar-cobar machine and the modular
characteristic $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ as the output of a chiral algebra;
the present volume constructs the \emph{input} to that machine, and
this first part prepares the categorical raw material from which the
input is fashioned.

Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} introduces the central question---how
does CY geometry produce chiral algebras?---states the four main
theorems CY-A through CY-D with their current status, and provides a
guide for the reader with three recommended paths through the volume.
Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-categories} develops CY categories: smooth proper
dg categories with Serre functors, the CY pairing, and the four
standard families (derived categories, Fukaya categories, matrix
factorizations, and quantum group representations).
Chapter~\ref{ch:cyclic-ainf} constructs cyclic $\Ainf$-structures and
the Connes $B$-operator: the CY trace lives in negative cyclic
homology $\HC^-_d(\cC)$, not merely as a map $\HH_d \to k$, and the
$\mathbb{S}^d$-framing that governs the operadic level of the output
is encoded in this cyclic refinement.  Chapter~\ref{ch:hochschild-calculus}
builds the Hochschild calculus---homology, cohomology, the
Gerstenhaber bracket, and the Lie conformal algebra
$\mathfrak{L}_\cC$---that mediates between the categorical CY
condition and the factorization envelope of
Part~\ref{part:bridge}.

The key theorem of this part is structural rather than dramatic: it
is the identification of the Hochschild complex $\HH_\bullet(\cC)$
as the correct receptacle for the CY data, carrying both the
$\mathbb{S}^d$-action from the trace and the Gerstenhaber bracket
from the $\Ainf$-structure.  These two pieces of data---the framing
and the bracket---are precisely what the functor~$\Phi$ of
Part~\ref{part:bridge} consumes.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Into Part~\ref{part:bridge}.}
$\mathfrak{L}_\cC = (\HH_{\bullet+1}(\cC),\ \text{Gerstenhaber bracket},\ \Sd\text{-framing})
 \;\xrightarrow{\;U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X\;}\;
 A_\cC \in \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(X)$,
with $n = 2$ at $d = 2$, $n = 1$ at $d \ge 3$.

\part{The CY-to-Chiral Functor}
\label{part:bridge}

\noindent
\[
 \Phi \colon \CY_d\text{-}\Cat
 \longrightarrow
 \En\text{-}\mathrm{ChirAlg}(X),
 \qquad
 n = \begin{cases} 2 & d = 2, \\ 1 & d \ge 3. \end{cases}
\]
Input: a smooth proper CY-category $\cC$ with cyclic $\Ainf$-structure
and CY trace $\Tr \colon \HC^-_d(\cC) \to k$. Output: a chiral algebra
$A_\cC$ on a smooth curve $X$. Four steps.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $\mathfrak{L}_\cC := \bigl(\HH_{\bullet+1}(\cC),\ \{\cdot_\lambda \cdot\},\ \partial\bigr)$:
Lie conformal algebra with $\lambda$-bracket from the Gerstenhaber
bracket on $\HH^\bullet$, translation $\partial$ from the $S^1$-action.
\item $A_\cC^{(0)} := U^{\mathrm{fact}}_X\bigl(\mathfrak{L}_\cC\bigr)$:
factorization envelope (Beilinson--Drinfeld).
\item $\mathbb{S}^d$-framing:
Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos enhancement of the factorization structure.
At $d = 2$: $\Etwo$-chiral. At $d \ge 3$: $\Eone$-stabilized.
\item Quantization: Maurer--Cartan closure.
\end{enumerate}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{CY-A (proved at every $d$).}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item[($d = 2$)]
$\mathbb{S}^2$-framing (Kontsevich--Vlassopoulos) $+$ factorization
envelope (Costello--Gwilliam) produce an $\Etwo$-chiral algebra.
\item[($d = 3$)]
$\infty$-categorical: $\HH^{-2}_{E_1}(\cC) = 0$ by unit-connectedness,
the space of $E_3$-liftings is contractible, all Goodwillie layers
vanish. Chain-level $[m_3, B^{(2)}] \ne 0$ is \emph{not} an obstruction.
\item[($d \ge 4$)]
$\Eone$-stabilization
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}):
$\pi_d(BU) \ne 0$ at $d$ even prevents additional $\Eone$-factors;
the output is $\Eone$.
\end{itemize}

Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-to-chiral} proves CY-A at every $d$.
Chapter~\ref{ch:m3-b2-saga} recounts the $[m_3, B^{(2)}]$ obstruction
analysis at the chain level---three retracted proofs, four
independent computations, the Costello TCFT resolution
$\{b, B^{(2)}\} = 0$ on the total differential with cross-arity
cancellation through Stasheff $\Ainf$-relations---as the pedagogical
anatomy of the $\infty$-categorical shift that CY-A$_3$ required.

Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-chiral-algebras} provides a second,
independent route to quantum chiral algebras via holomorphic
Chern--Simons theory, connecting to the Costello programme:
$3d$ holomorphic CS produces Kac--Moody algebras, $5d$ holomorphic
CS produces affine Yangians, and the conjectural $6d$ holomorphic
theory produces quantum toroidal algebras.  The hCS route does
not replace the functor~$\Phi$---it constructs chiral algebras
from gauge-theoretic rather than categorical input---but the two
routes agree where both apply, providing a powerful consistency
check.

\part{The $\En$ Hierarchy and Chiral Quantum Groups}
\label{part:en-hierarchy}

\noindent
The functor~$\Phi$ of Part~\ref{part:bridge} produces chiral algebras
at a specific operadic level: $\Etwo$ for $d = 2$, $\Eone$ for
$d \geq 3$.  This part develops the algebraic structures that live at
each level---the operadic heart of the volume.  Where
Part~\ref{part:bridge} asks ``does the functor exist?''\ and answers
yes, this part asks ``what algebraic structures does the output
carry?''\ and answers: $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axioms, $\Etwo$-braided
monoidal representation categories, the Drinfeld center passage from
$\Eone$ to~$\Etwo$, and the $\En$-stabilization theorem that controls
the entire hierarchy.  The material here is largely independent of the
proof of CY-A$_3$: the operadic theory applies to any chiral algebra
at any $\En$-level, whether or not it arises from a CY category.
The CY input provides the motivation; the algebraic infrastructure
stands on its own.

Chapter~\ref{ch:e1-chiral} treats $\Eone$-chiral algebras: ordered
factorization on $\Ran^{\mathrm{ord}}(X)$, the ordered bar complex
$B^{\mathrm{ord}}(A)$ that preserves the $R$-matrix (in contrast to
the symmetric bar $B^\Sigma(A)$ of Volume~I, which kills the Hopf
structure via averaging), and the five $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axioms
(H1--H5) that formalize the Hopf framework for CY quantum groups.
The spectral coproduct $\Delta_z$, coassociativity from Miura
multiplicativity, and the antipode are developed here; two concrete
instances---the Heisenberg algebra and $\cW_{1+\infty}$---are
verified in full.  Chapter~\ref{ch:e2-chiral} treats $\Etwo$-chiral
algebras: braided factorization on $\C \times \C$, the Deligne
conjecture giving $\Etwo$-structure on Hochschild cochains, and
braided monoidal representation categories---the natural habitat of
quantum groups.

Chapter~\ref{ch:en-factorization} develops $\En$-factorization
homology, the framing obstruction tower
$\mathrm{Obs}_{\mathrm{eff}}(d) \in \pi_d(BU)$, and the
$\Eone$-stabilization theorem: for $d \geq 3$, the native chiral
output stabilizes at~$\Eone$, not~$E_d$.  The Bott-periodic $8$-fold
pattern (trivial obstruction at $d \bmod 8 \in \{1,3,7\}$,
$\Z$-valued at all even~$d$) organizes the full CY landscape by
operadic level.  Chapters~\ref{ch:quantum-groups}
and~\ref{ch:braided-factorization} treat the classical theory of
quantum groups (Drinfeld--Jimbo $\Uq(\frakg)$, the universal
$R$-matrix, the FRT/RTT formalism, Kazhdan--Lusztig theory at
roots of unity) and braided factorization algebras.
Chapter~\ref{ch:drinfeld-center} develops the Drinfeld center
$\cZ(\Rep^{\Eone}(\cA)) \simeq \Rep^{\Etwo}(\Zder(\cA))$: the
categorified derived center that recovers $\Etwo$-braiding from
$\Eone$-monoidal input, with explicit computations for the K3
Heisenberg ($49$-dimensional double, Fock character $1/\eta^{48}$).

The key theorems of this part are the $\Eone$-stabilization theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:e1-stabilization-cy}), the $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra
formalism, and the Drinfeld center equivalence.  The reader interested
primarily in the K3 example may proceed to
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} after Chapters~\ref{ch:e1-chiral}
and~\ref{ch:e2-chiral}; the Drinfeld center material can be
consulted as needed.

\part{The K3 Yangian}
\label{part:k3-yangian}

\noindent
The preceding parts build the machine; this part feeds it the most
important input.  Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:bridge}
construct the functor~$\Phi$ and prove it exists at all~$d$;
Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy} develops the operadic algebra of the
output.  This part applies the entire apparatus to a single family of
examples---the K3 surface, the K3 Yangian, and the threefold
$K3 \times E$---and the result is the mathematical climax of the
volume.  The K3 family is the simplest setting in which all the
structures of the programme are visible: the lattice is explicit
(the Mukai lattice $\Lambda^{4,20}$), the chiral algebra is
computable (the lattice Heisenberg VOA $H_{\mathrm{Muk}}$), the
quantum group is concrete (the abelian Yangian
$Y(\frakg_{K3})$), and the automorphic form is classical (the
Igusa cusp form~$\Phi_{10}$).

The surface~$S = K3$ is CY of dimension~$2$, and the functor
$\Phi_2$ produces the Mukai-lattice Heisenberg VOA with central
charge $c = 24$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 2 = \chi(\cO_S)$.
The K3 double current algebra~$\frakg_{K3}$ quantizes to an
abelian Yangian $Y(\frakg_{K3})$ with $24$ generators,
Mukai-signature Serre relations, and degree-$(24,24)$ structure
function; the super-Yangian $Y(\fgl(4|20))$ provides the natural
envelope.  The threefold $K3 \times E$ is CY of dimension~$3$,
and six independent \emph{constructions}---BKM (Borcherds lift),
lattice VOA (Frenkel--Kac), Kummer (orbifold $+$ resolution),
CY-to-chiral ($\Phi_3$, the only route using the functor),
sigma model (BRST cohomology), BLLPR (4d $\cN{=}2$ Schur sector)---approach the quantum vertex
chiral group whose denominator identity is~$\Phi_{10}$.  Their
conjectural convergence (Conjecture~CY-C) is the strongest evidence for the
programme's coherence.  The $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum
$\operatorname{Spec}_{\kappa_\bullet}(K3 \times E) = \{2,3,5,24\}$
records four subscripted invariants: two manifold invariants
($\kappa_{\mathrm{cat}} = 2 = \chi(\cO_{K3})$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{fiber}} = 24 = \operatorname{rank}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{Muk}})$),
fixed by the geometry, and two construction-dependent invariants
($\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 3$ from the CY-to-chiral functor~$\Phi$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{BKM}} = 5$ from the Borcherds lift),
each arising from a distinct mathematical construction.

Chapter~\ref{ch:derived-cy} develops derived categories of CY
manifolds and homological mirror symmetry as the categorical
input to~$\Phi$.  Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-chiral-algebra} constructs the K3
chiral algebra, computes the $\kappa_\bullet$-spectrum, develops the
six routes to the quantum vertex chiral group, and connects to the
Niemeier lattice classification.  Chapter~\ref{ch:k3-yangian}
presents the K3 Yangian theorem in full: generators, Serre
relations, structure function, and the super-Yangian envelope.
Chapter~\ref{ch:k3e-bkm} develops the $K3 \times E$ BKM
superalgebra~$\frakg_{\Delta_5}$, the Borcherds denominator
identity, and BPS entropy.  Chapters~\ref{ch:k3-quantum-toroidal}
and~\ref{chap:toroidal-elliptic} treat the quantum toroidal algebra
$U_{q,t}(\widehat{\widehat{\fgl}}_1)$ and the toroidal/elliptic
algebraic framework.  The final sections develop the full
$K3 \times E$ CY$_3$ programme: CY gluing, the relative chiral
algebra, ADE Yangians, and the M-theory web.

The key theorems are the K3 Yangian theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:k3-abelian-yangian-presentation}), the
shadow--Siegel gap theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:shadow-siegel-gap})
establishing four structural obstructions between the shadow tower and
$\Phi_{10}$, and the Kummer route (Steps~1--4 proved in
Proposition~\ref{prop:kummer-orbifold}).

\part{The CY Landscape}
\label{part:examples}

\noindent
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} worked out the K3 family in detail---the
most important example, but far from the only one.  This part surveys
the broader landscape of CY geometries and the chiral algebras they
produce, organized by the shadow class G/L/C/M of Volume~I.  Where
Part~\ref{part:k3-yangian} is a deep vertical cut into a single family,
this part is a horizontal sweep across the CY$_3$ landscape, providing
both evidence for the general programme and a testing ground for the
conjectural identifications of Part~\ref{part:connections}.  The
landscape reveals structural patterns invisible from any single
example: compact CY$_3$ manifolds with $\chi \neq 0$ are generically
class~M (infinite shadow depth), the toric vertex bound
$r_{\max} \leq N$ predicts which toric geometries realize each shadow
class, and the LG/CY correspondence provides consistency checks
between the matrix factorization and derived category sides.

The CY-D stratification chapter opens the landscape by organising
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}$ across all dimensions: odd-$d$ manifolds carry
$\chi(\cO_X) = 0$ identically (Serre cancellation), strict-CY even-$d$
manifolds realise the BCOV correction $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} =
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}/24$, and holomorphic-symplectic even-$d$ manifolds
(K3, K3$^{[n]}$) realise $\Xi = n+1$; the Beauville--Bogomolov
trichotomy controls the full landscape and provides the dimensional
vocabulary for every example that follows.

Chapter~\ref{ch:toric-coha} treats toric CY$_3$ manifolds and their
critical cohomological Hall algebras: the vertex $\C^3$ (class~G,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1$, the Jordan quiver giving
$Y(\widehat{\fgl}_1) \simeq \cW_{1+\infty}$), the resolved conifold
(class~G, both resolutions giving the same shadow class by flop
invariance), and local~$\bP^2$ (class~M, infinite shadow depth---not
class~L, despite having three generators).  The CoHA wall-crossing
chapter sharpens the toric story: the Kontsevich--Soibelman pentagon
is the coalgebra shadow of the motivic Hall algebra multiplication,
and the chiral KSWC formula upgrades the pentagon to a bar-level
identity on $B^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathrm{MH}(\cC))$.
Chapter~\ref{ch:matrix-factorizations} develops matrix factorization
categories $\MF(W)$ and their CY structures: for
$W \colon \bA^n \to \bA^1$, the category $\MF(W)$ is CY of
dimension~$n-2$ (not $n-1$), and the ADE classification
in four variables gives CY$_2$ structures connected to
$\cW_N$-algebras.  Chapter~\ref{ch:fukaya} treats Fukaya categories
and the symplectic input to the functor~$\Phi$: Lagrangian
submanifolds, the $\Ainf$-structure on $\Fuk(X)$, and the connection
to the wrapped Fukaya category of Liouville manifolds.

The super-Riccati chapter
develops the super-Yangian $Y_\hbar(\mathfrak{sl}(m|n))^{\mathrm{ch}}$
at general rank---the chiral shadow tower, the super-complementarity
$\kappa(Y) + \kappa(Y^!) = \max(m,n)$, and the parity-sign Riccati
recurrence---providing the super-algebra extension of the classical
Yangian programme.

Chapter~\ref{ch:quantum-group-reps} develops quantum group
representations: Kazhdan--Lusztig theory at roots of unity (where
$\Rep_q(\frakg)$ is non-semisimple and the modular tensor category
structure is nontrivial), affine Yangians, and critical CoHAs.  The
representation categories $\Rep^{\Etwo}$ are the targets of
Conjecture~CY-C, which predicts that CY categories produce braided
monoidal categories equivalent to those of classical quantum groups.
Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-trace} constructs the modular trace on CY
chiral algebras and its connection to the genus-$g$ amplitudes of
Volume~I, completing the bridge from CY geometry to modular forms.

\part{The Seven Faces of $r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$}
\label{part:connections}

\noindent
The preceding parts construct CY chiral algebras
(Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:bridge}), develop their
operadic structure (Part~\ref{part:en-hierarchy}), and compute examples
(Parts~\ref{part:k3-yangian}--\ref{part:examples}).  This part connects
the CY output to the bar-cobar machine of Volume~I and the
holomorphic-topological framework of Volume~II.  The title refers to a
single object---the classical $r$-matrix $r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$ of a CY
chiral algebra---which admits seven distinct interpretations: Koszul
dual, bar-cobar, holographic, automorphic, combinatorial, physical,
and modular.  The coincidences among these seven faces are not
accidental; they constitute the structural content of the three-volume
programme.

Chapter~\ref{ch:modular-koszul-bridge} develops the modular Koszul
bridge: the proved identification $\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}} = \chi^{\CY}$
at $d = 2$ (via Serre duality $S_\cC = [2]$ killing the one-loop
correction) and the conductor formula
$\kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A) + \kappa_{\mathrm{ch}}(A^!) = K$ relating
an algebra to its Koszul dual.  For K3, the Koszul conductor vanishes
($K = 0$, the free-field/Kac--Moody branch), consistent with the mirror
symmetry $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X)/2 + \chi_{\mathrm{top}}(X^\vee)/2
= 0$.  Chapter~\ref{ch:bar-cobar-bridge} constructs the bar-cobar
bridge to Volume~I: the shadow--Feynman dictionary identifying
$L$-loop amplitudes with the shadow coefficient~$S_{L+1}$, and the
deeper identification of the shadow tower with the $\Ainf$-coproduct
correction tower ($\delta^{(k)}$ has coefficient $= S_k$).  The
class~M Borel summability result ($E_3$ bar $= 6^g$) is developed
here, connecting the shadow class to the analytic type of the
genus-$g$ amplitude.

Chapter~\ref{ch:cy-holographic-datum-master} assembles the CY holographic datum:
the seven faces of $r_{\mathrm{CY}}(z)$ as a unified structure, now
constrained by the observation that only $24\%$ of $3d$ Chern--Simons
structures lift to $6d$.  The BKM Serre relations concentrate at
discriminant $D = 3$: $182$ generators, finitely determined.  This
chapter draws on all preceding parts and provides the synthesis that
justifies the three-volume architecture: CY geometry
(Part~\ref{part:cy-categories}) $\to$ chiral algebra
(Part~\ref{part:bridge}) $\to$ bar complex (Volume~I) $\to$ modular
form (this chapter).

\part{Frontiers}
\label{part:frontier}

\noindent
This final part collects the genuine open problems and conjectural
extensions that lie beyond the proved results of
Parts~\ref{part:cy-categories}--\ref{part:connections}.  The
CY-to-chiral functor~$\Phi$ is proved at all~$d$, the K3 Yangian is
a theorem, and the $\Eone$-chiral bialgebra axioms are established.
What remains open is substantial: the interaction with the geometric
Langlands programme, the nonabelian extension beyond $\fgl_1$, the
Zamolodchikov tetrahedron correction, root-of-unity phenomena, and
$\Sp_4(\Z)$ modularity from the $E_3$ $S$-matrix.  These are not
cosmetic gaps; they are the load-bearing open problems whose
resolution would complete the CY-to-quantum-group pipeline.

The frontier has shifted.  In the first edition of this volume, the
central open problem was CY-A$_3$ itself---the existence of the
functor~$\Phi$ at $d = 3$.  With CY-A$_3$ now proved, the frontier
moves to three directions: upward (from abelian to nonabelian quantum
groups, from $\fgl_1$ to $\fsl_2$ and beyond), outward (from the K3
family to general compact CY$_3$ manifolds, where the chart-gluing
programme replaces toric techniques), and downward (from the chiral
algebra to its representation theory, where Conjecture~CY-C predicts
that CY categories produce braided monoidal categories equivalent to
those of classical quantum groups).

Chapter~\ref{ch:geometric-langlands} develops the geometric Langlands
connection: the relationship between CY chiral algebras and the
Beilinson--Drinfeld theory of chiral algebras on the moduli of
$G$-bundles, the role of the Drinfeld center in the local geometric
Langlands correspondence, and the prospects for a CY realization of
the global Langlands programme.  The Feigin--Frenkel center, D-modules
on $\mathrm{Bun}_G$, and CY Langlands duality are developed here.
The conjectural identification ``Langlands $=$ Koszul duality'' (the
geometric Langlands correspondence as a manifestation of the Koszul
duality of Part~\ref{part:connections}) is stated as a conjecture with
the evidence for and against.
